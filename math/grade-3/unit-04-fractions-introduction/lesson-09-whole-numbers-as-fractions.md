# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 4, Lesson 9

## Topic
Whole Numbers as Fractions

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Express whole numbers as fractions with a denominator of 1 (e.g., 3 = 3/1)
- Understand that when the numerator equals the denominator, the fraction equals 1 whole (e.g., 4/4 = 1)
- Generate multiple fraction names for whole numbers (e.g., 1 = 2/2 = 3/3 = 4/4)
- Place whole numbers on a fraction number line

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of numerator and denominator (Lessons 2-3)
- Equivalent fractions (Lesson 8)
- Fractions on a number line (Lessons 4-5)

## Materials Needed
- Fraction strips (halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, eighths)
- Fraction circles (divided into halves, thirds, fourths)
- Number lines (0 to 3, with space to mark fractions)
- Paper and pencil
- Dry-erase board and marker

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review equivalent fractions and activate thinking about wholes.

1. "Fill in the blank: 1/2 = ___/4." (2/4) "How do you know?" (Both cover the same length on fraction strips)
2. "If a pizza is cut into 4 slices and you eat all 4 slices, how much of the pizza did you eat?" (The whole pizza, or 1 pizza)
3. "What fraction would describe eating all 4 slices of a pizza cut into 4 pieces?" (4/4) "Is 4/4 the same as 1 whole? Let's find out today!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect whole numbers to fraction notation.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Whole:** The entire amount before any parts are taken away; equals 1
- **n/n:** Any fraction where the numerator and denominator are the same number; always equals 1 whole
- **n/1:** Any whole number written as a fraction with 1 as the denominator

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: When numerator equals denominator, the fraction equals 1.**

Hold up a fraction circle divided into 4 equal parts. Shade all 4 parts.

"How many parts does this circle have?" (4) "How many are shaded?" (4) "So the fraction is 4/4. But look — the entire circle is shaded. 4/4 means 4 out of 4 parts, which is the whole thing. 4/4 = 1."

Repeat with other fraction circles:
- Shade all parts of a circle cut into 2: "2/2 = 1."
- Shade all parts of a circle cut into 3: "3/3 = 1."
- Shade all parts of a circle cut into 8: "8/8 = 1."

"Do you see the pattern? Any time the numerator and denominator are the same number, the fraction equals 1 whole. You have all the pieces."

**Step 2: Writing whole numbers with a denominator of 1.**

"What does 3/1 mean? The denominator is 1, so the whole is just 1 piece — it is not cut at all. The numerator is 3, so you have 3 of those whole pieces. 3/1 = 3."

Draw 3 whole circles, each undivided. "Each circle is 1/1, which is 1. Three of them is 3/1, which is 3."

More examples:
- 5/1 = 5. "Five whole pieces, each piece is the whole."
- 1/1 = 1. "One whole piece."
- 7/1 = 7. "Seven whole pieces."

"Any whole number can be written as a fraction: just put the number on top and 1 on the bottom."

**Step 3: Multiple fraction names for whole numbers.**

"We already know 1 = 2/2 = 3/3 = 4/4 = 6/6 = 8/8. What about 2?"

Use fraction strips. "If one whole strip = 2/2, then two whole strips = 4/2. So 2 = 4/2."
- Two whole strips in thirds: "One strip = 3/3. Two strips = 6/3. So 2 = 6/3."
- Two whole strips in fourths: "One strip = 4/4. Two strips = 8/4. So 2 = 8/4."

"The pattern: to write a whole number as a fraction, multiply the whole number by the denominator to get the numerator. 2 = 4/2 because 2 times 2 = 4. 2 = 6/3 because 2 times 3 = 6."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Write 1 as a fraction with a denominator of 6.

"I need all the pieces. If the whole is cut into 6 parts, I need all 6. So 1 = 6/6."

Check with fraction strips: the full strip is the same length as 6 sixths lined up end to end.

**Example B:** Write 3 as a fraction with a denominator of 4.

"Each whole has 4 fourths. I have 3 wholes: 3 times 4 = 12. So 3 = 12/4."

Draw 3 rectangles, each divided into 4 parts. Count all the parts: 12. "12 fourths is 3 wholes."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice expressing whole numbers as fractions.

**Activity 1: Matching fractions to 1.**

"Which of these equal 1 whole? Circle all that apply: 2/2, 3/4, 6/6, 1/1, 5/8, 8/8, 4/3."

(Answers: 2/2, 6/6, 1/1, 8/8 — all fractions where numerator = denominator)

"Ask: What is the rule? How can you quickly tell if a fraction equals 1?" (Numerator and denominator are the same.)

**Activity 2: Writing whole numbers as fractions.**

- Write 4 as a fraction with a denominator of 1. (4/1)
- Write 2 as a fraction with a denominator of 3. (6/3) "How did you figure it out?" (2 times 3 = 6)
- Write 1 as a fraction with a denominator of 8. (8/8)
- Write 3 as a fraction with a denominator of 2. (6/2)

"If the student is unsure, go back to fraction strips. Line up 3 whole strips, each divided into halves. Count the halves: 6. So 3 = 6/2."

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
Use fraction strips or drawings to help.

1. 3/3 = ___ (1)
2. 6/6 = ___ (1)
3. Write 2 as a fraction with a denominator of 1. (2/1)
4. Write 5 as a fraction with a denominator of 1. (5/1)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
Fill in the blanks.

1. 4/4 = ___ (1)
2. 1 = ___/8 (8/8)
3. 2 = ___/4 (8/4)
4. 3 = ___/1 (3/1)
5. Write two fraction names for the number 1, using different denominators. (Any two correct answers, e.g., 2/2 and 5/5)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
Fill in the blanks and explain your reasoning.

1. 2 = ___/3 (6/3)
2. 4 = ___/2 (8/2)
3. 1 = ___/100 (100/100) "How do you know?" (100 out of 100 equal parts is the whole thing.)
4. "Is 7/1 greater than, less than, or equal to 7? Explain." (Equal. 7/1 means 7 whole pieces with no cuts — that is just 7.)
5. "Jose says 5/4 = 1 because the numerator and denominator are close to the same. Is he right? Explain." (No. 5/4 does not equal 1 because the numerator and denominator must be exactly the same for the fraction to equal 1. 5/4 is greater than 1 because you have more pieces than make up one whole.)

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. 4/4 = ___ (1)
2. Write 3 as a fraction with a denominator of 2. (6/2)
3. "Explain why 5/5 equals 1." (The whole is cut into 5 equal parts and you have all 5 parts, which is the whole thing.)

**Mastery = All 3 correct.**

## Teacher Notes

**This lesson is conceptually simpler than Lessons 7-8** and gives students a chance to consolidate their understanding. Most students grasp n/n = 1 quickly once they see it with fraction circles. The n/1 = n idea is also fairly intuitive — "no cuts means the whole piece."

**Why this matters for later math:** In Grades 4-5, students will need to convert between whole numbers and fractions regularly (e.g., rewriting 3 as 12/4 to subtract 3 - 3/4). Building this skill now prevents confusion later.

**Potential confusion with fractions greater than 1:** Some students wonder if 12/4 is "allowed" because the numerator is bigger than the denominator. Reassure them: "That just means you have more pieces than fit in one whole. You have 3 wholes' worth of fourths."

**Connection to division:** 12/4 can also be read as "12 divided by 4 = 3." This connection is powerful but can be introduced lightly here — it will be formalized in later grades.

**Standard:** 3.NF.3c — Express whole numbers as fractions and recognize fractions that are equivalent to whole numbers.
