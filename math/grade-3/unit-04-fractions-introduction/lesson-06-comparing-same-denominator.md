# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 4, Lesson 6

## Topic
Comparing Fractions with the Same Denominator

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Compare two fractions that have the same denominator
- Use the symbols >, <, and = to record fraction comparisons
- Explain that when pieces are the same size, more pieces means a larger fraction
- Verify comparisons using fraction strips and number lines

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of numerator and denominator (Lessons 2-3)
- Placing fractions on a number line (Lessons 4-5)
- Familiarity with >, <, = symbols from whole number comparisons

## Materials Needed
- Fraction strips (halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, eighths)
- Number line (0 to 1, marked in fourths, sixths, and eighths)
- Colored pencils or crayons
- Paper and pencil
- Dry-erase board and marker

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review fraction naming and number line placement.

1. "What fraction is shaded?" Show a rectangle divided into 6 equal parts with 4 shaded. (4/6)
2. "Place 3/8 on a number line from 0 to 1. Is it closer to 0 or to 1?" (Closer to 0, since it is less than half)
3. "Which is greater: 5 or 3? How do you know?" (5, because 5 is farther right on the number line) "Today we will use that same thinking with fractions."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce comparing fractions that share the same denominator.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Compare:** Decide which fraction is larger, which is smaller, or if they are equal
- **Greater than (>):** The fraction on the left is larger
- **Less than (<):** The fraction on the left is smaller
- **Equal to (=):** Both fractions name the same amount
- **Same denominator:** Both fractions have the same bottom number, meaning pieces are the same size

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Start concrete with fraction strips.**

Hold up two fraction strips for fourths. Shade 1/4 on one strip and 3/4 on the other.

"Both strips are divided into fourths. The pieces are exactly the same size. This strip shows 1 piece out of 4. This strip shows 3 pieces out of 4. Which strip has more shaded?"

"When the denominator is the same, the pieces are the same size. So we just count: more pieces means a bigger fraction."

**Step 2: Connect to the number line.**

Draw a number line from 0 to 1 divided into 6 equal parts. Mark 2/6 and 5/6.

"Look — 5/6 is farther to the right. Just like with whole numbers, the number farther right on the number line is greater. So 5/6 > 2/6."

**Step 3: Introduce the comparison symbols with fractions.**

"We use the same symbols we use for whole numbers."
- 3/4 > 1/4 means 3 fourths is greater than 1 fourth
- 2/8 < 5/8 means 2 eighths is less than 5 eighths
- 3/6 = 3/6 means 3 sixths is equal to 3 sixths

"Here is the rule: when the denominators are the same, just compare the numerators. The fraction with the bigger numerator is the bigger fraction."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Compare 2/6 and 5/6.

"Both fractions have a denominator of 6, so every piece is the same size — one sixth. I have 2 pieces versus 5 pieces. 2 < 5, so 2/6 < 5/6."

Draw two rectangles each divided into 6 parts. Shade 2 parts on the first and 5 parts on the second. "See? The second one clearly has more shaded."

**Example B:** Compare 7/8 and 3/8.

"Same denominator: 8. Both are eighths, so pieces are the same size. 7 pieces versus 3 pieces. 7 > 3, so 7/8 > 3/8."

Show on a number line: 7/8 is far to the right, close to 1. 3/8 is closer to the middle.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice comparing same-denominator fractions with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Fraction strip comparisons.**

Use fraction strips to compare each pair. Lay them side by side. Write the symbol.

- Compare 1/4 and 3/4. "Which has more fourths shaded?" (3/4 > 1/4)
- Compare 5/6 and 2/6. (5/6 > 2/6)
- Compare 4/8 and 4/8. "What happens when both numerators are the same?" (4/8 = 4/8)
- Compare 1/3 and 2/3. (1/3 < 2/3)

"If the student hesitates, ask: Are the pieces the same size? Then which one has more pieces?"

**Activity 2: Number line comparisons.**

Draw a number line from 0 to 1 divided into 8 equal parts. Place each fraction on the line, then compare.

- 6/8 and 2/8 → (6/8 > 2/8) "Which is farther right?"
- 3/8 and 7/8 → (3/8 < 7/8)
- 5/8 and 1/8 → (5/8 > 1/8)

"If the student writes the symbol backward, try this: The open mouth of the symbol always faces the larger number — like a hungry alligator eating the bigger portion."

---

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
Compare. Write >, <, or =. Use fraction strips if needed.

1. 1/4 ___ 2/4 (1/4 < 2/4)
2. 3/6 ___ 1/6 (3/6 > 1/6)
3. 2/3 ___ 2/3 (2/3 = 2/3)
4. 1/8 ___ 5/8 (1/8 < 5/8)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
Compare. Write >, <, or =.

1. 5/8 ___ 3/8 (5/8 > 3/8)
2. 2/6 ___ 4/6 (2/6 < 4/6)
3. 3/4 ___ 1/4 (3/4 > 1/4)
4. 7/8 ___ 6/8 (7/8 > 6/8)
5. Circle the greater fraction in each pair: 1/3 or 2/3 (2/3); 4/6 or 5/6 (5/6)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
Compare. Write >, <, or =. Then explain your reasoning.

1. 5/6 ___ 1/6 (5/6 > 1/6)
2. 3/8 ___ 7/8 (3/8 < 7/8)
3. "Sam says 2/4 > 3/4 because 2 comes before 3. Is Sam correct? Explain." (No. 3/4 > 2/4 because the pieces are the same size and 3 pieces is more than 2 pieces.)
4. "Write two fractions with a denominator of 6 where the first is greater than the second." (Any valid pair, e.g., 5/6 > 2/6)
5. "Can two fractions with the same denominator and different numerators ever be equal? Why or why not?" (No. Same-size pieces, different number of pieces means different amounts.)

---

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. Compare: 3/8 ___ 6/8. Write >, <, or =. (3/8 < 6/8)
2. Compare: 5/6 ___ 2/6. Write >, <, or =. (5/6 > 2/6)
3. "How do you know which fraction is greater when the denominators are the same?" (Compare the numerators. The bigger numerator means more same-size pieces, so it is the greater fraction.)

**Mastery = All 3 correct.**

## Teacher Notes

**This is one of the more intuitive fraction lessons.** Same-denominator comparison maps directly to whole number comparison — the student simply compares the numerators. Most students find this straightforward because the pieces are the same size and the logic is familiar.

**Common misconception:** A student who struggled with the idea that "bigger denominator means smaller pieces" (Lesson 2) may still carry residual confusion. Reinforce: in THIS lesson, the denominators are the same, so we do not need to worry about piece size. We only compare piece size when the denominators differ (Lesson 7).

**Comparison symbols:** Some students reverse > and <. The "hungry alligator" or "open mouth toward the bigger number" mnemonic helps. Another option: the pointed end always faces the smaller number.

**Standard:** 3.NF.3d — Compare two fractions with the same numerator or the same denominator by reasoning about their size.
