# Facilitator Guide — Grade 3 Math, Unit 4: Fractions (Introduction)

**Grade:** 3
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 4 — Fractions (Introduction)
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 4-5 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (adult facilitates instruction, student works with increasing independence)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit introduces fractions as a formal concept. Students learn that fractions describe equal parts of a whole, locate fractions on a number line, compare fractions, discover equivalent fractions, and connect whole numbers to fractions. This is one of the most critical units in the elementary curriculum — a strong fraction foundation here prevents the "fraction wall" that many students hit in Grades 4-5.

By the end of this unit, your student will be able to:

- Understand a fraction as a number that names part of a whole
- Identify the numerator (how many parts we have) and denominator (how many equal parts the whole is divided into)
- Recognize and name unit fractions: 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/6, 1/8
- Represent fractions with area models (shaded shapes) and set models (groups of objects)
- Place fractions on a number line between 0 and 1
- Compare two fractions with the same denominator
- Compare two fractions with the same numerator
- Identify simple equivalent fractions (e.g., 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6)
- Express whole numbers as fractions (e.g., 3 = 3/1 = 6/2)

**Why this matters:** Fractions are the gateway to rational numbers, ratios, proportions, algebra, and measurement. Students who build a strong conceptual understanding of fractions here — what they mean, how big they are, where they live on the number line — will navigate Grades 4-8 math with far greater confidence than those who memorize procedures without understanding.

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm that the student has mastered Units 1-3 skills:

- [ ] Knows multiplication facts for ×2, ×3, ×4, ×5, ×6, ×8, ×10 (Units 1-2)
- [ ] Understands division as the inverse of multiplication (Unit 3)
- [ ] Can divide shapes into 2 and 4 equal parts (Grade 1-2 informal experience)
- [ ] Understands "equal" / "fair share" language

If the student scored below 85% on the Unit 3 assessment, address gaps before starting this unit.

## Materials Needed for This Unit

- **Fraction strips or bars:** pre-cut strips showing halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, and eighths (print and cut, or fold paper strips)
- **Fraction circles:** paper circles divided into halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, eighths (print and cut)
- **Number lines:** printed number lines from 0 to 1 (and 0 to 2 for later lessons), with space to mark fractions
- **Colored pencils or crayons** (for shading fraction models)
- **Paper and scissors** (for folding and cutting fraction models)
- **Grid paper** (for drawing area models)
- **Dry-erase board and marker**
- **Small objects for set models:** 12-24 identical counters (buttons, blocks, or coins)

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | What It Looks Like | How to Address |
|---------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Bigger denominator = bigger fraction | Thinks 1/8 > 1/4 because 8 > 4 | Use fraction strips: physically show that 1/8 is smaller than 1/4 — more pieces means each piece is smaller |
| Numerator and denominator are separate numbers | Reads 3/4 as "3 and 4" | Emphasize: a fraction is ONE number that tells you "3 out of 4 equal parts" |
| Parts don't need to be equal | Divides a circle into unequal pieces and calls each piece "one third" | Always stress "EQUAL parts" — have the student check: "Are all the pieces the same size?" |
| Fractions only apply to shapes | Can shade 1/4 of a circle but can't identify 1/4 of a group of 8 objects | Use set models early and often alongside area models |
| Fractions only exist between 0 and 1 | Confused by 5/4 or 3/2 | Introduce fractions greater than 1 with physical models: "If each pizza is cut into 4 slices and you have 5 slices, you have more than 1 pizza" |
| Equivalent fractions are different amounts | Doesn't believe 1/2 = 2/4 | Place fraction strips on top of each other to show they cover the same length |

## Key Vocabulary for This Unit

| Term | Kid-Friendly Definition |
|------|----------------------|
| **Fraction** | A number that names part of a whole |
| **Numerator** | The top number — how many parts you have |
| **Denominator** | The bottom number — how many equal parts the whole is divided into |
| **Unit fraction** | A fraction with 1 as the numerator (e.g., 1/2, 1/3, 1/4) |
| **Equal parts** | Pieces that are all the same size |
| **Half (1/2)** | One of 2 equal parts |
| **Third (1/3)** | One of 3 equal parts |
| **Fourth / quarter (1/4)** | One of 4 equal parts |
| **Sixth (1/6)** | One of 6 equal parts |
| **Eighth (1/8)** | One of 8 equal parts |
| **Equivalent fractions** | Different fractions that name the same amount (e.g., 1/2 = 2/4) |
| **Number line** | A line marked with numbers at equal intervals |
| **Whole** | The entire thing before it is divided into parts |

## Conversation Starters

- "If we cut this sandwich into 4 equal pieces and you eat 1 piece, what fraction did you eat?"
- "Which would you rather have — 1/3 of a pizza or 1/6 of the same pizza? Why?"
- "Where would 3/4 go on a number line? Is it closer to 0 or to 1?"
- "Can you show me two fractions that are the same amount? How do you know they're the same?"
- "If the whole class has 12 students and 1/4 of them are wearing red, how many students are wearing red?"

---

## Differentiation Strategies

### If the Student Needs More Support
- Spend extra time with concrete models (folding paper, cutting circles) before drawings
- Focus on halves and fourths before introducing thirds, sixths, and eighths
- Use color-coding: shade the "parts we have" one color, leave the rest white
- Always pair the fraction symbol with a picture: draw 3/4 and shade 3 out of 4 parts
- Keep number line work to halves and fourths initially

### If the Student Needs More Challenge
- Introduce fifths and tenths
- Work with fractions greater than 1 and mixed numbers (1 and 1/2)
- Explore fraction addition with same denominators: 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4
- Connect fractions to division: 1/4 of 12 = 12 ÷ 4 = 3
- Number line challenges: place fractions like 5/8 and 7/8 and discuss proximity to 1
