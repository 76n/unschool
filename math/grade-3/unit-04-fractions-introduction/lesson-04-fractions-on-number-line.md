# Math -- Grade 3 -- Unit 4, Lesson 4

## Topic
Fractions on a Number Line (0 to 1)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Place fractions on a number line between 0 and 1
- Understand that the space between 0 and 1 can be divided into equal parts
- Locate both unit fractions and non-unit fractions on the number line
- Connect the number line representation to the area models from Lessons 2-3

## Prerequisites
- Read and write unit fractions and non-unit fractions (Lessons 2-3)
- Understand numerator and denominator (Lessons 2-3)
- Familiarity with a number line showing whole numbers (Grade 2)

## Materials Needed
- Pre-drawn number lines (0 to 1) on paper strips, divided into halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, and eighths
- Blank number line strips (0 to 1, no tick marks)
- Ruler
- Colored pencils
- Paper and pencil
- Tape or glue (optional, for assembling reference sheet)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review fraction meaning and activate prior knowledge of number lines.

1. "What fraction does this area model show?" (Show a rectangle divided into 4 parts, 3 shaded.) (3/4.) "What does the 4 mean?" (4 equal parts.) "What does the 3 mean?" (3 parts shaded.)
2. "You already know number lines with whole numbers: 0, 1, 2, 3... But did you know fractions live on the number line too? Fractions are numbers, and every number has a place on the number line."
3. "Today we are going to find where fractions live between 0 and 1."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students to place fractions on a number line by dividing the interval from 0 to 1 into equal parts. CRA progression: concrete (paper strip folding), representational (drawn number lines with tick marks), abstract (labeling points with fraction symbols).

### Key Vocabulary
- **Number line:** A straight line where numbers are placed at equal intervals
- **Interval:** The space between two numbers on the number line
- **Tick marks:** Small lines on the number line that mark equal parts
- **Endpoint:** The starting or ending point of the number line (here, 0 and 1)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Concrete -- fold a paper strip.**
Give the student a paper strip labeled 0 on the left end and 1 on the right end. "This strip represents the space from 0 to 1. The whole strip is 1. Fold it in half." The student folds. "Open it. Where is the fold line?" (In the middle.) "That fold mark is 1/2. It is halfway between 0 and 1." Write 1/2 at the fold mark.

Give a new strip. "Fold this one into 4 equal parts." (Fold in half, then in half again.) Open it. "You have 3 fold lines. Label them: the first mark is 1/4, the second is 2/4, the third is 3/4." The student labels. "Notice that 0 is the same as 0/4 and 1 is the same as 4/4."

**Step 2: Representational -- draw a number line.**
Draw a number line from 0 to 1. "Let's divide it into 3 equal parts." Make 2 tick marks between 0 and 1, spaced equally. "The first tick mark is 1/3. The second is 2/3. And 1 is the same as 3/3."

"How is this like an area model? Imagine a rectangle stretched out flat -- that's the number line. The tick marks are like the dividing lines in the rectangle. Each section between tick marks is one equal part."

**Step 3: Connecting area model to number line.**
Show a rectangle divided into 4 equal parts with 3 shaded, sitting above a number line divided into fourths. "In the area model, 3/4 means 3 out of 4 parts shaded. On the number line, 3/4 is the point you reach after covering 3 of the 4 equal spaces. Same fraction, different picture."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Place 1/4 on a number line from 0 to 1. Step 1: Divide the space from 0 to 1 into 4 equal parts (3 tick marks). Step 2: The first tick mark after 0 is 1/4. Step 3: Place a dot at the first tick mark and label it 1/4. "1/4 is one-fourth of the way from 0 to 1."

**Example B:** Place 5/6 on a number line from 0 to 1. Step 1: Divide the space from 0 to 1 into 6 equal parts (5 tick marks). Step 2: Count 5 tick marks from 0: 1/6, 2/6, 3/6, 4/6, 5/6. Step 3: Place a dot at the fifth tick mark and label it 5/6. "5/6 is five-sixths of the way from 0 to 1 -- almost at 1!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices placing fractions on number lines with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Label the Number Line**
Give the student a number line from 0 to 1 divided into 4 equal parts (tick marks already drawn). "Label each tick mark with the correct fraction."

- First tick mark: 1/4
- Second tick mark: 2/4
- Third tick mark: 3/4

"Where is 0/4?" (At 0.) "Where is 4/4?" (At 1.) "So 0/4 = 0 and 4/4 = 1."

Repeat with a number line divided into 6 equal parts. The student labels: 1/6, 2/6, 3/6, 4/6, 5/6.

**Activity 2: Plot the Point**
Give the student blank number lines (0 to 1) and ask them to:

- "Divide this number line into 3 equal parts and place a dot at 2/3." (2 tick marks; dot at second mark.)
- "Divide this number line into 8 equal parts and place a dot at 5/8." (7 tick marks; dot at fifth mark.)
- "Divide this number line into 2 equal parts and place a dot at 1/2." (1 tick mark; dot at the mark.)

"Each time, the denominator tells you how many equal parts to make, and the numerator tells you which tick mark to land on."

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
1. A number line from 0 to 1 is divided into 4 equal parts. Write the fraction at the first tick mark. (1/4)
2. A number line from 0 to 1 is divided into 2 equal parts. Where is 1/2? (At the tick mark in the middle.)
3. On a number line divided into 3 equal parts, label all the tick marks with fractions. (1/3, 2/3)
4. Place a dot at 3/4 on a number line divided into fourths. (Dot at the third tick mark.)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
1. Divide a number line from 0 to 1 into 6 equal parts. Label every tick mark. (1/6, 2/6, 3/6, 4/6, 5/6)
2. Place 3/8 on a number line divided into eighths. (Dot at the third tick mark.)
3. What fraction is at the same spot as 1 on a number line divided into fourths? (4/4)
4. A dot is placed at the 5th tick mark on a number line divided into 6 equal parts. What fraction is the dot at? (5/6)
5. Draw an area model AND a number line that both show 2/3. (Rectangle with 2 of 3 parts shaded; number line with dot at 2/3.)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
1. On the same number line divided into eighths, place dots at 1/8, 3/8, and 7/8. Which fraction is closest to 0? Which is closest to 1? (1/8 is closest to 0; 7/8 is closest to 1.)
2. A number line is divided into 4 equal parts. Another number line below it is divided into 8 equal parts. Both go from 0 to 1. Where does 1/2 fall on each line? Are they at the same point? (Yes -- 2/4 on the first and 4/8 on the second are both at the same spot, which is also 1/2.)
3. Place 2/6 and 4/6 on the same number line. Which fraction is closer to 1/2? (2/6 is closer to the left; 4/6 is closer to the right. Since 3/6 = 1/2, 2/6 and 4/6 are each one-sixth away from 1/2.)
4. Without a pre-drawn number line, draw your own from 0 to 1 and place 3/4 on it. Explain how you decided where to put it. (Divide into 4 equal parts, count 3 parts from 0.)
5. Carlos says 5/8 is past 1 on the number line because 5 is a big number. Is Carlos right? Explain. (No. The denominator is 8, so the whole from 0 to 1 has 8 parts. 5/8 is only 5 out of 8 parts, which is between 0 and 1.)

---

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. Divide a number line from 0 to 1 into 4 equal parts. Place a dot at 3/4 and label it. (Dot at the third tick mark, labeled 3/4.)
2. On a number line divided into 6 equal parts, what fraction is at the 2nd tick mark? (2/6)
3. How is a fraction on a number line like a fraction in an area model? (In both, the denominator tells how many equal parts, and the numerator tells how many parts you count or shade.)

## Teacher Notes

**CRA progression in this lesson:** We begin with concrete paper-strip folding (C), move to representational drawn number lines (R), and connect to abstract fraction symbols labeled on the number line (A). The connection between area models and number lines is a critical bridge -- spend extra time on Step 3 of Direct Instruction if the student needs it.

**Why the number line matters.** Area models show fractions as "parts of a shape," but the number line shows fractions as numbers with specific positions. This shift is essential: fractions are not just "pizza slices" -- they are numbers. The number line also prepares students for comparing fractions and for fractions greater than 1 (Lesson 5).

**The denominator determines the spacing.** If students place tick marks unevenly, the fractions will be at the wrong spots. Encourage use of a ruler and careful measuring. A common strategy: fold the paper strip first, then transfer the fold marks to the number line.

**Common errors:**
- Student puts tick marks at unequal intervals. Prompt: "Use your ruler. Each space must be the same length."
- Student counts tick marks instead of spaces (e.g., thinks 3 tick marks means thirds, but 3 tick marks between 0 and 1 actually make fourths). Prompt: "Count the spaces between tick marks, not the tick marks themselves."
- Student confuses 0 with the first fraction. Prompt: "0 is the starting point. The first fraction is at the first tick mark after 0."
- Student places the fraction label above a space rather than on a tick mark. Prompt: "A fraction names a specific point on the line, not the space between two points. Put the dot right on the tick mark."

**Facilitator language tip:** "The denominator tells you how many equal spaces to create between 0 and 1. The numerator tells you how many spaces to count from 0."

**Standard alignment:** This lesson addresses 3.NF.2 -- understanding a fraction as a number on the number line and representing fractions on a number line diagram. This standard specifically requires students to represent a fraction 1/b on a number line by partitioning the interval from 0 to 1 into b equal parts, and to represent a/b by marking off a lengths of 1/b from 0.

**Extension if time allows:** Have the student create a "fraction number line reference sheet" by taping together strips showing halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, and eighths, all aligned so that 0 and 1 line up. This visual comparison tool can be used throughout the rest of the unit and reveals relationships between fractions (for example, that 1/2, 2/4, and 4/8 all land at the same point).
