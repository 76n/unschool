# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Topic
Fraction Problem Solving

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Apply fraction concepts to solve word problems in context
- Use multiple representations (area model, number line, set model) to solve and justify answers
- Compare fractions in real-world situations
- Find a fraction of a set by connecting fractions to division (e.g., 1/4 of 12 = 12 divided by 4 = 3)

## Prerequisites
- All Unit 4 concepts: naming fractions, number line placement, comparing fractions, equivalent fractions, whole numbers as fractions (Lessons 1-9)
- Division facts (Unit 3)

## Materials Needed
- Counters or small objects (at least 24)
- Fraction strips
- Number lines (0 to 1 and 0 to 2)
- Colored pencils or crayons
- Grid paper
- Paper and pencil

**Purpose:** Quick review of skills from across the unit.

1. "Compare: 3/8 ___ 5/8." (3/8 < 5/8) "How do you know?" (Same denominator, so compare numerators)
2. "Compare: 1/4 ___ 1/6." (1/4 > 1/6) "Why is the one with the smaller denominator bigger?" (Fewer cuts means bigger pieces)
3. "Write 2 as a fraction with a denominator of 3." (6/3)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show how to apply fraction skills to word problems using multiple representations.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Fraction of a set:** Finding a part of a group of objects (e.g., 1/4 of 12 objects)
- **Set model:** Using a group of objects to show a fraction
- **Representation:** A way of showing your thinking — drawing, number line, fraction strips, or objects

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Fractions of a set — connecting to division.**

Place 12 counters on the table. "I want to find 1/4 of 12 counters. What does 1/4 mean? It means 1 out of 4 equal groups."

Divide the 12 counters into 4 equal groups. "How many are in each group?" (3) "So 1/4 of 12 = 3."

"Finding a fraction of a set is like dividing. 1/4 of 12 means 12 divided by 4, which equals 3. The denominator tells you how many groups to make."

Try another: "What is 1/3 of 18? Divide 18 into 3 equal groups." (6 in each group) "1/3 of 18 = 6."

**Step 2: Comparing fractions in context.**

"Mia ate 3/8 of a pizza. Noah ate 5/8 of the same pizza. Who ate more?"

"Same pizza means same denominator — both are eighths. 5 > 3, so 5/8 > 3/8. Noah ate more."

"Tara ran 1/4 of a mile. Desi ran 1/6 of a mile. Who ran farther?"

"Same numerator (both ran 1 part), but different denominators. Fourths are bigger than sixths because fewer cuts means bigger pieces. 1/4 > 1/6. Tara ran farther."

**Step 3: Using multiple representations to solve.**

"Good problem solvers show their thinking in more than one way. You can use a drawing, a number line, objects, or fraction strips. Let's practice."

Problem: "A garden has 8 equal sections. Flowers are planted in 3 sections. What fraction of the garden has flowers?"

- Area model: Draw a rectangle with 8 equal parts, shade 3. The fraction is 3/8.
- Number line: Mark 3/8 on a number line from 0 to 1.
- Words: "3 out of 8 equal sections, so 3/8."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** "There are 20 crayons. 1/4 of them are red. How many crayons are red?"

"1/4 of 20 means divide 20 into 4 equal groups: 20 divided by 4 = 5. So 5 crayons are red."

Check with a drawing: draw 20 circles in 4 equal groups of 5. One group (5 circles) represents the red crayons.

**Example B:** "Jake ate 2/6 of a pie. Emma ate 4/6 of the same pie. Who ate more? How much of the pie was eaten in total?"

"Same denominator (sixths). 4 > 2, so Emma ate more. 4/6 > 2/6."

"Total eaten: 2/6 + 4/6 = 6/6 = 1 whole pie. They ate the entire pie!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Solve word problems together, choosing appropriate representations.

**Activity 1: Fraction of a set.**

"There are 24 students in a class. 1/3 of them are wearing sneakers. How many students are wearing sneakers?"

Walk through: "1/3 means divide into 3 groups. 24 divided by 3 = 8. So 8 students are wearing sneakers."

"If the student is unsure, have them physically make 3 groups of counters from 24 counters."

**Activity 2: Comparison in context.**

"Two friends each have the same-size water bottle. Ana drank 3/4 of her bottle. Ben drank 3/8 of his bottle. Who drank more water?"

"Same numerator: both drank 3 parts. Fourths are bigger than eighths. So 3/4 > 3/8. Ana drank more."

"Ask the student: How did you decide? Can you show me on fraction strips?"

"If the student says Ben drank more because 8 > 4, redirect: Does a bigger denominator mean bigger pieces or smaller pieces? Use fraction strips to verify."

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
Solve each problem. You may use counters, drawings, or fraction strips to help.

1. "There are 8 apples. 1/2 of them are green. How many green apples are there?" (4)
2. "Sam colored 2/4 of a poster. Ali colored 3/4 of the same poster. Who colored more?" (Ali, because 3/4 > 2/4)
3. "A box has 6 toys. 1/3 of the toys are cars. How many cars are there?" (2)
4. "Draw a rectangle with 8 equal parts. Shade 5 parts. Write the fraction." (5/8)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
Solve each problem. Show your work with a drawing or number line.

1. "There are 12 cookies. 1/4 of them are chocolate. How many are chocolate?" (3)
2. "Zara read 5/6 of a book. Kai read 3/6 of the same book. Who read more?" (Zara, because 5/6 > 3/6)
3. "Leo walked 1/3 of a mile. Rosa walked 1/8 of a mile. Who walked farther? Explain." (Leo. 1/3 > 1/8 because thirds are bigger pieces than eighths.)
4. "A garden has 18 plants. 1/3 of them are tomatoes. How many tomatoes?" (6)
5. "There are 16 beads. 1/4 are blue and 1/4 are red. How many beads are blue? How many are red? How many are blue or red altogether?" (4 blue, 4 red, 8 total)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
Solve each problem. Explain your reasoning.

1. "There are 24 markers. 1/4 are red, 1/3 are blue, and the rest are green. How many of each color?" (Red: 24 divided by 4 = 6. Blue: 24 divided by 3 = 8. Green: 24 - 6 - 8 = 10.)
2. "Nora and James each have a candy bar the same size. Nora ate 2/3 of hers. James ate 2/4 of his. Who ate more candy? Explain using what you know about same-numerator comparisons." (Nora. Same numerator, but thirds are bigger pieces than fourths. 2/3 > 2/4.)
3. "A class of 30 students voted for their favorite fruit. 1/3 chose apples, 1/6 chose bananas, and the rest chose oranges. How many chose each fruit?" (Apples: 10. Bananas: 5. Oranges: 30 - 10 - 5 = 15.)
4. "Marcus says 1/2 of 10 is the same as 1/4 of 20. Is he right? Prove it." (1/2 of 10 = 5. 1/4 of 20 = 5. Yes, both equal 5.)
5. "Write your own fraction word problem that uses comparing and finding a fraction of a set. Then solve it." (Student-generated problem and solution.)

## Teacher Notes

**This lesson is a synthesis of the entire unit.** It pulls together naming fractions, comparing with same denominators and same numerators, equivalent fractions, and whole numbers as fractions. It also introduces the powerful connection between fractions and division through the "fraction of a set" concept.

**Fraction of a set is the bridge between fractions and division.** When a student computes 1/4 of 12 by dividing 12 by 4, they are seeing that fractions and division are deeply connected. This idea will be essential in Grades 4-5. Do not rush past it — make sure the student can explain WHY they divide.

**Use physical objects for the set model.** Even though students have been working with area models and number lines, set models (actual groups of counters) are a distinct representation. Some students who are confident with shaded rectangles still struggle when asked to find 1/3 of a group of 15 buttons. The physical act of making equal groups builds understanding.

**If a student struggles with word problems,** the difficulty is often in understanding the question rather than in fraction skills. Have the student retell the problem in their own words before solving. Ask: "What do you know? What are you trying to find?"

**This lesson prepares students for the Unit Assessment.** If a student scores below mastery on the exit ticket, identify which specific skill is weak (comparison? fraction of a set?) and revisit that lesson before the assessment.

**Standards addressed in this lesson:** This lesson synthesizes skills from 3.NF.3a, 3.NF.3b, 3.NF.3c, and 3.NF.3d.
