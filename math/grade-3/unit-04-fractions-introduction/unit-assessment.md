# Unit Assessment — Grade 3 Math, Unit 4: Fractions (Introduction)

**Grade:** 3
**Unit:** 4 — Fractions (Introduction)
**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** Written or one-on-one (word problems read aloud as needed)
**Estimated Duration:** 35-45 minutes
**Materials:** Pencil, number line template, fraction strips (optional)

---

## Overview

This assessment measures student understanding of foundational fraction concepts introduced in Unit 4. It covers identifying equal parts, naming and representing fractions, placing fractions on a number line, comparing fractions, recognizing equivalent fractions and whole numbers as fractions, and solving word problems involving fractions. The assessment contains 20 items worth a total of 60 points. Students write answers and draw or shade models as directed.

| Section | Skill Assessed | Items | Points |
|---------|---------------|-------|--------|
| 1 | Fraction Concepts | 1-5 | 12 |
| 2 | Fractions on a Number Line | 6-9 | 12 |
| 3 | Comparing Fractions | 10-14 | 16 |
| 4 | Equivalent Fractions and Whole Numbers | 15-17 | 10 |
| 5 | Problem Solving | 18-20 | 10 |
| — | **Total** | **20** | **60** |

**Mastery threshold:** 85% overall (51 of 60 points) AND at least partial credit in each section.

---

## Materials Needed

- Pencil
- Number line template (provided or drawn by facilitator)
- Fraction strips (optional — for students who benefit from hands-on support)

## Administration Instructions

Read word problems aloud for the student as needed. The student may use fraction strips, draw models, or use any strategy to solve. Do not provide hints about how to compare or name fractions. Allow the student to work at their own pace. For items requiring drawing or shading, provide blank shapes or number lines as needed.

## Section 1: Fraction Concepts

*Items 1-5 | 12 points*

**1.** A circle is divided into parts. Three of the parts are the same size and one part is larger than the others. Are the parts equal? Explain why or why not. (2 pts)

**2.** A rectangle is divided into 6 equal parts. 4 parts are shaded. Write the fraction that represents the shaded part. (2 pts)

**3.** What does the numerator of a fraction tell you? What does the denominator tell you? Use the fraction 3/8 as an example in your answer. (3 pts)

**4.** Shade 5/6 of the rectangle below. (The facilitator provides a rectangle divided into 6 equal parts.) (2 pts)

**5.** A pizza is cut into 8 equal slices. Maria eats 3 slices. Write the fraction of the pizza Maria ate and the fraction that is left. (3 pts)

| Item | Correct Response | Score |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| 1 | No, the parts are not equal; equal parts must all be the same size | ___ / 2 |
| 2 | 4/6 | ___ / 2 |
| 3 | The numerator (3) tells how many parts we are counting; the denominator (8) tells how many equal parts the whole is divided into | ___ / 3 |
| 4 | 5 out of 6 parts shaded | ___ / 2 |
| 5 | Maria ate 3/8; 5/8 is left | ___ / 3 |
| **Section 1 Total** | | **___ / 12** |

**Scoring notes:**

- **Item 1:** Award 2 points for correct answer with explanation (parts are not equal because they are different sizes). Award 1 point for correct answer ("no") without a clear explanation.
- **Item 3:** Award 3 points for correctly explaining both numerator and denominator with reference to 3/8. Award 2 points if both parts are explained but the example is not used or is used incorrectly. Award 1 point if only one part (numerator or denominator) is correctly explained.
- **Item 5:** Award 2 points for correctly writing 3/8 for the part eaten. Award 1 additional point for correctly writing 5/8 for the part remaining.

## Section 2: Fractions on a Number Line

*Items 6-9 | 12 points*

**6.** A number line from 0 to 1 is divided into 4 equal parts. Place and label the fractions 1/4, 2/4, and 3/4 at the correct points. (3 pts)

**7.** A number line from 0 to 1 is divided into 8 equal parts. A point is marked at the third tick mark after 0. What fraction does this point represent? (3 pts)

**8.** On a number line from 0 to 1 divided into 6 equal parts, place the fraction 5/6 at the correct location. (3 pts)

**9.** A number line goes from 0 to 2 and is divided into thirds (0, 1/3, 2/3, 1, 4/3, 5/3, 2). Place and label the fraction 4/3 at its correct location. (3 pts)

| Item | Correct Response | Score |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| 6 | 1/4 at first tick, 2/4 at second tick, 3/4 at third tick | ___ / 3 |
| 7 | 3/8 | ___ / 3 |
| 8 | 5/6 placed at the fifth tick mark after 0 | ___ / 3 |
| 9 | 4/3 placed one tick mark past 1 (between 1 and 5/3) | ___ / 3 |
| **Section 2 Total** | | **___ / 12** |

**Scoring notes:**

- **Item 6:** Award 1 point for each correctly placed and labeled fraction. All three must be on the correct tick marks.
- **Item 7:** Award 3 points for 3/8. Award 1 point if the student identifies the correct position but writes the fraction incorrectly (e.g., writes 3/7).
- **Item 9:** Award 3 points for placing 4/3 at the correct position (one-third past 1). Award 2 points if the student places it in approximately the right area but not precisely on the tick mark. Award 1 point if the student recognizes 4/3 is greater than 1 but places it incorrectly.

## Section 3: Comparing Fractions

*Items 10-14 | 16 points*

**10.** Compare each pair of fractions using >, <, or =. Both fractions have the same denominator. (4 pts, 1 pt each)

- a) 3/8 ___ 5/8
- b) 7/10 ___ 2/10
- c) 1/6 ___ 4/6
- d) 5/12 ___ 5/12

**11.** Compare each pair of fractions using >, <, or =. Both fractions have the same numerator. (4 pts, 1 pt each)

- a) 1/3 ___ 1/6
- b) 2/4 ___ 2/8
- c) 3/5 ___ 3/10
- d) 1/2 ___ 1/2

**12.** Which is larger: 2/6 or 5/6? Explain how you know. (3 pts)

**13.** Which is larger: 1/4 or 1/8? Explain how you know. (3 pts)

**14.** Put these three fractions in order from least to greatest: 1/8, 1/3, 1/6. (2 pts)

| Item | Correct Response | Score |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| 10a | 3/8 < 5/8 | ___ / 1 |
| 10b | 7/10 > 2/10 | ___ / 1 |
| 10c | 1/6 < 4/6 | ___ / 1 |
| 10d | 5/12 = 5/12 | ___ / 1 |
| 11a | 1/3 > 1/6 | ___ / 1 |
| 11b | 2/4 > 2/8 | ___ / 1 |
| 11c | 3/5 > 3/10 | ___ / 1 |
| 11d | 1/2 = 1/2 | ___ / 1 |
| 12 | 5/6 is larger; with explanation | ___ / 3 |
| 13 | 1/4 is larger; with explanation | ___ / 3 |
| 14 | 1/8, 1/6, 1/3 | ___ / 2 |
| **Section 3 Total** | | **___ / 16** |

**Scoring notes:**

- **Items 10-11:** Award 1 point per correct comparison symbol. No partial credit.
- **Item 12:** Award 2 points for the correct answer (5/6). Award 1 additional point for a valid explanation (e.g., "When the denominator is the same, the fraction with the bigger numerator is larger because you have more pieces of the same size"). Award 1 point total if the answer is correct but no explanation or an incorrect explanation is given.
- **Item 13:** Award 2 points for the correct answer (1/4). Award 1 additional point for a valid explanation (e.g., "When the numerator is the same, the fraction with the smaller denominator is larger because each piece is bigger"). Award 1 point total if the answer is correct but no explanation or an incorrect explanation is given.
- **Item 14:** Award 2 points for the fully correct order (1/8, 1/6, 1/3). Award 1 point if two of the three fractions are in the correct relative order.

## Section 4: Equivalent Fractions and Whole Numbers

*Items 15-17 | 10 points*

**15.** Circle all the pairs that are equivalent fractions. (4 pts, 1 pt each)

- a) 1/2 and 2/4
- b) 1/3 and 2/4
- c) 2/3 and 4/6
- d) 1/4 and 2/6

**16.** Write the number 3 as a fraction with a denominator of 1. Then write it as a fraction with a denominator of 4. (4 pts)

**17.** Any fraction where the numerator and denominator are the same number equals what whole number? Give two examples. (2 pts)

| Item | Correct Response | Score |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| 15a | Equivalent (circle) | ___ / 1 |
| 15b | Not equivalent (do not circle) | ___ / 1 |
| 15c | Equivalent (circle) | ___ / 1 |
| 15d | Not equivalent (do not circle) | ___ / 1 |
| 16 | 3/1 and 12/4 | ___ / 4 |
| 17 | Equals 1; examples such as 2/2, 3/3, 4/4, 5/5, etc. | ___ / 2 |
| **Section 4 Total** | | **___ / 10** |

**Scoring notes:**

- **Item 15:** Award 1 point for each correctly identified pair (circled if equivalent, not circled if not equivalent). Deduct 1 point for each incorrectly circled pair.
- **Item 16:** Award 2 points for 3/1. Award 2 points for 12/4. Award 1 point each if the student writes a fraction equal to 3 but with the wrong denominator (e.g., writes 3/1 correctly but writes 3/4 instead of 12/4).
- **Item 17:** Award 1 point for identifying the whole number 1. Award 1 point for providing at least two correct examples (e.g., 2/2, 5/5).

## Section 5: Problem Solving

*Items 18-20 | 10 points*

**18.** A rectangular garden is divided into 8 equal sections. Tomatoes are planted in 3 sections and peppers are planted in 2 sections. What fraction of the garden has tomatoes? What fraction has peppers? What fraction of the garden is empty? Draw and shade a model to support your answer. (4 pts)

**19.** A bag contains 6 marbles: 2 are red, 1 is blue, and 3 are green. What fraction of the marbles are red? What fraction are blue? What fraction are green? (3 pts)

**20.** Sam ate 2/6 of a pizza. Tara ate 4/6 of the same pizza. Who ate more? How do you know? Is there any pizza left? Explain. (3 pts)

| Item | Correct Response | Score |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| 18 | Tomatoes: 3/8; Peppers: 2/8; Empty: 3/8; model drawn and shaded correctly | ___ / 4 |
| 19 | Red: 2/6; Blue: 1/6; Green: 3/6 | ___ / 3 |
| 20 | Tara ate more because 4/6 > 2/6; no pizza left because 2/6 + 4/6 = 6/6 = 1 whole | ___ / 3 |
| **Section 5 Total** | | **___ / 10** |

**Scoring notes:**

- **Item 18:** Award 1 point for each correct fraction (3/8, 2/8, 3/8). Award 1 additional point for a correctly drawn and shaded model showing the three parts of the garden. Award partial credit (0.5) for a model that is drawn but incorrectly shaded.
- **Item 19:** Award 1 point for each correctly written fraction. Accept equivalent forms (e.g., 1/3 for 2/6) only if the student shows or explains the equivalence.
- **Item 20:** Award 1 point for correctly identifying Tara ate more. Award 1 point for a valid comparison explanation (same denominator, 4 > 2). Award 1 point for recognizing no pizza is left with explanation (2/6 + 4/6 = 6/6 = 1 whole pizza).

## Mastery Criteria

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Next Steps |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Mastered** | 51-60 points (85-100%) with at least partial credit in each section | Strong understanding of fraction concepts, representation, comparison, and equivalence | Advance to Unit 5. Continue fraction practice through warm-ups and interleaved review. |
| **Approaching** | 42-50 points (70-84%) | Most fraction skills present; specific gaps remain | Identify struggling sections and reteach targeted skills before advancing. |
| **Developing** | Below 42 points (<70%) | Significant gaps in fraction understanding | Return to specific lessons. Verify understanding of equal parts and whole-number division (prerequisites) before reteaching fractions. |

---

## Reteaching Recommendations

| If struggling in... | Focus on... |
|---|---|
| Section 1 (Fraction Concepts) | Return to early unit lessons on equal parts and fraction naming. Use physical fraction strips or paper folding to build the concept that a fraction names equal parts of a whole. Have the student fold paper into equal parts, shade a given number, and write the fraction. Emphasize: "The denominator tells how many equal parts. The numerator tells how many parts we are talking about." |
| Section 2 (Number Line) | Return to number line lessons. Build number lines with the student using a ruler and paper strips. Start with halves and fourths before moving to other denominators. Walk the student through counting tick marks: "The space from 0 to 1 is divided into ___ equal parts. Each jump is one part." For fractions greater than 1, extend the number line past 1 and practice counting by unit fractions (1/3, 2/3, 3/3, 4/3...). |
| Section 3 (Comparing Fractions) | Return to comparison lessons. Use fraction strips or fraction bar models placed side by side so the student can physically see which fraction is larger. For same-denominator comparisons: "Same-size pieces, so more pieces means a larger fraction." For same-numerator comparisons: "Same number of pieces, so the size of each piece matters — fewer total pieces means each piece is bigger." Always have the student draw models before writing symbols. |
| Section 4 (Equivalence and Whole Numbers) | Return to equivalence lessons. Use fraction strips to show that 1/2 and 2/4 cover the same length. For whole numbers as fractions, have the student build wholes from unit fractions: stack 4 pieces of 1/4 to make 1 whole, then extend to 2 wholes = 8/4. For n/n = 1, fold paper into different numbers of equal parts and shade all of them each time — the result is always 1 whole no matter the denominator. |
| Section 5 (Problem Solving) | Return to applied fraction lessons. If the student struggles with fractions of shapes, practice partitioning shapes into equal parts and labeling each section. If the student struggles with fractions of sets, start with concrete objects (sort 6 counters into color groups, then write the fraction for each group). If the student struggles with comparison in context, have the student draw a model for each fraction in the problem before comparing. Always ask: "What is the whole? How many equal parts?" |
