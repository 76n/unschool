# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 6, Lesson 10

## Topic
Mixed Measurement Review and Problem Solving

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Apply area, perimeter, time, mass/volume, and graph skills to multi-step word problems
- Identify which measurement concept(s) a problem requires before solving
- Explain the reasoning behind a solution using correct vocabulary and units
- Demonstrate readiness for the Unit 6 Assessment

## Prerequisites
- All Unit 6 skills: area (L1–L3), perimeter (L4–L5), area vs. perimeter (L6), elapsed time (L7), mass and volume (L8), scaled graphs (L9)
- Multiplication, division, addition, subtraction (Units 1–5)

## Materials Needed
- Rulers
- Grid paper
- Individual student clocks (geared)
- Number-line strips
- Multiplication fact chart (available for reference)
- Index cards for exit tickets
- "Measurement Mission" task cards (see Warm-Up — facilitator prepares these in advance)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Rapid-recall review of all four domains before problem solving.

"Measurement Lightning Round" — call out one problem per domain, students respond on whiteboards:

1. **Area:** A rectangle is 6 m long and 4 m wide. Area = ___? (24 sq m)
2. **Perimeter:** Same rectangle. Perimeter = ___? (20 m)
3. **Time:** A class starts at 9:20 A.M. and ends 45 minutes later. End time = ___? (10:05 A.M.)
4. **Mass:** A bag of grapes has a mass of 400 g. A bag of apples has a mass of 1 kg. Which is heavier? (Apples — 1,000 g > 400 g)
5. **Graphs:** A bar graph has scale = 10. A bar reaches the 7th gridline. Value = ___? (70)

"Today, we will put all these skills together to solve problems that use more than one measurement idea."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model the "identify → plan → solve → check" strategy for multi-step measurement problems; review key vocabulary and connections across domains.

### Key Vocabulary (Review)
- **Area (sq units):** inside surface; A = l × w
- **Perimeter (linear units):** distance around; add all sides
- **Elapsed time:** jumps on a number line from start to end
- **Mass (g, kg):** amount of matter; 1 kg = 1,000 g
- **Volume (mL, L):** liquid amount; 1 L = 1,000 mL
- **Scale (graph):** value of each unit or symbol; multiply to find totals

### Instruction Notes

**Modeling Multi-Step Problem Solving:**

Present this problem: "Mrs. Chen is building a rectangular garden that is 8 m long and 5 m wide. She wants to plant grass inside and put a fence around the outside. How much grass (area) does she need? How much fencing (perimeter) does she need? She buys the materials at 10:30 A.M. and finishes planting at 12:15 P.M. How long did the whole project take?"

Think aloud:
- Step 1: Area = 8 × 5 = 40 sq m (grass for inside)
- Step 2: Perimeter = 8+5+8+5 = 26 m (fencing for outside)
- Step 3: Elapsed time: 10:30 → 11:00 = 30 min; 11:00 → 12:00 = 60 min; 12:00 → 12:15 = 15 min; total = 105 min = 1 hr 45 min

"Notice that I did each part separately, labeled each answer with the right unit, and used the right measurement concept for each part."

**Connection map — draw on board:**
```
MEASUREMENT
├── Area (sq units) → A = l × w → multiplication
├── Perimeter (units) → add all sides → addition
├── Time → number line jumps → addition
├── Mass (g, kg) → scale / balance → add/subtract
├── Volume (mL, L) → measuring cup → add/subtract
└── Graphs → scale → multiply → compare
```

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together, narrating the strategy at each step.

**Problem 1 — Area + Perimeter:**
A square bedroom has side length 9 feet.
a) What is the area of the floor? (81 sq ft)
b) What is the perimeter of the room? (36 ft)
c) If carpet costs $2 per square foot, how much does carpet cost? (81 × $2 = $162)

**Problem 2 — Time + Mass:**
Rosa woke up at 6:45 A.M. She spent 35 minutes eating breakfast and getting ready, then walked 20 minutes to school.
a) What time did she leave her house? (6:45 + 35 min = 7:20 A.M.)
b) What time did she arrive at school? (7:20 + 20 min = 7:40 A.M.)
c) Her lunch box has a mass of 800 g. Her backpack (without lunch) has a mass of 2 kg. What is the total mass she carries? (800 g + 2,000 g = 2,800 g = 2 kg 800 g)

**Problem 3 — Graph + Multiplication:**
A picture graph shows the number of laps run by students. Each symbol = 4 laps. The data: Ali = 3 symbols, Ben = 5 symbols, Cara = 2 symbols.
a) Who ran the most laps? How many? (Ben — 5 × 4 = 20 laps)
b) How many laps did Ali and Cara run combined? (3×4 + 2×4 = 12+8 = 20 laps)
c) How many more laps did Ben run than the others combined? (20 − 20 = 0 — same amount! Interesting finding.)

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. A rectangular swimming pool is 12 m long and 5 m wide.
   a) What is the area of the pool floor? (60 sq m)
   b) What is the perimeter of the pool? (34 m)
   c) A rope is placed around the edge of the pool for safety. How long is the rope? (34 m)

2. A class event starts at 1:00 P.M. and lasts 2 hours and 30 minutes. What time does it end? (3:30 P.M.)

3. A bag of rice has a mass of 3 kg. A bag of beans has a mass of 850 g. What is the total mass of both bags in grams? (3,000 + 850 = 3,850 g)

**Stretch:**
4. A picture graph shows juice bottles sold each day. Scale = 5 bottles per symbol.
   - Monday: 4 symbols; Tuesday: 6 symbols; Wednesday: 3 symbols; Thursday: 5 symbols; Friday: 7 symbols
   a) On which day were the most bottles sold? (Friday — 35)
   b) How many more were sold Friday than Monday? (35 − 20 = 15)
   c) What was the total for the whole week? (4+6+3+5+7=25 symbols × 5 = 125 bottles)

5. Leo starts his homework at 3:45 P.M. He spends 30 minutes on math, 20 minutes on reading, and 15 minutes on spelling. What time does he finish? (3:45 + 65 min; 3:45→4:00=15min; 65−15=50 more; 4:00+50=4:50 P.M.)

6. A rectangle has an area of 48 sq cm and a width of 6 cm. What is its perimeter?
   (length = 48÷6 = 8 cm; P = 8+6+8+6 = 28 cm)

**Challenge:**
7. A farmer builds a rectangular chicken coop using 28 m of fence. The coop must have an area of at least 40 sq m. What dimensions work? Find two possible solutions.
   (P=28 means 2l+2w=28, so l+w=14. Pairs: 1+13=14 (A=13, too small), 2+12=14 (A=24, too small), 3+11=14 (A=33, too small), 4+10=14 (A=40 ✓), 5+9=14 (A=45 ✓), 6+8=14 (A=48 ✓), 7+7=14 (A=49 ✓). So: 4×10, 5×9, 6×8, or 7×7 work.)

8. Sam drinks 250 mL of water at breakfast, 500 mL at lunch, and 750 mL at dinner. His goal is to drink 2 L a day. How much more water does he need? (250+500+750=1,500 mL; 2,000−1,500=500 mL more)

### Interleaved Review
R1. (Fractions — Unit 4) What fraction of the rectangle is shaded if 3 out of 8 equal parts are shaded? (3/8)

R2. (Division — Unit 3) If 7 friends share 56 stickers equally, how many does each friend get? (56 ÷ 7 = 8)

R3. (Multi-digit arithmetic — Unit 5) 627 + 348 = ___? (975)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Exit Ticket (a mini-quiz to gauge readiness for assessment):**

1. A rectangle is 9 cm long and 4 cm wide. Find: (a) area __ sq cm; (b) perimeter __ cm.
2. A movie started at 2:15 P.M. and ended at 4:00 P.M. How long was the movie? (Use a number line.)
3. A picture graph shows: scale = 3. Category A: 5 symbols. How many in Category A? ___
4. Circle the better unit for the mass of a child: **grams / kilograms**

**Mastery = 3 out of 4 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the concept the student missed; use the corresponding lesson's anchor chart or activity as a refresher
- Identify whether the difficulty is procedural (computing) or conceptual (what to measure); address accordingly

### If Mastery Is Met
- Present a "mega problem": "A rectangular park (15 m × 10 m) will be filled with grass (area) and fenced (perimeter). Grass costs $3 per square meter. Fencing costs $5 per meter. What is the total cost?" (Grass: 150 sq m × $3 = $450; Fencing: 50 m × $5 = $250; Total = $700)

## Extension (Optional)
**Measurement Story:** Write a short story (at least 5 sentences) that includes all four measurement domains: area, perimeter, time, and mass/volume. Include at least one calculation for each domain. Exchange stories with a partner and solve each other's problems.

**Assessment Prep Review:** Go back through the unit and choose one problem from each lesson (L1–L9) that was challenging. Re-solve each one without looking at notes. Record which areas still feel uncertain.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson serves two purposes: consolidation of learning and assessment preparation. The balance of direct instruction and problem solving should lean more toward student work today (the guided and independent practice blocks are the most important).
- Use the mini-quiz exit ticket to make a final decision about student readiness for the unit assessment. If a student scores below 2/4 on the exit ticket, consider a brief targeted review session before administering the assessment.
- The connection map drawn during Direct Instruction should be photographed or copied by the student to use as a review reference before the assessment.
- This lesson intentionally recycles problem types from each domain rather than introducing new content. Encourage students to name the domain before solving each problem: "This is an area problem. This is a time problem."
- Challenge #7 (optimization — fencing and area) is a beautiful problem that previews algebraic reasoning. If the student engages with it, take time to explore why the square (7×7) gives the largest area — this is mathematically significant and age-accessible.
