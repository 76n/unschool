# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 6, Lesson 1

## Topic
Understanding Area — Covering Surfaces with Square Units

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain that area is the number of square units needed to cover a flat surface
- Identify a unit square (1 square unit) as the standard tile used to measure area
- Cover a shape with unit squares (no gaps, no overlaps) and count to find its area
- Use the label "square units" (or "sq cm," "sq in") when stating an area

## Prerequisites
- Understanding that measurement compares an attribute of an object to a unit
- Familiarity with flat (2D) shapes: rectangles, squares, triangles
- Counting to at least 50

## Materials Needed
- Color tiles or square inch tiles (at least 30 per student)
- 1-inch grid paper (one sheet per student)
- Crayons or colored pencils (two colors per student)
- Scissors (optional — for cutting out shapes)
- Anchor chart paper and markers (facilitator)
- Index cards for exit tickets

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of measurement and surface; preview the idea of "covering."

Place a large sheet of paper on a table. Ask: "If I wanted to know how big this table is — not how long, but how much SURFACE it has — what could I do?"

Let students share ideas. Accept all reasonable responses. Then say: "One way is to cover it completely with same-size tiles and count them. That's what we are going to learn today."

Hold up a single square tile. Ask: "What shape is this?" (square) "Every side is 1 inch long, so we call it one square inch. That is our measuring unit today."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Establish the definition of area and the concrete method of covering to measure it.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Area:** The number of square units needed to completely cover a flat surface with no gaps and no overlaps
- **Square unit:** A square with side length 1; the basic unit for measuring area (e.g., 1 square inch, 1 square centimeter)
- **Unit square:** Another name for square unit

### Instruction Notes

**CRA — Concrete phase**

Draw a large rectangle (about 3 × 4 tiles) on the anchor chart. Say: "I want to find how much surface this shape has. Watch me cover it."

Place tiles inside the outline one at a time, filling row by row with no gaps and no overlaps. Narrate: "Row 1: 1, 2, 3, 4. Row 2: 5, 6, 7, 8. Row 3: 9, 10, 11, 12. The area is 12 square inches."

Key points to emphasize:
- Tiles must touch edge to edge — no gaps
- Tiles must not pile up — no overlaps
- You count EVERY tile, including ones in the middle
- The unit is always "square [unit name]," never just a number

**Introduce grid paper as a picture of tiles:** Show how each small square on the grid paper represents one square unit. Shade a 3 × 4 rectangle and count: "This is the same as covering with 12 tiles."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Show a 2 × 5 rectangle on grid paper. "How many square units cover this shape?" Count together: 10 square units.

**Example 2:** Show an irregular staircase shape made of 7 unit squares (like an L-shape). Cover with tiles. "We count every square inside the outline: 7 square units."

**Example 3 — common error:** Show a 3 × 3 rectangle with a gap in the middle. Ask: "What is wrong here?" (There is a gap — a tile is missing.) "We must have NO gaps." Fill it in: area = 9 square units.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work together with the student. Have them use tiles on grid paper.

1. "Use tiles to cover this 2 × 3 rectangle drawn on your grid paper. Count the tiles. What is the area?" (6 square units)

2. "Cover this shape." (Show an L-shape that is 4 + 3 = 7 unit squares.) "Count your tiles. What is the area?" (7 square units)

3. On the grid paper, draw a 4 × 4 square together. "Before you place tiles, how many do you think you will need? Now cover it and check." (16 square units)

4. "Can you find a shape on your desk that looks flat? Trace around it on grid paper and estimate how many square units it covers." (open-ended — shapes like an index card, a sticky note; accept reasonable estimates)

After each problem, ask: "What units do we use for area?" (square units / square inches)

---

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Cover each shape below with tiles. Write the area.
   - Shape A: a 3 × 3 square on grid paper → area = ___
   - Shape B: a 2 × 6 rectangle on grid paper → area = ___
   - Shape C: an L-shape (5 squares on bottom, 2 more stacked on the left end) → area = ___

2. Circle the correct label: The area of a shape is measured in ___.
   (a) inches  (b) square inches  (c) pounds  (d) minutes

**Stretch:**
3. Draw two different shapes on grid paper that each have an area of 8 square units. (They do not need to be rectangles.)

4. Bella covers her notebook cover with tiles. She uses 4 rows of 7 tiles. What is the area of her notebook cover? (28 square units)

**Challenge:**
5. Can you draw a shape with area = 10 square units that is NOT a rectangle? Describe your shape in words.

6. Jake says the area of a shape is 15 square units. He shows a 3 × 4 rectangle. Is he right? Explain. (No — 3 × 4 = 12 square units, not 15.)

### Interleaved Review
R1. (Multiplication — Unit 1) What is 6 × 7? (42)

R2. (Division — Unit 3) 24 ÷ 4 = ___? (6)

R3. (Multi-digit addition — Unit 5) 345 + 278 = ___? (623)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

On an index card, answer:
1. What does "area" mean? Write one sentence in your own words.
2. Look at this shape (draw a 3 × 5 rectangle on the card). Cover it on grid paper with tiles or draw the tiles in. What is the area? ___ square units.

**Mastery = defining area correctly AND giving correct area (15 square units) on item 2.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Work one-on-one with tiles on a simple 2 × 3 rectangle; emphasize counting every square including inside ones
- Ask: "If I put one tile here (place it), that's 1 square unit. Where does the next tile go?" Build up counting slowly

### If Mastery Is Met
- Ask the student to sketch two different-shaped figures that both have an area of 12 square units

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Shape Library:** Create a "shape museum" — the student traces 5 real objects (book, pencil case, eraser, etc.) onto grid paper, covers with tiles, and labels each with its area. Compare: "Which object has the largest area? The smallest?"

**Pattern Investigation:** What is the area of a shape made of 1 row of 10 tiles? 2 rows? 3 rows? Record in a table and look for the pattern.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Students often count only the tiles on the border and forget the interior. If this happens, remove the border tiles and say, "There are still tiles here — what happened to them?" This physical demonstration is more powerful than verbal correction.
- The anchor chart showing a shaded rectangle on grid paper should remain posted throughout the unit as a reference. Label it: "Area = how much surface is covered (square units)."
- If tiles are not available, students can draw dots in each square of a grid and count the dots.
- This lesson is purely concrete. Do NOT mention the formula "length × width" today — that comes in Lesson 3.
