# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 6, Lesson 7

## Topic
Telling Time to the Minute and Elapsed Time

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read an analog clock and write the time to the nearest minute
- Identify times as A.M. or P.M. and connect them to events in daily life
- Find elapsed time (how much time has passed) using a number-line jump strategy
- Find a start time or end time when elapsed time and one endpoint are known

## Prerequisites
- Telling time to the nearest 5 minutes (G2)
- Number-line reasoning — jumping to benchmark numbers (Unit 4, fractions)
- Multi-digit addition and subtraction (Unit 5)
- Understanding of hours and minutes (60 minutes = 1 hour)

## Materials Needed
- Demonstration analog clock (one large, wall-mounted or teacher-held)
- Individual student geared clocks (one per student)
- Blank number-line strips (0 to 120 minutes, unlabeled) — one per student
- Pencils and markers
- Index cards for exit tickets

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate G2 clock-reading knowledge; extend to minutes (not just 5-minute intervals).

Display the demonstration clock at 8:35. Ask: "What time does this clock show?" (8:35)

"What number does the minute hand point to?" (7) "Why does it say 35 and not 7?" Guide: each number on the clock represents 5 minutes: 1→5, 2→10, 3→15... 7→35.

Quick drill: set the clock to 3:17, 11:52, 6:04, 12:48. Students write the times on index cards or whiteboards and hold them up.

"Now a harder question: If school starts at 8:15 A.M. and it's now 8:47 A.M., how much time has passed?" Accept guesses. "We're going to use a number line to figure this out precisely."

---

## Direct Instruction (12–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach reading to the nearest minute; establish the number-line jump strategy for elapsed time.

### Key Vocabulary
- **A.M.:** The hours from 12:00 midnight to 11:59 just before noon (morning)
- **P.M.:** The hours from 12:00 noon to 11:59 just before midnight (afternoon, evening, night)
- **Elapsed time:** The amount of time that passes between a start time and an end time
- **Number line (time):** A line used to show jumps of time from start to end

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1 — Reading to the nearest minute:**

Set the clock to 4:23. Walk through: "Hour hand is between 4 and 5 — so the hour is 4. Minute hand is between 4 and 5 on the clock face. The 4 means 20 minutes; count on by ones: 21, 22, 23. Time is 4:23."

Special cases to practice:
- Times with a zero in the minutes: 9:07 (say "nine oh seven" — write 9:07, not 9:7)
- Times close to the hour: 11:58, 3:02

**Part 2 — A.M. and P.M.:**
"Is 7:30 morning or evening? It depends — 7:30 A.M. is morning; 7:30 P.M. is evening. Whenever we write a time, we should say or write A.M. or P.M. so people know."

Quick examples: school starts (A.M.), lunch (A.M. or P.M. depending on grade — usually 12:00 P.M.), bedtime (P.M.), midnight (A.M.).

**Part 3 — Elapsed Time with a Number Line:**

IMPORTANT: Do NOT teach borrowing-from-hours subtraction. Use the jump strategy only.

Write: Start time: 2:15 P.M. End time: 3:45 P.M. How much time has passed?

Draw a number line. Mark 2:15 on the left and 3:45 on the right.

"Step 1: Jump to the next hour. From 2:15, jump to 3:00. How many minutes? 45 minutes."
Mark the jump: +45 min → land on 3:00.

"Step 2: Jump to the end time. From 3:00, jump to 3:45. How many minutes? 45 minutes."
Mark the jump: +45 min → land on 3:45.

"Step 3: Add all the jumps. 45 + 45 = 90 minutes = 1 hour 30 minutes."

Model 2–3 more examples with different configurations. Emphasize: there is no single "right" way to jump — some students may prefer jumping in 30s, others in 10s and 5s. Any strategy that arrives at the correct total is acceptable.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Start 10:20 A.M., End 11:05 A.M.
- Jump from 10:20 to 11:00: 40 minutes
- Jump from 11:00 to 11:05: 5 minutes
- Total elapsed: 40 + 5 = 45 minutes

**Example 2:** Start 1:45 P.M., End 3:10 P.M.
- Jump from 1:45 to 2:00: 15 minutes
- Jump from 2:00 to 3:00: 60 minutes
- Jump from 3:00 to 3:10: 10 minutes
- Total elapsed: 15 + 60 + 10 = 85 minutes = 1 hour 25 minutes

**Example 3 — Finding end time:** A movie starts at 4:30 P.M. and lasts 95 minutes. When does it end?
- Start at 4:30 on the number line
- Jump 30 min to 5:00
- 95 − 30 = 65 minutes left
- Jump 60 min to 6:00
- 65 − 60 = 5 minutes left
- Jump 5 min to 6:05
- End time: 6:05 P.M.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Set your student clock to 7:43. Draw the clock. Write the time: ___ : ___ (7:43)

2. A soccer practice starts at 3:10 P.M. and ends at 4:25 P.M. Draw a number line and find the elapsed time.
   (3:10 → 4:00: 50 min; 4:00 → 4:25: 25 min; total = 75 min = 1 hour 15 min)

3. School starts at 8:05 A.M. Art class starts 40 minutes later. What time does art class start?
   (8:05 + 40 min: jump to 8:45 A.M.)

4. Maya's piano lesson began at 2:30 P.M. and lasted 1 hour and 15 minutes. What time did it end?
   (2:30 + 60 min = 3:30; 3:30 + 15 min = 3:45 P.M.)

5. A train left at 9:50 A.M. and arrived at 11:20 A.M. How long was the trip?
   (9:50 → 10:00: 10 min; 10:00 → 11:00: 60 min; 11:00 → 11:20: 20 min; total = 90 min = 1 hour 30 min)

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Write the time shown on each clock face. (Facilitator draws 4 clock faces: 3:14, 8:07, 11:51, 6:29)

2. For each, find the elapsed time using a number line.
   - Start: 10:00 A.M., End: 10:45 A.M. → Elapsed: ___
   - Start: 1:15 P.M., End: 2:50 P.M. → Elapsed: ___
   - Start: 7:30 A.M., End: 9:00 A.M. → Elapsed: ___

3. A movie starts at 5:00 P.M. and is 2 hours long. What time does it end? (7:00 P.M.)

**Stretch:**
4. Kayla started reading at 3:25 P.M. and read for 55 minutes. What time did she stop? (3:25 + 55 min; jump to 4:00 = 35 min; 55−35=20 more; 4:00+20=4:20 P.M.)

5. A dentist appointment started at 10:40 A.M. and ended at 11:15 A.M. How long was the appointment? (10:40→11:00: 20 min; 11:00→11:15: 15 min; total = 35 min)

6. Ethan went to bed at 9:15 P.M. He woke up at 6:45 A.M. the next morning. How long did he sleep? (9:15 P.M. → midnight is 2 hr 45 min; midnight → 6:45 A.M. is 6 hr 45 min; total = 9 hr 30 min)

**Challenge:**
7. A basketball game was 1 hour and 45 minutes long. The game ended at 4:20 P.M. What time did it start? (work backwards: 4:20 − 45 min = 3:35; 3:35 − 1 hr = 2:35 P.M.)

8. Luis spent 25 minutes doing homework, then 15 minutes eating a snack, then 40 minutes watching TV. He finished at 5:30 P.M. What time did he start homework? (Total: 25+15+40=80 min before 5:30; 5:30 minus 80 min: 5:30→5:00=30 min back; 80−30=50 more back; 5:00→4:00=60 but only need 50: so 4:10 P.M.)

### Interleaved Review
R1. (Perimeter — L5) A rectangle is 8 cm long and 5 cm wide. What is its perimeter? (26 cm)

R2. (Multiplication — Unit 1) 6 × 7 = ___? (42)

R3. (Multi-digit subtraction — Unit 5) 600 − 138 = ___? (462)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Write the time shown on this clock. (Draw an analog clock showing 5:38.)
2. A library opens at 9:00 A.M. and closes at 5:30 P.M. How many hours and minutes is it open? Use a number line.

**Mastery = time read correctly as 5:38 AND elapsed time correctly found as 8 hours 30 minutes.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice reading the clock in 5-minute jumps first (count by 5s), then add the extra individual minutes
- For elapsed time, use a pre-labeled number line with every hour marked and model the jumps slowly

### If Mastery Is Met
- "If someone arrives at the library 20 minutes before it closes, what time do they arrive?" (5:10 P.M.)

---

## Extension (Optional)
**My Daily Schedule:** The student creates a personal daily schedule (wake up, meals, school, activities, bedtime) with times to the nearest minute. For each pair of consecutive events, calculate how much time passes in between. Find the total time spent at school, sleeping, and on activities.

**Clock Puzzles:** "The minute hand is pointing to the 9. The hour hand is between 2 and 3. What time is it?" (2:45) Create 5 such puzzles for a classmate.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The number-line jump strategy is the core of this lesson. Practice it yourself before teaching so you can model it fluidly on the board. Having a large number line on the board (or a roll of masking tape on the floor) helps students visualize the jumps.
- Never say "just subtract the minutes" — this fails for problems like 3:15 to 4:05. Always use the number line.
- For Challenge #8 (working backward), this is a high-ceiling problem. If the student struggles, have them work forward instead: "If you started at 4:10, would you finish at 5:30?" Then verify.
- The conversion between minutes and hours (60 min = 1 hr) should be stated explicitly each time an answer exceeds 60 minutes.
- Consistent use of A.M./P.M. in all answers is expected.
