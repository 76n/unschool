# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 6, Lesson 5

## Topic
Finding Perimeter of Polygons — Unknown Side Lengths and Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Find the perimeter of polygons by adding all known side lengths
- Find an unknown side length of a polygon when the perimeter and other side lengths are given
- Solve one- and two-step real-world word problems involving perimeter
- Recognize that a rectangle has two pairs of equal sides, and use this to find missing lengths

## Prerequisites
- Definition of perimeter and method of adding side lengths (Lesson 4)
- Multi-digit addition and subtraction within 1,000 (Unit 5)
- Multiplication and division facts (Units 1–3) for shortcut methods and unknown sides
- Understanding of rectangle properties (opposite sides equal)

## Materials Needed
- Rulers (inches and centimeters)
- Plain paper and pencils
- Grid paper (for drawing shapes)
- Colored pencils (red for perimeter tracing)
- Index cards for exit tickets

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review perimeter concept and addition; build to unknown side lengths.

Quick mental challenge: "I walked around a rectangular park. I went 50 meters on the first side, 30 meters on the second side. If the park is a rectangle, how far did I walk total?" (50+30+50+30 = 160 meters)

"What if I told you the perimeter was 160 meters, and one side was 50 meters. How long is the other (shorter) side?" Let students think and share. (160 − 50 − 50 = 60; 60 ÷ 2 = 30 meters)

"Today we will practice finding perimeters AND figuring out a missing side when we already know the perimeter."

---

## Direct Instruction (12–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Extend perimeter work to unknown side lengths; establish a systematic strategy for word problems.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Unknown side length:** A side whose length is not given; must be found using the perimeter and the other known sides
- **Equation:** A number sentence with an equal sign (e.g., 5 + ? + 5 + ? = 24)

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1 — Review: Finding perimeter from all known sides**

Show three shapes and quickly find each perimeter as a warm-up:
- Triangle: 5 cm, 7 cm, 9 cm → 21 cm
- Rectangle: 8 in × 3 in → 22 in
- Regular hexagon: all sides = 4 ft → 24 ft (6 × 4 = 24)

Reinforce: for a regular polygon (all sides equal), multiply the side length by the number of sides.

**Part 2 — Finding an unknown side length**

Draw a rectangle with sides labeled: top = 9 cm, left side = 4 cm, bottom = 9 cm, right side = ?
"I know three sides: 9, 4, 9. The total perimeter is 26 cm. What is the missing side?"

Strategy — "start with what you know":
1. Write: 9 + 4 + 9 + ? = 26
2. Add the known sides: 9 + 4 + 9 = 22
3. Subtract from the perimeter: 26 − 22 = 4
4. The missing side is 4 cm.
5. Check: 9 + 4 + 9 + 4 = 26. ✓

Point out: "Since it's a rectangle, we knew opposite sides are equal — so the right side must be the same as the left side (4 cm). We can check that quickly."

**Part 3 — More complex polygon (all sides different):**

Draw an irregular quadrilateral with sides: 8 cm, 5 cm, 6 cm, and ? cm. Perimeter = 25 cm.
- 8 + 5 + 6 = 19
- ? = 25 − 19 = 6 cm

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** A rectangular fence encloses a garden. The fence is 30 feet long in total. The garden is 9 feet wide. What is the length?
- P = 30; width = 9
- 9 + length + 9 + length = 30
- 18 + 2×length = 30
- 2×length = 12
- length = 6 feet

**Example 2:** A triangle has sides 12 cm, 15 cm, and ? cm. The perimeter is 40 cm. Find the missing side.
- 12 + 15 + ? = 40
- 27 + ? = 40
- ? = 13 cm

**Example 3:** A regular pentagon (5 equal sides) has a perimeter of 35 in. How long is each side?
- 35 ÷ 5 = 7 in

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. A rectangle has a perimeter of 28 cm. One side is 6 cm. Find the length of the other pair of sides.
   (6 + ? + 6 + ? = 28; 12 + 2×? = 28; ? = 8 cm)

2. A triangle has sides: 10 in, 13 in, and ? in. The perimeter is 36 in. Find the missing side.
   (10 + 13 + ? = 36; 23 + ? = 36; ? = 13 in)

3. A square has a perimeter of 32 feet. How long is each side?
   (32 ÷ 4 = 8 feet per side)

4. An L-shaped playground has these outer side lengths: 20 m, 10 m, 8 m, 6 m, 12 m, and 4 m. What is the perimeter?
   (20 + 10 + 8 + 6 + 12 + 4 = 60 m)

5. Word problem: "Lily is putting ribbon around the edge of a rectangular card. The card is 15 cm long and 10 cm wide. How much ribbon does she need?"
   (15 + 10 + 15 + 10 = 50 cm)

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Find each perimeter. Show your work.
   - A rectangle: 12 cm × 5 cm → P = ___
   - A triangle: sides 9 in, 9 in, 6 in → P = ___
   - A square: side = 8 m → P = ___

2. Find the missing side length.
   - Rectangle: perimeter = 22 cm, width = 4 cm, length = ___?
   - Triangle: perimeter = 30 in, two sides are 9 in and 11 in; third side = ___?

3. Omar wants to frame a rectangular painting. The painting is 14 cm tall and 10 cm wide. How much framing material does he need? (48 cm)

**Stretch:**
4. A rectangle has a perimeter of 36 cm. What could its length and width be? Find at least three options. (e.g., 1×17, 2×16, 3×15, 4×14, 5×13, 6×12, 7×11, 8×10, 9×9)

5. A regular octagon (8 equal sides) has a perimeter of 48 cm. How long is each side? (48 ÷ 8 = 6 cm)

6. A path goes around a rectangular park. Sam walks the path twice. The park is 25 m long and 15 m wide. How far does Sam walk in total? (P = 80 m; twice = 160 m)

**Challenge:**
7. A rectangle has a perimeter of 40 m and a length of 14 m. What is the area of the rectangle? (Width = 40−14−14=12; 12÷2=6; A = 14×6 = 84 sq m)

8. An irregular hexagon has perimeter 42 cm. Five of its sides measure: 6, 8, 7, 9, and 5 cm. What is the sixth side? (42 − (6+8+7+9+5) = 42−35 = 7 cm)

### Interleaved Review
R1. (Area — L3) What is the area of a rectangle with length 9 m and width 4 m? (36 sq m)

R2. (Multiplication — Unit 1) What is 7 × 8? (56)

R3. (Subtraction — Unit 5) 803 − 456 = ___? (347)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. A rectangle has a perimeter of 30 cm and a width of 5 cm. What is the length?
2. How is finding a missing side length like subtraction?

**Mastery = correct length (10 cm) AND a reasonable connection to subtraction ("subtract the known sides from the total perimeter").**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Walk through the equation: 5 + ? + 5 + ? = 30. "I know two sides add to 10. 30 − 10 = 20. So the two lengths add to 20. 20 ÷ 2 = 10."
- Confirm with addition: 5 + 10 + 5 + 10 = 30. ✓

### If Mastery Is Met
- Pose: "A rectangle has area = 30 sq cm and width = 5 cm. What is the perimeter?" (length = 6; P = 5+6+5+6 = 22 cm) — integrates area and perimeter

## Extension (Optional)
**Perimeter vs. Cost:** "Fencing costs $3 per foot. You want to fence a rectangular yard that is 20 feet by 15 feet. How much will the fencing cost?" (P = 70 ft; 70 × $3 = $210)

**Polygon Design:** Using dot paper or grid paper, design a polygon with exactly 10 sides where the perimeter is 40 cm. Find all side lengths and verify.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The "start with what you know" strategy for finding unknown sides is important and generalizable. Write the equation explicitly every time: [known sides] + ? = perimeter.
- The distinction between regular (all sides equal) and irregular polygons matters here. For regular polygons, both multiplication (to find total) and division (to find one side from total) apply.
- The Challenge problem (#7) that combines perimeter and area is high-leverage — it forces students to use perimeter to find a missing dimension and then apply the area formula. This is excellent preparation for the unit assessment.
- Ensure students still write the correct units: perimeter in linear units (cm, in, m) — not square units.
