# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 6, Lesson 8

## Topic
Mass and Volume — Grams, Kilograms, Liters, and Milliliters

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain the difference between mass and volume (liquid volume)
- Identify grams (g) and kilograms (kg) as metric units of mass; know that 1 kg = 1,000 g
- Identify liters (L) and milliliters (mL) as metric units of liquid volume; know that 1 L = 1,000 mL
- Estimate whether an object's mass is closer to 1 gram or 1 kilogram
- Estimate whether a liquid amount is closer to 1 mL or 1 liter
- Solve one-step word problems involving mass or volume using addition, subtraction, or multiplication

## Prerequisites
- Multiplication and division facts (Units 1–3) — for one-step word problems
- Multi-digit addition and subtraction (Unit 5)
- General understanding of measurement as comparison to a unit

## Materials Needed
- Balance scale or simple kitchen scale
- A paper clip (approximately 1 gram) and a textbook or water bottle (approximately 1 kilogram)
- Labeled gram and kilogram objects if available; otherwise, labeled reference cards
- Measuring cups: 100 mL, 250 mL, 500 mL, 1 L
- Water (or sand) in a pitcher
- Small clear cups for pouring
- Index cards for exit tickets
- A medicine dropper (approximately 1 mL) if available

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build intuition for relative sizes of gram, kilogram, liter, milliliter before formal instruction.

Hold up a paper clip. "I'm going to tell you this weighs about 1 gram. Hold this in your hand." (Pass around a paper clip or a similar very light object.) "Now hold this." (Pass a textbook or full water bottle.) "This weighs about 1 kilogram."

Ask: "Which unit — gram or kilogram — would you use to measure: a strawberry? A car? A pencil? An elephant?"

Then: Hold up a medicine dropper. "This holds about 1 milliliter of water." Hold up a 1-liter bottle. "This bottle holds 1 liter." Ask: "How many of these tiny droppers would fill this whole bottle?" (1,000!) "That is why 1 liter = 1,000 milliliters."

---

## Direct Instruction (12–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Formalize the vocabulary and relationships; give reference benchmarks; model solving word problems.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Mass:** The amount of matter in an object; stays the same everywhere (unlike weight, which can change in different gravity); measured in grams or kilograms
- **Gram (g):** A small metric unit of mass; about the mass of a paper clip or a small raisin
- **Kilogram (kg):** A larger metric unit of mass; 1 kg = 1,000 g; about the mass of a liter of water
- **Liquid volume:** The amount of space a liquid takes up; measured in liters or milliliters
- **Liter (L):** A metric unit of liquid volume; a large bottle of water holds about 1 liter
- **Milliliter (mL):** A tiny unit of liquid volume; 1 L = 1,000 mL; about 20 drops of water

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1 — Mass (grams and kilograms)**

Write: 1 kg = 1,000 g

Benchmark objects:
- 1 gram ≈ a paper clip, a raisin, a small button
- 1 kilogram ≈ a textbook, a liter of water, a large apple

Give the student objects (or pictures) and practice sorting: "Does this belong in the gram column or the kilogram column?"
Examples: feather → g; backpack → kg; pencil → g; watermelon → kg; coin → g; dog → kg

**Using a scale:** If available, weigh 5 objects and record mass in grams. If the scale reads in grams, discuss how to convert a reading of 2,500 g to 2 kg 500 g (or 2.5 kg — do not require decimals).

**Part 2 — Liquid Volume (liters and milliliters)**

Write: 1 L = 1,000 mL

Benchmark containers:
- 1 mL ≈ a tiny drop; about 1/5 of a teaspoon
- 250 mL ≈ one cup
- 500 mL ≈ one pint (about 2 cups)
- 1 L ≈ a large water bottle or 4 cups

Hands-on: Pour 100 mL of water into a small cup. "This is 100 mL — about the amount of a small juice box." Pour into the 1-liter container. "How many of these 100 mL cups would fill 1 liter?" (10)

**Word Problem Strategy — Label, Set Up, Solve:**
1. Identify what is being asked (mass or volume?)
2. Identify the operation (add, subtract, multiply, divide?)
3. Write the equation
4. Solve and label the answer with the correct unit

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Mass addition:**
"A bag of rice has a mass of 2 kg. A bag of flour has a mass of 3 kg. What is the total mass?"
- 2 kg + 3 kg = 5 kg

**Example 2 — Mass subtraction:**
"A watermelon has a mass of 4 kg. After cutting it, the piece left weighs 1 kg. How much did the removed piece weigh?"
- 4 kg − 1 kg = 3 kg

**Example 3 — Volume multiplication:**
"A recipe needs 250 mL of juice. Sofia is making the recipe 4 times. How much juice does she need?"
- 250 mL × 4 = 1,000 mL = 1 liter

**Example 4 — Estimation:**
"Would you measure the water in a swimming pool in liters or milliliters?" (liters — it is a huge amount)
"Would you measure medicine you take for a cold in liters or milliliters?" (milliliters — it is a very small amount)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Circle the better unit: A dog's mass → **grams / kilograms**; An eyelash's mass → **grams / kilograms**; Water in a bathtub → **mL / L**; Cough syrup dose → **mL / L**

2. "A bag of carrots has a mass of 800 g. A bag of potatoes has a mass of 2 kg. Which is heavier? By how much?"
   (Convert: 2 kg = 2,000 g; 2,000 − 800 = 1,200 g; the potatoes are heavier by 1,200 g)

3. "A fish tank holds 30 L of water. You add 5 more liters. How much water is in the tank?" (35 L)

4. "Each student gets 200 mL of juice. There are 5 students. How much juice is needed?" (200 × 5 = 1,000 mL = 1 L)

5. "Order these from least to greatest mass: 1 kg, 500 g, 2 kg, 300 g."
   (300 g, 500 g, 1 kg (=1,000 g), 2 kg (=2,000 g))

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Choose grams (g) or kilograms (kg):
   - A skateboard → ___
   - A grape → ___
   - A bicycle → ___
   - A penny → ___

2. Choose milliliters (mL) or liters (L):
   - A swimming pool → ___
   - A drop of rain → ___
   - A juice glass → ___
   - A bathtub → ___

3. Word problems:
   - A puppy has a mass of 3 kg. An adult dog has a mass of 22 kg. What is the difference in their masses? (19 kg)
   - A pitcher holds 2 L of lemonade. You pour out 750 mL. How much is left? (2,000 − 750 = 1,250 mL)

**Stretch:**
4. Three packages have masses of 500 g, 1 kg, and 750 g. What is the total mass in grams? (500 + 1,000 + 750 = 2,250 g)

5. A bottle has 600 mL of water. After a hike, only 150 mL is left. How much water was drunk? (450 mL)

6. A recipe calls for 400 mL of broth. The cook makes 3 batches. Does one 1-liter carton have enough broth? Show your thinking. (3 × 400 = 1,200 mL; 1 L = 1,000 mL; No — 1,200 mL > 1,000 mL)

**Challenge:**
7. A scale shows two identical cans together weigh 750 g. What is the mass of each can? (750 ÷ 2 = 375 g)

8. A bucket can hold 5 L. It is filled 3/4 full. How many milliliters of water are in the bucket? (5 L = 5,000 mL; 3/4 × 5,000 = 3,750 mL)

### Interleaved Review
R1. (Elapsed time — L7) A car trip started at 10:15 A.M. and ended at 12:00 P.M. How long was the trip? (1 hour 45 minutes)

R2. (Area — L3) A rectangle is 7 m long and 4 m wide. What is its area? (28 sq m)

R3. (Multiplication — Unit 2) 4 × 8 = ___? What is 400 × 8? (32; 3,200 — extend the fact)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. True or False: 1 kilogram = 1,000 grams. If True, give an example of something that weighs about 1 kilogram.
2. A bucket holds 3 L of water. You pour in 2 more liters. How much water is in the bucket now?
3. Would you measure the mass of a strawberry in grams or kilograms? Why?

**Mastery = True with a valid example AND 5 L AND grams with a correct reason.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Use the physical objects (paper clip = 1 g, textbook = 1 kg) as tactile anchors
- Have the student hold both and say which is closer to the mystery object
- Draw the liter container and count the 100 mL cups that fill it (10 cups × 100 mL = 1,000 mL = 1 L)

### If Mastery Is Met
- Ask: "If a sack of oranges has a mass of 2 kg 300 g, what is its mass in grams?" (2,300 g)

## Extension (Optional)
**Kitchen Measurement Day:** Using kitchen scales and measuring cups at home, the student measures 5 ingredients used in cooking. Record each as: Object, Estimated unit, Actual measurement.

**Conversion Challenges:** Create a table: Mass in kg → Convert to g (e.g., 3 kg = 3,000 g; 1.5 kg = 1,500 g). Do the same for L → mL.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson is highly tactile. The physical experience of holding a paper clip (1 g) and a textbook (1 kg) in rapid succession is memorable in a way that no worksheet can replicate. Do not skip this.
- Safety note: If using water, have paper towels ready and do the pouring activities over a tray. Using sand instead of water eliminates spill risk.
- Connecting 1 kg of water = 1 liter of water is a meaningful bridge between mass and volume. "A full 1-liter bottle of water has a mass of about 1 kilogram." This helps students remember both units.
- Students confuse the abbreviations: g (gram) vs. kg (kilogram) vs. L (liter) vs. mL (milliliter). Post a reference chart throughout the unit.
- The conversion between grams and kilograms (×1,000) connects to place-value understanding. Students who have strong place-value skills (Unit 5) will find these conversions easier.
- Do not expect students to convert mixed units (e.g., 2 kg 300 g) fluently in this lesson. This is a stretch skill addressed in the Challenge tier.
