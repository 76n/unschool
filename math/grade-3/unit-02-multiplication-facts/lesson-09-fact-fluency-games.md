# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 2, Lesson 9

## Topic
Fact Fluency Games and Activities

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Practice all single-digit multiplication facts in a game context
- Improve speed and accuracy through repeated, engaging practice
- Self-assess which facts are automatic and which still need work
- Experience multiplication practice as enjoyable, not stressful

## Prerequisites
- All fact families ×0 through ×10 (Unit 1 and Unit 2, Lessons 1-8)
- Strategy chart from Lesson 8

## Materials Needed
- Deck of playing cards (face cards removed; aces = 1, use cards 1-10 or 1-9)
- Index cards or flash cards with multiplication facts
- Blank bingo grid (5 × 5) — two copies
- Pencil and paper
- Timer or clock
- Counters or small objects (for bingo markers)

**Standards:** 3.OA.7

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Automaticity:** Knowing a fact so well that the answer comes instantly, without needing to think through a strategy
- **Fluency:** Being both fast and accurate with math facts

### Instruction Notes

**Brief Introduction (5 minutes)**

"You've learned strategies for every fact family. Now it's time to practice until these facts become automatic — like how you don't have to think about how to spell your name; you just know it."

"Today we're going to play three games. Each game practices the same facts but in a different, fun way. The goal isn't to be perfect — it's to get a little faster and more confident each time you play."

**Rules Overview:**

Introduce all three games briefly, then spend the bulk of the lesson playing them.

---

## Game 1: Multiplication War (10-12 minutes)

### Setup
- Use a deck of playing cards with face cards removed. Aces = 1.
- If playing with a facilitator: divide the deck evenly between two players.
- If the student is practicing alone: draw two cards from the top of a shuffled deck each round.

### How to Play
1. Both players flip one card face-up at the same time.
2. The first person to correctly call out the product of the two cards wins both cards.
3. If both players say the answer at the same time, each player flips another card, and the winner of the next round takes all four cards.
4. Play for 5 minutes or until the deck runs out. The player with more cards wins.

### Facilitator Tips
- *If the student is much slower, intentionally pause before answering to give them a chance. The goal is practice, not defeat.*
- *When the student struggles with a fact, pause and ask: "What strategy could you use for that one?"*
- *Variation for solo practice: Student flips two cards, says the product, and checks with a multiplication chart or answer key.*

### Example Round
- Player 1 flips a 7. Player 2 flips an 8.
- First to say "56!" wins both cards.

## Game 2: Multiplication Bingo (10-12 minutes)

### Setup
- Each player creates a 5 × 5 bingo grid.
- Fill the grid with products from the multiplication table (any products from 0 to 100). Each player can choose their own numbers. Suggest including a mix of easy and hard products.
- Write "FREE" in the center square.

### Sample Bingo Card
```
| 12 | 42 | 27 | 56 |  8 |
| 45 | 64 | 18 | 35 | 72 |
| 24 | 81 |FREE| 48 | 30 |
| 16 | 54 | 49 | 36 | 63 |
| 40 | 21 | 32 |  9 | 20 |
```

### How to Play
1. The facilitator (or the student, taking turns) calls out a multiplication fact — for example, "7 × 8."
2. Both players calculate the answer (56) and check if it's on their bingo card. If it is, cover it with a counter.
3. First player to get 5 in a row (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal) calls "Bingo!" and wins.

### Facilitator Tips
- *Prepare a list of 30+ multiplication facts to call out, mixing all families.*
- *When calling a fact, occasionally use the commutative form: call "8 × 7" after having called "7 × 8" earlier — they give the same product.*
- *If the student calculates incorrectly, don't correct immediately. Ask: "Are you sure? What strategy could you check with?"*

### Suggested Call List
6 × 7, 9 × 3, 8 × 8, 5 × 9, 4 × 3, 7 × 7, 6 × 4, 8 × 5, 3 × 9, 7 × 8, 9 × 6, 4 × 8, 6 × 6, 3 × 7, 9 × 9, 5 × 4, 8 × 3, 2 × 9, 7 × 4, 6 × 8, 4 × 4, 5 × 6, 9 × 2, 8 × 7, 3 × 3, 10 × 4, 7 × 6, 9 × 8, 5 × 7, 2 × 8

## Game 3: Around the World (8-10 minutes)

### Setup
- Write 15-20 multiplication facts on index cards (one fact per card).
- Arrange them in a circle or line on the table.

### How to Play (Solo Version)
1. Start at the first card. Flip it over, read the fact, and say the answer.
2. If correct (check with answer key), move to the next card.
3. If incorrect, stay at that card. Use a strategy to figure out the correct answer, then move on.
4. Time how long it takes to get "around the world" (through all cards).
5. Shuffle and play again. Try to beat your previous time!

### How to Play (Two-Player Version)
1. Two players stand next to each other at the first "station" (card).
2. The facilitator flips the card. First player to say the correct answer advances to the next card. The slower player stays.
3. Goal: travel all the way "around the world" by beating each opponent at every station.

### Suggested Fact Cards
Focus on the facts the student finds trickiest. A good mix:
3 × 8, 4 × 7, 6 × 6, 7 × 8, 8 × 9, 9 × 6, 7 × 7, 6 × 8, 4 × 9, 3 × 9, 8 × 8, 7 × 6, 9 × 9, 6 × 7, 8 × 3, 4 × 6, 9 × 7, 8 × 6, 3 × 7, 7 × 4

### Facilitator Tips
- *Record the student's time. Celebrate improvement, not perfection.*
- *After completing a round, ask: "Which card was the hardest? Let's practice that one."*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
1. 6 × 7 = ___ (42)
2. 8 × 9 = ___ (72)
3. 7 × 8 = ___ (56)
4. "What is your favorite strategy for solving a ×9 fact? Explain it." (Accept any valid strategy: ×10 minus one group, finger trick, etc.)

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 2-3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-1/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify the specific facts that are still weak. Focus game practice on those facts in future sessions.
- Continue playing Multiplication War or Around the World daily for 5-10 minutes until facts become more automatic.
- Consider making a "fact ladder": start with the easiest unsure facts and build toward the hardest.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 10 (Problem Solving with Facts). Preview: "Next time: word problems! Now that you know your facts, we'll use them to solve real-world problems."

**Create Your Own Game:** "Design a multiplication game that you'd want to play. Write the rules, explain what materials you need, and how a player wins. Your game must practice at least 10 different facts."

**Fact Challenge:** "How many of the 100 facts on the multiplication chart (0-9 × 0-9) can you answer correctly in 5 minutes? Track your score and try to improve."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Student gets frustrated during timed activities:** Remove the timer. The goal is practice, not pressure. Say: "Let's just play and have fun. Speed comes naturally with practice."
2. **Student memorizes the game answers but not the facts:** Mix up the games and the order of facts. Varied practice is key.
3. **Student relies on counting on fingers for every fact:** This is okay as a temporary strategy, but gently push toward using the learned strategies: "Can you try the double-the-double method instead of counting?"
4. **Competitive frustration in two-player games:** If the student gets upset about losing, switch to cooperative mode: "Let's see how fast we can solve all 20 cards together!"

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson is intentionally lighter on new instruction and heavier on practice. The games ARE the lesson.
- You don't have to play all three games in one session. Pick the one(s) that work best for your student and setting. The other games can be played in future practice sessions.
- If the student is already highly fluent, this lesson can be shortened. If they still have many weak facts, extend game time and reduce the independent practice set.
- These games can (and should) be replayed frequently beyond this lesson — they make excellent 5-10 minute warm-ups for future lessons.
- Total lesson time: approximately 40-50 minutes.
