# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 2, Lesson 6

## Topic
Multiplying by 7 (Break-Apart Strategy)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Fluently multiply any single-digit number by 7
- Use the break-apart strategy: split 7 into 5 + 2 (7 × n = 5 × n + 2 × n)
- Recognize that most ×7 facts are already known through the commutative property
- Apply the break-apart strategy to reconstruct any ×7 fact they forget

## Prerequisites
- Fluent ×5 facts (Unit 1, Lesson 8)
- Fluent ×2 facts (Unit 1, Lesson 7)
- Commutative property (Unit 1, Lesson 5)
- All prior fact families in this unit (×3, ×4, ×6, ×9, ×8)

## Materials Needed
- Counters (70+)
- Paper and pencil
- Grid paper (for arrays)
- Colored pencils or markers (two colors)

**Standards:** 3.OA.7

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review ×5 and ×2 facts (the components of the break-apart strategy) and celebrate how many facts the student already knows.

1. "What is 5 × 8?" (40)
2. "What is 2 × 8?" (16)
3. "What is 8 × 6?" (48)
4. "What is 9 × 7?" (63) *"Wait — you already know a ×7 fact from your ×9 lesson!"*

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Break-apart strategy (distributive property):** Splitting one factor into two parts, multiplying each part separately, and adding the results. (Students learn the name "distributive property" later; for now, call it "break-apart.")

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — How Many New Facts? (3 minutes)**

"Good news: you already know most ×7 facts! Let's check."

| Fact | Already Known From... |
|---|---|
| 7 × 0 = 0 | Zero property (Unit 1) |
| 7 × 1 = 7 | Identity property (Unit 1) |
| 7 × 2 = 14 | ×2 facts (Unit 1) |
| 7 × 3 = 21 | ×3 facts (Lesson 1) |
| 7 × 4 = 28 | ×4 facts (Lesson 2) |
| 7 × 5 = 35 | ×5 facts (Unit 1) |
| 7 × 6 = 42 | ×6 facts (Lesson 3) |
| 7 × 7 = ? | NEW! |
| 7 × 8 = 56 | ×8 facts (Lesson 5) |
| 7 × 9 = 63 | ×9 facts (Lesson 4) |
| 7 × 10 = 70 | ×10 facts (Unit 1) |

"The only truly new fact is 7 × 7 = 49! Everything else you've already learned through the commutative property. But let's learn a strategy that works for ALL ×7 facts in case you forget one."

**Step 2 — Break-Apart Strategy: 7 = 5 + 2 (6 minutes)**

"We can break 7 into 5 + 2. Then use ×5 and ×2 facts — both of which you know!"

Demonstrate with a 7 × 6 array on grid paper. Color 5 rows one color and 2 rows another:
```
● ● ● ● ● ●  ]
● ● ● ● ● ●  ] 5 × 6 = 30  (blue)
● ● ● ● ● ●  ]
● ● ● ● ● ●  ]
● ● ● ● ● ●  ]
● ● ● ● ● ●  ] 2 × 6 = 12  (red)
● ● ● ● ● ●  ]
```
"Blue section: 5 × 6 = 30. Red section: 2 × 6 = 12. Total: 30 + 12 = 42."

More examples:
- 7 × 7: 5 × 7 = 35, plus 2 × 7 = 14. Answer: 35 + 14 = 49
- 7 × 8: 5 × 8 = 40, plus 2 × 8 = 16. Answer: 40 + 16 = 56
- 7 × 9: 5 × 9 = 45, plus 2 × 9 = 18. Answer: 45 + 18 = 63

**Step 3 — 7 × 7 = 49 (2 minutes)**

"Since 7 × 7 is the only completely new fact, let's make sure it sticks."

- Break-apart: 5 × 7 = 35, plus 2 × 7 = 14. 35 + 14 = 49.
- Memory hook: "7 × 7 = 49. Think: 'Seven ate (8) nine? No — seven times seven is forty-nine!'"
- Visual: Draw a 7 × 7 square array. Count to confirm: 49.

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 7 × 6
- Break-apart: 5 × 6 = 30, plus 2 × 6 = 12. 30 + 12 = 42. Answer: 42
- Or recall from ×6: 6 × 7 = 42. Same answer!

**Example B:** 7 × 8
- Break-apart: 5 × 8 = 40, plus 2 × 8 = 16. 40 + 16 = 56. Answer: 56
- Or recall from ×8: 8 × 7 = 56.

**Example C:** 7 × 7
- Break-apart: 5 × 7 = 35, plus 2 × 7 = 14. 35 + 14 = 49. Answer: 49

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Problem 1:** 7 × 4 = ?
*"Break it apart: 5 × 4 = ?" (20) "Plus 2 × 4 = ?" (8) "Total?" (28)*
Answer: 28

**Problem 2:** 7 × 7 = ?
*"5 × 7 = ?" (35) "Plus 2 × 7 = ?" (14) "Total?" (49)*
Answer: 49

**Problem 3:** 7 × 9 = ?
*"Try break-apart, then check with ×9 strategy." (5 × 9 = 45, 2 × 9 = 18, total = 63. Check: 10 × 7 = 70, minus 7 = 63.)*
Answer: 63

**Problem 4:** 3 × 7 = ?
*"You know this from Lesson 1! But use break-apart to verify." (5 × 3 = 15, 2 × 3 = 6, 15 + 6 = 21)*
Answer: 21

**Problem 5:** "There are 7 days in a week. How many days are in 8 weeks?"
*"What fact?" (7 × 8) "Use break-apart: 5 × 8 + 2 × 8 = 40 + 16 = 56."*
Answer: 56 days

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
1. 7 × 6 = ___ (42)
2. 7 × 7 = ___ (49)
3. 7 × 8 = ___ (56)
4. "A week has 7 days. How many days are in 6 weeks?" (42)

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 2-3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-1/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- The break-apart strategy requires adding two products. If the addition is the issue (e.g., 35 + 14), practice those sums separately.
- Focus on the "hardest" ×7 facts: 7 × 6, 7 × 7, 7 × 8. These are the ones least likely to be recalled from other fact families. Write them on index cards and practice daily.
- Remind: "For ×7 facts, you can also use the strategy from the OTHER number. 7 × 8 = 8 × 7, so you can use double ×4 instead of break-apart."

### If Mastery Is Met
Celebrate: "You now have strategies for EVERY single-digit multiplication fact!" Move to Lesson 7 (Square Numbers). Preview: "Next time we'll explore special facts where both numbers are the same: 3 × 3, 4 × 4, and more."

## Extension (Optional)
**Break-Apart Flexibility:** "Pick any ×7 fact and solve it three different ways:
1. Break 7 into 5 + 2
2. Break 7 into 3 + 4
3. Use the commutative property and a different fact family strategy

Do all three give the same answer?"

Example for 7 × 9:
1. 5 × 9 + 2 × 9 = 45 + 18 = 63
2. 3 × 9 + 4 × 9 = 27 + 36 = 63
3. 9 × 7: use ×9 strategy: 10 × 7 = 70 - 7 = 63

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Adds the two parts incorrectly:** 35 + 14 = 47 (should be 49). Encourage stacking the addition vertically or breaking into place values: 30 + 10 = 40, 5 + 4 = 9, total = 49.
2. **Uses wrong ×5 or ×2 fact:** 5 × 8 = 45 (should be 40). If ×5 facts are shaky, review Unit 1 Lesson 8 briefly.
3. **Forgets the commutative property shortcut:** Struggles with 3 × 7 without realizing they already know it as 7 × 3 = 21 from Lesson 1. Keep reminding: "Check — do you already know this fact from another family?"
4. **Freezes on 7 × 7:** This is the one genuinely new fact. Give it extra attention. A mnemonic, a visual, or simple repetition all help.

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson is as much about celebrating how many facts are already known as it is about learning the break-apart strategy. Start by showing the "How many new facts?" table — this builds confidence.
- The break-apart strategy (distributive property in disguise) is powerful and will be important in Grade 4 for multi-digit multiplication. Even if the student prefers the commutative property shortcut for ×7 facts, make sure they understand how break-apart works.
- Total lesson time: approximately 40-45 minutes.
