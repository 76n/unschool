# Facilitator Guide — Grade 3 Math, Unit 2: Multiplication Facts (3-9)

**Grade:** 3
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 2 — Multiplication Facts (3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9)
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (facilitator leads instruction and guided practice; student works independently for practice sets)

---

## Unit Overview

In Unit 1, students learned what multiplication means (equal groups, arrays, repeated addition) and became fluent with the "friendly" facts: ×0, ×1, ×2, ×5, and ×10. This unit tackles the remaining single-digit facts — ×3, ×4, ×6, ×7, ×8, and ×9 — using powerful strategies rather than rote memorization. By the end, students will have fluency with all single-digit multiplication facts (0-10).

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Multiply by 3 using skip counting and "double plus one more group"
- Multiply by 4 using the "double the double" strategy
- Multiply by 6 using "×5 plus one more group" or "double ×3"
- Multiply by 9 using the "×10 minus one group" pattern and the finger trick
- Multiply by 8 using "double ×4" or "double-double-double"
- Multiply by 7 using the break-apart (5 + 2) strategy
- Identify and describe square numbers (perfect squares through 10 × 10)
- Select and apply efficient strategies for any single-digit multiplication fact
- Solve word problems that require quick fact recall

**Why this matters:** Multiplication fact fluency is the foundation for multi-digit multiplication, long division, fractions, and algebra. Students who know their facts automatically can focus their mental energy on higher-level problem solving rather than getting stuck on basic computations. Teaching strategies — not just rote drill — gives students a safety net: even if a fact slips their memory, they can reconstruct it.

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm:

- [ ] Understands multiplication as equal groups and arrays (Unit 1, Lessons 1-4)
- [ ] Can use the commutative property (Unit 1, Lesson 5)
- [ ] Fluently multiplies by 0, 1, 2, 5, and 10 (Unit 1, Lessons 6-9)
- [ ] Can solve basic multiplication word problems (Unit 1, Lesson 10)
- [ ] Fluent addition and subtraction within 100

If the student scored below 80% on the Unit 1 assessment, review weak areas before starting this unit.

## Materials Needed for This Unit

- **Counters or small objects** (beans, buttons, coins) — at least 100
- **Grid paper** (for arrays and square number exploration)
- **Paper and pencil**
- **Number line 0-100** (can be homemade)
- **Multiplication chart** (blank for student to fill in; completed version for reference)

- **Deck of playing cards** (face cards removed, or number cards 1-10) — for Lesson 9
- **Colored pencils or markers** (for coloring arrays and patterns)

## Pacing Guidance

| Lesson | Topic | Sessions |
|--------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Multiplying by 3 | 1 |
| 2 | Multiplying by 4 (Double the Double) | 1 |
| 3 | Multiplying by 6 | 1 |
| 4 | Multiplying by 9 (Patterns and Tricks) | 1 |
| 5 | Multiplying by 8 (Double-Double-Double) | 1 |
| 6 | Multiplying by 7 (Break-Apart Strategy) | 1 |
| 7 | Square Numbers | 1 |
| 8 | Mixed Fact Practice | 1 |
| 9 | Fact Fluency Games | 1 |
| 10 | Problem Solving with Facts | 1 |
| — | Unit Assessment | Separate session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lessons 1-6 each introduce a new fact family with a strategy. If the student masters the strategy quickly, move on. If not, spend a second session on that lesson before proceeding.
- The order of fact families is intentional: ×3 and ×4 are learned first because they serve as building blocks for ×6 (double ×3) and ×8 (double ×4).
- Lesson 4 (×9) comes before ×8 because the ×9 patterns are engaging and give students a confidence boost before tackling the trickiest facts (×7, ×8).
- Lessons 7-10 are consolidation and application. Do not skip them — fluency requires mixed practice, not just isolated fact families.
- If the student is struggling broadly, consider inserting a review session between Lessons 6 and 7 to revisit all strategies before mixing them.

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Student memorizes facts without a backup strategy | If they forget a fact, they have no way to reconstruct it | Always ask: "How could you figure that out if you forgot?" Reinforce the specific strategy for each fact family. |
| Confuses ×3 and ×4 strategies | Strategies are blurring together | Slow down. Post a "strategy chart" the student can reference: ×3 = skip count or double+1 group, ×4 = double the double, etc. |
| "6 × 7 is different from 7 × 6" — treats them as two separate facts to learn | Hasn't internalized the commutative property from Unit 1 | Remind them: "You only need to learn one — the other is free!" Build both arrays to show they're the same total. |
| Uses repeated addition for everything instead of strategies | Sticking with a comfortable but slow method | Acknowledge that repeated addition works, but guide toward efficiency: "That works! Now let me show you a shortcut that's faster." |
| Digit sum pattern for ×9 is applied to other fact families | Over-generalizing a pattern | Clarify: "The digit sum = 9 trick only works for ×9. Let's check: does it work for ×8? No! Each fact family has its own special strategy." |
| Struggles with ×7 and ×8 more than other facts | These are objectively the hardest single-digit facts | This is normal. Spend extra time on Lessons 5-6. Use the break-apart strategy and connect to known facts. |

## Conversation Starters

- "Which multiplication facts do you find easiest? Which are trickiest? Why do you think that is?"
- "If you forgot what 6 × 8 equals, what strategy could you use to figure it out?"
- "Why do you think we learn strategies instead of just memorizing every fact?"
- "Can you find ×9 facts using your fingers? Show me how that works!"
- "If you know 5 × 7 = 35, how can that help you figure out 6 × 7?"
- "What patterns do you notice in the ×9 facts?" (tens digit goes up, ones digit goes down, digits always sum to 9)
- "Is it easier to figure out 8 × 3 or 3 × 8? Why?" (same answer — commutative property — but one might feel easier to think about)

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if:

- The student cannot recall ×2, ×5, or ×10 facts within 5 seconds (need to solidify Unit 1 before building on it)
- The student cannot explain what multiplication means after completing Unit 1 (conceptual gap, not just fluency gap)
- After multiple sessions, the student cannot apply any strategy and relies entirely on counting by ones
- The student shows high frustration or anxiety specifically around timed fact practice — adjust approach to emphasize strategies and de-emphasize speed pressure

---

## What Comes Next

After this unit, students move to **Unit 3: Division as the Inverse of Multiplication** where they learn to share equally, find how many groups, and connect every division fact to a known multiplication fact.

Then **Unit 4: Patterns in Multiplication** explores the multiplication table as a whole, odd/even patterns, and the distributive property.
