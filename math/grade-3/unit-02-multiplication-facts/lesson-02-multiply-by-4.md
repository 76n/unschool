# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 2, Lesson 2

## Topic
Multiplying by 4 (Double the Double)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Fluently multiply any single-digit number by 4
- Use the "double the double" strategy (4 × n = 2 × (2 × n))
- Explain why doubling twice gives the same result as multiplying by 4
- Connect ×4 to skip counting by 4s

## Prerequisites
- Fluent ×2 facts (Unit 1, Lesson 7)
- Multiplying by 3 (Unit 2, Lesson 1)
- Understanding of the doubling concept

## Materials Needed
- Counters (40+)
- Paper and pencil
- Grid paper (for arrays)

**Standards:** 3.OA.7

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Double the double:** A strategy for multiplying by 4 — first multiply by 2, then double that result

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Why "Double the Double" Works (5 minutes)**

"Multiplying by 4 is like multiplying by 2, twice. Watch:"

Build 4 groups of 6 with counters:
```
Group 1: ● ● ● ● ● ●
Group 2: ● ● ● ● ● ●
Group 3: ● ● ● ● ● ●
Group 4: ● ● ● ● ● ●
```

"First, look at just 2 groups: 2 × 6 = 12."
"Now, 4 groups is double that: 12 + 12 = 24."
"So 4 × 6 = double(double 6) = double(12) = 24."

"Why? Because 4 = 2 × 2. So 4 groups = 2 groups doubled."

**Step 2 — Practice the Strategy (5 minutes)**

Write the two-step process:

| Fact | Step 1: Double the number | Step 2: Double again | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 × 3 | 2 × 3 = 6 | 2 × 6 = 12 | 12 |
| 4 × 5 | 2 × 5 = 10 | 2 × 10 = 20 | 20 |
| 4 × 7 | 2 × 7 = 14 | 2 × 14 = 28 | 28 |
| 4 × 8 | 2 × 8 = 16 | 2 × 16 = 32 | 32 |
| 4 × 9 | 2 × 9 = 18 | 2 × 18 = 36 | 36 |

"Every time: double it, then double again. Two easy steps!"

**Step 3 — Skip Counting Connection (2 minutes)**

"You can also skip count by 4s: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40."

"The double-the-double strategy is usually faster, but skip counting is a good backup."

**Step 4 — Even Number Pattern (1 minute)**

"Notice: every ×4 answer is even. In fact, every ×4 answer is also a ×2 answer. That makes sense — if you double twice, you definitely get an even number."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 4 × 6
- Step 1: Double 6 → 12
- Step 2: Double 12 → 24
- Answer: 24
- Check with skip counting: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24. Confirmed!

**Example B:** 4 × 9
- Step 1: Double 9 → 18
- Step 2: Double 18 → 36
- Answer: 36

**Example C:** 8 × 4
- Commutative property: 8 × 4 = 4 × 8
- Step 1: Double 8 → 16
- Step 2: Double 16 → 32
- Answer: 32

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Problem 1:** 4 × 3 = ?
*"Step 1: What's double 3?" (6) "Step 2: What's double 6?" (12)*
Answer: 12

**Problem 2:** 4 × 7 = ?
*"Double 7 first." (14) "Now double 14." (28)*
Answer: 28

**Problem 3:** 4 × 5 = ?
*"Double 5." (10) "Double 10." (20) "Did you already know this from your ×5 facts?" (Yes — 5 × 4 = 20)*
Answer: 20

**Problem 4:** 6 × 4 = ?
*"Same as 4 × 6. Double 6." (12) "Double 12." (24)*
Answer: 24

**Problem 5:** "A car has 4 tires. How many tires do 8 cars have?"
*"What fact is this?" (4 × 8) "Double 8." (16) "Double 16." (32)*
Answer: 32 tires

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
1. 4 × 3 = ___ (12)
2. 4 × 7 = ___ (28)
3. 4 × 9 = ___ (36)
4. "There are 4 shelves in a bookcase. Each shelf holds 6 books. How many books fit in the bookcase?" (24)

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 2-3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-1/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Check whether the issue is the doubling strategy or the doubling arithmetic. If the student can't double 16 or 18, practice doubling two-digit numbers first.
- Offer a simpler entry point: "Let's just do the first double for now" and have them look up the second double on a number line.
- Use arrays on grid paper: draw a 4 × 7 array, split it into two 2 × 7 arrays, and show how doubling one half gives the whole.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (Multiplying by 6). Preview: "Next time we'll use what you know about ×3 AND ×5 to conquer ×6!"

## Extension (Optional)
**Array Splitting:** "Draw a 4 × 9 array on grid paper. Color the top 2 rows one color and the bottom 2 rows another. What multiplication fact does each colored section show? How do they add up to 4 × 9?"
- Top half: 2 × 9 = 18 (colored blue)
- Bottom half: 2 × 9 = 18 (colored red)
- 18 + 18 = 36 = 4 × 9

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Doubles the wrong number:** For 4 × 7, student doubles 4 instead of 7 (gets 8, then 16). Clarify: "We're doubling the OTHER number — the number in the group. 4 tells us we have 4 groups; 7 is what we double."
2. **Forgets to double twice:** Student does 2 × 7 = 14 and stops. Remind: "That's only 2 groups. We need 4 groups. Double one more time!"
3. **Doubling arithmetic errors with larger numbers:** 2 × 18 = 34 (should be 36). Encourage checking: "18 + 18. Do 10 + 10 = 20, then 8 + 8 = 16, then 20 + 16 = 36."
4. **Resists the strategy because skip counting feels safer:** That's okay! Let them skip count but encourage trying the double strategy on at least a few problems. Speed and comfort will come with practice.

### Pacing Advice
- The critical skill here is doubling two-digit numbers (14, 16, 18). If the student needs extra time on this, it's time well spent — this skill carries forward to ×6 and ×8.
- Total lesson time: approximately 40-45 minutes.
