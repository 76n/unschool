# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 2, Lesson 8

## Topic
Mixed Fact Practice — Strategy Selection

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Recall multiplication facts from all fact families (×0 through ×10) with increasing speed
- Identify which strategy is most efficient for a given fact
- Identify their own "tricky" facts and develop a plan to master them
- Solve mixed multiplication problems without being told which fact family they belong to

## Prerequisites
- All fact families ×0 through ×10 (Unit 1 and Unit 2, Lessons 1-7)
- At least one strategy for each fact family

## Materials Needed
- Paper and pencil
- Blank multiplication chart (10 × 10)
- Index cards (for making personal flash cards)
- Timer or clock (optional — for self-timed practice)

**Standards:** 3.OA.7

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick review of one fact from each family — mixing it up from the start.

1. "5 × 8 = ?" (40)
2. "9 × 3 = ?" (27)
3. "7 × 7 = ?" (49)
4. "4 × 6 = ?" (24)

*Note the student's response time. Facts answered within 3 seconds are "automatic." Facts that take longer need more practice.*

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Fact fluency:** Being able to recall multiplication facts quickly and accurately
- **Strategy selection:** Choosing the best strategy for a particular problem

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Strategy Review Chart (5 minutes)**

"Let's review all our strategies. You have a toolbox now!"

Build this chart together:

| Fact Family | Primary Strategy | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ×0 | Zero property: anything × 0 = 0 | 7 × 0 = 0 |
| ×1 | Identity property: anything × 1 = itself | 7 × 1 = 7 |
| ×2 | Double the number | 2 × 8 = double 8 = 16 |
| ×3 | Skip count by 3s, or double + one more group | 3 × 7: 2 × 7 = 14, + 7 = 21 |
| ×4 | Double the double | 4 × 7: double 7 = 14, double 14 = 28 |
| ×5 | Skip count by 5s, or half of ×10 | 5 × 8: half of 80 = 40 |
| ×6 | ×5 + one more group, or double ×3 | 6 × 7: 5 × 7 = 35, + 7 = 42 |
| ×7 | Break-apart (5 + 2), or use commutative property | 7 × 8: 5 × 8 + 2 × 8 = 40 + 16 = 56 |
| ×8 | Double ×4, or double-double-double | 8 × 7: 4 × 7 = 28, double = 56 |
| ×9 | ×10 minus one group, or finger trick | 9 × 7: 10 × 7 = 70, - 7 = 63 |
| ×10 | Add a zero | 10 × 7 = 70 |

"Keep this chart as a reference. Over time, you'll need it less and less."

**Step 2 — Fill In the Multiplication Chart (5 minutes)**

Give the student a blank 10 × 10 multiplication chart (rows and columns labeled 0-10, cells empty). Have them fill it in, using strategies as needed.

"Start with the easy ones — ×0, ×1, ×2, ×5, ×10 — and fill those rows and columns first. Then fill in what you know from this unit."

*As the student fills in the chart, note which facts they recall quickly and which they struggle with.*

**Step 3 — Identify "Tricky" Facts (3 minutes)**

"Circle any facts on the chart that you had to really think about or weren't sure of. Those are your 'tricky' facts."

Common tricky facts for most students:
- 6 × 7 = 42
- 6 × 8 = 48
- 7 × 7 = 49
- 7 × 8 = 56
- 8 × 8 = 64

"Write each tricky fact on an index card. On the back, write the strategy you'd use to figure it out. These are your personal practice cards."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** "What is 8 × 6?"
- "Which strategy? I know ×8: double ×4. So 4 × 6 = 24, double = 48."
- OR: "I know ×6: ×5 + one more. 5 × 8 = 40, + 8 = 48."
- Both work! The student picks their preferred path.

**Example B:** "What is 7 × 9?"
- "I can use ×9: 10 × 7 = 70, minus 7 = 63."
- OR: "I can use ×7 break-apart: 5 × 9 = 45, 2 × 9 = 18, 45 + 18 = 63."
- Multiple paths to the same answer.

**Example C:** "What is 3 × 4?"
- "I just know this one: 12!"
- "But if I forgot: 2 × 4 = 8, + 4 = 12. Or double the double: double 4 = 8, double 8 = ... wait, that gives ×4 for 4 × 3. Same thing! 12."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

*Present facts randomly. For each, ask the student to state the answer AND name the strategy they used.*

**Problem 1:** 6 × 9 = ?
*"What's your answer and what strategy did you use?"*
Answer: 54 (e.g., "×9: 10 × 6 = 60, minus 6 = 54")

**Problem 2:** 8 × 7 = ?
*"This is one of the toughest facts. Take your time."*
Answer: 56 (e.g., "double ×4: 4 × 7 = 28, double = 56")

**Problem 3:** 4 × 9 = ?
*"Multiple strategies could work here."*
Answer: 36 (e.g., "double the double: double 9 = 18, double 18 = 36")

**Problem 4:** 7 × 3 = ?
*"Quick — do you just know it, or do you need a strategy?"*
Answer: 21

**Problem 5:** 8 × 8 = ?
*"A square number! What is it?"*
Answer: 64 (e.g., "double ×4: 4 × 8 = 32, double = 64")

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-Level
*Solve each fact. Try to answer within 5 seconds each.*
1. 3 × 9 = ___
2. 7 × 4 = ___
3. 6 × 6 = ___
4. 8 × 5 = ___
5. 9 × 9 = ___

### Stretch
*Solve each fact and write the strategy you used.*
6. 7 × 8 = ___  Strategy: _______________
7. 6 × 7 = ___  Strategy: _______________
8. 8 × 9 = ___  Strategy: _______________
9. "Which of the facts in problems 6-8 is hardest for you? Why?"

### Challenge
10. "Without looking at a chart, list all the square numbers from 1 to 100."
11. "A student says their hardest fact is 6 × 8. Describe TWO different strategies they could use to solve it."
12. "Name a multiplication fact that can be solved using BOTH the ×9 strategy AND the ×6 strategy. Show both solutions." (Example: 6 × 9. ×9 strategy: 10 × 6 = 60, minus 6 = 54. ×6 strategy: 5 × 9 = 45, + 9 = 54.)

### Interleaved Review
*(From Unit 1 — going back to foundational concepts)*
13. "Draw an array for 4 × 5. What is the total?" (20)
14. "Write a multiplication equation for: 3 groups of 8." (3 × 8 = 24)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
*Time the student (optional). Goal: answer all four within 20 seconds total.*
1. 6 × 8 = ___ (48)
2. 9 × 7 = ___ (63)
3. 7 × 7 = ___ (49)
4. "You forgot what 8 × 6 equals. Describe one strategy you could use to figure it out." (Accept any valid strategy: double ×4, ×5 plus one more, double ×3, etc.)

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 2-3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-1/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Use the student's completed multiplication chart to identify specific gaps. Target those fact families specifically.
- Spend an extra session on this lesson, focusing exclusively on the student's "tricky" facts with their index cards.
- Consider: is the issue strategy knowledge (student doesn't remember the strategy) or arithmetic fluency (student knows the strategy but makes addition/subtraction errors)? Address the root cause.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 9 (Fact Fluency Games). Preview: "Next time: games! We're going to play multiplication war, bingo, and more to make these facts stick."

## Extension (Optional)
**Multiplication Chart Patterns:** "Look at your completed chart. Color all the even numbers one color and all the odd numbers another. What patterns do you see?" (The even/odd pattern creates a checkerboard in parts of the chart. Products are odd only when BOTH factors are odd.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Strategy confusion — applies the wrong strategy:** Student uses "×10 minus one group" for ×8 instead of ×9. Refer to the strategy chart. Over time, with practice, this will clear up.
2. **Mental overload with too many strategies:** If the student feels overwhelmed, simplify: "For any fact you forget, you can always use break-apart. Split either number into parts you know." This is a universal fallback.
3. **Slow but accurate is still progress:** If the student gets all facts correct but takes 10-15 seconds each, that's approaching mastery. Speed comes with repeated practice over days and weeks, not in one lesson.
4. **Knows the fact but writes the wrong number:** Careless errors (writing 45 instead of 54). Encourage the student to double-check by reading the problem and answer back to themselves.

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson is a consolidation lesson. The pace should be brisk — lots of practice, not much new instruction.
- The multiplication chart activity is diagnostic: it tells you where to focus. If the student fills in 95% instantly, they're ready for games (Lesson 9). If there are significant gaps, consider an extra practice session.
- Don't introduce timing pressure if the student shows anxiety. Frame speed as a goal, not a requirement: "Let's see how many you can get in a minute — there's no wrong score, we're just tracking improvement."
- Total lesson time: approximately 40-45 minutes.
