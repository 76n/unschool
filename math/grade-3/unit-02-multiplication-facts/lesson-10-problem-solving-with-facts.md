# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 2, Lesson 10

## Topic
Problem Solving with Multiplication Facts

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve one-step word problems using any single-digit multiplication fact
- Solve two-step word problems that combine multiplication with addition or subtraction
- Identify the multiplication fact needed in a word problem before solving
- Write an equation to represent a word problem

## Prerequisites
- All fact families ×0 through ×10 (Unit 1 and Unit 2, Lessons 1-9)
- Word problem structure from Unit 1, Lesson 10
- Addition and subtraction within 100

## Materials Needed
- Paper and pencil
- Strategy chart from Lesson 8 (as reference, if needed)

**Standards:** 3.OA.7, 3.OA.8

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick fact recall and review of how to translate words into multiplication.

1. "7 × 8 = ?" (56)
2. "6 × 9 = ?" (54)
3. "If I say '4 groups of 7,' what equation is that?" (4 × 7 = 28)
4. "If I say 'each child gets 6 stickers and there are 8 children,' what equation is that?" (8 × 6 = 48)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **One-step problem:** A word problem that requires only one operation to solve
- **Two-step problem:** A word problem that requires two operations (e.g., multiply then add)
- **Equation:** A number sentence that shows the operation and the answer (e.g., 6 × 8 = 48)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Reading Word Problems Strategically (4 minutes)**

"Before you do any math, always ask yourself three questions:
1. What do I know? (Find the numbers and what they represent.)
2. What am I trying to find? (What is the question asking?)
3. What operation do I need? (Is this equal groups → multiplication?)"

Model with this problem:

"A baker makes 8 trays of muffins. Each tray holds 6 muffins. How many muffins did the baker make?"

- What do I know? 8 trays, 6 muffins per tray.
- What am I trying to find? Total number of muffins.
- What operation? Equal groups → multiplication.
- Equation: 8 × 6 = 48.
- Answer: 48 muffins.

**Step 2 — Two-Step Problems (5 minutes)**

"Sometimes you need to do MORE than one step."

Model:

"Maya has 3 bags of apples with 7 apples in each bag. She eats 5 apples. How many apples does she have left?"

- Step 1: Find the total. 3 × 7 = 21 apples.
- Step 2: Subtract the ones she ate. 21 - 5 = 16 apples.
- Answer: 16 apples.

Another model:

"Carlos buys 4 packs of pencils with 6 pencils in each pack. He already had 10 pencils. How many pencils does he have now?"

- Step 1: Find how many he bought. 4 × 6 = 24 pencils.
- Step 2: Add the pencils he already had. 24 + 10 = 34 pencils.
- Answer: 34 pencils.

"For two-step problems: solve the multiplication first, then do the addition or subtraction."

**Step 3 — Writing Your Own Equation (3 minutes)**

"Always write an equation BEFORE solving. This shows your thinking and helps you check your work."

Practice: "There are 9 rows of chairs with 8 chairs in each row."
- Equation: 9 × 8 = ?
- Solve: 9 × 8 = 72
- Answer: 72 chairs

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** (One-step)
"A parking lot has 7 rows of cars. Each row has 9 cars. How many cars are in the lot?"
- Equation: 7 × 9 = 63
- Answer: 63 cars

**Example B:** (Two-step, multiply then add)
"Emma reads 6 pages every night. She reads for 8 nights. Then she reads 12 more pages on Saturday morning. How many pages did she read in total?"
- Step 1: 6 × 8 = 48 pages (nightly reading)
- Step 2: 48 + 12 = 60 pages (total)
- Answer: 60 pages

**Example C:** (Two-step, multiply then subtract)
"A farmer plants 9 rows of tomato plants with 6 plants in each row. During a storm, 8 plants are damaged. How many undamaged plants remain?"
- Step 1: 9 × 6 = 54 plants (total planted)
- Step 2: 54 - 8 = 46 plants (remaining)
- Answer: 46 plants

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Problem 1:** "A classroom has 6 tables. Each table has 4 chairs. How many chairs are there?"
*"What do you know? What are you finding? What operation?"*
Equation: 6 × 4 = 24. Answer: 24 chairs.

**Problem 2:** "A store has 8 shelves of books. Each shelf has 7 books. How many books does the store have?"
*"Write your equation first, then solve."*
Equation: 8 × 7 = 56. Answer: 56 books.

**Problem 3:** "Sam collects 3 boxes of rocks with 9 rocks in each box. He gives 4 rocks to his friend. How many rocks does Sam have now?"
*"This is a two-step problem. What's Step 1? Step 2?"*
Step 1: 3 × 9 = 27. Step 2: 27 - 4 = 23. Answer: 23 rocks.

**Problem 4:** "A school has 7 classes. Each class has 8 students. 6 students are absent today. How many students are at school?"
*"Step 1: total students. Step 2: subtract the absent ones."*
Step 1: 7 × 8 = 56. Step 2: 56 - 6 = 50. Answer: 50 students.

**Problem 5:** "Tina bakes 4 batches of cookies. Each batch makes 9 cookies. She already had 15 cookies. How many cookies does she have now?"
*"Multiply first, then add."*
Step 1: 4 × 9 = 36. Step 2: 36 + 15 = 51. Answer: 51 cookies.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
1. "A box holds 8 oranges. You have 6 boxes. How many oranges do you have?" Equation: ___ Answer: ___ (8 × 6 = 48; 48 oranges)
2. "There are 7 teams with 9 players each. 3 players go home. How many players are left?" Steps: ___ Answer: ___ (7 × 9 = 63, 63 - 3 = 60; 60 players)
3. 7 × 8 = ___ (56)
4. 9 × 9 = ___ (81)

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 2-3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-1/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If fact fluency is the issue (gets the right approach but wrong product), continue practicing facts with games from Lesson 9.
- If problem comprehension is the issue (doesn't know which operation to use), return to the three-question framework: What do I know? What am I finding? What operation?
- For two-step problems specifically: practice identifying when "extra" information (an additional addition or subtraction) is present. Have the student underline the key numbers and label what each represents.

### If Mastery Is Met
Congratulations — the unit is complete! Preview: "You've mastered all your multiplication facts and can use them to solve problems. Next up: the Unit Assessment, where you'll show off everything you've learned."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Adds when they should multiply (or vice versa):** Student sees "6 tables, 4 chairs each" and writes 6 + 4 = 10. Redirect: "Are there 4 chairs TOTAL, or 4 chairs at EACH table? If each table has 4, that's equal groups — multiplication!"
2. **Skips a step in two-step problems:** Student solves only the multiplication and gives that as the final answer. Encourage: "Re-read the problem. Is there anything else that happens after the multiplication?"
3. **Writes the equation but makes an arithmetic error:** The setup is right but the fact recall is wrong (e.g., 7 × 9 = 61). This is a fluency issue, not a problem-solving issue. Check the answer against the strategy.
4. **Includes unnecessary information:** In a problem with extra numbers, student tries to use all of them. Practice identifying: "Which numbers answer 'how many groups?' and 'how many in each group?'"

### Pacing Advice
- Read all word problems aloud for any student who struggles with reading comprehension. The goal of this lesson is multiplication problem solving, not reading fluency.
- Two-step problems are a significant jump in complexity. If the student struggles, focus on one-step problems first and introduce two-step problems in a follow-up session.
- Total lesson time: approximately 40-50 minutes.
