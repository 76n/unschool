# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 2, Lesson 1

## Topic
Multiplying by 3

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Fluently multiply any single-digit number by 3
- Use skip counting by 3s as a multiplication strategy
- Use the "double plus one more group" strategy (3 × n = 2 × n + n)
- Choose between strategies based on which feels faster

## Prerequisites
- Multiplication as equal groups and arrays (Unit 1, Lessons 1-4)
- Commutative property (Unit 1, Lesson 5)
- Fluent ×2 facts (Unit 1, Lesson 7)

## Materials Needed
- Counters (30+)
- Paper and pencil
- Number line 0-30

**Standards:** 3.OA.7

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate ×2 facts (the foundation for the "double plus one more group" strategy) and review skip counting.

1. "What is 2 × 6?" (12)
2. "What is 2 × 8?" (16)
3. "What is 2 × 7?" (14)
4. "Count by 3s to 30." (3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30)

*If the student cannot skip count by 3s, practice this first. Write the sequence on paper and read it together several times before proceeding.*

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Skip counting:** Counting forward by a number other than 1 (e.g., 3, 6, 9, 12...)
- **Strategy:** A method or plan for solving a problem efficiently

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Skip Counting Strategy (4 minutes)**

"Today we're going to learn our ×3 facts. One strategy is skip counting by 3s."

Demonstrate on the number line:
- 3 × 1: Jump to 3. Answer: 3
- 3 × 2: Jump to 3, then 6. Answer: 6
- 3 × 3: Jump to 3, 6, 9. Answer: 9
- 3 × 4: Jump to 3, 6, 9, 12. Answer: 12

"When we multiply 3 × 4, we skip count by 3s four times: 3, 6, 9, 12."

Build the ×3 table together using skip counting:

| Fact | Skip Count | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 3 × 1 | 3 | 3 |
| 3 × 2 | 3, 6 | 6 |
| 3 × 3 | 3, 6, 9 | 9 |
| 3 × 4 | 3, 6, 9, 12 | 12 |
| 3 × 5 | 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 | 15 |
| 3 × 6 | 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18 | 18 |
| 3 × 7 | 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21 | 21 |
| 3 × 8 | 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24 | 24 |
| 3 × 9 | 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27 | 27 |
| 3 × 10 | 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30 | 30 |

"Skip counting works, but for bigger numbers like 3 × 8, it takes a while. Let me show you a faster shortcut."

**Step 2 — "Double Plus One More Group" Strategy (5 minutes)**

"You already know your ×2 facts. Here's the trick: 3 groups is the same as 2 groups plus 1 more group."

Write on paper:
- 3 × 6 = 2 × 6 + 6 = 12 + 6 = 18
- 3 × 7 = 2 × 7 + 7 = 14 + 7 = 21
- 3 × 8 = 2 × 8 + 8 = 16 + 8 = 24

Show with counters:
- Make 3 groups of 6 counters
- Circle the first 2 groups: "That's 2 × 6 = 12. You already know that!"
- Point to the third group: "Plus one more group of 6 = 12 + 6 = 18."

"So whenever you need a ×3 fact, think: double it, then add one more group."

**Step 3 — Commutative Property Reminder (2 minutes)**

"Remember from Unit 1: 3 × 7 = 7 × 3. So if someone asks you 8 × 3, that's the same as 3 × 8 = 24. You don't need to learn these separately!"

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 3 × 5
- Skip counting: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15. Answer: 15
- Double plus one more: 2 × 5 = 10, then 10 + 5 = 15. Answer: 15
- (You might already know this from Unit 1: ×5 facts!)

**Example B:** 3 × 8
- Skip counting: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24. Answer: 24
- Double plus one more: 2 × 8 = 16, then 16 + 8 = 24. Answer: 24
- "Which strategy felt faster?" *(Most students will say the double strategy.)*

**Example C:** 7 × 3
- Commutative property: 7 × 3 = 3 × 7
- Double plus one more: 2 × 7 = 14, then 14 + 7 = 21. Answer: 21

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Problem 1:** 3 × 4 = ?
*"What's 2 × 4?" (8) "Now add one more group of 4." (8 + 4 = 12)*
Answer: 12

**Problem 2:** 3 × 6 = ?
*"Try the double plus one more strategy. What's your double?" (2 × 6 = 12) "Plus one more group of 6?" (12 + 6 = 18)*
Answer: 18

**Problem 3:** 9 × 3 = ?
*"Is this the same as 3 × 9?" (Yes — commutative property) "What's 2 × 9?" (18) "Plus one more group of 9?" (18 + 9 = 27)*
Answer: 27

**Problem 4:** 3 × 3 = ?
*"What's 2 × 3?" (6) "Plus one more group of 3?" (6 + 3 = 9) "Or you might just know: 3, 6, 9!"*
Answer: 9

**Problem 5:** "A tricycle has 3 wheels. How many wheels do 7 tricycles have?"
*"What multiplication fact is this?" (3 × 7 or 7 × 3) "Use your strategy." (2 × 7 = 14, plus 7 = 21)*
Answer: 21 wheels

### Stretch
6. 3 × ___ = 24 (What number times 3 gives 24?)
7. "Each triangle has 3 sides. How many sides do 8 triangles have?"
8. "Show two different strategies for solving 3 × 6. Do you get the same answer?"

### Challenge
9. "What is 3 × 3 × 3?" (3 × 3 = 9, then 9 × 3 = 27)
10. "I multiply a number by 3 and get 21. What number did I start with?" (7)
11. "Is 3 × 8 + 3 the same as 3 × 9? Explain why." (Yes: 3 × 8 is 8 groups of 3 = 24. Adding one more 3 gives 9 groups of 3 = 27. 3 × 9 = 27.)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
1. 3 × 4 = ___ (12)
2. 3 × 8 = ___ (24)
3. 7 × 3 = ___ (21)
4. "There are 3 rows of desks with 6 desks in each row. How many desks are there?" (18)

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 2-3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-1/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to skip counting by 3s. Have the student write the sequence 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30 and practice reading it aloud.
- If the "double plus one more" strategy is confusing, stick with skip counting for now — the student can revisit the doubling strategy later.
- Practice 5 ×3 facts with flash cards, using the skip counting sequence as a reference.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 2 (Multiplying by 4). Preview: "Tomorrow we'll learn a cool trick for ×4: double the double!"

## Extension (Optional)
**Pattern Hunt:** "Write all the ×3 answers from 3 × 1 to 3 × 10. Add the digits of each answer. What do you notice?"
- 3 (3), 6 (6), 9 (9), 12 (1+2=3), 15 (1+5=6), 18 (1+8=9), 21 (2+1=3), 24 (2+4=6), 27 (2+7=9), 30 (3+0=3)
- The digit sums repeat: 3, 6, 9, 3, 6, 9, 3, 6, 9, 3!

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Confuses ×3 with +3:** Student writes 3 × 4 = 7. Redirect: "3 × 4 means 3 groups of 4 — not 3 plus 4. Let's build 3 groups of 4 counters."
2. **Loses count during skip counting:** Student miscounts (e.g., 3, 6, 9, 11). Have them write the skip counting sequence as a reference and practice it orally.
3. **Adds wrong in the doubling strategy:** Student gets 2 × 8 = 16, then 16 + 8 = 22 (addition error). Remind them to check the addition carefully. Use a number line if needed.
4. **Forgets to apply the commutative property:** Student freezes on 9 × 3 because they only practiced 3 × 9. Consistently remind: "Flip it! 9 × 3 is the same as 3 × 9."

### Pacing Advice
- If the student already skip counts by 3s easily, spend less time on Step 1 and more time on the doubling strategy.
- The doubling strategy is important for later lessons (×6 = double ×3, ×8 = double ×4), so make sure the student understands the idea even if they prefer skip counting for now.
- Total lesson time: approximately 40-45 minutes.
