# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 2, Lesson 3

## Topic
Multiplying by 6

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Fluently multiply any single-digit number by 6
- Use the "×5 plus one more group" strategy (6 × n = 5 × n + n)
- Use the "double ×3" strategy (6 × n = 2 × (3 × n))
- Choose the strategy that feels most efficient for a given problem

## Prerequisites
- Fluent ×5 facts (Unit 1, Lesson 8)
- Fluent ×3 facts (Unit 2, Lesson 1)
- Fluent ×2 facts / doubling (Unit 1, Lesson 7)
- Commutative property (Unit 1, Lesson 5)

## Materials Needed
- Counters (60+)
- Paper and pencil
- Grid paper (for arrays)

**Standards:** 3.OA.7

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate ×5 and ×3 facts — both are building blocks for ×6.

1. "What is 5 × 7?" (35)
2. "What is 3 × 8?" (24)
3. "What is 5 × 9?" (45)
4. "What is 3 × 6?" (18)

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Break-apart strategy:** Splitting a multiplication problem into two easier problems and adding the results

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Strategy 1: "×5 Plus One More Group" (5 minutes)**

"You already know all your ×5 facts. Here's the secret: 6 groups is just 5 groups plus 1 more group."

Demonstrate with counters — make 6 groups of 4:
```
Group 1: ● ● ● ●
Group 2: ● ● ● ●
Group 3: ● ● ● ●
Group 4: ● ● ● ●
Group 5: ● ● ● ●  ← These 5 groups = 5 × 4 = 20
Group 6: ● ● ● ●  ← Plus 1 more group of 4
```
"5 × 4 = 20. Plus 4 more = 24. So 6 × 4 = 24."

More examples:
- 6 × 7: 5 × 7 = 35, plus 7 = 42
- 6 × 3: 5 × 3 = 15, plus 3 = 18
- 6 × 9: 5 × 9 = 45, plus 9 = 54

"Every time: find the ×5 fact, then add one more group."

**Step 2 — Strategy 2: "Double ×3" (5 minutes)**

"Here's another way to think about ×6: six is double three. So 6 groups = double the amount of 3 groups."

- 6 × 4: 3 × 4 = 12, double 12 = 24. Answer: 24
- 6 × 7: 3 × 7 = 21, double 21 = 42. Answer: 42
- 6 × 8: 3 × 8 = 24, double 24 = 48. Answer: 48

Show with an array on grid paper — draw a 6 × 4 array and split it horizontally:
```
● ● ● ●  ]
● ● ● ●  ] 3 × 4 = 12
● ● ● ●  ]
● ● ● ●  ]
● ● ● ●  ] 3 × 4 = 12
● ● ● ●  ]
```
"Top half: 3 × 4 = 12. Bottom half: 3 × 4 = 12. Total: 12 + 12 = 24."

**Step 3 — Which Strategy to Choose? (2 minutes)**

"Both strategies give the same answer. Use whichever feels easier to you!"

| Fact | ×5 Plus One More | Double ×3 |
|---|---|---|
| 6 × 4 | 5 × 4 = 20, + 4 = 24 | 3 × 4 = 12, double = 24 |
| 6 × 7 | 5 × 7 = 35, + 7 = 42 | 3 × 7 = 21, double = 42 |
| 6 × 8 | 5 × 8 = 40, + 8 = 48 | 3 × 8 = 24, double = 48 |

"Some students prefer ×5 plus one more because ×5 facts are easy. Others prefer doubling ×3. Try both and pick your favorite."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 6 × 5
- ×5 plus one more: 5 × 5 = 25, plus 5 = 30. Answer: 30
- Double ×3: 3 × 5 = 15, double = 30. Answer: 30
- (Or you might already know: 5 × 6 = 30 from your ×5 facts!)

**Example B:** 6 × 8
- ×5 plus one more: 5 × 8 = 40, plus 8 = 48. Answer: 48
- Double ×3: 3 × 8 = 24, double = 48. Answer: 48

**Example C:** 9 × 6
- Commutative property: 9 × 6 = 6 × 9
- ×5 plus one more: 5 × 9 = 45, plus 9 = 54. Answer: 54
- Double ×3: 3 × 9 = 27, double = 54. Answer: 54

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Problem 1:** 6 × 3 = ?
*"Use ×5 plus one more. What's 5 × 3?" (15) "Plus one more group of 3?" (15 + 3 = 18)*
Answer: 18

**Problem 2:** 6 × 6 = ?
*"Try double ×3. What's 3 × 6?" (18) "Double 18?" (36)*
Answer: 36

**Problem 3:** 6 × 7 = ?
*"Your choice of strategy! Which will you use?" (Either: 5 × 7 = 35 + 7 = 42, or 3 × 7 = 21 doubled = 42)*
Answer: 42

**Problem 4:** 4 × 6 = ?
*"Same as 6 × 4. What strategy would you pick?" (5 × 4 = 20 + 4 = 24, or 3 × 4 = 12 doubled = 24)*
Answer: 24

**Problem 5:** "A six-pack of juice boxes costs $6. How many juice boxes are in 8 six-packs?"
*"What fact is this?" (6 × 8) "Solve it your way."*
Answer: 48 juice boxes

2. 6 × 4 = ___
3. 6 × 6 = ___

### Stretch
6. 6 × ___ = 42  (What number times 6 gives 42?)
7. "An insect has 6 legs. How many legs do 9 insects have?"
8. "Use TWO different strategies to solve 6 × 7. Show your work for both."

### Challenge
9. "If 6 × 8 = 48, what is 6 × 9? How did you figure it out without starting over?" (48 + 6 = 54, because 6 × 9 is one more group of 6 than 6 × 8)
10. "A student says 6 × 7 = 36. What mistake might they have made? What is the correct answer?" (They might have confused it with 6 × 6 = 36. Correct: 6 × 7 = 42.)
11. "Is 6 × n always even? Why?" (Yes — 6 is even, so any number of groups of 6 gives an even total. Also, 6 × n = 2 × 3 × n, and the factor of 2 makes it always even.)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
1. 6 × 4 = ___ (24)
2. 6 × 7 = ___ (42)
3. 6 × 9 = ___ (54)
4. "A carton holds 6 eggs. You buy 6 cartons. How many eggs do you have?" (36)

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 2-3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-1/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Determine which strategy the student prefers and practice that one. Don't require both strategies if one is working well.
- If the ×5 plus one more strategy is weak, check whether the student knows ×5 facts. If not, review Unit 1 Lesson 8 first.
- Create a reference card: "6 × ? = (5 × ?) + ?" for the student to use during practice.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Multiplying by 9). Preview: "Next time: the ×9 facts — they have some of the coolest patterns in math!"

## Extension (Optional)
**Even Products:** "All ×6 answers are even, AND they're all multiples of both 2 and 3. Pick any ×6 answer and prove it's a ×2 answer and a ×3 answer."
- Example: 6 × 7 = 42. Is 42 a ×2 answer? Yes: 2 × 21 = 42. Is 42 a ×3 answer? Yes: 3 × 14 = 42.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Adds the wrong "one more group":** For 6 × 7, student computes 5 × 7 = 35, then adds 6 instead of 7. Clarify: "The extra group has 7 in it — the same number as all the other groups."
2. **Doubling errors with larger numbers:** Double 21 = 42, but student writes 40. Break the doubling into parts: "20 + 20 = 40, 1 + 1 = 2, total = 42."
3. **Confuses ×6 with ×5:** Student gives the ×5 answer and stops. The ×5 fact is the starting point, not the final answer!
4. **Strategy overload:** Student is overwhelmed by having two strategies. Focus on ONE strategy for now and let the other be a backup. Most students prefer ×5 plus one more.

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson introduces two strategies. If the student is comfortable with both, great. If not, prioritize the one that clicks and let the other be introduced casually.
- The "double ×3" strategy prepares the student for Lesson 5, where ×8 = "double ×4" follows the same logic.
- Total lesson time: approximately 40-45 minutes.
