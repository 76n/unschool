# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 2, Lesson 5

## Topic
Multiplying by 8 (Double-Double-Double)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Fluently multiply any single-digit number by 8
- Use the "double ×4" strategy (8 × n = 2 × (4 × n))
- Use the "double-double-double" strategy (8 × n = 2 × 2 × 2 × n)
- Connect ×8 to known ×4 facts

## Prerequisites
- Fluent ×4 facts using "double the double" (Unit 2, Lesson 2)
- Fluent ×2 facts / doubling (Unit 1, Lesson 7)
- Commutative property (Unit 1, Lesson 5)

## Materials Needed
- Counters (80+)
- Paper and pencil
- Grid paper (for arrays)

**Standards:** 3.OA.7

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Double-double-double:** A strategy for multiplying by 8 — double three times (because 8 = 2 × 2 × 2)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Strategy 1: "Double ×4" (5 minutes)**

"You already mastered ×4 facts using 'double the double.' Now here's the cool part: 8 is double 4. So ×8 is just double your ×4 answer!"

Demonstrate:
- 8 × 3: First, 4 × 3 = 12. Double 12 = 24. Answer: 24
- 8 × 6: First, 4 × 6 = 24. Double 24 = 48. Answer: 48
- 8 × 7: First, 4 × 7 = 28. Double 28 = 56. Answer: 56

Show with an array on grid paper — draw an 8 × 3 array and split it:
```
● ● ●  ]
● ● ●  ] 4 × 3 = 12
● ● ●  ]
● ● ●  ]
● ● ●  ]
● ● ●  ] 4 × 3 = 12
● ● ●  ]
● ● ●  ]
```
"Top half: 4 × 3 = 12. Bottom half: 4 × 3 = 12. Total: 12 + 12 = 24."

**Step 2 — Strategy 2: "Double-Double-Double" (4 minutes)**

"Here's another way to think about it. Since 8 = 2 × 2 × 2, you can just double three times."

Walk through 8 × 7:
- Start with 7
- Double once: 7 × 2 = 14 (that's ×2)
- Double twice: 14 × 2 = 28 (that's ×4)
- Double three times: 28 × 2 = 56 (that's ×8!)

"Three doublings! 7 → 14 → 28 → 56."

More examples:
| Start | Double once (×2) | Double twice (×4) | Double three times (×8) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 6 | 12 | 24 |
| 5 | 10 | 20 | 40 |
| 6 | 12 | 24 | 48 |
| 9 | 18 | 36 | 72 |

"This is really the same as 'double ×4' — you're just doing all the doublings in a row."

**Step 3 — Which Strategy? (2 minutes)**

"If you already KNOW the ×4 fact, use 'double ×4' — it's only one step. If you need to figure out the ×4 fact too, use 'double-double-double' from scratch."

Build the ×8 table together:

| Fact | ×4 Fact | Double It | ×8 Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 × 1 | 4 × 1 = 4 | 4 × 2 = 8 | 8 |
| 8 × 2 | 4 × 2 = 8 | 8 × 2 = 16 | 16 |
| 8 × 3 | 4 × 3 = 12 | 12 × 2 = 24 | 24 |
| 8 × 4 | 4 × 4 = 16 | 16 × 2 = 32 | 32 |
| 8 × 5 | 4 × 5 = 20 | 20 × 2 = 40 | 40 |
| 8 × 6 | 4 × 6 = 24 | 24 × 2 = 48 | 48 |
| 8 × 7 | 4 × 7 = 28 | 28 × 2 = 56 | 56 |
| 8 × 8 | 4 × 8 = 32 | 32 × 2 = 64 | 64 |
| 8 × 9 | 4 × 9 = 36 | 36 × 2 = 72 | 72 |
| 8 × 10 | 4 × 10 = 40 | 40 × 2 = 80 | 80 |

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 8 × 4
- Double ×4: 4 × 4 = 16, double 16 = 32. Answer: 32
- Double-double-double: 4 → 8 → 16 → 32. Answer: 32

**Example B:** 8 × 7
- Double ×4: 4 × 7 = 28, double 28 = 56. Answer: 56
- Check: 28 + 28 = 56. Confirmed!

**Example C:** 6 × 8
- Commutative property: 6 × 8 = 8 × 6
- Double ×4: 4 × 6 = 24, double 24 = 48. Answer: 48

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Problem 1:** 8 × 3 = ?
*"What's 4 × 3?" (12) "Double 12?" (24)*
Answer: 24

**Problem 2:** 8 × 5 = ?
*"What's 4 × 5?" (20) "Double 20?" (40) "Or you might know: 5 × 8 = 40 from your ×5 facts!"*
Answer: 40

**Problem 3:** 8 × 8 = ?
*"This is a tough one! 4 × 8 = ?" (32) "Double 32?" (64)*
Answer: 64

**Problem 4:** 8 × 9 = ?
*"4 × 9 = ?" (36) "Double 36?" (72) "Check with ×9 strategy: 10 × 8 = 80, minus 8 = 72. Same answer!"*
Answer: 72

**Problem 5:** "An octopus has 8 arms. How many arms do 6 octopuses have?"
*"What fact?" (8 × 6) "Use your strategy."* (4 × 6 = 24, double = 48)
Answer: 48 arms

11. "A student says 8 × 7 = 54. Use what you know to show why this is wrong and find the correct answer." (4 × 7 = 28, double 28 = 56, not 54. Also: 5 + 4 = 9, which is a ×9 pattern, not ×8.)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
1. 8 × 3 = ___ (24)
2. 8 × 7 = ___ (56)
3. 8 × 9 = ___ (72)
4. "A box has 8 crayons. You buy 8 boxes. How many crayons do you have?" (64)

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 2-3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-1/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Check whether the student knows their ×4 facts. If ×4 is shaky, go back to Lesson 2 before continuing with ×8.
- If ×4 is solid but doubling the ×4 product is hard, practice doubling two-digit numbers: 12→24, 16→32, 24→48, 28→56, 32→64, 36→72.
- Use the "double-double-double" chain as a scaffold: write the chain framework (___ → ___ → ___ → ___) and have the student fill in each step.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 6 (Multiplying by 7). Preview: "Next time: ×7 — the last new fact family! We'll use a clever break-apart trick."

## Extension (Optional)
**Doubling Chains:** "Start with any number. Double it. Double again. Double again. How far can you go?"
- Start with 3: 3 → 6 → 12 → 24 → 48 → 96 → 192...
- Start with 5: 5 → 10 → 20 → 40 → 80 → 160...

"The third number in the chain is always your starting number × 4. The fourth is your starting number × 8!"

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Only doubles once instead of twice (for "double ×4" strategy):** Student computes 4 × 7 = 28 and gives 28 as the ×8 answer. Remind: "28 is the ×4 answer. You need ×8, so double one more time."
2. **Doubling errors on larger products:** Double 28 = 54 (should be 56). Break it apart: "20 + 20 = 40, 8 + 8 = 16, 40 + 16 = 56."
3. **Confuses ×6 and ×8 strategies:** ×6 = double ×3; ×8 = double ×4. A strategy chart on the wall or desk helps keep these straight.
4. **Struggles with 8 × 7 and 8 × 8:** These are among the hardest multiplication facts. Extra practice is normal and expected.

### Pacing Advice
- The doubling of two-digit numbers (especially 28→56, 32→64, 36→72) is the hardest arithmetic in this lesson. Budget extra time for this if needed.
- Cross-check with the ×9 strategy from Lesson 4: for 8 × 9, students can verify using ×10 minus one group. This builds confidence when two strategies produce the same answer.
- Total lesson time: approximately 40-45 minutes.
