## Bonus — Answer Key

**B1.** Numbers that round to 500 (nearest 100) AND round to 460 (nearest 10):
- Must have tens digit 4 or 5 such that nearest 100 is 500: tens digit ≥ 5 → 500. So hundreds=4, tens≥5.
- Must round to 460 (nearest 10): ones digit makes it round to 460. Ones digit 0–4 → round down to 460. Ones digit 5–9 → round up to 470.
- So nearest 10 = 460 means ones digit ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3, 4} and tens digit = 6 (between 460 and 470).
- Wait: round to 500 (nearest 100) means the number is between 450 and 499 inclusive (450 rounds up, 449 rounds down).
- Numbers between 450–499 that round to 460: 455, 456, 457, 458, 459, 460, 461, 462, 463, 464.
- But wait: nearest 100 of 455–464: all have hundreds=4, tens=5 or 6. Nearest 100: tens digit ≥ 5 → round up to 500.
  - 455–459: tens digit 5 → rounds to 500 ✓; rounds to nearest 10: 455→460, 456→460, 457→460, 458→460, 459→460 ✓
  - 460–464: tens digit 6 → rounds to 500 ✓; rounds to nearest 10: 460→460, 461→460, 462→460, 463→460, 464→460 ✓
- All possible values: **455, 456, 457, 458, 459, 460, 461, 462, 463, 464**.
- Award 1 point for any 3+ correct values listed (accept partial credit).

**B2.** 900 − ___ = 367. So ___ = 900 − 367.
900 − 367: tens=0, ones 0<7: regroup from hundreds: 9→8, 10 tens; regroup: 9 tens, 10 ones; 10−7=3; 9−6=3; 8−3=5 → wait: 900−367: 9|0|0. Ones 0<7: regroup. 10−7=3. 9−6=3. 8−3=5. Answer: **533**.
Check: 533 + 367 = 900 ✓

## Notes for Facilitator

**Common error patterns to watch for:**
- Regrouping notation absent (student gets correct answer but shows no regrouping marks — could indicate memorization without understanding; require notation)
- Subtracting smaller digit from larger in the wrong direction (e.g., in 653−218, writes 2 in ones place instead of regrouping to get 15−8=5)
- Forgetting to use regrouped digit when adding tens/hundreds
- Across-zero subtraction: incorrectly leaving the zero unchanged
- Two-step word problems: stopping after step 1, or using result from step 1 incorrectly in step 2
- Estimation labeled as exact answer (student writes "800" with no ≈ symbol)
