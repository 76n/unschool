# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 5, Lesson 6

## Topic
Subtracting Within 1,000 — Regrouping from the Tens Place

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract a three-digit number from a three-digit number with no regrouping.
- Identify when regrouping from the tens place is needed (ones digit of the top number is smaller than the ones digit of the bottom number).
- Regroup 1 ten into 10 ones and subtract three-digit numbers with regrouping in the ones place.
- Use estimation to check whether a difference is reasonable.

## Prerequisites
- Adding with regrouping (Lessons 4–5)
- Subtraction within 100 with regrouping (G2)
- Rounding and estimation (Lessons 1–3)
- Understanding of the relationship between addition and subtraction

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks: ones, tens, hundreds
- Place value mat (Hundreds | Tens | Ones)
- Grid paper or lined paper
- Whiteboard and markers

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate subtraction knowledge and connect addition/subtraction as inverse operations.

**Inverse connection:** Write on the board: 246 + 137 = 383.
Ask: "If I know this addition fact, what two subtraction facts do I also know?"
(383 − 137 = 246; 383 − 246 = 137.)

Then pose: "247 − 130 = ____" Work this together. "How is this different from subtraction with regrouping?" (Here ones: 7−0=7, tens: 4−3=1 — no regrouping needed.)

Next pose: "243 − 127 = ____" Ask: "In the ones column, we need to subtract 7 from 3. Can we? What should we do?" This launches the lesson.

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build understanding of regrouping in subtraction — regrouping 1 ten as 10 ones — using CRA.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Regroup (subtraction):** Exchange 1 ten for 10 ones so you have enough ones to subtract from.
- **Minuend:** The number being subtracted FROM (the top number).
- **Subtrahend:** The number being subtracted (the bottom number).
- **Difference:** The result of subtraction.

### Instruction Notes

**CRA Phase: Concrete → Representational → Abstract**

**Critical language:** Say "regroup" — not "borrow." We are not borrowing anything; we are exchanging 1 ten for 10 ones (equivalent trade).

**Procedure (regrouping ones from tens):**
1. Look at the ones column. Can you subtract the bottom digit from the top digit?
2. If not (top < bottom), regroup: take 1 ten-rod from the tens place and break it into 10 ones.
3. Add those 10 ones to the existing ones. Now subtract the ones.
4. Continue with tens and hundreds.

**Model with blocks — 243 − 127:**
- Set up 2 hundreds, 4 tens, 3 ones.
- Ones: need to subtract 7 from 3. Can't! Take 1 ten-rod, break into 10 ones. Now have 3 tens and 13 ones.
- 13 − 7 = 6 ones.
- Tens: 3 − 2 = 1 ten.
- Hundreds: 2 − 1 = 1 hundred.
- Answer: 116.

**Show the algorithm:**
```
     3 13
  2  4  3
−  1  2  7
----------
  1  1  6
```
Cross out the 4 in tens, write 3. Write small 1 next to the 3 in ones to show 13.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1: No regrouping — 568 − 234**
Estimate: 600 − 200 = 400.
```
  568
− 234
-----
  334
```
Ones: 8−4=4. Tens: 6−3=3. Hundreds: 5−2=3. Answer: **334**. Reasonable? (Yes.)

**Example 2: Regrouping ones — 452 − 127**
Estimate: 500 − 100 = 400.
```
  4  4 12
  4  5  2
−  1  2  7
----------
  3  2  5
```
Ones: 2 < 7, regroup. Take 1 ten from 5 tens → 4 tens, 12 ones. 12−7=5. Tens: 4−2=2. Hundreds: 4−1=3. Answer: **325**. Reasonable? (Yes, close to 400.)

**Example 3: Regrouping ones — 735 − 318**
Estimate: 700 − 300 = 400.
```
  7  2 15
  7  3  5
−  3  1  8
----------
  4  1  7
```
Ones: 5 < 8, regroup. Take 1 ten → 2 tens, 15 ones. 15−8=7. Tens: 2−1=1. Hundreds: 7−3=4. Answer: **417**. Reasonable? (Yes, close to 400.)

**Check with addition:** 417 + 318 = 735. ✓ (Model checking a subtraction answer by adding back the subtrahend.)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Use base-ten blocks for problems 1–2, drawings for problems 3–4, and the algorithm alone for problem 5.

1. 364 − 218 = ____ (ones: 4 < 8, regroup; result: 146)
2. 571 − 243 = ____ (ones: 1 < 3, regroup; result: 328)
3. 843 − 325 = ____ (no regroup needed; result: 518)
4. 627 − 419 = ____ (ones: 7 > 9? No, 7 < 9, regroup; result: 208)
5. 956 − 337 = ____ (no regroup; result: 619)

For each regrouping problem:
- Estimate first.
- Identify whether regrouping is needed (compare ones digits).
- Regroup using blocks or show the notation.
- Check by adding back.

**Facilitator prompts:**
- "Before you start, look at the ones column. Top digit or bottom digit — which is bigger? If the bottom is bigger, you need to regroup."
- "When you regroup, what changes in the tens column?" (The tens digit decreases by 1.)
- "How can you check your answer? Try adding your answer to the number you subtracted."

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
Subtract. Show your regrouping.

a) 543 − 217 = ____
b) 761 − 328 = ____

**Mastery = both correct with regrouping notation visible where needed.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to base-ten blocks. Have the student physically break apart a ten-rod into 10 ones.
- Use the "see if you need to regroup" check as a two-step process: (1) look at ones column, (2) ask "Is the top number smaller? Then I need to regroup."
- Practice with simpler problems: two-digit minus one-digit with regrouping (e.g., 43 − 7), then move to three-digit.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Preview: "Today we regrouped from the tens place. What if the tens place is 0, or if you need to regroup from the hundreds? That's tomorrow's challenge."
- Challenge: "Can you write a subtraction problem where regrouping gives you exactly 5 ones?"

## Extension (Optional)
**Subtraction word problem creation:** Students write their own subtraction word problem using a real-world context (a school, a store, a sports event). They must include: the starting amount (three-digit), the amount removed, and the remaining amount. Share and solve each other's problems.

**Fact family practice:** Given 3 numbers (e.g., 254, 118, 136), write the full fact family:
- 118 + 136 = 254
- 136 + 118 = 254
- 254 − 118 = 136
- 254 − 136 = 118

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Students who have been taught "borrow" terminology from other sources may need explicit reframing: "We use the word 'regroup' because we're not borrowing — we're making an equal trade."
- The crossed-out digit and small number notation (see algorithm examples above) should be modeled slowly until students replicate it precisely. Sloppy notation leads to errors, especially when double regrouping is introduced in Lesson 7.
- The "check by adding" strategy is extremely valuable — it reinforces the inverse relationship between addition and subtraction and gives students an independent self-checking tool.
- Some students will make the common error of subtracting the smaller digit from the larger regardless of position (e.g., in the ones place of 452 − 127, writing 7−2=5 instead of regrouping). Counter this with blocks: "Show me 2 ones. Now take away 7 of them." The impossibility is physically apparent.
