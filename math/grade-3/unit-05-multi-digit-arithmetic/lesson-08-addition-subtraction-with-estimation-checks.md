# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 5, Lesson 8

## Topic
Addition and Subtraction with Estimation Checks

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Estimate a sum or difference by rounding to the nearest 10 or 100.
- Compute exact sums and differences using the standard algorithm with regrouping.
- Use estimation to judge whether an exact answer is reasonable.
- Identify and explain errors in computation by comparing to an estimate.

## Prerequisites
- Rounding to nearest 10 and 100 (Lessons 1–2)
- Estimation (Lesson 3)
- Adding with single and double regrouping (Lessons 4–5)
- Subtracting with regrouping (Lessons 6–7)

## Materials Needed
- Grid paper or lined paper
- Whiteboard and markers
- Math journal

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Fluency with both addition and subtraction, setting up estimation-as-checking habit.

**Error hunt:** Show three "student work" problems on the board. Two are correct, one has an error. Students identify the error.

Problem A: 347 + 285 = 632 ✓ or ✗?
Problem B: 624 − 387 = 337 ✓ or ✗?
Problem C: 453 + 179 = 532 ✓ or ✗?

Tell students: "Use estimation to catch the error before you check the exact math."
- A: 300+300=600. 632 is close. Likely correct. (Exact: 632 ✓)
- B: 600−400=200. 337 is not far from 200, but let's check. (Exact: 624−387=237, NOT 337 — error!)
- C: 500+200=700. 532 is NOT close to 700. Error! (Exact: 453+179=632, not 532 — the student likely forgot to regroup.)

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Consolidate the estimate–compute–check habit as a three-step process.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Reasonable:** An answer makes sense (is close to the estimate and appropriate in context).
- **Unreasonable:** An answer is too large, too small, or doesn't make sense given the estimate or context.
- **Estimate–Compute–Check (ECC):** The three-step habit for every multi-digit computation.

### Instruction Notes

**The Estimate–Compute–Check (ECC) habit:**
1. **Estimate first:** Round both numbers and compute mentally.
2. **Compute exactly:** Use the standard algorithm with regrouping as needed.
3. **Check:** Is the exact answer close to the estimate? (Usually within 50–100 for problems in this unit.) If not, something is wrong — find the error.

**When estimation reveals an error:**
- If exact answer is wildly different from the estimate, look for:
  - Wrong operation (added instead of subtracted, or vice versa)
  - Regrouping error (forgot to add/subtract the regrouped digit)
  - Column misalignment
  - Subtracted smaller from larger without regrouping

### Worked Examples

**Example 1: Addition — estimate, compute, check.**
Problem: 467 + 354
Step 1 (Estimate): 500 + 400 = **900** (approx)
Step 2 (Compute):
```
  1 1
  467
+ 354
-----
  821
```
Step 3 (Check): Is 821 close to 900? Yes (within 100). Reasonable. ✓

**Example 2: Subtraction — estimate, compute, check.**
Problem: 823 − 456
Step 1 (Estimate): 800 − 500 = **300** (approx)
Step 2 (Compute):
```
  7  11  13
  8   2   3
−  4   5   6
-----------
  3   6   7
```
Step 3 (Check): Is 367 close to 300? Yes. Reasonable. ✓

**Example 3: Catching an error.**
A student found: 714 − 268 = 554.
Step 1 (Estimate): 700 − 300 = 400.
Is 554 close to 400? No — 554 is 154 away from 400. Suspicious.
Exact check:
```
  6  10  14
  7   1   4
−  2   6   8
-----------
  4   4   6
```
Correct answer is 446. The student made an error (likely did not regroup properly in tens).

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
Solve using ECC. Write your estimate and mark whether your answer is reasonable.

a) 476 + 358 = ____  Estimate: ____  Reasonable? ____
b) 703 − 264 = ____  Estimate: ____  Reasonable? ____

**Mastery = both answers correct, estimates calculated correctly, and correct reasonableness judgment.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice estimation alone (return to Lesson 3 warm-up format) before reintegrating exact computation.
- Require students to write the estimate before touching the exact computation every single time — make it non-negotiable habit.
- Use think-alouds: "I'm estimating FIRST so I know what to expect. My estimate is 800. Now I'll compute and I'm expecting an answer near 800."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Advance to multi-step problems: "Estimate 347 + 265 − 189. How would you handle estimating a multi-step problem?"
- Introduce mental math strategies: compensating (e.g., 398 + 256 = 400 + 256 − 2 = 654), making 10s/100s.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Estimation detective:** Present 5 addition or subtraction problems, each with three possible answers. Students use estimation only (no exact computation) to identify the correct answer from the three choices. Then verify with exact computation.

**Mental math shortcut:** "Try adding 299 + 376 in your head. Hint: think of 299 as 300 − 1." (300 + 376 = 676; subtract 1 = 675.) Try a few similar near-hundreds mental math problems.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Lesson 8 is a synthesis lesson — do not introduce new procedures. The goal is to cement habits and fluency.
- Some students will have memorized the algorithm and skip estimation. Push back: estimation is a mathematical discipline, not an optional extra step.
- The "error hunt" warm-up format can be used periodically in future lessons — it motivates careful computation and teaches self-checking.
- Students who are procedurally fluent but skip the check step are not yet fully meeting the standard. 3.NBT.2 specifically references "strategies based on place value," which includes the judgment of reasonableness.
