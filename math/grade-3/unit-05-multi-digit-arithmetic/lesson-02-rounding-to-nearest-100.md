# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 5, Lesson 2

## Topic
Rounding to the Nearest 100

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Locate a three-digit number on an open number line between two consecutive hundreds.
- Identify the nearest hundred below and the nearest hundred above a given number.
- Apply the midpoint rule to round any number within 1,000 to the nearest 100.
- Round numbers to either the nearest 10 or the nearest 100 as directed.

## Prerequisites
- Round to the nearest 10 (Lesson 1)
- Understanding of place value: hundreds, tens, ones
- Counting by 100s to 1,000

## Materials Needed
- Open number line strips (blank, one per student)
- Whiteboard and markers
- Place value chart (hundreds | tens | ones)
- Math journal or lined paper

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Bridge from rounding to the nearest 10 to the new skill of rounding to the nearest 100.

**Quick review:** Write three numbers on the board: 46, 253, 817.
Ask: "Round each to the nearest 10." (50; 250; 820.)

Then ask: "If I wanted to round 253 to the nearest hundred instead, which two hundreds would it fall between?" Draw a number line:
```
200 ----------------------------- 300
```
Ask: "What is halfway between 200 and 300?" (250.) "Where does 253 fall?" (Just past 250, so it rounds up to 300.)

Tell students: "Today we're doing the same thing we did yesterday, but now we round to the nearest hundred instead of the nearest ten."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Extend the number-line rounding strategy to the nearest 100.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Nearest 100:** The multiple of 100 (100, 200, 300, …) that a number is closest to.
- **Midpoint (for hundreds):** The number exactly halfway between two consecutive hundreds; it ends in 50 (e.g., 150 is the midpoint between 100 and 200; 350 is the midpoint between 300 and 400).

### Instruction Notes

**CRA Phase: Representational (number line)**
Continue using the number line. The strategy is identical to Lesson 1 — find the two benchmarks, find the midpoint, plot the number, apply the rule — but the benchmarks and midpoints are now hundreds.

**Step-by-step procedure:**
1. Look at the tens digit (and ones digit together, i.e., the last two digits).
2. Find the two consecutive hundreds the number falls between.
3. Find the midpoint (the hundred + 50).
4. Draw the number line and plot the number.
5. Apply the rule:
   - Last two digits form a number less than 50 → round down (keep the lower hundred).
   - Last two digits form a number equal to or greater than 50 → round up (go to the higher hundred).

**Simplified rule:** Look at the tens digit only:
- Tens digit is 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4 → round down.
- Tens digit is 5, 6, 7, 8, or 9 → round up.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1: Round 347 to the nearest 100.**
- Falls between 300 and 400; midpoint is 350.
- Last two digits: 47 < 50 (or tens digit: 4 < 5) → round down → **300**

```
300 ----347---- 350 ----------- 400
               midpoint
```

**Example 2: Round 672 to the nearest 100.**
- Falls between 600 and 700; midpoint is 650.
- Last two digits: 72 > 50 (tens digit: 7 ≥ 5) → round up → **700**

```
600 ----------- 650 ---672---- 700
```

**Example 3: Round 850 to the nearest 100.**
- Falls between 800 and 900; midpoint is 850.
- Last two digits: 50 = 50 (right at midpoint) → round up → **900**

```
800 ----------- 850 ----------- 900
               (at midpoint → round up)
```

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
For each number, have the student:
(a) Name the two hundreds it falls between.
(b) Name the midpoint.
(c) Draw a number line.
(d) State the rounded number.

1. Round 134 to the nearest 100. (Between 100 and 200; midpoint 150; 34 < 50 → round down → **100**)
2. Round 276 to the nearest 100. (Between 200 and 300; midpoint 250; 76 > 50 → round up → **300**)
3. Round 550 to the nearest 100. (Between 500 and 600; midpoint 550; at midpoint → round up → **600**)
4. Round 923 to the nearest 100. (Between 900 and 1,000; midpoint 950; 23 < 50 → round down → **900**)
5. Round 489 to the nearest 100. (Between 400 and 500; midpoint 450; 89 > 50 → round up → **500**)

**Facilitator prompts:**
- "Cover the hundreds digit. What are the last two digits? Is that less than, equal to, or more than 50?"
- For #4, point out that 1,000 is a valid answer when rounding a number in the 900s — it shows rounding can reach 1,000.

### Difficulty Tiers

4. 612 → ____
5. 749 → ____
6. 863 → ____
7. 950 → ____
8. 309 → ____

**Stretch:**
Round each number to BOTH the nearest 10 and the nearest 100.
1. 347 → nearest 10: ____, nearest 100: ____
2. 682 → nearest 10: ____, nearest 100: ____
3. 955 → nearest 10: ____, nearest 100: ____

**Challenge:**
1. I am a three-digit number. When rounded to the nearest 100, I become 400. What is the smallest number I could be? What is the largest?
2. Carlos rounded 350 to the nearest 100 and got 300. Is he correct? Explain using a number line.
3. A number rounded to the nearest 10 is 380. When rounded to the nearest 100, it is 400. What could the number be? List at least three possibilities.

### Interleaved Review
*(Review from Units 1–4)*
1. What is 8 × 9? (72)
2. 56 ÷ 7 = ____? (8)
3. Which fraction is larger: 1/2 or 1/4? How do you know? (1/2; the larger the denominator, the smaller each piece when the whole is the same.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
Round each number to the nearest 100. Draw a number line for at least one.

a) 423 → ____
b) 751 → ____
c) 650 → ____

**Mastery = at least 2 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the number line with the student and work through the midpoint concept again.
- Have the student cover the hundreds digit and focus only on the last two digits: "Is this less than 50, equal to 50, or more than 50?"
- Use hundred charts to count the distance from the number to each nearest hundred.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Preview estimation: "If you rounded 347 to 300 and 612 to 600, what would a quick estimate of 347 + 612 be?" (900.)
- Ask: "When might you use rounding in real life? Why would you round to the nearest 100 instead of the nearest 10?"

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Rounding target practice:** Give the student a target hundred (e.g., 700). Challenge them to write 8 different three-digit numbers that round to 700. Can they write one that ends in 0? One that ends in 9? One at the midpoint?

**Compare and discuss:** "346 rounded to the nearest 10 is 350. Rounded to the nearest 100, it's 300. Which rounded number is more precise (closer to the actual number)? Why?"

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Students frequently confuse rounding to the nearest 10 vs. nearest 100. Use the vocabulary consistently: "We are rounding to the nearest hundred. I'm looking at the tens digit."
- The "cover the hundreds digit, look at the last two digits" strategy helps students avoid looking at the ones digit when rounding to the nearest 100.
- Lesson 3 requires both skills simultaneously (rounding to whichever place is asked), so make sure students are fluent with both before proceeding.
- If a student asks why the midpoint rule "rounds up," validate the question: mathematicians made a convention so everyone agrees. You could also mention that "rounding half up" is the most common rule in everyday life.
