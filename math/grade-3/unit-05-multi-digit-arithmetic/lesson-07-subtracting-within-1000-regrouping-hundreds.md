# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 5, Lesson 7

## Topic
Subtracting Within 1,000 — Regrouping from the Hundreds; Regrouping Across Zeros

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract with regrouping from the hundreds place (when the tens digit of the minuend is insufficient).
- Subtract with regrouping in both the ones and tens places (double regrouping in subtraction).
- Subtract when the tens digit of the minuend is zero (regrouping across a zero).
- Use estimation to verify that a difference is reasonable.

## Prerequisites
- Subtracting with regrouping from the tens place (Lesson 6)
- Adding with double regrouping (Lesson 5)
- Rounding and estimation (Lessons 1–3)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks: ones, tens, hundreds
- Place value mat (Hundreds | Tens | Ones)
- Grid paper or lined paper
- Whiteboard and markers

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review single-place regrouping and set up the need for regrouping from hundreds.

**Review problem (Lesson 6):** 743 − 218 = ____
(Ones: 3<8, regroup. 13−8=5. Tens: 3−1=2. Hundreds: 7−2=5. Answer: 525.)

Now pose: "What about 724 − 356?"
Ask: "Ones: 4 < 6. I need to regroup. I'll take 1 ten from the tens place. How many tens are in 724?" (2 tens.) "OK, so after regrouping I have 1 ten and 14 ones. Now the tens: 1 − 5? That doesn't work either! What do I do?"

Let students think and share ideas. This launches the lesson.

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Extend subtraction regrouping to two places, including across a zero in the tens place.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Regroup from hundreds:** Exchange 1 hundred for 10 tens so you have enough tens to subtract from.
- **Double regrouping (subtraction):** When both the ones place and the tens place need regrouping in the same problem.
- **Regrouping across zero:** When the tens digit is 0, you must first regroup from the hundreds (getting 10 tens), then regroup 1 of those tens into 10 ones.

### Instruction Notes

**CRA Phase: Concrete through Abstract — emphasize blocks for zero-regrouping**

**Part 1: Regrouping from hundreds (when tens is not 0)**
Model 724 − 356 with blocks.
- Start: 7 hundreds, 2 tens, 4 ones.
- Ones: 4 < 6. Regroup 1 ten → 1 ten, 14 ones. 14−6=8.
- Tens: now 1 ten. 1 < 5. Regroup 1 hundred → 6 hundreds, 11 tens. 11−5=6.
- Hundreds: 6−3=3.
- Answer: 368.

**Part 2: Regrouping across zero**
This is the hardest case. Model 603 − 247 with blocks.
- Start: 6 hundreds, 0 tens, 3 ones.
- Ones: 3 < 7. Need to regroup a ten. But there are 0 tens! So first regroup 1 hundred into 10 tens: now 5 hundreds, 10 tens, 3 ones. Now regroup 1 ten into 10 ones: 5 hundreds, 9 tens, 13 ones.
- 13 − 7 = 6 ones.
- Tens: 9 − 4 = 5 tens.
- Hundreds: 5 − 2 = 3 hundreds.
- Answer: 356.

**Algorithm notation for across-zero:**
```
    5  9  13
    6  0   3
−   2  4   7
-----------
    3  5   6
```
Cross out 6 hundreds, write 5. Cross out 0 tens, write 9 (we took 10 tens from the hundred, then gave 1 ten away to make 10 ones, leaving 9 tens). Write 13 in ones.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1: Double regrouping (from tens AND hundreds) — 724 − 356**
Estimate: 700 − 400 = 300.
```
     6  11  14    ← 7 hundreds became 6; 2 tens became 11 after regroup from hundreds; 4 ones became 14 after regroup from tens
     7   2   4
−    3   5   6
-----------
     3   6   8
```
Step-by-step:
- Ones: 4 < 6. Regroup: borrow from tens. Cross 2 tens → 1 ten. Write 14 ones.
- Tens: 1 < 5. Regroup: borrow from hundreds. Cross 7 hundreds → 6 hundreds. Cross 1 ten → 11 tens.
- 14−6=8. 11−5=6. 6−3=3. Answer: **368**. Reasonable? (Yes, close to 300.)

**Example 2: Regrouping across zero — 500 − 273**
Estimate: 500 − 300 = 200.
```
     4   9  10
     5   0   0
−    2   7   3
-----------
     2   2   7
```
- Ones: 0 < 3. Tens digit is 0. Regroup from hundreds: 5→4 hundreds, 10 tens. Then regroup 1 ten: 9 tens, 10 ones. 10−3=7.
- Tens: 9 − 7 = 2.
- Hundreds: 4 − 2 = 2.
- Answer: **227**. Reasonable? (Yes, close to 200.)

**Example 3: Regrouping across zero — 406 − 148**
Estimate: 400 − 100 = 300.
```
     3   9  16
     4   0   6
−    1   4   8
-----------
     2   5   8
```
- Ones: 6 < 8. Tens digit is 0. Regroup from hundreds: 4→3 hundreds, 10 tens. Regroup 1 ten: 9 tens, 16 ones. 16−8=8.
- Tens: 9 − 4 = 5.
- Hundreds: 3 − 1 = 2.
- Answer: **258**. Reasonable? (Yes, close to 300.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
Subtract. Show all regrouping marks.

a) 645 − 378 = ____
b) 802 − 456 = ____

**Mastery = both answers correct with proper notation.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Go back to base-ten blocks exclusively for zeros-regrouping problems.
- Use a verbal script: "I need to subtract ones. Can I? (No.) Is there a ten I can break apart? (No — tens digit is zero.) What do I do? (Break a hundred first.)" Practice the script with physical blocks until the procedure is automatic.
- Separate the two steps visually: first "unstack" the hundred into 10 tens (show this exchange), then "unstack" a ten into 10 ones (second exchange).

### If Mastery Is Met
- Extend to mental math: "How would you subtract 299 from 500? Hint: think of 299 as 300 − 1."
  (500 − 300 = 200; add back 1 = 201.)
- Preview: "We've now mastered both addition and subtraction with regrouping. Next lesson we'll use estimation to double-check all kinds of problems."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Regrouping across zero is the single most difficult skill in this unit. Allocate extra time if needed; mastery here is essential before Lesson 9 (word problems).
- The notation must be crystal clear. Students often get confused by multiple crossed-out digits. Require them to use a systematic approach: cross out → write new value above → move to next column.
- Physical base-ten blocks remain the most powerful intervention for this lesson. The visual of "there are no tens to break apart, so I must break a hundred first" is immediately concrete.
- "Regrouping from hundreds into tens" language: some students benefit from calling this "double-un-stacking" — first un-stacking a hundred-flat into ten ten-rods, then un-stacking one ten-rod into ten ones.
- If time allows, model the check-by-adding strategy prominently here: 802 − 456 = 346. Check: 346 + 456 = 802. This builds the habit before word problems in Lesson 9.
