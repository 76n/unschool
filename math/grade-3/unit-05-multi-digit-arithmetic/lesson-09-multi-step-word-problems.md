# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 5, Lesson 9

## Topic
Multi-Step Word Problems (Addition and Subtraction)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read and interpret one- and two-step addition and subtraction word problems within 1,000.
- Use the Read–Draw–Write (RDW) strategy to organize and solve word problems.
- Choose the correct operation (addition or subtraction) for each step of a multi-step problem.
- Write a complete answer sentence with a label.

## Prerequisites
- Adding with regrouping (Lessons 4–5)
- Subtracting with regrouping (Lessons 6–7)
- Estimation (Lesson 3)
- Basic word problem comprehension from prior grades

## Materials Needed
- Math journal or lined paper
- Whiteboard and markers
- Pencils

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate operation-selection thinking and estimation habit.

**Operation sort:** Read each scenario aloud. Students hold up a "+" card or "−" card (or thumbs up/down) to show which operation.
1. "A store had 450 apples. They sold some." (−)
2. "Two libraries combined their book collections." (+)
3. "A class collected cans. Another class added more." (+)
4. "A pitcher had some water. Some was poured out." (−)

Then ask: "How do you decide which operation to use?" (Look at whether you're joining/combining or separating/removing.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce and model the Read–Draw–Write (RDW) strategy for solving word problems.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Read–Draw–Write (RDW):** A three-step problem-solving strategy.
  - Read: Read the problem carefully; identify what is known and what you need to find.
  - Draw: Create a visual model (bar model, number line, or place value drawing).
  - Write: Write a number sentence and a complete answer sentence.
- **Bar model:** A rectangular diagram that shows the parts and total of a problem.
- **Multi-step problem:** A problem that requires two or more calculations to reach the final answer.

### Instruction Notes

**Why RDW?**
Students who jump straight to numbers often make operation errors. Drawing a model forces them to think about the structure (part + part = whole, or whole − part = part) before calculating.

**Bar model structure:**
- Addition (join): Two parts fit inside a whole.
  ```
  [part 1][part 2]
  [     whole    ]
  ```
- Subtraction (separate): Take part away from the whole.
  ```
  [     whole    ]
  [part 1][  ?  ]
  ```

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (One-step addition):**
"The school garden has 347 red flowers and 215 yellow flowers. How many flowers are there in all?"

*Read:* Known: 347 red, 215 yellow. Find: total.
*Draw:*
```
[  347  ][  215  ]
[      ?        ]
```
*Write:* 347 + 215 = 562. There are **562 flowers** in all.

Algorithm:
```
  347
+ 215
-----
  562
```

**Example 2 (One-step subtraction):**
"A farmer picked 624 tomatoes. She sold 378 tomatoes at the market. How many tomatoes does she have left?"

*Read:* Known: started with 624, sold 378. Find: remaining.
*Draw:*
```
[      624      ]
[  378  ][  ?  ]
```
*Write:* 624 − 378 = 246. The farmer has **246 tomatoes** left.

Algorithm (double regroup):
```
  5  11  14
  6   2   4
−  3   7   8
-----------
  2   4   6
```

**Example 3 (Two-step problem):**
"A library had 512 books. On Monday, 137 books were checked out. On Tuesday, 84 books were returned. How many books does the library have now?"

*Read:* Step 1: books after Monday. Step 2: books after Tuesday.
*Draw:*
```
Step 1:
[    512    ]
[137][  ?  ]   → 512 − 137 = 375

Step 2:
[375][84]
[   ?   ]      → 375 + 84 = 459
```
*Write:*
Step 1: 512 − 137 = 375 books after Monday.
Step 2: 375 + 84 = 459 books after Tuesday.

The library has **459 books** now.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Solve each using RDW. Write the draw, the number sentence, and a complete answer sentence.

**Problem 1:**
"The third grade classes collected 438 cans in October and 275 cans in November. How many cans did they collect in all?"

(Draw: two parts → whole. 438 + 275 = 713 cans total.)

**Problem 2:**
"A pet shelter had 356 animals. Then 129 animals were adopted. How many animals remain at the shelter?"

(Draw: whole → subtract part. 356 − 129 = 227 animals.)

**Problem 3:**
"Mia had 245 stickers. She gave 78 stickers to her sister and then earned 130 more stickers for good behavior. How many stickers does Mia have now?"

(Step 1: 245 − 78 = 167. Step 2: 167 + 130 = 297. Mia has 297 stickers now.)

**Facilitator prompts:**
- "Before you calculate, draw the bar model. What is the whole? What are the parts?"
- "Does this problem have one step or two steps? How do you know?"
- "Write your answer in a complete sentence with a label (cans, animals, stickers, etc.)."
- For Problem 3: "After the first step, what is the new starting number for step 2?"

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
Solve each word problem using RDW. Show your work.

1. A toy factory made 523 blue cars and 348 red cars. How many cars were made in all?

2. A school had 875 students. After some moved away, 648 students remained. How many students moved away?

3. The school store had 400 erasers. On Monday, they sold 156 erasers. On Tuesday they received a shipment of 200 more. How many erasers are there now?

4. In a reading challenge, James read 247 pages in Week 1 and 318 pages in Week 2. How many total pages did he read?

**Stretch:**
Solve these two-step problems. Show all work and write answer sentences.

1. A baker baked 325 muffins in the morning and 248 muffins in the afternoon. She sold 417 muffins by end of day. How many muffins are left?

2. A town collected 563 pounds of recycling in March. In April they collected 189 more pounds than in March. How many total pounds did they collect over both months?

**Challenge:**
1. There are 3 classes collecting bottled water. Class A collected 245 bottles, Class B collected 187 bottles, and Class C collected 312 bottles. After donating 500 bottles to a shelter, how many bottles do the three classes have together?

2. Write your own two-step word problem that uses both addition and subtraction. Include the answer key. The numbers in your problem should be three-digit numbers.

3. The town library had an unknown number of DVDs. After receiving 138 new DVDs and then lending out 294, there were 267 DVDs remaining. How many DVDs did the library start with? (Hint: work backwards.)

### Interleaved Review
*(Review from Units 1–4)*
1. Maria has 7 bags with 8 apples in each bag. How many apples does she have in all? (7 × 8 = 56 apples)
2. There are 36 students split equally into 4 groups. How many students are in each group? (36 ÷ 4 = 9 students)
3. A sandwich is cut into 8 equal pieces. You eat 3 pieces. What fraction of the sandwich remains? (5/8)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
Solve using RDW. Show your model and number sentence.

"A school collected 482 canned goods on Friday and 316 canned goods on Saturday. After donating 250 to a food bank, how many canned goods does the school have left?"

**Mastery = correct bar model drawn for at least one step, both calculations correct (482+316=798; 798−250=548), complete answer sentence.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Separate problem solving from computation: have students identify operation and draw bar model on one day, then compute on the next.
- Reduce to one-step problems until confident with operation selection and bar models.
- Read the problem aloud together and mark the key information (given numbers, the question) with different colors.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Extend to three-step problems: add another event to a two-step problem.
- Ask: "Could you solve Problem 3 in a different order? Does the order of steps matter?"

## Extension (Optional)
**Class word problem book:** Each student writes a multi-step word problem using real numbers from the school or home (e.g., books in the classroom library, steps walked in a day, items in a pantry). Compile into a class problem book for others to solve.

**Missing value problems:** Give students problems with an unknown in different positions:
- "Jasmine had ____ apples. She ate 37 and had 265 left. How many did she start with?" (265 + 37 = 302)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The most common word problem error in Grade 3 is choosing the wrong operation. The bar model visual is the most effective intervention — it makes the part-whole structure visible.
- Require complete answer sentences always. This is not a punctuation preference — it forces students to connect the number to a real-world meaning, which develops mathematical communication.
- For two-step problems, label each step clearly (Step 1, Step 2) in the written work. This prevents students from losing track of intermediate results.
- Real-world context matters: use numbers and scenarios relevant to the students' lives (school events, local sports, hobbies) to increase engagement.
- The "work backwards" problem in the Challenge section is an excellent preview of algebraic thinking — finding an unknown start value using inverse operations.
