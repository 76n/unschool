# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 1, Lesson 6

## Topic
Multiplying by 0 and 1

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- State and apply the identity property: any number × 1 = that number (n × 1 = n)
- State and apply the zero property: any number × 0 = 0 (n × 0 = 0)
- Explain why these properties make sense using groups and arrays
- Solve multiplication problems involving 0 and 1

## Prerequisites
- Multiplication notation (Lesson 3)
- Equal groups and arrays (Lessons 1, 4)
- Commutative property (Lesson 5)

## Materials Needed
- Counters and cups
- Paper and pencil

**Standards:** 3.OA.5

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Identity property:** Multiplying by 1 gives the same number (the number keeps its identity)
- **Zero property:** Multiplying by 0 always gives 0

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Multiplying by 1 (5 minutes)**

Place 1 cup with 5 counters. "How many groups?" (1.) "How many in the group?" (5.)

"1 group of 5 = 5. So 1 × 5 = 5."

Try another: 1 cup with 8 counters. "1 × 8 = 8."

And another: 3 cups with 1 counter each. "3 × 1 = 3."

Write the pattern:
- 1 × 5 = 5
- 1 × 8 = 8
- 3 × 1 = 3
- 7 × 1 = 7

"What do you notice?" (When you multiply by 1, you get the other number.)

"This is the IDENTITY PROPERTY. Multiplying by 1 doesn't change the number — it keeps its identity. It's like the number stays the same."

"Think of it this way: 1 group of anything is just that thing. And any number of groups with 1 in each is just that many."

**Step 2 — Multiplying by 0 (5 minutes)**

Place 3 cups. "How many groups?" (3.)

"Now I'm going to put ZERO counters in each cup." (Put nothing in them.)

"3 groups of 0. How many total?" (0.)

"3 × 0 = 0."

Try: 5 cups, 0 in each. "5 × 0 = 0."

And: 0 groups of 4. "I have NO groups at all. 0 × 4 = 0."

Write the pattern:
- 3 × 0 = 0
- 5 × 0 = 0
- 0 × 4 = 0
- 100 × 0 = 0

"What do you notice?" (Multiplying by 0 always gives 0.)

"This is the ZERO PROPERTY. If you have no groups, or groups with nothing in them, the total is always zero."

"Think of it this way: 5 bags with 0 apples in each is still 0 apples!"

**Step 2 — Quick contrast (2 minutes)**

Write:
- 6 × 1 = 6 (multiplying by 1 gives the number)
- 6 × 0 = 0 (multiplying by 0 gives zero)
- 6 + 1 = 7 (don't confuse × 1 with + 1!)
- 6 + 0 = 6 (don't confuse × 0 with + 0!)

"Be careful: adding 0 and multiplying by 0 are VERY different!"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 9 × 1 = 9 (1 group of 9, or 9 groups of 1 — either way, 9)

**Example 2:** 0 × 7 = 0 (0 groups of 7 — no groups at all — 0)

**Example 3:** 1 × 1 = 1 (1 group of 1. Just 1.)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Problem 1:** "What is 4 × 0?" (0) "Why?" (4 groups with nothing in them.)

**Problem 2:** "What is 1 × 9?" (9) "Why?" (1 group of 9 is just 9.)

**Problem 3:** "What is 0 × 0?" (0) "Why?" (0 groups of 0. Nothing times nothing is nothing.)

**Problem 4:** "What is 1,000 × 1?" (1,000) "You don't even need to calculate — 1 doesn't change anything!"

**Problem 5:** "What is 1,000,000 × 0?" (0) "Even a million times zero is still zero!"

**Problem 6 (comparison):** "Which is bigger: 8 × 0 or 8 × 1?"
8 × 0 = 0, 8 × 1 = 8. So 8 × 1 is bigger. ✓

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. 8 × 0 = ___  (0)
2. 1 × 5 = ___  (5)
3. "Why does any number times 0 equal 0?" (Because if there are 0 in each group, or 0 groups, there's nothing to count.)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student confuses ×0 with +0: write both side by side and use cups to demonstrate. "Adding 0 means nothing extra. Multiplying by 0 means 0 in every group."
- If the student says "6 × 1 = 1": they may be confusing ×1 with ×0. Say: "1 group of 6 — put 6 in 1 cup. How many total? 6."

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (Multiplying by 2). Preview: "Now you know what multiplication means and the special rules for 0 and 1. Starting tomorrow, we build fluency — first with the 2s!"

## Extension (Optional)
**Zero and One Debate:** "Is 0 × 5 the same as 5 × 0? Prove it." (Yes — commutative property. Both = 0.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**These properties seem obvious but aren't.** Many students confuse ×1 with +1, or ×0 with +0. The physical model (cups with counters) makes the distinction concrete.

**"Multiplying by 0 gives 0" is not intuitive to all children.** Some think "If I multiply, the number gets bigger." Zero breaks that expectation. The cups model helps: "Your bags are empty. No matter how many empty bags you have, you still have nothing."

**Challenge Problem 10 is subtle.** If n × 0 = 0, then n could be anything. This is a preview of algebraic thinking — the student doesn't need to formalize it, but it's a wonderful observation if they make it.
