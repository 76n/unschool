# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 1, Lesson 2

## Topic
Repeated Addition

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Express equal groups as a repeated addition sentence (3 groups of 4 = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12)
- Use skip-counting as a strategy for repeated addition
- Show repeated addition on a number line
- Solve equal-groups problems using repeated addition

## Prerequisites
- Equal groups (Lesson 1)
- Addition within 100 (Grade 2)
- Skip-counting by 2s, 5s, and 10s

## Materials Needed
- Counters and cups (from Lesson 1)
- Number line 0-50
- Paper and pencil

**Standards:** 3.OA.1

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review equal groups and activate skip-counting.

1. "What does '4 groups of 3' mean?" (4 groups, each with 3 objects.)
2. "Count by 5s to 30." (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30.)
3. "Count by 2s to 16." (2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16.)
4. "I have 3 groups of 4. If I count all the objects, what addition problem am I solving?" (4 + 4 + 4.)

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect equal groups to repeated addition and skip-counting.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Repeated addition:** Adding the same number over and over (4 + 4 + 4)
- **Skip-counting:** Counting by a number (counting by 4s: 4, 8, 12...)
- **Number line:** A line with numbers marked on it, used to show jumps

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — From groups to addition (5 minutes)**

Place 3 cups with 4 counters each.

"3 groups of 4. Let's count the total by adding."

Write: 4 + 4 + 4 = ?

"I'm adding 4 three times because there are 3 groups and each group has 4."

4 + 4 = 8, then 8 + 4 = 12.

"3 groups of 4 = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12."

**Step 2 — Skip-counting connection (3 minutes)**

"There's an even faster way. Instead of adding 4 + 4 + 4, I can count by 4s: 4... 8... 12. I skip-count 3 times because there are 3 groups."

"Skip-counting IS repeated addition — you're just doing the adding in your head!"

**Step 3 — Number line model (5 minutes)**

Draw a number line from 0 to 15. Show 3 jumps of 4:

"Start at 0. Jump 4: land on 4. Jump 4 again: land on 8. Jump 4 again: land on 12."

Draw arcs from 0→4, 4→8, 8→12. Label each arc "+4."

"3 jumps of 4. I land on 12. So 3 groups of 4 = 12."

Do a second example: 5 groups of 2.

Number line: 5 jumps of 2: 0→2→4→6→8→10.
Repeated addition: 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10.
Skip-count by 2s: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10.

"All three methods give 10."

**Step 4 — Writing the repeated addition sentence (2 minutes)**

Practice the pattern:
- 4 groups of 5 = 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20
- 2 groups of 7 = 7 + 7 = 14
- 6 groups of 3 = 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 18

"How many times do I write the number? The same as the number of groups."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 4 groups of 5
- Repeated addition: 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20
- Number line: 4 jumps of 5 from 0 → 5 → 10 → 15 → 20
- Skip-count: 5, 10, 15, 20

**Example 2:** 3 groups of 6
- Repeated addition: 6 + 6 + 6 = 18
- Skip-count: 6, 12, 18

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Problem 1:** "5 groups of 3. Write the repeated addition."
3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 15. ✓
"Show it on the number line." (5 jumps of 3.)

**Problem 2:** "I see the addition 7 + 7 + 7 + 7. How many groups? How many in each?"
4 groups of 7. Total: 28. ✓

**Problem 3:** "Skip-count by 5 four times. What's the total?"
5, 10, 15, 20. "That's 4 groups of 5 = 20." ✓

**Problem 4:** "2 groups of 8. Write the repeated addition and solve."
8 + 8 = 16. ✓

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

**★ On-level:**
1. Write the repeated addition: 3 groups of 2. (2 + 2 + 2 = 6)
2. Write the repeated addition: 4 groups of 10. (10 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 40)
3. Draw a number line showing 5 groups of 2. What is the total? (10)
4. 6 + 6 + 6 = ___ groups of ___ = ___ (3 groups of 6 = 18)

**★★ Stretch:**
5. "I count by 3s and say: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15. How many groups of 3 is that?" (5 groups of 3 = 15)
6. "Which is more: 4 groups of 5 or 5 groups of 4? How do you know?" (Both equal 20 — the same!)

**★★★ Challenge:**
7. "A caterpillar has 6 groups of 3 legs. How many legs does a caterpillar have?" (18. Real caterpillars have different numbers, but this is the math!)
8. "I have 24 objects. I want to put them in equal groups of 4. How many groups will I have?" (6 — count by 4s to 24.)

### Interleaved Review
9. 56 + 27 = ___
10. 81 − 35 = ___

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. Write the repeated addition for 4 groups of 3 and solve. (3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12)
2. 5 + 5 + 5 = ___ groups of ___ = ___ (3 groups of 5 = 15)
3. Show 3 groups of 5 on a number line. (3 jumps of 5: 0 → 5 → 10 → 15)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to cups and counters. Have the student make the groups, then write the addition sentence while looking at the groups.
- If skip-counting is weak, practice counting by 2s, 3s, 5s with a number line before connecting to groups.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (The Multiplication Sign). Preview: "Writing 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 is a lot of writing. Tomorrow we'll learn a shorter way!"

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Skip-Count Challenge:** "How high can you count by 4s?" Start at 4 and go as far as possible. Then connect: "You counted by 4s seven times and got to 28. That's 7 groups of 4 = 28."

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Repeated addition is the bridge.** Lesson 1 established equal groups. This lesson connects groups to addition — a familiar operation. Lesson 3 will replace the repeated addition with the × sign. The progression is: groups → addition → multiplication.

**Three representations, one idea.** Equal groups, repeated addition, and number-line jumps are all the same thing shown differently. Help the student see the connections between them.

**Skip-counting is a strategy, not a requirement.** Some students find skip-counting intuitive; others prefer writing out the addition. Both paths lead to multiplication understanding.
