# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 1, Lesson 4

## Topic
Arrays

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Build and draw an array (objects arranged in equal rows and columns)
- Describe an array using rows and columns (e.g., "3 rows of 4")
- Write a multiplication equation for an array
- Count the total in an array using multiplication or repeated addition

## Prerequisites
- Equal groups and repeated addition (Lessons 1-2)
- The × sign (Lesson 3)

## Materials Needed
- Counters (at least 40)
- Grid paper (or plain paper with dot arrays)
- Paper and pencil

**Standards:** 3.OA.1, 3.OA.3

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

1. "What is 3 × 5?" (15) *(Lesson 3)*
2. "What does 4 × 6 mean?" (4 groups of 6) *(Lesson 3)*
3. "Look at this arrangement of 12 eggs in a carton. How are they organized?" (In rows — 2 rows of 6, or 6 columns of 2.)

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Array:** Objects arranged in equal rows and columns
- **Row:** A horizontal line of objects (goes left to right, like reading)
- **Column:** A vertical line of objects (goes up and down)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Build an array (5 minutes)**

Place 12 counters in a 3 × 4 arrangement:
```
● ● ● ●
● ● ● ●
● ● ● ●
```

"This is an ARRAY. Objects are arranged in rows and columns. How many rows?" (3.) "How many in each row?" (4.)

"So this is 3 rows of 4. What multiplication equation fits?" (3 × 4 = 12.)

"Let's check: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. ✓"

**Step 2 — Rows vs. columns (3 minutes)**

Point to the same array. "Rows go across (like lines in a book). I see 3 rows."

"Now look at the columns — they go up and down. How many columns?" (4.) "How many in each column?" (3.)

"I could also say: 4 columns of 3. That's 4 × 3 = 12. Same total!"

**Step 3 — Drawing arrays on grid paper (5 minutes)**

Show how to draw arrays on grid paper: color in a 2 × 5 rectangle.

```
● ● ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ●
```

"2 rows of 5 = 2 × 5 = 10."

Have the student draw a 4 × 3 array:
```
● ● ●
● ● ●
● ● ●
● ● ●
```

"4 rows of 3 = 4 × 3 = 12."

**Step 4 — Arrays vs. groups (2 minutes)**

"An array is a special kind of equal groups. Instead of putting objects in cups, we line them up in neat rows and columns. It makes it easy to see how many groups and how many in each group."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** A 5 × 2 array
```
● ●
● ●
● ●
● ●
● ●
```
5 rows of 2 = 5 × 2 = 10

**Example 2:** A 3 × 6 array
```
● ● ● ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ● ●
```
3 rows of 6 = 3 × 6 = 18

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Problem 1:** Student builds a 4 × 5 array with counters.
"How many rows?" (4.) "How many in each row?" (5.)
"Equation?" (4 × 5 = 20.) ✓

**Problem 2:** Draw an array for 2 × 8 on grid paper.
2 rows of 8 = 16. ✓

**Problem 3:** Look at this array:
```
● ● ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ●
```
"How many rows? Columns? Write two multiplication equations."
3 × 5 = 15 (rows × columns) and 5 × 3 = 15 (columns × rows). ✓

**Problem 4:** "I have 20 counters. Can you make an array with 4 rows?"
4 rows of 5 = 20. Student arranges a 4 × 5 array. ✓

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

**★ On-level:**
1. Draw a 3 × 4 array. Write the multiplication equation. (3 × 4 = 12)
2. Draw a 5 × 3 array. How many total? (15)
3. Look at this array and write the equation:
   ```
   ● ● ● ● ● ●
   ● ● ● ● ● ●
   ```
   (2 × 6 = 12)

**★★ Stretch:**
4. "Can you draw two different arrays that both have a total of 12?" (3×4, 4×3, 2×6, 6×2, 1×12, 12×1)
5. "A classroom has 5 rows of desks with 6 desks in each row. Write a multiplication equation and find the total." (5 × 6 = 30)

**★★★ Challenge:**
6. "I have a 4 × 4 array. How many objects? What's special about this array?" (16. It's a square — same number of rows and columns.)
7. "Can you make a rectangular array with 7 counters?" (Only 1 × 7 or 7 × 1 — 7 is prime, though we don't use that word yet.)

### Interleaved Review
8. 3 × 2 = ___
9. Write 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 as multiplication: ___ × ___ = ___

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. Draw a 3 × 5 array and write the multiplication equation. (3 × 5 = 15)
2. How many rows and columns in this array?
   ```
   ● ● ● ●
   ● ● ● ●
   ● ● ● ●
   ● ● ● ●
   ● ● ● ●
   ```
   (5 rows, 4 columns. 5 × 4 = 20.)
3. "What is an array?" (Objects arranged in equal rows and columns.)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Build arrays physically with counters before drawing them.
- Use grid paper with larger squares so the student can place one counter per square.
- If rows/columns are confused: "Rows go across like reading. Columns go up and down like a column in a building."

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 5 (The Commutative Property). Preview: "You noticed that a 3×4 and a 4×3 array have the same total. Tomorrow we'll explore why — and prove it always works!"

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Array Hunt:** Find arrays in real life — window panes, tiles on a floor, muffin tins, ice cube trays. Describe each as ___ rows of ___ and write the multiplication equation.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Arrays are the visual model for multiplication.** They will be used throughout the year — for the commutative property (Lesson 5), for area (later unit), and for understanding division. Invest time here.

**The 3×4 array read both ways is the key discovery.** A 3×4 array has 3 rows of 4 AND 4 columns of 3. This naturally leads to the commutative property in Lesson 5.

**Grid paper is your friend.** Drawing neat arrays freehand is hard. Grid paper keeps things lined up and makes counting easier.
