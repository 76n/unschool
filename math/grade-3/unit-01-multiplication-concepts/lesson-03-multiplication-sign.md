# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 1, Lesson 3

## Topic
The Multiplication Sign (×)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read and write multiplication expressions using the × sign
- Translate between "groups of" language and multiplication notation (3 groups of 4 → 3 × 4)
- Translate between repeated addition and multiplication (4 + 4 + 4 = 3 × 4)
- Solve basic multiplication facts by connecting to equal groups or repeated addition

## Prerequisites
- Equal groups (Lesson 1)
- Repeated addition (Lesson 2)

## Materials Needed
- Counters and cups
- Paper and pencil
- Number line (optional)

**Standards:** 3.OA.1, 3.OA.7

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

1. "Write the repeated addition for 4 groups of 3." (3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12) *(Lesson 2)*
2. "Skip-count by 5 three times." (5, 10, 15) *(Lesson 2)*
3. "6 + 6 = ___ groups of ___ = ___" (2 groups of 6 = 12) *(Lesson 2)*

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Multiply:** A short way of doing repeated addition; finding the total in equal groups
- **Times (×):** The multiplication sign; means "groups of"
- **Factor:** A number being multiplied (in 3 × 4, both 3 and 4 are factors)
- **Product:** The answer to a multiplication problem (3 × 4 = 12; 12 is the product)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce the × sign (5 minutes)**

Place 3 cups with 4 counters each.

"We said: 3 groups of 4 = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. That's a lot of writing! Mathematicians invented a shortcut."

Write: **3 × 4 = 12**

"This is multiplication. The × sign means 'groups of.' 3 × 4 means '3 groups of 4.' We read it as '3 times 4 equals 12.'"

"Instead of writing 4 + 4 + 4, we write 3 × 4. Same answer, shorter notation."

**Step 2 — Practice reading multiplication (3 minutes)**

Write several expressions and have the student read them:
- 2 × 5 → "2 times 5" or "2 groups of 5"
- 4 × 3 → "4 times 3" or "4 groups of 3"
- 6 × 2 → "6 times 2" or "6 groups of 2"

For each, ask: "How many groups? How many in each group?"

**Step 3 — Translate in all three forms (5 minutes)**

Show the three-way connection:

| Groups Language | Repeated Addition | Multiplication |
|---|---|---|
| 3 groups of 4 | 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 | 3 × 4 = 12 |
| 5 groups of 2 | 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10 | 5 × 2 = 10 |
| 2 groups of 7 | 7 + 7 = 14 | 2 × 7 = 14 |

"All three columns say the same thing in different ways. Multiplication is just the shortest way."

**Step 4 — Vocabulary: factor and product (2 minutes)**

Write: 3 × 4 = 12

"The numbers we multiply are called FACTORS: 3 and 4 are factors."
"The answer is called the PRODUCT: 12 is the product."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Write a multiplication equation for 5 groups of 3.
- 5 × 3 = 15
- Check: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 15 ✓

**Example 2:** What does 4 × 6 mean?
- "4 groups of 6"
- 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 = 24
- 4 × 6 = 24

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Problem 1:** "Write a multiplication equation for: 6 groups of 5."
6 × 5 = 30. ✓

**Problem 2:** "What does 3 × 7 mean? Solve it."
3 groups of 7 = 7 + 7 + 7 = 21. 3 × 7 = 21. ✓

**Problem 3:** "I see 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2. Write this as multiplication."
6 × 2 = 12. ✓

**Problem 4:** "Draw a picture for 4 × 3 and find the product."
Student draws 4 circles with 3 dots each. Product: 12. ✓

**Problem 5:** "What are the factors in 5 × 8 = 40? What is the product?"
Factors: 5 and 8. Product: 40. ✓

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

**★ On-level:**
1. 2 × 4 = ___
2. 5 × 3 = ___
3. 3 × 6 = ___
4. Write as multiplication: 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = ___ × ___ = ___

**★★ Stretch:**
5. "What are the factors in 7 × 3 = 21?" (7 and 3)
6. "A pack of gum has 5 pieces. You have 4 packs. Write a multiplication equation." (4 × 5 = 20)
7. "True or false: 3 × 4 means 3 + 4." (False — 3 × 4 means 3 groups of 4 = 12, not 3 + 4 = 7.)

**★★★ Challenge:**
8. "I multiplied two numbers and got 18. The first number is 3. What is the second number?" (6, because 3 × 6 = 18.)
9. "Which is bigger: 5 × 3 or 3 + 5? How much bigger?" (5 × 3 = 15, 3 + 5 = 8. 15 is 7 more.)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. 4 × 5 = ___
2. Write as multiplication: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = ___ × ___ = ___
3. "What does 6 × 2 mean in words?" (6 groups of 2, or 6 times 2.)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student adds instead of multiplying (e.g., 4 × 5 = 9), return to groups and repeated addition. Build with objects every time.
- If the student can solve but can't read/write × notation, practice translating 5 problems between groups language and multiplication notation.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Arrays). Preview: "There's another way to show multiplication — arranging objects in rows and columns!"

## Extension (Optional)
**Multiplication Matchup:** Write 6 multiplication expressions and 6 repeated addition expressions on separate cards. Student matches each multiplication to its repeated addition partner.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The × sign is just notation.** The student already understands the concept (equal groups → repeated addition). This lesson adds the symbolic shortcut. If the student can translate fluently between all three forms, they truly understand what multiplication means.

**"Times" and "groups of" are interchangeable.** Use both freely: "3 times 4" and "3 groups of 4." The student should be comfortable with both phrasings.

**Don't rush to memorized facts.** At this point, it's fine for the student to solve 4 × 3 by thinking "3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12." Fluency with facts comes later (Lessons 7-9 and Unit 2). Understanding comes first.
