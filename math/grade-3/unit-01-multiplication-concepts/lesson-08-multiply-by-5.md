# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 1, Lesson 8

## Topic
Multiplying by 5

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Fluently multiply any single-digit number by 5
- Connect ×5 to skip-counting by 5s
- Recognize the pattern: products of 5 end in 0 or 5
- Connect ×5 to telling time (5 minutes per number on a clock)

## Prerequisites
- Multiplication notation (Lesson 3)
- Skip-counting by 5s (Grade 2)
- Multiplying by 2 (Lesson 7)

## Materials Needed
- Counters (optional)
- Number line 0-50
- Paper and pencil
- Analog clock (optional, for extension)

**Standards:** 3.OA.7

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

1. "Count by 5s to 50." (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50)
2. "What is 2 × 8?" (16) *(Lesson 7)*
3. "What is 2 × 5?" (10) *(Lesson 7)*
4. "How many fingers do 3 people have altogether?" (15 — each person has 5 fingers on one hand.)

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Skip-counting IS ×5 (4 minutes)**

"You already know the ×5 facts — you just don't know you know them!"

Count by 5s: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50.

"The 1st number is 1 × 5. The 2nd is 2 × 5. The 3rd is 3 × 5..."

| Count | Skip-count value | Multiplication |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 5 | 1 × 5 = 5 |
| 2nd | 10 | 2 × 5 = 10 |
| 3rd | 15 | 3 × 5 = 15 |
| 4th | 20 | 4 × 5 = 20 |
| 5th | 25 | 5 × 5 = 25 |
| 6th | 30 | 6 × 5 = 30 |
| 7th | 35 | 7 × 5 = 35 |
| 8th | 40 | 8 × 5 = 40 |
| 9th | 45 | 9 × 5 = 45 |
| 10th | 50 | 10 × 5 = 50 |

**Step 2 — The 0-or-5 pattern (3 minutes)**

"Look at the ones digit of every product: 5, 0, 5, 0, 5, 0, 5, 0, 5, 0."

"Products of 5 ALWAYS end in 0 or 5. If your answer ends in a different digit, check your work!"

- Odd number × 5 ends in 5 (1×5=5, 3×5=15, 5×5=25, 7×5=35, 9×5=45)
- Even number × 5 ends in 0 (2×5=10, 4×5=20, 6×5=30, 8×5=40, 10×5=50)

**Step 3 — The "half of 10" strategy (3 minutes)**

"Here's a trick: 5 is half of 10. So you can find ×5 by doing ×10 and then taking half."

- 6 × 5: Think 6 × 10 = 60. Half of 60 = 30. So 6 × 5 = 30.
- 8 × 5: Think 8 × 10 = 80. Half of 80 = 40. So 8 × 5 = 40.

"This is an advanced strategy. Use whichever method works best for you — skip-counting, the table, or the half-of-ten trick."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 7 × 5
- Skip-count: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35
- Answer: 35

**Example 2:** 5 × 4
- Commutative: same as 4 × 5 = 20
- Check: 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20 ✓

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Problem 1:** "3 × 5 = ?" Count by 5s three times: 5, 10, 15. ✓
**Problem 2:** "5 × 6 = ?" 6 × 5 = 30. ✓
**Problem 3:** "5 × 9 = ?" Count by 5s nine times: 45. ✓
**Problem 4:** "8 × 5 = ?" Half of 8 × 10 = half of 80 = 40. ✓
**Problem 5:** "A nickel is worth 5 cents. How much are 7 nickels worth?" 7 × 5 = 35 cents. ✓

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. 5 × 6 = ___  (30)
2. 5 × 8 = ___  (40)
3. 7 × 5 = ___  (35)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct. Target: ≤5 seconds per problem.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If skip-counting by 5s is not fluent, practice that first — it must be automatic.
- Use a number line with jumps of 5 to visualize.
- Use nickels: "If I have 6 nickels, how much money?" makes ×5 tangible.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 9 (Multiplying by 10). Preview: "The 10s are the easiest multiplication facts of all!"

## Extension (Optional)
**Clock Connection:** Look at an analog clock. "The 4 means 20 minutes because 4 × 5 = 20. What does the 7 mean?" (35 minutes.) Go around the clock and practice all ×5 facts.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Skip-counting by 5s is the foundation.** If the student can fluently count 5, 10, 15, 20... up to 50, the ×5 facts are essentially free. The lesson just formalizes the connection.

**The 0-or-5 pattern is a built-in error check.** Teach the student to look at their answer: "I got 5 × 7 = 33. Does 33 end in 0 or 5? No — let me recount."

**The "half of ten" strategy is optional but powerful.** It previews the relationship between ×5 and ×10 and introduces halving as a mental math strategy. Don't push it if the student isn't ready.
