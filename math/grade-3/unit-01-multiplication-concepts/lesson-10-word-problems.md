# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 1, Lesson 10

## Topic
Multiplication Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify word problems that require multiplication (equal groups situations)
- Write a multiplication equation to match a word problem
- Solve one-step multiplication word problems using known facts (×0, ×1, ×2, ×5, ×10)
- Distinguish multiplication problems from addition problems
- Draw a model (groups or array) to represent a word problem

## Prerequisites
- All Lessons 1-9 (multiplication concepts and facts for 0, 1, 2, 5, 10)

## Materials Needed
- Paper and pencil
- Counters (available for support)

**Standards:** 3.OA.3

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Equal groups problem:** A word problem where items are arranged in groups of the same size
- **Model:** A picture or drawing that shows the math in a problem

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Recognizing multiplication situations (5 minutes)**

"Multiplication is about EQUAL GROUPS. When a word problem describes equal groups, you multiply."

Read aloud: "There are 5 bags. Each bag has 10 marbles. How many marbles are there altogether?"

"Do I see equal groups?" (Yes — 5 bags, each with 10.)
"How many groups?" (5.) "How many in each?" (10.)
"Equation: 5 × 10 = 50 marbles."

Read aloud: "Sam has 7 red crayons and 5 blue crayons. How many crayons does Sam have?"

"Equal groups?" (No — these are two different amounts being combined.)
"This is ADDITION: 7 + 5 = 12."

"The key question: Are there equal groups? If yes → multiply. If things are being combined or compared → add or subtract."

**Step 2 — The modeling strategy (5 minutes)**

Read: "A spider has 8 legs. How many legs do 2 spiders have?"

"Let me draw a model." Draw 2 circles labeled "spider," each with 8 inside.

"2 groups of 8 = 2 × 8 = 16 legs."

Read: "Each table at a restaurant seats 5 people. There are 6 tables. How many people can sit down?"

Draw 6 rectangles labeled "table," each with 5 inside.

"6 groups of 5 = 6 × 5 = 30 people."

**Step 3 — Writing the equation (5 minutes)**

Practice the full process: Read → Model → Equation → Solve → Check

Read: "A farmer has 10 rows of apple trees. Each row has 5 trees. How many apple trees are there?"

Model: 10 groups of 5 (or a 10 × 5 array).
Equation: 10 × 5 = 50.
Check: "Does 50 trees make sense for 10 rows of 5? Yes."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "There are 3 fish tanks. Each tank has 5 fish. How many fish?"
- Model: 3 circles with 5 in each
- Equation: 3 × 5 = 15
- Answer: 15 fish

**Example 2:** "A pack of stickers has 10 stickers. You buy 2 packs. How many stickers?"
- Model: 2 rectangles with 10 in each
- Equation: 2 × 10 = 20
- Answer: 20 stickers

**Example 3:** "Each hand has 5 fingers. How many fingers on 4 hands?"
- Equation: 4 × 5 = 20
- Answer: 20 fingers

## Guided Practice (10-15 minutes)

**Problem 1:** "A bookshelf has 5 shelves. Each shelf holds 10 books. How many books?"
Student draws model and writes: 5 × 10 = 50 books. ✓

**Problem 2:** "You have 2 boxes of donuts. Each box has 6 donuts. How many donuts?"
2 × 6 = 12 donuts. ✓

**Problem 3:** "There are 8 teams. Each team has 2 players. How many players?"
8 × 2 = 16 players. ✓

**Problem 4 (addition vs. multiplication):** "Is this multiplication or addition? 'There are 12 red flowers and 8 yellow flowers. How many flowers?'"
Addition: 12 + 8 = 20. (No equal groups — just combining two amounts.) ✓

**Problem 5:** "Each pencil box holds 5 pencils. Mia has 0 pencil boxes. How many pencils?"
0 × 5 = 0 pencils. ✓

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. "There are 5 vases. Each vase has 2 flowers. How many flowers?"
   - Equation: _______________
   - Answer: ___ flowers
   (5 × 2 = 10 flowers)

2. "A roll of tape costs 10 cents. How much do 3 rolls cost?"
   - Equation: _______________
   - Answer: ___ cents
   (3 × 10 = 30 cents)

3. "How do you know a word problem needs multiplication instead of addition?" (When there are equal groups — the same number in each group — you multiply. When you're combining different amounts, you add.)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student can compute but can't identify when to multiply: practice sorting 6 word problems into "addition" and "multiplication" piles before solving.
- If the student struggles to write the equation: practice the sequence "How many groups? How many in each? ___ × ___ = ___" with 5 more problems.

### If Mastery Is Met
The student is ready for the Unit Assessment.

## Extension (Optional)
**Write Your Own:** Create three multiplication word problems — one using ×2, one using ×5, one using ×10. Solve each and trade with a partner to check.

**Two-Step Challenge:** "A bakery makes 5 trays of cookies. Each tray has 10 cookies. They sell 15 cookies. How many are left?" (5 × 10 = 50, then 50 − 15 = 35.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is a synthesis lesson.** It doesn't introduce new computation — it applies everything to real-world contexts. The challenge is reading comprehension and choosing multiplication vs. addition.

**The model is the bridge.** Drawing circles with numbers inside forces the student to visualize the groups before writing the equation. Don't skip the drawing step, even for students who can compute easily.

**"Equal groups" is the key identifier.** Teach the student to ask: "Does this problem describe equal groups?" If yes → multiply. If different amounts are being combined → add. If something is being taken away or compared → subtract.

**Problem 9 previews division.** If the student says "I need to figure out how many groups," that's division thinking. Celebrate it as a preview but don't formalize it yet.
