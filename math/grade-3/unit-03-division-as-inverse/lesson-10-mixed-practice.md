# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 3, Lesson 10

## Topic
Mixed Practice — Multiplication and Division

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve a mixture of multiplication and division problems
- Choose the correct operation for word problems
- Use fact families to move between multiplication and division
- Demonstrate readiness for the unit assessment

## Prerequisites
- All prior lessons in this unit (Lessons 1-9)

## Materials Needed
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Rapid-fire fact recall.

1. "6 × 8 = ?" (48) "48 ÷ 8 = ?" (6) "48 ÷ 6 = ?" (8)
2. "7 × 9 = ?" (63) "63 ÷ 7 = ?" (9)
3. "32 ÷ 4 = ?" (8) "What multiplication fact helped?" (8 × 4 = 32)

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model choosing between multiplication and division in context.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Multiplication vs. division — when to use each.**

"Multiplication: you know the number of groups AND the group size. Find the total."
"Division: you know the total and one other piece. Find the missing piece."

**Decision framework:**
- Know groups and group size → Multiply to find total.
- Know total and number of groups → Divide to find group size.
- Know total and group size → Divide to find number of groups.

**Step 2: Model mixed problems.**

Problem A: "5 boxes with 7 books in each. How many books?" (Multiplication: 5 × 7 = 35)

Problem B: "35 books split equally into 5 boxes. How many in each?" (Division: 35 ÷ 5 = 7)

Problem C: "35 books, 7 in each box. How many boxes?" (Division: 35 ÷ 7 = 5)

"Same three numbers (5, 7, 35) — three different problems, three different operations!"

**Step 3: Check your answer.**
"For division, multiply to check. For multiplication, you can divide to check. They undo each other."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Mixed problem-solving with facilitator coaching.

**Problem 1:** 9 × 6 = ? (54) — multiplication

**Problem 2:** 54 ÷ 9 = ? (6) — division

**Problem 3:** "A store has 8 shelves with 7 toys on each. How many toys?" (8 × 7 = 56) — multiplication

**Problem 4:** "There are 56 toys shared equally on 8 shelves. How many per shelf?" (56 ÷ 8 = 7) — division

**Problem 5:** "There are 40 chairs and 20 more are delivered. How many chairs now?" (40 + 20 = 60) — addition! Not multiplication or division.

**Problem 6:** Write the fact family for 7, 8, 56. (7×8=56, 8×7=56, 56÷7=8, 56÷8=7)

## Independent Practice (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Comprehensive mixed practice.

**★ On-level — Computation (mixed × and ÷):**
1. 7 × 4 = ___ (28)
2. 28 ÷ 4 = ___ (7)
3. 9 × 8 = ___ (72)
4. 72 ÷ 9 = ___ (8)
5. 6 × 6 = ___ (36)
6. 36 ÷ 6 = ___ (6)
7. 45 ÷ 5 = ___ (9)
8. 8 × 3 = ___ (24)
9. 56 ÷ 7 = ___ (8)
10. 81 ÷ 9 = ___ (9)

**★★ Stretch — Word Problems (choose the operation):**
11. "There are 6 bags with 9 oranges each. How many oranges?" (6 × 9 = 54)
12. "54 oranges split equally into 6 bags. How many per bag?" (54 ÷ 6 = 9)
13. "A runner jogs 5 miles every day for 7 days. How many miles total?" (5 × 7 = 35)
14. "A 35-mile race is divided into 7 equal sections. How long is each section?" (35 ÷ 7 = 5 miles)

**★★★ Challenge:**
15. "Fill in the blanks: ___ × 8 = 64, 64 ÷ ___ = 8, 64 ÷ 8 = ___." (8, 8, 8) "This is a special fact family — why?" (Because both factors are 8; it's a square.)
16. "I multiply a number by 6 and get 42. What was my number?" (7, because 42 ÷ 6 = 7) "Now I divide 42 by that number. What do I get?" (42 ÷ 7 = 6)

### Interleaved Review
- "What is half of 18?" (9) "Write as division." (18 ÷ 2 = 9)
- "3 × 3 × 3 = ?" (27)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Final readiness check before the unit assessment.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 63 ÷ 7 = ___ (9)
2. 8 × 7 = ___ (56)
3. "There are 48 markers shared equally among 6 children. How many does each child get?" (48 ÷ 6 = 8)

**Mastery = All three correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify the specific gap:
  - Computation errors → practice the specific fact family.
  - Operation choice → practice identifying "total being split" (division) vs. "groups being combined" (multiplication).
- Provide a few more targeted problems before the assessment.

### If Mastery Is Met
Proceed to the Unit Assessment.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson is a consolidation day.** No new concepts — just practice switching between multiplication and division. The key skill is flexible thinking: "I know 7 × 8 = 56, so I also know 56 ÷ 7 = 8 and 56 ÷ 8 = 7."

**Include all four operations in discussion.** Students should not assume every problem is × or ÷ just because it's a multiplication/division unit. Throwing in a few addition/subtraction problems keeps critical thinking active.

**Celebrate the fact family insight.** A student who truly understands that knowing one fact gives them three more for free has a powerful mathematical tool. Acknowledge this explicitly: "You don't need to memorize 200 facts — you know about 55 families, and each family gives you 4 facts."
