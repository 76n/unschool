## Section E: Word Problems

Read each problem aloud if needed.

**E1.** "There are 32 apples shared equally among 8 children. How many apples does each child get?"

Answer: 32 ÷ 8 = 4 apples.

**E2.** "A teacher has 54 pencils and puts 9 in each cup. How many cups does she need?"

Answer: 54 ÷ 9 = 6 cups.

**E3.** "There are 7 boxes of crayons with 8 crayons in each box. How many crayons altogether?"

Answer: 7 × 8 = 56 crayons. (This is multiplication, not division.)

**E4.** "A garden has 42 flowers planted in 6 equal rows. How many flowers in each row?"

Answer: 42 ÷ 6 = 7 flowers.

## Rubric and Mastery Determination

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Next Steps |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Mastered** | 17-20 (85-100%) with at least 1 correct in each section | Strong division understanding and fact fluency | Advance to Unit 4: Fractions (Introduction). Continue fact practice through warm-ups. |
| **Approaching** | 14-16 (70-84%) | Most skills present; specific gaps remain | Reteach struggling sections. Extra fact practice before advancing. |
| **Developing** | Below 14 (<70%) | Significant gaps | Return to specific lessons. Verify multiplication fluency (prerequisite) before reteaching division. |

### Section-Specific Reteaching Guide

| If struggling in... | Focus on... |
|---|---|
| Section A (Sharing/Grouping) | Return to Lessons 1-2. Use physical objects: deal into cups (sharing) or count out fixed groups (grouping). The student needs to see division as a physical action before it becomes abstract. |
| Section B (Notation) | Return to Lesson 3. Practice translating between physical models, words, and symbols: "24 shared among 4" → "24 ÷ 4 = 6." Always start with the total (dividend). |
| Section C (Inverse/Fact Families) | Return to Lessons 4-5. Use fact family triangle cards daily. The key insight: "If you know a multiplication fact, you know the related division facts." Practice converting: given 7 × 8 = 56, write 56 ÷ 7 and 56 ÷ 8. |
| Section D (Division Facts) | Return to Lessons 6-8. Identify which specific facts are weak. Almost always, the root cause is weak multiplication recall. Drill the multiplication fact first (e.g., 8 × 6 = 48), THEN derive the division (48 ÷ 6 = 8). Use the multiplication chart to find and practice gaps. |
| Section E (Word Problems) | Return to Lesson 9. If the student picks the wrong operation, practice problem interpretation before solving: "What do you know? What are you finding? Is a total being split up?" Include non-division problems to keep critical thinking sharp. |
