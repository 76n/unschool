# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 3, Lesson 5

## Topic
Fact Families

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Write all four facts in a fact family (2 multiplication + 2 division)
- Explain why a fact family has exactly 3 numbers
- Use fact families to find missing numbers

## Prerequisites
- Division as inverse of multiplication (Lesson 4)
- Multiplication fact fluency (Units 1-2)

## Materials Needed
- Paper and pencil
- Index cards (for making fact family triangles)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review the multiplication-division connection.

1. "7 × 4 = ?" (28) "28 ÷ 4 = ?" (7) "28 ÷ 7 = ?" (4)
2. "I just said three facts using the same three numbers: 7, 4, and 28. Can you say a fourth?" (4 × 7 = 28)
3. "Those four facts are called a fact family!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Define and explore fact families.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Fact family:** A set of related multiplication and division facts using the same three numbers
- **Related facts:** Facts that use the same numbers

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Build a fact family.**
Numbers: 3, 5, 15.
- 3 × 5 = 15
- 5 × 3 = 15
- 15 ÷ 3 = 5
- 15 ÷ 5 = 3

"Four facts, three numbers. That's a fact family."

**Step 2: Make a fact family triangle.**
Draw a triangle. Write 15 at the top, 3 on the bottom left, 5 on the bottom right.
"The product goes at the top. The two factors go at the bottom. You can make all four facts from this triangle."

**Step 3: Special case — squares.**
Numbers: 4, 4, 16.
- 4 × 4 = 16
- 16 ÷ 4 = 4

"When both factors are the same, the family only has 2 unique facts."

**Step 4: Why fact families matter.**
"If you're stuck on a division fact, think of the fact family. If you know ANY ONE of the four facts, you know all four."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Numbers 6, 8, 48.
- 6 × 8 = 48
- 8 × 6 = 48
- 48 ÷ 6 = 8
- 48 ÷ 8 = 6

**Example 2:** Numbers 7, 7, 49.
- 7 × 7 = 49
- 49 ÷ 7 = 7
(Only 2 unique facts.)

**Example 3:** Fill in the missing number: ?, 9, 63.
Think: ? × 9 = 63. Answer: 7. Family: 7 × 9 = 63, 9 × 7 = 63, 63 ÷ 7 = 9, 63 ÷ 9 = 7.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student writes fact families.

**Activity 1: Write all four facts**
- Numbers 4, 6, 24:
  - 4 × 6 = 24, 6 × 4 = 24, 24 ÷ 4 = 6, 24 ÷ 6 = 4.
- Numbers 8, 3, 24:
  - 8 × 3 = 24, 3 × 8 = 24, 24 ÷ 8 = 3, 24 ÷ 3 = 8.

**Activity 2: Find the missing number**
- Fact family: 5, ___, 40. (8, because 5 × 8 = 40)
- Fact family: ___, 6, 54. (9, because 9 × 6 = 54)
- Fact family: 7, 9, ___. (63, because 7 × 9 = 63)

**Activity 3: Make a fact family triangle**
Student makes a triangle card for the numbers 6, 7, 42. Tests themselves: cover 42, figure it out (6 × 7). Cover 6, figure it out (42 ÷ 7). Cover 7, figure it out (42 ÷ 6).

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fact family fluency.

**★ On-level:**
Write all four facts for each set of numbers:
1. 3, 8, 24 (3×8=24, 8×3=24, 24÷3=8, 24÷8=3)
2. 5, 9, 45 (5×9=45, 9×5=45, 45÷5=9, 45÷9=5)
3. 7, 6, 42 (7×6=42, 6×7=42, 42÷7=6, 42÷6=7)

Find the missing number:
4. 4, ___, 32 (8)
5. ___, 7, 56 (8)
6. 9, 3, ___ (27)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. "Write the fact family for 8, 8, 64. How is this different?" (Only 2 unique facts: 8×8=64, 64÷8=8)
8. "If you know 6 × 9 = 54, name ALL the facts you automatically know." (9×6=54, 54÷6=9, 54÷9=6)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "A fact family has the numbers 72, ?, and 9. What's the missing number? Write all four facts." (8; 8×9=72, 9×8=72, 72÷8=9, 72÷9=8)
10. "Can 5, 3, and 20 be a fact family? Why or why not?" (No — 5 × 3 = 15, not 20. The numbers don't work together.)

### Interleaved Review
- "63 ÷ 9 = ___" (7) *(Lesson 4)*
- "8 × 6 = ___" (48) *(Unit 2)*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm fact family understanding.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Write all four facts for: 4, 7, 28. (4×7=28, 7×4=28, 28÷4=7, 28÷7=4)
2. Find the missing number: ___, 6, 48. (8)

**Mastery = Both correct with all four facts written.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Use fact family triangle cards. Practice covering one number and finding it from the other two.
- If the student can write multiplication facts but not division, revisit Lesson 4 (inverse relationship).

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 6 (Dividing by 2 and 5).

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Fact family triangles are a powerful study tool.** Students can make a set and quiz themselves. Cover the top number → multiply the two bottom numbers. Cover a bottom number → divide the top by the remaining bottom number.

**The insight:** Knowing one fact gives you three more for free. This dramatically reduces the memorization load. 100 multiplication facts + 100 division facts sounds overwhelming, but fact families mean you only need to learn about 55 unique sets (since multiplication is commutative).

**Common errors:**
- Writing a wrong product in the family (e.g., 6, 7, 48 instead of 42). Always verify: multiply the two smaller numbers to check the third.
- Forgetting one of the four facts. Use the systematic approach: two multiplications, two divisions.
