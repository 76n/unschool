# Facilitator Guide — Grade 3 Math, Unit 3: Division as Inverse of Multiplication

**Grade:** 3
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 3 — Division as Inverse of Multiplication
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Fully facilitated (adult present and actively guiding)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit introduces division as the reverse of multiplication. Students learn that division answers two kinds of questions: "How many in each group?" (sharing/partitive) and "How many groups?" (grouping/measurement). The ÷ sign is introduced, and students build fluency by connecting division facts directly to the multiplication facts they already know.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Model equal sharing (partitive division) with objects
- Model grouping (measurement division) with objects
- Read and write division sentences using the ÷ sign
- Explain that division is the inverse of multiplication
- Write and use fact families (e.g., 3 × 4 = 12, 4 × 3 = 12, 12 ÷ 3 = 4, 12 ÷ 4 = 3)
- Solve division problems with divisors 1-10 using multiplication facts
- Solve one-step word problems involving division

**Why this matters:** Division completes the four operations. A student who understands the relationship between multiplication and division can use known facts to derive unknown ones, reason about fractions (which are division), and solve proportional problems. The fact-family approach means students don't need to memorize a separate set of division facts — they leverage what they already know about multiplication.

---

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm:

- [ ] Knows all multiplication facts for ×2, ×5, and ×10 fluently (Unit 1 or Grade 2)
- [ ] Knows most multiplication facts for ×3, ×4, ×6, ×7, ×8, ×9 (Unit 2 mastery)
- [ ] Understands multiplication as "groups of" (conceptual understanding)
- [ ] Can represent multiplication with arrays and repeated addition
- [ ] Understands the commutative property: 3 × 4 = 4 × 3

## Materials Needed for This Unit

- **Counting objects:** At least 50 (cubes, beans, buttons)
- **Small cups or plates:** For equal sharing
- **Paper and pencil**
- **Fact family triangle cards** (can make from index cards: write the three numbers of a fact family, one at each corner)
- **Multiplication chart** (10 × 10, for reference)

## Pacing Guidance

| Lesson | Topic | Sessions |
|--------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Equal Sharing (Partitive Division) | 1 |
| 2 | Grouping (Measurement Division) | 1 |
| 3 | The Division Sign (÷) | 1-2 |
| 4 | Division as the Inverse of Multiplication | 1 |
| 5 | Fact Families | 1 |
| 6 | Dividing by 2 and 5 | 1 |
| 7 | Dividing by 3 and 4 | 1 |
| 8 | Dividing by 6, 7, 8, and 9 | 1-2 |
| 9 | Division Word Problems | 1 |
| 10 | Mixed Practice — Multiplication and Division | 1 |
| — | Unit Assessment | Separate session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lesson 3 (÷ sign) may need 2 sessions if the student needs time connecting physical models to notation.
- Lesson 8 (dividing by 6-9) may need 2 sessions because these facts are less familiar.
- If the student's multiplication fact fluency is weak (especially ×6-×9), pause and reinforce Unit 2 facts before proceeding with Lessons 7-8.

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Student confuses ÷ with − | Both "take away," but they're different operations | Build: "12 − 3 means start with 12, take away 3, you have 9. But 12 ÷ 3 means split 12 into 3 equal groups — you get 4 in each group." |
| Student says 12 ÷ 3 = 3 | Sees the 3 in the problem and repeats it | Build with objects: put 12 items into 3 groups. "How many in each group?" Count: 4. Connecting to multiplication: "What × 3 = 12?" |
| Student cannot connect division to multiplication | Sees them as separate operations | Use fact families explicitly: "If you know 4 × 3 = 12, you automatically know 12 ÷ 3 = 4 and 12 ÷ 4 = 3." |
| Student divides in the wrong direction: thinks 3 ÷ 12 = 4 | Confuses dividend and divisor | "3 ÷ 12 asks: can you split 3 things into 12 groups? No! 12 ÷ 3 asks: split 12 into 3 groups. The bigger number comes first." |
| Student doesn't know what to do with remainders | This unit doesn't cover remainders, but the question may arise | "Sometimes things don't divide evenly. For now, we're working with numbers that divide evenly. You'll learn about remainders soon." |

## Conversation Starters

- "12 cookies shared equally among 3 kids — how many does each kid get?"
- "I have 20 apples and I put 5 in each bag. How many bags do I need?"
- "If you know 6 × 7 = 42, what division facts do you know?"
- "Is division just multiplication backward? How?"
- "What does the ÷ sign mean?"

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if:

- The student cannot connect division to multiplication after multiple lessons with physical models
- Multiplication fact recall is so slow that division practice is frustrating (go back to Unit 2)
- The student consistently confuses the two division models (sharing vs. grouping) after Lessons 1-2
- The student shows persistent difficulty understanding inverse operations

---

## What Comes Next

After this unit, students move to **Unit 4: Fractions (Introduction)**, where they learn that fractions represent division (1/2 = 1 ÷ 2). The division understanding from this unit is essential groundwork for fraction concepts.
