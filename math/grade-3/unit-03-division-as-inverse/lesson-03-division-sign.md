# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 3, Lesson 3

## Topic
The Division Sign (÷)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read and write the ÷ sign
- Write division sentences for sharing and grouping problems (e.g., 12 ÷ 3 = 4)
- Identify the dividend, divisor, and quotient

## Prerequisites
- Equal sharing and grouping (Lessons 1-2)
- Multiplication notation (Grade 2/3)

## Materials Needed
- Counting objects (for reference)
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review sharing and grouping, set up the need for notation.

1. "15 shared among 3 = ?" (5) "20 in groups of 4 = ?" (5)
2. "In multiplication, we write 3 × 5 = 15. What if we want to write the sharing problem with a symbol? 15 ... something ... 3 = 5. What goes in the middle?"
3. "Today you learn the division sign!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce ÷ and the structure of a division sentence.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Division sign (÷):** The symbol for division; read as "divided by"
- **Dividend:** The total being divided (the number before ÷)
- **Divisor:** The number you divide by (the number after ÷)
- **Quotient:** The answer to a division problem

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the symbol.**
Write: 12 ÷ 3 = 4.
"This is a division sentence. We read it as 'twelve divided by three equals four.'"

**Step 2: Name the parts.**
"12 is the **dividend** — the total we're dividing. 3 is the **divisor** — what we divide by. 4 is the **quotient** — the answer."

dividend ÷ divisor = quotient

**Step 3: Connect to sharing and grouping.**
"12 ÷ 3 = 4 means EITHER:
- 12 shared among 3 groups = 4 in each, OR
- 12 in groups of 3 = 4 groups.
Both give us 12 ÷ 3 = 4."

**Step 4: Write division sentences from models.**
- 20 objects, 4 groups, 5 in each: 20 ÷ 4 = 5.
- 20 objects, groups of 5, 4 groups: 20 ÷ 5 = 4.
- "Notice: the dividend (total) always comes first."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 18 shared among 6 groups = 3. Division sentence: 18 ÷ 6 = 3.

**Example 2:** 24 in groups of 8 = 3 groups. Division sentence: 24 ÷ 8 = 3.

**Example 3:** 35 ÷ 5 = ? "35 divided by 5. What × 5 = 35?" (7) So 35 ÷ 5 = 7.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student writes and solves division sentences.

**Activity 1: Write the sentence**
- 15 shared among 5 = 3. Write: ___ ÷ ___ = ___ (15 ÷ 5 = 3)
- 28 in groups of 7 = 4. Write: ___ ÷ ___ = ___ (28 ÷ 7 = 4)
- 30 objects, 6 groups: 30 ÷ 6 = ___ (5)

**Activity 2: Solve division sentences**
- 21 ÷ 3 = ? (7)
- 40 ÷ 8 = ? (5)
- 45 ÷ 9 = ? (5)

**Activity 3: Label the parts**
"In 36 ÷ 6 = 6, what is the dividend?" (36) "Divisor?" (6) "Quotient?" (6)

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency with division notation.

**★ On-level:**
1. 12 ÷ 4 = ___ (3)
2. 20 ÷ 5 = ___ (4)
3. 18 ÷ 2 = ___ (9)
4. 30 ÷ 10 = ___ (3)
5. Write the division sentence: 24 shared among 3 = 8. (24 ÷ 3 = 8)
6. Write the division sentence: 42 in groups of 6 = 7. (42 ÷ 6 = 7)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. "In the sentence 56 ÷ 7 = 8, name the dividend, divisor, and quotient." (Dividend: 56, Divisor: 7, Quotient: 8)
8. Fill in: ___ ÷ 4 = 9 (36)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "Is 0 ÷ 5 possible? What does it mean?" (Yes — 0 objects shared among 5 groups = 0 in each group. 0 ÷ 5 = 0.)
10. "Is 5 ÷ 0 possible? Why or why not?" (No — you can't divide by zero. You can't split things into 0 groups.)

### Interleaved Review
- "7 × 8 = ___" (56) *(Unit 2)*
- "24 objects, groups of 4 = ___ groups" (6) *(Lesson 2)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm division notation.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 32 ÷ 8 = ___ (4)
2. Write a division sentence for: 18 objects shared among 6 groups = 3 in each. (18 ÷ 6 = 3)

**Mastery = Both correct with proper ÷ notation.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Pair every division sentence with a physical model: build it, then write it.
- If the student puts numbers in the wrong order, emphasize: "The total comes FIRST. ÷ goes between the total and the divisor."

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Division as the Inverse of Multiplication).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The ÷ sign is just notation for what they already can do.** After Lessons 1-2, students already understand sharing and grouping. This lesson just gives them the symbol. Keep it connected to meaning — don't let it become abstract symbol manipulation.

**Vocabulary is a tool, not a test.** "Dividend," "divisor," and "quotient" are useful labels. Introduce them but don't require memorization. What matters is that the student knows what each number in a division sentence represents.

**Common errors:**
- Writing the dividend and divisor in the wrong order: 3 ÷ 12 instead of 12 ÷ 3. "The total being divided always comes first."
- Confusing ÷ with −. Reinforce: "÷ means splitting into equal groups. − means taking some away."
