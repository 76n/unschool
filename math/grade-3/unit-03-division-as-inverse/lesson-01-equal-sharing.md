# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 3, Lesson 1

## Topic
Equal Sharing (Partitive Division)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Share a collection of objects equally among a given number of groups
- Determine how many are in each group after equal sharing
- Recognize equal sharing as a type of division

## Prerequisites
- Multiplication fact fluency (Units 1-2)
- Equal groups concept (Grade 2 or Unit 1)

## Materials Needed
- 40+ counting objects (cubes, beans, buttons)
- Small cups or plates (for groups)
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate multiplication as a springboard to division.

1. "3 × 5 = ?" (15) "4 × 6 = ?" (24) "7 × 3 = ?" (21)
2. "If I have 3 groups of 5, that's 15. But what if I STARTED with 15 and wanted to make 3 equal groups? How many would be in each group?"
3. Let the student think. If they say "5," great — that's the intuition we're building.

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce equal sharing (partitive division) through physical models.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Share equally:** Give the same amount to each group or person
- **Divide:** Split a total into equal parts
- **Partitive division:** Division where you know the number of groups and find how many in each group

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Act out sharing.**
Place 12 objects on the table. Set out 3 cups. "We have 12 objects and 3 cups. Let's share them equally."

Deal objects one at a time into cups (like dealing cards): 1 to cup A, 1 to cup B, 1 to cup C, then repeat. Continue until all 12 are distributed.

"How many in each cup?" (4) "We shared 12 equally among 3 groups. Each group got 4."

**Step 2: Try another one.**
20 objects, 4 cups. Deal them out. "How many in each?" (5) "20 shared among 4 groups = 5 in each."

**Step 3: Connect to multiplication.**
"Notice: 4 cups × 5 each = 20. We already knew that! Division is finding the missing piece: 20 shared among 4 = ? We're looking for the number that goes with 4 to make 20."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 15 objects shared among 5 groups. Deal: 3 in each. Check: 5 × 3 = 15. Correct!

**Example 2:** 18 objects shared among 3 groups. Deal: 6 in each. Check: 3 × 6 = 18.

**Example 3:** 8 objects shared among 2 groups. Deal: 4 in each. Check: 2 × 4 = 8.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student shares objects with decreasing scaffold.

**Activity 1: Physical sharing**
- 16 objects, 4 cups. "Share equally. How many in each?" (4)
- 24 objects, 6 cups. Share. (4)
- 21 objects, 3 cups. Share. (7)

**Activity 2: Think without objects**
- "10 shared among 2 groups. How many in each?" (5) "How did you know?" (Because 2 × 5 = 10)
- "28 shared among 4 groups." (7) "Because 4 × 7 = 28."
- "36 shared among 9 groups." (4)

*Facilitator prompt: Always ask: "What multiplication fact helped you?"*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency with equal sharing.

**★ On-level:**
1. 12 shared among 4 groups = ___ in each (3)
2. 20 shared among 5 groups = ___ in each (4)
3. 18 shared among 2 groups = ___ in each (9)
4. 30 shared among 6 groups = ___ in each (5)
5. 27 shared among 3 groups = ___ in each (9)
6. 40 shared among 8 groups = ___ in each (5)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. "There are 24 cookies and 8 children. If each child gets the same number, how many does each child get?" (3)
8. "32 stickers shared equally among 4 friends. How many each?" (8)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "Can you share 15 objects equally among 4 groups? Why or why not?" (No — 15 ÷ 4 doesn't come out even. You'd have 3 in each group with 3 left over.)
10. "I shared some objects equally among 6 groups and each group got 7. How many objects did I start with?" (42, because 6 × 7 = 42)

### Interleaved Review
- "8 × 7 = ___" (56) *(Unit 2)*
- "6 × 9 = ___" (54) *(Unit 2)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm equal sharing understanding.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 15 objects shared among 3 groups = ___ in each group (5)
2. 24 objects shared among 4 groups = ___ in each group (6)

**Mastery = Both correct, preferably connecting to multiplication facts.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to physical sharing with objects. Deal one at a time until the connection between total, groups, and per-group is clear.
- If the student can share but can't verbalize the multiplication connection, it's OK — that connection deepens in Lesson 4.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 2 (Grouping).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Two types of division:** This lesson covers partitive division (known groups, unknown group size). Lesson 2 covers measurement division (known group size, unknown number of groups). Students don't need to name these types — they need to experience both.

**Dealing is the key action.** Distributing objects one at a time into cups (like dealing cards) guarantees equal shares and makes the process visible. Let the student do ALL the dealing.

**Common errors:**
- Student puts unequal amounts in cups. "Count each cup — are they the same?"
- Student gives up if there's a remainder. "For now, we're using numbers that share evenly. If there's leftover, check your count."
