# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 3, Lesson 4

## Topic
Division as the Inverse of Multiplication

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain that division "undoes" multiplication
- Use a known multiplication fact to solve a division problem (e.g., 42 ÷ 6 = ? → "What × 6 = 42?" → 7)
- Verify division answers using multiplication

## Prerequisites
- The division sign (Lesson 3)
- Multiplication fact fluency (Units 1-2)

## Materials Needed
- Paper and pencil
- Multiplication chart (10×10, for reference)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate the multiplication-division connection.

1. "6 × 4 = ?" (24) "Now: 24 ÷ 4 = ?" (6) "See the connection?"
2. "5 × 8 = ?" (40) "40 ÷ 8 = ?" (5)
3. "If multiplication puts groups together, what does division do?" (Takes them apart / splits them up)

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Explicitly teach that division is the inverse of multiplication.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Inverse:** The opposite operation; undoes what the other did
- **Think multiplication:** Strategy for division — ask "What times the divisor equals the dividend?"

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Show the relationship.**
Write: 3 × 7 = 21.
"Multiplication: 3 groups of 7 makes 21."
Now write: 21 ÷ 7 = 3.
"Division: 21 split into groups of 7 gives 3 groups."
"Division undoes what multiplication did."

**Step 2: The "Think Multiplication" strategy.**
"To solve any division problem, think: What times the divisor equals the dividend?"

42 ÷ 6 = ? → "What × 6 = 42?" → 7 × 6 = 42 → Answer: 7.

"You don't need to memorize division facts separately. If you know your multiplication, you know your division!"

**Step 3: Check with multiplication.**
"After you divide, check by multiplying: quotient × divisor should equal the dividend."
- 42 ÷ 6 = 7. Check: 7 × 6 = 42. Correct!
- 36 ÷ 9 = 4. Check: 4 × 9 = 36. Correct!

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 56 ÷ 8 = ? Think: ? × 8 = 56. Answer: 7. Check: 7 × 8 = 56.

**Example 2:** 27 ÷ 3 = ? Think: ? × 3 = 27. Answer: 9. Check: 9 × 3 = 27.

**Example 3:** 63 ÷ 9 = ? Think: ? × 9 = 63. Answer: 7. Check: 7 × 9 = 63.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student uses "Think Multiplication" to solve division.

**Activity 1: Think multiplication aloud**
- 48 ÷ 6 = ? "What times 6 is 48?" (8) Check: 8 × 6 = 48.
- 35 ÷ 7 = ? (5) Check: 5 × 7 = 35.
- 72 ÷ 8 = ? (9) Check: 9 × 8 = 72.

**Activity 2: Multiplication → division pairs**
- "I'll say a multiplication fact. You tell me two division facts."
- 4 × 9 = 36 → 36 ÷ 9 = 4 and 36 ÷ 4 = 9.
- 6 × 7 = 42 → 42 ÷ 7 = 6 and 42 ÷ 6 = 7.
- 8 × 5 = 40 → 40 ÷ 5 = 8 and 40 ÷ 8 = 5.

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency with the inverse relationship.

**★ On-level:**
1. 24 ÷ 6 = ___ (4) Think: ? × 6 = 24
2. 45 ÷ 5 = ___ (9)
3. 54 ÷ 9 = ___ (6)
4. 32 ÷ 4 = ___ (8)
5. 81 ÷ 9 = ___ (9)
6. 36 ÷ 6 = ___ (6)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. "Write two division facts from: 7 × 8 = 56." (56 ÷ 7 = 8, 56 ÷ 8 = 7)
8. Fill in: ___ ÷ 7 = 6 (42) "How did you figure it out?" (7 × 6 = 42)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "If 12 × 12 = 144, what is 144 ÷ 12?" (12)
10. "Why can't you divide by 0?" (There's no number times 0 that gives you anything other than 0. You'd need ? × 0 = something, and that's impossible unless the dividend is 0.)

### Interleaved Review
- "9 × 7 = ___" (63) *(Unit 2)*
- "15 ÷ 3 = ___" (5) *(Lesson 3)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can use multiplication to solve division.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 63 ÷ 7 = ___ (9) What multiplication fact did you use? (9 × 7 = 63)
2. 48 ÷ 8 = ___ (6) Check your answer with multiplication. (6 × 8 = 48)

**Mastery = Both correct with multiplication verification.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student knows the multiplication fact but can't connect it to division, practice the verbal pattern: "If __ × __ = __, then __ ÷ __ = __." Repeat with many examples.
- If the student doesn't know the multiplication fact, this is a Unit 2 gap. Review the specific fact family.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 5 (Fact Families).

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is the most important lesson in the unit.** The insight that "division is just multiplication with a missing factor" eliminates the need to memorize a separate set of 100 division facts. Students who internalize this have a massive advantage.

**"Think multiplication" is the primary division strategy for life.** Even adults solve 56 ÷ 8 by thinking "What times 8 is 56?" Teach this explicitly and practice it until it's automatic.

**Common errors:**
- Student tries to subtract repeatedly instead of using multiplication. This works but is slow and error-prone. Redirect: "Can you think of a multiplication fact instead?"
- Student confuses which number to think about: for 42 ÷ 6, they think "What × 42 = 6?" Rephrase: "What times 6 gives you 42?"
