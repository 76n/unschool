# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 3, Lesson 2

## Topic
Grouping (Measurement Division)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Make groups of a given size from a collection of objects and count how many groups
- Distinguish between sharing ("how many in each group?") and grouping ("how many groups?")
- Recognize grouping as the second type of division

## Prerequisites
- Equal sharing (Lesson 1)
- Multiplication facts (Units 1-2)

## Materials Needed
- 40+ counting objects
- Small cups or bags
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review sharing and set up the new question.

1. "18 shared among 3 groups = ?" (6 in each)
2. "Now different question: I have 18 objects. I put 3 in each bag. How many bags do I fill?" (6 bags)
3. "Both problems used 18 and 3, but the questions were different! Today we explore the second kind."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce grouping — making equal-sized groups and counting how many.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Grouping:** Taking a fixed number of objects at a time and counting how many groups you can make
- **Measurement division:** Division where you know the group size and find how many groups

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Act out grouping.**
Place 20 objects on the table. "I want to make groups of 5. Let's count out 5 and put them in a bag." Count 5, bag them. Repeat. "How many bags did we fill?" (4)

"20 objects, groups of 5 = 4 groups."

**Step 2: Compare to sharing.**
"In Lesson 1, I said '20 objects, 4 groups — how many in each?' (5). Today: '20 objects, 5 in each group — how many groups?' (4). Same numbers, different question!"

**Step 3: Multiple examples.**
- 15 objects, groups of 3. Make groups: 3, 3, 3, 3, 3. Count groups: 5.
- 24 objects, groups of 6. Make groups: 6, 6, 6, 6. Count groups: 4.
- 10 objects, groups of 2. Count groups: 5.

**Step 4: Connect to multiplication.**
"4 groups of 5 = 20. If I know 4 × 5 = 20, then 20 in groups of 5 = 4 groups."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 12 objects, groups of 4. Make 3 groups. Check: 3 × 4 = 12.

**Example 2:** 30 objects, groups of 10. Make 3 groups. Check: 3 × 10 = 30.

**Example 3:** 14 objects, groups of 7. Make 2 groups. Check: 2 × 7 = 14.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student makes groups and identifies the division model.

**Activity 1: Physical grouping**
- 18 objects, groups of 6. How many groups? (3)
- 25 objects, groups of 5. How many groups? (5)
- 16 objects, groups of 8. How many groups? (2)

**Activity 2: Sharing or grouping?**
For each problem, identify whether it's sharing or grouping:
- "24 apples split equally among 6 kids." (Sharing — finding how many each kid gets.)
- "24 apples, 6 in each bag." (Grouping — finding how many bags.)
- "15 pencils, 3 in each cup." (Grouping)
- "15 pencils shared among 3 friends." (Sharing)

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency with grouping and distinguish the two models.

**★ On-level:**
1. 20 objects, groups of 4 = ___ groups (5)
2. 35 objects, groups of 7 = ___ groups (5)
3. 18 objects, groups of 9 = ___ groups (2)
4. 40 objects, groups of 10 = ___ groups (4)
5. 27 objects, groups of 3 = ___ groups (9)
6. 16 objects, groups of 2 = ___ groups (8)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. "You have 30 stickers and put 5 on each page. How many pages?" (6)
8. "Is this sharing or grouping? '32 students sit in rows of 8.' " (Grouping — finding how many rows)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "I have 24 objects. I can make ___ groups of 3, or ___ groups of 4, or ___ groups of 6, or ___ groups of 8." (8, 6, 4, 3)
10. "I made 7 groups of some objects and used 42 total. How many in each group?" (6) "Is this sharing or grouping?" (Sharing — you know the groups, finding the size.)

### Interleaved Review
- "9 × 8 = ___" (72) *(Unit 2)*
- "12 shared among 3 groups = ___" (4) *(Lesson 1)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm grouping understanding.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 24 objects, groups of 8 = ___ groups (3)
2. "Is this sharing or grouping? '30 cupcakes, 5 on each plate.' " (Grouping)

**Mastery = Both correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to physical grouping. Count out the group size, bag them, repeat, count bags.
- If the student confuses sharing and grouping, focus on the question: "Are we finding how many in each group (sharing) or how many groups (grouping)?"

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (The Division Sign).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Both models produce the same equation.** 20 ÷ 5 = 4 whether it's "20 shared among 5" (4 each) or "20 in groups of 5" (4 groups). Students don't need to distinguish the models when writing equations — they just need to experience both physically so division has rich meaning.

**Common errors:**
- Student confuses the answer with the group size: "20 in groups of 5 = 5 groups." That's just repeating the 5. Build physically to verify.
- Student can't figure out when to stop grouping. "Keep making groups of 5 until you run out of objects. Then count the groups."
