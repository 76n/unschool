# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 3, Lesson 9

## Topic
Division Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify division situations in word problems
- Determine whether a problem is sharing or grouping
- Write a division sentence and solve
- Distinguish division problems from multiplication, addition, and subtraction problems

## Prerequisites
- All division facts (Lessons 6-8)
- Division concepts: sharing and grouping (Lessons 1-2)

## Materials Needed
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick fact review and problem interpretation.

1. "48 ÷ 6 = ?" (8) "35 ÷ 7 = ?" (5) "72 ÷ 8 = ?" (9)
2. "Is this addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division? 'There are 24 cookies shared equally among 6 friends.' " (Division)
3. "What about: 'There are 24 cookies and 6 more arrive.' " (Addition)

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach how to identify and solve division word problems.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Two types of division problems.**

**Type 1 — Sharing:** "You know the total and the number of groups. Find how many in each group."
- "24 pencils shared equally among 4 cups. How many in each cup?"
- 24 ÷ 4 = 6. Each cup gets 6 pencils.

**Type 2 — Grouping:** "You know the total and the group size. Find how many groups."
- "24 pencils, 6 in each box. How many boxes?"
- 24 ÷ 6 = 4. You need 4 boxes.

**Step 2: Key language clues.**
- "shared equally," "divided among," "split evenly" → Division (sharing)
- "How many groups?" "How many ___ needed?" → Division (grouping)
- "each," "every," "per" often signal division OR multiplication — read carefully!

**Step 3: The problem-solving process.**
1. Read the problem. What do I know? What am I finding?
2. Is there a total being split? → Division.
3. Write the division sentence: total ÷ (groups or group size) = ?
4. Solve using "think multiplication."
5. Check: Does my answer make sense?

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "There are 36 students in a class. The teacher makes 9 equal teams. How many students on each team?"
- Know: 36 total, 9 teams. Find: how many per team. Sharing.
- 36 ÷ 9 = 4. Each team has 4 students.

**Example 2:** "A farmer has 42 eggs. He packs 6 eggs in each carton. How many cartons does he need?"
- Know: 42 total, 6 per carton. Find: how many cartons. Grouping.
- 42 ÷ 6 = 7. He needs 7 cartons.

**Example 3:** "A ribbon is 56 inches long. It is cut into 8 equal pieces. How long is each piece?"
- Know: 56 total inches, 8 pieces. Find: length of each piece. Sharing.
- 56 ÷ 8 = 7. Each piece is 7 inches.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student solves word problems with coaching.

**Problem 1:** "There are 40 apples. They are packed 5 to a bag. How many bags?" (40 ÷ 5 = 8 bags)

**Problem 2:** "A teacher has 28 books to give equally to 7 students. How many books does each student get?" (28 ÷ 7 = 4 books)

**Problem 3:** "There are 54 chairs arranged in 6 equal rows. How many chairs in each row?" (54 ÷ 6 = 9 chairs)

**Problem 4 (not division!):** "A store has 45 red apples and 30 green apples. How many apples total?" (Addition: 45 + 30 = 75. This is NOT division.)

---

## Independent Practice (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Solve a variety of word problems.

**★ On-level:**
1. "There are 21 crayons shared equally among 3 boxes. How many in each box?" (21 ÷ 3 = 7)
2. "A baker made 48 muffins. She puts 8 in each tray. How many trays?" (48 ÷ 8 = 6)
3. "There are 30 students. They sit in 5 equal rows. How many in each row?" (30 ÷ 5 = 6)
4. "A garden has 18 flowers in 2 equal sections. How many flowers in each section?" (18 ÷ 2 = 9)

**★★ Stretch:**
5. "A parking lot has 63 cars in 7 equal rows. How many cars in each row?" (63 ÷ 7 = 9)
6. "There are 45 minutes of recess this week, spread equally over 5 days. How many minutes per day?" (45 ÷ 5 = 9)
7. "Which operation? 'Sam has 8 bags with 6 marbles in each.' " (Multiplication: 8 × 6 = 48. Tricky — this is NOT division.)

**★★★ Challenge:**
8. "A school has 72 students and 9 classrooms. If students are divided equally, how many per classroom?" (72 ÷ 9 = 8)
9. "Write your own division word problem using the fact 56 ÷ 7 = 8. Then solve it." (Student-generated)
10. "Maya has 36 stickers. She gives an equal number to each of her friends. If each friend gets 4 stickers, how many friends does she have?" (36 ÷ 4 = 9 friends)

### Interleaved Review
- "7 × 9 = ___" (63) *(Unit 2)*
- "56 ÷ 8 = ___" (7) *(Lesson 8)*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm word problem solving.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "There are 32 books shared equally on 4 shelves. How many books on each shelf?" Write the division sentence and solve. (32 ÷ 4 = 8)
2. "There are 45 cookies, 9 in each box. How many boxes?" (45 ÷ 9 = 5)

**Mastery = Both correct with proper division sentences.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student can compute but can't identify division in word problems: practice the identification step separately. Read problems aloud and ask "Is there a total being split up?" before solving.
- If the student writes the wrong operation: act out the story with objects.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 10 (Mixed Practice).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The hardest part is usually identifying the operation, not computing.** Spend most of your coaching time on Step 1 (understanding the problem) and Step 2 (choosing division), not on the arithmetic.

**Include non-division problems intentionally.** Students in a "division unit" may assume everything is division. Throw in addition, subtraction, and multiplication problems to keep them thinking.

**Common errors:**
- Student multiplies when they should divide: "24 shared among 6 → 24 × 6 = 144." Help them see that the answer must be SMALLER than 24 (you're splitting it up, not combining more).
- Student uses the wrong numbers: "24 cookies, 6 friends" → writes 6 ÷ 24. "The total goes first."
