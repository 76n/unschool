# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 3, Lesson 8

## Topic
Dividing by 6, 7, 8, and 9

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve ÷6, ÷7, ÷8, and ÷9 problems using "think multiplication"
- Identify which division facts are hardest and practice them specifically
- Use all division facts from 1-10 with reasonable fluency

## Prerequisites
- Dividing by 2, 3, 4, 5 (Lessons 6-7)
- ×6 through ×9 fact fluency (Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- Paper and pencil
- Fact family triangle cards
- Multiplication chart (for reference)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Warm up with easier division and harder multiplication facts.

1. "24 ÷ 4 = ?" (6) "30 ÷ 5 = ?" (6) "27 ÷ 3 = ?" (9)
2. "6 × 7 = ?" (42) "8 × 9 = ?" (72) "7 × 8 = ?" (56)
3. "If those are hard to remember as multiplication, they're hard as division too. But the same trick works: think multiplication!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency with the harder division facts.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: The "hard" division facts.**
"Most ÷6, ÷7, ÷8, ÷9 facts are ones you already know from dividing by 2-5 (because of fact families). For example, 42 ÷ 7 = 6, and you already know 42 ÷ 6 = 7 from the same family."

"The truly new facts — the ones you haven't practiced from the other direction — are a small group:"

| Fact | Think Multiplication |
|---|---|
| 36 ÷ 6 = 6 | 6 × 6 = 36 |
| 42 ÷ 6 = 7 | 7 × 6 = 42 |
| 48 ÷ 6 = 8 | 8 × 6 = 48 |
| 54 ÷ 6 = 9 | 9 × 6 = 54 |
| 49 ÷ 7 = 7 | 7 × 7 = 49 |
| 56 ÷ 7 = 8 | 8 × 7 = 56 |
| 63 ÷ 7 = 9 | 9 × 7 = 63 |
| 64 ÷ 8 = 8 | 8 × 8 = 64 |
| 72 ÷ 8 = 9 | 9 × 8 = 72 |
| 81 ÷ 9 = 9 | 9 × 9 = 81 |

"That's only 10 facts. And if you know the multiplication, you know the division."

**Step 2: Strategies for the hardest facts.**
- 56 ÷ 7 = 8. "5, 6, 7, 8" — the digits are consecutive.
- 72 ÷ 8 = 9. "7 × 8 = 56, 8 × 8 = 64, 9 × 8 = 72." Count up.
- 63 ÷ 7 = 9 and 63 ÷ 9 = 7. "Same family."

**Step 3: Practice the strategy.**
For each: say the division, think the multiplication, give the answer, verify.

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build full division fact fluency.

**★ On-level:**
1. 42 ÷ 7 = ___ (6)
2. 54 ÷ 9 = ___ (6)
3. 48 ÷ 8 = ___ (6)
4. 56 ÷ 7 = ___ (8)
5. 72 ÷ 9 = ___ (8)
6. 81 ÷ 9 = ___ (9)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. Fill in: ___ ÷ 8 = 7 (56)
8. Fill in: ___ ÷ 6 = 9 (54)
9. "A book has 63 pages. You read 7 pages each day. How many days to finish?" (9)
10. "There are 48 markers shared equally among 8 boxes. How many in each box?" (6)

**★★★ Challenge:**
11. "Solve all of these: 72 ÷ 8, 72 ÷ 9, 72 ÷ 6." (9, 8, 12) "Wait — is 72 ÷ 6 a 'known fact'?" (12 × 6 = 72, so yes — but 12 is outside our 1-10 range. The student may need to think harder for this one.)
12. "What is the largest single-digit quotient you can get from dividing by 7?" (9, from 63 ÷ 7)

### Interleaved Review
- "Write the fact family for 8, 6, 48." *(Lesson 5)*
- "24 ÷ 3 = ___" (8) *(Lesson 7)*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm fluency with harder division facts.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 56 ÷ 8 = ___ (7)
2. 63 ÷ 9 = ___ (7)

**Mastery = Both correct. Some hesitation is OK — speed will come with practice.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which multiplication facts are the gap. If the student doesn't know 8 × 7 = 56, that's the root issue.
- Make fact family triangle cards for the hardest families and practice daily.
- Focus on the smallest number of "truly hard" facts (typically 6-8 families).

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 9 (Division Word Problems).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The truly hard facts are few.** There are only about 10-15 division facts that students find genuinely difficult (the ÷6/÷7/÷8/÷9 range). Everything else was covered in earlier lessons. Focus energy on these specific facts.

**Speed comes with time.** At this stage, accuracy is more important than speed. If the student consistently gets the right answer using "think multiplication" — even if it takes 5-10 seconds — that's success. Automaticity develops through continued practice in subsequent units.

**This lesson may need 2 sessions** if the ×6-×9 facts are still developing. Don't rush — weak multiplication creates frustrating division experiences.
