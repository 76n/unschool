# Math — Grade 3 — Unit 3, Lesson 7

## Topic
Dividing by 3 and 4

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve ÷3 problems fluently using ×3 facts
- Solve ÷4 problems fluently using ×4 facts
- Apply "think multiplication" strategy consistently

## Prerequisites
- Dividing by 2 and 5 (Lesson 6)
- ×3 and ×4 fact fluency (Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- Paper and pencil
- Fact family triangle cards

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review ÷2 and ÷5, activate ×3 and ×4.

1. "18 ÷ 2 = ?" (9) "45 ÷ 5 = ?" (9)
2. "3 × 8 = ?" (24) "4 × 7 = ?" (28)
3. "So 24 ÷ 3 = ?" (8) "28 ÷ 4 = ?" (7) "Think multiplication!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build ÷3 and ÷4 fact fluency.

### Instruction Notes

**÷3 facts:**
"Think: ? × 3 = dividend."

| Problem | Think | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 6 ÷ 3 | ? × 3 = 6 | 2 |
| 9 ÷ 3 | ? × 3 = 9 | 3 |
| 12 ÷ 3 | ? × 3 = 12 | 4 |
| 15 ÷ 3 | ? × 3 = 15 | 5 |
| 18 ÷ 3 | ? × 3 = 18 | 6 |
| 21 ÷ 3 | ? × 3 = 21 | 7 |
| 24 ÷ 3 | ? × 3 = 24 | 8 |
| 27 ÷ 3 | ? × 3 = 27 | 9 |
| 30 ÷ 3 | ? × 3 = 30 | 10 |

**÷4 facts:**
"Think: ? × 4 = dividend."

| Problem | Think | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 8 ÷ 4 | ? × 4 = 8 | 2 |
| 12 ÷ 4 | ? × 4 = 12 | 3 |
| 16 ÷ 4 | ? × 4 = 16 | 4 |
| 20 ÷ 4 | ? × 4 = 20 | 5 |
| 24 ÷ 4 | ? × 4 = 24 | 6 |
| 28 ÷ 4 | ? × 4 = 28 | 7 |
| 32 ÷ 4 | ? × 4 = 32 | 8 |
| 36 ÷ 4 | ? × 4 = 36 | 9 |
| 40 ÷ 4 | ? × 4 = 40 | 10 |

**Pattern for ÷4:** "Dividing by 4 is the same as halving twice: 24 ÷ 4 = half of 24 is 12, half of 12 is 6. So 24 ÷ 4 = 6."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency.

**★ On-level:**
1. 9 ÷ 3 = ___ (3)
2. 24 ÷ 3 = ___ (8)
3. 30 ÷ 3 = ___ (10)
4. 16 ÷ 4 = ___ (4)
5. 28 ÷ 4 = ___ (7)
6. 40 ÷ 4 = ___ (10)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. Fill in: ___ ÷ 3 = 7 (21)
8. Fill in: ___ ÷ 4 = 6 (24)
9. "A rope is 24 feet long. It's cut into 3 equal pieces. How long is each piece?" (8 feet)
10. "There are 32 students. The teacher makes groups of 4. How many groups?" (8)

**★★★ Challenge:**
11. "24 ÷ 3 = ___ and 24 ÷ 4 = ___. Why are the answers different even though both start with 24?" (Different divisors: dividing by 3 gives bigger groups but fewer of them; dividing by 4 gives more groups but smaller ones.)
12. "Use halving twice to solve 36 ÷ 4." (Half of 36 = 18. Half of 18 = 9. So 36 ÷ 4 = 9.)

### Interleaved Review
- "Write the fact family for 6, 5, 30." *(Lesson 5)*
- "50 ÷ 5 = ___" (10)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm ÷3 and ÷4 fluency.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 21 ÷ 3 = ___ (7)
2. 32 ÷ 4 = ___ (8)

**Mastery = Both correct within a few seconds.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the corresponding ×3 or ×4 facts. If the student doesn't know 8 × 3 = 24, they can't solve 24 ÷ 3.
- Use the "halving twice" strategy for ÷4 as a backup.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 8 (Dividing by 6, 7, 8, and 9).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**÷4 has a useful shortcut:** halving twice. This is especially helpful for students whose ×4 facts are weak. 28 ÷ 4: half of 28 = 14, half of 14 = 7. It always works.

**If ×3 or ×4 facts aren't fluent,** this lesson will be frustrating. Pause and practice multiplication before pushing division. The "think multiplication" strategy only works if the multiplication is accessible.
