# Math — Grade 7 — Unit 1, Lesson 8

## Topic
Percent of a Number

## Learning Objectives
- Find the percent of a number by converting the percent to a decimal and multiplying.
- Set up and solve a proportion to find the percent of a number.
- Use mental math strategies (10%, 1%, and benchmark combinations) to compute common percents efficiently.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to convert between percents, decimals, and fractions (Lesson 7).
- Fluency with decimal multiplication.
- Understanding of percent as "per 100" (Lesson 7).
- Experience with proportional equations y = kx (Lesson 4).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Calculator (optional — for checking work with complex decimals).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.RP.3 — Use proportional relationships to solve multi-step ratio and percent problems.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Practice mental math with benchmark percents.

1. Ask: "What is 50% of 80?" (40 — half of 80.)
2. Ask: "What is 10% of 250?" (25 — divide by 10.)
3. Ask: "What is 25% of 60?" (15 — divide by 4.)
4. Ask: "If you know 10% of a number, how could you find 20%?" (Double the 10% amount.) "How about 5%?" (Take half of the 10% amount.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Percent of a number** — the result of taking a given percent of a quantity. "20% of 80" means 20% times 80.
- **Proportion method** — setting up the equation part/whole = percent/100 and solving for the unknown.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a concrete model.**
"Imagine a class of 30 students. 60% of the students brought lunch from home. How many students is that?"

Draw a bar model: a bar representing 30 students. Divide it into 10 equal sections (each section is 10% = 3 students). Shade 6 sections (60%).

"6 sections x 3 students = 18 students. 60% of 30 = 18."

**Step 2 — Method 1: Convert to decimal and multiply.**
"The most common method: convert the percent to a decimal, then multiply."

60% of 30:
0.60 x 30 = 18.

15% of 200:
0.15 x 200 = 30.

7.5% of 400:
0.075 x 400 = 30.

"In general: Percent of a Number = (Percent as a decimal) x (Number)."

**Step 3 — Method 2: Set up a proportion.**
"You can also think of it as a proportion."

"What is 35% of 80?"

Set up: part / 80 = 35 / 100.
Cross-multiply: part x 100 = 35 x 80.
part x 100 = 2,800.
part = 2,800 / 100 = 28.

So 35% of 80 = 28.

**Step 4 — Method 3: Mental math with benchmarks.**
"For quick calculations, break the percent into benchmark pieces."

Find 15% of 60:
- 10% of 60 = 6.
- 5% of 60 = 3 (half of 10%).
- 15% of 60 = 6 + 3 = 9.

Find 35% of 200:
- 25% of 200 = 50.
- 10% of 200 = 20.
- 35% of 200 = 50 + 20 = 70.

"This strategy is especially useful when you do not have a calculator."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Decimal method**
Find 24% of 75.

Step 1: Convert 24% to a decimal: 0.24.
Step 2: Multiply: 0.24 x 75 = 18.
Answer: 24% of 75 = 18.

**Example B — Proportion method**
What is 65% of 120?

Set up: part / 120 = 65 / 100.
Cross-multiply: part x 100 = 65 x 120 = 7,800.
part = 7,800 / 100 = 78.
Answer: 65% of 120 = 78.

**Example C — Mental math**
Find 45% of 80.

Strategy: 45% = 50% - 5%.
50% of 80 = 40.
5% of 80 = 4 (half of 10% of 80 = 8, divided by 2 = 4).
45% of 80 = 40 - 4 = 36.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Find 30% of 150.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Convert 30% to a decimal first. Or use 10% of 150 = 15, so 30% = 15 x 3."

2. What is 8% of 250?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Convert 8% to 0.08. Multiply by 250."

3. A shirt costs $45. It is 20% off. How much is the discount?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find 20% of $45. What do you multiply?"

4. There are 360 students in a school. 55% ride the bus. How many students ride the bus?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Set up as a decimal multiplication or a proportion."

5. Use mental math to find 15% of 240.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find 10% first. Then find 5%. Add them."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-Level
1. Find 40% of 90.
2. Find 12% of 350.
3. A test has 80 questions. A student answered 85% correctly. How many questions did the student get right?
4. Find 75% of 64 using mental math. Show your strategy.

### Stretch
5. A basketball player made 72% of her 125 free throw attempts this season. How many free throws did she make?
6. A tablet costs $299. Sales tax is 8.25%. How much is the tax (round to the nearest cent)?
7. Marcus saved $180 toward a $450 bicycle. What percent of the total price has he saved?

### Challenge
8. A number's 15% is 27. What is the number? (Hint: set up an equation.)
9. Restaurant bill: $68.50. You want to leave a tip that is between 18% and 22% of the bill. Give the range of acceptable tip amounts (round to the nearest cent). Then find exactly 20%.

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: converting between fractions, decimals, and percents from Lesson 7)*
10. Convert 5/6 to a percent (round to the nearest tenth).
11. Convert 0.375 to a fraction in simplest form and to a percent.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Find 35% of 260.

**Problem 2:** A store has 480 items in stock. 15% of them are on clearance. How many items are on clearance?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the bar model. Draw a bar for the total, divide it into 10 equal sections (each representing 10%), and have the student count sections to find the percent. This builds the connection between the visual and the computation.
- If the student understands the concept but makes multiplication errors, allow calculator use temporarily so they can focus on the percent reasoning without arithmetic barriers.
- Provide a step-by-step card: (1) Convert percent to decimal (divide by 100). (2) Multiply by the number. (3) Label your answer.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Assign the Challenge problems if not yet completed.
- Preview: "Next lesson, we use percent to calculate how much a value has increased or decreased — percent increase and percent decrease."

## Extension (Optional)
- **Mental Math Challenge:** Present a chain of percent problems to solve mentally: "Start with 200. Find 50%. Now find 10% of that result. Now find 200% of that result." (200 -> 100 -> 10 -> 20.)
- **Real-World Application:** "A survey says 73% of seventh graders prefer pizza for lunch. If your school has 425 seventh graders, about how many prefer pizza? Is your answer exact or an estimate? Why?"

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Moving the decimal the wrong way.** A student computes "12% of 350" as 1.2 x 350 = 420 instead of 0.12 x 350 = 42. Have the student estimate first: 12% is a little more than 10%, and 10% of 350 is 35, so the answer should be around 35-40, not 420.
- **Setting up the proportion backward.** In the proportion method, the student writes 100/percent instead of percent/100. Emphasize: the percent always goes over 100.
- **Not labeling the answer.** When the problem is a word problem, the student writes "42" without specifying "42 students" or "$42." Encourage labeling with units.

### Pacing Advice
- If the student is strong with decimal multiplication, the decimal method is fastest and should be the primary tool. Use the proportion method as an alternative for students who prefer it.
- Mental math strategies (Step 4) are worth investing in — they build number sense and are useful in everyday life. Spend at least 3-4 minutes on this.
- If the student finishes early, have them try Problems 8-9 from the Challenge tier, which involve "reverse" percent problems (finding the whole given the part and percent).
