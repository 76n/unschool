# Math — Grade 7 — Unit 1, Lesson 2

## Topic
Proportional Relationships

## Learning Objectives
- Determine whether two quantities are in a proportional relationship by testing whether the ratio between them is constant across all pairs.
- Distinguish between proportional relationships (multiplicative) and non-proportional relationships (additive or inconsistent).
- Use tables to represent and test proportional relationships.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to write and simplify ratios (Lesson 1).
- Ability to compute unit rates (Lesson 1).
- Fluency with division of whole numbers and decimals.
- Experience with ratio tables (Grade 6).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.RP.2a — Decide whether two quantities are in a proportional relationship, e.g., by testing for equivalent ratios in a table or graphing on a coordinate plane and observing whether the graph is a straight line through the origin.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review unit rates and set the stage for comparing ratios across a table.

1. Display: "A bakery charges $2.50 per cupcake." Ask the student to fill in a table:

   | Cupcakes | Cost ($) |
   |---|---|
   | 1 | ? |
   | 2 | ? |
   | 3 | ? |
   | 5 | ? |

2. Ask: "What is the cost per cupcake for each row?" (Always $2.50.)
3. Ask: "What do you notice about the ratio of cost to cupcakes?" (It is the same every time.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Proportional relationship** — a relationship between two quantities in which the ratio of one quantity to the other is constant for all pairs of values.
- **Constant ratio** — the unchanging value of y/x (or the unit rate) across every row of a table.
- **Non-proportional relationship** — a relationship in which the ratio y/x is NOT the same for all pairs.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a concrete model.**
Present two scenarios on the board:

Scenario A: "A cell phone plan charges $0.10 per text message."

| Texts | Cost ($) |
|---|---|
| 10 | 1.00 |
| 20 | 2.00 |
| 50 | 5.00 |
| 100 | 10.00 |

Scenario B: "A cell phone plan charges a $5 monthly fee plus $0.10 per text message."

| Texts | Cost ($) |
|---|---|
| 10 | 6.00 |
| 20 | 7.00 |
| 50 | 10.00 |
| 100 | 15.00 |

Ask: "What is different about these two scenarios?"

**Step 2 — Test the ratios.**
"Let's check: is the ratio of cost to texts constant in each table?"

Scenario A: 1.00/10 = 0.10, 2.00/20 = 0.10, 5.00/50 = 0.10, 10.00/100 = 0.10. Yes — the ratio is always 0.10. This is a proportional relationship.

Scenario B: 6.00/10 = 0.60, 7.00/20 = 0.35, 10.00/50 = 0.20, 15.00/100 = 0.15. No — the ratio changes. This is NOT a proportional relationship.

**Step 3 — Identify the key test.**
"To test whether a relationship is proportional, compute y/x for every pair of values in the table. If the quotient is the same every time, the relationship is proportional."

Write the rule on the board:
- Proportional: y/x = same value for all rows.
- Non-proportional: y/x changes from row to row.

**Step 4 — Real-world reasoning.**
"In Scenario B, there is a fixed $5 fee that does not depend on the number of texts. That flat fee breaks the proportional relationship. Proportional relationships always start at zero — 0 texts should cost $0."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Proportional**
A car travels at a steady speed. Is the relationship between time and distance proportional?

| Time (hours) | Distance (miles) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 55 |
| 2 | 110 |
| 3 | 165 |
| 4 | 220 |

Check: 55/1 = 55, 110/2 = 55, 165/3 = 55, 220/4 = 55.
The ratio is constant (55). This is a proportional relationship.

**Example B — Not proportional**
A taxi charges a $3 base fare plus $2 per mile. Is the relationship between miles and total fare proportional?

| Miles | Total fare ($) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 5 |
| 2 | 7 |
| 3 | 9 |
| 5 | 13 |

Check: 5/1 = 5, 7/2 = 3.5, 9/3 = 3, 13/5 = 2.6.
The ratio is NOT constant. This is not a proportional relationship. (The $3 base fare disrupts proportionality.)

**Example C — Proportional (with fractions)**
A faucet fills a bucket at a constant rate.

| Time (minutes) | Water (gallons) |
|---|---|
| 2 | 1/2 |
| 4 | 1 |
| 6 | 1 1/2 |
| 10 | 2 1/2 |

Check: (1/2)/2 = 1/4, 1/4 = 1/4, (3/2)/6 = 3/12 = 1/4, (5/2)/10 = 5/20 = 1/4.
The ratio is constant (1/4 gallon per minute). This is a proportional relationship.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. A store sells bananas at the following prices. Is this proportional?

   | Bananas | Cost ($) |
   |---|---|
   | 2 | 0.50 |
   | 4 | 1.00 |
   | 6 | 1.50 |
   | 10 | 2.50 |

   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Compute cost divided by bananas for each row. Is the quotient the same?"

2. A gym membership has the following costs. Is this proportional?

   | Months | Total cost ($) |
   |---|---|
   | 1 | 45 |
   | 2 | 70 |
   | 3 | 95 |
   | 6 | 170 |

   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What happens when you divide total cost by months? Is there a constant?"

3. An ice cream truck sells cones at $3.50 each. Create a table with 4 rows showing the relationship between number of cones and total cost. Is this relationship proportional? How do you know?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Does every row give the same ratio?"

4. Tell whether this relationship is proportional or not and explain why: "Jordan is 5 years older than his sister."
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "If Jordan is 10 and his sister is 5, what is the ratio? If Jordan is 15 and his sister is 10, what is the ratio? Same or different?"

5. A factory produces widgets at a constant rate. In 3 hours it produces 45 widgets. In 5 hours it produces 75 widgets. In 8 hours it produces 120 widgets. Is this proportional?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find the unit rate for each time period."

   |---|---|
   | 2 | 3 |
   | 4 | ? |
   | 6 | ? |

### Stretch
5. A runner trains at a steady pace. She runs 2.4 miles in 20 minutes and 4.2 miles in 35 minutes. Is her running rate proportional? Show your work.
6. A plumber charges $50 for a service call plus $30 per hour. Make a table showing the cost for 1, 2, 3, and 4 hours. Is this a proportional relationship? Why or why not?

   | 7 | 82 |

### Challenge
8. Two students are debating. Alex says: "If y is always bigger than x, the relationship is proportional." Sam says: "That is not necessarily true." Who is correct? Explain with an example.
9. Create a table of values that is NOT proportional but where the first and last rows DO have the same ratio y/x. Explain why the table is still not proportional.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Is the following relationship proportional? Explain how you know.

| x | y |
|---|---|
| 4 | 12 |
| 7 | 21 |
| 10 | 30 |

**Problem 2:** A movie streaming service costs $10 per month with no additional fees. Is the relationship between months and total cost proportional? Explain.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the two cell phone scenarios from Direct Instruction. Have the student compute y/x for both tables side by side to see the contrast clearly.
- Provide 2-3 additional tables and have the student sort them into "proportional" and "not proportional" piles, explaining their reasoning for each.
- Use a concrete manipulative: set out groups of objects (e.g., 2 red for every 3 blue) and ask if the pattern holds when you double, triple, and quadruple the groups.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Assign the Challenge problems if not yet completed.
- Preview: "Next lesson, we give the constant ratio a name — the constant of proportionality — and learn to find it from tables, equations, and word problems."

## Extension (Optional)
- **Real-World Investigation:** Have the student look at a receipt, menu, or online store. Identify a relationship (e.g., number of items and total price) and determine whether it is proportional.
- **Always/Sometimes/Never:** "The relationship between a person's age and their height is _____ proportional." Have the student explain and justify their answer.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Checking only one or two rows.** Students may declare a table proportional after checking a single ratio. Emphasize: you must check EVERY row. One different ratio means the relationship is not proportional.
- **Confusing additive and multiplicative patterns.** In Problem 2 of Independent Practice, y increases by 6 each time, which may look like a pattern. But proportional means the ratio y/x is constant, not that the difference is constant. Ask: "Is y always the SAME NUMBER TIMES x?"
- **Assuming all word problems are proportional.** The plumber and taxi examples show that a starting fee or fixed charge makes a relationship non-proportional.

### Pacing Advice
- The distinction between proportional and non-proportional is the big conceptual idea of this lesson. Spend the most time on the side-by-side comparison in Step 1 and Step 2 of Direct Instruction.
- If the student easily grasps the concept with whole numbers, move quickly to Guided Practice Problem 4 (the age example) — non-obvious non-proportional relationships are more challenging.
- This lesson sets up Lesson 3 (constant of proportionality) and Lesson 6 (identifying proportional relationships from tables, graphs, and equations). Solid understanding here pays off later.
