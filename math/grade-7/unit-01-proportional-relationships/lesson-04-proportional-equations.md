# Math — Grade 7 — Unit 1, Lesson 4

## Topic
Proportional Equations

## Learning Objectives
- Write equations in the form y = kx to represent proportional relationships.
- Use a proportional equation to find missing values of x or y.
- Translate word problems into proportional equations and solve them.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to identify the constant of proportionality (k) from tables and word problems (Lesson 3).
- Understanding that a proportional relationship has the form y = kx (Lesson 3).
- Fluency with multiplication and division of decimals and fractions.
- Ability to solve one-step equations by dividing or multiplying (Grade 6).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.RP.2c — Represent proportional relationships by equations. For example, if total cost t is proportional to the number n of items purchased at a constant price p, the relationship between the total cost and the number of items can be expressed as t = pn.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Practice finding k and connect it to multiplication.

1. Display: "A printing company charges $0.08 per page." Ask: "What is the constant of proportionality?" (k = 0.08)
2. Ask: "How would you find the cost of printing 250 pages?" (Multiply: 0.08 x 250 = $20.00.)
3. Ask: "What if I told you the cost was $6.00 — how many pages were printed?" (Divide: 6.00 / 0.08 = 75 pages.)
4. Say: "Today we write this pattern as an equation: Cost = 0.08 x pages, or C = 0.08p."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Proportional equation** — an equation of the form y = kx, where k is the constant of proportionality.
- **Variable** — a letter that represents an unknown or changing quantity (e.g., x, y, t, n).
- **Solve** — to find the value of a variable that makes the equation true.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a concrete model.**
Return to the printing example. Write on the board:
- "The cost is $0.08 per page."
- In equation form: C = 0.08 x p, or C = 0.08p.

"C is the total cost (the y-value). p is the number of pages (the x-value). 0.08 is k."

**Step 2 — Writing an equation from a table.**
Present a table:

| Hours (h) | Pay ($) (P) |
|---|---|
| 3 | 45 |
| 5 | 75 |
| 8 | 120 |

"Step 1: Find k. 45/3 = 15. Confirm: 75/5 = 15, 120/8 = 15. k = 15."
"Step 2: Write the equation. P = 15h."
"This equation works for ANY number of hours. For 10 hours: P = 15 x 10 = $150."

**Step 3 — Writing an equation from a word problem.**
"A recipe calls for 2.5 cups of rice for every cup of beans. Write an equation."

Identify the variables: let r = cups of rice, b = cups of beans.
k = 2.5 (rice per bean).
Equation: r = 2.5b.

"How much rice for 4 cups of beans? r = 2.5 x 4 = 10 cups."

**Step 4 — Using the equation to find x.**
"We can also work backward. If we know y and k, we can find x."

Using P = 15h: If the worker earned $210, how many hours did they work?
210 = 15h
h = 210 / 15
h = 14 hours.

### Worked Examples

**Example A — From table to equation**

| Minutes (m) | Words typed (w) |
|---|---|
| 2 | 90 |
| 5 | 225 |
| 10 | 450 |

Step 1: k = 90/2 = 45. Check: 225/5 = 45, 450/10 = 45.
Step 2: Equation: w = 45m.
Step 3: Predict — how many words in 15 minutes? w = 45 x 15 = 675 words.

**Example B — From word problem to equation**
A hose fills a pool at a rate of 8.5 gallons per minute. Write an equation for the gallons (g) after m minutes. How long to fill 340 gallons?

k = 8.5. Equation: g = 8.5m.
To find m when g = 340: 340 = 8.5m, so m = 340 / 8.5 = 40 minutes.

**Example C — Equation with fractions**
For every 1/2 pound of cheese, a deli charges $3.25. Write an equation for the cost (C) based on the weight in pounds (w). Find the cost of 3 pounds.

k = 3.25 / (1/2) = 3.25 x 2 = $6.50 per pound.
Equation: C = 6.50w.
For 3 pounds: C = 6.50 x 3 = $19.50.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Write an equation for the table:

   | x | y |
   |---|---|
   | 4 | 14 |
   | 7 | 24.5 |
   | 10 | 35 |

   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find k first. Then write y = ___x."

2. A worker earns $13.50 per hour. Write an equation for total pay (P) based on hours (h). How much does the worker earn in 24 hours?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What is k? Write the equation. Plug in 24 for h."

3. Using the equation from Problem 2, the worker earned $202.50 this week. How many hours did they work?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "You know P = 202.50 and k = 13.50. Solve for h by dividing."

4. A car travels 32 miles per gallon. Write an equation. How many gallons are needed for a 480-mile trip?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "The equation is miles = 32 x gallons. Rearrange to find gallons."

5. A recipe uses 3/4 cup of sugar for every 2 cups of flour. Write an equation for sugar (s) in terms of flour (f). How much sugar for 6 cups of flour?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find k = (3/4) / 2 first."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-Level
1. Write an equation for the table, then find y when x = 15.

   | x | y |
   |---|---|
   | 2 | 9 |
   | 6 | 27 |
   | 10 | 45 |

2. A bike rider travels at 14 miles per hour. Write an equation for distance (d) in terms of hours (h). How far does the rider go in 3.5 hours?

3. Using the equation from Problem 2, how many hours does it take to ride 91 miles?

4. A factory makes 280 widgets in 8 hours. Write an equation for the number of widgets (w) in terms of hours (h).

### Stretch
5. A recipe uses 2/3 cup of oil for every 1 1/2 cups of vinegar. Write an equation for oil (o) in terms of vinegar (v). How much oil for 6 cups of vinegar?
6. Two workers are paid proportionally. Worker A earns $187.50 for 15 hours. Worker B earns $168 for 12 hours. Write an equation for each. Who earns more per hour, and by how much?
7. The cost of gasoline is $3.45 per gallon. A driver has $50. Write an equation and determine the maximum number of full gallons the driver can buy. How much change is left?

### Challenge
8. A proportional equation is y = (5/3)x. Find y when x = 2/7. Express your answer as a fraction in simplest form.
9. A swimming pool is being filled at a constant rate. After 4 hours, the pool has 3,400 gallons. After 7 hours, it has 5,950 gallons. Write a proportional equation. Then determine how long it takes to fill the 10,200-gallon pool completely.

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: determining whether a relationship is proportional from Lesson 2)*
10. Is this table proportional? Explain.

    | x | y |
    |---|---|
    | 3 | 12 |
    | 5 | 20 |
    | 9 | 35 |

11. A gym charges $25 per month plus a $50 sign-up fee. Is the relationship between months and total cost proportional? Explain.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A store sells ribbon for $0.75 per foot. Write a proportional equation for the total cost (C) in terms of feet (f). How much does 18 feet of ribbon cost?

**Problem 2:** Using the equation y = 4.5x, find x when y = 31.5.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to simple examples: "Apples cost $2 each. Fill in the table for 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 apples. Now write the equation. Now use the equation to find the cost of 10 apples."
- If the student can write the equation but cannot solve for x, review one-step equations (dividing both sides) with 3-4 examples using whole numbers.
- Create a reference card: "y = kx. To find y: multiply k times x. To find x: divide y by k. To find k: divide y by x."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Assign the Challenge problems if not yet completed.
- Preview: "Next lesson, we graph proportional relationships and see what they look like on a coordinate plane."

## Extension (Optional)
- **Equation Match:** Provide a set of 5 word problems and 5 equations (shuffled). Have the student match each word problem to its equation and then solve one problem using its equation.
- **Create Your Own:** Have the student write a word problem that can be modeled by the equation y = 7.25x, then solve it.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Writing the equation with k in the wrong position.** For example, writing y = x/k instead of y = kx. Remind the student: "k multiplies x. If apples cost $2 each, the cost is 2 times the number of apples, not the number of apples divided by 2."
- **Using the wrong operation to solve for x.** When given y = kx and asked to find x, some students multiply instead of divide. Have them check: "If y = 45 and k = 15, does x = 45 x 15 = 675 make sense? No — you would need to work way more than 675 hours to earn $45."
- **Inconsistent variable naming.** Students may confuse which letter represents which quantity. Encourage them to write a key: "Let d = distance, t = time, k = 55 mph."

### Pacing Advice
- If Lesson 3 was solid, Steps 1-2 of Direct Instruction should move quickly. Spend more time on Step 4 (solving for x), as this is the new skill.
- Guided Practice Problems 3 and 5 involve solving for x and fractional k, respectively — these are the most common stumbling points.
- If the student is confident, combine this lesson with the first part of Lesson 5 (graphing).
