# Math — Grade 7 — Unit 1, Lesson 5

## Topic
Graphing Proportional Relationships

## Learning Objectives
- Plot ordered pairs from a proportional relationship on a coordinate plane.
- Recognize that the graph of a proportional relationship is a straight line through the origin.
- Determine the constant of proportionality (k) from a graph by computing the slope (rise over run) or reading the y-value when x = 1.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to write proportional equations in the form y = kx (Lesson 4).
- Ability to find the constant of proportionality from tables and word problems (Lesson 3).
- Experience plotting points on a coordinate plane (Grade 6).
- Understanding of ordered pairs (x, y).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Graph paper (at least 1 sheet per student).
- Ruler or straightedge.
- Colored pencils (optional — for distinguishing multiple graphs).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.RP.2a — Decide whether two quantities are in a proportional relationship by graphing on a coordinate plane and observing whether the graph is a straight line through the origin. 7.RP.2d — Explain what a point (x, y) on the graph of a proportional relationship means in terms of the situation.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review plotting points on a coordinate plane.

1. Provide graph paper. Ask the student to draw a coordinate plane with x-axis from 0 to 10 and y-axis from 0 to 30.
2. Ask the student to plot these points: (1, 3), (2, 6), (4, 12), (5, 15).
3. Ask: "What do you notice about these points?" (They appear to form a straight line.)
4. Ask: "Do they pass through the origin (0, 0)?" (Yes — if you extend the line, it hits (0, 0).)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Coordinate plane** — a grid with a horizontal axis (x-axis) and a vertical axis (y-axis) that meet at the origin (0, 0).
- **Origin** — the point (0, 0) on the coordinate plane.
- **Slope** — the steepness of a line, calculated as the rise (change in y) divided by the run (change in x). In a proportional graph, the slope equals k.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a concrete model.**
"You just plotted points from the equation y = 3x. Let's verify: when x = 1, y = 3(1) = 3. When x = 2, y = 3(2) = 6. When x = 0, y = 3(0) = 0."

Draw or display the line on the board. Label the origin. Trace the line and say: "This line has two special features: it is straight, and it passes through the origin."

**Step 2 — State the rule.**
Write on the board:
"The graph of every proportional relationship is a STRAIGHT LINE through the ORIGIN (0, 0)."

Ask: "Why must it pass through the origin?" (Because when x = 0, y = k x 0 = 0. Zero input gives zero output — 0 hours of work means $0 earned, 0 gallons of gas means 0 miles driven.)

**Step 3 — Finding k from a graph.**
"We can find k from a graph in two ways."

Method 1 — Read the y-value when x = 1. "On our graph, when x = 1, y = 3. So k = 3."

Method 2 — Pick any point and compute y / x. "The point (4, 12) is on the line. k = 12 / 4 = 3."

Method 3 — Compute rise over run between any two points. "From (2, 6) to (5, 15): rise = 15 - 6 = 9, run = 5 - 2 = 3. Slope = 9 / 3 = 3. So k = 3."

"All three methods give the same answer because the relationship is proportional."

**Step 4 — Comparing two proportional graphs.**
Draw two lines on the same coordinate plane:
- Line A: y = 3x (points: (0,0), (2,6), (4,12))
- Line B: y = 5x (points: (0,0), (2,10), (4,20))

Ask: "Which line is steeper?" (Line B.) "Which has a larger k?" (Line B, k = 5.) "What do you notice?" (A larger k means a steeper line — the y-values grow faster.)

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Graphing from a table**
A dog walker charges $8 per dog.

| Dogs (d) | Earnings ($) (E) |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 8 |
| 2 | 16 |
| 3 | 24 |
| 5 | 40 |

Plot the points: (0, 0), (1, 8), (2, 16), (3, 24), (5, 40). Connect with a straight line through the origin.

k = 8 (read from the point (1, 8), or compute 16/2 = 8, 24/3 = 8, 40/5 = 8).
Equation: E = 8d.

**Example B — Finding k from a graph**
A graph of a proportional relationship passes through (0, 0) and (6, 15).

k = y / x = 15 / 6 = 2.5.
Equation: y = 2.5x.

Check: when x = 6, y = 2.5 x 6 = 15. Correct.

**Example C — Interpreting a point on the graph**
The graph of a proportional relationship between hours and miles passes through the point (3, 135).

What does this point mean? "In 3 hours, the car travels 135 miles."
What is k? k = 135 / 3 = 45 miles per hour.
What does the point (1, 45) mean? "In 1 hour, the car travels 45 miles."
What does the origin (0, 0) mean? "At 0 hours, the car has traveled 0 miles."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Plot the proportional relationship y = 4x on graph paper. Use x-values 0, 1, 2, 3, and 5. Draw the line.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Calculate y for each x. Does your line pass through the origin? Is it straight?"

2. A graph of a proportional relationship passes through (0, 0) and (4, 22). What is k? Write the equation.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Use k = y/x with the point (4, 22)."

3. The graph below shows two proportional relationships. Line A passes through (3, 12). Line B passes through (3, 9). Which line is steeper? Which has a larger k?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find k for each by dividing y by x. The larger k means a steeper line."

4. A proportional graph passes through (5, 17.50). The context is the cost of movie tickets. What does the point (5, 17.50) mean? What is the cost of 1 ticket?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Translate: 5 tickets cost $17.50. To find the cost of 1, divide."

5. Does the graph of y = 2x + 1 pass through the origin? Is this a proportional relationship? Explain.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Substitute x = 0. What is y? Is (0, 0) on the graph?"

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-Level
1. Complete the table for y = 6x and plot the points on graph paper. Draw the line.

   | x | y |
   |---|---|
   | 0 | ? |
   | 1 | ? |
   | 3 | ? |
   | 5 | ? |

2. A proportional graph passes through (0, 0) and (8, 36). Find k and write the equation.

3. A graph shows the relationship between gallons of gas and miles driven. The point (4, 112) is on the line. How many miles per gallon does the car get? How far can the car travel on 10 gallons?

4. True or false: "If a graph is a straight line, then the relationship is proportional." Explain.

### Stretch
5. Two pizza shops have proportional pricing. Shop A's graph passes through (3, 22.50). Shop B's graph passes through (5, 35). Which shop charges more per pizza?
6. Graph y = 2.5x and y = 4x on the same coordinate plane (use x = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4). Which line is steeper? At what x-value is the difference between the two y-values equal to 6?
7. A proportional graph passes through (2, 7) and (6, 21). Another point on the line is (k, 35). Find k.

### Challenge
8. The graph of a proportional relationship passes through (1/2, 3). Find k and the equation. What is y when x = 5/4?
9. Create a context (word problem) for a proportional relationship where k = 15. Draw the graph, label at least 4 points, and write the equation.

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: writing proportional equations from Lesson 4)*
10. Write a proportional equation for: "A machine fills 45 bottles per minute."
11. Using the equation from Problem 10, how many minutes to fill 1,350 bottles?

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Plot the proportional relationship y = 3.5x using x-values 0, 2, 4, and 6 on graph paper. Does your graph pass through the origin?

**Problem 2:** A proportional graph passes through (0, 0) and (5, 40). What is the constant of proportionality? Write the equation.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Provide a pre-made coordinate grid with points already plotted for y = 2x. Have the student connect the dots, identify the origin, and compute k from two points. This isolates the concept from the mechanics of plotting.
- If the student can plot but cannot find k from the graph, return to Lesson 3's table method — have them read values from the graph into a table, then compute y/x.
- Practice with 2-3 additional proportional graphs, each time asking: "Is it straight? Does it pass through (0, 0)? What is k?"

### If Mastery Is Met
- Assign the Challenge problems if not yet completed.
- Preview: "In the next lesson, we will combine tables, graphs, and equations to decide whether a relationship is proportional or not."

## Extension (Optional)
- **Speed Comparison Graph:** Graph the relationship between time and distance for three different speeds (e.g., walking at 3 mph, biking at 12 mph, driving at 60 mph) on the same coordinate plane. Discuss how k affects the steepness.
- **Real Data:** Have the student measure a real proportional relationship (e.g., pour measured amounts of water into a container and mark the height) and graph the results. Is the graph approximately a straight line through the origin?

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Not including (0, 0).** Students sometimes plot only the data points from a table and forget that proportional graphs must pass through the origin. Ask: "What happens when x = 0?"
- **Uneven scaling on axes.** Students may not space tick marks evenly, resulting in a curved-looking line. Remind them to count carefully along each axis using consistent intervals.
- **Confusing slope with y-intercept.** At this level, the y-intercept is always 0 for proportional relationships. If a student says the y-intercept is k, redirect: "k is the steepness (slope), not where the line starts."

### Pacing Advice
- Allow extra time for the graphing mechanics. Some students may need a refresher on setting up axes and choosing a scale.
- If time is limited, have the student graph one relationship fully and read k from a pre-made graph for the other examples.
- The comparison of two proportional graphs (Step 4 and Guided Practice Problem 3) is especially powerful for building intuition about k. Prioritize these if you must skip something else.
