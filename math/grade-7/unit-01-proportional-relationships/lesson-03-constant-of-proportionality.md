# Math — Grade 7 — Unit 1, Lesson 3

## Topic
Constant of Proportionality

## Learning Objectives
- Define the constant of proportionality (k) as the constant ratio y/x in a proportional relationship.
- Find k from tables, equations, and word problems.
- Interpret the meaning of k in real-world contexts.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to determine whether a relationship is proportional by computing y/x for each row of a table (Lesson 2).
- Fluency with computing unit rates, including those involving fractions (Lesson 1).
- Understanding that a proportional relationship has a constant ratio across all pairs of values (Lesson 2).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.RP.2b — Identify the constant of proportionality (unit rate) in tables, graphs, equations, diagrams, and verbal descriptions of proportional relationships.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Reinforce the concept of a constant ratio in a proportional table.

1. Display this table:

   | Hours worked | Pay ($) |
   |---|---|
   | 2 | 24 |
   | 5 | 60 |
   | 8 | 96 |

2. Ask: "Is this relationship proportional? How do you know?" (Yes — 24/2 = 12, 60/5 = 12, 96/8 = 12.)
3. Ask: "What does the number 12 represent in this situation?" (The pay per hour — $12 per hour.)
4. Say: "That constant value — 12 — has a special name. Today we learn what it is called and how to find it in different situations."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Constant of proportionality (k)** — the constant ratio y/x in a proportional relationship. It is the same as the unit rate. In the equation y = kx, k is the number you multiply x by to get y.
- **Unit rate** — another name for the constant of proportionality when the context involves rates with units (e.g., dollars per hour, miles per gallon).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a concrete model.**
Return to the warm-up example. "When we computed 24/2, 60/5, and 96/8, we got 12 every time. We call this number the constant of proportionality. We use the letter k to represent it."

Write on the board: k = y / x.

"In this problem, k = 12, and it means the worker earns $12 for every 1 hour of work."

**Step 2 — Finding k from a table.**
Present a new table:

| Gallons of gas | Miles driven |
|---|---|
| 3 | 84 |
| 5 | 140 |
| 7 | 196 |

"To find k, pick any row and divide y by x: 84 / 3 = 28. Check with another row: 140 / 5 = 28. And another: 196 / 7 = 28. So k = 28 miles per gallon."

Emphasize: always check more than one row to confirm the relationship is proportional.

**Step 3 — Finding k from a word problem.**
"A recipe uses 3 cups of flour for every 2 cups of sugar." Ask: "What is the constant of proportionality for flour per cup of sugar?"

k = flour / sugar = 3/2 = 1.5 cups of flour per cup of sugar.

Note: k depends on which quantity you put in the numerator. If we asked for cups of sugar per cup of flour, k would be 2/3.

**Step 4 — Interpreting k in context.**
"The constant of proportionality always answers the question: how much of y do I get for 1 unit of x?"

Examples:
- k = 55 miles per hour means you travel 55 miles for every 1 hour.
- k = $3.50 per pound means each pound costs $3.50.
- k = 1.5 cups of flour per cup of sugar means you use 1.5 cups of flour for every 1 cup of sugar.

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Finding k from a table**

| Shirts | Cost ($) |
|---|---|
| 2 | 17 |
| 5 | 42.50 |
| 8 | 68 |

k = 17 / 2 = 8.50
Check: 42.50 / 5 = 8.50. Check: 68 / 8 = 8.50.
k = $8.50 per shirt. Each shirt costs $8.50.

**Example B — Finding k from a word problem**
A car uses 2/5 of a gallon of gas to travel 6 miles. What is the constant of proportionality in miles per gallon?

k = miles / gallons = 6 / (2/5) = 6 x (5/2) = 30/2 = 15 miles per gallon.

The car travels 15 miles for every 1 gallon of gas.

**Example C — Interpreting k**
The constant of proportionality for a water hose is k = 3.5 gallons per minute.

This means:
- In 1 minute, the hose fills 3.5 gallons.
- In 2 minutes, the hose fills 7 gallons.
- In 10 minutes, the hose fills 35 gallons.
- To find the gallons for any number of minutes, multiply minutes by 3.5.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Find the constant of proportionality.

   | Pounds of apples | Cost ($) |
   |---|---|
   | 3 | 5.25 |
   | 6 | 10.50 |
   | 10 | 17.50 |

   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Divide cost by pounds for each row. What is k? What does it mean?"

2. A school bus travels 90 miles in 2 hours. What is the constant of proportionality in miles per hour?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Which quantity is y (what you want per unit) and which is x?"

3. A painter uses 3/4 of a gallon of paint for every 1/2 of a room. What is the constant of proportionality in gallons per room?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Set up the division: (3/4) divided by (1/2). Remember to multiply by the reciprocal."

4. The constant of proportionality for a cookie recipe is k = 2.5 cups of flour per batch. How much flour is needed for 4 batches? For 7 batches?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "If k = 2.5, what do you multiply by the number of batches?"

5. Two tables are shown below. Both are proportional. Find k for each. Which relationship grows faster?

   Table P: (2, 7), (4, 14), (6, 21)
   Table Q: (3, 8.25), (5, 13.75), (9, 24.75)

   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find k for each table. Which k is larger?"

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-Level
1. Find k for the table:

   | x | y |
   |---|---|
   | 4 | 10 |
   | 8 | 20 |
   | 12 | 30 |

2. A machine produces 42 parts in 6 hours. What is the constant of proportionality in parts per hour?

3. The constant of proportionality for a jogger is k = 5.5 miles per hour. How far does the jogger run in 3 hours?

4. A recipe uses 4 tablespoons of cocoa for every 3 cups of milk. What is k in tablespoons of cocoa per cup of milk?

### Stretch
5. A faucet fills 3/8 of a tub in 1/4 of an hour. What is the constant of proportionality in tubs per hour?
6. A store sells trail mix for $4.80 per pound. Another store sells the same trail mix: 2.5 pounds for $11.50. Find k for each store and determine which is the better deal.
7. The table below shows the distance a drone flies over time. Find k, then use it to predict the distance after 12 minutes.

   | Time (min) | Distance (m) |
   |---|---|
   | 2 | 150 |
   | 5 | 375 |
   | 8 | 600 |

### Challenge
8. In a proportional relationship, when x = 2/3, y = 5/6. Find k. Then find y when x = 4.
9. The constant of proportionality between pounds of steel and cost is $1.85/lb. The constant of proportionality between pounds of steel and weight of a beam is 12.5 lb/beam. How much does the steel for 6 beams cost?

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: computing unit rates with fractions from Lesson 1)*
10. Find the unit rate: a snail travels 2/3 of a foot in 1/5 of a minute.
11. Find the unit rate: a machine packages 5/4 pounds of rice in 1/3 of a minute.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Find the constant of proportionality.

| Yards of fabric | Cost ($) |
|---|---|
| 3 | 13.50 |
| 5 | 22.50 |
| 8 | 36 |

What does k represent in this context?

**Problem 2:** A cyclist rides 7/8 of a mile in 1/4 of an hour. What is the constant of proportionality in miles per hour?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Revisit the table approach with whole-number examples only. Have the student practice dividing y by x for 3-4 simple tables (e.g., items and cost at $2 each) until the routine of "divide y by x" becomes automatic.
- If the struggle is with fractional rates, review fraction division separately before combining it with the concept of k.
- Provide a sentence frame: "k = ___. This means for every 1 ___, there are ___ ___."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Assign the Challenge problems if not yet completed.
- Preview: "In the next lesson, we write the equation y = kx and use it to solve problems."

## Extension (Optional)
- **Constant of Proportionality Hunt:** Give the student 3-4 different real-world scenarios (a map scale, a recipe, a speed limit sign, a price tag) and ask them to identify k in each context and explain what it means.
- **Two Perspectives:** "A car gets 30 miles per gallon. What is k if we think of gallons per mile instead? How are the two values of k related?" (k = 30 miles per gallon or k = 1/30 gallon per mile — they are reciprocals.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Computing x/y instead of y/x.** Students may divide in the wrong order. Anchor to the phrase "y per 1 x" — y is on top, x is on the bottom. Use the unit rate context: "We want cost per pound, so cost goes on top."
- **Forgetting to check multiple rows.** A student may compute k from one row and assume the table is proportional. Always require at least two checks.
- **Misinterpreting k.** The student finds k = 8.50 but cannot explain what it means. Practice with the sentence frame: "For every 1 [x-unit], there are [k] [y-units]."

### Pacing Advice
- Steps 1-2 should move quickly if Lesson 2 was solid. Spend more time on Steps 3-4, which introduce fractional rates and interpretation.
- Guided Practice Problems 3 and 5 are the most challenging. If time is short, prioritize these over the simpler ones.
- If the student is ready, this lesson can flow directly into Lesson 4 (writing equations) in the same session.
