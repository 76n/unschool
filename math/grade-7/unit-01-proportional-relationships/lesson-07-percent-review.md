# Math — Grade 7 — Unit 1, Lesson 7

## Topic
Percent Review

## Learning Objectives
- Define percent as "per hundred" and represent percents using 100-grids.
- Convert fluently between fractions, decimals, and percents.
- Estimate benchmark percents (10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%) of a whole.

## Prerequisites
- Understanding that a fraction represents parts of a whole.
- Ability to divide whole numbers and decimals.
- Familiarity with equivalent fractions and simplifying fractions.
- Basic understanding of percent as "out of 100" (Grade 6).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Printed 100-grids (10 x 10 grids) — at least 3 per student.
- Colored pencils (for shading grids).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.RP.3 — Use proportional relationships to solve multi-step ratio and percent problems (this lesson provides foundational review for 7.RP.3).

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of percent from Grade 6.

1. Ask: "What does 'percent' mean?" (Per hundred, or out of 100.)
2. Display a 100-grid (10 x 10 square). Shade 25 small squares. Ask: "What percent is shaded?" (25%)
3. Ask: "What fraction is that?" (25/100 = 1/4) "What decimal?" (0.25)
4. Quick challenge: "Without computing, estimate: is 30% of 80 closer to 20 or 30?" (Closer to 24, which is near 20-25. Accept any reasonable estimate.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Percent** — a ratio that compares a number to 100. The symbol % means "per 100." So 45% means 45 out of 100, or 45/100.
- **Benchmark percent** — a commonly used percent that is easy to calculate mentally: 1%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%.
- **Equivalent forms** — a percent, a fraction, and a decimal that all represent the same value (e.g., 25% = 1/4 = 0.25).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a concrete model.**
Hold up (or display) a 100-grid. "This grid has 100 small squares. Each square represents 1% of the whole. If I shade 40 squares, that is 40% of the grid."

Shade 40 squares. Write on the board:
- Percent: 40%
- Fraction: 40/100 = 2/5
- Decimal: 0.40

"These are three ways to write the same amount."

**Step 2 — Converting percent to decimal.**
"To convert a percent to a decimal, divide by 100 (move the decimal point two places to the left)."

- 75% = 75 / 100 = 0.75
- 8% = 8 / 100 = 0.08
- 125% = 125 / 100 = 1.25
- 0.5% = 0.5 / 100 = 0.005

**Step 3 — Converting decimal to percent.**
"To convert a decimal to a percent, multiply by 100 (move the decimal point two places to the right)."

- 0.35 = 0.35 x 100 = 35%
- 0.07 = 7%
- 1.5 = 150%
- 0.004 = 0.4%

**Step 4 — Converting between fractions and percents.**
"Fraction to percent: divide the numerator by the denominator, then multiply by 100."

- 3/5: 3 / 5 = 0.6, and 0.6 x 100 = 60%. So 3/5 = 60%.
- 7/8: 7 / 8 = 0.875, and 0.875 x 100 = 87.5%. So 7/8 = 87.5%.

"Percent to fraction: write the percent over 100, then simplify."

- 60% = 60/100 = 3/5.
- 87.5% = 87.5/100 = 875/1000 = 7/8.

**Step 5 — Benchmark percents.**
Write the benchmark chart on the board:

| Percent | Fraction | Decimal | Mental Math Trick |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1% | 1/100 | 0.01 | Divide by 100 |
| 10% | 1/10 | 0.10 | Divide by 10 |
| 25% | 1/4 | 0.25 | Divide by 4 |
| 50% | 1/2 | 0.50 | Divide by 2 |
| 75% | 3/4 | 0.75 | Multiply 25% by 3 |
| 100% | 1 | 1.00 | The whole thing |

"These benchmarks help you estimate and check your work."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Convert 36% to a fraction and a decimal**
Decimal: 36% = 36/100 = 0.36.
Fraction: 36/100 = 9/25 (divide numerator and denominator by 4).

**Example B — Convert 5/8 to a percent**
Divide: 5 / 8 = 0.625.
Multiply by 100: 0.625 x 100 = 62.5%.
So 5/8 = 62.5%.

**Example C — Shade a 100-grid to show 65%**
Shade 65 out of 100 squares (6 full rows of 10 plus 5 more squares).
Fraction: 65/100 = 13/20. Decimal: 0.65.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Convert 85% to a decimal and a fraction (simplified).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "For the decimal, divide by 100. For the fraction, write 85/100 and simplify."

2. Convert 0.42 to a percent and a fraction (simplified).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "For the percent, multiply by 100. For the fraction, think: 0.42 = 42/100."

3. Convert 3/4 to a decimal and a percent.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Divide 3 by 4 first."

4. Shade a 100-grid to show 30%. Then write 30% as a fraction and a decimal.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "How many squares out of 100 is 30%?"

5. Without computing, estimate which is greater: 40% of 90 or 50% of 70. Then calculate to check.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Use benchmarks. 40% is close to half of 90, and 50% is exactly half of 70."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-Level
1. Convert to a decimal: (a) 72% (b) 5% (c) 130%.
2. Convert to a percent: (a) 0.68 (b) 0.03 (c) 1.4.
3. Convert 7/20 to a percent and a decimal.
4. Convert 45% to a fraction in simplest form.

### Stretch
5. Order from least to greatest: 0.6, 58%, 3/5.
6. A student says "0.5% is the same as one-half." Is this correct? Explain.
7. Convert 2/3 to a percent. Is the percent exact or a repeating decimal? Round to the nearest tenth of a percent.

### Challenge
8. Find a fraction with a denominator of 8 that is between 30% and 40%. Convert it to a percent to verify.
9. A store advertises "Save 33 1/3%." Express this percent as a fraction in simplest form. Why might the store use this odd-looking percent?

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: proportional relationships from Lessons 2-6)*
10. Is the equation y = 0.4x proportional? If so, what is k?
11. A proportional table has the values (3, 21) and (7, 49). Find k and determine the y-value when x = 10.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Convert 7/8 to a decimal and a percent.

**Problem 2:** Convert 0.035 to a percent and a fraction in simplest form.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Focus on one conversion direction at a time. Start with percent to decimal (the simplest — just divide by 100). Once that is automatic, add decimal to percent. Then tackle fraction conversions.
- Use the 100-grid as a visual anchor for every conversion. If the student cannot convert 35% to a decimal, have them shade 35 squares and say "35 out of 100 is 35 hundredths, which is 0.35."
- Provide a conversion flowchart: Percent -> Decimal (divide by 100). Decimal -> Percent (multiply by 100). Fraction -> Decimal (divide top by bottom). Decimal -> Fraction (write over the place value, simplify).

### If Mastery Is Met
- Assign the Challenge problems if not yet completed.
- Preview: "Next lesson, we use percents to find the percent of a number — like 25% of 80."

## Extension (Optional)
- **Percent Scavenger Hunt:** Have the student find 5 examples of percents used in everyday life (nutrition labels, sports statistics, weather forecasts, sale signs). For each, convert the percent to a fraction and a decimal.
- **Fraction-Decimal-Percent Memory Game:** Create pairs of cards with matching values in different forms (e.g., one card says "3/8" and its match says "37.5%"). Play a matching game.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Moving the decimal the wrong direction.** When converting 8% to a decimal, a student writes 80 instead of 0.08. Remind: percent to decimal = divide by 100 = decimal moves LEFT two places. A smaller number makes sense (8% is a small part of the whole).
- **Forgetting to simplify fractions.** 40% = 40/100 — the student stops here instead of simplifying to 2/5. Always ask: "Can both the top and bottom be divided by the same number?"
- **Confusing 0.5% with 50%.** Emphasize: 0.5% means "half of one percent," which is extremely small (0.005 as a decimal). 50% is "fifty percent," which is one-half.

### Pacing Advice
- This is a review lesson from Grade 6 content. If the student demonstrates quick mastery in the Warm-Up and Guided Practice, condense the lesson and begin Lesson 8 in the same session.
- If conversions are shaky, slow down. The next three lessons (8-10) all depend on percent fluency.
- The benchmark chart in Step 5 is a powerful reference. Consider having the student copy it onto an index card for use in later lessons.
