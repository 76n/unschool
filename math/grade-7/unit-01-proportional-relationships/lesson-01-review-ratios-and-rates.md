# Math — Grade 7 — Unit 1, Lesson 1

## Topic
Review: Ratios and Rates

## Learning Objectives
- Write ratios in three forms (a:b, a/b, and "a to b") and identify part-to-part and part-to-whole ratios.
- Generate equivalent ratios by multiplying or dividing both terms.
- Compute unit rates from ratio relationships, including rates with fractional values.

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of multiplication and division with whole numbers and decimals.
- Familiarity with ratios as comparisons of two quantities (Grade 6).
- Ability to simplify fractions by dividing numerator and denominator by a common factor.
- Experience using ratio tables (Grade 6).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.RP.1 — Compute unit rates associated with ratios of fractions, including ratios of lengths, areas, and other quantities measured in like or different units.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of ratios from Grade 6.

1. Display: "In a bag of trail mix, there are 8 almonds and 12 raisins." Ask the student to write the ratio of almonds to raisins in three forms (8:12, 8/12, and "8 to 12").
2. Ask: "Can you simplify this ratio?" (2:3)
3. Ask: "What is the ratio of almonds to total pieces of trail mix?" (8:20, or simplified 2:5)
4. Quick check: "What is the difference between a part-to-part ratio and a part-to-whole ratio?" (Part-to-part compares one part to another, like almonds to raisins. Part-to-whole compares one part to the total.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Ratio** — a comparison of two quantities by division (e.g., 3:5 means "3 for every 5").
- **Equivalent ratios** — ratios that express the same relationship (e.g., 2:3 and 4:6).
- **Rate** — a ratio that compares two quantities measured in different units (e.g., miles per hour, dollars per pound).
- **Unit rate** — a rate expressed as a quantity per 1 unit of another quantity (e.g., $4.50 per pound, 60 miles per hour).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a concrete model.**
Present a scenario: "A lemonade recipe calls for 3 cups of water for every 1 cup of lemon juice." Write the ratio 3:1 on the board. Build a ratio table:

| Water (cups) | Lemon juice (cups) |
|---|---|
| 3 | 1 |
| 6 | 2 |
| 9 | 3 |
| 12 | 4 |

Ask: "How did I get from one row to the next?" (Multiplied both quantities by the same number.) "Are all of these ratios equivalent? How do you know?" (Yes — 3/1 = 6/2 = 9/3 = 12/4.)

**Step 2 — Review finding equivalent ratios.**
"To find equivalent ratios, multiply or divide both terms by the same nonzero number." Show:
- 3:1 multiply both by 4 gives 12:4.
- 12:4 divide both by 4 gives 3:1.

Emphasize: whatever you do to one term, you must do to the other.

**Step 3 — Introduce rates and unit rates.**
"A rate is a special kind of ratio where the two quantities have different units." Write: "$8.40 for 3 pounds of apples."
"A unit rate tells us how much for 1 unit. To find the unit rate, divide both quantities by the second term."
$8.40 / 3 = $2.80 per 1 pound.
The unit rate is $2.80 per pound.

**Step 4 — Unit rates involving fractions (Grade 7 extension).**
"In Grade 7, unit rates can involve fractions." Write: "A runner covers 3/4 of a mile in 1/6 of an hour."
To find the unit rate (miles per hour), compute (3/4) / (1/6) = (3/4) x (6/1) = 18/4 = 4 1/2 miles per hour.
Review: dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Writing equivalent ratios**
Write three ratios equivalent to 5:8.

Multiply both terms by 2: 10:16.
Multiply both terms by 3: 15:24.
Multiply both terms by 5: 25:40.

Equivalent ratios: 10:16, 15:24, 25:40.

**Example B — Finding a unit rate (whole numbers)**
A car travels 225 miles on 9 gallons of gas. What is the unit rate in miles per gallon?

Unit rate = 225 / 9 = 25 miles per gallon.

**Example C — Finding a unit rate (fractions)**
A recipe uses 2/3 cup of sugar for every 1/2 cup of butter. How many cups of sugar are used per 1 cup of butter?

Unit rate = (2/3) / (1/2) = (2/3) x (2/1) = 4/3 = 1 1/3 cups of sugar per cup of butter.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Write two ratios equivalent to 6:10.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What can you multiply or divide both terms by?"

2. A store sells 5 notebooks for $8.75. What is the unit price per notebook?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Which quantity should be 1? What do you divide?"

3. A printer prints 48 pages in 4 minutes. What is the unit rate in pages per minute?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What operation finds the 'per 1 minute' rate?"

4. A garden snail travels 1/3 of a foot in 1/4 of a minute. What is its speed in feet per minute?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Remember — dividing by 1/4 is the same as multiplying by what?"

5. In a class of 30 students, 18 are wearing sneakers. Write the ratio of students wearing sneakers to students not wearing sneakers in simplest form.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "How many students are NOT wearing sneakers? Now simplify the ratio."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-Level
1. Write three equivalent ratios for 4:7.
2. A 6-pack of juice boxes costs $4.50. What is the cost per juice box?
3. A cyclist rides 36 miles in 3 hours. What is the unit rate in miles per hour?
4. Simplify the ratio 24:36.

### Stretch
5. A recipe calls for 3/4 cup of oats for every 1/2 cup of milk. How many cups of oats are needed per cup of milk?
6. Who has the better deal: Store A selling 8 pencils for $3.60, or Store B selling 12 pencils for $5.16?
7. Marcus reads 42 pages in 1.5 hours. Leah reads 70 pages in 2.5 hours. Who reads faster, and by how much?

### Challenge
8. A machine fills 5/6 of a bottle every 2/3 of a second. How many bottles does it fill per second? How many per minute?
9. The ratio of boys to girls in a school is 7:8. If there are 360 students in total, how many boys are there and how many girls?

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: fraction division from Grade 6)*
10. Compute: 4/5 / 2/3.
11. Compute: 7 / (1/4).

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** A car uses 4 gallons of gas to travel 100 miles. What is the unit rate in miles per gallon?

**Problem 2:** A painter uses 3/4 of a gallon of paint every 1/2 hour. How many gallons of paint does the painter use per hour?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to ratio tables with simple whole-number rates (e.g., $3 per pound). Have the student practice finding unit rates from tables before moving to fractional rates.
- Provide a step-by-step template: (1) Identify the two quantities with units. (2) Write as a fraction. (3) Divide to get a denominator of 1.
- If the student struggles specifically with fraction division, review the "multiply by the reciprocal" rule with 3-4 additional examples before re-attempting the Exit Ticket.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Assign the Challenge problems if not yet completed.
- Preview: "In the next lesson, we will explore what makes a relationship proportional — and how to tell when it is not."

## Extension (Optional)
- **Comparison Shopping Activity:** Give the student a list of 4-5 grocery items with different package sizes and prices. Have them calculate the unit price for each and determine the best deal.
- **Speed Challenge:** "A cheetah runs 1/4 mile in 15 seconds. A car drives 1/2 mile in 25 seconds. Express both speeds in miles per hour and compare."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Dividing in the wrong order for unit rates.** For example, computing 9 / 225 instead of 225 / 9. Remind the student: "The unit rate is the quantity you want per 1 of the other quantity. Ask yourself: miles per gallon means miles divided by gallons."
- **Errors with fraction division.** Students may forget to flip the second fraction when dividing. Reinforce the rule: dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal. Write out the step: (3/4) / (1/2) = (3/4) x (2/1).
- **Not simplifying ratios.** The student computes 18:30 and leaves it without simplifying to 3:5. Remind them to check for a common factor.

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson is a review of Grade 6 content. If the student demonstrates quick mastery during Guided Practice, condense the lesson and move into Lesson 2.
- If the student is strong with whole-number rates but struggles with fractional unit rates (Step 4), spend extra time on that skill — it is the new Grade 7 extension and will reappear throughout the unit.
- The Interleaved Review problems on fraction division directly support the fractional unit rate skill. Use them diagnostically.
