## Section A: Ratios, Rates, and Unit Rates (3 items)

**A1.** A runner completes 6 laps in 15 minutes. What is the unit rate in minutes per lap?

(15 / 6 = 2.5 minutes per lap)

**A2.** A recipe calls for 3/4 cup of oil for every 1/3 cup of vinegar. What is the unit rate of oil per cup of vinegar?

Show your work.

((3/4) / (1/3) = (3/4) x (3/1) = 9/4 = 2 1/4 cups of oil per cup of vinegar)

**A3.** Is the following table proportional? Explain your reasoning.

| x | y |
|---|---|
| 3 | 12 |
| 5 | 20 |
| 8 | 32 |
| 10 | 40 |

(12/3 = 4, 20/5 = 4, 32/8 = 4, 40/10 = 4. Yes, proportional — the ratio y/x is always 4.)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correct answer with ratios computed and explanation | 1 point |
| Correct answer but no ratios shown or incomplete explanation | 0.5 points |

## Section B: Constant of Proportionality and Proportional Equations (3 items)

**B1.** Find the constant of proportionality for the table below. Then write the equation.

| Hours (h) | Distance (miles) (d) |
|---|---|
| 2 | 110 |
| 5 | 275 |
| 7 | 385 |

(k = 110/2 = 55. Check: 275/5 = 55, 385/7 = 55. Equation: d = 55h.)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correct k and correct equation | 1 point |
| Correct k but incorrect or missing equation | 0.5 points |

**B2.** A store charges $4.25 per pound of deli meat. Write a proportional equation for the total cost (C) based on the number of pounds (p). How much does 6 pounds cost?

(C = 4.25p. For 6 pounds: C = 4.25 x 6 = $25.50.)

**B3.** Using the equation y = 3.5x, find x when y = 24.5.

(24.5 = 3.5x. x = 24.5 / 3.5 = 7.)

## Section C: Graphing and Identifying Proportional Relationships (3 items)

**C1.** A proportional graph passes through the origin and the point (4, 18). What is the constant of proportionality? Write the equation.

(k = 18/4 = 4.5. Equation: y = 4.5x.)

**C2.** Tell whether each representation shows a proportional relationship. Write "proportional" or "not proportional" and explain briefly.

(a) y = 6x + 2

(b) A straight line through (0, 0) and (5, 35)

((a) Not proportional — when x = 0, y = 2 (not 0). The equation has the form y = kx + b where b is not 0.)

((b) Proportional — straight line through the origin. k = 35/5 = 7.)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Both parts correct with explanations | 1 point |
| One part correct with explanation | 0.5 points |

**C3.** Complete the table for the equation y = 7x. Then plot the points on graph paper and draw the line.

| x | y |
|---|---|
| 0 | ? |
| 1 | ? |
| 3 | ? |
| 5 | ? |

(Table: (0, 0), (1, 7), (3, 21), (5, 35). Points should form a straight line through the origin.)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correct table AND correct graph (straight line through origin) | 1 point |
| Correct table but graph has errors (not through origin, not straight, or points misplotted) | 0.5 points |

## Section D: Percent Conversions and Percent of a Number (3 items)

**D1.** Convert the following:

(a) 7/8 to a percent.

(b) 0.045 to a percent.

(c) 160% to a decimal.

((a) 7/8 = 0.875 = 87.5%. (b) 0.045 = 4.5%. (c) 160% = 1.60.)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| All three correct | 1 point |
| Two correct | 0.5 points |

**D2.** Find 35% of 180. Show your work.

(0.35 x 180 = 63.)

**D3.** A school has 520 students. 65% of them participate in at least one extracurricular activity. How many students participate?

(0.65 x 520 = 338 students.)

## Section E: Percent Increase/Decrease and Multi-Step Percent Problems (4 items)

**E1.** A jacket originally costs $75. It is on sale for 20% off. What is the sale price?

(Discount: 0.20 x 75 = $15. Sale price: 75 - 15 = $60.)

**E2.** A town's population grew from 6,000 to 6,900. What is the percent increase?

Show your work.

(Change: 6,900 - 6,000 = 900. Percent increase: (900 / 6,000) x 100 = 15%.)

**E3.** A dinner bill is $55. Tax is 7% and you leave a 20% tip on the pre-tax bill. What is the total amount paid?

Show your work.

(Tax: 0.07 x 55 = $3.85. Tip: 0.20 x 55 = $11.00. Total: 55 + 3.85 + 11.00 = $69.85.)

**E4.** You deposit $900 in a savings account that earns 4% annual simple interest. How much interest do you earn in 3 years? What is the total amount in the account after 3 years?

Show your work.

(I = Prt = 900 x 0.04 x 3 = $108. Total: 900 + 108 = $1,008.)

### Section-Specific Reteaching Guide

| If struggling in... | Focus on... |
|---|---|
| Section A | Return to Lesson 1. Practice computing unit rates with whole numbers first, then fractions. Use ratio tables to organize thinking. |
| Section B | Return to Lessons 3-4. The process of finding k (divide y by x) must be automatic. Practice writing equations from tables with at least 5 additional examples. |
| Section C | Return to Lessons 5-6. If graphing mechanics are the issue, practice plotting points on a coordinate plane. If identification is the issue, review the "three tests" (table: constant ratio; graph: straight line through origin; equation: y = kx form). |
| Section D | Return to Lessons 7-8. Practice the conversion flowchart (percent to decimal: divide by 100; decimal to percent: multiply by 100; fraction to decimal: divide; fraction to percent: divide then multiply by 100). Use 100-grids for visual support. |
| Section E | Return to Lessons 9-10. If percent change is the issue, practice the formula with 3-4 simple examples. If multi-step problems are the issue, break them into explicit steps (template: list the bill, compute tax, compute tip, add). |

## Error Analysis Guide

| Error Pattern | Example | Likely Cause | Intervention |
|--------------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Divides x by y instead of y by x for unit rates | Computes 2/110 = 0.018 instead of 110/2 = 55 | Confusion about which quantity goes in the numerator | Anchor to the context: "We want MILES per HOUR — miles on top, hours on bottom." |
| Declares a table proportional after checking only one ratio | Checks 12/3 = 4 and stops | Does not understand that ALL rows must be checked | Require checking every row. Provide a table where the first row matches but later rows do not. |
| Writes y = k + x instead of y = kx | Writes d = 55 + h instead of d = 55h | Confuses additive and multiplicative relationships | Return to the meaning of k: "For every 1 hour, the car travels 55 miles. That's multiplication, not addition." |
| Converts percent to decimal incorrectly | Writes 7% as 0.7 instead of 0.07 | Moves decimal one place instead of two | Return to the definition: 7% means 7 per 100 = 7/100 = 0.07. Have the student estimate: 7% of 100 should be 7, not 70. |
| Computes percent change using the new value as the base | (900/6900) x 100 instead of (900/6000) x 100 | Divides by the wrong value | Emphasize: "Always divide by the ORIGINAL (starting) value." Highlight the word "original" in the problem. |
| Forgets to add/subtract after finding the percent amount | Finds 20% of 75 = 15 and writes $15 as the sale price | Confuses the discount amount with the final price | Ask: "Is $15 the discount or the sale price? What do you do with the discount?" |

## Facilitator Observations

- Can the student find unit rates quickly and accurately, including those involving fractions?
- Does the student automatically check whether a graph passes through the origin, or do they need reminding?
- When writing equations, does the student consistently use y = kx form, or do they sometimes add constants?
- Does the student use mental math strategies (benchmarks like 10%, 50%) or rely entirely on decimal multiplication?
- In multi-step problems, does the student organize their work into clear steps, or do they try to do everything at once and make errors?
