# Math — Grade 7 — Unit 1, Lesson 10

## Topic
Multi-Step Percent Problems

## Learning Objectives
- Solve problems involving tax and tip (two percent calculations applied to the same base).
- Solve problems involving successive percent changes (e.g., markup then discount).
- Calculate simple interest using the formula I = Prt.
- Set up and solve multi-step word problems that combine percent skills from Lessons 7-9.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to find the percent of a number (Lesson 8).
- Ability to calculate percent increase and percent decrease (Lesson 9).
- Fluency with decimal multiplication and addition.
- Understanding of the multiplier shortcut for percent change (Lesson 9).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Calculator (recommended for this lesson — multi-step decimal calculations).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.RP.3 — Use proportional relationships to solve multi-step ratio and percent problems. Examples: simple interest, tax, markups and markdowns, gratuities and commissions, fees, percent increase and decrease, percent error.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate skills from Lessons 8-9 that will be combined in today's lesson.

1. Find 8% of $45. ($3.60)
2. Find 20% of $45. ($9.00)

4. Say: "Today we solve problems like this — where we need to apply percents more than once."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Tax** — a percent of the purchase price added by the government. Applied to the price of goods.
- **Tip (gratuity)** — a percent of the bill given to a service worker (e.g., waiter, hairdresser). Usually calculated on the pre-tax amount.
- **Simple interest** — interest earned (or owed) on an original amount (principal) over time. Formula: I = Prt, where P = principal, r = annual interest rate (as a decimal), and t = time in years.
- **Commission** — a percent of sales earned as income by a salesperson.
- **Successive percent changes** — applying one percent change after another (e.g., a markup followed by a discount).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a concrete model: Tax and Tip.**
"You and your family eat at a restaurant. The food bill (before tax and tip) is $65. Tax is 7% and you leave a 20% tip on the pre-tax bill."

Step by step:
- Tax: 0.07 x 65 = $4.55.
- Tip: 0.20 x 65 = $13.00.
- Total: 65 + 4.55 + 13.00 = $82.55.

"Notice: we calculated both tax and tip based on the original $65 bill."

Alternative (combined percent): Tax + tip = 7% + 20% = 27% of $65. 0.27 x 65 = $17.55. Total = 65 + 17.55 = $82.55. Same answer.

"You can add the percents first if they are both based on the same amount."

**Step 2 — Successive percent changes.**
"A store buys a jacket wholesale for $50. They mark it up 80% and then offer a 25% discount."

Step 1 — Markup: 1.80 x 50 = $90 (marked-up price).
Step 2 — Discount: 0.75 x 90 = $67.50 (sale price).

"IMPORTANT: The discount is applied to the marked-up price ($90), not the original ($50). You cannot just combine 80% - 25% = 55% because they are applied to DIFFERENT bases."

**Step 3 — Simple interest.**
"Simple interest is the money earned (or owed) on a principal amount over time."

Formula: I = Prt.
- P = principal (the starting amount of money).
- r = annual interest rate (as a decimal).
- t = time (in years).

"You deposit $500 in a savings account that pays 3% annual simple interest. How much interest do you earn in 2 years?"

I = 500 x 0.03 x 2 = $30.

"The total amount in the account after 2 years is $500 + $30 = $530."

**Step 4 — Commission.**
"A real estate agent earns a 6% commission on every home sale. She sells a house for $275,000. What is her commission?"

Commission = 0.06 x 275,000 = $16,500.

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Tax and tip**
A family's restaurant bill is $84. Tax is 6.5% and the tip is 18% (on the pre-tax bill). What is the total amount paid?

Tax: 0.065 x 84 = $5.46.
Tip: 0.18 x 84 = $15.12.
Total: 84 + 5.46 + 15.12 = $104.58.

**Example B — Successive percent changes**
A smartphone costs $400. The price increases by 10%, then later decreases by 10%. What is the final price?

After 10% increase: 1.10 x 400 = $440.
After 10% decrease: 0.90 x 440 = $396.

The final price ($396) is LESS than the original ($400) by $4. A 10% increase followed by a 10% decrease does not return to the original price.

**Example C — Simple interest**
Maya borrows $1,200 at 5% annual simple interest for 3 years. How much interest does she owe? What is the total amount she must repay?

I = Prt = 1,200 x 0.05 x 3 = $180.
Total repayment = 1,200 + 180 = $1,380.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. A dinner bill is $52. Tax is 8% and the tip is 15% (on the pre-tax bill). What is the total?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find tax and tip separately, then add all three. Or combine the percents: 8% + 15% = 23%."

2. A store buys shoes for $35 wholesale. They mark them up 100%, then offer a 30% discount. What is the sale price?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Apply the markup first (to $35). Then apply the discount to the marked-up price."

3. You deposit $800 in a savings account that earns 4% annual simple interest. How much interest do you earn in 18 months? (Hint: convert 18 months to years.)
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "18 months = 1.5 years. Use I = Prt."

4. A salesperson earns 8% commission on sales. Last month she sold $12,500 worth of products. What was her commission?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Commission = percent x total sales."

5. A jacket costs $120. It is first discounted by 20%, then the discounted price has an additional 10% taken off. Is this the same as a single 30% discount? Calculate both ways to compare.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Do the successive discounts step by step. Then compute a single 30% discount on $120. Compare."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-Level
1. A book costs $22. Sales tax is 7%. What is the total price including tax?
2. A meal costs $38. You leave a 20% tip on the pre-tax amount. Tax is 6%. What is the total you pay?
3. You invest $1,500 at 3.5% annual simple interest for 2 years. How much interest do you earn? What is the total amount?
4. A bicycle is on sale for 15% off the original price of $340. What is the sale price?

### Stretch
5. A real estate agent earns 5% commission. She needs to earn at least $8,000 this month. What is the minimum total home value she must sell?
6. A toy store buys a board game for $12 and marks it up by 150%. During a sale, the store offers 40% off. What is the sale price? How much profit does the store make per game?
7. Derek deposits $2,000 in Account A (4% simple interest) and $2,000 in Account B (5% simple interest). After 3 years, how much more money is in Account B than Account A?

### Challenge
8. After a 15% discount, a customer pays $178.50 for a television. Tax of 8% is then added to the discounted price. What is the total the customer pays? What was the original price before the discount?
9. A population of 20,000 increases by 5% one year, then increases by 8% the next year. What is the population after both increases? What single percent increase applied to 20,000 would give the same result?

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: percent increase and decrease from Lesson 9)*
10. A shirt's price increased from $25 to $31. What is the percent increase?
11. A laptop's price dropped from $800 to $680. What is the percent decrease?

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A meal costs $46. Tax is 7% and you leave a 18% tip (on the pre-tax amount). What is the total cost?

**Problem 2:** You invest $600 at 5% annual simple interest for 4 years. How much interest do you earn?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Break problems into explicit steps. For tax-and-tip problems, provide a template: Step 1 — Write the bill amount. Step 2 — Compute tax. Step 3 — Compute tip. Step 4 — Add all three.
- For simple interest, have the student practice plugging values into I = Prt with 3-4 simple examples (whole-number rates and whole-year durations) before introducing fractional years or rates.
- If the student is overwhelmed by multi-step problems, practice single-percent problems (Lesson 8-9 level) until they are automatic, then layer on the additional step.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Assign the Challenge problems if not yet completed.
- Celebrate: "You have completed all 10 lessons in this unit! Next session, we take the Unit Assessment to see how much you have learned."

## Extension (Optional)
- **Restaurant Receipt Activity:** Provide a mock restaurant receipt with food items totaling a given amount. Have the student compute the tax (at the local rate), add a tip of their choice (15%, 18%, or 20%), and determine the total. Then challenge: "If you have $75 to spend, what is the maximum bill you can afford before tax and tip?"
- **Savings Goal:** "You want to have $1,000 in your savings account after 2 years. The account pays 4% annual simple interest. How much do you need to deposit now?" (Set up: P + P(0.04)(2) = 1000, so P(1.08) = 1000, P = 1000/1.08 ≈ $925.93.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Computing tip on the post-tax amount.** In most real-world situations, tip is calculated on the pre-tax bill. If the student computes tip on the total including tax, the answer will be slightly higher. Clarify what the problem specifies.
- **Adding successive percents directly.** Students may think a 50% markup followed by a 20% discount is the same as a 30% increase. It is not, because the percentages are applied to different base values. Always work step by step.
- **Confusing interest rate and time units.** In I = Prt, r is the ANNUAL rate and t is in YEARS. If the problem gives months, the student must convert (e.g., 6 months = 0.5 years).

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson covers four contexts (tax/tip, successive changes, simple interest, commission). If the student needs two sessions, split as follows: Session 1 — tax/tip and commission. Session 2 — successive percent changes and simple interest.
- Commission problems are the most straightforward (single multiplication). If time is tight, demonstrate briefly and let the student practice independently.
- The Challenge problems (especially Problem 9 on equivalent single percent) are algebraically rich. These are excellent for students who are ready for the connection between proportional reasoning and algebra.

## Answer Key

### Guided Practice
1. Tax: 0.08 x 52 = $4.16. Tip: 0.15 x 52 = $7.80. Total: 52 + 4.16 + 7.80 = $63.96. (Or: 0.23 x 52 = $11.96, total = $63.96.)
2. Markup: 1.00 x 35 = $35 increase (100% markup). Marked-up price: 35 + 35 = $70. Discount: 0.30 x 70 = $21. Sale price: 70 - 21 = $49. (Or: 2.00 x 35 = $70, then 0.70 x 70 = $49.)
3. t = 18/12 = 1.5 years. I = 800 x 0.04 x 1.5 = $48.
4. Commission = 0.08 x 12,500 = $1,000.
5. Successive discounts: 0.80 x 120 = $96. Then 0.90 x 96 = $86.40. Single 30% discount: 0.70 x 120 = $84. They are NOT the same. The successive discounts result in a higher price ($86.40 vs. $84) because the second discount is applied to a smaller base.

### Independent Practice

**On-Level**
1. Tax: 0.07 x 22 = $1.54. Total: 22 + 1.54 = $23.54.
2. Tax: 0.06 x 38 = $2.28. Tip: 0.20 x 38 = $7.60. Total: 38 + 2.28 + 7.60 = $47.88.
3. I = 1,500 x 0.035 x 2 = $105. Total: 1,500 + 105 = $1,605.
4. Discount: 0.15 x 340 = $51. Sale price: 340 - 51 = $289. (Or: 0.85 x 340 = $289.)

**Stretch**
5. 0.05 x Sales = 8,000. Sales = 8,000 / 0.05 = $160,000.
6. Markup: 1.50 x 12 = $18. Marked-up price: 12 + 18 = $30. (Or: 2.50 x 12 = $30.) Sale: 0.40 x 30 = $12 off. Sale price: 30 - 12 = $18. (Or: 0.60 x 30 = $18.) Profit per game: 18 - 12 = $6.
7. Account A interest: 2,000 x 0.04 x 3 = $240. Total A: $2,240. Account B interest: 2,000 x 0.05 x 3 = $300. Total B: $2,300. Difference: 2,300 - 2,240 = $60 more in Account B.

**Challenge**
8. Discounted price = $178.50. Since this is 85% of the original: Original = 178.50 / 0.85 = $210. Tax on discounted price: 0.08 x 178.50 = $14.28. Total paid: 178.50 + 14.28 = $192.78.
9. After 5% increase: 1.05 x 20,000 = 21,000. After 8% increase: 1.08 x 21,000 = 22,680. Combined multiplier: 1.05 x 1.08 = 1.134. So the equivalent single percent increase is 13.4%.
