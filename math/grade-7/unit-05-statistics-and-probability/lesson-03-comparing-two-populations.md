# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 5, Lesson 3

## Topic
Comparing Two Populations

## Learning Objectives
- Compute and compare measures of center (mean, median) for two data sets.
- Compute and compare measures of variability (range, IQR, MAD) for two data sets.
- Use the difference in measures of center relative to the variability to draw comparative inferences.
- Express comparisons using precise language (e.g., "Group A has a higher median, but more spread").

## Prerequisites
- Calculating mean, median, mode (Grade 6).
- Calculating range, IQR, and MAD (Grade 6).
- Drawing inferences from samples (Lesson 2).

## Materials Needed
- Calculator (for mean and MAD)
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice)
- Index cards for Exit Ticket

**Standards:** 7.SP.3 and 7.SP.4 -- Informally assess visual overlap of two data distributions; use measures of center and variability for data from random samples to draw informal comparative inferences.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate Grade 6 fluency with measures of center and variability.

Present two small data sets:

- Set A: 12, 15, 14, 16, 13
- Set B: 8, 22, 10, 18, 12

Ask:
1. "Without calculating, which set do you think has a higher mean? Which has greater spread?"
2. "Calculate the mean and range for each set."
   - Set A: mean = (12+15+14+16+13)/5 = 70/5 = 14; range = 16-12 = 4
   - Set B: mean = (8+22+10+18+12)/5 = 70/5 = 14; range = 22-8 = 14

Say: "Interesting -- both sets have the same mean, but very different spreads. This is why we need BOTH center AND variability to compare two groups fairly."

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary Review

- **Mean** -- average: sum divided by count
- **Median** -- middle value when data is ordered
- **Range** -- max minus min
- **IQR (Interquartile Range)** -- Q3 minus Q1; describes the spread of the middle 50% of data
- **MAD (Mean Absolute Deviation)** -- average distance from the mean; describes typical spread around the center

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Why we need both center and variability for comparisons.**

Say: "When we compare two populations, we want to answer: 'Which group tends to be higher or lower, and how consistently?' A group can have a higher mean but also much more variability -- meaning the group is more unpredictable. We need both pieces of information to make a fair comparison."

**Step 2 -- The comparison framework.**

Introduce the following structure for comparing two populations:

| Measure | Group A | Group B | Who is higher? |
|---------|---------|---------|----------------|
| Mean | | | |
| Median | | | |
| Range | | | |
| IQR | | | |
| MAD | | | |

Say: "Fill in this table for any two data sets, then write a summary that explains what the numbers tell us about the two groups."

**Step 3 -- Expressing difference as a multiple of variability (7.SP.3).**

Say: "One way to judge whether a difference in means is meaningful is to compare it to the variability. If Group A's mean is 10 points higher than Group B's mean, and both groups have a MAD of 2 points, the difference is 5 MADs -- that is a large, meaningful difference. But if the MAD is 15 points, a difference of 10 points is less than one MAD -- that might not be practically significant."

Write: Difference / MAD = number of MADs the centers differ by.

### Worked Example

**Comparing homework time for athletes vs. non-athletes:**

A random sample of 7 student athletes and 7 non-athletes was asked how many hours per week they spend on homework.

Athletes: 4, 6, 5, 7, 3, 5, 6

Non-athletes: 8, 10, 7, 9, 11, 8, 9

Step 1: Compute measures for each group.

**Athletes:**
- Ordered: 3, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 7
- Mean = (3+4+5+5+6+6+7)/7 = 36/7 ≈ 5.1 hours
- Median = 5 (4th value)
- Range = 7-3 = 4
- Q1 = 4, Q3 = 6, IQR = 2
- MAD: Distances from mean (≈5.1): |3-5.1|=2.1, |4-5.1|=1.1, |5-5.1|=0.1, |5-5.1|=0.1, |6-5.1|=0.9, |6-5.1|=0.9, |7-5.1|=1.9. Sum = 7.1. MAD ≈ 7.1/7 ≈ 1.0.

**Non-athletes:**
- Ordered: 7, 8, 8, 9, 9, 10, 11
- Mean = (7+8+8+9+9+10+11)/7 = 62/7 ≈ 8.9 hours
- Median = 9 (4th value)
- Range = 11-7 = 4
- Q1 = 8, Q3 = 10, IQR = 2
- MAD: Distances from mean (≈8.9): |7-8.9|=1.9, |8-8.9|=0.9, |8-8.9|=0.9, |9-8.9|=0.1, |9-8.9|=0.1, |10-8.9|=1.1, |11-8.9|=2.1. Sum = 7.1. MAD ≈ 1.0.

Step 2: Compare.

| Measure | Athletes | Non-athletes |
|---------|----------|--------------|
| Mean | ≈5.1 hr | ≈8.9 hr |
| Median | 5 hr | 9 hr |
| Range | 4 | 4 |
| IQR | 2 | 2 |
| MAD | ≈1.0 | ≈1.0 |

Step 3: Draw the comparative inference.

"Non-athletes have a higher median (9 hours) and mean (8.9 hours) for weekly homework compared to athletes (median: 5 hours, mean: 5.1 hours). The difference in means is about 3.8 hours -- approximately **3.8 MADs** -- which is a very large difference. Both groups have similar variability (MAD ≈ 1 hour, IQR = 2 hours), meaning the groups are clearly separated, not just overlapping at the edges."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Scenario:** A researcher surveyed random samples of 7th and 8th graders about the number of songs they have saved in their music playlist. Here are the data sets.

7th graders: 45, 60, 55, 70, 50, 65, 80, 40, 75, 50

8th graders: 80, 95, 70, 110, 85, 90, 75, 100, 85, 120

Complete the comparison table together:

1. Find the mean for each grade. (Use a calculator.)
   - 7th grade: sum = 590, mean = **59 songs**
   - 8th grade: sum = 910, mean = **91 songs**
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Add all values carefully, then divide by 10."

2. Find the median for each grade. (Order the data first.)
   - 7th grade ordered: 40, 45, 50, 50, 55, 60, 65, 70, 75, 80. Median = (55+60)/2 = **57.5 songs**
   - 8th grade ordered: 70, 75, 80, 85, 85, 90, 95, 100, 110, 120. Median = (85+90)/2 = **87.5 songs**
   - **Facilitator tip:** "With 10 values, the median is the average of the 5th and 6th values."

3. Find the range for each grade.
   - 7th grade: 80 - 40 = **40**
   - 8th grade: 120 - 70 = **50**

4. Find the IQR for each grade.
   - 7th grade: Q1 = (45+50)/2 = 47.5; Q3 = (65+70)/2 = 67.5; IQR = **20**
   - 8th grade: Q1 = (80+85)/2 = 82.5; Q3 = (95+100)/2 = 97.5; IQR = **15**

5. Write a two-sentence comparative inference using the measures above.
   - Sample answer: "8th graders have more songs in their playlists than 7th graders on average (mean of 91 vs. 59 songs; median 87.5 vs. 57.5 songs). The spread is similar between groups (7th grade range = 40, 8th grade range = 50), but the centers differ by about 32 songs, which is larger than either group's IQR, suggesting the difference is meaningful."

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

A middle school tracked the number of points scored per game by two basketball teams over a 6-game sample.

Team Eagles: 58, 62, 54, 70, 65, 71

Team Hawks: 72, 68, 80, 75, 77, 72

1. Calculate the mean for each team.

2. Find the median for each team.

3. Find the range for each team.

4. Write a comparison of the two teams using your results. Which team scores more points per game on average? Which team is more consistent?

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

5. Use the basketball data above. Calculate the IQR for each team.
   - (a) Which team has a smaller IQR? What does that tell us?
   - (b) The difference in means is approximately ___ points. The MAD for Team Eagles is approximately 5.7 points. Express the difference as a multiple of the MAD.

6. A researcher claims: "Team Hawks is definitely better because they have a higher mean." Write a response that agrees with part of her claim but adds important nuance using the variability data.

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

7. Interleaved review (Unit 3 -- Equations): A data set has a mean of 14. The values are: 10, 12, x, 16, 18. Find the missing value x using an equation. Show your work.

8. Two classes took the same math quiz. Here are their scores:

Class 1: 72, 85, 68, 91, 77, 83, 74, 89, 81, 80

Class 2: 55, 98, 60, 95, 82, 88, 50, 99, 65, 58

Both classes have a mean score of approximately 80. Compute the range and IQR for each class. What do these measures tell you that the mean alone does not? Which class has a more consistent set of scores?

## Exit Ticket

**Data:** Two samples of daily steps walked:

- Group A (middle school students): 4000, 5500, 6000, 4500, 5000
- Group B (high school students): 7000, 8500, 9000, 7500, 8000

**Problem 1:** Calculate the mean for each group.

**Problem 2:** Which group has greater variability? How do you know? (Use range or IQR.)

**Problem 3:** Write one sentence comparing the two groups.

**Mastery:** All three parts correctly answered = Mastery. Means correct but comparison incomplete = Review comparison language. Cannot compute means = Review Grade 6 mean calculation before next lesson.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. Eagles: sum = (58+62+54+70+65+71) = 380; mean = 380/6 ≈ **63.3 points per game**. Hawks: sum = (72+68+80+75+77+72) = 444; mean = 444/6 = **74 points per game**.

2. Eagles ordered: 54, 58, 62, 65, 70, 71. Median = (62+65)/2 = **63.5**. Hawks ordered: 68, 72, 72, 75, 77, 80. Median = (72+75)/2 = **73.5**.

3. Eagles: range = 71-54 = **17**. Hawks: range = 80-68 = **12**.

4. Sample comparison: "Team Hawks scores more points per game on average (mean 74 vs. 63.3 points; median 73.5 vs. 63.5). Team Hawks is also more consistent -- their range is 12 points compared to Eagles' range of 17 points. Hawks both scores more and varies less."

5. (a) Eagles ordered: 54, 58, 62, 65, 70, 71. Q1 = (54+58)/2 = 56; Q3 = (70+71)/2 = 70.5. Eagles IQR = 70.5-56 = **14.5**. Hawks ordered: 68, 72, 72, 75, 77, 80. Q1 = (68+72)/2 = 70; Q3 = (77+80)/2 = 78.5. Hawks IQR = 78.5-70 = **8.5**. (b) Hawks have a smaller IQR (8.5 vs. 14.5), meaning the middle 50% of their scores are more tightly packed -- they are more consistent in their performance. (c) Difference in means ≈ 74 - 63.3 = 10.7 points. Expressed as a multiple of MAD (5.7): 10.7/5.7 ≈ **1.9 MADs**.

6. Sample answer: "I agree that Team Hawks has a higher mean score (74 vs. 63.3 points), which suggests they generally score more. However, this is a meaningful advantage -- the difference in means is about 1.9 MADs, which indicates that the teams' typical performances are genuinely different. In addition, Hawks' smaller IQR (8.5 vs. Eagles' 14.5) shows they are more consistent. Both the higher mean and lower variability support the claim that Hawks is a stronger-scoring team."

7. Set up equation: (10+12+x+16+18)/5 = 14. (56+x)/5 = 14. 56+x = 70. x = **14**.

8. Class 1 ordered: 68, 72, 74, 77, 80, 81, 83, 85, 89, 91. Range = 91-68 = **23**. Q1 = (72+74)/2 = 73; Q3 = (83+85)/2 = 84. IQR = 84-73 = **11**. Class 2 ordered: 50, 55, 58, 60, 65, 82, 88, 95, 98, 99. Range = 99-50 = **49**. Q1 = (55+58)/2 = 56.5; Q3 = (88+95)/2 = 91.5. IQR = 91.5-56.5 = **35**. The mean alone (both ≈80) hides the fact that Class 2 has scores spread across a much wider range (50 to 99) while Class 1 is much more tightly grouped (68 to 91). Class 1 has a much more consistent set of scores (range 23, IQR 11 vs. range 49, IQR 35 for Class 2).

### Exit Ticket

1. Group A: mean = (4000+5500+6000+4500+5000)/5 = 25000/5 = **5,000 steps**. Group B: mean = (7000+8500+9000+7500+8000)/5 = 40000/5 = **8,000 steps**.

2. Group A range = 6000-4000 = 2000; Group B range = 9000-7000 = 2000. Both groups have the same range. (If IQR is computed: Group A ordered: 4000, 4500, 5000, 5500, 6000. Q1=4500, Q3=5500, IQR=1000. Group B ordered: 7000, 7500, 8000, 8500, 9000. Q1=7500, Q3=8500, IQR=1000.) Variability is approximately equal for both groups.

3. Sample: "High school students (Group B) walk more steps per day on average (mean 8,000 vs. 5,000 steps), and both groups have similar variability, so the difference in means reflects a genuine gap in activity levels."

## Teacher Notes

- MAD calculation can be time-consuming by hand. Use a calculator for the addition steps. The key insight is the interpretation, not the arithmetic.
- Many students confuse IQR and range. Reinforce: range covers ALL data (min to max); IQR covers only the MIDDLE 50%.
- The expression "difference in means as a multiple of MAD" (7.SP.3) is a conceptual introduction to what becomes the z-score in high school statistics. Do not over-formalize it -- the intuition is: "Is the difference big compared to the typical spread?"
- Lesson 4 extends this comparison work to visual displays (dot plots and box plots). These two lessons pair well. If the student is solid after Lesson 3, move quickly to Lesson 4 for the visual reinforcement.
