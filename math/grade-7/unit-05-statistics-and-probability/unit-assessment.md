## Section 1: Sampling and Inference (12 points)

**1.1.** A student wants to know the favorite music genre of students at her school. She surveys 30 of her own friends. (2 points)

(a) Is this a random sample? Why or why not?

(b) Identify the specific type of bias present in this sampling method.

---

**1.2.** A researcher randomly selects 50 students from a school of 600 and asks how many hours per night they sleep. Results: 28 students report sleeping 8 or more hours per night. (2 points)

(a) What is the sample proportion of students sleeping 8+ hours per night?

(b) Based on this sample, estimate the number of students in the whole school who sleep 8 or more hours per night.

---

**1.3.** A random sample of 40 students from a school of 800 is surveyed about their preferred social media app. The results are shown below.

| App | Students |
|-----|----------|
| Video-sharing | 16 |
| Photo-sharing | 12 |
| Messaging | 8 |
| Gaming | 4 |

Based on the sample, estimate the number of students in the whole school who prefer each app. Show your work. (4 points)

---

**1.4.** Two students each take a different random sample of 30 students from the same school to estimate the proportion of students who have a sibling.

- Student A finds 18 out of 30 have a sibling.
- Student B finds 21 out of 30 have a sibling.

Explain why these two students got different answers. What is this phenomenon called? (2 points)

---

**1.5.** A school of 500 students wants to find out how many students have read a book this month. They put all 500 student names in a bowl and randomly draw 50. Is this a valid random sample? What inference can be drawn about the whole school if 22 of the 50 sampled students have read a book this month? (2 points)

(a) Find the mean for each group. (Use a calculator.) (2 points)

## Error Analysis Guide

| Error Pattern | Example | Likely Cause | Intervention |
|--------------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Treats sample results as exact population facts | "82% of teenagers prefer X" stated as a certainty | Does not understand sampling variability | Emphasize: sample results are estimates. Use the word "approximately" in all inferences. Run multiple sample simulations to show variability. |
| Inverts probability fraction | P(A) = total/favorable instead of favorable/total | Reverses numerator and denominator | Reinforce: probability is the part we want out of the whole. "Part / Whole." Use physical models to check answers for reasonableness. |
| Lists incomplete sample space for compound events | Missing outcomes in organized list | Does not use a systematic method | Require tree diagram first. Count branches to verify total. Apply Fundamental Counting Principle as a check. |
| Adds probabilities for compound "AND" events | P(A and B) = P(A) + P(B) | Confuses "and" with "or"; does not understand compound probability | Use sample space explicitly. Count favorable outcomes from the complete list. Discuss: "Adding probabilities is for 'or' in specific situations; 'and' requires the sample space." |
| Concludes die/coin is unfair after small experiment | "Got 7 heads in 10 flips -- the coin is biased" | Does not understand Law of Large Numbers | Demonstrate: run the same experiment 5 times. Show that results vary. Explain: with few trials, deviation from theory is normal. Need many trials to conclude unfairness. |
| Confuses random sample with large sample | "I asked 1,000 friends, so it must be representative" | Does not understand that size alone cannot fix bias | Ask: "Did every member of the population have an equal chance of being selected?" Large biased samples are still biased. |

## Facilitator Observations

- Can the student reliably distinguish between a random sample and a biased sample, and articulate why the distinction matters?
- Does the student express inferences with appropriate uncertainty ("I estimate approximately...") rather than as certainties?
- Can the student compute all five measures (mean, median, range, IQR, and -- with help -- MAD) for a data set?
- Does the student use both center AND variability when comparing two populations?
- Can the student set up P = favorable/total correctly, identifying both the favorable count and the total sample space size?
- Does the student use the complement rule (P(not A) = 1 - P(A)) when appropriate?
- Can the student construct a complete sample space for a compound event using at least one of the three methods (list, tree, table)?
- Does the student understand why experimental probability approaches theoretical probability with many trials?
- Can the student design a valid simulation and identify a simulation tool that has the correct probability?
- On problem-solving tasks, does the student identify the relevant concept/tool before attempting the calculation?
