# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 4, Lesson 6

## Topic
Circumference of Circles

## Learning Objectives
- Define circumference, diameter, and radius, and describe the relationship between diameter and radius.
- Discover the constant ratio pi by measuring circular objects.
- Apply the formulas C = pi * d and C = 2 * pi * r to calculate the circumference of circles.
- Solve real-world problems involving circumference.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to multiply decimals (Units 1-2).
- Understanding of ratios (Unit 1 -- Proportional Relationships).
- Ability to solve equations of the form ax = b (Unit 3).

## Materials Needed
- String or flexible measuring tape.
- Ruler (metric).
- At least 3-4 circular objects of different sizes (lids, plates, cups, coins, cans).
- Compass (for drawing circles).
- Calculator (optional, for checking decimal multiplication).
- Student worksheet.
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.G.4 -- Know the formulas for the area and circumference of a circle and use them to solve problems; give an informal derivation of the relationship between the circumference and area of a circle.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of circles and ratios.

1. Draw a circle on the board. Label the center, a radius, and a diameter. Ask: "What is the relationship between the radius and the diameter?" (The diameter is twice the radius: d = 2r.)

2. A bicycle wheel has a diameter of 26 inches. What is the radius? (26 / 2 = 13 inches)

3. From Unit 1: simplify the ratio 22/7. (Approximately 3.14 -- this foreshadows pi.)

Say: "Today we are going to discover one of the most famous numbers in mathematics by measuring circles."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Circle** -- the set of all points that are the same distance from a center point.
- **Radius (r)** -- the distance from the center of a circle to any point on the circle.
- **Diameter (d)** -- the distance across a circle through its center. d = 2r.
- **Circumference (C)** -- the distance around a circle (the perimeter of a circle).
- **Pi (pi)** -- the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter. Pi is the same for every circle: approximately 3.14 or 22/7. Pi is an irrational number -- its decimal form never ends and never repeats.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Hands-on measurement activity.**

Say: "We are going to measure several circular objects. For each one, we will measure the circumference (the distance around) and the diameter (the distance across through the center). Then we will compute the ratio circumference / diameter."

Provide the student with 3-4 circular objects, string, and a ruler.

For each object:
1. Wrap the string around the outside of the circular object. Mark where it overlaps. Lay the string flat and measure its length -- this is the circumference.
2. Measure across the widest part of the circle (through the center) -- this is the diameter.
3. Compute: circumference / diameter.

Say: "Record your results in a table."

| Object | Circumference (cm) | Diameter (cm) | C / d |
|--------|-------------------|---------------|-------|
| (e.g., coin) | (e.g., 7.5) | (e.g., 2.4) | (e.g., 3.13) |
| (e.g., cup) | (e.g., 25.1) | (e.g., 8.0) | (e.g., 3.14) |
| (e.g., plate) | (e.g., 78.5) | (e.g., 25.0) | (e.g., 3.14) |

Say: "What do you notice about the ratio C / d?" (It is always approximately the same number -- about 3.14.)

Say: "This ratio has a special name: pi, written with the Greek letter. Pi is approximately 3.14, but its exact value has an infinite number of decimal places. Pi is the same for every circle, no matter how big or small."

**Step 2 -- Developing the circumference formulas.**

Say: "Since C / d = pi, we can rearrange to get: C = pi * d."

Say: "And since d = 2r, we can also write: C = pi * 2r = 2 * pi * r."

Write on the board:
- **C = pi * d** (Circumference = pi times the diameter)
- **C = 2 * pi * r** (Circumference = 2 times pi times the radius)

Say: "For calculations, we will use pi approximately equal to 3.14. When a problem says 'use pi = 3.14,' that is an approximation."

**Step 3 -- Using the formulas.**

Example: Find the circumference of a circle with a diameter of 10 cm.

C = pi * d = 3.14 * 10 = **31.4 cm**.

Example: Find the circumference of a circle with a radius of 7 m.

C = 2 * pi * r = 2 * 3.14 * 7 = 6.28 * 7 = **43.96 m**.

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Given Diameter**

A circular garden has a diameter of 14 meters. Find its circumference. Use pi = 3.14.

Step 1: Identify the given measurement. d = 14 m.
Step 2: Use C = pi * d.
Step 3: C = 3.14 * 14 = **43.96 meters**.

**Example B: Given Radius**

A circular swimming pool has a radius of 5 meters. How much fencing is needed to go around it? Use pi = 3.14.

Step 1: Identify the given measurement. r = 5 m.
Step 2: Use C = 2 * pi * r.
Step 3: C = 2 * 3.14 * 5 = 6.28 * 5 = **31.4 meters** of fencing.

**Example C: Finding Diameter from Circumference**

A circular track has a circumference of 400 meters. What is its diameter? Use pi = 3.14.

Step 1: C = pi * d, so d = C / pi.
Step 2: d = 400 / 3.14 = **127.39 meters** (rounded to two decimal places).

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Computing Circumference**

1. A pizza has a diameter of 12 inches. What is the circumference? Use pi = 3.14.
   - C = 3.14 * 12 = **37.68 inches**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Which formula do you use when given the diameter?"

2. A clock has a radius of 15 cm. What is the circumference of the clock face? Use pi = 3.14.
   - C = 2 * 3.14 * 15 = 6.28 * 15 = **94.2 cm**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "When given the radius, use C = 2 * pi * r."

3. A hula hoop has a circumference of 314 cm. What is the diameter? Use pi = 3.14.
   - d = 314 / 3.14 = **100 cm** (or 1 meter).
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Rearrange the formula: if C = pi * d, then d = C / pi."

**Activity 2: Real-World Application**

4. A bicycle wheel has a diameter of 24 inches. How far does the bicycle travel in one complete rotation of the wheel?
   - One rotation = one circumference. C = 3.14 * 24 = **75.36 inches**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "One full spin of the wheel moves the bike forward by exactly one circumference."

5. How far does the bicycle travel in 10 complete rotations?
   - 10 * 75.36 = **753.6 inches** (or 62.8 feet).

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. Find the circumference of a circle with a diameter of 20 cm. Use pi = 3.14.

2. Find the circumference of a circle with a radius of 9 m. Use pi = 3.14.

3. A circular pond has a circumference of 157 m. What is the diameter? Use pi = 3.14.

4. A dinner plate has a diameter of 10 inches. What is the distance around the plate?

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

5. A semicircular garden has a diameter of 8 m. What is the perimeter of the garden? (Include both the curved part and the straight diameter.)

6. A car tire has a radius of 14 inches. How many complete rotations does the tire make when the car travels 1 mile (63,360 inches)?

7. The minute hand of a clock is 12 cm long. How far does the tip of the minute hand travel in one hour?

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

8. A circular track has an inner radius of 30 m and an outer radius of 32 m. How much longer is one lap on the outer edge than one lap on the inner edge?

9. A wheel has a circumference of 188.4 cm. What is its radius? If the wheel makes 500 rotations, how far does it travel in meters?

---

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Find the circumference of a circle with a radius of 6 cm. Use pi = 3.14.

**Problem 2:** A circular fountain has a circumference of 47.1 meters. What is the diameter? Use pi = 3.14.

**Mastery:** 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated. 1 out of 2 = Review which formula to use. 0 out of 2 = Reteach with measurement activity.

---

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. C = 3.14 * 20 = **62.8 cm**.
2. C = 2 * 3.14 * 9 = **56.52 m**.
3. d = 157 / 3.14 = **50 m**.
4. C = 3.14 * 10 = **31.4 inches**.
5. Curved part (semicircle) = (1/2) * pi * d = (1/2) * 3.14 * 8 = 12.56 m. Straight part = 8 m. Total perimeter = 12.56 + 8 = **20.56 m**.
6. C = 2 * 3.14 * 14 = 87.92 inches. Rotations = 63,360 / 87.92 = approximately **720.4 rotations**, so approximately **720 complete rotations**.
7. The tip traces a circle with radius 12 cm. In one hour (one full revolution): C = 2 * 3.14 * 12 = **75.36 cm**.
8. Outer circumference = 2 * 3.14 * 32 = 200.96 m. Inner circumference = 2 * 3.14 * 30 = 188.4 m. Difference = 200.96 - 188.4 = **12.56 m** longer on the outer edge. (Note: the difference depends only on the difference in radii: 2 * pi * (32 - 30) = 2 * 3.14 * 2 = 12.56.)
9. C = 2 * pi * r. 188.4 = 2 * 3.14 * r. 188.4 = 6.28r. r = 188.4 / 6.28 = **30 cm**. Distance for 500 rotations = 500 * 188.4 = 94,200 cm = **942 meters**.

### Exit Ticket

1. C = 2 * 3.14 * 6 = **37.68 cm**.
2. d = 47.1 / 3.14 = **15 meters**.

## Teacher Notes

- The measurement activity in Step 1 is the heart of this lesson. Even if measurements are imprecise, the student should see that C/d is always close to 3.14. This experiential discovery of pi is far more meaningful than being told "pi equals 3.14."
- Some students confuse radius and diameter. Require the student to always write "r = ___" or "d = ___" before substituting into a formula. If given the diameter, the first mental check is: "Do I need to find the radius?"
- The bicycle wheel problem (Guided Practice, Problem 4) is an excellent real-world connection. One rotation = one circumference. This is a concept students will revisit when studying angular velocity in high school.
- Problem 8 in Tier 3 reveals a surprising and elegant fact: the difference in circumferences between two concentric circles depends only on the difference in radii (2 * pi * delta r), not on the actual radii themselves. If the student notices this, discuss why.
- Use pi = 3.14 for all calculations in this lesson and the rest of the unit. Some problems may also accept 22/7, which is a slightly different approximation. Consistency matters more than the choice of approximation.
- Next lesson: Area of Circles. The circumference formula will be used in deriving the area formula, so it must be solid before proceeding.
