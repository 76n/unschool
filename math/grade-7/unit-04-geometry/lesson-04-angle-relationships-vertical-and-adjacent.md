# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 4, Lesson 4

## Topic
Angle Relationships: Vertical and Adjacent Angles

## Learning Objectives
- Define vertical angles and adjacent angles.
- Explain and apply the property that vertical angles are congruent (equal in measure).
- Explain and apply the property that adjacent angles on a line (a linear pair) sum to 180 degrees.
- Use vertical and adjacent angle relationships to find unknown angles in diagrams with intersecting lines.

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 3: Complementary and Supplementary Angles.
- Ability to measure angles with a protractor (Grade 6).
- Ability to solve one-step and two-step equations (Unit 3).

## Materials Needed
- Protractor.
- Ruler or straightedge.
- Two pencils or craft sticks (to model intersecting lines).
- Student worksheet.
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.G.5 -- Use facts about supplementary, complementary, vertical, and adjacent angles in a multi-step problem to write and solve simple equations for an unknown angle in a figure.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review complementary and supplementary angles from Lesson 3.

1. Two supplementary angles measure x degrees and 72 degrees. Find x. (x = 180 - 72 = 108 degrees)

2. Two complementary angles measure (2x + 5) degrees and 35 degrees. Find x. (2x + 5 + 35 = 90. 2x + 40 = 90. 2x = 50. x = 25.)

3. An angle measures 64 degrees. Is its supplement greater than or less than 90 degrees? (Supplement = 116 degrees. Greater than 90.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Vertical angles** -- two non-adjacent angles formed by two intersecting lines. Vertical angles are always across from each other at the intersection point. They are always congruent (equal in measure).
- **Adjacent angles** -- two angles that share a common vertex and a common side, but do not overlap.
- **Linear pair** -- two adjacent angles that together form a straight line. A linear pair is always supplementary (their measures sum to 180 degrees).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Exploring intersecting lines.**

Say: "Take two pencils and cross them to form an X shape. Look at the four angles formed at the intersection. Today we are going to discover the relationships between these angles."

Draw two intersecting lines on the board. Label the four angles as Angle 1, Angle 2, Angle 3, and Angle 4, going clockwise from the top.

Say: "Angle 1 is at the top. Angle 2 is on the right. Angle 3 is at the bottom. Angle 4 is on the left."

**Step 2 -- Vertical angles are congruent.**

Say: "Angle 1 and Angle 3 are across from each other -- they are vertical angles. Angle 2 and Angle 4 are also across from each other -- they are vertical angles too."

Measure Angle 1 with a protractor. Suppose it measures 70 degrees.

Say: "Now measure Angle 3." (It will also measure 70 degrees.)

Say: "Measure Angle 2." (It will measure 110 degrees.) "And Angle 4?" (Also 110 degrees.)

Say: "Vertical angles are ALWAYS equal. This is not a coincidence -- it is a mathematical fact we can prove."

**Step 3 -- Why vertical angles are equal (informal proof).**

Say: "Angle 1 and Angle 2 are a linear pair -- they sit on a straight line. So Angle 1 + Angle 2 = 180. Angle 2 and Angle 3 are also a linear pair. So Angle 2 + Angle 3 = 180. Since both sums equal 180, we have Angle 1 + Angle 2 = Angle 2 + Angle 3. Subtract Angle 2 from both sides: Angle 1 = Angle 3. That is why vertical angles are always equal."

**Step 4 -- Adjacent angles and linear pairs.**

Say: "Adjacent angles share a side. Angle 1 and Angle 2 are adjacent. Angle 2 and Angle 3 are adjacent. When two adjacent angles form a straight line, they are called a linear pair, and they are supplementary -- they add up to 180 degrees."

Write: If Angle 1 = 70 degrees, then Angle 2 = 180 - 70 = 110 degrees.

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Finding All Four Angles**

Two lines intersect. One of the angles measures 45 degrees. Find the other three angles.

Step 1: Label the 45-degree angle as Angle 1.
Step 2: The vertical angle (Angle 3) = 45 degrees (vertical angles are equal).
Step 3: Angle 2 = 180 - 45 = 135 degrees (linear pair with Angle 1).
Step 4: Angle 4 = 135 degrees (vertical to Angle 2).
The four angles are **45, 135, 45, 135 degrees**.

**Example B: Using Variables**

Two intersecting lines form angles of (3x + 15) degrees and (5x - 25) degrees that are vertical angles. Find x and the angle measures.

Step 1: Vertical angles are equal: 3x + 15 = 5x - 25.
Step 2: 15 + 25 = 5x - 3x. 40 = 2x. x = 20.
Step 3: Each vertical angle = 3(20) + 15 = 75 degrees. Check: 5(20) - 25 = 75 degrees. Correct.
Step 4: The adjacent angles = 180 - 75 = 105 degrees.
The four angles are **75, 105, 75, 105 degrees**.

**Example C: Multi-Step Problem**

In a diagram, two lines intersect. One angle is labeled (2x + 10) degrees, and the adjacent angle is labeled (3x) degrees. Find x.

Step 1: These are a linear pair (adjacent angles on a line), so they sum to 180.
Step 2: (2x + 10) + 3x = 180. 5x + 10 = 180. 5x = 170. x = 34.
Step 3: Angle 1 = 2(34) + 10 = 78 degrees. Angle 2 = 3(34) = 102 degrees.
Step 4: Check: 78 + 102 = 180. Correct.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Identifying Angle Types**

Draw two intersecting lines on the board and label all four angles.

1. Which pairs are vertical angles?
   - Angle 1 and Angle 3; Angle 2 and Angle 4.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Vertical angles are across from each other, like an X."

2. Which pairs are linear pairs?
   - Angle 1 and Angle 2; Angle 2 and Angle 3; Angle 3 and Angle 4; Angle 4 and Angle 1.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Linear pairs are side by side and form a straight line."

3. If Angle 1 = 130 degrees, find all other angles.
   - Angle 3 = 130 degrees (vertical). Angle 2 = 50 degrees (linear pair). Angle 4 = 50 degrees (vertical to Angle 2).

**Activity 2: Solving Equations**

4. Two vertical angles measure (4x) degrees and (x + 60) degrees. Find x and the angle measures.
   - 4x = x + 60. 3x = 60. x = 20. Each angle = 4(20) = **80 degrees**. Check: 20 + 60 = 80.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Remember: vertical angles are equal, so set them equal to each other."

5. Two angles form a linear pair. One measures (x + 45) degrees and the other measures (2x) degrees. Find x.
   - (x + 45) + 2x = 180. 3x + 45 = 180. 3x = 135. x = 45.
   - Angle 1 = 45 + 45 = 90 degrees. Angle 2 = 2(45) = 90 degrees.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "This is a linear pair, so the sum is 180. Interesting -- both angles turned out to be right angles!"

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. Two lines intersect. One angle is 58 degrees. Find the other three angles.

2. Two vertical angles measure (2x + 8) degrees and (3x - 12) degrees. Find x and the angle measures.

3. Two angles form a linear pair. One measures 3x degrees and the other measures (x + 40) degrees. Find x and both angles.

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

4. Two lines intersect. One angle is (5x - 15) degrees. The adjacent angle is (3x + 7) degrees. Find x and all four angle measures.

5. In a figure, three angles meet at a point on a straight line: 2x degrees, (x + 30) degrees, and 50 degrees. Find x and all three angles.

6. Two vertical angles are each 90 degrees. What can you say about the two lines? (What is the name for lines that form 90-degree angles?)

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

7. Two lines intersect. One of the four angles is twice the measure of another. Find all four angle measures. (Hint: a vertical angle pair and a linear pair relationship both apply.)

8. Three lines meet at a single point, forming six angles. One angle measures 40 degrees and another measures 75 degrees. These two angles are adjacent. Find all six angle measures.

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Two lines intersect. One angle measures 72 degrees. What is the measure of the vertical angle? What is the measure of each adjacent angle?

**Problem 2:** Two vertical angles measure (4x - 10) degrees and (2x + 30) degrees. Find x.

**Mastery:** 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated. 1 out of 2 = Review errors. 0 out of 2 = Reteach vertical and linear pair properties.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. Vertical angle = 58 degrees. Adjacent angles = 180 - 58 = 122 degrees each. The four angles: **58, 122, 58, 122 degrees**.
2. 2x + 8 = 3x - 12. 8 + 12 = 3x - 2x. 20 = x. x = 20. Each angle = 2(20) + 8 = **48 degrees**. Check: 3(20) - 12 = 48 degrees. Adjacent angles = 180 - 48 = 132 degrees.
3. 3x + (x + 40) = 180. 4x + 40 = 180. 4x = 140. x = 35. Angle 1 = 3(35) = **105 degrees**. Angle 2 = 35 + 40 = **75 degrees**. Check: 105 + 75 = 180.
4. (5x - 15) + (3x + 7) = 180. 8x - 8 = 180. 8x = 188. x = 23.5. Angle 1 = 5(23.5) - 15 = 102.5 degrees. Angle 2 = 3(23.5) + 7 = 77.5 degrees. The four angles: **102.5, 77.5, 102.5, 77.5 degrees**. Check: 102.5 + 77.5 = 180.
5. 2x + (x + 30) + 50 = 180. 3x + 80 = 180. 3x = 100. x = 33.33... (or 100/3). Angle 1 = 2(100/3) = 200/3 approximately **66.7 degrees**. Angle 2 = 100/3 + 30 = 190/3 approximately **63.3 degrees**. Angle 3 = **50 degrees**. Check: 66.7 + 63.3 + 50 = 180.
6. If vertical angles are each 90 degrees, all four angles are 90 degrees. The two lines are **perpendicular**.
7. Let one angle = x and the adjacent angle = 2x. Then x + 2x = 180. 3x = 180. x = 60. The four angles: **60, 120, 60, 120 degrees**. (Or: if vertical angles are in a 1:2 ratio with their adjacent pair, the angles are 60 and 120.)
8. The two adjacent angles are 40 and 75 degrees. The third angle on one side of a line through the point: 180 - 40 - 75 = 65 degrees. The six angles around the point (going around): 40, 75, 65, 40, 75, 65 degrees. (Each angle is vertical to the angle across from it: 40 and 40, 75 and 75, 65 and 65.)

### Exit Ticket

1. Vertical angle = **72 degrees**. Each adjacent angle = 180 - 72 = **108 degrees**.
2. 4x - 10 = 2x + 30. 2x = 40. x = **20**. Check: 4(20) - 10 = 70 degrees. 2(20) + 30 = 70 degrees. Correct.

## Teacher Notes

- Students commonly confuse "vertical" with "on top" (vertically above). Emphasize that "vertical angles" comes from the word "vertex" -- they share a vertex and are across from each other. Drawing the X shape and pointing across helps.
- The informal proof that vertical angles are equal (Step 3 in Direct Instruction) is an important reasoning exercise. Even though students are not writing formal proofs at this level, understanding WHY vertical angles are equal builds mathematical thinking.
- Problem 5 in Tier 2 involves a non-integer answer (x = 100/3). This is a good opportunity to discuss that angle measures do not have to be whole numbers. If the student is uncomfortable with fractions, they can use decimals.
- Problem 7 in Tier 3 requires students to combine vertical angle and linear pair reasoning. This is a good challenge for students who have mastered the basic problems.
- Connection to Lesson 5: the triangle angle sum property (180 degrees) can be proven using the parallel lines and transversals concept, which extends vertical and adjacent angle reasoning. While parallel lines are not a focus of this unit, mention the connection for interested students.
