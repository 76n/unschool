# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 4, Lesson 3

## Topic
Angle Relationships: Complementary and Supplementary

## Learning Objectives
- Define complementary angles and supplementary angles.
- Identify complementary and supplementary angle pairs in diagrams.
- Write and solve equations to find missing angle measures using complementary and supplementary relationships.
- Determine whether two given angles are complementary, supplementary, or neither.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to identify and measure angles using a protractor (Grade 6).
- Understanding of right angles (90 degrees) and straight angles (180 degrees) (Grade 6).
- Ability to solve one-step and two-step equations (Unit 3).

## Materials Needed
- Protractor (one per student).
- Ruler.
- Student worksheet.
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.
- Colored pencils (optional, for labeling angle pairs).

**Standards:** 7.G.5 -- Use facts about supplementary, complementary, vertical, and adjacent angles in a multi-step problem to write and solve simple equations for an unknown angle in a figure.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review angle measurement and activate prior knowledge.

1. Draw a right angle on the board. Ask: "What is the measure of this angle?" (90 degrees)

2. Draw a straight line with a ray pointing upward from a point on the line, creating two angles. Ask: "What do the two angles on this straight line add up to?" (180 degrees)

3. Review from Unit 3: Solve x + 35 = 90. (x = 55)

Say: "Today we are going to name these special angle relationships and use equations to find missing angles."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Complementary angles** -- two angles whose measures add up to exactly 90 degrees. Example: 30 degrees and 60 degrees are complementary because 30 + 60 = 90.
- **Supplementary angles** -- two angles whose measures add up to exactly 180 degrees. Example: 110 degrees and 70 degrees are supplementary because 110 + 70 = 180.
- **Right angle** -- an angle that measures exactly 90 degrees.
- **Straight angle** -- an angle that measures exactly 180 degrees (a straight line).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Complementary angles.**

Say: "Two angles are complementary when they add up to 90 degrees -- a right angle. Think of a corner of a piece of paper. If you draw a line from the corner, you split that right angle into two smaller angles. Those two angles are complementary."

Draw a right angle and split it with a ray. Label one angle as 55 degrees and the other as a question mark.

Say: "If one angle is 55 degrees, and the two angles are complementary, what is the other angle?"

Write: 55 + ? = 90. So ? = 90 - 55 = 35 degrees.

Say: "Here is a memory trick: the letter C in Complementary comes before S in the alphabet, and 90 comes before 180. So Complementary goes with 90."

**Step 2 -- Supplementary angles.**

Say: "Two angles are supplementary when they add up to 180 degrees -- a straight angle. Imagine a straight line. If a ray extends from a point on that line, it creates two angles. Those two angles are supplementary."

Draw a straight line with a ray extending upward. Label one angle as 125 degrees.

Say: "What is the other angle?" Write: 125 + ? = 180. So ? = 180 - 125 = 55 degrees.

Say: "Memory trick: Supplementary starts with S for Straight angle -- 180 degrees."

**Step 3 -- Solving with variables.**

Say: "In many problems, the angles are expressed with variables. We set up an equation and solve."

Write on the board: Two complementary angles measure (2x + 10) degrees and (3x) degrees. Find x and both angle measures.

Set up: (2x + 10) + 3x = 90
5x + 10 = 90
5x = 80
x = 16

Angle 1: 2(16) + 10 = 42 degrees. Angle 2: 3(16) = 48 degrees. Check: 42 + 48 = 90. Correct.

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Complementary Angles**

Angle A and Angle B are complementary. Angle A measures 27 degrees. What is the measure of Angle B?

Step 1: Complementary means the sum is 90 degrees.
Step 2: 27 + B = 90.
Step 3: B = 90 - 27 = **63 degrees**.
Step 4: Check: 27 + 63 = 90. Correct.

**Example B: Supplementary Angles**

Two supplementary angles measure (4x) degrees and (x + 50) degrees. Find x and both angle measures.

Step 1: Supplementary means the sum is 180 degrees.
Step 2: 4x + (x + 50) = 180.
Step 3: 5x + 50 = 180. 5x = 130. x = 26.
Step 4: Angle 1 = 4(26) = 104 degrees. Angle 2 = 26 + 50 = 76 degrees.
Step 5: Check: 104 + 76 = 180. Correct.

**Example C: Classify the Relationship**

Are the angles 53 degrees and 37 degrees complementary, supplementary, or neither?

53 + 37 = 90. They are **complementary**.

Are the angles 85 degrees and 105 degrees complementary, supplementary, or neither?

85 + 105 = 190. Not 90 or 180. They are **neither**.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Identifying Relationships**

1. Angle P = 48 degrees and Angle Q = 42 degrees. Are they complementary, supplementary, or neither?
   - 48 + 42 = 90. **Complementary.**
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Add them up. Does it equal 90 or 180?"

2. Angle M = 113 degrees and Angle N = 67 degrees. Are they complementary, supplementary, or neither?
   - 113 + 67 = 180. **Supplementary.**

3. Angle X = 45 degrees and Angle Y = 55 degrees. Are they complementary, supplementary, or neither?
   - 45 + 55 = 100. **Neither.**
   - **Facilitator tip:** "If the sum is not exactly 90 or exactly 180, the answer is neither."

**Activity 2: Solving for Missing Angles**

4. Two complementary angles measure x degrees and 52 degrees. Find x.
   - x + 52 = 90. x = **38 degrees**.

5. Two supplementary angles measure (3x - 5) degrees and (x + 25) degrees. Find x and both angle measures.
   - (3x - 5) + (x + 25) = 180. 4x + 20 = 180. 4x = 160. x = 40.
   - Angle 1: 3(40) - 5 = 115 degrees. Angle 2: 40 + 25 = 65 degrees.
   - Check: 115 + 65 = 180. Correct.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "If the student struggles with the equation, remind them of the techniques from Unit 3 -- combine like terms first, then solve."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. Angle A and Angle B are complementary. Angle A = 34 degrees. Find Angle B.

2. Angle C and Angle D are supplementary. Angle C = 98 degrees. Find Angle D.

3. Are angles of 72 degrees and 18 degrees complementary, supplementary, or neither?

4. Two supplementary angles measure x degrees and 115 degrees. Find x.

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

5. Two complementary angles measure (x + 15) degrees and (2x) degrees. Find x and both angles.

6. Two supplementary angles measure (5x - 10) degrees and (3x + 30) degrees. Find x and both angles.

7. The supplement of an angle is three times the angle. Find the angle. (Hint: let the angle be x. Its supplement is 180 - x.)

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

8. The complement of an angle is 20 degrees less than the angle itself. Find both angles.

9. An angle is 30 degrees more than twice its supplement. Find the angle.

10. Three angles are formed at a point on a straight line: (2x + 5) degrees, (x + 10) degrees, and (3x - 15) degrees. Find x and all three angle measures. (Hint: the three angles sum to 180 degrees because they are on a straight line.)

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Two complementary angles measure (x + 12) degrees and (2x) degrees. Find x and both angle measures.

**Problem 2:** An angle measures 137 degrees. What is the measure of its supplement?

**Mastery:** 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated. 1 out of 2 = Review errors. 0 out of 2 = Reteach definitions and equation setup.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. B = 90 - 34 = **56 degrees**.
2. D = 180 - 98 = **82 degrees**.
3. 72 + 18 = 90. **Complementary.**
4. x + 115 = 180. x = **65 degrees**.
5. (x + 15) + 2x = 90. 3x + 15 = 90. 3x = 75. x = 25. Angle 1 = 25 + 15 = **40 degrees**. Angle 2 = 2(25) = **50 degrees**. Check: 40 + 50 = 90.
6. (5x - 10) + (3x + 30) = 180. 8x + 20 = 180. 8x = 160. x = 20. Angle 1 = 5(20) - 10 = **90 degrees**. Angle 2 = 3(20) + 30 = **90 degrees**. Check: 90 + 90 = 180.
7. Let the angle = x. Supplement = 180 - x. Given: 180 - x = 3x. 180 = 4x. x = 45. The angle is **45 degrees** and its supplement is **135 degrees**. Check: 45 + 135 = 180 and 135 = 3(45).
8. Let the angle = x. Complement = 90 - x. Given: 90 - x = x - 20. 90 + 20 = 2x. 110 = 2x. x = 55. The angle is **55 degrees** and its complement is **35 degrees**. Check: 55 - 35 = 20 and 55 + 35 = 90.
9. Let the angle = x. Supplement = 180 - x. Given: x = 2(180 - x) + 30. x = 360 - 2x + 30. x = 390 - 2x. 3x = 390. x = 130. The angle is **130 degrees**. Check: supplement = 50. 2(50) + 30 = 130. Correct.
10. (2x + 5) + (x + 10) + (3x - 15) = 180. 6x + 0 = 180. 6x = 180. x = 30. Angle 1 = 2(30) + 5 = **65 degrees**. Angle 2 = 30 + 10 = **40 degrees**. Angle 3 = 3(30) - 15 = **75 degrees**. Check: 65 + 40 + 75 = 180.

### Exit Ticket

1. (x + 12) + 2x = 90. 3x + 12 = 90. 3x = 78. x = 26. Angle 1 = 26 + 12 = **38 degrees**. Angle 2 = 2(26) = **52 degrees**. Check: 38 + 52 = 90.
2. 180 - 137 = **43 degrees**.

## Teacher Notes

- The memory tricks for complementary (C for Corner, 90 degrees) and supplementary (S for Straight, 180 degrees) are extremely helpful. Repeat them frequently until the student internalizes the definitions.
- Watch for the common error of adding angles and checking against the wrong total (e.g., checking complementary angles against 180 instead of 90). Always ask: "Are we looking for complementary or supplementary?"
- Problem 6 has an interesting result -- both angles are 90 degrees. Point this out: "Two supplementary angles that are equal must each be 90 degrees."
- Problem 10 extends the supplementary concept to three angles on a line. This previews the multi-angle work in Lesson 4.
- If the student finishes early, ask: "Can two obtuse angles be supplementary? Can two acute angles be supplementary?" (No and no -- explore why.)
- Next lesson: Vertical and adjacent angles, which builds directly on the supplementary relationship.
