# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 4, Lesson 8

## Topic
Area and Circumference Problem Solving

## Learning Objectives
- Apply circumference and area formulas to solve multi-step real-world problems.
- Calculate the area of shaded regions involving circles and rectangles (or squares).
- Find missing dimensions (radius, diameter, or side length) when area or circumference is given.
- Combine circumference and area calculations in composite-figure problems.

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 6: Circumference of Circles (C = pi * d, C = 2 * pi * r).
- Lesson 7: Area of Circles (A = pi * r^2).
- Area formulas for rectangles and squares (Grade 6).
- Ability to solve multi-step equations (Unit 3).

## Materials Needed
- Ruler.
- Colored pencils (for shading regions in diagrams).
- Calculator (optional, for checking decimal work).
- Student worksheet with diagrams.
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.G.4 -- Know the formulas for the area and circumference of a circle and use them to solve problems.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review circle formulas with quick computation.

1. Find the circumference of a circle with radius 4 cm. (C = 2 * 3.14 * 4 = 25.12 cm)

2. Find the area of a circle with radius 4 cm. (A = 3.14 * 16 = 50.24 sq cm)

3. A square has a side length of 10 cm. What is its area? (100 sq cm)

Say: "Today we are going to combine everything we know about circles, rectangles, and squares to solve problems that involve more than one shape."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Shaded region** -- the part of a figure that is highlighted, often representing the area you need to calculate. In problems with overlapping shapes, the shaded region is typically found by subtraction.
- **Composite figure** -- a shape made up of two or more simpler shapes combined.
- **Semicircle** -- half of a circle.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Shaded region problems: rectangle with a circle cut out.**

Say: "Imagine a square piece of cardboard with a circular hole cut in the center. The shaded region is the leftover cardboard -- everything that is NOT the circle."

Draw a square with side 10 cm and a circle with diameter 10 cm inscribed inside it.

Say: "To find the shaded area, we subtract the area of the circle from the area of the square."

Square area = 10 * 10 = 100 sq cm.
Circle: d = 10 cm, so r = 5 cm. Circle area = 3.14 * 25 = 78.5 sq cm.
Shaded area = 100 - 78.5 = **21.5 sq cm**.

**Step 2 -- Composite figures with semicircles.**

Say: "Some shapes include semicircles attached to rectangles -- like the end of a running track."

Draw a rectangle with a semicircle on one end. Label the rectangle as 20 m by 10 m, and the semicircle has a diameter of 10 m.

Say: "The total area is the rectangle area plus the semicircle area."

Rectangle area = 20 * 10 = 200 sq m.
Semicircle: r = 5 m. Full circle area = 3.14 * 25 = 78.5 sq m. Semicircle = 78.5 / 2 = 39.25 sq m.
Total area = 200 + 39.25 = **239.25 sq m**.

Say: "For the perimeter, we add the three straight sides of the rectangle plus the curved part of the semicircle."

Perimeter = 20 + 10 + 20 + (semicircle circumference).
Semicircle circumference = (1/2) * pi * d = (1/2) * 3.14 * 10 = 15.7 m.
Total perimeter = 20 + 10 + 20 + 15.7 = **65.7 m**.

**Step 3 -- Finding missing dimensions.**

Say: "Some problems give you the area or circumference and ask you to find a missing measurement."

Example: The area of a circular window is 200.96 sq cm. What length of trim do you need to go around the window?

Step 1: Find the radius from the area. A = pi * r^2. 200.96 = 3.14 * r^2. r^2 = 64. r = 8 cm.
Step 2: Find the circumference. C = 2 * 3.14 * 8 = 50.24 cm.
You need **50.24 cm** of trim.

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Circle Inside a Square**

A square frame has a side of 14 cm. A circular photo with a diameter of 14 cm is placed inside. What is the area of the frame (the visible part of the square around the photo)?

Step 1: Square area = 14 * 14 = 196 sq cm.
Step 2: Circle: r = 7 cm. A = 3.14 * 49 = 153.86 sq cm.
Step 3: Shaded (frame) area = 196 - 153.86 = **42.14 sq cm**.

**Example B: Semicircular Garden**

A garden is shaped like a semicircle with a diameter of 6 m. Find the area and the perimeter.

Step 1: Area of full circle = 3.14 * 3^2 = 3.14 * 9 = 28.26 sq m. Semicircle area = 28.26 / 2 = **14.13 sq m**.
Step 2: Perimeter = straight edge (diameter) + curved edge (half circumference). Half circumference = (1/2) * 3.14 * 6 = 9.42 m. Perimeter = 6 + 9.42 = **15.42 m**.

**Example C: Combined Circumference and Area**

A circular track has a circumference of 314 m. What is the area enclosed by the track?

Step 1: Find the radius. C = 2 * pi * r. 314 = 6.28 * r. r = 50 m.
Step 2: A = 3.14 * 50^2 = 3.14 * 2,500 = **7,850 sq m**.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Shaded Region**

1. A rectangular piece of paper is 20 cm by 16 cm. A circle with a diameter of 12 cm is cut from the center. What is the area of the remaining paper?
   - Rectangle: 20 * 16 = 320 sq cm. Circle: r = 6 cm. A = 3.14 * 36 = 113.04 sq cm. Remaining = 320 - 113.04 = **206.96 sq cm**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "The shaded area is the big shape minus the small shape."

2. A square has a side of 8 cm. A quarter-circle with radius 8 cm is drawn in one corner. Find the area of the region inside the square but outside the quarter-circle.
   - Square: 64 sq cm. Quarter-circle: (1/4) * 3.14 * 64 = (1/4) * 200.96 = 50.24 sq cm. Shaded = 64 - 50.24 = **13.76 sq cm**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "A quarter-circle is one-fourth of a full circle."

**Activity 2: Perimeter of Composite Figures**

3. A running track consists of a rectangle (100 m by 50 m) with a semicircle on each short end. What is the total distance around the track?
   - Two semicircles make one full circle with diameter 50 m. Circle circumference = 3.14 * 50 = 157 m. Two long sides = 100 + 100 = 200 m. Total = 200 + 157 = **357 m**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "The two semicircles together make one complete circle. The short sides of the rectangle are replaced by the curved parts."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. A circle with radius 5 cm is cut from a square piece of paper with side 12 cm. What is the area of the remaining paper?

2. A semicircular doormat has a diameter of 80 cm. What is the area of the doormat?

3. A circular pond has a radius of 10 m. A path 2 m wide goes around the pond. What is the area of the path? (Hint: the path is a ring -- outer circle minus inner circle.)

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

4. A rectangular garden is 30 m by 20 m. At each end, there is a semicircular flower bed with diameter 20 m. What is the total area of the garden plus the two flower beds?

5. A window is shaped like a rectangle (1.2 m tall by 0.8 m wide) with a semicircle on top (diameter 0.8 m). What is the total area of the window?

6. A circular dartboard has a diameter of 40 cm. The bullseye in the center has a diameter of 4 cm. What fraction of the dartboard's area is the bullseye?

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

7. Two circles are drawn inside a rectangle that is 24 cm by 12 cm. Each circle has a diameter of 12 cm, and they sit side by side, touching each other and the edges of the rectangle. What is the area inside the rectangle but outside both circles?

8. A running track has a total distance of 400 m. The track consists of two straight sections of 100 m each and two semicircular ends. What is the radius of each semicircular end?

---

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A circle with a diameter of 10 cm is inscribed in a square. What is the area of the shaded region (the square minus the circle)?

**Problem 2:** A semicircular window has a diameter of 2 m. Find the area and the perimeter of the window.

**Mastery:** 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated. 1 out of 2 = Review which formulas apply. 0 out of 2 = Reteach Lessons 6-7 before continuing.

---

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. Square: 12 * 12 = 144 sq cm. Circle: 3.14 * 25 = 78.5 sq cm. Remaining = 144 - 78.5 = **65.5 sq cm**.
2. r = 40 cm. Full circle: 3.14 * 1,600 = 5,024 sq cm. Semicircle = 5,024 / 2 = **2,512 sq cm**.
3. Outer circle (r = 12 m): A = 3.14 * 144 = 452.16 sq m. Inner circle (r = 10 m): A = 3.14 * 100 = 314 sq m. Path area = 452.16 - 314 = **138.16 sq m**.
4. Rectangle: 30 * 20 = 600 sq m. Two semicircles = one full circle with d = 20 m, r = 10 m. A = 3.14 * 100 = 314 sq m. Total = 600 + 314 = **914 sq m**.
5. Rectangle: 1.2 * 0.8 = 0.96 sq m. Semicircle: r = 0.4 m. A = (1/2) * 3.14 * 0.16 = 0.2512 sq m. Total = 0.96 + 0.2512 = **1.2112 sq m** (approximately **1.21 sq m**).
6. Dartboard area: r = 20 cm. A = 3.14 * 400 = 1,256 sq cm. Bullseye area: r = 2 cm. A = 3.14 * 4 = 12.56 sq cm. Fraction = 12.56 / 1,256 = **1/100** (or 1%).
7. Rectangle: 24 * 12 = 288 sq cm. Each circle: r = 6 cm. A = 3.14 * 36 = 113.04 sq cm. Two circles = 226.08 sq cm. Shaded = 288 - 226.08 = **61.92 sq cm**.
8. Total distance = 400 m. Two straight sections = 2 * 100 = 200 m. Curved parts = 400 - 200 = 200 m. Two semicircles = one full circle circumference = 200 m. C = pi * d = 200. d = 200 / 3.14 = 63.69 m. r = 63.69 / 2 = **31.85 m** (approximately).

### Exit Ticket

1. Square side = 10 cm (since the circle with d = 10 is inscribed). Square area = 100 sq cm. Circle: r = 5. A = 3.14 * 25 = 78.5 sq cm. Shaded = 100 - 78.5 = **21.5 sq cm**.
2. r = 1 m. Area = (1/2) * 3.14 * 1 = **1.57 sq m**. Perimeter = diameter + half circumference = 2 + (1/2) * 3.14 * 2 = 2 + 3.14 = **5.14 m**.

## Teacher Notes

- Shaded-region problems require careful reading. Students must determine which areas to subtract from which. Drawing and shading the diagram first helps enormously.
- The most common error in these problems is computing the wrong radius. When a circle is inscribed in a square of side s, the diameter equals s and the radius is s/2. Encourage students to label r explicitly on the diagram.
- Problem 3 (ring/annulus) is a classic problem type. The key insight is: path area = outer area - inner area. Some students try to compute the path width times the circumference, which gives an approximation but is not the correct method.
- Problem 6 is a ratio problem. The bullseye has 1/10 the diameter, but 1/100 the area. This reinforces the quadratic relationship between radius and area.
- Problem 8 in Tier 3 works backwards from the track perimeter to find the radius. This is a multi-step problem that combines circumference reasoning with subtraction.
- For students who finish early: "Design a park that includes a rectangular lawn, a circular fountain, and a semicircular garden. Choose your own dimensions, then calculate the total area of each feature."
- This lesson is the culmination of the circles sub-unit (Lessons 6-8). If the student is confident with these problems, they are ready for the cross-sections lesson. If not, spend an additional session on practice problems.
