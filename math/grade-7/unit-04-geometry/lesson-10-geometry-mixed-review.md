# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Topic
Geometry Mixed Review

## Learning Objectives
- Apply scale drawing, angle relationship, circle, and cross-section skills in a mixed-practice setting.
- Solve multi-step geometry problems that combine two or more unit topics.
- Identify and correct common errors in geometry solutions.
- Demonstrate readiness for the unit assessment.

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-9 of this unit.
- All circle formulas (C = pi * d, C = 2 * pi * r, A = pi * r^2).
- Angle relationships (complementary, supplementary, vertical, triangle angle sum).
- Scale drawing and scale factor concepts.
- Cross-section identification.

## Materials Needed
- Protractor.
- Ruler.
- Calculator (optional).
- Student worksheet.
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.G.1, 7.G.2, 7.G.3, 7.G.4, 7.G.5

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Rapid review of key formulas and definitions.

Answer each question without looking at notes:

1. What is the formula for the circumference of a circle? (C = pi * d or C = 2 * pi * r)

2. What is the formula for the area of a circle? (A = pi * r^2)

3. Complementary angles add up to ___? (90 degrees)

4. Supplementary angles add up to ___? (180 degrees)

5. The interior angles of a triangle add up to ___? (180 degrees)

Say: "If you got all five, great -- you have the key facts memorized. If you missed any, write them down now and keep them visible as a reference for today's practice."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
Review -- no new vocabulary in this lesson. This is a consolidation and application lesson.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Multi-step problem strategy.**

Say: "Many geometry problems require more than one skill. The key is to break the problem into steps and identify which skill applies at each step."

Write on the board a sample multi-step problem:

"A scale drawing of a circular garden uses the scale 1 cm = 3 m. On the drawing, the garden has a diameter of 4 cm. What is the actual area of the garden? How much fencing is needed to enclose it?"

Walk through the solution:
- Step 1 (Scale drawing): Actual diameter = 4 * 3 = 12 m. Actual radius = 6 m.
- Step 2 (Area): A = 3.14 * 6^2 = 3.14 * 36 = 113.04 sq m.
- Step 3 (Circumference): C = 2 * 3.14 * 6 = 37.68 m of fencing.

Say: "This problem combined scale drawings with circle formulas. On the assessment, you will see problems like this."

**Step 2 -- Error analysis.**

Say: "Part of being a strong math student is finding and fixing errors. Let us practice."

Write on the board:
"A student solved this problem: Find the area of a circle with diameter 10 cm.
Student's work: A = 3.14 * 10^2 = 3.14 * 100 = 314 sq cm."

Ask: "What error did the student make?"

(The student used the diameter instead of the radius. The correct solution: r = 5 cm. A = 3.14 * 25 = 78.5 sq cm. The student's answer is 4 times too large.)

**Step 3 -- Strategy for the unit assessment.**

Say: "On the unit assessment, you will see problems from every topic in this unit. Here are some strategies:"
- Read each problem carefully. Identify what is given and what you need to find.
- Draw a diagram if one is not provided.
- Label known measurements on the diagram.
- Write the formula before substituting numbers.
- Check your answer: does it make sense? Is the area larger than the circumference (in the same units)? Is the missing angle positive?

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Scale Drawing + Angles**

On a scale drawing (1 cm = 2 m), a triangular garden is drawn with one side of 5 cm opposite a 90-degree angle. The other two angles are equal. Find the actual length of the 5-cm side and the measures of the other two angles.

Step 1: Actual length = 5 * 2 = **10 m**.
Step 2: Triangle angle sum = 180. One angle = 90. The other two are equal: x + x + 90 = 180. 2x = 90. x = 45. Each of the other angles = **45 degrees**. (This is a 45-45-90 triangle.)

**Example B: Angles + Circles**

Two supplementary angles are formed by a diameter and a radius of a circle. One angle measures (2x + 10) degrees and the other measures (x + 50) degrees. Find x and both angle measures. If the radius is 7 cm, find the circumference.

Step 1: (2x + 10) + (x + 50) = 180. 3x + 60 = 180. 3x = 120. x = 40.
Step 2: Angle 1 = 2(40) + 10 = 90 degrees. Angle 2 = 40 + 50 = 90 degrees.
Step 3: C = 2 * 3.14 * 7 = **43.96 cm**.

**Example C: Cross-Section + Dimensions**

A rectangular prism is 15 cm long, 8 cm wide, and 10 cm tall. A horizontal cross-section is taken. What is the area of the cross-section?

Step 1: Horizontal cross-section of a rectangular prism = rectangle with the same length and width as the base.
Step 2: Cross-section dimensions: 15 cm by 8 cm.
Step 3: Area = 15 * 8 = **120 sq cm**.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Mixed Problems**

1. A map uses the scale 1 cm = 15 km. A circular lake has a diameter of 3 cm on the map. What is the actual circumference of the lake? Use pi = 3.14.
   - Actual diameter = 3 * 15 = 45 km. C = 3.14 * 45 = **141.3 km**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "First convert to actual dimensions, then use the circle formula."

2. Two lines intersect. One angle measures (3x + 5) degrees. The vertical angle measures 65 degrees. Find x. Then find the measure of each of the four angles.
   - Vertical angles are equal: 3x + 5 = 65. 3x = 60. x = 20. Vertical angles = 65 degrees each. Adjacent angles = 180 - 65 = 115 degrees each.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Which property tells us vertical angles are equal?"

**Activity 2: Error Analysis**

3. A student found the circumference of a circle with radius 6 cm:
   "C = pi * r^2 = 3.14 * 36 = 113.04 cm"
   Find and correct the error.
   - The student used the area formula instead of the circumference formula. Correct: C = 2 * pi * r = 2 * 3.14 * 6 = **37.68 cm**. (113.04 is the area, not the circumference.)

4. A student said: "A triangle has angles of 50, 60, and 80 degrees." Is this possible?
   - 50 + 60 + 80 = 190. Not 180. **This is not possible.** A triangle's angles must sum to exactly 180.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. A blueprint uses the scale 1 cm = 5 m. A room is drawn as 6 cm by 4 cm. What is the actual area of the room?

2. Two complementary angles measure (x + 15) degrees and 40 degrees. Find x.

3. Find the area and circumference of a circle with a radius of 8 cm. Use pi = 3.14.

4. A right rectangular prism is sliced vertically through the center of the longest dimension. If the prism is 20 cm long, 10 cm wide, and 12 cm tall, what is the shape and dimensions of the cross-section?

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

5. A triangle has angles of (2x) degrees, (x + 25) degrees, and 75 degrees. Find x. Is this an acute, right, or obtuse triangle?

6. A circular park has a diameter of 50 m on a map drawn at the scale 1 cm = 10 m. What is the diameter of the park on the map? What is the actual area of the park?

7. A square with side 16 cm has a circle inscribed inside it (the circle's diameter equals the square's side). What is the ratio of the area of the circle to the area of the square? Express as a decimal.

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

8. Two intersecting lines form four angles. One angle is 35 degrees more than the adjacent angle. Find all four angle measures.

9. A cylindrical water tank has a radius of 4 m and a height of 10 m. You slice it horizontally at any height. What is the area of the cross-section? If you slice it vertically through the center, what shape do you get and what are its dimensions?

10. A scale drawing (1 cm = 6 m) shows a rectangular yard with a semicircular garden at one end. The yard is drawn as 5 cm by 3 cm, and the semicircle has a diameter equal to the short side. Find the actual total area of the yard plus the garden.

---

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A circle has a circumference of 31.4 cm. What is its area? Use pi = 3.14.

**Problem 2:** On a scale drawing (1 cm = 8 m), a triangular plot is drawn with two sides forming a right angle. The sides forming the right angle are 3 cm and 4 cm on the drawing. What are the actual lengths of these two sides? What is the actual area of the triangular plot?

**Mastery:** 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated. 1 out of 2 = Review specific skill area. 0 out of 2 = Additional review needed before assessment.

---

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. Actual dimensions: 6 * 5 = 30 m by 4 * 5 = 20 m. Area = 30 * 20 = **600 sq m**.
2. (x + 15) + 40 = 90. x + 55 = 90. x = **35 degrees**.
3. A = 3.14 * 64 = **200.96 sq cm**. C = 2 * 3.14 * 8 = **50.24 cm**.
4. Shape: **rectangle**, 20 cm by 12 cm (the slice passes through the length and the height, since the cut is vertical through the longest dimension).
5. 2x + (x + 25) + 75 = 180. 3x + 100 = 180. 3x = 80. x = 80/3 approximately 26.67. Angles: 2(80/3) = 160/3 approximately 53.3 degrees, 80/3 + 25 = 155/3 approximately 51.7 degrees, 75 degrees. All angles are less than 90, so this is an **acute triangle**. Check: 53.3 + 51.7 + 75 = 180.
6. Map diameter: 50 / 10 = **5 cm**. Actual radius = 25 m. Actual area = 3.14 * 625 = **1,962.5 sq m**.
7. Square area = 256 sq cm. Circle: r = 8. A = 3.14 * 64 = 200.96 sq cm. Ratio = 200.96 / 256 = **0.785** (which is pi/4, approximately 0.785).
8. Let the smaller angle = x. Adjacent angle = x + 35. Linear pair: x + (x + 35) = 180. 2x + 35 = 180. 2x = 145. x = 72.5. The four angles: **72.5, 107.5, 72.5, 107.5 degrees**.
9. Horizontal cross-section: **circle** with area = 3.14 * 16 = **50.24 sq m**. Vertical slice through center: **rectangle**, 8 m wide (diameter) by 10 m tall (height).
10. Actual yard: 5 * 6 = 30 m by 3 * 6 = 18 m. Yard area = 30 * 18 = 540 sq m. Semicircle diameter = 18 m, radius = 9 m. Semicircle area = (1/2) * 3.14 * 81 = 127.17 sq m. Total = 540 + 127.17 = **667.17 sq m**.

### Exit Ticket

1. C = 31.4 cm. Find r: 31.4 = 2 * 3.14 * r. 31.4 = 6.28r. r = 5 cm. Area = 3.14 * 25 = **78.5 sq cm**.
2. Actual sides: 3 * 8 = 24 m and 4 * 8 = 32 m. Area of right triangle = (1/2) * 24 * 32 = **384 sq m**.

## Teacher Notes

- This lesson serves as both review and assessment preparation. Use the student's performance to identify which topics need additional practice before the unit assessment.
- If the student struggles with a particular problem type, note which lesson(s) it relates to and spend extra time reviewing that material before the assessment.
- The error analysis problems are especially valuable. They require deeper understanding than just getting the right answer -- the student must recognize what went wrong and why.
- Problem 7 introduces the ratio pi/4 (approximately 0.785), which is the ratio of a circle's area to its circumscribing square's area. This is a beautiful and useful constant that appears throughout mathematics and physics.
- For students who are confident with all topics, Problem 10 is an excellent culminating problem that combines scale drawings, rectangles, and semicircles in a realistic context.
- Encourage the student to make their own formula sheet as test preparation. The act of writing out all the formulas and relationships helps with memorization and understanding.
- If time permits, have the student create one original problem for each unit topic and exchange with the facilitator to solve. Creating problems is a higher-level skill than solving them.
