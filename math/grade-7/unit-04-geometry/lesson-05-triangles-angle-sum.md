# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 4, Lesson 5

## Topic
Triangles: Angle Sum Property

## Learning Objectives
- State and apply the triangle angle-sum property: the interior angles of any triangle sum to 180 degrees.
- Verify the angle-sum property through measurement and a paper-tearing demonstration.
- Write and solve equations to find missing angle measures in triangles.
- Classify triangles by their angles (acute, right, obtuse) and use the angle sum to reason about triangle types.

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 3: Complementary and Supplementary Angles.
- Lesson 4: Vertical and Adjacent Angles.
- Ability to measure angles with a protractor (Grade 6).
- Ability to solve one-step and two-step equations (Unit 3).

## Materials Needed
- Protractor.
- Ruler.
- Scissors (for paper-tearing demonstration).
- Plain paper (for drawing and tearing triangles).
- Student worksheet.
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.G.2 -- Draw geometric shapes with given conditions; 7.G.5 -- Use facts about supplementary, complementary, vertical, and adjacent angles in a multi-step problem to write and solve simple equations for an unknown angle in a figure.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review supplementary angles and solve equations.

1. Two angles form a linear pair. One measures 53 degrees. Find the other. (180 - 53 = 127 degrees)

2. Solve: x + 47 + 85 = 180. (x + 132 = 180. x = 48 degrees.)

3. A triangle has angles of 60, 60, and 60 degrees. What is the sum of these angles? (60 + 60 + 60 = 180 degrees.) What type of triangle is this? (Equilateral.)

Say: "That sum of 180 degrees is not a coincidence. Today we are going to prove that the angles in EVERY triangle add up to 180 degrees."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Interior angles** -- the angles inside a polygon, formed by two sides of the polygon meeting at a vertex.
- **Triangle angle-sum property** -- the three interior angles of any triangle always sum to exactly 180 degrees.
- **Acute triangle** -- a triangle in which all three angles are less than 90 degrees.
- **Right triangle** -- a triangle with one angle that is exactly 90 degrees.
- **Obtuse triangle** -- a triangle with one angle that is greater than 90 degrees.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Hands-on discovery: paper-tearing demonstration.**

Say: "Draw any triangle on a piece of paper. It can be any shape -- big, small, skinny, fat. Use a ruler to make the sides straight."

Say: "Now label the three angles as A, B, and C. Tear off the three corners of the triangle." (Demonstrate tearing.) "Now arrange the three torn corners so that the points all meet at a single point, with the straight edges along a line."

Say: "What do you notice?" (The three angles form a straight line -- they sum to 180 degrees.)

Say: "Try it again with a different triangle. Does it still work?" (Yes.) "It always works, no matter what triangle you draw. The three interior angles of any triangle sum to 180 degrees."

**Step 2 -- Measurement verification.**

Draw three different triangles on the board: one acute, one right, one obtuse. Have the student measure all three angles in each triangle with a protractor and add them up.

Say: "Due to measurement imprecision, you might get 179 or 181, but the mathematical truth is exactly 180 degrees. The more precisely you measure, the closer you get to 180."

**Step 3 -- Using the property to find missing angles.**

Say: "If we know two angles in a triangle, we can find the third. We set up an equation."

Write on the board: A triangle has angles of 50 degrees, 70 degrees, and x degrees. Find x.

50 + 70 + x = 180
120 + x = 180
x = 60 degrees

Say: "The third angle is 60 degrees. Since all three angles are less than 90 degrees, this is an acute triangle."

**Step 4 -- Variable expressions for angles.**

Write: A triangle has angles of (2x) degrees, (3x) degrees, and (x + 30) degrees. Find x and all three angles.

2x + 3x + (x + 30) = 180
6x + 30 = 180
6x = 150
x = 25

Angle 1 = 2(25) = 50 degrees. Angle 2 = 3(25) = 75 degrees. Angle 3 = 25 + 30 = 55 degrees.
Check: 50 + 75 + 55 = 180. Correct.

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Finding a Missing Angle**

A triangle has angles of 38 degrees and 94 degrees. Find the third angle.

Step 1: The sum of all three angles = 180.
Step 2: 38 + 94 + x = 180.
Step 3: 132 + x = 180.
Step 4: x = 180 - 132 = **48 degrees**.
Step 5: Since one angle (94 degrees) is greater than 90, this is an **obtuse triangle**.

**Example B: Isosceles Triangle**

An isosceles triangle has two equal angles of 70 degrees each. Find the third angle.

Step 1: 70 + 70 + x = 180.
Step 2: 140 + x = 180.
Step 3: x = **40 degrees**.

**Example C: Right Triangle**

A right triangle has one angle of 90 degrees and another angle of 37 degrees. Find the third angle.

Step 1: 90 + 37 + x = 180.
Step 2: 127 + x = 180.
Step 3: x = **53 degrees**.
Step 4: Notice that 37 + 53 = 90. In a right triangle, the two non-right angles are always complementary.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Measure and Verify**

1. Draw a triangle with sides roughly 6 cm, 8 cm, and 10 cm. Measure all three angles with a protractor. Add them up.
   - Expected: approximately 180 degrees (this is close to a right triangle: roughly 37, 53, 90 degrees).
   - **Facilitator tip:** "If the sum is a little off from 180, that is due to measurement -- the exact answer is always 180."

2. A triangle has angles of 65 degrees and 45 degrees. Find the third angle without measuring.
   - 65 + 45 + x = 180. 110 + x = 180. x = **70 degrees**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "What equation do you set up? The three angles equal what number?"

**Activity 2: Solving with Variables**

3. A triangle has angles of x degrees, (x + 20) degrees, and (x + 40) degrees. Find x and all three angles.
   - x + (x + 20) + (x + 40) = 180. 3x + 60 = 180. 3x = 120. x = 40.
   - Angles: 40, 60, 80 degrees. Check: 40 + 60 + 80 = 180.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Combine like terms first, then solve the equation just like in Unit 3."

4. Can a triangle have angles of 50, 60, and 80 degrees? Why or why not?
   - 50 + 60 + 80 = 190. No -- the sum exceeds 180, so these cannot be the angles of a triangle.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Always check: do the angles add up to exactly 180?"

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. A triangle has angles of 72 degrees and 58 degrees. Find the third angle.

2. A right triangle has one angle of 28 degrees. Find the third angle.

3. An isosceles triangle has two equal base angles of 55 degrees each. Find the vertex angle.

4. Can a triangle have two right angles? Explain.

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

5. A triangle has angles of (3x) degrees, (2x + 10) degrees, and (x - 10) degrees. Find x and all three angles.

6. One angle of a triangle is twice the second angle and three times the third angle. Find all three angles.

7. An exterior angle of a triangle is formed by extending one side. If two interior angles of a triangle are 55 degrees and 48 degrees, what is the exterior angle adjacent to the third interior angle? (Hint: the third interior angle and the exterior angle form a linear pair.)

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

8. In triangle ABC, angle A is 20 degrees more than angle B, and angle C is 20 degrees more than angle A. Find all three angles.

9. A triangle has angles in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. Find all three angle measures.

10. Can a triangle have angles of x degrees, 2x degrees, and (180 - 3x) degrees for any positive value of x? For what values of x does this represent a valid triangle? (Hint: each angle must be greater than 0 degrees.)

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A triangle has angles of 42 degrees, 85 degrees, and x degrees. Find x.

**Problem 2:** A triangle has angles of (2x + 5) degrees, (3x) degrees, and 70 degrees. Find x and all three angles.

**Mastery:** 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated. 1 out of 2 = Review errors. 0 out of 2 = Reteach with hands-on demonstration.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. 72 + 58 + x = 180. 130 + x = 180. x = **50 degrees**.
2. 90 + 28 + x = 180. 118 + x = 180. x = **62 degrees**. (Note: 28 + 62 = 90 -- the acute angles are complementary.)
3. 55 + 55 + x = 180. 110 + x = 180. x = **70 degrees**.
4. No. Two right angles = 90 + 90 = 180 degrees. The third angle would be 0 degrees, which is impossible. **A triangle cannot have two right angles.**
5. 3x + (2x + 10) + (x - 10) = 180. 6x = 180. x = 30. Angles: 3(30) = **90 degrees**, 2(30) + 10 = **70 degrees**, 30 - 10 = **20 degrees**. Check: 90 + 70 + 20 = 180. This is a right triangle.
6. Let the third angle = x. Then the second angle = 3x (since the first is three times the third) -- wait. Let us re-read: "One angle is twice the second and three times the third." Let the first angle = A. A = 2B and A = 3C. So B = A/2 and C = A/3. Sum: A + A/2 + A/3 = 180. Multiply by 6: 6A + 3A + 2A = 1080. 11A = 1080. A = 1080/11 approximately 98.18 degrees. Alternatively: let the third angle = x. First angle = 3x, second angle = 3x/2. Then 3x + 3x/2 + x = 180. Multiply by 2: 6x + 3x + 2x = 360. 11x = 360. x = 360/11 approximately 32.7 degrees. Angles: approximately **98.2, 49.1, 32.7 degrees**. Check: 98.2 + 49.1 + 32.7 = 180. (Exact values: 1080/11, 540/11, 360/11.)
7. Third interior angle = 180 - 55 - 48 = 77 degrees. Exterior angle = 180 - 77 = **103 degrees**. (Note: the exterior angle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles: 55 + 48 = 103. This is the Exterior Angle Theorem.)
8. Let angle B = x. Angle A = x + 20. Angle C = (x + 20) + 20 = x + 40. Sum: x + (x + 20) + (x + 40) = 180. 3x + 60 = 180. 3x = 120. x = 40. Angles: B = **40 degrees**, A = **60 degrees**, C = **80 degrees**. Check: 40 + 60 + 80 = 180.
9. Let the angles be 2k, 3k, and 4k. Then 2k + 3k + 4k = 180. 9k = 180. k = 20. Angles: **40, 60, 80 degrees**. Check: 40 + 60 + 80 = 180.
10. Sum: x + 2x + (180 - 3x) = 180 -- this simplifies to 180 = 180, which is always true. But each angle must be positive: x > 0, 2x > 0 (true when x > 0), and 180 - 3x > 0 means x < 60. So this is a valid triangle for any **0 < x < 60**.

### Exit Ticket

1. 42 + 85 + x = 180. 127 + x = 180. x = **53 degrees**.
2. (2x + 5) + 3x + 70 = 180. 5x + 75 = 180. 5x = 105. x = 21. Angles: 2(21) + 5 = **47 degrees**, 3(21) = **63 degrees**, **70 degrees**. Check: 47 + 63 + 70 = 180.

## Teacher Notes

- The paper-tearing activity is powerful because it lets students physically see that three angles form a straight line. If scissors are not available, students can fold the corners instead.
- Problem 4 ("Can a triangle have two right angles?") is an excellent reasoning question. Encourage the student to explain WHY, not just say "no."
- The Exterior Angle Theorem (Problem 7) is a preview of Grade 8 content. If the student notices that the exterior angle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles, acknowledge and celebrate this observation. It follows directly from the angle-sum property.
- Problem 10 in Tier 3 is an algebraic exploration. The equation reduces to a tautology (180 = 180), meaning the sum is always 180 regardless of x. The real constraint is that each angle must be positive, which gives the range 0 < x < 60.
- Common error: students forget to include the right angle when finding missing angles in a right triangle. Remind them: "The right angle IS one of the three angles -- it is 90 degrees."
- Connection to circles: in Lessons 6-7, students will work with central angles and circles. The angle-sum property reinforces that angles have fixed relationships, a theme that continues throughout geometry.
