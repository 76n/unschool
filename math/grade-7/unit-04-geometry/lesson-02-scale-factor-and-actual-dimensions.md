# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 4, Lesson 2

## Topic
Scale Factor and Actual Dimensions

## Learning Objectives
- Define and compute the scale factor between a drawing and the actual object.
- Use a scale factor to find missing dimensions in either direction (drawing to actual or actual to drawing).
- Reproduce a figure at a different scale on grid paper.
- Explain how changing the scale factor affects the size of the drawing.

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: Scale Drawings (reading and interpreting scales).
- Ability to set up and solve proportions (Unit 1).
- Fluency with multiplication and division of decimals and fractions.

## Materials Needed
- Ruler (metric preferred).
- Grid paper (two sheets per student).
- Colored pencils (optional, for drawing).
- Student worksheet.
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.G.1 -- Solve problems involving scale drawings of geometric figures, including computing actual lengths and areas from a scale drawing and reproducing a scale drawing at a different scale.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review Lesson 1 skills and activate proportional reasoning.

1. A map uses the scale 1 cm = 12 km. Two towns are 5.5 cm apart on the map. What is the actual distance? (5.5 x 12 = 66 km)

2. A blueprint uses the scale 1 cm = 4 m. A room is actually 36 m long. How long is it on the blueprint? (36 / 4 = 9 cm)

Say: "In Lesson 1, we used scales like '1 cm = 4 m' to convert between drawing and actual measurements. Today, we are going to zoom in on the idea of scale factor -- a single number that tells you how much bigger or smaller the actual object is compared to the drawing."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Scale factor** -- the ratio of a length in the actual object to the corresponding length in the drawing (or vice versa, depending on direction). If a scale drawing uses 1 cm = 5 m (= 500 cm), the scale factor from drawing to actual is 500.
- **Enlarge** -- to make a figure bigger; the scale factor from original to copy is greater than 1.
- **Reduce** -- to make a figure smaller; the scale factor from original to copy is less than 1.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Scale factor from a given scale.**

Say: "When we say the scale is 1 cm = 5 m, that is a ratio: 1 cm on the drawing corresponds to 5 m in real life. The scale factor from drawing to actual is 5 m per 1 cm, or simply 5 (when we keep the same units, it would be 500, but in scale drawing problems we often work across units)."

Say: "For our purposes, the scale factor tells us: multiply any drawing measurement by the scale factor to get the actual measurement. Divide any actual measurement by the scale factor to get the drawing measurement."

Write on the board:
- Actual = Drawing measurement x Scale factor
- Drawing = Actual measurement / Scale factor

**Step 2 -- Computing a scale factor from two measurements.**

Say: "Sometimes you are not given the scale directly. Instead, you know the actual length and the drawing length, and you need to find the scale factor."

Example: A room is 18 meters long. On the drawing, it is 6 cm long. What is the scale factor?

Scale factor = Actual / Drawing = 18 m / 6 cm = 3 m per cm.
The scale is 1 cm = 3 m, and the scale factor is 3.

**Step 3 -- Reproducing a figure at a different scale.**

Say: "Architects sometimes need to redraw a plan at a larger or smaller scale. If you have a drawing at one scale and need to create a new drawing at a different scale, you can convert each measurement."

Example: A floor plan is drawn at 1 cm = 2 m. You need to redraw it at 1 cm = 4 m (a smaller drawing). A room that is 8 cm on the original drawing represents 8 x 2 = 16 m. On the new drawing: 16 / 4 = 4 cm.

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Finding the Scale Factor**

A scale model of a car is 15 cm long. The actual car is 4.5 m (450 cm) long.

Step 1: Both measurements must be in the same units. Convert 4.5 m to 450 cm.
Step 2: Scale factor = Actual / Model = 450 / 15 = 30.
Step 3: The scale factor is **30**. The actual car is 30 times as long as the model.
Step 4: The scale is **1 cm = 30 cm**, or equivalently **1 : 30**.

**Example B: Using Scale Factor to Find Dimensions**

A scale drawing has a scale factor of 1 cm = 8 m. A rectangular playground is drawn as 6 cm by 3.5 cm.

Actual length: 6 x 8 = **48 m**
Actual width: 3.5 x 8 = **28 m**
Actual perimeter: 2(48) + 2(28) = 96 + 56 = **152 m**
Actual area: 48 x 28 = **1,344 square meters**

**Example C: Reproducing at a New Scale**

A map is drawn at a scale of 1 cm = 5 km. A river is 8 cm long on this map. Redraw the river on a new map with a scale of 1 cm = 10 km.

Step 1: Find the actual length. 8 x 5 = 40 km.
Step 2: Convert to the new scale. 40 / 10 = 4 cm.
Step 3: On the new map, the river is **4 cm** long.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Compute Scale Factors**

1. A model building is 12 cm tall. The actual building is 36 m tall. What is the scale factor?
   - Scale factor = 36 m / 12 cm = **3 m per cm** (or 1 cm = 3 m).
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Divide the actual measurement by the drawing measurement."

2. A photograph shows a flower that is 8 cm wide. The actual flower is 4 cm wide. What is the scale factor? Is this an enlargement or a reduction?
   - Scale factor from actual to photo = 8 / 4 = 2. The photo **enlarges** the flower by a factor of 2.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "When the image is bigger than the real object, it is an enlargement."

3. On a blueprint, a wall drawn as 9 cm is actually 27 m long. What is the scale?
   - 27 / 9 = 3. Scale: **1 cm = 3 m**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "What do you divide to find the scale?"

**Activity 2: Reproduce a Figure at a New Scale**

Provide grid paper. Draw a simple L-shaped figure with the following drawing measurements (at scale 1 cm = 2 m):
- The L is 6 cm tall and 4 cm wide, with a notch of 3 cm by 2 cm removed from the upper right.

4. What are the actual dimensions of the L-shape?
   - Full height: 6 x 2 = 12 m. Full width: 4 x 2 = 8 m. Notch: 3 x 2 = 6 m by 2 x 2 = 4 m.

5. Redraw the L-shape on grid paper at a new scale of 1 cm = 4 m.
   - New height: 12 / 4 = 3 cm. New width: 8 / 4 = 2 cm. New notch: 6 / 4 = 1.5 cm by 4 / 4 = 1 cm.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "First convert each drawing measurement to actual, then convert from actual to the new drawing scale."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. A model airplane is 20 cm long. The actual airplane is 40 m long. What is the scale factor? Write the scale in the form "1 cm = ___ m."

2. A scale drawing uses the scale 1 cm = 6 m. A building is drawn as 9 cm tall and 12 cm wide. What are the actual height and width? What is the actual area of the building's front face?

3. A drawing of a garden uses the scale 1 cm = 3 m. The garden is drawn as 10 cm by 7 cm. Redraw the garden at a scale of 1 cm = 5 m. What will the new drawing dimensions be?

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

4. On a map with scale 1 cm = 25 km, a lake is drawn as 3.2 cm long. On a second map, the same lake is drawn as 8 cm long. What is the scale of the second map?

5. A scale drawing shows a rectangular room as 5 cm by 4 cm, with an actual area of 120 square meters. What is the scale factor? (Hint: find the actual dimensions first.)

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

6. A scale drawing of a park is made at the scale 1 cm = 20 m. The park is rectangular, 8 cm by 5 cm on the drawing. A path runs diagonally across the park from one corner to the opposite corner. On the drawing, the path is 9.4 cm long. What is the actual length of the path? (Bonus: verify using the Pythagorean idea: 160^2 + 100^2 = 25,600 + 10,000 = 35,600. The square root of 35,600 is approximately 188.7 m.)

7. An architect has two scale drawings of the same house. Drawing A uses the scale 1 cm = 2 m. Drawing B uses the scale 1 cm = 5 m. The living room is 8 cm wide on Drawing A. How wide is the living room on Drawing B?

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A model train is 18 cm long. The actual train is 54 m long. What is the scale factor? Express the scale as "1 cm = ___ m."

**Problem 2:** A drawing uses the scale 1 cm = 5 m. A room is drawn as 7 cm by 4 cm. You need to redraw it at the scale 1 cm = 10 m. What will the new drawing dimensions be?

**Mastery:** 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated. 1 out of 2 = Review errors. 0 out of 2 = Reteach scale factor concept.

---

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. Scale factor = 40 m / 20 cm = 2 m per cm. Scale: **1 cm = 2 m**.
2. Actual height: 9 x 6 = 54 m. Actual width: 12 x 6 = 72 m. Actual area: 54 x 72 = **3,888 square meters**.
3. Actual dimensions: 10 x 3 = 30 m by 7 x 3 = 21 m. New drawing: 30 / 5 = 6 cm by 21 / 5 = 4.2 cm. New dimensions: **6 cm by 4.2 cm**.
4. Actual lake length: 3.2 x 25 = 80 km. On second map: 80 / 8 = 10 km per cm. Scale of second map: **1 cm = 10 km**.
5. Drawing area: 5 x 4 = 20 sq cm. Actual area = 120 sq m. Area scale factor = 120 / 20 = 6. But scale factor for length is the square root of the area scale factor... Actually: Let the scale be 1 cm = k m. Then actual dimensions are 5k by 4k. Area = 20k^2 = 120. k^2 = 6. k = sqrt(6) approximately 2.45. Scale factor: **1 cm = approximately 2.45 m**. (Alternatively: if actual area = 120 and ratio of sides is 5:4, then actual dimensions are 5k and 4k where 20k^2 = 120, k = sqrt(6).)
6. Actual path length: 9.4 x 20 = **188 meters**. Verification: actual park is 160 m by 100 m. Diagonal = sqrt(160^2 + 100^2) = sqrt(25,600 + 10,000) = sqrt(35,600) approximately 188.7 m. The drawing measurement of 9.4 cm is consistent.
7. Actual width: 8 x 2 = 16 m. Drawing B width: 16 / 5 = **3.2 cm**.

### Exit Ticket

1. Scale factor = 54 / 18 = 3. Scale: **1 cm = 3 m**.
2. Actual dimensions: 7 x 5 = 35 m by 4 x 5 = 20 m. New drawing: 35 / 10 = 3.5 cm by 20 / 10 = 2 cm. New dimensions: **3.5 cm by 2 cm**.

## Teacher Notes

- The distinction between "scale" and "scale factor" can be confusing. For this lesson, reinforce that the scale factor is the single multiplier that converts from drawing to actual (or actual to drawing). The scale is the ratio written out (e.g., 1 cm = 5 m).
- Problem 5 in the Tier 2 section introduces a subtle point: the area scale factor is the square of the length scale factor. This is a stretch concept; do not require it for all students, but use it to challenge those who are ready.
- When reproducing figures at a new scale, emphasize the two-step process: (1) convert to actual, (2) convert to new drawing. Some students try to go directly from one scale to another, which is valid but requires computing the ratio of scale factors. The two-step method is more reliable.
- Connection to Unit 1: scale factor problems are proportional reasoning problems. If students struggle, return to the proportion setup from Unit 1.
- Next lesson: Angle Relationships -- Complementary and Supplementary. This is a shift from measurement-based geometry to angle-based geometry. Preview by asking: "What do you remember about angles from last year?"
