# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 3, Lesson 7

## Topic
Writing Equations from Real-World Situations

## Learning Objectives
- Define a variable to represent an unknown quantity in a real-world context.
- Translate verbal descriptions and real-world situations into two-step equations.
- Solve the equation and interpret the solution in context.
- Verify the solution makes sense within the original situation.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to solve two-step equations (Lesson 5).
- Familiarity with translating verbal phrases into algebraic expressions (Grade 6).
- Understanding of operations with rational numbers (Unit 2).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.EE.4a -- Solve word problems leading to equations of the form px + q = r and p(x + q) = r, where p, q, and r are specific rational numbers. Solve equations of these forms fluently. Compare an algebraic solution to an arithmetic solution, identifying the sequence of operations used in each approach.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Practice translating verbal phrases into algebraic expressions.

Translate each phrase into an algebraic expression:
1. "Five more than a number" → n + 5
2. "Three times a number, decreased by 7" → 3n - 7
3. "Twice a number, plus 10, equals 26" → 2n + 10 = 26
4. Ask: "In problem 3, we wrote an equation. Today we will practice setting up equations like this from real-world stories and solving them."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Define a variable** -- choose a letter to represent the unknown quantity, and write a clear statement of what it represents. Example: "Let h = the number of hours worked."
- **Translate** -- convert a verbal description into a mathematical equation.
- **Interpret** -- explain what the solution means in the context of the original problem.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- The four-step process for word problems.**

Say: "Every word problem we solve in this lesson will follow four steps."

Write on the board:
1. **Define** -- What is unknown? Assign it a variable.
2. **Translate** -- Write an equation that models the situation.
3. **Solve** -- Solve the equation using inverse operations.
4. **Interpret** -- State the answer in a complete sentence and check that it makes sense.

**Step 2 -- Model the process with a complete example.**

Say: "A streaming service charges $8 per month plus a one-time setup fee of $15. After several months, you have paid a total of $63. How many months have you been subscribed?"

**Define:** Let m = the number of months.

**Translate:** The total cost is $8 per month times m months, plus the $15 setup fee.
- 8m + 15 = 63

**Solve:**
- 8m + 15 - 15 = 63 - 15
- 8m = 48
- m = 6

**Interpret:** "You have been subscribed for 6 months."

**Check:** 8(6) + 15 = 48 + 15 = 63. This matches the total paid.

Say: "Notice how I defined my variable first. This is critical -- you must know what your variable represents before you can write an equation."

**Step 3 -- A second example with a different structure.**

Say: "Jasmine has some money in her wallet. She spends $12 on lunch and then divides the remaining money equally among 4 friends. Each friend gets $5. How much money did Jasmine start with?"

**Define:** Let d = the amount of money Jasmine started with (in dollars).

**Translate:** She starts with d, subtracts 12, then divides by 4, and the result is 5.
- (d - 12) / 4 = 5

Say: "This is a p(x + q) = r form. We can also think of it as: the remaining money divided among 4 gives 5 each, so the remaining money is 4 times 5."

**Solve:**
- Multiply both sides by 4: d - 12 = 20
- Add 12: d = 32

**Interpret:** "Jasmine started with $32."

**Check:** 32 - 12 = 20. Divide 20 by 4 = 5. Each friend gets $5. Correct.

**Step 4 -- Key phrases and their mathematical meanings.**

| Verbal Phrase | Mathematical Operation |
|---|---|
| more than, increased by, plus | addition (+) |
| less than, decreased by, minus | subtraction (-) |
| times, product of, each, per | multiplication (x) |
| divided by, split equally, per (in some contexts) | division (/) |
| is, equals, was, gives, results in | equals sign (=) |
| total, altogether, combined | sum of parts |

Say: "Watch out for 'less than' -- it reverses order. 'Five less than a number' is n - 5, not 5 - n."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:**
"A taxi ride costs $3 plus $2 per mile. If the total fare was $17, how many miles was the ride?"

Define: Let m = number of miles.
Equation: 2m + 3 = 17
Solve: 2m = 14, m = **7 miles**.
Check: 2(7) + 3 = 14 + 3 = 17.

**Example B:**
"Marcus earns $9 per hour at his part-time job. After buying a $15 book, he has $57 left from his earnings. How many hours did he work?"

Define: Let h = number of hours worked.
Equation: 9h - 15 = 57
Solve: 9h = 72, h = **8 hours**.
Check: 9(8) - 15 = 72 - 15 = 57.

**Example C:**
"A group of friends orders pizzas at $12 each and pays $5 for a delivery fee. The total bill is $41. How many pizzas did they order?"

Define: Let p = number of pizzas.
Equation: 12p + 5 = 41
Solve: 12p = 36, p = **3 pizzas**.
Check: 12(3) + 5 = 36 + 5 = 41.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. "An online store charges $6 per item plus $4 for shipping. You spent $34 total. How many items did you buy?"
   - Let n = number of items. 6n + 4 = 34. 6n = 30, n = **5 items**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "What repeats? The $6 per item. What is added once? The $4 shipping."

2. "A rectangle's length is 5 cm more than its width. If the length is 18 cm, what is the width?"
   - Let w = width. w + 5 = 18. w = **13 cm**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "This is actually one step, but it practices translation."

3. "After depositing $25 per week for several weeks and starting with $30, your savings account has $205. How many weeks have you been saving?"
   - Let w = weeks. 25w + 30 = 205. 25w = 175, w = **7 weeks**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "The starting balance is the constant term."

4. "Three friends split the cost of a gift equally, and each also paid $4 for wrapping. If each person paid $16 total, how much did the gift cost?"
   - Let g = cost of gift. g/3 + 4 = 16. g/3 = 12, g = **$36**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Each person's share of the gift is g divided by 3."

5. "The temperature was -5 degrees Celsius at midnight. It rose the same number of degrees each hour. By 6 AM, the temperature was 13 degrees. How many degrees did it rise per hour?"
   - Let r = degrees per hour. -5 + 6r = 13. 6r = 18, r = **3 degrees per hour**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "The starting temperature is -5. Over 6 hours, the total rise is 6r."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Write and Solve Equations

1. A plumber charges $50 for a house call plus $35 per hour of work. If the total bill was $155, how many hours did the plumber work?
2. You have a coupon for $10 off your purchase. Each book costs $7. After using the coupon, you pay $25. How many books did you buy?
3. A parking garage charges $3 to enter plus $2 for each hour parked. You paid $13. How many hours were you parked?

### Part B: More Complex Situations

4. Maria and her brother collect baseball cards. Maria has three times as many cards as her brother, minus 10 cards. If Maria has 50 cards, how many does her brother have?
5. The perimeter of a square is found by multiplying the side length by 4. If a square's perimeter is 52 cm, what is the side length? (Write and solve an equation.)
6. A cell phone plan costs $35 per month. There is also a one-time activation fee. After 4 months, the total paid is $160. What was the activation fee?

### Part C: Multi-Step Word Problems

7. Two friends go to the movies. Tickets cost $9 each, and they share a popcorn that costs $6. If they split the entire cost equally and each pays $12, verify that this is correct by writing and solving an equation for the total cost.
8. A school fundraiser sells candles for $8 each. The school spent $120 on supplies. After selling all the candles, the profit was $200. How many candles were sold? (Profit = Revenue - Cost)

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. Let h = hours. 35h + 50 = 155. 35h = 105, h = **3 hours**. Check: 35(3) + 50 = 105 + 50 = 155.
2. Let b = number of books. 7b - 10 = 25. 7b = 35, b = **5 books**. Check: 7(5) - 10 = 35 - 10 = 25.
3. Let h = hours. 2h + 3 = 13. 2h = 10, h = **5 hours**. Check: 2(5) + 3 = 13.

**Part B**
4. Let c = brother's cards. 3c - 10 = 50. 3c = 60, c = **20 cards**. Check: 3(20) - 10 = 60 - 10 = 50.
5. Let s = side length. 4s = 52. s = **13 cm**. Check: 4(13) = 52.
6. Let a = activation fee. 35(4) + a = 160. 140 + a = 160. a = **$20**. Check: 140 + 20 = 160.

**Part C**
7. Total cost: 2(9) + 6 = 18 + 6 = 24. Each pays: 24 / 2 = **$12**. Equation: (2 x 9 + 6) / 2 = 12, which gives 24 / 2 = 12. Correct.
8. Let c = number of candles. Revenue = 8c. Profit = 8c - 120 = 200. 8c = 320, c = **40 candles**. Check: 8(40) - 120 = 320 - 120 = 200.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
**Problem:** A school trip costs $5 per student plus a $60 bus rental fee. If the total cost is $185, how many students are going on the trip? Define a variable, write an equation, solve, and interpret your answer.

**Target response:**
Let s = number of students. 5s + 60 = 185. 5s = 125. s = **25 students**.
Check: 5(25) + 60 = 125 + 60 = 185.

**Scoring:**
- Variable defined, equation written, solved correctly, answer interpreted = Mastery demonstrated.
- Correct equation and solution but no variable definition or interpretation = Partial; emphasize the full 4-step process.
- Cannot write the equation = Reteach; practice identifying the repeated quantity (rate) and the one-time quantity (constant).

## Extension
- **Comparison Problem:** Plan A for a field trip costs $200 for the bus plus $3 per student. Plan B costs $8 per student with no bus fee. For what number of students do both plans cost the same? (200 + 3s = 8s, so 200 = 5s, s = 40 students.)
- **Create Your Own:** Write a real-world scenario that leads to the equation 4x + 10 = 50. Solve it and explain the context.
