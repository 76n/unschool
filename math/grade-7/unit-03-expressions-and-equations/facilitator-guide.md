# Facilitator Guide — Grade 7 Math, Unit 3: Expressions and Equations

**Grade:** 7
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 3 — Expressions and Equations
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (facilitator leads instruction; student works independently for practice)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit builds on students' prior work with one-step equations (Grade 6) and operations with rational numbers (Unit 2) to develop fluency with algebraic expressions and multi-step equations. Students begin by reviewing the structure of algebraic expressions and the commutative, associative, and distributive properties. They then learn to combine like terms, apply the distributive property with variables, and simplify complex expressions that require both skills. The unit progresses to solving two-step equations, equations with variables on both sides (introductory cases), and writing equations from real-world situations. The final portion introduces inequalities -- solving one- and two-step inequalities, graphing solutions on a number line, and applying inequalities to word problems. The unit closes with a comprehensive review.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Identify and combine like terms to simplify algebraic expressions
- Apply the distributive property to expand and factor expressions with variables
- Simplify multi-step expressions using both the distributive property and combining like terms
- Solve two-step equations of the form ax + b = c using inverse operations
- Solve equations with variables on both sides (simple cases)
- Translate real-world situations into two-step equations and solve them
- Solve one-step and two-step inequalities and graph solutions on a number line
- Write and solve inequalities from real-world contexts
- Explain and justify each step of an equation or inequality solution using properties of operations and equality/inequality

**Why this matters:** Expressions, equations, and inequalities are the core language of algebra. Every scientific formula, every budget spreadsheet, every engineering calculation relies on the ability to write, simplify, and solve equations. Students who master this unit will be prepared for the more complex equation work in Grade 8 and beyond -- systems of equations, linear functions, and eventually calculus. More importantly, they will develop the logical reasoning skills that come from justifying each step of a solution.

| 2 | Combining Like Terms | 1 |
| 3 | Distributive Property with Variables | 1-2 |
| 4 | Simplifying Complex Expressions | 1-2 |
| 5 | Solving Two-Step Equations | 1-2 |
| 6 | Solving Equations with Variables on Both Sides | 1-2 |

| 8 | Solving Inequalities | 1-2 |
| 9 | Inequality Word Problems | 1 |

| -- | Unit Assessment | Separate session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lessons 1 and 2 form a natural pair. If the student grasps the review quickly in Lesson 1, begin Lesson 2 in the same session.
- Lesson 3 (distributive property with variables) is a critical skill. Students who can distribute with numbers but struggle with variables need extra time here. Do not rush past this lesson.
- Lesson 4 combines distribution and combining like terms. This is where many students first encounter difficulty. Budget two sessions if the student is not yet automatic with Lessons 2-3.
- Lesson 5 (two-step equations) is the conceptual heart of the unit. The "undo" strategy must be solid before moving to Lesson 6. Allow extra time and consider revisiting if Lesson 6 reveals gaps.
- Lesson 6 (variables on both sides) is an extension. If the student is struggling with two-step equations, spend additional time on Lesson 5 and simplify Lesson 6 to exposure only.
- Lessons 8 and 9 introduce inequalities. The mechanics are similar to equations, but the sign-flip rule when multiplying or dividing by a negative number is a new concept that requires careful attention.
- Lesson 10 (review) can be extended to two sessions: one for structured review and one for mixed problem-solving.

## Lesson Summaries

| Lesson | Summary |
|--------|---------|
| 1 | Students review the structure of algebraic expressions (terms, coefficients, constants, variables) and revisit the commutative, associative, and distributive properties in preparation for simplifying expressions. |
| 2 | Students identify like terms (same variable raised to the same power) and simplify expressions by combining like terms, including terms with negative coefficients. |
| 3 | Students apply the distributive property to expand expressions of the form a(b + c) with variables and begin factoring simple expressions by identifying a common factor. |
| 4 | Students simplify multi-step expressions that require both distributing and combining like terms, working with increasingly complex expressions including negative coefficients and multiple variable terms. |
| 5 | Students solve two-step equations of the form ax + b = c by undoing operations in reverse order (addition/subtraction first, then multiplication/division). They verify solutions by substitution. |
| 6 | Students encounter equations with variables on both sides (e.g., 3x + 5 = x + 13) and learn to collect variable terms on one side using inverse operations. This is an introductory treatment. |
| 7 | Students translate real-world situations into two-step equations, define variables, write equations, solve them, and interpret the solution in context. |
| 8 | Students solve one-step and two-step inequalities, learn the sign-flip rule when multiplying or dividing by a negative number, and graph solution sets on a number line. |
| 9 | Students write inequalities from real-world contexts (budget constraints, minimum requirements, maximum capacities), solve them, and interpret the solution in context. |
| 10 | Students complete a comprehensive review covering all unit topics through mixed practice, error analysis, and multi-step problem-solving. |

## Facilitation Notes

### Building from Unit 2

This unit directly extends the rational number operations from Unit 2. Students will encounter negative coefficients, negative constants, and equations whose solutions are negative numbers. Reinforce that the sign rules learned in Unit 2 apply throughout this unit.

### The Transition from Arithmetic to Algebra

Many students are comfortable computing with specific numbers but struggle when variables are introduced. Key facilitation strategies:
- Use substitution to verify that algebraic rules "work" -- for example, after combining 3x + 5x into 8x, substitute x = 2 to verify: 3(2) + 5(2) = 6 + 10 = 16, and 8(2) = 16.
- Use algebra tiles or area models to make the distributive property visual.
- Encourage students to "check" every equation solution by substituting back into the original equation.

### Equation-Solving as "Undoing"

The central metaphor for solving equations is "undoing" operations in reverse order. For ax + b = c, the operations applied to x are: multiply by a, then add b. To solve, undo in reverse: subtract b, then divide by a. This metaphor extends naturally to inequalities.

## Differentiation Strategies

**For the student who needs more support:**
- Use algebra tiles throughout Lessons 1-5 to provide a concrete model for combining like terms and the distributive property.
- Provide a reference sheet listing the steps for solving two-step equations: (1) Undo addition/subtraction, (2) Undo multiplication/division, (3) Check by substitution.
- Break multi-step simplifications into single steps. Have the student write the result after each operation before proceeding.
- For inequalities, always have the student solve the corresponding equation first, then determine the direction of the inequality.
- Use color-coding: highlight like terms in the same color to make combining visual.

**For the student who needs more challenge:**
- Introduce equations requiring three or more steps, such as 2(3x + 1) - 4 = 10.
- Challenge the student to solve equations with rational number coefficients (e.g., (2/3)x + 5 = 11).
- Ask the student to create their own word problems for a given equation.
- Explore compound inequalities informally: "What values of x make both x > 2 AND x < 7 true?"
- Introduce absolute value equations informally: "If |x| = 5, what are the possible values of x?"

## Common Errors

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Student combines 3x + 5y as 8xy | Does not understand "like terms" -- combines unlike terms | Return to the definition: like terms must have the same variable(s) raised to the same power. Use substitution to disprove: let x = 1, y = 2. Then 3(1) + 5(2) = 13, but 8(1)(2) = 16. |
| Student writes 4(x + 3) = 4x + 3 | Distributes to the first term only | Emphasize: "The 4 multiplies EVERY term inside the parentheses." Draw arrows from the 4 to each term. Verify: let x = 2. 4(2 + 3) = 20, but 4(2) + 3 = 11. |
| Student solves 2x + 5 = 13 by dividing first: x + 5 = 6.5 | Applies operations in the wrong order | Reinforce the "undo" order: addition was the last operation applied, so it must be undone first. Show the error: if x + 5 = 6.5, then x = 1.5, but 2(1.5) + 5 = 8, not 13. |
| Student writes 2x + 3 = 2x + 3 as solution | Does not know what to do when variables cancel and a true statement remains | Explain: "This means every number is a solution -- the equation is an identity." This is a Lesson 6 extension topic. |
| Student forgets to flip the inequality sign when dividing by a negative | Does not understand or remember the sign-flip rule | Use a numeric example: 2 < 6, but divide both sides by -2: -1 > -3 (the order flips). Repeat with several examples until the pattern is clear. |
| Student writes the inequality solution backwards (e.g., 5 > x instead of x < 5) | Reads the inequality from the number side rather than the variable side | Encourage always writing the variable on the left. Rewrite 5 > x as x < 5. Practice reading inequalities both ways. |

## Assessment Guidance

The unit assessment is a 50-point summative assessment with five sections:

| Section | Skill | Points |
|---------|-------|--------|
| 1 | Simplifying Expressions | 12 |
| 2 | Solving Two-Step Equations | 12 |
| 3 | Equations from Word Problems | 10 |
| 4 | Inequalities | 10 |
| 5 | Mixed Reasoning | 6 |
| -- | **Total** | **50** |

**Mastery threshold:** 80% (40 of 50 points) AND at least 50% in each section.

If the student does not meet mastery:
- Identify the weakest section(s) and reteach the corresponding lessons.
- For expression simplification (Section 1): Return to Lessons 2-4 with algebra tiles.
- For equation-solving (Section 2): Return to Lesson 5 and rebuild the "undo" process step by step.
- For word problems (Section 3): Return to Lesson 7 and practice the translation process with simpler contexts.
- For inequalities (Section 4): Return to Lesson 8 with a focus on the sign-flip rule.
- Reassess the weak sections after targeted reteaching.

## Conversation Starters

- "If I have 3 bags of marbles and 5 extra marbles, and my friend has 2 bags of marbles and 11 extra marbles, and we have the same total -- how many marbles are in each bag? How would you write that as an equation?"
- "You earn $8 per hour at your part-time job. After paying $15 for lunch, you have $49 left. How many hours did you work? What equation describes this?"
- "A phone plan costs $25 per month plus $0.10 per text message. Your budget is $40 per month. How many text messages can you send? Is this an equation problem or an inequality problem?"
- "What is the difference between 3x + 2x and 3x + 2y? Why can you simplify one but not the other?"
- "When you solve 2x + 5 = 13, why do you subtract 5 before dividing by 2? What happens if you try dividing first?"

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if:

- The student cannot identify terms, coefficients, and variables in an expression (prerequisite gap from Grade 6)
- The student cannot perform operations with negative numbers reliably (prerequisite gap from Unit 2; solving equations with negative numbers will be extremely frustrating without this fluency)
- The student cannot solve one-step equations after reteaching (Grade 6 prerequisite; two-step equations build directly on this skill)
- Combining like terms remains inconsistent after explicit instruction with algebra tiles -- all subsequent lessons depend on this skill
- The student cannot translate a verbal phrase into an algebraic expression (critical for Lessons 7 and 9)
