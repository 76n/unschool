# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 3, Lesson 8

## Topic
Solving Inequalities

## Learning Objectives
- Solve one-step and two-step inequalities using inverse operations.
- Apply the sign-flip rule: when multiplying or dividing both sides of an inequality by a negative number, reverse the inequality symbol.
- Graph the solution set of an inequality on a number line using open and closed circles.
- Distinguish between strict inequalities (< , >) and non-strict inequalities (≤, ≥).

## Prerequisites
- Ability to solve two-step equations (Lesson 5).
- Fluency with operations on integers, especially multiplication and division with negative numbers (Unit 2).
- Familiarity with inequality symbols <, >, ≤, ≥ and their meanings.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Number lines (printed or drawn; range -10 to 10 is sufficient for most problems).
- Colored pencils (for graphing solutions).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.EE.4b -- Solve word problems leading to inequalities of the form px + q > r or px + q < r, where p, q, and r are specific rational numbers. Graph the solution set of the inequality and interpret it in the context of the problem.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review inequality symbols and set up the contrast between equations and inequalities.

1. True or false: 3 < 7. (True)
2. True or false: -5 > -2. (False; -5 is to the left of -2 on the number line.)
3. Solve the equation: 2x + 3 = 11. (x = 4)
4. Ask: "What if I change the equals sign to a less-than sign: 2x + 3 < 11? Is x = 4 still a solution? What about x = 3? What about x = 0? Today we learn how to solve inequalities and describe ALL the solutions."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Inequality** -- a mathematical statement that compares two expressions using <, >, ≤, or ≥. Example: 2x + 3 < 11.
- **Solution set** -- the set of all values that make the inequality true. Example: for x < 4, the solution set is all numbers less than 4.
- **Open circle** -- used on a number line graph to show that the endpoint is NOT included (for < or >).
- **Closed circle** -- used on a number line graph to show that the endpoint IS included (for ≤ or ≥).
- **Sign-flip rule** -- when you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, you must reverse (flip) the direction of the inequality symbol.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Solving a one-step inequality.**

Say: "Solving an inequality is just like solving an equation, with one important extra rule. Let me start with a simple example."

Write: x + 5 > 12

Say: "Subtract 5 from both sides, just like an equation."
- x + 5 - 5 > 12 - 5
- x > 7

Say: "The solution is all numbers greater than 7. Let me graph this on a number line."

Draw a number line. Place an open circle at 7, and shade to the right.

Say: "The open circle at 7 means 7 is NOT included. If the symbol were ≥, I would use a closed (filled-in) circle."

**Step 2 -- Solving a two-step inequality.**

Write: 3x - 4 ≤ 11

Say: "Step 1 -- Add 4 to both sides."
- 3x ≤ 15

Say: "Step 2 -- Divide both sides by 3."
- x ≤ 5

Say: "The solution is all numbers less than or equal to 5. On the number line, I place a closed circle at 5 and shade to the left."

Say: "Let me check a value in the solution set. Try x = 3: 3(3) - 4 = 9 - 4 = 5. Is 5 ≤ 11? Yes."

Say: "And check a value NOT in the solution set. Try x = 6: 3(6) - 4 = 18 - 4 = 14. Is 14 ≤ 11? No. Good -- x = 6 should not be a solution."

**Step 3 -- The sign-flip rule (CRITICAL).**

Say: "Here is the most important new rule in this lesson. Watch what happens when I multiply both sides of an inequality by a negative number."

Write on the board: 2 < 6. This is true.

Say: "Now multiply both sides by -1."
- -2 and -6. Is -2 < -6? NO. -2 is to the RIGHT of -6, so -2 > -6.

Say: "When we multiplied by a negative number, the inequality FLIPPED. This always happens. Let me show another example: 10 > 4. Divide both sides by -2: -5 and -2. Is -5 > -2? NO. -5 < -2. The inequality flipped again."

Write the rule:

> **When you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a NEGATIVE number, you must FLIP the inequality symbol.**

**Step 4 -- Apply the sign-flip rule.**

Write: -2x + 3 > 9

Say: "Step 1 -- Subtract 3 from both sides."
- -2x > 6

Say: "Step 2 -- Divide both sides by -2. Since -2 is negative, FLIP the inequality sign."
- x < -3

Say: "The solution is all numbers less than -3. On the number line, open circle at -3, shade to the left."

Say: "Check with x = -5 (in the solution set): -2(-5) + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13. Is 13 > 9? Yes."
Say: "Check with x = 0 (not in the solution set): -2(0) + 3 = 0 + 3 = 3. Is 3 > 9? No. Correct."

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Two-step inequality, no sign flip**
Solve and graph: 4x + 7 < 23

Step 1: 4x < 16
Step 2: x < 4
Graph: Open circle at 4, shade left.
Check: x = 2: 4(2) + 7 = 15 < 23. True.

**Example B: Two-step inequality with sign flip**
Solve and graph: -5x - 3 ≥ 12

Step 1: -5x ≥ 15
Step 2: Divide by -5, FLIP the sign: x ≤ -3
Graph: Closed circle at -3, shade left.
Check: x = -4: -5(-4) - 3 = 20 - 3 = 17 ≥ 12. True.
Check: x = 0: -5(0) - 3 = -3. Is -3 ≥ 12? No. Correct.

**Example C: Inequality with subtraction of the variable term**
Solve and graph: 8 - x > 5

Step 1: Subtract 8: -x > -3
Step 2: Divide by -1, FLIP: x < 3
Graph: Open circle at 3, shade left.
Check: x = 1: 8 - 1 = 7 > 5. True.
Check: x = 4: 8 - 4 = 4. Is 4 > 5? No. Correct.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Solve and graph: x + 6 > 10
   - x > 4. Open circle at 4, shade right.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Just like an equation -- subtract 6 from both sides."

2. Solve and graph: 2x - 1 ≤ 7
   - 2x ≤ 8, x ≤ 4. Closed circle at 4, shade left.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Add 1, then divide by 2. No sign flip because 2 is positive."

3. Solve and graph: -3x > 12
   - x < -4. Open circle at -4, shade left.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Divide by -3. What happens to the inequality sign?"

4. Solve and graph: -4x + 5 ≤ -7
   - -4x ≤ -12, x ≥ 3. Closed circle at 3, shade right.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "After subtracting 5, you divide by -4. Flip the sign."

5. Solve and graph: 6 - 2x < 0
   - -2x < -6, x > 3. Open circle at 3, shade right.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Subtract 6 first, then divide by -2 and flip."

6. Solve and graph: 5x + 8 ≥ 8
   - 5x ≥ 0, x ≥ 0. Closed circle at 0, shade right.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "The boundary value is 0. Check: 5(0) + 8 = 8 ≥ 8. True."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Solve and Graph

1. x - 4 > 3
2. 3x + 2 < 17
3. 5x - 10 ≥ 15
4. -2x + 6 > 10
5. -x - 3 ≤ 4
6. 7 - 3x ≥ 1

### Part B: Multiple Choice (circle the correct graph)

7. Which inequality has the solution x > -2?
   (a) x + 3 > 1    (b) x + 3 < 1    (c) x - 3 > 1

8. Which value is NOT a solution to 2x + 1 < 9?
   (a) x = 0    (b) x = 3    (c) x = 4    (d) x = -5

### Part C: Word Problems

9. A movie theater charges $8 per ticket. You have $50. What is the maximum number of whole tickets you can buy? Write an inequality and solve.
10. The temperature must stay below 40 degrees Fahrenheit for a science experiment. The current temperature is 15 degrees and is rising at 5 degrees per hour. For how many hours will the experiment still be valid? Write an inequality and solve.

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. x - 4 > 3 → x > **7**. Open circle at 7, shade right.
2. 3x + 2 < 17 → 3x < 15 → x < **5**. Open circle at 5, shade left.
3. 5x - 10 ≥ 15 → 5x ≥ 25 → x ≥ **5**. Closed circle at 5, shade right.
4. -2x + 6 > 10 → -2x > 4 → x < **-2** (sign flip). Open circle at -2, shade left.
5. -x - 3 ≤ 4 → -x ≤ 7 → x ≥ **-7** (sign flip). Closed circle at -7, shade right.
6. 7 - 3x ≥ 1 → -3x ≥ -6 → x ≤ **2** (sign flip). Closed circle at 2, shade left.

**Part B**
7. (a) x + 3 > 1 → x > -2. Correct answer: **(a)**.
8. 2x + 1 < 9 → 2x < 8 → x < 4. Check: x = 4 gives 2(4) + 1 = 9, and 9 is not less than 9. Correct answer: **(c) x = 4**.

**Part C**
9. Let t = number of tickets. 8t ≤ 50. t ≤ 6.25. Since you can only buy whole tickets, t ≤ **6 tickets**. Check: 8(6) = 48 ≤ 50. And 8(7) = 56 > 50.
10. Let h = number of hours. 15 + 5h < 40. 5h < 25. h < **5 hours**. The experiment is valid for fewer than 5 hours (so for 0 to just under 5 hours; in whole hours, 4 hours). Check: After 4 hours: 15 + 5(4) = 35 < 40. After 5 hours: 15 + 5(5) = 40, which is NOT below 40.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** Solve and graph: 3x - 5 > 7

**Problem 2:** Solve and graph: -2x + 1 ≤ 9

**Target response:**
1. 3x > 12, x > 4. Open circle at 4, shade right.
2. -2x ≤ 8, x ≥ -4 (sign flip). Closed circle at -4, shade right.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct (solution and graph) = Mastery demonstrated.
- Correct solutions but graphing errors (wrong circle type or direction) = Partial; review graphing conventions.
- Forgot to flip the sign in Problem 2 = Reteach the sign-flip rule with numeric examples.

## Extension
- **Compound Inequality:** Solve -3 < 2x + 1 < 9. (Subtract 1: -4 < 2x < 8. Divide by 2: -2 < x < 4. Graph: open circles at -2 and 4, shade between.)
- **Exploration:** "Is there a value of x that makes 3x + 2 > 3x + 5 true?" (No -- subtract 3x and you get 2 > 5, which is false. No solution.)
