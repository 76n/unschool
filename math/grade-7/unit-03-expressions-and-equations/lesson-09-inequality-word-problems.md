# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 3, Lesson 9

## Topic
Inequality Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
- Identify when a real-world situation requires an inequality rather than an equation.
- Translate phrases like "at most," "at least," "no more than," and "fewer than" into inequality symbols.
- Write, solve, and graph inequalities from real-world contexts.
- Interpret the solution set in context, including determining whole-number answers when appropriate.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to solve one-step and two-step inequalities (Lesson 8).
- Ability to graph inequality solutions on a number line (Lesson 8).
- Familiarity with translating verbal descriptions into algebraic expressions (Lesson 7).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Number lines (printed or drawn).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 7.EE.4b -- Solve word problems leading to inequalities of the form px + q > r or px + q < r, where p, q, and r are specific rational numbers. Graph the solution set of the inequality and interpret it in the context of the problem.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Practice translating key inequality phrases into symbols.

Match each phrase with the correct symbol:

1. "at most 50" → ≤ 50
2. "at least 20" → ≥ 20
3. "fewer than 10" → < 10
4. "more than 100" → > 100
5. "no more than 75" → ≤ 75

Say: "These phrases are your keys to writing inequalities from word problems. Let me say something important: 'at most' means less than or equal to, and 'at least' means greater than or equal to."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **At most** -- less than or equal to (≤). "You can spend at most $50" means you can spend $50 or less.
- **At least** -- greater than or equal to (≥). "You need at least 70 points to pass" means 70 or more.
- **No more than** -- less than or equal to (≤). Same as "at most."
- **Fewer than / less than** -- strictly less than (<). "Fewer than 10 people" means 9 or fewer.
- **More than / greater than** -- strictly greater than (>).
- **Constraint** -- a limitation or restriction on a quantity.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Equation vs. inequality: when to use which.**

Say: "In Lesson 7, we translated word problems into equations. We used an equation when the problem asked for an exact value -- 'How many hours did she work?' or 'What is the cost?' Today, we use inequalities when the problem involves a constraint or limit."

Write two problems side by side:

| Equation Problem | Inequality Problem |
|---|---|
| "You earned $8/hour and made exactly $64. How many hours did you work?" | "You earn $8/hour and need at least $64. How many hours must you work?" |
| 8h = 64, h = 8 | 8h ≥ 64, h ≥ 8 |

Say: "The equation has one answer: exactly 8 hours. The inequality has many answers: 8 hours, 9 hours, 10 hours, and so on."

**Step 2 -- Full example with the four-step process.**

Say: "You have $120 to spend at a store. Each shirt costs $15, and you also need to buy a $30 pair of shoes. How many shirts can you buy?"

**Define:** Let s = number of shirts.

**Translate:** The total cost must be at most $120.
- 15s + 30 ≤ 120

**Solve:**
- 15s ≤ 90
- s ≤ 6

**Interpret:** "You can buy at most 6 shirts. Since you cannot buy a fraction of a shirt, the possible numbers of shirts are 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6."

**Graph:** Closed circle at 6, shade left to 0 (or shade left from 6 on a number line, noting that only whole numbers 0-6 apply in context).

**Check:** 6 shirts: 15(6) + 30 = 90 + 30 = 120 ≤ 120. True. 7 shirts: 15(7) + 30 = 105 + 30 = 135 > 120. False. Correct.

**Step 3 -- Example with "at least."**

Say: "A student needs at least 80 points on the final test to pass the course. The test has two parts: Part A is worth 35 points (already scored), and Part B is worth up to 65 points. The student scored 28 on Part A. How many points does the student need on Part B?"

**Define:** Let p = points needed on Part B.

**Translate:** 28 + p ≥ 80

**Solve:** p ≥ 52

**Interpret:** "The student needs at least 52 points on Part B to pass."

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Budget constraint**
"You have $40 to spend on lunch for the week (5 days). Each day, lunch costs the same amount. What is the maximum you can spend per day?"

Define: Let d = cost per day.
Inequality: 5d ≤ 40
Solve: d ≤ 8
Interpret: You can spend **at most $8 per day** on lunch.

**Example B: Minimum requirement**
"A delivery service charges $4 per package. The driver needs to earn at least $100 per day. How many packages must the driver deliver?"

Define: Let p = number of packages.
Inequality: 4p ≥ 100
Solve: p ≥ 25
Interpret: The driver must deliver **at least 25 packages**.

**Example C: Combined constraint with a fixed cost**
"A school dance has a DJ fee of $200 and charges $3 per student for admission. The school wants to make more than $500 in total revenue. How many students must attend?"

Define: Let n = number of students.
Inequality: 3n + 200 > 500 (Wait -- the $200 DJ fee is a cost, and the revenue is $3 per student. Let me re-read.)

Actually, the revenue is 3n (from admission). The cost is $200. To "make more than $500 in total revenue" means: 3n > 500. But if "make more than $500" means profit (revenue - cost > 500), then: 3n - 200 > 500.

Say: "Let me clarify: the school wants total revenue (not profit) to exceed $500. Revenue = $3 per student."

Inequality: 3n > 500
Solve: n > 166.67
Interpret: **At least 167 students** must attend (round up since you cannot have a fraction of a student).

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. "A roller coaster requires you to be at least 48 inches tall. Write an inequality for the height h."
   - h ≥ 48
   - **Facilitator tip:** "'At least' means greater than or equal to."

2. "You have $25 and want to buy notebooks that cost $3 each. How many can you buy?"
   - 3n ≤ 25, n ≤ 8.33, so n ≤ **8 notebooks** (whole number).
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Can you buy 8.33 notebooks? No -- round down."

3. "A parking meter allows a maximum of 3 hours. You have already parked for 45 minutes (0.75 hours). How much more time can you stay?"
   - 0.75 + t ≤ 3. t ≤ **2.25 hours** (2 hours 15 minutes).
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Subtract 0.75 from both sides."

4. "A fundraiser needs to raise at least $600. They have already raised $175. Each car wash earns $10. How many more car washes do they need?"
   - 10c + 175 ≥ 600. 10c ≥ 425. c ≥ 42.5, so c ≥ **43 car washes**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "Round up here -- 42 car washes is not enough."

5. "The temperature must remain below -10 degrees Celsius. The current temperature is 5 degrees and is dropping 3 degrees per hour. After how many hours will the temperature be below -10?"
   - 5 - 3h < -10. -3h < -15. h > 5 (flip the sign). After **more than 5 hours**.
   - **Facilitator tip:** "You divide by -3, so flip the inequality."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Translate and Solve

1. A gym requires members to be at least 13 years old. Write and graph an inequality for the age a of eligible members.
2. A bag can hold at most 25 pounds. You already have 8 pounds in the bag. How many more pounds can you add? Write and solve an inequality.
3. A prepaid phone card has $30 on it. Each call costs $0.50 per minute. How many minutes can you talk?

### Part B: Two-Step Inequality Word Problems

4. A camp charges $50 for registration plus $12 per day. A family's budget for camp is at most $170. How many days can the child attend?
5. A salesperson earns $200 per week plus $15 per item sold. The salesperson wants to earn more than $350 per week. How many items must be sold?
6. The school band needs to raise at least $1,000 for new instruments. They have $280 saved and plan to hold bake sales that earn $60 each. How many bake sales do they need?

### Part C: Reasoning

7. Write a real-world situation that could be modeled by the inequality 5x + 20 ≤ 100.
8. Tyler says the solution to -4x > 20 is x > -5. Is Tyler correct? Explain.

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. a ≥ 13. Graph: Closed circle at 13, shade right.
2. Let w = additional weight. 8 + w ≤ 25. w ≤ **17 pounds**. Check: 8 + 17 = 25 ≤ 25.
3. Let m = minutes. 0.50m ≤ 30. m ≤ **60 minutes**. Check: 0.50(60) = 30 ≤ 30.

**Part B**
4. Let d = days. 12d + 50 ≤ 170. 12d ≤ 120. d ≤ **10 days**. Check: 12(10) + 50 = 170 ≤ 170.
5. Let n = items sold. 15n + 200 > 350. 15n > 150. n > **10 items**. The salesperson must sell at least 11 items. Check: 15(11) + 200 = 165 + 200 = 365 > 350. And 15(10) + 200 = 350, which is NOT more than 350.
6. Let b = number of bake sales. 60b + 280 ≥ 1000. 60b ≥ 720. b ≥ **12 bake sales**. Check: 60(12) + 280 = 720 + 280 = 1000 ≥ 1000.

**Part C**
7. Sample answer: "You have $100. You want to buy some books at $5 each and also pay a $20 membership fee. How many books can you buy?" (Many correct answers are possible.)
8. Tyler is **incorrect**. When dividing both sides of -4x > 20 by -4, you must flip the inequality sign. The correct solution is x < -5. Check: x = -6: -4(-6) = 24 > 20. True. x = -4: -4(-4) = 16 > 20. False. So values greater than -5 do NOT work.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
**Problem:** A school trip costs $8 per student plus a $45 bus fee. The school has a budget of no more than $125. How many students can go on the trip? Define a variable, write an inequality, solve, and interpret.

**Target response:**
Let s = number of students. 8s + 45 ≤ 125. 8s ≤ 80. s ≤ 10.
**At most 10 students** can go on the trip. Check: 8(10) + 45 = 125 ≤ 125.

**Scoring:**
- Correct inequality, solution, and interpretation = Mastery demonstrated.
- Correct work but uses = instead of ≤ = Partial; review when to use an inequality vs. equation.
- Cannot set up the inequality = Reteach; practice identifying the constraint phrase ("no more than").

## Extension
- **Multi-Constraint Problem:** You are packing a suitcase that can hold at most 50 pounds. You have packed 18 pounds of clothes. Each book weighs 2 pounds. How many books can you add, and what is the maximum total weight? (2b + 18 ≤ 50, b ≤ 16 books, max weight = 2(16) + 18 = 50 pounds.)
- **Comparison:** "Plan A charges $20 plus $0.10 per text. Plan B charges $0.15 per text. For how many texts is Plan A cheaper?" (20 + 0.10t < 0.15t, 20 < 0.05t, t > 400 texts. Plan A is cheaper when you send MORE than 400 texts.)
