# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 2, Lesson 3

## Topic
Subtracting Integers

## Learning Objectives
- Rewrite any subtraction expression as an equivalent addition expression using the rule a - b = a + (-b).
- Apply the addition rules from Lessons 1-2 to evaluate subtraction problems.
- Model integer subtraction on a number line.
- Solve subtraction problems involving negative integers, including subtracting a negative number.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to add integers with the same sign (Lesson 1).
- Ability to add integers with different signs (Lesson 2).
- Understanding of the additive inverse: a + (-a) = 0.
- Fluency with subtraction of whole numbers.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Number line (printed or drawn, from -20 to 20).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.
- Two-color counters (optional).

**Standards:** 7.NS.1 -- Apply and extend previous understandings of addition and subtraction to add and subtract rational numbers; represent addition and subtraction on a horizontal or vertical number line diagram.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review addition of integers and set up the subtraction-as-addition connection.

1. Compute: 8 + (-3). (Answer: 5)
2. Compute: 8 - 3. (Answer: 5)
3. Ask: "What do you notice about the two results?" (They are the same.)
4. Compute: -2 + (-6). (Answer: -8)
5. Ask: "Can you predict what -2 - 6 equals? Why?" (Also -8. Subtracting 6 is the same as adding -6.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Subtraction** -- finding the difference between two numbers. In the integer context, subtraction can be rewritten as addition of the opposite.
- **Additive inverse** -- the opposite of a number. The additive inverse of b is -b, because b + (-b) = 0.
- **Add the opposite** -- the strategy of converting subtraction to addition: a - b = a + (-b).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Establish the pattern.**
Write several pairs on the board:
- 10 - 3 = 7 and 10 + (-3) = 7
- 5 - 8 = ? and 5 + (-8) = ?

"We already know 5 + (-8) from Lesson 2: different signs, |8| - |5| = 3, the -8 has the larger absolute value, so the answer is -3."

Now ask: "Does 5 - 8 also equal -3?" Use a real-world context: "If you have $5 and spend $8, you are $3 in debt. So yes, 5 - 8 = -3."

**Step 2 -- State the rule.**
Write on the board:

> **Rule: To subtract an integer, add its opposite.**
> **a - b = a + (-b)**

"This means ANY subtraction problem can be turned into an addition problem, and then we can use the rules we already know from Lessons 1 and 2."

**Step 3 -- Apply the rule to subtracting a positive number.**
Example: -4 - 7
Rewrite: -4 + (-7)
Now it is same-sign addition: |-4| + |-7| = 4 + 7 = 11. Both negative, so: -11.
Result: -4 - 7 = -11.

Number line: Start at -4. Subtracting 7 means moving 7 units left. Land on -11.

**Step 4 -- Apply the rule to subtracting a negative number.**
"This is the case that surprises most students."
Example: 3 - (-5)
Rewrite: 3 + (-(-5)) = 3 + 5 = 8.

"Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive."

Real-world reasoning: "If you remove a $5 debt, your balance goes UP by $5."

Number line: Start at 3. Subtracting -5 means moving 5 units to the right. Land on 8.

More examples:
- -2 - (-9): Rewrite as -2 + 9 = 7.
- -6 - (-4): Rewrite as -6 + 4 = -2.

**Step 5 -- Summarize all cases.**

| Expression Type | Rewrite | Example | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positive - Positive | a + (-b) | 8 - 3 = 8 + (-3) | 5 |
| Negative - Positive | a + (-b) | -4 - 7 = -4 + (-7) | -11 |
| Positive - Negative | a + (+b) | 3 - (-5) = 3 + 5 | 8 |
| Negative - Negative | a + (+b) | -6 - (-4) = -6 + 4 | -2 |

### Worked Examples

**Example A -- Subtracting a positive from a negative**
Compute: -8 - 5

Rewrite: -8 + (-5).
Same-sign addition: |-8| + |-5| = 8 + 5 = 13. Both negative.
Answer: -8 - 5 = **-13**.

**Example B -- Subtracting a negative from a positive**
Compute: 7 - (-4)

Rewrite: 7 + 4 = 11.
Answer: 7 - (-4) = **11**.

**Example C -- Subtracting a negative from a negative**
Compute: -3 - (-10)

Rewrite: -3 + 10.
Different signs: |10| - |-3| = 10 - 3 = 7. Positive has greater absolute value.
Answer: -3 - (-10) = **7**.

**Example D -- Subtracting a larger positive from a smaller positive**
Compute: 4 - 15

Rewrite: 4 + (-15).
Different signs: |-15| - |4| = 15 - 4 = 11. Negative has greater absolute value.
Answer: 4 - 15 = **-11**.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Compute: 6 - 10. First rewrite as addition.
   - *Facilitator tip:* "What is the opposite of 10? Rewrite as 6 + (-10)."

2. Compute: -3 - 8. First rewrite as addition.
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Rewrite: -3 + (-8). Now use the same-sign rule."

3. Compute: 5 - (-7). First rewrite as addition.
   - *Facilitator tip:* "The opposite of -7 is +7. What is 5 + 7?"

4. Compute: -9 - (-4). First rewrite as addition.
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Rewrite: -9 + 4. Now use the different-sign rule."

5. Show 2 - 8 on a number line. Write the result.
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Start at 2 and move 8 units left."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A -- Rewrite and Compute
1. 9 - 14
2. -7 - 5
3. 4 - (-6)
4. -10 - (-3)

### Part B -- Mixed Operations (Addition and Subtraction)
5. -8 + 3 - 5
6. 12 - 15 + 4
7. -6 - (-2) + (-9)

### Part C -- Applied and Extended
8. The temperature was 3 degrees Celsius in the afternoon. By midnight, it had fallen to -8 degrees Celsius. What was the temperature change? Write a subtraction expression.
9. Mount Everest has a peak at 29,032 feet above sea level. The Dead Sea is at -1,412 feet (below sea level). What is the difference in elevation? Write a subtraction expression.
10. Is it always true that a - b is different from b - a? Give an example where they are different and an example where they are the same (if one exists). Explain.

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. 9 - 14 = 9 + (-14) = -5
2. -7 - 5 = -7 + (-5) = -12
3. 4 - (-6) = 4 + 6 = 10
4. -10 - (-3) = -10 + 3 = -7

**Part B**
5. -8 + 3 - 5 = -8 + 3 + (-5) = -5 + (-5) = -10
6. 12 - 15 + 4 = 12 + (-15) + 4 = -3 + 4 = 1
7. -6 - (-2) + (-9) = -6 + 2 + (-9) = -4 + (-9) = -13

**Part C**
8. Temperature change: -8 - 3 = -8 + (-3) = -11. The temperature dropped 11 degrees.
9. 29,032 - (-1,412) = 29,032 + 1,412 = 30,444. The difference in elevation is 30,444 feet.
10. a - b and b - a are different unless a = b. Example where they differ: 5 - 3 = 2 but 3 - 5 = -2. They are opposites of each other: a - b = -(b - a). The only case where they are the same is when a = b (e.g., 4 - 4 = 0 and 4 - 4 = 0).

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** Compute -6 - (-11). Show your rewrite-as-addition step.

**Problem 2:** The high temperature on Monday was 5 degrees Fahrenheit. The low temperature was -12 degrees Fahrenheit. What is the difference between the high and low temperatures?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Focus on the single most important idea: "Subtracting is the same as adding the opposite." Have the student practice JUST the rewriting step on 10 expressions without computing the answer. Once the rewriting is automatic, add the computation.
- Use physical counters to model "removing negatives." Place 6 red counters (representing -6). To subtract -11, you need to remove 11 red counters, but you only have 6. Add 5 zero pairs (5 red + 5 yellow). Now remove 11 red counters. You are left with 5 yellow counters = +5.
- Provide 5 additional problems focused on subtracting negatives, since this is the most counterintuitive case.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Assign Part B and Part C problems if not yet completed.
- Preview: "Next, we move to multiplication. Does multiplying two negatives give a positive or a negative? We will find out."

## Extension
- **Difference Chains:** Start with the number 20. Subtract -5, then subtract 8, then subtract -3, then subtract 12. What is the final result? (20 - (-5) = 25, 25 - 8 = 17, 17 - (-3) = 20, 20 - 12 = 8.)
- **Explore Absolute Value Differences:** "Is |a - b| always equal to |b - a|? Test with at least three pairs of integers and explain why."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Treating 8 - (-3) as 8 - 3 = 5 instead of 8 + 3 = 11.** The student ignores or misreads the double negative. Emphasize: "Circle the subtraction sign and the negative sign. Two negatives next to each other become a plus."
- **Not rewriting before computing.** The student tries to compute -4 - 7 directly and gets confused. Insist on the rewrite step for every problem until it becomes automatic.
- **Confusing the direction on the number line.** When subtracting a negative, the student moves left instead of right. Reinforce: "Subtracting a negative flips the direction. You move RIGHT."

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson is conceptually critical and may need two sessions. The idea that "subtracting a negative is adding a positive" is counterintuitive and requires repeated exposure.
- Prioritize the rewrite step (a - b = a + (-b)) above all else. If the student can reliably rewrite, they already know how to compute (using Lessons 1-2 rules).
- The real-world problems (temperature change, elevation difference) are powerful for building intuition. Use them liberally.
- Problem 10 (exploring whether a - b equals b - a) foreshadows the non-commutative nature of subtraction, which connects to Lesson 9 (properties of operations).
