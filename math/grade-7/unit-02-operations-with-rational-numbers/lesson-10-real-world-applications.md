# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 2, Lesson 10

## Topic
Real-World Applications of Rational Number Operations

## Learning Objectives
- Translate real-world situations into mathematical expressions and equations using rational numbers.
- Solve multi-step word problems involving temperature change, financial gain and loss, elevation change, and net motion.
- Combine all four operations with rational numbers to solve applied problems.
- Interpret the meaning of positive and negative results in context.

## Prerequisites
- Mastery of all four operations with integers and rational numbers (Lessons 1-7).
- Ability to apply order of operations with signed numbers (Lesson 8).
- Familiarity with properties of operations (Lesson 9).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.
- Calculator (optional, for complex computations).
- Thermometer diagram (optional, for temperature problems).

**Standards:** 7.NS.3 -- Solve real-world and mathematical problems involving the four operations with rational numbers.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Connect rational number operations to familiar real-world contexts.

1. "The temperature is -8 degrees Fahrenheit. It rises 12 degrees. What is the new temperature?" (-8 + 12 = 4 degrees F.)
2. "You have $25 in your bank account. You write a check for $40. What is your balance?" (25 - 40 = -15, or $15 overdrawn.)
3. "A diver is 60 feet below sea level. She descends another 25 feet. Write an expression and find her new depth." (-60 + (-25) = -85 feet.)
4. Ask: "In each of these problems, what does a negative answer mean in the real world?"

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Net change** -- the overall result after multiple increases and decreases. Found by adding all changes together.
- **Elevation** -- height above or below sea level. Above sea level is positive; below sea level is negative.
- **Profit/Loss** -- financial gain (positive) or financial loss (negative).
- **Displacement** -- the net distance and direction from a starting point.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Temperature change problems.**
Context: A weather station records temperatures at different times.

Example: "At 6:00 AM, the temperature was -5 degrees Celsius. It rose 8 degrees by noon, then dropped 12 degrees by midnight. What was the midnight temperature?"

Expression: -5 + 8 + (-12)
Step by step: -5 + 8 = 3. Then 3 + (-12) = -9.
Answer: -9 degrees Celsius.

"Interpret: The midnight temperature was 9 degrees below zero."

**Step 2 -- Financial gain/loss problems.**
Context: A small business tracks weekly profits and losses.

Example: "A business had the following results over four weeks: Week 1: profit of $1,200. Week 2: loss of $850. Week 3: loss of $475. Week 4: profit of $1,600. What was the net result?"

Expression: 1200 + (-850) + (-475) + 1600
Group positives: 1200 + 1600 = 2800.
Group negatives: (-850) + (-475) = -1325.
Net: 2800 + (-1325) = 1475.
Answer: Net profit of $1,475.

**Step 3 -- Elevation change problems.**
Context: Comparing elevations of geographic features.

Example: "A hiker starts at an elevation of 350 feet above sea level. She descends into a canyon that is 120 feet below sea level. How many feet did she descend?"

Expression: 350 - (-120) = 350 + 120 = 470.
Answer: She descended 470 feet.

Interpretation: "Even though the canyon is at -120 feet, the total distance she traveled down is 470 feet because she started 350 feet above zero."

**Step 4 -- Net motion problems.**
Context: Movement in opposite directions.

Example: "A robot moves forward 3.5 meters, backward 5.2 meters, and then forward 2.8 meters. What is the robot's displacement from its starting point?"

Expression: 3.5 + (-5.2) + 2.8
Step by step: 3.5 + (-5.2) = -1.7. Then -1.7 + 2.8 = 1.1.
Answer: 1.1 meters forward from the starting point.

**Step 5 -- Multi-operation problems.**
"Some problems require multiplication and division in addition to addition and subtraction."

Example: "A parking garage charges $2.75 per hour. Marcus parks for 3 hours and pays with a $20 bill. How much change does he receive?"

Cost: 3 x $2.75 = $8.25.
Change: $20 - $8.25 = $11.75.
Answer: Marcus receives $11.75 in change.

### Worked Examples

**Example A -- Temperature with fractions**
The temperature at dawn was -2 1/2 degrees Celsius. By noon, it had risen 7 3/4 degrees. By evening, it had dropped 4 1/4 degrees from the noon temperature. What was the evening temperature?

Dawn: -2 1/2 = -5/2.
Noon: -5/2 + 7 3/4 = -5/2 + 31/4 = -10/4 + 31/4 = 21/4 = 5 1/4 degrees C.
Evening: 21/4 - 4 1/4 = 21/4 - 17/4 = 4/4 = 1 degree C.
Answer: The evening temperature was **1 degree Celsius**.

**Example B -- Financial multi-step**
Anya earned $145.50 babysitting and spent $67.25 on supplies. She then earned $32.00 tutoring and spent $89.75 on a textbook. What is her net financial change?

Expression: 145.50 + (-67.25) + 32.00 + (-89.75)
Positives: 145.50 + 32.00 = 177.50.
Negatives: (-67.25) + (-89.75) = -157.00.
Net: 177.50 + (-157.00) = 20.50.
Answer: Anya gained a net of **$20.50**.

**Example C -- Elevation with multiplication**
A submersible descends at a constant rate of 12.5 feet per minute. It starts at sea level (0 feet). After 8 minutes, it stops descending and then ascends at 5 feet per minute for 6 minutes. What is its final depth?

Descent: 0 + 8 x (-12.5) = -100 feet.
Ascent: -100 + 6 x 5 = -100 + 30 = -70 feet.
Answer: The submersible is at **-70 feet** (70 feet below sea level).

**Example D -- Average of signed numbers**
A city recorded the following low temperatures over 5 days: -8, -3, 2, -1, 5 (degrees Fahrenheit). What was the average low temperature?

Sum: -8 + (-3) + 2 + (-1) + 5 = -5.
Average: -5 / 5 = -1.
Answer: The average low temperature was **-1 degree Fahrenheit**.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. The temperature was 4 degrees Celsius at noon. It dropped 3 degrees per hour for 5 hours. What was the temperature at 5:00 PM?
   - *Facilitator tip:* "What operation represents a repeated drop? Multiply first, then add to the starting temperature."

2. A company's stock started at $34.50 per share. On Monday it dropped $2.75, on Tuesday it gained $1.25, and on Wednesday it dropped $4.50. What was the share price at the end of Wednesday?
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Represent drops as negative and gains as positive. Add all changes to the starting price."

3. A football team is on their own 25-yard line. They gain 8 yards, lose 3 yards, gain 15 yards, and lose 6 yards. Where are they now?
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Start at 25 and add each change (positive for gains, negative for losses)."

4. A scuba diver is at -45 feet. She ascends 1/3 of the way to the surface. What is her new depth?
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Ascending 1/3 of the way means she covers 1/3 of 45 feet upward."

5. The average of four numbers is -6. Three of the numbers are -10, 2, and -5. What is the fourth number?
   - *Facilitator tip:* "If the average of four numbers is -6, the sum must be -6 x 4 = -24. Find the missing addend."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A -- Temperature Problems
1. At midnight, the temperature was -14 degrees Fahrenheit. By 8:00 AM, it had risen 22 degrees. By 4:00 PM, it had dropped 9 degrees from the 8:00 AM temperature. What was the 4:00 PM temperature?
2. The high temperature on five consecutive days was: -2, 3, -5, 1, -7 (degrees Celsius). What was the average high temperature?

### Part B -- Financial Problems
3. Marco starts with a bank balance of $230.00. He makes the following transactions: deposit of $75.50, withdrawal of $120.00, withdrawal of $95.25, deposit of $40.00. What is his final balance?
4. A store buys 24 items at $3.75 each and sells them at $6.50 each. What is the profit per item? What is the total profit?

### Part C -- Elevation and Motion Problems
5. The highest point in North America (Denali) has an elevation of 20,310 feet. The lowest point (Death Valley) has an elevation of -282 feet. What is the difference in elevation?
6. A drone starts at ground level (0 feet). It rises 150 feet, descends 80 feet, rises 45 feet, and descends 30 feet. What is its final altitude? What was its total distance traveled (up and down combined)?
7. A submarine is at -250 feet. It ascends at 8.5 feet per second for 12 seconds, then descends at 3 feet per second for 20 seconds. What is its final depth?

### Part D -- Multi-Operation Challenge
8. Evaluate in context: A delivery truck starts with a full tank of 25 gallons. It uses 3.5 gallons per delivery and makes 4 deliveries. How many gallons remain? If gas costs $3.25 per gallon, how much would it cost to refill the truck?
9. The temperature at the base of a mountain is 18.5 degrees Celsius. The temperature drops 2.3 degrees for every 1,000 feet of elevation gain. What is the temperature at 5,000 feet above the base?

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. Midnight: -14. 8:00 AM: -14 + 22 = 8. 4:00 PM: 8 - 9 = -1 degree Fahrenheit.
2. Sum: -2 + 3 + (-5) + 1 + (-7) = -10. Average: -10 / 5 = -2 degrees Celsius.

**Part B**
3. 230 + 75.50 + (-120) + (-95.25) + 40 = 230 + 75.50 + 40 + (-120) + (-95.25) = 345.50 + (-215.25) = $130.25.
4. Profit per item: $6.50 - $3.75 = $2.75. Total profit: 24 x $2.75 = $66.00.

**Part C**
5. 20,310 - (-282) = 20,310 + 282 = 20,592 feet.
6. Final altitude: 0 + 150 + (-80) + 45 + (-30) = 85 feet. Total distance traveled: 150 + 80 + 45 + 30 = 305 feet.
7. Start: -250. Ascent: 8.5 x 12 = 102 feet up. After ascent: -250 + 102 = -148. Descent: 3 x 20 = 60 feet down. Final: -148 + (-60) = -208 feet.

**Part D**
8. Gas used: 4 x 3.5 = 14 gallons. Remaining: 25 - 14 = 11 gallons. Gallons to refill: 25 - 11 = 14 gallons. Cost: 14 x $3.25 = $45.50.
9. Temperature drop: 5 x (-2.3) = -11.5 degrees. Temperature at 5,000 feet: 18.5 + (-11.5) = 7 degrees Celsius.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** The temperature at 6:00 AM was -7 degrees Celsius. It rose 3.5 degrees per hour for 4 hours. What was the temperature at 10:00 AM?

**Problem 2:** A hiker starts at an elevation of 200 feet above sea level and descends into a valley that is 85 feet below sea level. What is the total number of feet she descended?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

**Exit Ticket Answers:**
1. Rise: 3.5 x 4 = 14 degrees. Temperature: -7 + 14 = **7 degrees Celsius**.
2. 200 - (-85) = 200 + 85 = **285 feet**.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify whether the difficulty is translating words into expressions or computing with signed numbers. If translation is the issue, practice identifying "key words" (drop = subtract/negative, gain = add/positive, per hour = multiply/divide).
- Provide a problem-solving template: (1) Identify the starting value. (2) List each change as positive or negative. (3) Write the expression. (4) Compute step by step.
- Return to simpler one-step word problems before attempting multi-step problems.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Assign Part D problems if not yet completed.
- Preview: "You have now mastered all the skills for this unit. The unit assessment will cover all 10 lessons. Review your notes and practice any topics where you feel less confident."

## Extension
- **Create Your Own:** Write a real-world problem that requires at least three operations with rational numbers. Solve it and create an answer key.
- **Research Project:** "Find the elevations of the highest and lowest points on each continent. Calculate the range (highest - lowest) for each. Which continent has the greatest range?"

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Translation errors.** The student writes addition when the problem describes a loss or decrease. Encourage underlining action words: "dropped," "lost," "descended" = negative. "Rose," "gained," "earned" = positive.
- **Forgetting to include the starting value.** The student computes only the changes without adding them to the starting point. Remind: "What was the STARTING temperature/balance/elevation?"
- **Confusing total distance with displacement.** In Problem 6, the student may report 85 feet as the "total distance traveled" when that is actually the displacement (net change). Total distance counts every movement regardless of direction.
- **Errors with rates (per hour, per minute).** The student may add the rate instead of multiplying. Remind: "If the temperature drops 3.5 degrees PER HOUR for 4 HOURS, that is multiplication: 3.5 x 4."

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson integrates all prior skills. Plan for 1-2 sessions depending on the student's fluency.
- If time is limited, prioritize Guided Practice problems and Part A-C of Independent Practice. Part D is for students who are ready for a challenge.
- Consider using this lesson as a review session before the unit assessment. The variety of problem types provides natural review of all operations.
- The "Create Your Own" Extension is particularly valuable because writing problems requires deeper understanding than solving them.
