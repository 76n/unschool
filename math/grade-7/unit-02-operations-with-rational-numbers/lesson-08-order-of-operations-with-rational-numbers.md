# Math -- Grade 7 -- Unit 2, Lesson 8

## Topic
Order of Operations with Rational Numbers

## Learning Objectives
- Apply the order of operations (PEMDAS) to expressions containing negative integers, fractions, and decimals.
- Distinguish between -a^2 and (-a)^2 and evaluate each correctly.
- Evaluate multi-step expressions that combine addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and exponents with signed numbers.

## Prerequisites
- Mastery of all four operations with integers and rational numbers (Lessons 1-7).
- Understanding of the order of operations (PEMDAS) with positive numbers.
- Understanding of exponents (e.g., 3^2 = 9, 2^3 = 8).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.
- Calculator (optional, for verification of complex computations).

**Standards:** 7.NS.3 -- Solve real-world and mathematical problems involving the four operations with rational numbers. 7.NS.1 -- Add and subtract rational numbers. 7.NS.2 -- Multiply and divide rational numbers.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review order of operations with positive numbers and introduce the challenge of signed numbers.

1. Evaluate: 3 + 4 x 2. (Answer: 3 + 8 = 11, not 14. Multiplication before addition.)
2. Evaluate: (3 + 4) x 2. (Answer: 7 x 2 = 14. Parentheses first.)
3. Evaluate: 5^2 - 3 x 4. (Answer: 25 - 12 = 13.)
4. Ask: "What do you think -3^2 equals? Is it 9 or -9?" (Let the student make a prediction. We will resolve this in instruction.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **PEMDAS** -- the order of operations: **P**arentheses, **E**xponents, **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision (left to right), **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction (left to right).
- **Base** -- the number being raised to a power. In (-3)^2, the base is -3. In -3^2, the base is 3.
- **Exponent** -- the power to which the base is raised.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 -- Review PEMDAS.**
Write on the board:
1. **P**arentheses (and other grouping symbols)
2. **E**xponents
3. **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision (left to right)
4. **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction (left to right)

"This order does not change when we work with negative numbers. The only new challenge is keeping track of signs at each step."

**Step 2 -- The critical distinction: -3^2 vs. (-3)^2.**

Write both on the board:

**-3^2:** The exponent applies ONLY to 3. This means -(3^2) = -(9) = **-9**.
"Think of it as: the negative sign is a coefficient of -1 multiplied by 3^2."

**(-3)^2:** The exponent applies to the entire quantity -3. This means (-3) x (-3) = **9**.
"The parentheses tell us that -3 is the base."

This is one of the most important distinctions in all of pre-algebra. Emphasize:
> **Without parentheses, the exponent applies ONLY to the number immediately before it, not the negative sign.**

More examples:
- -5^2 = -(5^2) = -25.
- (-5)^2 = (-5)(-5) = 25.
- -2^3 = -(2^3) = -8.
- (-2)^3 = (-2)(-2)(-2) = -8. (Same result here because the exponent is odd.)

**Step 3 -- Evaluate a multi-step expression.**
Example: 2 x (-4) + 6 / (-2)

Step 1: No parentheses (beyond the signs) and no exponents.
Step 2: Multiplication and division, left to right.
  - 2 x (-4) = -8
  - 6 / (-2) = -3
Step 3: Addition: -8 + (-3) = -11.

Result: 2 x (-4) + 6 / (-2) = **-11**.

**Step 4 -- Evaluate an expression with parentheses and exponents.**
Example: (-3)^2 - 4 x (-2) + 1

Step 1: Parentheses: (-3) is already simplified.
Step 2: Exponents: (-3)^2 = 9.
Step 3: Multiplication: 4 x (-2) = -8.
Step 4: Addition and subtraction, left to right: 9 - (-8) + 1 = 9 + 8 + 1 = 18.

Result: (-3)^2 - 4 x (-2) + 1 = **18**.

**Step 5 -- Expression with fractions.**
Example: -1/2 + 3/4 x (-2/3)

Step 1: Multiplication first: 3/4 x (-2/3) = -6/12 = -1/2.
Step 2: Addition: -1/2 + (-1/2) = -1.

Result: -1/2 + 3/4 x (-2/3) = **-1**.

### Worked Examples

**Example A -- Mixed operations with integers**
Evaluate: -5 + 3 x (-4) - (-2)

Step 1: Multiplication: 3 x (-4) = -12.
Step 2: Rewrite: -5 + (-12) - (-2) = -5 + (-12) + 2.
Step 3: Left to right: -5 + (-12) = -17. Then -17 + 2 = -15.
Answer: **-15**.

**Example B -- Exponents and operations**
Evaluate: -4^2 + (-2)^3

Step 1: Exponents: -4^2 = -(4^2) = -16. (-2)^3 = (-2)(-2)(-2) = -8.
Step 2: Addition: -16 + (-8) = -24.
Answer: **-24**.

**Example C -- Parentheses change the outcome**
Evaluate: (-6 + 2) x (-3)

Step 1: Parentheses: -6 + 2 = -4.
Step 2: Multiplication: (-4) x (-3) = 12.
Answer: **12**.

**Example D -- Decimals and order of operations**
Evaluate: -1.5^2 + 4.2 / (-0.6)

Step 1: Exponents: -1.5^2 = -(1.5^2) = -(2.25) = -2.25.
Step 2: Division: 4.2 / (-0.6) = -7.
Step 3: Addition: -2.25 + (-7) = -9.25.
Answer: **-9.25**.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Evaluate: -2^2 and (-2)^2. What is the difference?
   - *Facilitator tip:* "In -2^2, the exponent applies only to 2. In (-2)^2, the exponent applies to -2."

2. Evaluate: 5 - 3 x (-2)
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Multiplication first: 3 x (-2) = -6. Then 5 - (-6) = ?"

3. Evaluate: (-8) / 4 + (-3)^2
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Division and exponents before addition. Compute each piece first."

4. Evaluate: -1/3 x 6 - 2/5
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Multiplication first: -1/3 x 6 = -2. Then -2 - 2/5 = ?"

5. Evaluate: (4 - 7)^2 - 3 x (-5)
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Parentheses first, then exponents, then multiplication, then subtraction."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A -- Exponent Distinction
1. Evaluate: -5^2
2. Evaluate: (-5)^2
3. Evaluate: -2^4
4. Evaluate: (-2)^4

### Part B -- Multi-Step Expressions
5. 6 + (-4) x 3
6. (-3) x (-2) + 8 / (-4)
7. -10 - (-3)^2 + 7
8. (5 - 9) / (-2) + 3

### Part C -- Expressions with Fractions and Decimals
9. -1/2 x 4/3 + 5/6
10. 2.5 x (-4) - (-3.5)^2 (Note: -3.5^2 would be -(3.5^2), but here we have (-3.5)^2.)
    Actually evaluate: 2.5 x (-4) - 3.5^2
11. (-1/4)^2 + 1/2 x (-3/4)
12. Create your own expression that uses at least one negative number, one exponent, and two different operations. Evaluate it and provide the answer.

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. -5^2 = -(5^2) = -25.
2. (-5)^2 = (-5)(-5) = 25.
3. -2^4 = -(2^4) = -16.
4. (-2)^4 = (-2)(-2)(-2)(-2) = 16.

**Part B**
5. 6 + (-4) x 3: Multiplication first: (-4) x 3 = -12. Then 6 + (-12) = -6.
6. (-3) x (-2) + 8 / (-4): Multiply: 6. Divide: -2. Add: 6 + (-2) = 4.
7. -10 - (-3)^2 + 7: Exponent: (-3)^2 = 9. Rewrite: -10 - 9 + 7 = -10 + (-9) + 7 = -19 + 7 = -12.
8. (5 - 9) / (-2) + 3: Parentheses: 5 - 9 = -4. Division: (-4) / (-2) = 2. Addition: 2 + 3 = 5.

**Part C**
9. -1/2 x 4/3 + 5/6: Multiplication first: -1/2 x 4/3 = -4/6 = -2/3. Addition: -2/3 + 5/6 = -4/6 + 5/6 = 1/6.
10. 2.5 x (-4) - 3.5^2: Multiply: 2.5 x (-4) = -10. Exponent: 3.5^2 = 12.25. Subtract: -10 - 12.25 = -22.25.
11. (-1/4)^2 + 1/2 x (-3/4): Exponent: (-1/4)^2 = 1/16. Multiply: 1/2 x (-3/4) = -3/8. Add: 1/16 + (-3/8) = 1/16 + (-6/16) = -5/16.
12. Answers will vary. Accept any correct expression that meets the criteria.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** Evaluate: (-3)^2 - 4 x 2

**Problem 2:** Evaluate: -2^3 + 6 / (-3)

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

**Exit Ticket Answers:**
1. (-3)^2 = 9. 4 x 2 = 8. 9 - 8 = **1**.
2. -2^3 = -(2^3) = -8. 6 / (-3) = -2. -8 + (-2) = **-10**.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Provide a PEMDAS checklist card. Have the student check off each step as they work through an expression.
- Focus specifically on the -a^2 vs. (-a)^2 distinction with 5 additional pairs of problems.
- Break complex expressions into labeled steps: "Step 1: Parentheses. Step 2: Exponents. Step 3: Multiply/Divide. Step 4: Add/Subtract."
- If sign errors persist in the final addition/subtraction step, review Lessons 1-3.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Assign Part C problems if not yet completed.
- Preview: "In the next lesson, we explore properties like the commutative, associative, and distributive properties. These help us simplify expressions before computing."

## Extension
- **Nested Parentheses:** Evaluate: [(-2)^2 + 3] x (-1) - (-4). (Answer: [4 + 3] x (-1) - (-4) = 7 x (-1) + 4 = -7 + 4 = -3.)
- **Error Analysis:** "A student evaluated -6^2 / (-3) + 2 as follows: -6^2 = 36, 36 / (-3) = -12, -12 + 2 = -10. Find the error and correct the work." (Error: -6^2 = -36, not 36. Correct: -36 / (-3) = 12. 12 + 2 = 14.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **The -a^2 vs. (-a)^2 error.** This is the single most common error in this lesson and persists well into high school. Drill it repeatedly. Write -3^2 and (-3)^2 side by side, evaluate both, and ask the student to explain the difference.
- **Performing addition before multiplication.** The student evaluates 6 + (-4) x 3 as 2 x 3 = 6 instead of 6 + (-12) = -6. Ask: "Which operation comes first in PEMDAS?"
- **Sign errors in the final step.** The student computes each piece correctly but makes a sign error when combining. Encourage writing each intermediate result explicitly before combining.

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson is procedurally dense. Consider splitting across two sessions: Session 1 covers Steps 1-3 (exponent distinction and integer expressions), Session 2 covers Steps 4-5 (expressions with fractions and decimals).
- The exponent distinction (Step 2) is the highest-priority concept. Spend extra time here even if it means shortening other sections.
- The error analysis Extension problem is excellent for deepening understanding. Assign it as homework or use it to open the second session.
