# Facilitator Guide — Grade 7 Math, Unit 2: Operations with Rational Numbers

**Grade:** 7
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 2 — Operations with Rational Numbers
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (facilitator leads instruction; student works independently for practice)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit extends the four arithmetic operations to the full set of rational numbers, including negative integers, negative fractions, and negative decimals. Students begin with integer addition and subtraction using number line models, build sign rules for multiplication and division, and then apply these rules to rational numbers in fractional and decimal form. The unit culminates with order of operations involving signed numbers, the commutative, associative, and distributive properties applied to rational numbers, and multi-step real-world problems that require all four operations.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Add integers with the same sign and with different signs using number line models and sign rules
- Subtract integers by rewriting subtraction as adding the opposite
- Multiply and divide integers using sign rules, including reasoning about why negative times negative is positive
- Extend all four operations to rational numbers in fraction and decimal form
- Apply the order of operations (PEMDAS) to expressions containing negative numbers
- Distinguish between expressions such as -3^2 and (-3)^2
- Apply the commutative, associative, and distributive properties to simplify expressions with rational numbers
- Solve multi-step real-world problems involving operations with rational numbers (temperature change, financial gain/loss, elevation, net motion)

**Why this matters:** Operations with rational numbers are the gateway to algebra. Every equation students will solve, every function they will graph, and every real-world model they will build depends on fluency with positive and negative numbers. Temperature, debt, elevation below sea level, stock market changes -- the real world is full of quantities that require signed numbers. Students who master this unit will approach algebraic expressions and equations with confidence.

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm:

- [ ] Can locate positive and negative integers on a number line
- [ ] Understands absolute value as a number's distance from zero
- [ ] Is fluent with addition and subtraction of whole numbers
- [ ] Is fluent with multiplication and division of whole numbers
- [ ] Can add, subtract, multiply, and divide fractions and decimals (positive values)
- [ ] Understands the concept of opposites (e.g., the opposite of 5 is -5)
- [ ] Can apply order of operations (PEMDAS) to expressions with positive numbers

## Pacing Guidance

| Lesson | Topic | Sessions |
|--------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Adding Integers: Same Sign | 1 |
| 2 | Adding Integers: Different Signs | 1 |
| 3 | Subtracting Integers | 1-2 |
| 4 | Multiplying Integers | 1 |
| 5 | Dividing Integers | 1 |
| 6 | Adding and Subtracting Rational Numbers | 1-2 |
| 7 | Multiplying and Dividing Rational Numbers | 1-2 |
| 8 | Order of Operations with Rational Numbers | 1-2 |
| 9 | Properties of Operations | 1 |
| 10 | Real-World Applications | 1-2 |
| -- | Unit Assessment | Separate session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lessons 1 and 2 form a natural pair. If the student grasps same-sign addition quickly in Lesson 1, begin Lesson 2 in the same session.
- Lesson 3 (subtraction as adding the opposite) is a critical conceptual shift. Allow extra time here -- students who do not internalize the "add the opposite" rule will struggle with every subsequent lesson.
- Lessons 4 and 5 share the same sign rules. If the student masters multiplication sign rules quickly, Lesson 5 can follow immediately.
- Lessons 6 and 7 extend integer rules to fractions and decimals. Students who are already fluent with fraction/decimal arithmetic may move quickly; students who are not will need extra time on the mechanics.
- Lesson 8 (order of operations) is the most procedurally dense. Budget extra time and consider splitting across two sessions if the student struggles with nested operations.
- Lesson 10 (applications) integrates all skills. It works well as a two-session lesson, with the first session focused on structured examples and the second on independent problem-solving.

## Lesson Summaries

| Lesson | Summary |
|--------|---------|
| 1 | Students add integers with the same sign using number line models. They develop the rule: add the absolute values and keep the common sign. |
| 2 | Students add integers with different signs using number line models. They develop the rule: subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger and take the sign of the number with the greater absolute value. |
| 3 | Students rewrite subtraction as adding the opposite (a - b = a + (-b)) and use Lessons 1-2 rules to compute. The number line model reinforces the concept. |
| 4 | Students discover sign rules for multiplication through pattern-based reasoning. They understand why positive x positive = positive, positive x negative = negative, and negative x negative = positive. |
| 5 | Students apply the same sign rules to division, recognizing division as the inverse of multiplication. They address 0 / n = 0 and n / 0 as undefined. |
| 6 | Students extend integer addition and subtraction rules to fractions and decimals, combining sign rules with fraction/decimal arithmetic. |
| 7 | Students extend integer multiplication and division rules to fractions and decimals, reinforcing sign determination before computing. |
| 8 | Students apply order of operations (PEMDAS) to multi-step expressions with signed numbers. They distinguish -3^2 from (-3)^2 and evaluate complex expressions. |
| 9 | Students verify and apply the commutative, associative, and distributive properties with rational numbers. They use properties to simplify expressions. |
| 10 | Students solve multi-step real-world problems involving all four operations with rational numbers: temperature change, financial gain/loss, elevation, and net motion. |

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Student writes -5 + (-3) = -2 (subtracts instead of adds) | Confuses same-sign addition with different-sign addition | Return to the number line: "Start at -5, move 3 more units left. Did you get closer to zero or farther from zero?" Both negatives move in the same direction. |
| Student says -4 + 7 = -3 (keeps the wrong sign) | Applies the rule mechanically but assigns the wrong sign | Anchor to absolute values: "|7| = 7 is larger than |-4| = 4, and 7 is positive, so the answer is positive." Use the number line to verify. |
| Student writes 8 - (-3) = 5 instead of 11 | Does not convert subtraction to adding the opposite | Emphasize the rule: "Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive. Rewrite: 8 + 3 = 11." Connect to real life: "If you remove a $3 debt, your balance goes UP by $3." |
| Student says negative x negative = negative | Has not internalized the sign rule for multiplication | Use the pattern approach: 3 x (-2) = -6, 2 x (-2) = -4, 1 x (-2) = -2, 0 x (-2) = 0, -1 x (-2) = ? The pattern shows the product increases by 2 each time, so -1 x (-2) = 2. |
| Student writes -3^2 = 9 | Confuses -3^2 with (-3)^2 | Clarify: "-3^2 means 'the negative of 3 squared' = -(3^2) = -9. Only (-3)^2 means 'negative 3, squared' = 9." Parentheses determine what the exponent applies to. |
| Student applies integer rules but makes fraction arithmetic errors | Integer sign rules are correct, but fraction computation is weak | Separate the two skills: first determine the sign, then compute with absolute values. Address fraction arithmetic gaps with targeted review. |
| Student treats 0 / 5 and 5 / 0 as the same | Does not distinguish zero as numerator versus zero as denominator | "Zero divided by anything is 0 -- you are sharing nothing. Anything divided by zero is undefined -- you cannot split something into zero groups." |

## Differentiation Strategies

**For the student who needs more support:**
- Use two-color counters (red = negative, yellow = positive) for integer addition in Lessons 1-3. Pair positive and negative chips to find zero pairs.
- Provide a sign-rule reference card for Lessons 4-7 until rules become automatic.
- Break multi-step problems into single operations. Have the student solve one step at a time, writing the result before proceeding.
- For Lessons 6-7, practice the sign rules with integers first, then repeat the same problem structure with fractions/decimals.

**For the student who needs more challenge:**
- Introduce expressions with multiple operations early (e.g., "Evaluate -3 x 4 + (-2)^2 - 7" in Lesson 5).
- Ask the student to write their own word problems that require specific combinations of operations.
- Explore absolute value equations informally: "What values of x make |x| = 5 true?"
- In Lesson 9, challenge the student to prove properties using specific rational number examples and then explain WHY the property holds in general.

## Conversation Starters

- "If the temperature is -8 degrees and drops another 5 degrees, what is the new temperature? How would you write that as an addition problem?"
- "You owe your friend $12 and then earn $20. Are you in debt or do you have money left? How does that connect to -12 + 20?"
- "Why does multiplying two negative numbers give a positive result? Can you convince me using a pattern?"
- "A submarine is at -200 feet. It rises 75 feet, then sinks 30 feet. Where is it now? What operations did you use?"
- "Is -5^2 the same as (-5)^2? Why does it matter where the parentheses go?"

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if:

- The student cannot reliably locate negative numbers on a number line or does not understand that -8 < -3
- Same-sign addition (Lesson 1) remains inconsistent after reteaching -- all subsequent lessons depend on this skill
- The student cannot distinguish subtraction from addition of the opposite after explicit instruction in Lesson 3
- Sign rules for multiplication and division are not retained across sessions despite repeated practice
- Fraction and decimal arithmetic errors are so pervasive that they mask understanding of the sign rules
- The student cannot follow order of operations with positive numbers (prerequisite gap that must be addressed before Lesson 8)

## What Comes Next

After this unit: **Unit 3: Expressions and Equations** -- simplifying expressions using properties of operations, and solving multi-step equations and inequalities. The fluency with rational number operations built here is the direct foundation for that work.

Then: **Unit 4: Geometry** -- scale drawings, angles, area, surface area, and volume, many of which require computation with rational numbers.
