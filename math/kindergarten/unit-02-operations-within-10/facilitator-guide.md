# Facilitator Guide — Kindergarten Math, Unit 2: Addition and Subtraction Within 10

**Grade:** Kindergarten
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 2 — Addition and Subtraction Within 10
**Recommended Duration:** 8 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Fully facilitated (adult present and actively guiding throughout)

---

## Unit Overview

In this unit, students take their counting skills and use them to solve their first real math problems: putting groups together (addition) and taking groups apart (subtraction). Everything here is concrete — physical objects, not symbols.

By the end of this unit, your student will be able to:

- Represent addition as putting two groups together and counting the total
- Represent subtraction as taking objects away from a group and counting what remains
- Solve addition and subtraction problems within 10 using objects and drawings
- Understand the meaning of the + , −, and = signs
- Create and solve simple story problems (word problems) involving addition and subtraction within 10

**Why this matters:** Addition and subtraction are the two foundational operations of arithmetic. Everything in later math — multiplication, division, fractions, algebra — builds on the ability to combine and separate quantities. By starting with objects and stories (not worksheets), we build genuine understanding rather than rote procedure.

---

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm that the student has mastered Unit 1 skills:

- [ ] Counts from 1 to 10 fluently
- [ ] Counts objects up to 10 using one-to-one correspondence
- [ ] Answers "how many?" without recounting (cardinality)
- [ ] Recognizes numerals 0-10
- [ ] Compares groups using "more," "fewer," and "the same"

If the student scored below 85% on the Unit 1 assessment, address gaps before starting this unit.

## Materials Needed for This Unit

- **Counting objects:** 20+ small objects (two types/colors are helpful — e.g., 10 red blocks and 10 blue blocks)
- **Paper plates or bowls** (to hold groups)
- **Number cards 0-10**
- **Pencil, crayons, and paper**
- **Ten-frame** (the 2x5 grid from Unit 1)
- **+ − = signs** (write these on index cards in large print)
- **Small toy figures or pictures** (optional — for story problems)

## Pacing Guidance

| Lesson | Topic | Sessions |
|--------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Putting Together (Addition Concept) | 1 session |
| 2 | Addition with Objects — Sums to 5 | 1 session |
| 3 | Addition with Objects — Sums to 10 | 1 session |
| 4 | The + and = Signs | 1 session |
| 5 | Taking Away (Subtraction Concept) | 1 session |
| 6 | Subtraction with Objects — Within 10 | 1 session |
| 7 | The − Sign; Mixed Addition and Subtraction | 1 session |
| 8 | Story Problems (Word Problems) | 1 session |
| — | Unit Assessment | Part of Lesson 8 or a separate short session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lessons 1-3 (addition) form a natural block. If the student needs more time, add a session of pure practice between Lessons 3 and 4.
- The + , −, and = signs (Lessons 4 and 7) are about notation, not new concepts. If the student already knows what these symbols mean, these lessons can go faster.
- Story problems (Lesson 8) are critical — they connect math to real life. Don't skip or rush this lesson.

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Student counts all objects from 1 instead of "counting on" from the first group | Counting-all is the first strategy; counting-on develops later | This is normal and appropriate for kindergarten. Do not push counting-on. Let the student count all objects. Efficiency comes with maturity. |
| Student loses track when combining two groups | Working memory is still developing | Use physical separation: keep the two groups on separate plates, then push them together. Count the combined group from 1. |
| Student doesn't understand "take away" | Subtraction is more abstract than addition for many children | Make it dramatic and physical: "I have 5 cookies... and I EAT 2!" (Remove 2 objects.) "How many are left?" Start with very small numbers (take 1 away from 3). |
| Student writes 3 + 2 = 32 or 3 + 2 = 3 | The = sign doesn't mean "the answer is" to them yet | Emphasize: "The equals sign means 'is the same as.' 3 + 2 is the same as 5." Use a balance metaphor: both sides must balance. |
| Student can do 3 + 2 but not 2 + 3 | Hasn't discovered commutativity | Show with objects: "Look — 3 red and 2 blue makes 5. Now 2 red and 3 blue also makes 5! The order doesn't change the total." Don't use the word "commutative" — just show it. |
| Student gets the right answer by guessing but can't show it with objects | May be memorizing rather than understanding | Always ask: "Show me with the blocks." Understanding means being able to model the problem physically. |

## Conversation Starters

Use these throughout the day to reinforce addition and subtraction thinking:

- "You have 3 crackers and I give you 2 more. How many do you have now?"
- "There are 6 birds on the fence. 2 fly away. How many are left?"
- "We need 5 plates for dinner. We have 3 out. How many more do we need?"
- "You drew 4 circles. If you draw 3 more, how many will there be?"
- "How many ways can we split 5 blocks into two groups?"

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if, after completing this unit:

- The student cannot consistently model addition (putting groups together) with objects, even with sums to 5
- The student shows persistent confusion about what subtraction means despite repeated concrete demonstrations
- The student becomes very anxious or avoidant when presented with math problems (this may indicate math anxiety developing)
- The student cannot bridge from objects to any symbolic representation after several weeks of practice
