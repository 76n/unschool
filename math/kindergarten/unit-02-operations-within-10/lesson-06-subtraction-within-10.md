# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 2, Lesson 6

## Topic
Subtraction with Objects — Within 10

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve subtraction problems starting from numbers up to 10 using objects or drawings
- Use the ten-frame to model subtraction (start with objects and remove some)
- Connect subtraction results to counting — verifying by counting what remains

## Prerequisites
- Understands subtraction as taking away (Lesson 5)
- Can use the ten-frame (Unit 1, Lesson 5)
- Addition with sums to 10 (Lesson 3)

## Materials Needed
- 10 counting objects
- Ten-frame
- Paper and crayons
- Number cards 0-10

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review subtraction concept and earlier skills.

1. "5 take away 2 leaves ___?" (Answer: 3) *(Lesson 5 review)*
2. "4 take away 1 leaves ___?" (Answer: 3) *(Lesson 5 review)*
3. "3 + 4 = ___?" (Answer: 7) *(Lesson 3 review — interleaved addition)*
4. Show a ten-frame with 8 filled. "How many?" (Answer: 8) *(Unit 1 review)*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Extend subtraction to starting quantities of 6-10.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Difference:** The result of subtracting — how many are left (introduced as vocabulary, not required from the student)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Subtraction on the ten-frame.**
Fill the ten-frame with 8 counters. "I have 8. I'm going to take away 3."

Remove 3 counters from the ten-frame. "Let's count what's left: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Eight take away three leaves five."

Point to the ten-frame: "See? We started with 8 (top 5 + bottom 3). We removed 3 from the bottom. Now only the top 5 are left."

**Step 2: More ten-frame problems.**
- Start with 7, take away 2: "1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Leaves 5."
- Start with 10, take away 4: "1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. Leaves 6."
- Start with 9, take away 5: "1, 2, 3, 4. Leaves 4."

**Step 3: Introduce crossing out in drawings.**
Draw 7 circles on paper. "I have 7. I need to take away 3."

Cross out 3 circles with an X. "Now I count the ones that aren't crossed out: 1, 2, 3, 4. Seven take away three leaves four."

"Crossing out in a drawing is like taking away objects. The X means 'this one is gone.'"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Ten-frame shows 10. Remove 6. Count remaining: 4. "10 take away 6 leaves 4."

**Example 2:** Draw 9 dots. Cross out 4. Count remaining: 5. "9 take away 4 leaves 5."

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student models subtraction within 10.

**Activity 1: Ten-frame subtraction (5 minutes)**
For each problem, student fills the ten-frame, removes the specified amount, and counts what remains.

- 8 take away 2 → ___ (6)
- 7 take away 4 → ___ (3)
- 10 take away 5 → ___ (5)
- 9 take away 3 → ___ (6)
- 6 take away 6 → ___ (0) — "All gone!"

*Facilitator prompt: Watch for students who remove the wrong number of objects. If they take away 3 instead of 2, the error is in the taking-away counting, not the subtraction concept. Say: "Let's count carefully as we remove: 1, 2. That's 2 taken away."*

**Activity 2: Draw and cross out (5 minutes)**
Student draws circles and crosses some out to solve:

- Draw 6 circles. Cross out 2. How many left? ___ (4)
- Draw 8 circles. Cross out 5. How many left? ___ (3)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student's crossed-out circles are hard to distinguish from uncrossed ones, suggest: "Make a big X right through the middle of each one you're taking away."*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student solves subtraction problems within 10 independently.

**★ On-level:**
1. 6 take away 2 leaves ___ (4)
2. 8 take away 3 leaves ___ (5)
3. 7 take away 1 leaves ___ (6)
4. 10 take away 5 leaves ___ (5)

**★★ Stretch:**
5. 9 take away 6 leaves ___ (3)
6. Draw 10 circles. Cross out 7. How many left? ___ (3)
7. "Start with 8. Take away some to have 5 left. How many did you take away?" (3)

**★★★ Challenge:**
8. "10 take away what number leaves 4?" ___ (6)
9. "I had 7 blocks. Now I have 3. How many did I take away?" (4)

### Interleaved Review
- "4 + 5 = ___?" (9) *(Lesson 3 review)*
- "Write the number sentence: three plus two equals five." (3 + 2 = 5) *(Lesson 4 review)*
- "5 take away 5 = ___?" (0) *(Lesson 5 review)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess subtraction within 10.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Use objects or ten-frame: "9 take away 4 leaves ___?" (Answer: 5)
2. Draw 7 circles and cross out 3. "How many left?" (Answer: 4)
3. "10 take away 10 leaves ___?" (Answer: 0)

**Mastery = Correct on at least 2 of 3 items.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice with starting numbers of 5-7 before attempting 8-10.
- Always use the ten-frame — the visual structure prevents losing count.
- Ensure the student can reliably count the remaining objects. If counting is the issue, revisit Unit 1.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (The − Sign; Mixed Addition and Subtraction).

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Subtraction on a number line:** Draw a number line 0-10. Start at 8, hop back 3 spaces. Where do you land? (5.) This previews the number line as a subtraction tool.
- **Connection between addition and subtraction:** "If 3 + 4 = 7, then what is 7 take away 4?" (3.) "And 7 take away 3?" (4.) Show this with objects to make the connection visible.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Crossing out in drawings is a key representational strategy.** It bridges from physical objects (concrete) to paper-based math (representational). Some students resist drawing and prefer objects — that's fine. Others will naturally move to drawing. Both are valid strategies.

**Subtraction from 10 is especially important.** Just as "make 10" pairs matter for addition, subtraction from 10 (10-1=9, 10-2=8, etc.) is foundational for later mental math. Students will encounter this pattern repeatedly.

**Watch for the addition-subtraction connection.** If a student solving "8 take away 3" says "5, because 5 and 3 makes 8," they are demonstrating fact family understanding — a sophisticated insight. Celebrate it but don't require it; most kindergarteners solve by counting.
