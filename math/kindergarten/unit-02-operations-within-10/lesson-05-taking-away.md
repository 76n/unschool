# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 2, Lesson 5

## Topic
Taking Away (Introduction to Subtraction)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Understand that subtraction means taking some away from a group
- Remove objects from a group and count how many remain
- Use the phrase "___ take away ___ leaves ___"

## Prerequisites
- Counts objects to 10 with one-to-one correspondence (Unit 1)
- Understands addition as putting groups together (Lessons 1-3)

## Materials Needed
- 10 counting objects
- A paper plate or bowl
- Paper and crayons
- Small toy figures or snack items (optional — makes stories more engaging)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review addition and Unit 1 skills.

1. "3 + 2 = ___?" (Answer: 5) *(Lesson 4 review)*
2. "Write the number sentence: two plus four equals six." (2 + 4 = 6) *(Lesson 4 review)*
3. "Name a pair that makes 10." (Any valid pair) *(Lesson 3 review)*
4. Place 6 objects. "How many?" (6) *(Unit 1 review)*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce subtraction as the physical act of removing objects from a group.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Take away:** To remove some from a group
- **Subtract:** The math word for "take away"
- **Left / Remaining:** What stays after you take some away

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Tell a dramatic story.**
Place 5 objects on the plate. "I have 5 cookies on my plate. Yum! I'm going to EAT 2 of them." (Dramatically remove 2 objects and hide them behind your back.)

"How many cookies are LEFT on the plate? Let's count: 1, 2, 3. Three!"

"I started with 5. I took away 2. Now I have 3. Five take away two leaves three."

**Step 2: Repeat with another story.**
Place 4 blocks on the table. "There are 4 birds on a branch. 1 bird flies away!" (Remove 1 block.)

"How many birds are left? 1, 2, 3. Three birds."

"Four take away one leaves three."

**Step 3: Identify the pattern.**
"When we TAKE AWAY, we:
1. Start with a group.
2. Remove some.
3. Count what's left."

"Adding makes the group BIGGER. Taking away makes the group SMALLER."

**Step 4: Connect to comparison.**
Place 5 objects. Remove 2. "I started with 5 and now I have 3. Is 3 more or fewer than 5?" (Fewer.) "When we take away, we always end up with fewer than we started with."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Start with 4 objects. Take away 1. Count: 3 left. "Four take away one leaves three."

**Example 2:** Start with 3 objects. Take away 3. Count: 0 left! "Three take away three leaves ZERO. Nothing left!"

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices subtraction with objects.

**Activity 1: Take-away stories (5 minutes)**
Set up each problem. Student removes objects and counts what remains.

- Start with 5, take away 1 → "Five take away one leaves ___?" (4)
- Start with 4, take away 2 → "Four take away two leaves ___?" (2)
- Start with 3, take away 1 → "Three take away one leaves ___?" (2)
- Start with 5, take away 5 → "Five take away five leaves ___?" (0)

Have the student say the full sentence each time.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student doesn't know where to start, say: "First, put 5 objects on the plate. Good. Now take 2 away — move them OFF the plate. Now count what's left on the plate." The physical movement is key.*

**Activity 2: Student creates problems (5 minutes)**
"Your turn to make a take-away story! Put some objects on the plate. Tell me how many. Now take some away. How many are left?"

Let the student create 3-4 problems. For each, prompt them to say the sentence: "___ take away ___ leaves ___."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student tries to take away MORE than they started with (e.g., start with 3 and take away 5), say: "Hmm, you only have 3. You can only take away 3 or fewer. You can't eat more cookies than you have!" This is a natural boundary.*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student solves subtraction problems independently.

**★ On-level:**
1. Start with 4, take away 1. How many left? ___ (3)
2. Start with 5, take away 2. How many left? ___ (3)
3. Start with 3, take away 2. How many left? ___ (1)

**★★ Stretch:**
4. Start with 5, take away 3. How many left? ___ (2)
5. Start with 4, take away 4. How many left? ___ (0)
6. Draw 5 circles. Cross out 2. How many are not crossed out? ___ (3)

**★★★ Challenge:**
7. "I had some cookies. I ate 2 and now I have 3. How many did I start with?" (5) *(Preview: missing starting number)*
8. "Start with 5. Take away 1. Take away 1 more. How many now?" (3)

### Interleaved Review
- "2 + 3 = ___?" (5) *(Lesson 2 review)*
- "What number comes before 8?" (7) *(Unit 1 review)*
- "Write the number 4." *(Unit 1 review)*

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess understanding of subtraction as taking away.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Place 5 objects. "Take away 3. How many are left?" (Answer: 2)
2. "Three take away one leaves ___?" (Answer: 2, shown with objects)
3. "What does 'take away' mean in math?" (Accept: "remove some," "subtract," or equivalent)

**Mastery = Correct on at least 2 of 3 items.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Use very small numbers: start with 3, take away 1. Start with 2, take away 1.
- Make it more concrete and fun: use snack items (pretend to eat them), toy animals (they "walk away").
- Repeat this lesson before Lesson 6.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 6 (Subtraction with Objects — Within 10).

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Subtraction stories:** Student invents subtraction stories: "There were 4 fish. 2 swam away. Now there are 2."
- **Addition and subtraction contrast:** Place 3 blocks. "If I add 2, how many?" (5.) Start over with 3. "If I take away 2?" (1.) "Adding makes more, taking away makes less."

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Subtraction is harder than addition for most children.** With addition, both groups are visible before and after combining. With subtraction, objects disappear. This is more abstract. Be patient and keep everything physical.

**Drama helps.** The more dramatic the "taking away" — eating the cookie, the bird flying away, the ball rolling under the couch — the more memorable and meaningful it becomes. Stories with action beat abstract problems.

**Don't introduce the minus sign yet.** Just as we introduced addition physically before symbols (Lesson 1 before Lesson 4), we introduce subtraction physically here. The minus sign comes in Lesson 7.

**"Take away" is the first meaning of subtraction.** Later (in Grade 1-2), students learn comparison subtraction ("How many more does group A have than group B?"). For now, "take away" is sufficient and concrete.
