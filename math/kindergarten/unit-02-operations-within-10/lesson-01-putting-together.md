# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 2, Lesson 1

## Topic
Putting Together (Introduction to Addition)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Understand that addition means putting two groups together to make a bigger group
- Combine two groups of objects and count the total (sums to 5)
- Use the phrase "__ and __ makes __"

## Prerequisites
- Counts objects to 10 with one-to-one correspondence (Unit 1)
- Understands cardinality (Unit 1, Lesson 3)

## Materials Needed
- 10 counting objects in two colors (e.g., 5 red blocks and 5 blue blocks)
- Two paper plates or bowls
- Paper and crayons (for drawing)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review Unit 1 counting and comparison skills.

1. Place 7 objects in a scattered group. "Count these and tell me how many." (Answer: 7) *(Unit 1 review)*
2. Flash number cards 3, 8, 5. Student names each. *(Unit 1 review)*
3. Place 4 objects on one plate, 6 on another. "Which has more?" (Answer: the plate with 6) *(Unit 1 review)*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce addition as a physical act of combining two groups.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Add:** To put groups together
- **Total:** How many there are after you put groups together (also called "altogether")
- **And:** The word that connects two groups being added

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Tell a story.**
"I have 2 red blocks on this plate." (Place 2 red blocks on the left plate.)
"And I have 1 blue block on this plate." (Place 1 blue block on the right plate.)
"Now I'm going to put them TOGETHER." (Slide both groups onto one plate.)
"Let's count how many there are altogether: 1, 2, 3. Two and one makes THREE."

**Step 2: Repeat with emphasis on the language.**
"Let's try another one. I have 3 red blocks." (Place on left plate.)
"And 2 blue blocks." (Place on right plate.)
"I put them together..." (Combine onto one plate.)
"And count: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Three and two makes FIVE."

Point out: "When we put groups together, we get a BIGGER group. That's called ADDING."

**Step 3: Introduce the pattern.**
"Every time we add, we do three things:
1. We start with two groups.
2. We put them together.
3. We count to find the total."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 1 red block and 3 blue blocks. Put together: 1, 2, 3, 4. "One and three makes four."

**Example 2:** 4 red blocks and 1 blue block. Put together: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. "Four and one makes five."

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices combining groups with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: You try (5 minutes)**
Set up each problem with two groups on separate plates. The student combines them and counts the total.

- 2 red and 2 blue → "Two and two makes ___?" (4)
- 1 red and 4 blue → "One and four makes ___?" (5)
- 3 red and 1 blue → "Three and one makes ___?" (4)

After each one, have the student say the full sentence: "___ and ___ makes ___."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student forgets to count from 1 after combining, that's fine — it means they might be intuitively adding! But if they get the wrong answer, redirect: "Let's count them all from the beginning."*

**Activity 2: Make your own (5 minutes)**
Give the student both colors of blocks. Say: "Put some red blocks on one plate and some blue blocks on the other plate. Then put them together and tell me how many."

Let the student choose the quantities (keep total to 5 or below). They combine and count.

*Facilitator prompt: Ask "How many red? How many blue? How many altogether?" Use the language consistently.*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student combines groups and states totals on their own.

**★ On-level:**
1. 2 objects and 1 object. Put together. How many? ___ (3)
2. 3 objects and 2 objects. Put together. How many? ___ (5)
3. 1 object and 1 object. Put together. How many? ___ (2)

**★★ Stretch:**
4. 4 objects and 1 object. How many altogether? ___ (5)
5. Draw 2 circles and 3 circles. How many in all? ___ (5)

**★★★ Challenge:**
6. "I have 3 blocks. How many more do I need to make 5?" (2) *(Preview: missing addend)*
7. "Can you find two groups that make 4?" (Accept any: 1+3, 2+2, 3+1, 4+0)

### Interleaved Review
- "Count backward from 10 to 1." *(Unit 1 extension)*
- Show numeral 9. "What number is this?" *(Unit 1 review)*

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess whether the student understands addition as combining.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Place 2 and 3 on separate plates. "Put them together and tell me how many." (Answer: 5)
2. "Two and one makes ___?" (Student uses objects to show 3.)
3. "What does 'add' mean?" (Accept any version of "put together" or "combine.")

**Mastery = Student correctly combines groups and states the total for at least 2 of 3 items.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Continue with physical combining activities. Use real-life scenarios: combining food items, toys, etc.
- Keep sums at 3-4 until the student is confident, then build to 5.
- Repeat this lesson before Lesson 2.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 2 (Addition with Objects — Sums to 5).

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Addition stories:** "There were 2 puppies playing. Then 3 more puppies came. How many puppies now?" Student acts out with toys.
- **Five-frame addition:** Use a five-frame (1x5 grid). Place 2 counters, then add 1 more. See how many spaces are filled.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**No symbols yet.** This lesson is entirely about the physical act and the language. The + and = signs come in Lesson 4. Don't introduce them early — understanding the concept before learning the notation is critical.

**"And" is the bridge word.** The phrase "___ and ___ makes ___" is the verbal precursor to "___ + ___ = ___." Use it consistently so the transition to symbols feels natural.

**Let students count all.** The most efficient strategy would be "counting on" (starting from the larger number), but kindergarteners are not developmentally ready for that. Let them count all objects from 1. Efficiency develops over time.

**Keep sums to 5 for now.** Larger sums come in Lesson 3. The cognitive load of a new concept (addition) combined with larger numbers can overwhelm. Start small.
