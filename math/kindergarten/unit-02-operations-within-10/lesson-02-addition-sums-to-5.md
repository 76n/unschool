# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 2, Lesson 2

## Topic
Addition with Objects — Sums to 5

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve addition problems with sums up to 5 using objects
- Record addition using drawings (draw circles or dots to represent objects)
- Identify all the ways to make a target number (compositions of 3, 4, and 5)

## Prerequisites
- Understands addition as putting two groups together (Lesson 1)
- Counts objects to 10 with one-to-one correspondence (Unit 1)

## Materials Needed
- 10 counting objects in two colors
- Paper and crayons
- Five-frame (a 1x5 grid — draw on paper)
- Number cards 0-5

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review addition concept and Unit 1 skills.

1. "2 and 1 makes ___?" Student uses objects to show 3. *(Review: Lesson 1)*
2. Place 8 objects. "How many?" (Answer: 8) *(Unit 1 review)*
3. "3 and 2 makes ___?" (Answer: 5) *(Review: Lesson 1)*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency with sums to 5 and introduce drawing as a representation.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Sum:** The total when you add two numbers together
- **Altogether:** Another word for the total or sum

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Systematic exploration of sums to 5.**
Using the five-frame, demonstrate:

"Let's find all the ways to make 5."

Place 5 red, 0 blue: "5 and 0 makes 5."
Place 4 red, 1 blue: "4 and 1 makes 5."
Place 3 red, 2 blue: "3 and 2 makes 5."
Place 2 red, 3 blue: "2 and 3 makes 5."
Place 1 red, 4 blue: "1 and 4 makes 5."
Place 0 red, 5 blue: "0 and 5 makes 5."

"Wow — there are many ways to make 5!"

**Step 2: Introduce drawing as a tool.**
"Sometimes we don't have blocks. But we can draw! Watch."

Draw on paper: 3 circles, then 2 circles next to them.
"3 circles and 2 circles. Let me count all of them: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. The answer is 5."

"You can always draw circles or dots to help you add."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "What is 2 and 2?"
Draw: ○ ○ and ○ ○. Count: 1, 2, 3, 4. "2 and 2 makes 4."

**Example 2:** "What is 1 and 3?"
Use objects: 1 red block and 3 blue blocks. Combine. Count: 4. "1 and 3 makes 4."

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice addition with objects and drawings.

**Activity 1: Object problems (4 minutes)**
Present each problem. Student uses objects to solve and states the answer.

- 2 and 3 → ___ (5)
- 4 and 1 → ___ (5)
- 1 and 2 → ___ (3)
- 0 and 4 → ___ (4) — "Zero means no objects on this plate!"

*Facilitator prompt: For the zero problem, show an empty plate next to 4 objects. "Nothing plus 4 — we still have 4." Zero in addition often surprises children.*

**Activity 2: Draw and solve (3 minutes)**
Say each problem. Student draws circles and counts.

- "Draw 2 circles. Draw 1 more circle. How many altogether?" (3)
- "Draw 3 circles. Draw 2 more circles. How many altogether?" (5)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student's drawings are scattered, suggest: "Draw the first group here, and the second group right next to it. That makes it easy to count."*

**Activity 3: Ways to make 4 (3 minutes)**
"Can you find all the ways to make 4 using two groups of blocks?"

Help the student discover: 4+0, 3+1, 2+2, 1+3, 0+4.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student finds 2-3 pairs, that's great. Finding all pairs is a stretch goal.*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student solves addition problems using objects or drawings.

**★ On-level:**
1. 1 and 2 makes ___ (3)
2. 2 and 2 makes ___ (4)
3. 3 and 1 makes ___ (4)
4. 2 and 3 makes ___ (5)

**★★ Stretch:**
5. 0 and 5 makes ___ (5)
6. Draw a picture to show "1 and 4 makes 5."
7. Find two ways to make 3 using blocks. (1+2, 2+1, 3+0, 0+3)

**★★★ Challenge:**
8. "I put 2 blocks on one plate. My total is 5. How many are on the other plate?" (3)
9. "How many different ways can you make 5? Write or show them all."

### Interleaved Review
- "What number comes before 7?" (Answer: 6) *(Unit 1 review)*
- Show number cards 4 and 2. "Which is more?" (4) *(Unit 1 review)*

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess addition fluency within 5.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "3 and 2 makes ___?" (Answer: 5 — student uses objects or drawing)
2. "Show me a way to make 4 with two groups." (Any valid pair)
3. "4 and 0 makes ___?" (Answer: 4)

**Mastery = Correct on at least 2 of 3 items.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice with sums to 3 only (1+1, 1+2, 2+1, 0+3, 3+0) until confident.
- Use real objects exclusively (save drawing for later).
- Repeat this lesson next session.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (Addition — Sums to 10).

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Five-frame fill:** Roll a die (or pick a card 1-5). Place that many objects in the five-frame. "How many more do I need to fill the frame?" (This is addition to make 5.)
- **Domino addition:** If you have dominoes, the student counts dots on each half and finds the total.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Compositions of numbers are powerful.** Knowing all the ways to make 5 (and later, 10) is one of the most important number sense skills. It forms the foundation for mental math strategies. Revisit "ways to make 5" frequently.

**Zero is a valid addend.** "0 and 5 makes 5" is confusing for some students because "nothing" is being added. Use the empty plate to make zero concrete. "You started with nothing, and I gave you 5. Now you have 5."

**Drawing is a bridge.** Objects are concrete (Tier 1 of CRA). Drawing is representational (Tier 2). The transition from objects to drawings is an important step toward abstraction. Some students will prefer drawing immediately; others will cling to objects. Both are appropriate.
