# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 2, Lesson 8

## Topic
Story Problems (Word Problems)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Listen to a story problem and determine whether to add or subtract
- Use objects or drawings to model and solve story problems within 10
- Write a number sentence that matches a story problem
- Create their own story problems for a given number sentence

## Prerequisites
- Solves addition and subtraction within 10 (Lessons 1-7)
- Reads and writes number sentences with +, −, and = (Lessons 4, 7)

## Materials Needed
- 10 counting objects (small toy figures or animals make stories more engaging)
- Paper and crayons
- Pencil
- Number cards and sign cards (+, −, =)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Mixed review of addition and subtraction.

1. "6 + 3 = ___?" (Answer: 9) *(Lesson 3 review)*
2. "8 − 5 = ___?" (Answer: 3) *(Lesson 6 review)*
3. "Read this: 10 − 4 = 6." (Student reads aloud) *(Lesson 7 review)*
4. "Name a pair that makes 10." (Any valid pair) *(Lesson 3 review)*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students to solve math problems embedded in stories.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Story problem (word problem):** A math problem told as a story — you have to figure out what to do
- **Model:** To show the problem using objects or drawings

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Solve an addition story.**
"Listen to this story: Maria has 3 apples. Her dad gives her 4 more apples. How many apples does Maria have now?"

Think aloud: "Maria STARTS with 3. She GETS more — that means the number is getting bigger. I need to ADD."

Model with objects: place 3, then add 4 more. Count: 7.

"Maria has 7 apples. Let me write the number sentence: 3 + 4 = 7."

**Step 2: Solve a subtraction story.**
"There are 8 birds on a fence. 3 birds fly away. How many birds are on the fence now?"

Think aloud: "We START with 8. Some FLY AWAY — that means the number is getting smaller. I need to SUBTRACT."

Model: place 8, remove 3. Count: 5.

"5 birds are left. The number sentence: 8 − 3 = 5."

**Step 3: Teach the decision process.**
"How do I know if it's adding or taking away? Listen for CLUE WORDS."

**Adding clues:** "gets more," "joins," "in all," "altogether," "total," "how many now" (when something is added)
**Subtracting clues:** "flies away," "gives away," "eats," "loses," "how many left," "how many remain"

"The clue is in the story. If things come together, ADD. If things go away, SUBTRACT."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "Sam has 2 toy cars. He finds 5 more in a box. How many does he have now?"
Analysis: "finds more" → add. 2 + 5 = 7.

**Example 2:** "There are 9 cookies on a plate. Emma eats 4. How many are left?"
Analysis: "eats" → subtract. 9 − 4 = 5.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student solves story problems with facilitator guidance.

**Problem 1 (3 minutes):**
"There are 5 ducks on a pond. 3 more ducks come to the pond. How many ducks are on the pond now?"

Ask: "Should we add or subtract?" (Add — more ducks COME.)
Student models with objects and writes: 5 + 3 = 8.

**Problem 2 (3 minutes):**
"Jake has 7 stickers. He gives 2 stickers to his friend. How many stickers does Jake have now?"

Ask: "Add or subtract?" (Subtract — he GIVES AWAY.)
Student models and writes: 7 − 2 = 5.

**Problem 3 (2 minutes):**
"There are 4 red balloons and 4 blue balloons. How many balloons altogether?"

Student solves: 4 + 4 = 8.

**Problem 4: Student creates a story (2 minutes):**
Write: 6 − 1 = 5. Say: "Can you make up a story that matches this number sentence?"

Accept any sensible story (e.g., "I had 6 grapes. I ate 1. Now I have 5.").

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles to decide add vs. subtract, re-read the story and ask: "Are we getting MORE or are some going AWAY?" Emphasize the action verb.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student solves story problems and creates their own.

**★ On-level:**
1. "Lily has 3 books. She gets 5 more for her birthday. How many books does Lily have?" ___ (8) Number sentence: ___________
2. "There are 6 fish in a tank. 2 fish are moved to another tank. How many fish are left?" ___ (4) Number sentence: ___________
3. "There are 4 boys and 3 girls at the park. How many children are at the park?" ___ (7) Number sentence: ___________

**★★ Stretch:**
4. "I have 10 grapes. I eat some and now I have 6 left. How many did I eat?" ___ (4) Number sentence: ___________
5. Make up an addition story for 2 + 6 = 8. Tell it or draw it.

**★★★ Challenge:**
6. "There were some birds on a tree. 3 flew away and now there are 4 left. How many birds were on the tree at first?" ___ (7) *(Missing start number)*
7. Make up a subtraction story. Write the number sentence for it.

### Interleaved Review
- "Write the number sentence: five plus five equals ten." (5 + 5 = 10) *(Lesson 4 review)*
- "9 take away 9 = ___?" (0) *(Lesson 6 review)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess ability to solve story problems and choose the correct operation.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "There are 5 apples on a tree. 2 apples fall down. How many apples are still on the tree? Write the number sentence." (Answer: 5 − 2 = 3)
2. "Ben has 4 toy trucks. He gets 3 more. How many toy trucks does Ben have now?" (Answer: 7; 4 + 3 = 7)
3. "Make up your own story problem." (Accept any problem that makes sense and the student can solve)

**Mastery = Correct on at least 2 of 3 items, with correct operation choice.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Focus on the decision step: act out each story physically. "Are things coming together or going apart?"
- Use visual cues: draw an arrow pointing together for addition (→ ←) and an arrow pointing away for subtraction (← →).
- Practice with very simple stories using the student's own toys or snacks.

### If Mastery Is Met
The student is ready for the Unit 2 Assessment.

## Extension (Optional)
- **Story problem book:** Student draws and writes (or dictates) their own book of story problems, with answers on the back of each page.
- **Two-step stories (preview):** "I have 3 blocks. I get 4 more. Then I give 2 away. How many do I have?" (5.) This previews multi-step problems.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Story problems are where math meets real life.** Some students who are fluent with bare number sentences (3 + 2 = ?) struggle with story problems because they must interpret language, identify the operation, and then compute. These are separate skills, and each one can be a sticking point.

**The language barrier is real.** If a student understands addition and subtraction but cannot solve story problems, the issue is likely comprehension, not math. Strategies:
- Read the story twice.
- Have the student re-tell the story in their own words.
- Act it out with objects before attempting to write a number sentence.

**Student-created stories are powerful.** When a student can invent a story for "5 − 3 = 2," it shows deep understanding of the operation's meaning. Make time for this activity even if it seems simple.

**"How many more?" problems are harder.** Questions like "How many more does Jake have than Emma?" involve comparison subtraction, which is more abstract than "take away." These are addressed in Grade 1. For now, stick with action-based stories (joining and removing).
