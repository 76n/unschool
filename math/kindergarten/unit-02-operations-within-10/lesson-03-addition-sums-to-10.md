# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 2, Lesson 3

## Topic
Addition with Objects — Sums to 10

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve addition problems with sums from 6 to 10 using objects or drawings
- Use the ten-frame to organize and count combined groups
- Begin to see relationships between numbers and 10 (e.g., 6 + 4 fills the frame)

## Prerequisites
- Solves addition problems with sums to 5 (Lesson 2)
- Can use a ten-frame (Unit 1, Lesson 5)

## Materials Needed
- 10+ counting objects in two colors
- Ten-frame (2x5 grid)
- Paper and crayons
- Number cards 0-10

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review addition to 5 and Unit 1 skills.

1. "4 and 1 makes ___?" (Answer: 5) *(Lesson 2 review)*
2. "2 and 2 makes ___?" (Answer: 4) *(Lesson 2 review)*
3. "Name a way to make 5 with two groups." (Accept any valid pair) *(Lesson 2 review)*
4. Show a ten-frame with 7 filled. "How many?" (Answer: 7) *(Unit 1 review)*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Extend addition to larger sums (6-10) using the ten-frame.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Ten-frame:** Our 10-space grid that helps us organize counting
- **Fill the frame:** When all 10 spaces are used — the total is 10

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Model with the ten-frame.**
"We've been adding small groups. Now let's add bigger groups using our ten-frame."

Place 4 red objects on the ten-frame (filling the top row minus one).
"I have 4. Now I'm going to add 3 more." Place 3 blue objects.
"Let's count everything: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. Four and three makes 7."

Point to the ten-frame: "See how 7 looks? The top row is full (5) and 2 are on the bottom. That's 5 and 2."

**Step 2: Build toward 10.**
"Let's try one that fills the whole frame."

Place 6 red objects on the ten-frame. "I have 6. How many empty spaces?" (4.)
"If I add 4 blue blocks..." Place them. "The frame is FULL! 6 and 4 makes 10."

"Whenever two numbers fill the whole frame, they make 10. Let's remember that!"

**Step 3: Practice several problems.**
- 5 and 3: Place on ten-frame, count → 8
- 3 and 4: Place on ten-frame, count → 7
- 7 and 3: Place on ten-frame → 10 (full frame!)
- 5 and 5: Place on ten-frame → 10 (full frame!)

Each time, describe what the ten-frame looks like: "8 is 5 on top and 3 on the bottom."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 4 + 5. Place 4 red, then 5 blue on the ten-frame. Count: 9. "4 and 5 makes 9. The frame has 1 empty space."

**Example 2:** 6 + 2. Place 6 red, then 2 blue. Count: 8. "6 and 2 makes 8."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student solves addition problems with sums 6-10.

**Activity 1: Ten-frame addition (5 minutes)**
Present each problem. Student places objects on the ten-frame and counts.

- 3 and 5 → ___ (8)
- 4 and 4 → ___ (8)
- 6 and 3 → ___ (9)
- 5 and 5 → ___ (10)
- 7 and 2 → ___ (9)

*Facilitator prompt: After each problem, ask: "How many empty spaces are left on the ten-frame?" This builds "distance from 10" thinking.*

**Activity 2: Make 10 hunt (5 minutes)**
"Let's find all the pairs that make 10!"

Student tries combinations: 10+0, 9+1, 8+2, 7+3, 6+4, 5+5...

For each pair, place on the ten-frame to verify the frame is full.

*Facilitator prompt: Help the student organize by starting from 0+10 and working through. Keep a list as you go. This is a key number sense activity.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student solves addition problems with sums to 10.

**★ On-level:**
1. 3 and 4 makes ___ (7)
2. 5 and 2 makes ___ (7)
3. 4 and 6 makes ___ (10)
4. 2 and 5 makes ___ (7)

**★★ Stretch:**
5. 3 and 6 makes ___ (9)
6. Draw a ten-frame. Show 5 + 4. How many? ___ (9)
7. "What number plus 3 makes 10?" ___ (7)

**★★★ Challenge:**
8. "Name all the pairs that make 8." (0+8, 1+7, 2+6, 3+5, 4+4, 5+3, 6+2, 7+1, 8+0)
9. "I have 6 blocks. I add some more and now I have 9. How many did I add?" (3)

### Interleaved Review
- "1 and 4 makes ___?" (5) *(Lesson 2 review)*
- "Write the number 10." *(Unit 1 review)*
- Place 5 objects and 3 objects. "Which has fewer?" (3) *(Unit 1 review)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess addition with sums to 10.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Use objects: "5 and 3 makes ___?" (Answer: 8)
2. Use the ten-frame: "6 and 4 makes ___?" (Answer: 10)
3. "Name a pair of numbers that makes 10." (Any valid pair)

**Mastery = Correct on at least 2 of 3 items.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice more with the ten-frame. The visual structure helps students track larger quantities.
- If the student is accurate with sums to 5 but struggles with 6-10, the issue is likely counting (losing track of larger groups). Use the ten-frame consistently and count slowly.
- Add one extra practice session before Lesson 4.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (The + and = Signs).

## Extension (Optional)
- **Pairs that make 10 poster:** Create a reference poster with all pairs that make 10. Hang it up and quiz the student throughout the week.
- **Double ten-frame:** For sums that go past 10 (preview only) — "What's 7 + 5?" Use two ten-frames. Fill one completely and start the second. (7 + 5 = 12: one full frame + 2 more.)

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The ten-frame transforms addition.** It gives students a visual anchor for quantities 6-10 that they don't get from a random pile of objects. Use it for every problem in this lesson and continue using it in future lessons.

**"Make 10" is one of the most important number relationships.** Pairs that make 10 (1+9, 2+8, 3+7, 4+6, 5+5) will be used constantly in mental math through elementary school. Start building familiarity now, but don't demand memorization — that comes with repeated exposure over months.

**Count-all is still the expected strategy.** The student should count every object from 1 to find the total. "Counting on" (starting from the larger number) is a more advanced strategy that typically develops in late kindergarten or first grade. Don't teach it now — it will emerge naturally as counting becomes more fluent.
