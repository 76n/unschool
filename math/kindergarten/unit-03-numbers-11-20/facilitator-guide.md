# Facilitator Guide — Kindergarten Math, Unit 3: Numbers 11-20

**Grade:** Kindergarten
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 3 — Numbers 11-20 (Place Value Introduction)
**Recommended Duration:** 8 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Fully facilitated (adult present and actively guiding throughout)

---

## Unit Overview

In this unit, students extend their counting beyond 10 and begin to understand the most important structural idea in our number system: **place value**. They learn that teen numbers are made of a group of ten and some extra ones. This is the conceptual foundation for all multi-digit arithmetic in later grades.

By the end of this unit, your student will be able to:

- Count forward from 1 to 20 fluently
- Recognize and identify quantities from 11 to 20
- Understand that the numbers 11-19 are composed of ten ones and some more ones (e.g., 14 = 10 + 4)
- Build teen numbers using a group of 10 and additional ones
- Write the numerals 11 through 20
- Order and sequence numbers from 11 to 20
- Compare quantities of 11-20 using "more" and "fewer"
- Skip count by 10s to 100

**Why this matters:** Place value is the backbone of our entire number system. Understanding that "14" means one group of ten and four ones — not just a sequence of "1" and "4" — is what separates genuine number sense from rote memorization. Students who build this understanding now will find multi-digit addition, subtraction, and all later arithmetic dramatically easier.

---

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm that the student has mastered Unit 1 and Unit 2 skills:

- [ ] Counts from 1 to 10 fluently and accurately
- [ ] Counts objects to 10 using one-to-one correspondence
- [ ] Recognizes and names numerals 0-10
- [ ] Compares groups using "more," "fewer," and "the same"
- [ ] Adds and subtracts within 10 using objects (Unit 2)
- [ ] Understands the + , −, and = signs (Unit 2)

If the student scored below 85% on the Unit 2 assessment, address gaps before starting this unit.

## Materials Needed for This Unit

- **Counting objects:** 20+ small objects (e.g., blocks, buttons, dried beans, small toys)
- **Two ten-frames** (2x5 grids — the same style used in Units 1-2; you will need two for this unit)
- **Counters or tokens:** 20 tokens that fit in ten-frame cells
- **Rubber bands or small bags** (to bundle groups of 10)
- **Number cards 0-20** (make new cards for 11-20 or write on index cards)
- **A number line 1-20** (tape one to the table or print one out)
- **Pencil, crayons, and paper**
- **A hundred chart** (for Lesson 8 — print or draw a 10x10 grid with numbers 1-100)

## Pacing Guidance

| Lesson | Topic | Sessions |
|--------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Counting 11 to 15 | 1 session |
| 2 | Counting 16 to 20 | 1 session |
| 3 | Ten and Some More (Place Value Concept) | 1-2 sessions |
| 4 | Making Teen Numbers with Objects | 1 session |
| 5 | Writing Numerals 11-20 | 1-2 sessions |
| 6 | Ordering Numbers 11 to 20 | 1 session |
| 7 | Comparing Teen Numbers | 1 session |
| 8 | Counting by Tens to 100 | 1 session |
| -- | Unit Assessment | Part of Lesson 8 or a separate short session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lessons 1-2 (counting to 20) form a natural pair. If the student already counts to 20 orally, these can be combined into one session focused on recognizing quantities.
- **Lesson 3 is the most important lesson in this unit.** The "ten and some more" concept is new and requires time. Do not rush it. If the student needs a second session, take it.
- Lesson 5 (writing numerals) may require extra practice for students still developing fine motor skills. The teen numbers require writing two digits, which is new. Allow a second session if needed.
- Lesson 8 (skip counting by 10s) is a fun preview. Keep it light and musical — this is exposure, not mastery.

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Student counts "...8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 30, 40" | Confusing teen numbers with decade names | This is very common. Slow down between 12 and 13. Use objects so the student can see each number is "one more." Practice the sequence 10-20 daily with a song or chant. |
| Student says "fourteen" but writes "41" | Reversal of digits — hears "four" first in "fourteen" | Explain: "Fourteen is tricky! The word says 'four' first, but we write the 1 first because there is one group of ten." Use a ten-frame to show 10+4. Practice this specific reversal with 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19. |
| Student thinks 12 = "one and two" (1 + 2 = 3) | Reading digits separately instead of as a two-digit number | Emphasize that 12 is TWELVE — it means one group of TEN and TWO more. Always connect to objects on a ten-frame. |
| Student cannot see "ten-ness" in a group of 10 | Has not yet internalized 10 as a unit | Spend more time with the full ten-frame. Practice filling the ten-frame and saying "TEN!" with emphasis. Bundle 10 objects with a rubber band and treat the bundle as one thing. |
| Student counts each object in a group of 10 every time | Does not trust that the group of 10 contains 10 without recounting | This is normal early on. Let the student recount if needed. Over time, use a full ten-frame as a visual anchor: "We know this is 10 because every box is full." |
| Student thinks 20 comes after 12 (or skips to 20) | Has not learned the full sequence 13-19 | The teens are irregular in English. Practice 11-20 as a sequence daily. Use objects to count one-by-one through the teens. |

## Conversation Starters

Use these throughout the day to reinforce teen number thinking:

- "We have 10 grapes in this bowl. I added 3 more. How many grapes do we have?" (13)
- "Count the stairs as we walk up. Did we pass 10? How many extra after 10?"
- "Can you find something in this room where there are more than 10 but fewer than 20?"
- "If I have a full egg carton (10 eggs) and 2 extra eggs, how many eggs is that?"
- "Let's count by tens: 10, 20, 30... Can you keep going?"
- "Which is more — 14 crayons or 17 crayons? How do you know?"

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if, after completing this unit:

- The student cannot count to 20 in sequence after repeated daily practice
- The student shows no understanding that teen numbers involve a group of 10 (cannot model 14 as 10 + 4 with objects even with help)
- The student consistently reverses digits (writes 41 for 14) despite targeted practice over multiple sessions
- The student becomes very anxious or avoidant when working with numbers beyond 10

## What Comes Next

After this unit, students move to **Unit 4: Geometry — Identifying and Describing Shapes**, where they explore 2D and 3D shapes. The number sense built here will continue to be reinforced through spaced review in all future units. In Grade 1, students will extend place value to understand tens and ones through 99.
