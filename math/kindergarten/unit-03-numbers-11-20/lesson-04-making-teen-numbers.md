# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 3, Lesson 4

## Topic
Making Teen Numbers with Objects

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Build any teen number (11-19) using a group of 10 objects and additional ones
- Decompose a teen number into 10 and some ones (e.g., given 16 objects, separate into a group of 10 and a group of 6)
- State the equation form: "10 + ___ = ___" for teen numbers

## Prerequisites
- Understands teen numbers as "ten and some more" (Unit 3, Lesson 3)
- Uses ten-frames to represent quantities (Unit 1; Unit 3, Lesson 3)
- Addition within 10 (Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- 20 counting objects (blocks, buttons, beans)
- Two ten-frames and counters
- Rubber bands or small bags (for bundling groups of 10)
- Number cards 11-19
- Paper and crayons

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review the "ten and some more" concept from Lesson 3.

1. Show a full ten-frame + 2 extra counters. "How many is this?" (Answer: 12) "Twelve is ten and ___ more?" (Answer: 2) *(Lesson 3 review)*
2. "Count from 11 to 20." (Student counts the sequence) *(Lessons 1-2 review)*
3. "What is 7 + 3?" (Answer: 10) *(Unit 2 review)*

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice building teen numbers from a group of 10 and ones, and decomposing teen numbers back into 10 and ones.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Build:** Make a number by putting objects together
- **Break apart (decompose):** Split a number into groups
- **Ones:** Single objects (not in a group of ten)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Build teen numbers — "Give me a number" game.**
"I'm going to say a teen number, and you build it! First, always start by making your group of ten."

"Build 13!" Student takes 10 objects and bundles them (or fills a ten-frame). Then adds 3 more. "You made a group of ten and three ones. TEN plus THREE equals THIRTEEN. 10 + 3 = 13."

"Build 18!" Student starts with the group of 10 and adds 8 more. "Ten plus eight equals eighteen. 10 + 8 = 18."

**Step 2: Break apart teen numbers.**
Place 15 objects in a scattered pile. "There are 15 objects here. Can you break them into a group of ten and some extras?"

Student counts out 10 and sets them aside (or fills a ten-frame). The leftover objects are the ones.

"How many in your big group?" (10) "How many left over?" (5) "So 15 is 10 + 5!"

Repeat with 11 objects: "Break these into 10 and extras." (10 and 1.) "Eleven is 10 + 1."

**Step 3: Introduce "10 +" language.**
"Every teen number can be written as 10 + something."
- Hold up the number card 14. "Fourteen is 10 + 4."
- Hold up 17. "Seventeen is 10 + 7."
- Hold up 11. "Eleven is 10 + 1."

"The ones part — the extra objects — that tells us which teen number it is."

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Build 16.**
- Start with a group of 10 (fill ten-frame or bundle with rubber band).
- Add 6 more objects.
- "Ten plus six equals sixteen. 10 + 6 = 16."

**Example B: Break apart 19.**
- Start with 19 objects in a pile.
- Count out 10 and set them aside.
- Count the remaining: 9.
- "Nineteen is ten and nine. 10 + 9 = 19."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student builds and breaks apart teen numbers with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Build it (5 minutes)**

Facilitator says a number; student builds it with objects.

- "Build 12." (Student makes a group of 10 + 2 ones.) *Ask: "What is 10 + 2?" (12)*
- "Build 15." (Group of 10 + 5 ones.) *Ask: "What is 10 + 5?" (15)*
- "Build 19." (Group of 10 + 9 ones.) *Ask: "What is 10 + 9?" (19)*

*Facilitator prompt: If the student counts out objects one at a time to the target number instead of starting with 10, say: "That works! But let's try it another way — first make your group of ten, then add more."*

**Activity 2: Break it apart (5 minutes)**

Facilitator places a group of objects; student separates into 10 and extras.

- Place 14 objects. "Break these into a group of ten and some ones." (10 and 4) *Ask: "So 14 equals 10 + what?" (4)*
- Place 17 objects. "Break them apart." (10 and 7) *Ask: "17 = 10 + ___?" (7)*
- Place 11 objects. "Break them apart." (10 and 1) *Ask: "11 = 10 + ___?" (1)*

*Facilitator prompt: If the student has trouble counting exactly 10, suggest using the ten-frame: "Fill the ten-frame first — that's your group of ten. Whatever doesn't fit is the ones."*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-Level
1. Build 13 with objects. "13 = 10 + ___." (Answer: 3)
2. Build 16 with objects. "16 = 10 + ___." (Answer: 6)
3. Facilitator places 18 objects. Student breaks apart. "18 = 10 + ___." (Answer: 8)

### Stretch
4. "10 + 4 = ___?" (Answer: 14)
5. "___ = 10 + 7?" (Answer: 17)

### Challenge
6. "I have 10 + 5. If I add one more, what number do I have now?" (Answer: 16; because 10 + 5 = 15, and 15 + 1 = 16)
7. "Build 19. Now take away 9. What do you have left?" (Answer: 10)

### Interleaved Review
*(From prior units/lessons)*
- "How many is this?" (Show 15 objects.) (Answer: 15) *(Lesson 1 review)*
- "What is 9 - 4?" (Answer: 5) *(Unit 2 review)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. "Build 14 with a group of ten and some ones. How many ones?" (Answer: student builds 10 + 4; says "4")
2. Facilitator places 16 objects. "Break this into ten and extras. How many extras?" (Answer: 6)

**Scoring:**
- 2/2 correct = Mastery
- 1/2 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0/2 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the ten-frame approach from Lesson 3. Practice filling one ten-frame completely and adding extras to the second.
- Use the rubber band bundle so the "group of ten" is physically distinct from the ones.
- Practice with just a few teen numbers (11, 12, 13) before expanding to all teen numbers.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 5 (Writing Numbers 11-20). Preview: "You can build these numbers with objects. Next time, you will learn how to write them with a pencil!"

## Extension (Optional)
- **Teen number cards:** Student draws ten-frame pictures on index cards for each teen number (11-19). On one side, draw the full ten-frame + extras. On the other side, write "10 + ___ = ___."
- **Secret number:** "I have 10 and some more. My number is between 14 and 16. What is it?" (15)

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Not starting with 10 when building:** Students may count out objects one at a time (e.g., counting 1-16 to build 16). This gives the right answer but misses the point of the lesson. Redirect: "First make your ten, then add the ones."
- **Saying "10 + 4 = 4" (forgetting the ten):** Emphasize: "The ten doesn't disappear! You still have ten AND four. That is fourteen." Point to the group of 10.
- **Getting confused about which number the extras represent:** If the student says "10 + 6 = 15," count together: "10... 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16. We got to 16, not 15. Let's try again."

### Pacing Advice
- Building and decomposing are two sides of the same skill. If time is short, prioritize building (Activity 1) over decomposing (Activity 2). The student will get more practice decomposing in later lessons.
- Let the student use whichever method they prefer (ten-frame or rubber band bundle). Both are valid representations of the same concept.
