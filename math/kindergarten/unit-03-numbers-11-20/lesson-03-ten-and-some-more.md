# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 3, Lesson 3

## Topic
Ten and Some More (Introduction to Place Value)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Understand that each teen number (11-19) is made of one group of 10 and some extra ones
- Show a teen number on a ten-frame as "10 and some more" (e.g., 14 = a full ten-frame + 4 more)
- Say "___ is ten and ___ more" for numbers 11-19

## Prerequisites
- Counts from 1 to 20 (Unit 3, Lessons 1-2)
- Fills a ten-frame to show quantities to 10 (Unit 1)
- Addition within 10 (Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- 20 counters (tokens, buttons, or small blocks)
- Two ten-frames (2x5 grids) — this is the first lesson requiring two
- A rubber band or small bag (to bundle 10 objects)
- Number cards 10-19
- Paper and crayons

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review counting to 20 and the ten-frame.

1. "Count from 1 to 20 with me!" Count together using a chant or song. *(Lessons 1-2 review)*
2. "Fill the ten-frame with counters. How many does it hold?" (Answer: 10) *(Unit 1 review)*
3. "What is 6 + 4?" Student may use the ten-frame. (Answer: 10) *(Unit 2 review)*

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the foundational idea that teen numbers are "ten and some more."

### Key Vocabulary
- **Ten-frame:** A grid with 10 boxes arranged in 2 rows of 5
- **Teen number:** A number from 11 to 19
- **Group of ten:** Ten objects bundled or grouped together

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Fill the ten-frame and go beyond.**
"Let's fill our ten-frame." (Student fills all 10 spaces.) "How many is that?" (10)

"Now I want to show ELEVEN. I need one more counter, but my ten-frame is FULL! What should I do?"

(Let the student think. If they don't suggest it, say:) "I need ANOTHER ten-frame! Watch." Place 1 counter on the second ten-frame. "Now I have a FULL ten-frame — that's 10 — and 1 more. TEN and ONE more is ELEVEN."

**Step 2: Build more teen numbers together.**
"Let's show 13." Keep the full ten-frame. Place 3 counters on the second ten-frame. "I have 10" (point to full frame) "and 3 more" (point to second frame). "Ten and three more is THIRTEEN."

"Let's show 15." Keep the full ten-frame. Place 5 counters on the second ten-frame. "I have 10 and 5 more. Ten and five more is FIFTEEN."

**Step 3: Emphasize the pattern.**
"Did you notice something? Every teen number starts with TEN!" Write or say:

- 11 = ten and 1 more
- 12 = ten and 2 more
- 13 = ten and 3 more
- 14 = ten and 4 more
- 15 = ten and 5 more

"The first ten-frame is ALWAYS full. The extra ones go on the second ten-frame. The extra ones tell us WHICH teen number it is."

**Step 4: Bundle the ten.**
Take 10 objects and wrap a rubber band around them (or put them in a small bag). "This is ONE group of ten. It has ten objects inside, but we can think of it as ONE group."

Hold up the bundle and 6 loose objects. "One group of ten and six more. That makes... SIXTEEN."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Show 12 on two ten-frames.
- Fill the first ten-frame completely (10 counters).
- Place 2 counters on the second ten-frame.
- "Twelve is ten and two more. 10 + 2 = 12."

**Example B:** Show 17 on two ten-frames.
- Fill the first ten-frame completely (10 counters).
- Place 7 counters on the second ten-frame.
- "Seventeen is ten and seven more. 10 + 7 = 17."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student builds teen numbers on ten-frames and describes them as "ten and some more."

**Activity 1: Build and describe (5 minutes)**

"Show me 14 on the ten-frames." (Student fills the first ten-frame, places 4 on the second.)
*Ask: "How many in the first frame?" (10) "How many in the second frame?" (4) "So 14 is ten and ___ more?" (4)*

"Show me 11 on the ten-frames." (Full ten-frame + 1 more.)
*Ask: "Eleven is ten and how many more?" (1)*

"Show me 16 on the ten-frames." (Full ten-frame + 6 more.)
*Ask: "Sixteen is ten and how many more?" (6)*

*Facilitator prompt: If the student does not fill the first ten-frame completely before starting the second, redirect: "We always fill the first ten-frame ALL the way. Then the extras go on the second one."*

**Activity 2: Bundle and loose (5 minutes)**

Give the student a bundle of 10 (rubber-banded) and loose objects.

"Hold up the bundle. That's your TEN. Now take 8 loose ones. What number did you make?" (18)
"Put the bundle down with 3 loose ones. What number?" (13)
"Bundle and 9 loose ones. What number?" (19)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student tries to count the objects in the bundle each time, that's okay at first. Over time, encourage: "We know the bundle has 10. We don't need to recount it. Just count the extras and add them to 10."*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-Level
1. Show 15 on the ten-frames. "Fifteen is ten and ___ more." (Answer: 5)
2. Show 12 on the ten-frames. "Twelve is ten and ___ more." (Answer: 2)
3. Show 18 on the ten-frames. "Eighteen is ten and ___ more." (Answer: 8)

### Stretch
4. "I have ten and six more. What number is that?" (Answer: 16)
5. "I have ten and nine more. What number is that?" (Answer: 19)

### Challenge
6. "How many more do I need to add to 10 to make 14?" (Answer: 4)
7. "If I have 10 and some more and the total is 17, how many extras do I have?" (Answer: 7)

### Interleaved Review
*(From prior units/lessons)*
- "Count backward from 20 to 15." (Answer: 20, 19, 18, 17, 16, 15) *(Lessons 1-2 review)*
- "What is 8 - 3?" (Answer: 5) *(Unit 2 review)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. "Show me 13 on the ten-frames." Then ask: "Thirteen is ten and how many more?" (Answer: student shows full ten-frame + 3; says "3")
2. "I have ten and five more. What number did I make?" (Answer: 15)

**Scoring:**
- 2/2 correct = Mastery
- 1/2 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0/2 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Spend additional time with the ten-frames. Practice filling the first frame and then adding extras for several different teen numbers.
- Use the rubber band bundle. Have the student hold the bundle in one hand and loose objects in the other: "Your left hand has ten. Your right hand has ___. Together that makes ___."
- Repeat this lesson before moving to Lesson 4. This is the most important concept in the unit.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Making Teen Numbers). Preview: "Now you know that teen numbers are ten and some more. Next time you will build teen numbers in lots of different ways!"

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Draw it:** Student draws a full ten-frame and extra dots for 11, 14, and 19. Label each: "10 + ___ = ___."
- **Body counting:** "Show me 10 fingers. Now how can you show 12?" (Student shows 10 fingers, then asks a friend or parent to hold up 2 more.) "Ten fingers and two more is twelve!"

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Not filling the first ten-frame before starting the second:** Students may spread counters across both frames randomly. Always redirect: "Fill the first frame completely. THEN put extras on the second."
- **Not seeing 10 as a unit:** This is the hardest part. The student may want to count all objects from 1 every time. That is fine in the beginning. The goal is for them to gradually trust that the full ten-frame holds 10 without recounting.
- **Confusing "ten and three more" with "three":** Some students focus only on the extras and forget the ten. Point to the full ten-frame each time: "Don't forget about these ten! They count too."

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson may need two sessions. The "ten and some more" concept is the conceptual core of the unit. It is better to spend extra time here than to rush and build on a shaky foundation.
- If the student grasps it quickly, spend extra time on the Challenge problems and Extension. These deepen understanding without rushing ahead.
