# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 4, Lesson 4

## Topic
Positional Words (Above, Below, Beside, In Front, Behind)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Use positional words accurately: above, below, beside, next to, in front of, behind, between
- Follow directions that use positional language (e.g., "Put the block above the book")
- Describe the position of objects relative to other objects using complete sentences
- Give directions to another person using positional words

## Prerequisites
- Identifies circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles by name (Unit 4, Lessons 1-2)
- Describes shapes using sides and corners (Unit 4, Lesson 3)
- Follows multi-step oral directions (general readiness)

## Materials Needed
- A small stuffed animal or toy figure
- A box or container (large enough for the toy to go inside, on top of, and behind)
- A book
- A chair
- 3-5 small objects of different types (block, ball, crayon, cup, toy car)
- Paper and crayons for drawing activity
- Shape cutouts from previous lessons

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review shape names and properties before introducing a new concept within geometry.

1. Hold up shape cutouts one at a time in random order (circle, square, triangle, rectangle). "Name each shape!" *(Lessons 1-3 review)*
2. "I have 4 sides and 4 corners, but my sides are NOT all the same length. What shape am I?" (Answer: rectangle) *(Lesson 3 review)*
3. "What is 10 - 3?" Student may use fingers or objects. (Answer: 7) *(Unit 2 review)*

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce positional vocabulary through concrete physical demonstrations, building spatial reasoning skills that connect to geometry and measurement.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Above:** Higher than something else; over it — "The light is ABOVE your head"
- **Below:** Lower than something else; under it — "Your feet are BELOW your knees"
- **Beside / Next to:** Right next to something, on the left side or right side — "The chair is BESIDE the table"
- **In front of:** Facing the front of something; closer to you — "The toy is IN FRONT OF the box"
- **Behind:** On the back side; farther from you — "The toy is BEHIND the box"
- **Between:** In the middle of two things — "The crayon is BETWEEN the block and the ball"
- **Position:** Where something is located compared to other things

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce "above" and "below."**
Place a book flat on the table. Hold the toy figure above the book (in the air, not touching).

"Where is the toy? The toy is ABOVE the book. Say it with me: ABOVE." The student repeats.

Now place the toy under the table (or under the book if using a raised surface). "Now where is the toy? The toy is BELOW the book. Say it with me: BELOW."

"ABOVE means up higher. BELOW means down lower. The light is above us. The floor is below us." Point up for above, point down for below.

Move the toy above the book again. "Is the toy above or below the book?" (Above.) Move it below. "Now?" (Below.) Repeat 2-3 times, varying speed so it becomes a playful game.

**Step 2: Introduce "beside" and "next to."**
Place the toy right next to the book, on its left side.

"Now the toy is BESIDE the book. It is right next to it — not above, not below, but on the SIDE. Say it with me: BESIDE."

Move the toy to the right side of the book. "The toy is still beside the book — just on the other side. BESIDE means on the left OR the right."

"NEXT TO means the same thing as BESIDE. The toy is next to the book. The book is next to the toy. They are side by side."

**Step 3: Introduce "in front of" and "behind."**
Stand the box upright on the table. Place the toy so it faces you, between you and the box.

"The toy is IN FRONT OF the box. I can see the toy because it is facing me — it is in the front." Say with me: IN FRONT OF.

Now move the toy to the other side of the box, so the box blocks the toy from your view. "Now the toy is BEHIND the box. I cannot see it easily because it is hiding behind the box." Say with me: BEHIND.

"IN FRONT OF means on the side that faces you. BEHIND means on the other side — hidden in the back."

Move the toy back and forth. "Where is the toy now? In front of or behind?" Let the student answer each time.

**Step 4: Introduce "between."**
Place three objects in a row on the table: a block, a crayon, and a ball.

"Look at the crayon. It is in the middle. The block is on one side and the ball is on the other side. The crayon is BETWEEN the block and the ball. Say it with me: BETWEEN."

"Between means in the MIDDLE of two things — one thing on each side."

Rearrange: put the ball between the block and the crayon. "Now which object is BETWEEN the other two?" (The ball.)

**Step 5: Quick full review.**
Use the toy and the box. Rapidly place the toy in different positions:
- Above the box. "Where is it?" (Above!)
- Below the box. "Where?" (Below!)
- Beside the box. "Where?" (Beside! / Next to!)
- In front of the box. "Where?" (In front of!)
- Behind the box. "Where?" (Behind!)
- Between the box and a block. "Where?" (Between!)

"Great! You know ALL the position words: above, below, beside, next to, in front of, behind, and between!"

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Place a crayon on top of a book (resting on it, higher). "The crayon is ABOVE the book — it is up higher. The book is BELOW the crayon." Ask the student: "If I put the crayon under the book, where would it be?" (Below the book.)

**Example B:** Line up three shape cutouts in a row: circle, square, triangle. "The square is BETWEEN the circle and the triangle. The circle is BESIDE the square. The triangle is BESIDE the square on the other side." Ask: "What is between the circle and the triangle?" (The square.)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices using and responding to positional language through hands-on activities with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Following directions (5 minutes)**

Give the student the toy figure and several objects (book, box, block, ball). Give positional directions and the student places the toy accordingly.

- "Put the toy ABOVE the book." (Student holds or places the toy higher than the book.)
- "Put the toy BEHIND the box." (Student places the toy on the far side of the box.)
- "Put the toy BETWEEN the block and the ball." (Student places the toy in the middle.)
- "Put the toy BESIDE the crayon." (Student places the toy next to the crayon.)
- "Put the toy BELOW the table." (Student places the toy under the table.)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student confuses "above" and "below," use hand gestures — point up for above, point down for below. Say: "Above is UP. Below is DOWN." Physical gestures help anchor the vocabulary.*

*Facilitator prompt: If the student confuses "in front of" and "behind," use yourself as a reference: "Stand in front of the chair — I should be able to see your face. Now go behind the chair — I see the back of your head."*

**Activity 2: Describing positions (5 minutes)**

Arrange several objects on the table in a deliberate layout. Ask the student to describe where things are.

Place a block on the table, a ball to the right of it, and a crayon between them. Place a toy above the block (on a higher surface or held up).

- "Where is the ball?" (Beside the block / next to the block.)
- "Where is the crayon?" (Between the block and the ball.)
- "Where is the toy?" (Above the block.)
- "Where is the block compared to the ball?" (Beside the ball / next to the ball.)

"Now YOU arrange the objects however you like, and tell ME where everything is using our position words."

*Facilitator prompt: Encourage full sentences: "The ball is beside the block" rather than just "beside." Model the sentence first if needed: "Say: The ball is beside the block."*

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
1. The facilitator places a toy above a book. "Is the toy above or below the book?" (Answer: above)
2. The facilitator places a block behind a box. "Is the block in front of or behind the box?" (Answer: behind)
3. "Put the crayon beside the ball." (Answer: student places the crayon next to the ball)
4. The facilitator places a toy below the table. "Where is the toy?" (Answer: below the table)
5. "Point to something that is above you right now." (Answer: student points to the ceiling, a light, a shelf, etc.)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
1. The facilitator arranges: block, crayon, ball in a row. "What is between the block and the ball?" (Answer: the crayon)
2. "Put the toy in front of the box. Now move it behind the box. What changed?" (Answer: the toy went from the front to the back — from in front of to behind)
3. "Where is the floor compared to the ceiling?" (Answer: below the ceiling / the floor is below the ceiling)
4. The facilitator places objects and asks: "Tell me where the ball is. Use TWO position words." (Answer: e.g., "The ball is beside the block and below the shelf" — any two correct positional descriptions)
5. "Draw a circle. Draw a square ABOVE the circle. Draw a triangle BELOW the circle." (Answer: student draws three shapes in the correct vertical arrangement)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
1. "Give me directions to place three objects using position words. Tell me EXACTLY where to put each one." (Answer: student gives clear positional directions, e.g., "Put the block on the table. Put the ball beside the block. Put the crayon between them.")
2. "The toy is behind the box. If I walk around to the other side, is the toy still behind the box?" (Answer: It depends on where I am standing — the toy is now in front of the box from the new viewpoint. This introduces the idea that position can be relative to the observer.)
3. "Draw a picture: a rectangle house in the middle of the paper. Draw a circle sun ABOVE the house. Draw a triangle tree BESIDE the house. Draw a square window BETWEEN the door and the edge of the house." (Answer: student draws a scene using shapes in the correct positions)
4. "Name three things in this room that are BELOW the table." (Answer: any three correct items — floor, shoes, chair legs, etc.)
5. The facilitator arranges 5 objects in a row. "Tell me the position of the MIDDLE object using the word 'between.'" (Answer: "The [middle object] is between the [second object] and the [fourth object].")

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. The facilitator places a toy above a box. "Where is the toy? Use a position word." (Answer: above the box)
2. The facilitator places a crayon between a block and a ball. "Where is the crayon?" (Answer: between the block and the ball)
3. "Put the toy behind the book." (Answer: student correctly places the toy behind the book)

## Teacher Notes

### Common Errors
- **Confusing "above" and "below":** This is the most common mix-up. Use consistent hand gestures every time — point up for above, point down for below. Some students benefit from a visual anchor taped to the wall: an arrow pointing up labeled "above" and an arrow pointing down labeled "below."
- **Confusing "in front of" and "behind":** These terms are relative to the observer. For kindergarteners, keep it simple: "in front of" means where you can see the front, "behind" means it is hidden in back. If the student walks around and sees the toy from the other side, acknowledge that position can change depending on where you stand, but do not belabor the point.
- **Using "beside" only for one side:** Some students think "beside" means only on the right or only on the left. Show that beside means either side: "The toy is beside the box — on this side. Now it is beside the box — on THAT side. Both are beside!"
- **Difficulty with "between":** Students may not understand that "between" requires exactly two reference objects. Always name both: "between the block AND the ball." If only one reference is given, the concept breaks down.

### Tips
- Use the CRA progression: start with the student physically moving their own body and real objects (concrete), then use pictures and drawings (representational), then use verbal descriptions without visuals (abstract). The kinesthetic experience is especially powerful for spatial vocabulary.
- Play "Simon Says" with positional words: "Simon says put your hands above your head!" "Simon says put your hands below your knees!" "Simon says stand beside the chair!" This energizes the lesson and builds physical memory of each word.
- Positional language connects to reading and writing readiness. When students learn "left to right" and "top to bottom" in literacy, the spatial vocabulary from this lesson provides a foundation.
- For English Language Learners, pair each positional word with a consistent gesture or picture card. Spatial vocabulary is challenging across languages because the terms do not always translate directly.
- Revisit positional words regularly in future lessons: "Put the tens on the LEFT and the ones on the RIGHT" or "Write the number BELOW the picture." Embedding spatial language across the curriculum reinforces these concepts.

### Look-Fors
- Can the student correctly follow directions using each positional word (above, below, beside, in front of, behind, between)?
- Can the student describe the position of an object using at least one correct positional word in a complete sentence?
- Does the student distinguish between "above" and "below"?
- Does the student distinguish between "in front of" and "behind"?
- Can the student use "between" with two reference objects?
