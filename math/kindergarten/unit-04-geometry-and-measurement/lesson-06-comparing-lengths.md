# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 4, Lesson 6

## Topic
Comparing Lengths (Longer, Shorter, Taller)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Compare two objects by length and correctly identify which is longer and which is shorter
- Compare two objects by height and correctly identify which is taller and which is shorter
- Use direct comparison (lining up objects end to end) to determine relative length
- Order three objects from shortest to longest (or tallest to shortest)

## Prerequisites
- Identifies and describes basic shapes by attributes (Lessons 1-3)
- Uses positional words: above, below, beside, next to (Lesson 4)
- Compares groups using "more" and "fewer" (Unit 1, Lesson 6)

## Materials Needed
- 5-6 crayons or colored pencils of different lengths
- 3-4 pieces of string or ribbon cut to different lengths
- Unsharpened pencils (2-3 of different lengths, or one pencil and one pen)
- Small classroom or household objects: a spoon, a fork, a shoe, a book, a toy
- Paper and crayons for drawing
- A ruler or straight edge (for demonstration only — not for measuring in standard units)
- Strips of construction paper cut to various lengths (at least 6 strips)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review shape knowledge with a quick identification game.

1. Hold up a shape card (or draw a shape). "What shape? How many sides?" Do 3-4 shapes rapidly. (Circle: 0 sides; square: 4; triangle: 3; rectangle: 4) *(Lessons 1-3 review)*
2. Place a block on top of a book. "Is the block ABOVE or BELOW the book?" (Above.) "Is the book ABOVE or BELOW the block?" (Below.) *(Lesson 4 review)*
3. "If I put two triangles together, what shape could I make?" (A square or a rectangle.) *(Lesson 5 review)*

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce comparing objects by length and height using direct comparison, with precise vocabulary.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Longer:** Takes up more space from one end to the other
- **Shorter:** Takes up less space from one end to the other
- **Taller:** Reaches higher up from the ground or base
- **Length:** How far something stretches from end to end
- **Compare:** To look at two things and see how they are alike or different

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce "longer" and "shorter" with two objects.**
Hold up two crayons of obviously different lengths — one long, one short. Hold them up in the air, NOT lined up.

"I have two crayons. Which one do you THINK is longer?" (Let the student point.)

"Good guess! But how can we be SURE? We need to CHECK. Watch." Place both crayons flat on the table and line them up so one end of each crayon is even (touching the same edge or a book).

"I lined them up at this end. Now I look at the OTHER end. This crayon sticks out farther. That means it is LONGER. This one stops sooner. That means it is SHORTER."

Point to the longer crayon. "This crayon is LONGER." Point to the shorter one. "This crayon is SHORTER."

**Step 2: Emphasize lining up at one end.**
Take the same two crayons. This time, deliberately place them so they are NOT lined up — stagger them so the short one starts farther to the right.

"Hmm, now which one looks longer?" The student may be confused or tricked. "This is tricky! They LOOK the same because I did not line them up. We ALWAYS have to start at the same place."

Slide the crayons so one end is even. "NOW we can tell. This one is still longer."

"The rule is: LINE UP ONE END. Then look at the other end to see which sticks out farther."

**Step 3: Introduce "taller" and "shorter" for vertical comparison.**
Stand a tall book and a short book upright on the table (or use two toy figures of different heights).

"Now these are standing UP. When things stand up, we use the words TALLER and SHORTER instead of longer and shorter."

"Which book is TALLER?" (Student points.) "Which is SHORTER?" (Student points.)

"We line them up at the BOTTOM — both are standing on the table — and look at the TOP. Whichever reaches higher is TALLER."

**Step 4: Compare three objects.**
Place three ribbons or strips of different lengths on the table.

"Now I have THREE ribbons. Let me line them all up at one end." Arrange them so one end is even.

"Which is the LONGEST? Which is the SHORTEST? Which is in the MIDDLE?" Point to each as the student answers.

"We can put them in order: shortest, medium, longest. This is called ORDERING by length."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Place a spoon and a fork flat on the table, lined up at one end.
- "Which is longer — the spoon or the fork?" (Depends on actual objects — student identifies the one that extends farther.)
- "How do you know?" ("I lined them up and the ___ sticks out more.")

**Example B:** Stand two toy figures on the table.
- "Which figure is taller?" (Student identifies the one that reaches higher.)
- "Which is shorter?" (The other one.)
- "How did you check?" ("They are both standing on the table, and I looked at the top.")

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices direct comparison of length and height with real objects.

**Activity 1: Compare pairs of objects (5 minutes)**
Give the student pairs of objects to compare. For each pair, the student must:
1. Line them up at one end.
2. Say which is longer (or taller) and which is shorter.
3. Use a complete sentence: "The ___ is longer/taller than the ___."

Pairs to compare:
- A pencil and a crayon. "Which is longer?" (Student lines up, compares, answers.)
- Two strips of paper. "Which is shorter?" (Student lines up, compares, answers.)
- Two books standing up. "Which is taller?" (Student places both on table, compares tops.)
- The student's hand and the facilitator's hand (place palms flat, wrists at same edge). "Whose hand is longer?" (Facilitator's, typically.)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student eyeballs it without lining objects up, say: "I can see you are making a guess, and that is smart! But let's CHECK by lining them up at one end. Then we know for SURE."*

**Activity 2: Order three objects (5 minutes)**
Give the student three strips of construction paper (clearly different lengths).

"Put these in order from SHORTEST to LONGEST."

The student arranges them. Then ask:
- "Which is the shortest?" (Student points.)
- "Which is the longest?" (Student points.)
- "Which is in the middle?" (Student points.)

Repeat with three crayons. Then try three ribbons.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles to order three at once, start by finding the longest: "First, find the LONGEST one. Put it over here. Now look at these two. Which is longer? That one goes in the middle. The last one is the shortest."*

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
1. Place a long crayon and a short crayon on the table, lined up. "Which crayon is LONGER? Point to it." (Student points to the longer one.)
2. Place a tall block tower (3 blocks) and a short block tower (1 block) on the table. "Which tower is TALLER?" (The 3-block tower.)
3. "Draw a long line. Now draw a short line under it." (Student draws two lines; the first should be visibly longer.)
4. Place two strips of paper, one clearly longer. "Which strip is SHORTER?" (Student points to the shorter one.)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
1. Place two ribbons, NOT lined up. "Are these the same length? How can you check?" (Line them up at one end; then compare the other end.)
2. Place three crayons of different lengths in a jumbled pile. "Put these in order from shortest to longest." (Student arranges correctly.)
3. "Draw three lines: a short line, a medium line, and a long line." (Student draws three lines in increasing length.)
4. Stand two books upright. "Which book is taller? Which is shorter? How do you know?" (Student answers using comparison language.)
5. Place two objects of the same length. "Which is longer?" (They are the same! Neither is longer.)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
1. "Find two things in the room that are about the SAME length. Show me and explain how you checked." (Student finds two objects and lines them up.)
2. Order 4 strips of paper from shortest to longest. (Student correctly orders all four.)
3. "I have a string. Can you cut a piece of paper that is SHORTER than the string? Now cut one that is LONGER." (Student cuts two strips, one shorter and one longer than the string.)
4. "Draw a line. Now draw a line that is a LITTLE BIT longer. Now draw a line that is a LOT longer." (Student shows understanding of degree of difference.)
5. "Is the door taller or shorter than you? How could we check?" (The door is taller. We could stand next to it and see where the top of my head reaches.)

---

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. Place two pencils on the table, one longer than the other, lined up at one end. "Which pencil is longer?" (Student points to the correct one.)
2. "What do we need to do before we compare two things?" (Line them up at one end / start at the same place.)
3. Show two toy figures of different heights. "Which is taller? Which is shorter?" (Student identifies each correctly.)

---

## Answer Key

**Tier 1 (Approaching):**
1. The longer crayon (student points)
2. The 3-block tower
3. Student draws a long line above a short line (visual check)
4. The shorter strip (student points)

**Tier 2 (On Level):**
1. Student lines them up at one end and compares; answers based on actual ribbons
2. Student arranges crayons from shortest to longest correctly
3. Student draws three lines of increasing length
4. Student identifies taller and shorter book with reasoning
5. They are the same length — neither is longer

**Tier 3 (Advanced):**
1. Student finds two same-length objects and demonstrates comparison
2. Student correctly orders all 4 strips
3. Student cuts one strip shorter and one strip longer than the string
4. Student draws three lines showing progressive length difference
5. The door is taller; stand next to it to check

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Student points to the longer pencil
2. Line them up at one end (or "start at the same place")
3. Student correctly identifies taller and shorter figure

## Teacher Notes

**Common Errors:**
- **Not lining up at one end:** This is the single most common error. Students look at both objects loosely placed on the table and guess. Always redirect: "Slide them together so they START at the same spot." You may want to use a book or a box as a "starting wall" — both objects touch the wall, then the student looks at the other ends.
- **Confusing "longer" and "taller":** Young children may say "taller" for a horizontal ribbon. Gently correct: "When something is lying down flat, we say longer or shorter. When it is standing up, we say taller or shorter." Over time, students learn that both describe the same attribute (length) in different orientations.
- **Assuming longer = bigger in all ways:** A student may say a longer ribbon is "bigger" than a shorter but wider book. Clarify: "We are just looking at LENGTH — how far it goes from this end to that end. We are not talking about how wide it is or how heavy it is."

**Tips for Success:**
- Use the CRA progression: start with Concrete objects (real crayons, ribbons, strips), then move to Representational (drawing lines of different lengths on paper), and finally approach Abstract language ("The pencil is longer because it extends farther from the starting point").
- Let the student hold objects side by side. Kinesthetic learners benefit from physically aligning objects with their own hands rather than watching the facilitator do it.
- For ordering three objects, teach a strategy: "First find the longest. Set it aside. Now compare the two that are left. The longer one goes in the middle. The shorter one is the shortest."

**Look-Fors:**
- Does the student consistently line up objects at one end before comparing?
- Can the student use the words "longer," "shorter," and "taller" correctly in context?
- Can the student order three objects by length?
- Does the student understand that two objects can be "the same length"?

**Connection to Future Learning:**
Direct comparison is the foundation of measurement. In Grade 1, students will measure length using non-standard units (paper clips, blocks) and eventually standard units (inches, centimeters). The concept of "lining up at one end" becomes "starting at zero" on a ruler.
