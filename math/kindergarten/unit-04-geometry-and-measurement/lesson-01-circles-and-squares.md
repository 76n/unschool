# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 4, Lesson 1

## Topic
Identifying Circles and Squares

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify a circle by its properties (round, no straight sides, no corners)
- Identify a square by its properties (4 equal straight sides, 4 corners)
- Find circles and squares in everyday objects
- Trace and draw circles and squares

## Prerequisites
- Counts to 20 fluently (Unit 3, Lessons 1-2)
- Understands teen numbers as "ten and some more" (Unit 3, Lesson 3)
- Sorts objects by a single attribute such as color or size (general readiness)

## Materials Needed
- Large cutout shapes: 3-4 circles and 3-4 squares in different sizes and colors
- Real-world objects: a plate, a coin, a clock face (circles); a cracker, a sticky note, a block face (squares)
- Shape tracing templates (circle and square)
- Crayons or markers
- Paper
- A small bag or box for the shape mystery game

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce circles and squares as distinct shapes with specific, describable properties using concrete objects.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Shape:** The form or outline of an object
- **Circle:** A round shape with no straight sides and no corners; it curves all the way around
- **Square:** A shape with 4 straight sides that are all the same length and 4 corners
- **Side:** A straight line that is part of a shape
- **Corner:** The point where two sides meet (also called a vertex)
- **Round:** Curved, with no straight parts — like a ball or a wheel

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the circle.**
Hold up a large circle cutout and a real circular object (plate or coin).

"Today we are going to learn about SHAPES! Look at this." Hold up the circle cutout. "This is a CIRCLE. Say it with me: CIRCLE."

Run your finger slowly around the edge of the circle. "Watch my finger. It goes round and round and round. It never stops at a corner. It never goes straight. A circle is ROUND."

Hand the student the plate or coin. "Feel the edge. Is it bumpy? Does it have pointy parts?" (No.) "That's right! A circle has NO straight sides and NO corners. It is smooth and round all the way around."

Place the circle on the table and trace around it with your finger again. "A circle looks like a wheel, or the sun, or a pizza! Can you think of something that looks like a circle?" Allow the student to share ideas.

**Step 2: Introduce the square.**
Hold up a large square cutout and a real square object (cracker or sticky note).

"Now let's learn a different shape. This is a SQUARE. Say it with me: SQUARE."

Point to each side of the square. "Let's count the sides together. Ready? 1, 2, 3, 4. A square has FOUR sides." Run your finger along each side. "See how each side is straight? And look — this side is the same length as this side, and this side, and this side. All four sides are the SAME length."

Now point to each corner. "And where two sides meet, we get a CORNER. Let's count: 1, 2, 3, 4. A square has FOUR corners."

Hand the student the cracker or sticky note. "Feel the edges. Are they straight or round?" (Straight.) "Feel the corners. Can you count them?" (4.) "That's right! A square has 4 straight sides that are all the same, and 4 corners."

**Step 3: Compare circle and square side by side.**
Place the circle cutout and the square cutout next to each other on the table.

"Let's look at our two shapes together. How are they DIFFERENT?"

Point to the circle. "The circle is round — no straight sides, no corners." Point to the square. "The square has straight sides and corners."

"Let me ask you: Does a circle have any corners?" (No.) "Does a square have corners?" (Yes — 4!) "How many sides does a square have?" (4.) "Does a circle have sides?" (No — it is just round.)

"Here is a trick to remember: if you can ROLL it easily, it might be a circle. If it has flat sides and sits still, it might be a square."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Hold up a clock face (or a picture of one). "What shape is this clock? Let's check. Is it round?" (Yes.) "Does it have corners?" (No.) "Does it have straight sides?" (No.) "It is a CIRCLE!"

**Example B:** Hold up a square block or a square cracker. "What shape is this? Let's check. Does it have straight sides?" (Yes.) "Let's count them: 1, 2, 3, 4. Are they all the same length?" (Yes.) "How many corners?" (4.) "It is a SQUARE!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices identifying circles and squares with facilitator support using concrete and representational activities.

**Activity 1: Shape sorting (5 minutes)**

Spread a collection of circle and square cutouts on the table — use different sizes and colors. Include at least 3 circles and 3 squares.

"Let's sort these shapes into two groups. Put all the CIRCLES in one pile and all the SQUARES in another pile."

As the student sorts each shape, ask: "How do you know that one is a circle?" or "How do you know that one is a square?"

*Facilitator prompt: If the student hesitates, say: "Feel the edges. Are they round or straight? Does it have corners?"*

*Facilitator prompt: Include a small circle and a large circle. If the student thinks the large circle is different, say: "This one is bigger, but is it still round? Does it still have no corners? It is still a circle! Circles can be big or small."*

**Activity 2: Shape hunt (5 minutes)**

"Now let's go on a SHAPE HUNT! Look around the room. Can you find something shaped like a circle? Point to it and tell me."

Student identifies circular objects (clock, plate, cup rim, wheel, button). For each one, confirm: "Yes! That is round with no corners — it is a circle shape!"

"Now can you find something shaped like a square?" Student identifies square objects (window pane, book cover, tile, screen). For each one, confirm: "Let's check — does it have 4 sides that look the same? Does it have 4 corners? Yes — it is a square shape!"

*Facilitator prompt: If the student points to a rectangle (like a door), say: "Good thinking! That does have 4 sides and 4 corners. But look carefully — are all the sides the same length? This one has two long sides and two short sides. That is a different shape we will learn about next time! Today we are looking for shapes where ALL sides are the same length."*

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
1. The facilitator holds up a circle cutout. "What shape is this?" (Answer: circle)
2. The facilitator holds up a square cutout. "What shape is this?" (Answer: square)
3. "Does a circle have corners?" (Answer: no)
4. The facilitator places a round plate and a square cracker on the table. "Point to the circle shape." (Answer: student points to the plate)
5. "Trace around this circle with your finger." (Answer: student traces smoothly around the circle)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
1. The facilitator holds up a coin. "What shape is this? How do you know?" (Answer: circle — it is round, no straight sides, no corners)
2. The facilitator holds up a sticky note. "What shape is this? Tell me about its sides and corners." (Answer: square — 4 straight sides, all the same length, 4 corners)
3. "Draw a circle on your paper." (Answer: student draws a roughly round closed shape)
4. "Draw a square on your paper." (Answer: student draws a shape with 4 roughly equal sides and 4 corners)
5. "Name one thing in your house that is shaped like a circle." (Answer: any reasonable circular object — plate, clock, cup top, etc.)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
1. "How are a circle and a square DIFFERENT? Tell me two ways." (Answer: a circle has no sides/corners and is round; a square has 4 sides and 4 corners)
2. The facilitator shows a circle cutout and asks: "Could this shape roll? Why?" (Answer: yes, because it is round and has no flat sides to stop it)
3. "A square has 4 corners. If I cut off one corner, is it still a square? Why or why not?" (Answer: no, because a square needs 4 corners and 4 equal sides)
4. "Draw a big square. Now draw a small circle INSIDE the square." (Answer: student draws both shapes correctly with the circle inside)
5. "I am thinking of a shape. It has no sides and no corners. What is it?" (Answer: circle)

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. The facilitator holds up a circle cutout. "Name this shape and tell me one thing about it." (Answer: circle — it is round / has no corners / has no straight sides)
2. The facilitator holds up a square cutout. "Name this shape. How many sides and corners does it have?" (Answer: square — 4 sides, 4 corners)
3. The facilitator points to a round object in the room. "What shape is that?" (Answer: circle)

## Teacher Notes

### Common Errors
- **Confusing circles with ovals:** Some students may call an oval a circle. If this comes up, acknowledge that ovals are also round, but point out that a circle is perfectly round — the same distance from the center in every direction. An oval is stretched. At the kindergarten level, gentle awareness is enough; do not require mastery of ovals yet.
- **Calling rectangles "squares":** Students may identify any four-sided shape as a square. Redirect by counting sides and checking: "Are ALL the sides the same length? If two sides are longer, it is not a square." The formal introduction of rectangles comes in Lesson 2.
- **Difficulty tracing shapes:** Fine motor skills vary widely in kindergarten. If a student struggles to draw a circle, have them trace a round object first (like a cup bottom), then try freehand. For squares, suggest drawing four dots first for the corners and then connecting them.
- **Thinking size changes the shape name:** A small circle and a large circle are both circles. Reinforce: "It doesn't matter how big or small — if it is round with no corners, it is a circle."

### Tips
- Use the CRA (Concrete-Representational-Abstract) progression: start with real objects the student can hold and feel (concrete), move to shape cutouts and pictures (representational), and only then use the shape name without a visual (abstract).
- Let the student physically roll a circle cutout and try to roll a square cutout. The square will not roll smoothly. This concrete experience makes the difference between "round" and "straight sides" memorable.
- Sing a shape song if the student enjoys music: "A circle goes round, never stops, no corners found! A square has four sides, all the same, four corners is its claim!"

### Look-Fors
- Can the student name "circle" and "square" when shown each shape?
- Can the student give at least one property of each shape (e.g., "round" for circle, "4 sides" for square)?
- Does the student correctly sort mixed shapes into circle and square groups?
- Can the student find at least one real-world example of each shape?
