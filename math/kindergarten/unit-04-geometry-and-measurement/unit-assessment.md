# Unit Assessment — Kindergarten Math, Unit 4: Geometry and Measurement

**Grade:** Kindergarten
**Unit:** 4 — Geometry and Measurement
**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** One-on-one with facilitator (oral and hands-on; this is not a written test)
**Estimated Duration:** 20-30 minutes
**Materials:** Shape cutouts (circles, squares, triangles, rectangles in various sizes and orientations), a stuffed animal or small toy, a box or container, 3 objects of clearly different lengths (e.g., a short crayon, a medium pencil, and a long ruler), 3 objects of different weights (e.g., a cotton ball, a wooden block, a small rock), a balance scale, a collection of 10-12 small objects that vary by shape, color, and size (e.g., assorted buttons or blocks)

---

## Overview

This assessment measures the student's understanding of two-dimensional shapes, positional language, and basic measurement comparison skills taught in Unit 4 (Lessons 1-8). It is administered one-on-one with the facilitator in a comfortable, conversational setting. There are no time limits — let the student work at their own pace.

The assessment contains 21 items across 6 sections, worth a total of 30 points. Some items are worth more than 1 point because they require multiple responses (e.g., naming a shape AND explaining an attribute).

Before beginning, say: "We're going to play some games with shapes and objects today. I'll ask you some questions and we'll do some activities together. There are no tricks — just show me what you know. It's okay if some are tricky."

Do NOT provide hints or corrections during the assessment. If the student is stuck for more than 15 seconds, say: "That's okay — let's try the next one."

---

## Materials Needed

- [ ] Shape cutouts: at least 2 circles, 2 squares, 2 triangles, and 2 rectangles (include different sizes and orientations — e.g., a triangle pointing up and one pointing down, a large rectangle and a small one)
- [ ] 1 stuffed animal or small toy (for positional words section)
- [ ] 1 box or container (for positional words section)
- [ ] 3 objects of clearly different lengths (e.g., short crayon, medium pencil, long ruler)
- [ ] 3 objects of different weights (e.g., cotton ball, wooden block, small rock)
- [ ] A balance scale (a simple hanger-and-cups version works)
- [ ] A collection of 10-12 small objects that vary by shape, color, and size (e.g., assorted buttons, blocks, or counters)
- [ ] This assessment sheet and a pencil for recording

## Section 1: Shape Identification

*This section assesses whether the student can identify and name circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles. (Lessons 1-2)*

**Item 1.** Place a circle cutout on the table. "What shape is this?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Says "circle" | 1 point |
| Any other answer | 0 points |

**Item 2.** Place a square cutout on the table. "What shape is this?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Says "square" | 1 point |
| Any other answer | 0 points |

**Item 3.** Place a triangle cutout on the table, oriented with one point facing down (inverted). "What shape is this?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Says "triangle" | 1 point |
| Any other answer (including "upside-down triangle") | 0 points |

*Note: The inverted orientation tests whether the student recognizes triangles regardless of position. If the student says "upside-down triangle," prompt once: "What kind of shape is it?" If they then say "triangle," give full credit.*

**Item 4.** Place a rectangle cutout on the table (not a square — use one where the sides are clearly different lengths). "What shape is this?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Says "rectangle" | 1 point |
| Says "square" | 0 points |
| Any other answer | 0 points |

**Item 5.** Lay out one circle, one square, one triangle, and one rectangle in a row. "Point to the triangle." Then: "Point to the circle."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correctly points to BOTH the triangle and the circle | 2 points |
| Correctly points to one of the two | 1 point |
| Points to wrong shapes for both | 0 points |

**Section 1 Total: ___ / 6 points**

## Section 2: Shape Attributes

*This section assesses whether the student can describe shapes by their attributes — number of sides, number of corners, curved vs. straight. (Lesson 3)*

**Item 6.** Place a triangle on the table. "How many sides does this triangle have?" Then: "How many corners does it have?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Says 3 sides AND 3 corners | 2 points |
| Says 3 for one but not the other | 1 point |
| Incorrect for both | 0 points |

*If the student is unsure what "sides" or "corners" means, you may say: "Sides are the straight lines. Corners are the pointy parts where the sides meet." Then ask the question. This is a vocabulary clarification, not a hint about the answer.*

**Item 7.** Place a square on the table. "How many sides does this square have?" Then: "How many corners?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Says 4 sides AND 4 corners | 2 points |
| Says 4 for one but not the other | 1 point |
| Incorrect for both | 0 points |

**Item 8.** Place a circle and a square side by side. "How are these two shapes different? Tell me one thing that is different about them."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Gives a valid attribute difference (e.g., "the circle is round / has no corners / has no straight sides" or "the square has corners / has straight sides") | 1 point |
| Gives only a non-attribute difference (e.g., "this one is bigger" or "this one is blue") | 0 points |

**Item 9.** Place a square and a rectangle side by side. "How are these shapes the same? Tell me one thing that is the same."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Gives a valid shared attribute (e.g., "they both have 4 sides" or "they both have 4 corners" or "they both have straight sides") | 1 point |
| Cannot identify a similarity or gives a non-attribute response | 0 points |

**Section 2 Total: ___ / 6 points**

## Section 3: Positional Words

*This section assesses whether the student can use and respond to positional language. (Lesson 4)*

**Item 10.** Place the box on the table. Hand the student the stuffed animal. "Put the bear ON TOP OF the box."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Places the animal on top of the box | 1 point |
| Places the animal in any other position | 0 points |

**Item 11.** "Now put the bear BEHIND the box."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Places the animal behind the box (from the student's perspective) | 1 point |
| Places the animal in any other position | 0 points |

*Note: Accept "behind" from the student's perspective. If the student places the animal on the far side of the box (away from themselves), that is correct.*

**Item 12.** "Now put the bear BESIDE the box."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Places the animal to the left or right side of the box | 1 point |
| Places the animal on top, behind, in front of, or under the box | 0 points |

**Item 13.** Place the stuffed animal UNDER the table (or under the box if the table is not practical). "Where is the bear? Tell me using a position word."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Says "under" or "below" or "underneath" (the table/box) | 2 points |
| Uses a different but accurate position word for a different placement you may have used | 1 point |
| Cannot describe the position or uses an incorrect word | 0 points |

*Note for Item 13: This item is worth 2 points because the student must produce (not just follow) a positional word. Producing positional language is harder than responding to it.*

**Section 3 Total: ___ / 5 points**

## Section 4: Comparing Lengths

*This section assesses whether the student can compare objects by length. (Lesson 6)*

**Item 14.** Place a short crayon and a long pencil side by side, aligned at one end. "Which one is longer?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Points to or names the longer object (pencil) | 1 point |
| Points to the shorter object | 0 points |

**Item 15.** Place the same two objects side by side but NOT aligned — stagger the starting points so the shorter object extends beyond the starting point of the longer one. "Which one is longer? How can you tell?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Identifies the longer object AND explains or demonstrates how to compare (e.g., "line them up," "put the ends together," or physically aligns them) | 2 points |
| Identifies the longer object but cannot explain how to compare fairly | 1 point |
| Identifies the wrong object | 0 points |

**Item 16.** Place 3 objects of clearly different lengths on the table (e.g., short crayon, medium pencil, long ruler). "Put these in order from shortest to longest."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Places all 3 in correct order (shortest to longest) | 2 points |
| Gets 2 out of 3 in the right position | 1 point |
| Random or fully incorrect order | 0 points |

**Section 4 Total: ___ / 5 points**

## Section 5: Comparing Weights

*This section assesses whether the student can compare objects by weight. (Lesson 7)*

**Item 17.** Hold up a cotton ball in one hand and a small rock in the other. Let the student hold both. "Which one is heavier?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Says the rock (or the heavier object) is heavier | 1 point |
| Says the cotton ball or guesses | 0 points |

**Item 18.** Hold up a wooden block and a small rock (choose objects where the answer is not obvious from size alone). "Which do you think is heavier — the block or the rock? Make a prediction." Then say: "Let's check with the balance scale." Place both objects on the scale. "Which one IS heavier? How does the scale show you?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Makes a prediction (any prediction is fine — award this point for the willingness to predict) | 1 point |
| After using the scale, correctly identifies which is heavier AND explains the scale (e.g., "that side went down" or "that side is lower") | 1 point |

*Score each part independently. Maximum 2 points for this item.*

| Part | Score |
|------|-------|
| Makes a prediction | ___ / 1 |
| Correctly reads the scale and explains | ___ / 1 |

**Item 19.** Point to the cotton ball and the wooden block (still on the table). "Which one is lighter?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Says the cotton ball (or the lighter object) is lighter | 1 point |
| Incorrect | 0 points |

**Section 5 Total: ___ / 4 points**

## Section 6: Sorting

*This section assesses whether the student can sort objects by an attribute and explain their sorting rule. (Lesson 8)*

**Item 20.** Place 10-12 small objects on the table that vary by at least two attributes (e.g., color and shape, or shape and size). "Sort these into groups. Put the ones that go together in a pile."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Sorts all objects into groups using a consistent attribute (e.g., all by color, all by shape, all by size) | 2 points |
| Begins sorting by one attribute but is inconsistent (some objects placed incorrectly) | 1 point |
| Cannot sort or places objects randomly | 0 points |

**Item 21.** After the student sorts the objects in Item 20, ask: "Tell me your rule. Why did you put these together?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Clearly states the sorting rule (e.g., "I put all the red ones together" or "these are all circles") | 2 points |
| Gives a vague or partial explanation (e.g., "they go together" or "they're the same") without naming the attribute | 1 point |
| Cannot explain the sorting rule | 0 points |

**Section 6 Total: ___ / 4 points**

## Mastery Criteria

| Level | Score Range | Description |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Mastery** | 26-30 points (85-100%) | The student demonstrates solid understanding of shape identification and attributes, positional language, and measurement comparison. They can name and describe shapes, follow and produce positional words, compare objects by length and weight, and sort objects with a clear rule. |
| **Approaching** | 21-25 points (70-84%) | The student demonstrates understanding in most areas but has specific gaps. Some skills are solid while others need reinforcement. The student is close to mastery and needs targeted practice in weaker sections. |
| **Developing** | Below 21 points (<70%) | The student has significant gaps in geometry and measurement understanding. Foundational concepts — particularly shape identification and basic comparison — need additional instruction before the student is ready to advance. |

**Mastery threshold:** 85% overall (26 of 30 points) AND at least 50% of available points in each section (Sections 1-6).

## Reteaching Recommendations

### Section 1: Shape Identification (if score < 3 of 6)

The student needs more exposure to shapes in varied contexts. Return to Lessons 1-2 and:

- Go on daily "shape hunts" — find circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles in the environment (plates, windows, signs, tiles)
- Use shape cutouts in multiple sizes, colors, and orientations so the student learns that a triangle is still a triangle no matter how it is turned
- Practice with sorting activities: "Put all the triangles in this pile and all the circles in that pile"
- Pair shape names with a physical gesture (e.g., trace a circle in the air while saying "circle")

### Section 2: Shape Attributes (if score < 3 of 6)

The student needs practice connecting shape names to their defining features. Return to Lesson 3 and:

- Trace each side of a shape with a finger while counting aloud: "One side, two sides, three sides — three sides!"
- Touch each corner while counting: "One corner, two corners, three corners — three corners!"
- Compare shapes side by side: "This shape has 3 sides. This shape has 4 sides. They're different!"
- Build shapes with craft sticks or pipe cleaners so the student physically constructs the sides and corners
- Ask "How do you KNOW it's a triangle?" to develop reasoning beyond just visual recognition

### Section 3: Positional Words (if score < 3 of 5)

The student needs more practice with spatial language. Return to Lesson 4 and:

- Play "Simon Says" with positional words: "Simon says put your hands ABOVE your head," "Simon says stand BEHIND the chair"
- Use a stuffed animal and a box for daily positional word practice — the student places the animal and describes its position, or follows your instructions
- Read picture books and describe where things are: "Where is the cat? The cat is UNDER the table."
- Practice both following positional instructions (receptive) AND describing positions (expressive) — expressive is harder and needs more practice

### Section 4: Comparing Lengths (if score < 3 of 5)

The student needs more practice with direct comparison. Return to Lesson 6 and:

- Always align objects at one end before comparing — teach this as a rule: "Line up the ends first, then look at the other end"
- Use hands-on comparison with everyday objects: "Which crayon is longer? Let's line them up and check"
- Practice the vocabulary: say "longer" and "shorter" many times in context rather than just "bigger" and "smaller"
- Build up from comparing 2 objects to ordering 3 objects — do not skip to 3 until the student is confident with 2

### Section 5: Comparing Weights (if score < 2 of 4)

The student needs more hands-on experience with weight comparison. Return to Lesson 7 and:

- Let the student hold objects in both hands and feel the difference: "Which hand feels heavier? Which feels lighter?"
- Use the balance scale frequently — let the student predict first, then verify. The predict-and-check cycle builds understanding
- Directly address the misconception that bigger = heavier by comparing a large cotton ball with a small rock
- Practice the vocabulary: "heavier" and "lighter" rather than "bigger" and "smaller" when talking about weight

### Section 6: Sorting (if score < 2 of 4)

The student needs practice identifying and articulating attributes. Return to Lesson 8 and:

- Start with sorting by one very obvious attribute (e.g., color, using objects that are clearly red or blue)
- Model the process: "I'm going to sort these. I'm putting all the RED ones here and all the BLUE ones here. My rule is COLOR."
- After the student sorts, always ask "What is your rule?" — the ability to explain the rule is as important as the sorting itself
- Gradually introduce sorting the same set of objects by different attributes: "Now sort them a different way"

## Facilitator Observations

Note during the assessment:

- Can the student name shapes confidently, or do they hesitate and guess?
- Does the student recognize shapes in non-standard orientations (e.g., a rotated triangle)?
- When counting sides and corners, does the student use a tracking strategy (touching or tracing)?
- Does the student use positional words spontaneously, or only when following instructions?
- When comparing lengths, does the student naturally align objects at one end, or do they need prompting?
- Does the student understand that the balance scale shows weight, or do they seem confused by the tool?
- When sorting, does the student choose an attribute quickly, or does it take time to find a rule?
- Overall, does the student seem comfortable with spatial and measurement concepts?

These observations inform differentiation decisions for future units and help identify whether struggles are conceptual or vocabulary-based.
