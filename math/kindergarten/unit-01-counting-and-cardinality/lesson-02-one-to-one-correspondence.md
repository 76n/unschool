# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 1, Lesson 2

## Topic
One-to-One Correspondence

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Touch or move one object for each number word when counting a set of objects
- Understand that each object gets counted exactly once

## Prerequisites
- Can count orally from 1 to 10 (Lesson 1)

## Materials Needed
- 10 counting objects (blocks, buttons, beans)
- A small bowl or cup
- Number line strip (1-10)
- 5 paper plates or pieces of paper (optional, for Activity 3)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge — practice the counting sequence from Lesson 1.

1. "Let's count to 10 together with claps." (Count 1-10, clapping on each number.)
2. "What number comes after 6?" (Answer: 7)
3. "What number comes after 3?" (Answer: 4)
4. "Count to 10 by yourself — I'll listen!" (Student counts independently.)

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the idea that counting means matching one number word to one object.

### Key Vocabulary
- **One-to-one correspondence:** Counting one number for each thing — no skipping, no double-counting
- **Touch and say:** The strategy of touching each object as you say its number

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Demonstrate the problem.**
Place 5 objects in a messy cluster. Count them incorrectly on purpose — count too fast, pointing vaguely at the pile: "1-2-3-4-5-6-7!" Look confused.

Say: "Hmm, that didn't seem right. I think I counted some of these twice! I need a better way."

**Step 2: Model "Touch and Say."**
Spread the 5 objects into a clear line. Say: "Watch what I do. I'm going to touch each one and say one number."

Touch the first object: "1." (Move it aside or to a "counted" area.)
Touch the second: "2." (Move it.)
Continue through all 5.

Say: "I touched each one exactly once, and I said one number for each touch. That's how we count carefully."

**Step 3: Model with emphasis on moving objects.**
Demonstrate again with 7 objects. This time, move each object from one side to the other as you count. Say:

"I move each one to this side after I count it. That way I know which ones I've already counted."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Place 4 blocks in a row. Touch each one, saying "1, 2, 3, 4." Then say: "There are 4 blocks. I touched each one, I said one number each time, and the last number I said — 4 — tells me how many."

**Example 2:** Place 6 buttons in a messy group. Move each to a "counted" pile: "1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. There are 6 buttons."

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices touch-and-say counting with your support.

**Activity 1: Counting together (4 minutes)**
Place 5 objects in a row. Say: "Your turn. Touch each one and say the number."

Guide the student's hand if needed. If the student skips an object, gently redirect: "Oops — this one didn't get counted yet. Let's start over."

If the student double-counts (touches the same object twice), say: "That one already got counted. Let's move the counted ones over here." Introduce the move-aside strategy.

Repeat with 3 objects, then 7 objects.

*Facilitator prompt: Watch the student's hand. The most common error is the hand moving faster than the words, or words going faster than the hand. If you see this, say: "Let's slow down. One touch, one number."*

**Activity 2: Objects in a bowl (3 minutes)**
Place 6 objects in a bowl. Say: "Take them out one at a time and count as you go."

The student removes one object: "1." Removes another: "2." Continue until the bowl is empty.

Ask: "How many were in the bowl?" The student should say the last number they counted.

**Activity 3: Matching sets (3 minutes)** *(If time allows)*
Place 3 paper plates in a row. Say: "Can you put exactly 1 block on each plate?"

The student places one object on each plate. Then ask: "How many plates have a block? Let's count."

This extends one-to-one correspondence from counting to matching — a key conceptual bridge.

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student counts sets of objects without facilitator guidance.

Give the student a pile of counting objects and ask them to complete these tasks:

**★ On-level:**
1. Count out 4 objects from the pile. (Student should pull out exactly 4.)
2. Count out 6 objects from the pile.
3. Here are some objects (place 5 in a row). How many are there?

**★★ Stretch:**
4. Count out 8 objects from the pile.
5. Here are some objects (place 7 in a messy group). How many?

**★★★ Challenge:**
6. Count out exactly 10 objects.
7. Here are some objects (place 9 scattered). How many? (Requires careful tracking without a neat row.)

### Interleaved Review (from Lesson 1)
- "Count from 1 to 10 for me." (Oral counting review)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess one-to-one correspondence and cardinality.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Place 5 objects in a row. "Count these for me." (Watch for touch-and-say.)
2. After the student counts, ask: "How many are there?" (Answer: 5 — tests cardinality.)
3. Place 8 objects in a scattered arrangement. "How many are here?" (Answer: 8 — tests correspondence with unorganized sets.)

**Mastery = Student correctly counts at least one set using touch-and-say AND correctly answers "how many?" with the last number counted.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Spend the next session re-practicing with sets of 3-5 objects (smaller is easier to track).
- Use the "move aside" strategy consistently until the student does it automatically.
- Count real objects throughout the day: "How many plates do we need for dinner? Let's count the people."

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (Counting Objects — How Many?). Continue practicing one-to-one correspondence with varied objects in warm-ups.

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Body counting:** Count body parts — "How many fingers on both hands? Let's touch each one."
- **Line them up:** Give the student 10 objects and ask them to arrange them in a straight line, counting as they place each one.
- **Counting walk:** Walk around the room or house counting specific things (windows, doors, chairs).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is the most important lesson in this unit.** One-to-one correspondence is the foundation of meaningful counting. Without it, a child can recite numbers but can't actually count things. Give this skill as much time as it needs.

**Physical objects matter.** At this stage, students need to physically touch and move objects. Counting pictures on a page is harder because the student can't move them to track what's been counted. Start with physical objects; pictures come later.

**Speed is the enemy.** Young children often want to count fast. Encourage slow, deliberate counting. Model slow counting yourself. Speed comes with fluency — it should not be a goal right now.

**The "how many?" question is key.** Many students will happily count "1, 2, 3, 4, 5" and then, when asked "how many?", start counting again from 1. This means they haven't connected the last number to the total quantity (cardinality). This is normal and addressed more deeply in Lesson 3, but start planting the seed now by always asking "how many?" after counting.
