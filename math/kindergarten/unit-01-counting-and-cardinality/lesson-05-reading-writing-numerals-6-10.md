# Math — Kindergarten — Unit 1, Lesson 5

## Topic
Reading and Writing Numerals 6-10

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Recognize and name the numerals 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10
- Write the numerals 6-10 (recognizable, not necessarily perfect)
- Connect each numeral 6-10 to its corresponding quantity

## Prerequisites
- Can count orally from 1 to 10 (Lesson 1)
- Can count objects with one-to-one correspondence (Lesson 2)
- Understands cardinality (Lesson 3)
- Recognizes and writes numerals 0-5 (Lesson 4)

## Materials Needed
- Number cards 0-10 (large, clear numerals)
- 10+ counting objects
- Pencil or crayon and paper
- Optional: sand/salt tray for tactile writing
- Number line strip (1-10)
- Ten-frame (a 2x5 grid — can be drawn on paper)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review numeral recognition 0-5 and counting skills.

1. Flash number cards 0-5 in random order. Student names each one. *(Review: Lesson 4)*
2. "Write the number 3." Then: "Write the number 5." *(Review: Lesson 4)*
3. Place 6 objects. "Count these and tell me how many." (Answer: 6) *(Review: Lessons 2-3)*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Extend numeral recognition and writing from 0-5 to include 6-10.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Ten-frame:** A grid with 10 spaces (2 rows of 5) that helps us see quantities up to 10
- **Digit:** A single numeral (0-9); the number 10 uses two digits

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the ten-frame.**
Draw or show a ten-frame (2 rows, 5 columns). Say: "This is a ten-frame. It has 10 spaces. We'll use it to see how numbers from 6 to 10 look."

Fill in 6 counters on the ten-frame (top row of 5, plus 1): "Six is a full row of 5, plus 1 more."

**Step 2: Connect numerals to quantities (6-10).**
For each numeral from 6 to 10:
1. Show the number card.
2. Say: "This is the number [X]."
3. Fill the ten-frame to show [X]. Describe it: "8 is 5 on top and 3 on the bottom."
4. The student says the number and counts the objects on the ten-frame to verify.

Emphasize the pattern: "6 is 5 and 1. 7 is 5 and 2. 8 is 5 and 3. 9 is 5 and 4. 10 is 5 and 5 — the whole frame is full!"

**Step 3: Special attention to 10.**
"10 is special because it uses TWO numerals: a 1 and a 0. But it's one number — ten."

Show the full ten-frame. "When the whole frame is full, that's 10."

**Step 4: Model writing each numeral.**
Write each numeral while describing the strokes:

- **6:** "Curve down from the top, and loop around at the bottom."
- **7:** "Line across the top, then a diagonal line down."
- **8:** "Start with a curve at the top, cross in the middle, curve at the bottom — like a snowman."
- **9:** "Circle at the top, then a line straight down."
- **10:** "A 1 and a 0, right next to each other."

Let the student trace in the air, in sand, or over dotted models.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Show the numeral 7. Fill 7 spaces on the ten-frame. "This says 7. Let's count: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. Seven — 5 on top, 2 on the bottom."

**Example 2:** Show the numeral 10. Fill all 10 spaces. "This says 10. The whole ten-frame is full. We write it with a 1 and a 0 together."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices recognizing 6-10 and connecting to quantities.

**Activity 1: Ten-Frame Flash (4 minutes)**
Show the ten-frame with different amounts filled (6, 8, 7, 10, 9). For each:
- Student counts the filled spaces.
- Student finds the matching number card.

*Facilitator prompt: After a few rounds, try: "Can you tell how many WITHOUT counting each one? Look at how many are filled on the bottom row." This builds toward subitizing (instantly recognizing quantities) — don't push it if the student isn't ready.*

**Activity 2: Match the Card — Full Set (3 minutes)**
Spread all number cards 0-10 face up. Place quantities of objects (6, 8, 10, 3, 9). Student counts each and finds the matching card.

Mix in some 0-5 quantities to review.

**Activity 3: Writing Practice (3 minutes)**
Student traces and then writes each numeral 6-10. Provide dotted models to trace first.

For 10: show how the 1 and 0 go next to each other — "They're friends, not far apart."

*Facilitator prompt: 6 and 9 look similar — if the student confuses them, say: "6 has its circle at the bottom. 9 has its circle at the top." Use the mnemonic consistently.*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student identifies and writes numerals 0-10 and connects them to quantities.

**★ On-level:**
1. Lay out number cards 0-10 in random order. Student names each one.
2. Write the numeral 7 three times.
3. Write the numeral 9 three times.
4. Fill a ten-frame to show 8. Write the number 8 next to it.

**★★ Stretch:**
5. Write each numeral 0-10 in order without a model.
6. Show the student a ten-frame filled to 7. "Write the number that matches."
7. Write 10. Circle the two digits. "How many digits does 10 use?" (Answer: 2)

**★★★ Challenge:**
8. Shuffle all number cards 0-10. Student puts them in order.
9. Student draws their own ten-frames showing 6, 8, and 10, and writes the matching numeral next to each.

### Interleaved Review
- "Count backward from 10 to 1." (Extension of Lesson 1)
- Show 5 objects. Scatter them. "How many?" (Lesson 3 review — conservation)
- Flash number cards 2 and 4. Student names them. (Lesson 4 review)

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess numeral recognition, writing, and quantity connection for 0-10.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Show the numeral 8. "What number is this?" (Answer: 8)
2. Show the numeral 6. "Count out this many objects." (Student counts out 6)
3. "Write the number 10." (Student writes 10)

**Mastery = Student correctly identifies the numeral in item 1, counts the right quantity in item 2, AND writes a recognizable 10 in item 3.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Continue daily flash-card practice with the full 0-10 set.
- Use the ten-frame consistently — it provides a visual structure that helps students "see" 6-10 as "5 and some more."
- Common sticking points: confusing 6 and 9 (use the circle position mnemonic), writing 10 as a single entity rather than two separate digits. Both resolve with practice.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 6 (Comparing Groups). Continue reviewing numeral recognition 0-10 in all future warm-ups.

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Number line ordering:** Give the student all cards 0-10 shuffled. Time how long it takes to put them in order. Try to beat the time next session.
- **Ten-frame bingo:** Call out a number; student fills a ten-frame to match. Great for repeated practice.
- **Number book:** Student makes a booklet with one page per number (0-10). Each page has the numeral, the word, and that many drawings.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The ten-frame is one of the most powerful tools in early math.** It builds visual number sense by showing quantities in relation to 5 and 10 — the benchmarks of our number system. Use it frequently, not just in this lesson. Ask the student to show various quantities on the ten-frame during warm-ups throughout the year.

**6 and 9 confusion is universal.** Every kindergartener mixes these up. Be patient and use a consistent cue. Some teachers say "6 rolls down" (the circle is at the bottom) and "9 stands on its tail" (the circle is at the top). Pick a cue that works and stick with it.

**10 is conceptually different.** It's the first two-digit number the student encounters. Don't belabor the place-value explanation at this point — just establish that 10 is written with two numerals together. Place value is formally introduced later.

**Backward counting is a stretch goal.** It appeared in the interleaved review. If the student can't do it, that's fine — it will develop naturally. If they can, it's a great sign of strong number sense.
