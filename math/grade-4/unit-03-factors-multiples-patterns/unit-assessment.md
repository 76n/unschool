# Unit Assessment — Grade 4 Math, Unit 3: Factors, Multiples, and Patterns

**Grade:** 4
**Unit:** 3 — Factors, Multiples, and Patterns

## Section 1: Factors and Factor Pairs (10 points)

**1.1** Find ALL factor pairs of 28. List them in order. (3 points — 1 point per correct pair)

**1.2** Find ALL factor pairs of 54. List them in order. (4 points — 1 point per correct pair)

**1.3** Is 7 a factor of 49? Explain how you know. (1 point)

**1.4** Is 8 a factor of 50? Explain how you know. (1 point)

**1.5** List all the factors of 40. (1 point)

|--------|---------------|

| 2 | ___ |

**2.2** List all prime numbers between 30 and 50. (2 points)

**2.3** Explain why 1 is NOT a prime number. (1 point)

**2.4** Tyler says "All odd numbers are prime." Give TWO examples of odd numbers that are NOT prime to show Tyler is wrong. For each, name a factor other than 1 and the number itself. (2 points — 1 point per example)

## Section 3: Multiples, Common Factors, and Common Multiples (12 points)

**3.1** List the first 6 multiples of 9. (2 points)

**3.2** Is 56 a multiple of 8? Explain. (1 point)

**3.3** Is 45 a multiple of 7? Explain. (1 point)

**3.4** Fill in the blanks with "factor" or "multiple." (2 points — 0.5 each)

- 6 is a ___ of 42.
- 42 is a ___ of 6.
- 9 is a ___ of 72.
- 72 is a ___ of 9.

**3.5** Find the GCF (greatest common factor) of 24 and 36. Show your work by listing the factors of each number. (3 points — 1 for factors of 24, 1 for factors of 36, 1 for correct GCF)

**3.6** Find the LCM (least common multiple) of 6 and 9. Show your work by listing multiples. (3 points — 1 for multiples of 6, 1 for multiples of 9, 1 for correct LCM)

**4.2** Generate the first 6 terms of a pattern with starting number 3 and rule "add 8." (3 points)

## Section 5: Word Problems (8 points)

**5.1** A teacher has 36 colored pencils and 24 markers. She wants to make identical art kits for her students. Each kit must have the same number of colored pencils and the same number of markers, with nothing left over. What is the greatest number of kits she can make? How many colored pencils and markers will be in each kit? Show your work. (3 points — 1 for finding GCF, 1 for number of kits, 1 for items per kit)

**5.2** Hot dog buns come in packs of 6. Hot dogs come in packs of 8. What is the smallest number of each you can buy so that you have the same number of hot dogs and buns with none left over? How many packs of each do you need? (3 points — 1 for LCM, 1 for number of each item, 1 for packs)

**5.3** Marcus says the number 91 is prime because it is odd and not divisible by 2, 3, or 5. Is Marcus correct? If not, prove that 91 is composite by finding a factor pair other than (1, 91). (2 points — 1 for correct answer, 1 for proof)

## Answer Key

### Section 1: Factors and Factor Pairs

**1.1** Factor pairs of 28: (1, 28), (2, 14), (4, 7). [3 points]

**1.2** Factor pairs of 54: (1, 54), (2, 27), (3, 18), (6, 9). [4 points]

**1.3** Yes, 7 is a factor of 49 because 49 / 7 = 7 with no remainder (7 x 7 = 49). [1 point]

**1.4** No, 8 is not a factor of 50 because 50 / 8 = 6 R2. There is a remainder. [1 point]

**1.5** Factors of 40: 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20, 40. [1 point — full credit for complete list; 0.5 if missing 1-2 factors]

### Section 2: Prime and Composite Numbers

**2.1** Classifications: [5 points]

| Number | Classification |
|--------|---------------|
| 1 | Neither |
| 2 | Prime |
| 27 | Composite |
| 43 | Prime |
| 51 | Composite |

**2.2** Prime numbers between 30 and 50: 31, 37, 41, 43, 47. [2 points — full credit for all 5; 1 point if missing 1; 0 if missing 2 or more or if a non-prime is included]

**2.3** The number 1 has only ONE factor (itself). Prime numbers must have exactly TWO factors (1 and itself). Since 1 does not have two different factors, it is not prime. [1 point]

**2.4** Acceptable examples (any two): [2 points]
- 9 is odd but NOT prime: 3 x 3 = 9, so 3 is a factor.
- 15 is odd but NOT prime: 3 x 5 = 15, so 3 and 5 are factors.
- 21 is odd but NOT prime: 3 x 7 = 21.
- 25 is odd but NOT prime: 5 x 5 = 25.
- 27 is odd but NOT prime: 3 x 9 = 27.
- 33 is odd but NOT prime: 3 x 11 = 33.
- 35 is odd but NOT prime: 5 x 7 = 35.
- 39 is odd but NOT prime: 3 x 13 = 39.
- 45 is odd but NOT prime: 5 x 9 = 45.
- 49 is odd but NOT prime: 7 x 7 = 49.
(And many others.)

**3.4** [2 points — 0.5 each]

- 72 is a **multiple** of 9.

- Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, ... [1 point]

### Section 4: Patterns

**4.1** [3 points]
- Rule: add 7. [1 point]
- Next 3 terms: 39, 46, 53. [2 points — 1 point if only 1-2 are correct]

**4.2** [3 points]
- Pattern: 3, 11, 19, 27, 35, 43. [3 points — deduct 1 point per error after first error, minimum 0]

**4.3** [4 points]
- (a) Rule for Pattern A: add 3 (or start at 0, add 3). [1 point]
- (b) Rule for Pattern B: add 15 (or start at 0, add 15). [1 point]

### Section 5: Word Problems

**5.1** [3 points]
- Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36. Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24. GCF = 12. [1 point for identifying GCF]
- She can make 12 kits. [1 point]
- Each kit: 36/12 = 3 colored pencils and 24/12 = 2 markers. [1 point]

**5.2** [3 points]
- LCM of 6 and 8: Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24. Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24. LCM = 24. [1 point]
- You need 24 buns and 24 hot dogs. [1 point]
- Packs: 24/6 = 4 packs of buns, 24/8 = 3 packs of hot dogs. [1 point]

**5.3** [2 points]
- Marcus is NOT correct. 91 is composite. [1 point]
- Proof: 91 / 7 = 13. So 7 x 13 = 91. The factor pair (7, 13) proves 91 is composite. [1 point]

## Scoring Guide

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Next Steps |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Mastered** | 40-50 (80-100%) | Strong understanding of factors, multiples, primes, composites, and patterns | Advance to Unit 4. Celebrate the achievement! |
| **Approaching** | 30-39 (60-79%) | Partial understanding; specific concept gaps | Identify weak sections. Reteach targeted lessons (see below). Reassess weak sections only. |
| **Developing** | Below 30 (<60%) | Significant gaps in understanding | Review prerequisite skills (multiplication facts, division). Reteach core concepts from Lessons 1-5. Consider additional practice time before reassessment. |

### Section-Specific Reteaching Guide

| If struggling in... | Focus on... |
|---|---|
| Section 1 (Factors/Factor Pairs) | Return to arrays with tiles (Lesson 1). Reteach the rainbow method with the T-chart (Lesson 2). Practice with numbers under 30 first, then gradually increase. |
| Section 2 (Prime/Composite) | Redo the Sieve of Eratosthenes (Lesson 3). Build factor lists to classify each number. Reinforce: 1 = neither, exactly 2 factors = prime, more than 2 = composite. |
| Section 3 (Multiples/GCF/LCM) | Reconnect multiples to skip counting (Lesson 5). For GCF: practice listing all factors of two numbers and circling common ones (Lesson 6). For LCM: practice listing multiples until finding a match (Lesson 7). |
| Section 4 (Patterns) | Start with simple add/subtract rules (Lesson 8). Use tables to organize corresponding terms (Lesson 9). Practice generating before identifying. |
| Section 5 (Word Problems) | Help students identify the key question: "Is this asking about factors, multiples, GCF, or LCM?" Use graphic organizers to map problem information to operations. |

## Facilitator Observations

Use these prompts to record qualitative observations during the assessment:

- Does the student use a systematic method for finding factor pairs, or rely on guessing?
- Can the student distinguish between prime and composite numbers with confidence?
- Does the student confuse factors and multiples?
- Can the student extend a pattern beyond what is given?
- Does the student show organized work, or is work scattered and hard to follow?
- For word problems, can the student identify which concept (GCF or LCM) applies?
