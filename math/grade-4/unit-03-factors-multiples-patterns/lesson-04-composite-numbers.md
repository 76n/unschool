# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 3, Lesson 4

## Topic
Composite Numbers

## Learning Objectives
- Define composite number as a whole number greater than 1 that has more than two factors
- Classify whole numbers from 1 to 100 as prime, composite, or neither (1)
- Explain that 1 is neither prime nor composite
- Recognize that 2 is the only even prime number and that all other even numbers greater than 2 are composite

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of factors and factor pairs (Lessons 1-2)
- Definition and identification of prime numbers (Lesson 3)
- Fluency with multiplication and division facts

## Materials Needed
- Completed hundred chart from Lesson 3 (Sieve of Eratosthenes)
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper
- Color tiles or counters (optional)

**Standards:** 4.OA.4 — Determine whether a given whole number in the range 1-100 is prime or composite.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review prime numbers and set up the contrast with composite numbers.

1. Quick review — students write "Prime" or "Not Prime" on their boards:
   - 7 (Prime)
   - 12 (Not Prime)
   - 2 (Prime)
   - 15 (Not Prime)
   - 1 (Not Prime)
2. Ask: "For the numbers that were NOT prime, how many factors did each have?"
   - 12 has 6 factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
   - 15 has 4 factors: 1, 3, 5, 15
3. Say: "Today we will learn the name for numbers that have MORE than two factors."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
### Key Vocabulary
- **Composite number:** A whole number greater than 1 that has more than two factors. Examples: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12.
- **Prime number (review):** A whole number greater than 1 that has exactly two factors: 1 and itself.
- **Neither prime nor composite:** The number 1, which has exactly one factor.

### Instruction Notes
**Step 1 — Define composite numbers.**
Say: "In Lesson 3, we learned that prime numbers have exactly two factors. Today, we are learning about composite numbers. A composite number is a whole number greater than 1 that has MORE than two factors."

Write on the board:
- Prime: exactly 2 factors (1 and itself)
- Composite: more than 2 factors (can be broken into smaller factor pairs)

Say: "The word composite means 'made up of parts.' A composite number can be broken apart — it is made up of smaller factors combined together. Think of it like a composite material that is made by combining different things."

**Step 2 — Show examples.**
Say: "Let's look at 12. What are its factors?"

Write: Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 (6 factors)

Say: "12 has six factors — that is way more than two. So 12 is composite. We can build 12 from several different factor pairs: 1 x 12, 2 x 6, 3 x 4."

Say: "Now let's look at 9. What are its factors?"

Write: Factors of 9: 1, 3, 9 (3 factors)

Say: "9 has three factors — more than two. So 9 is composite, even though it is odd. Remember, being odd does not automatically make a number prime!"

**Step 3 — Classify 1 explicitly.**
Say: "What about the number 1? We said 1 is not prime because it only has one factor. Is 1 composite? To be composite, a number needs MORE than two factors. The number 1 has only one factor. So 1 is NOT composite either."

Write on the board:
- 1 is NEITHER prime NOR composite (special case — only 1 factor)

**Step 4 — Classify the first 20 whole numbers.**
Work through this as a class, building a chart on the board:

| Number | Factors | Classification |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| 1 | 1 | Neither |
| 2 | 1, 2 | Prime |
| 3 | 1, 3 | Prime |
| 4 | 1, 2, 4 | Composite |
| 5 | 1, 5 | Prime |
| 6 | 1, 2, 3, 6 | Composite |
| 7 | 1, 7 | Prime |
| 8 | 1, 2, 4, 8 | Composite |
| 9 | 1, 3, 9 | Composite |
| 10 | 1, 2, 5, 10 | Composite |
| 11 | 1, 11 | Prime |
| 12 | 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 | Composite |
| 13 | 1, 13 | Prime |
| 14 | 1, 2, 7, 14 | Composite |
| 15 | 1, 3, 5, 15 | Composite |
| 16 | 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 | Composite |
| 17 | 1, 17 | Prime |
| 18 | 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18 | Composite |
| 19 | 1, 19 | Prime |
| 20 | 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20 | Composite |

Say: "Look at the pattern. After 2, every even number in the list is composite. Why?" (Because every even number has 2 as a factor in addition to 1 and itself, giving it at least 3 factors.)

**Step 5 — Quick classification strategy.**
Say: "Here is a shortcut for classifying numbers. To show a number is composite, you only need to find ONE factor besides 1 and the number itself. You do not need to find all the factors — just one extra factor is enough to prove it is composite."

### Worked Examples
**Example A:** Is 24 prime or composite?
- Check: Is 24 divisible by 2? Yes! 24 / 2 = 12.
- Since 24 has factors 1, 2, 12, and 24 (at minimum), it has more than 2 factors.
- 24 is composite.

**Example B:** Is 31 prime or composite?
- Check: 31 / 2 = 15 R1 (no). 31 / 3 = 10 R1 (no). 31 / 4 = 7 R3 (no). 31 / 5 = 6 R1 (no). Since 6 > 5, stop.
- Only factors: 1 and 31. Exactly 2 factors.
- 31 is prime.

**Example C:** Is 51 prime or composite?
- This is a tricky one! 51 looks like it might be prime.
- Check: 51 / 2 = 25 R1 (no). 51 / 3 = 17 (yes!).
- Since 3 x 17 = 51, the number 51 has more than 2 factors.
- 51 is composite.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Classify each number as prime, composite, or neither. Explain your reasoning.

1. **Is 35 prime or composite?**
   Facilitator prompt: "Can you find any factor of 35 besides 1 and 35?"
   Answer: 5 x 7 = 35. Composite (has factors 1, 5, 7, 35).

2. **Is 43 prime or composite?**
   Facilitator prompt: "Test divisibility by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. Do any work?"
   Answer: 43/2=21R1, 43/3=14R1, 43/4=10R3, 43/5=8R3, 43/6=7R1. Since 7>6, stop. Prime.

3. **Is 1 prime or composite?**
   Answer: Neither. 1 has only one factor.

4. **Is 50 prime or composite?**
   Answer: 50/2 = 25. Composite (has factors 1, 2, 25, 50 at minimum).

5. **Is 39 prime or composite?**
   Facilitator prompt: "Try dividing by 3."
   Answer: 39/3 = 13. Composite (3 x 13 = 39).

6. **Is 97 prime or composite?**
   Answer: Test 2 (R1), 3 (R1), 4 (R1), 5 (R2), 6 (R1), 7 (R6), 8 (R1), 9 (R7). Since 9 < 97/9 = 10R7, keep going. Test 10 (R7). Since 10 > 9, stop. Prime.

**Facilitator tip:** Remind students that to prove a number is composite, they only need ONE factor pair besides (1, number). But to prove a number is prime, they must check all possible factors up to the stopping point.

### Part C: Word Problems
1. Alex says that all even numbers are composite. Is Alex correct? Explain your answer.

2. A teacher asks her class to think of a number between 50 and 60 that is prime. Marcus says 51, Emma says 53, and Jade says 57. Who is correct? Prove your answer by testing each number.

3. How many composite numbers are there between 1 and 10? List them.

### Answer Key

**Part A:**
1. 8: Composite (2 x 4 = 8)
2. 11: Prime
3. 1: Neither
4. 22: Composite (2 x 11 = 22)
5. 37: Prime
6. 45: Composite (5 x 9 = 45)
7. 53: Prime
8. 64: Composite (8 x 8 = 64)
9. 71: Prime
10. 91: Composite (7 x 13 = 91)

**Part B:**
| Prime | Composite | Neither |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| 2, 7, 13, 19, 29 | 4, 10, 16, 20, 25, 30 | 1 |

**Part C:**
1. Alex is NOT correct. The number 2 is even AND prime. It has exactly two factors: 1 and 2. However, Alex is right that all other even numbers (4, 6, 8, 10, ...) are composite, because they all have 2 as a factor in addition to 1 and themselves.

2. Test each number:
   - 51: 51 / 3 = 17. So 51 = 3 x 17. Composite. Marcus is wrong.
   - 53: 53 / 2 = 26R1, 53 / 3 = 17R2, 53 / 4 = 13R1, 53 / 5 = 10R3, 53 / 6 = 8R5, 53 / 7 = 7R4. Since 7 = 7, stop. Only factors are 1 and 53. Prime. Emma is correct!
   - 57: 57 / 3 = 19. So 57 = 3 x 19. Composite. Jade is wrong.

3. Composite numbers between 1 and 10: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10. That is 5 composite numbers. (The primes are 2, 3, 5, 7, and the number 1 is neither.)

## Assessment Check
1. Classify the number 87 as prime or composite. Show your work.
2. In your own words, explain the difference between prime and composite numbers.

**Target response:**
1. Test: 87 / 2 = 43 R1 (no). 87 / 3 = 29 (yes!). Since 3 x 29 = 87, the number 87 has more than two factors. 87 is composite.
2. A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. A composite number has more than two factors — it can be divided evenly by at least one number other than 1 and itself. The number 1 is special and is neither prime nor composite.

## Extension
- **Composite number challenge:** Find the composite number between 1 and 100 that has the MOST factors. (Hint: try numbers like 60, 72, 84, 96.) Answer: 96 has 12 factors (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 96). Other good candidates: 72 has 12 factors, 60 has 12 factors.
