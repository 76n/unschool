# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 3, Lesson 6

## Topic
Common Factors and Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

## Learning Objectives
- Find all factors of two different numbers and identify the factors they share (common factors)
- Define and find the greatest common factor (GCF) of two numbers
- Use a Venn diagram to organize factors and identify common factors
- Apply GCF to real-world sharing and grouping problems

## Prerequisites
- Finding all factor pairs of a number systematically (Lessons 1-2)
- Understanding of prime and composite numbers (Lessons 3-4)
- Understanding of multiples and the factor-multiple relationship (Lesson 5)

## Materials Needed
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper
- Venn diagram template (two overlapping circles) — one per student or draw on paper
- Colored pencils (optional)

**Standards:** 4.OA.4 — Find all factor pairs for a whole number in the range 1-100.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice listing factors and notice overlap between factor lists.

1. Ask students to list all factors of 12: (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12)
2. Ask students to list all factors of 18: (1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18)
3. Ask: "Look at both lists. Which factors appear in BOTH lists?" (1, 2, 3, 6)
4. Ask: "What is the largest factor that appears in both lists?" (6)
5. Say: "Those shared factors are called common factors. The largest one has a special name: the greatest common factor. Today we will learn how to find them."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
### Key Vocabulary
- **Common factor:** A factor that two (or more) numbers share. For example, 3 is a common factor of 12 and 18 because 3 is a factor of both.
- **Greatest common factor (GCF):** The largest factor that two (or more) numbers have in common. The GCF of 12 and 18 is 6.
- **Venn diagram:** A diagram with overlapping circles used to organize and compare items. The overlapping region shows what the two groups have in common.

### Instruction Notes
**Step 1 — Define common factors.**
Say: "When two numbers share a factor, we call it a common factor. The word 'common' means 'shared.' For example, 4 is a common factor of 12 and 20 because 4 divides evenly into both 12 and 20."

**Step 2 — Demonstrate the list-and-compare method.**
Say: "The easiest way to find common factors is to list all the factors of each number, then compare the lists."

Write on the board:
- Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
- Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36

Say: "Now I circle every number that appears in BOTH lists."

Circle: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12

Say: "The common factors of 24 and 36 are: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12."

**Step 3 — Introduce the greatest common factor.**
Say: "The greatest common factor, or GCF, is the biggest number on our common factors list. What is the biggest common factor of 24 and 36?" (12)

Write: GCF of 24 and 36 = 12.

Say: "The GCF tells us the largest group size that divides evenly into both numbers."

**Step 4 — Show the Venn diagram method.**
Draw two overlapping circles on the board. Label one "Factors of 24" and the other "Factors of 36."

Say: "In the overlapping middle section, we write the common factors. In the left section, we write factors that belong ONLY to 24. In the right section, factors that belong ONLY to 36."

Fill in:
- Left only (24 only): 8, 24
- Middle (common): 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
- Right only (36 only): 9, 18, 36

Say: "The Venn diagram makes it easy to see what is shared and what is different. The GCF is the largest number in the overlapping region: 12."

**Step 5 — A note about 1.**
Say: "The number 1 is ALWAYS a common factor of any two numbers, because 1 is a factor of every number. So the common factor list always has at least one number in it."

### Worked Examples
**Example A:** Find the GCF of 16 and 20.
- Factors of 16: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16
- Factors of 20: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20
- Common factors: 1, 2, 4
- GCF = 4

**Example B:** Find the GCF of 15 and 25.
- Factors of 15: 1, 3, 5, 15
- Factors of 25: 1, 5, 25
- Common factors: 1, 5
- GCF = 5

**Example C:** Find the GCF of 8 and 9.
- Factors of 8: 1, 2, 4, 8
- Factors of 9: 1, 3, 9
- Common factors: 1
- GCF = 1
- Note: When the GCF of two numbers is 1, we say the numbers are "relatively prime" — they share no factors other than 1.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Find the common factors and GCF for each pair. Students work on boards; facilitator circulates.

1. **Find the GCF of 12 and 16.**
   Facilitator prompt: "List all factors of 12, then all factors of 16. Which appear on both lists?"
   Answer: Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Factors of 16: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. Common factors: 1, 2, 4. GCF = 4.

2. **Find the GCF of 18 and 27.**
   Answer: Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. Factors of 27: 1, 3, 9, 27. Common factors: 1, 3, 9. GCF = 9.

3. **Find the GCF of 20 and 30.**
   Answer: Factors of 20: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20. Factors of 30: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30. Common factors: 1, 2, 5, 10. GCF = 10.

4. **Find the GCF of 14 and 21.**
   Answer: Factors of 14: 1, 2, 7, 14. Factors of 21: 1, 3, 7, 21. Common factors: 1, 7. GCF = 7.

5. **Find the GCF of 11 and 13.**
   Facilitator prompt: "Both of these are prime numbers. What factors do prime numbers have?"
   Answer: Factors of 11: 1, 11. Factors of 13: 1, 13. Common factor: 1. GCF = 1.

**Facilitator tip:** If students struggle, suggest drawing the Venn diagram. The visual organizer helps many students see the overlap more clearly.

### Part B: Venn Diagram
Draw a Venn diagram for the factors of 20 and 28. Label each section with the correct factors. State the GCF.

### Part C: Word Problems
1. A teacher has 24 red crayons and 36 blue crayons. She wants to make identical packs for her students so that each pack has the same number of red crayons and the same number of blue crayons, with no crayons left over. What is the greatest number of packs she can make?

2. A baker made 30 chocolate cookies and 18 vanilla cookies. He wants to create gift bags where every bag has the same combination of chocolate and vanilla cookies, with none left over. What is the greatest number of bags he can make? How many of each type of cookie will be in each bag?

3. Two pieces of ribbon are 16 inches and 24 inches long. They need to be cut into equal-length pieces with no ribbon left over. What is the longest each piece can be?

### Answer Key

**Part A:**
1. Factors of 10: 1, 2, 5, 10. Factors of 15: 1, 3, 5, 15. Common: 1, 5. GCF = 5.
2. Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24. Factors of 32: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32. Common: 1, 2, 4, 8. GCF = 8.
3. Factors of 28: 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28. Factors of 42: 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 14, 21, 42. Common: 1, 2, 7, 14. GCF = 14.
4. Factors of 30: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30. Factors of 45: 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 45. Common: 1, 3, 5, 15. GCF = 15.
5. Factors of 9: 1, 3, 9. Factors of 16: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. Common: 1. GCF = 1.
6. Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36. Factors of 48: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 48. Common: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. GCF = 12.
7. Factors of 25: 1, 5, 25. Factors of 40: 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20, 40. Common: 1, 5. GCF = 5.
8. Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24. Common: 1, 2, 3, 6. GCF = 6.

**Part B:**
- Factors of 20: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20
- Factors of 28: 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28
- Venn diagram:
  - Left only (20 only): 5, 10, 20
  - Middle (common): 1, 2, 4
  - Right only (28 only): 7, 14, 28
- GCF = 4

**Part C:**
1. GCF of 24 and 36: Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24. Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36. GCF = 12. She can make 12 packs. Each pack: 24/12 = 2 red crayons and 36/12 = 3 blue crayons.

2. GCF of 30 and 18: Factors of 30: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30. Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. GCF = 6. He can make 6 bags. Each bag: 30/6 = 5 chocolate cookies and 18/6 = 3 vanilla cookies.

3. GCF of 16 and 24: Factors of 16: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24. GCF = 8. The longest each piece can be is 8 inches. (16/8 = 2 pieces from the first ribbon, 24/8 = 3 pieces from the second ribbon.)

## Assessment Check
1. Find the GCF of 18 and 30.
2. Explain what the GCF tells you in a real-world context.

**Target response:**
1. Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. Factors of 30: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30. Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6. GCF = 6.
2. The GCF tells you the biggest group size (or largest equal piece) that works for both numbers without anything left over. For example, if you have 18 of one thing and 30 of another and want to divide them into equal groups, the most groups you can make is 6.

## Extension
- **Three-number GCF:** Find the GCF of 12, 18, and 30. (Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. Factors of 30: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30. Common to all three: 1, 2, 3, 6. GCF = 6.)
- **Preview of Lesson 7:** We found common factors — factors shared by two numbers. What about common multiples — multiples shared by two numbers? If multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, ... and multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, ... which multiples do they share?
