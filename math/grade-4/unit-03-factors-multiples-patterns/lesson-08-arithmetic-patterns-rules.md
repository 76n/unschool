# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 3, Lesson 8

## Topic
Arithmetic Patterns — Identifying and Generating Rules

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the rule governing a number pattern (addition, subtraction, or multiplication)
- Generate a number pattern given a starting number and a rule
- Use a table to organize and extend patterns
- Describe patterns using mathematical language

## Prerequisites
- Skip counting and multiplication facts (used throughout this unit)
- Understanding of multiples (Lesson 5)
- Comfort with addition, subtraction, and multiplication operations

## Materials Needed
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper
- Rulers (for drawing tables)
- Grid paper (optional)

**Standards:** 4.OA.5 — Generate a number or shape pattern that follows a given rule. Identify apparent features of the pattern that were not explicit in the rule itself.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate pattern-recognition skills with familiar sequences.

1. Display each pattern. Students write the next three numbers on their boards:
   - 2, 4, 6, 8, ___, ___, ___ (10, 12, 14 — rule: add 2)
   - 5, 10, 15, 20, ___, ___, ___ (25, 30, 35 — rule: add 5)
   - 100, 90, 80, 70, ___, ___, ___ (60, 50, 40 — rule: subtract 10)
2. Ask: "How did you know what comes next? What rule did you use?"
3. Say: "In Lessons 1-7, we explored factors and multiples. Today we shift to a new topic: patterns. A pattern is a sequence that follows a rule. Being able to find the rule is a powerful math skill."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
### Key Vocabulary
- **Pattern:** A sequence of numbers or shapes that follows a predictable rule.
- **Rule:** The operation or set of operations used to get from one term to the next. For example, "add 3" or "multiply by 2."
- **Term:** Each number in a pattern. In the pattern 4, 7, 10, 13, ..., each number is a term.
- **Starting number:** The first term in a pattern.
- **Sequence:** An ordered list of numbers that follows a rule.

### Instruction Notes
**Step 1 — Introduce the idea of a rule.**
Say: "A number pattern is a list of numbers that follows a rule. The rule tells us what to do to get from one number to the next. Today we will practice two things: figuring out the rule from a given pattern, and creating a pattern from a given rule."

**Step 2 — Finding the rule of a given pattern.**
Write on the board: 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, ...

Say: "To find the rule, I look at what changes from one term to the next."
- 3 to 7: difference is 4 (7 - 3 = 4)
- 7 to 11: difference is 4 (11 - 7 = 4)
- 11 to 15: difference is 4 (15 - 11 = 4)
- 15 to 19: difference is 4 (19 - 15 = 4)

Say: "The difference is the same each time: 4. So the rule is 'add 4.' The next terms would be 23, 27, 31, ..."

**Step 3 — Generating a pattern from a rule.**
Say: "Now let's go the other direction. I will give you a rule and a starting number, and you build the pattern."

Write: Starting number: 2. Rule: add 6.

Say: "Start with 2. Add 6 to get the next term."
- 2, 8, 14, 20, 26, 32, ...

Say: "Each term is 6 more than the previous one. This pattern could go on forever."

**Step 4 — Multiplication rules.**
Say: "Some patterns use multiplication instead of addition."

Write: 1, 3, 9, 27, ...

Say: "Look at the changes. From 1 to 3, we multiply by 3. From 3 to 9, multiply by 3. From 9 to 27, multiply by 3. The rule is 'multiply by 3.' The next terms are 81, 243, ..."

Say: "Notice how quickly multiply-patterns grow compared to add-patterns!"

**Step 5 — Subtraction rules.**
Write: 50, 44, 38, 32, ...

Say: "From 50 to 44 is -6. From 44 to 38 is -6. From 38 to 32 is -6. The rule is 'subtract 6.' The next terms are 26, 20, 14, 8, 2."

Say: "Subtraction patterns decrease and will eventually reach 0 or go below 0."

**Step 6 — Features not stated in the rule.**
Say: "Sometimes a pattern has features that the rule does not mention."

Write: Starting number: 1. Rule: add 4. Pattern: 1, 5, 9, 13, 17, 21, 25, ...

Say: "The rule says 'add 4,' but look at the ones digits: 1, 5, 9, 3, 7, 1, 5, ... The ones digits repeat in a cycle! The rule does not tell us that, but we can observe it in the pattern. This is what it means to identify features of a pattern that are not explicit in the rule."

### Worked Examples
**Example A:** Identify the rule: 10, 17, 24, 31, 38, ...
- 17 - 10 = 7
- 24 - 17 = 7
- 31 - 24 = 7
- 38 - 31 = 7
- Rule: add 7
- Next three terms: 45, 52, 59

**Example B:** Generate the pattern. Starting number: 5. Rule: multiply by 2.
- Term 1: 5
- Term 2: 5 x 2 = 10
- Term 3: 10 x 2 = 20
- Term 4: 20 x 2 = 40
- Term 5: 40 x 2 = 80
- Term 6: 80 x 2 = 160
- Pattern: 5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, ...

**Example C:** Identify the rule: 64, 56, 48, 40, 32, ...
- 56 - 64 = -8
- 48 - 56 = -8
- 40 - 48 = -8
- Rule: subtract 8
- Next three terms: 24, 16, 8

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Work through these problems as a class.

1. **Identify the rule and give the next 3 terms: 4, 9, 14, 19, 24, ...**
   Facilitator prompt: "What is the difference between consecutive terms?"
   Answer: Rule: add 5. Next terms: 29, 34, 39.

2. **Generate the first 6 terms. Starting number: 7. Rule: add 8.**
   Answer: 7, 15, 23, 31, 39, 47.

3. **Identify the rule and give the next 3 terms: 2, 6, 18, 54, ...**
   Facilitator prompt: "Is this an add pattern or a multiply pattern? Try dividing each term by the one before it."
   Answer: Rule: multiply by 3. Next terms: 162, 486, 1,458.

4. **Identify the rule and give the next 3 terms: 80, 72, 64, 56, ...**
   Answer: Rule: subtract 8. Next terms: 48, 40, 32.

5. **Generate the first 6 terms. Starting number: 3. Rule: add 9.**
   Answer: 3, 12, 21, 30, 39, 48.
   Facilitator prompt: "Look at the ones digits. Do you see a pattern?" (3, 2, 1, 0, 9, 8 — the ones digit decreases by 1 each time! This is a feature not stated in the rule.)

**Facilitator tip:** Always ask students to verify their rule works for every pair of consecutive terms, not just the first pair.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)
### Part A: Identify the Rule
Find the rule and write the next 3 terms.

1. 6, 11, 16, 21, 26, ___, ___, ___
2. 1, 4, 16, 64, ___, ___, ___
3. 95, 86, 77, 68, ___, ___, ___
4. 8, 15, 22, 29, 36, ___, ___, ___
5. 3, 12, 48, 192, ___, ___, ___
6. 100, 88, 76, 64, ___, ___, ___

### Part B: Generate the Pattern
Write the first 7 terms.

1. Starting number: 4. Rule: add 6.
2. Starting number: 1. Rule: multiply by 5.
3. Starting number: 200. Rule: subtract 15.
4. Starting number: 0. Rule: add 11.

### Part C: Word Problems
1. A baker makes 12 muffins on Monday. Each day after that, she makes 8 more muffins than the day before. How many muffins does she make on Friday?

2. Marcus starts with $5 in his savings jar. Each week he adds $3. How much money does he have after 8 weeks? Write the pattern and identify how much he has at each week.

3. Look at this pattern: 2, 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, ... Are any of the terms multiples of 3? Which ones? (Connect to what we learned about multiples in Lesson 5.)

### Answer Key

**Part A:**
1. Rule: add 5. Next terms: 31, 36, 41.
2. Rule: multiply by 4. Next terms: 256, 1,024, 4,096.
3. Rule: subtract 9. Next terms: 59, 50, 41.
4. Rule: add 7. Next terms: 43, 50, 57.
5. Rule: multiply by 4. Next terms: 768, 3,072, 12,288.
6. Rule: subtract 12. Next terms: 52, 40, 28.

**Part B:**
1. 4, 10, 16, 22, 28, 34, 40.
2. 1, 5, 25, 125, 625, 3,125, 15,625.
3. 200, 185, 170, 155, 140, 125, 110.
4. 0, 11, 22, 33, 44, 55, 66.

**Part C:**
1. Monday: 12 muffins. Tuesday: 12 + 8 = 20. Wednesday: 20 + 8 = 28. Thursday: 28 + 8 = 36. Friday: 36 + 8 = 44. She makes 44 muffins on Friday.

2. Week 0 (start): $5. Week 1: $8. Week 2: $11. Week 3: $14. Week 4: $17. Week 5: $20. Week 6: $23. Week 7: $26. Week 8: $29. After 8 weeks, Marcus has $29.

3. Pattern: 2, 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, ... Terms that are multiples of 3: 2 (no), 5 (no), 8 (no), 11 (no), 14 (no), 17 (no), 20 (no). Actually, let's check: 8/3=2R2, 11/3=3R2, 14/3=4R2, 17/3=5R2, 20/3=6R2. None of the given terms are multiples of 3. Every term gives a remainder of 2 when divided by 3. This is a feature of the pattern — since we start at 2 and add 3 each time, every term has the same remainder when divided by 3.

## Assessment Check
1. What is the rule for this pattern: 7, 13, 19, 25, 31, ...? What are the next two terms?
2. Generate the first 5 terms for: Starting number = 10, Rule = subtract 2.

**Target response:**
1. Rule: add 6. Next two terms: 37, 43.
2. 10, 8, 6, 4, 2.
