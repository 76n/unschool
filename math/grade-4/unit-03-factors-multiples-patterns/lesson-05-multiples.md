# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 3, Lesson 5

## Topic
Multiples

## Learning Objectives
- Define multiples as the products you get when you multiply a number by 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...
- List multiples of a given number
- Connect skip counting to listing multiples
- Explain the factor-multiple relationship: if A is a factor of B, then B is a multiple of A

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of factors and factor pairs (Lessons 1-2)
- Knowledge of prime and composite numbers (Lessons 3-4)
- Skip counting by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10
- Fluency with multiplication facts

## Materials Needed
- Hundred chart (1-100) — one per student
- Colored pencils or crayons
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper
- Number line (optional, for visual support)

**Standards:** 4.OA.4 — Recognize that a whole number is a multiple of each of its factors.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate skip counting skills, which are the foundation of listing multiples.

1. As a class, skip count together:
   - By 3s, starting from 3: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30
   - By 7s, starting from 7: 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49, 56, 63, 70
2. Ask: "When we skip count by 3, what operation are we actually doing each time?" (Adding 3, or multiplying 3 by 1, 2, 3, 4, ...)
3. Say: "Today we will learn that when you skip count by a number, you are listing its multiples. Let's find out exactly what that means."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
### Key Vocabulary
- **Multiple:** A multiple of a number is the product of that number and any whole number. The multiples of 4 are: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, ...
- **Factor-multiple relationship:** If A is a factor of B, then B is a multiple of A. For example, 3 is a factor of 12, so 12 is a multiple of 3.
- **Skip counting:** Counting by a fixed number (e.g., counting by 5s: 5, 10, 15, 20, ...).

### Instruction Notes
**Step 1 — Define multiples.**
Say: "A multiple of a number is what you get when you multiply that number by any whole number: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and so on."

Write on the board:
Multiples of 5:
- 5 x 1 = 5
- 5 x 2 = 10
- 5 x 3 = 15
- 5 x 4 = 20
- 5 x 5 = 25
- 5 x 6 = 30
- ...

Say: "The multiples of 5 are: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, ... This list goes on forever! There is no largest multiple of 5."

**Step 2 — Connect multiples to skip counting.**
Say: "Notice that listing multiples of 5 is the same as skip counting by 5. When you skip count by a number, you are listing its multiples. You already know how to find multiples — you have been doing it since second grade!"

**Step 3 — Show multiples on the hundred chart.**
Give students a hundred chart. Say: "Let's color in all the multiples of 4 on our chart."

Guide students to color: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48, 52, 56, 60, 64, 68, 72, 76, 80, 84, 88, 92, 96, 100.

Say: "Every colored number is a multiple of 4. Notice the pattern — every 4th number is colored."

**Step 4 — The factor-multiple relationship.**
Say: "Here is a very important connection between factors and multiples. They are two sides of the same coin."

Write on the board:
- 3 x 4 = 12
- 3 is a FACTOR of 12
- 4 is a FACTOR of 12
- 12 is a MULTIPLE of 3
- 12 is a MULTIPLE of 4

Say: "If you know that 3 is a factor of 12, you automatically know that 12 is a multiple of 3. Factors and multiples are partners — they describe the same relationship from opposite directions."

Say: "Think of it this way: factors go INTO a number, multiples come OUT of a number."

**Step 5 — Key distinction: every number has infinitely many multiples but finitely many factors.**
Say: "How many factors does 12 have?" (Six: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12.)
"How many multiples does 12 have?" (Infinitely many: 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, ... and it never stops.)

Say: "The list of factors is always finite — it has a definite end. The list of multiples goes on forever."

### Worked Examples
**Example A:** List the first 8 multiples of 6.
- 6 x 1 = 6
- 6 x 2 = 12
- 6 x 3 = 18
- 6 x 4 = 24
- 6 x 5 = 30
- 6 x 6 = 36
- 6 x 7 = 42
- 6 x 8 = 48
- First 8 multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48

**Example B:** Is 56 a multiple of 8?
- Divide: 56 / 8 = 7, no remainder.
- Yes, 56 is a multiple of 8 (because 8 x 7 = 56).
- We can also say: 8 is a factor of 56.

**Example C:** Is 50 a multiple of 7?
- Divide: 50 / 7 = 7 R1. There IS a remainder.
- No, 50 is NOT a multiple of 7.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Work through these problems as a class. Students answer on their boards.

1. **List the first 6 multiples of 9.**
   Facilitator prompt: "Skip count by 9 six times."
   Answer: 9, 18, 27, 36, 45, 54.

2. **Is 42 a multiple of 6?**
   Facilitator prompt: "Divide 42 by 6. Is there a remainder?"
   Answer: 42 / 6 = 7, no remainder. Yes, 42 is a multiple of 6.

3. **Is 35 a multiple of 4?**
   Answer: 35 / 4 = 8 R3. No, 35 is not a multiple of 4.

4. **Name two numbers that are multiples of BOTH 3 and 5.**
   Facilitator prompt: "List some multiples of 3 and some multiples of 5. Which numbers appear on both lists?"
   Answer: Multiples of 3: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30... Multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30... Common multiples include 15 and 30.

5. **If 7 is a factor of 63, what can you say about 63?**
   Answer: 63 is a multiple of 7.

6. **If 48 is a multiple of 8, what can you say about 8?**
   Answer: 8 is a factor of 48.

**Facilitator tip:** Use the phrase "factors fit IN, multiples move OUT" repeatedly to help students internalize the relationship.

### Part B: Multiple or Not?
Write "Yes" or "No." If yes, write the multiplication fact that proves it.

2. Is 45 a multiple of 6? ___
3. Is 54 a multiple of 6? ___
4. Is 72 a multiple of 8? ___

### Part C: Factor-Multiple Connections
Fill in the blank with "factor" or "multiple."

1. 5 is a ___ of 30.
2. 30 is a ___ of 5.
3. 8 is a ___ of 64.
4. 64 is a ___ of 8.

### Part D: Word Problems
1. A bus comes every 8 minutes. If the first bus arrives at 8:00, list the arrival times of the next 5 buses. (Hint: list multiples of 8 and add them to 8:00.)

2. Mia has 45 stickers. She wants to divide them equally among her friends with no stickers left over. Can she divide them among 5 friends? Among 6 friends? Among 9 friends? Explain using factors and multiples.

   Among 6 friends: 45 / 6 = 7 R3. No. 45 is NOT a multiple of 6.

## Assessment Check
1. List the first 5 multiples of 11.
2. Explain in your own words: what is the relationship between factors and multiples?

**Target response:**
1. 11, 22, 33, 44, 55.
2. Factors and multiples describe the same relationship from different directions. If A is a factor of B, then B is a multiple of A. For example, since 4 is a factor of 20 (4 x 5 = 20), we know that 20 is a multiple of 4. Factors divide into a number evenly; multiples are what you get when you multiply a number.
