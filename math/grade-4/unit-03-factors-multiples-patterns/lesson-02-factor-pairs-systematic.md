# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 3, Lesson 2

## Topic
Factor Pairs — Systematic Method

## Learning Objectives
- Use the rainbow method to systematically find ALL factor pairs of a number
- Organize factor pairs in a T-chart
- Know when to stop testing for new factor pairs
- Verify that no factor pairs have been missed

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of factors and factor pairs (Lesson 1)
- Fluency with multiplication and division facts
- Ability to check divisibility by dividing

## Materials Needed
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper
- Grid paper (optional)
- Color tiles (optional, for verification)

**Standards:** 4.OA.4 — Find all factor pairs for a whole number in the range 1-100.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review factor pairs from Lesson 1 and motivate the need for a system.

1. Ask students to find all factor pairs of 12. Give them 2 minutes.
2. Collect answers. Did everyone find all three pairs? ((1,12), (2,6), (3,4))
3. Now ask: "Find all factor pairs of 36." Give them 2 minutes.
4. Ask: "How do you know you found them ALL? Is there a way to be sure you didn't miss any?"
5. Say: "Today we are going to learn a system that guarantees we find every single factor pair."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
### Key Vocabulary
- **Rainbow method:** A systematic strategy for finding all factor pairs by starting at 1 and testing each number in order, drawing arcs (like a rainbow) to connect each factor pair.
- **T-chart:** A two-column chart used to organize factor pairs.
- **Stopping rule:** You can stop looking for new factor pairs when the number you are testing is greater than or equal to its partner (the quotient).

### Instruction Notes
**Step 1 — Introduce the rainbow method.**
Say: "In Lesson 1, we found factor pairs by building arrays and testing numbers. Today we will learn the rainbow method — a step-by-step system that makes sure we never miss a pair."

Write 24 at the top of the board.

Say: "We want to find ALL factor pairs of 24. Here is how we do it systematically."

**Step 2 — Demonstrate the T-chart approach.**
Draw a T-chart on the board:
| Small Factor | Large Factor |
|---|---|

Say: "We start with 1. Is 1 a factor of 24? Yes — 1 is a factor of every number. 24 / 1 = 24. So our first pair is 1 and 24."

Write: 1 | 24

Say: "Next, we try 2. Is 2 a factor of 24? 24 / 2 = 12, no remainder. Yes! Our pair is 2 and 12."

Write: 2 | 12

Say: "Next, try 3. 24 / 3 = 8. Yes! Pair: 3 and 8."

Write: 3 | 8

Say: "Next, try 4. 24 / 4 = 6. Yes! Pair: 4 and 6."

Write: 4 | 6

Say: "Next, try 5. 24 / 5 = 4 R4. There is a remainder, so 5 is NOT a factor. Skip it."

Say: "Next would be 6. But look — 6 already appears in our chart as the partner of 4. When the number we are testing shows up as a partner we already wrote down, we know we have found ALL the factor pairs. We can stop!"

**Step 3 — Show the rainbow visual.**
Write the factors of 24 in order: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24.
Draw arcs connecting the pairs: 1-24, 2-12, 3-8, 4-6.

Say: "See how the arcs look like a rainbow? That is why it is called the rainbow method. The small factors are on the left, and the large factors are on the right, and each pair connects across."

**Step 4 — State the stopping rule clearly.**
Say: "Here is the rule for when to stop: keep testing numbers in order starting from 1. When you reach a number that is bigger than or equal to its quotient (the answer when you divide), you can stop. For 24, when we tested 4, we got 24 / 4 = 6, and 4 < 6, so we kept going. If we tested 5, we got a remainder. If we tested 6, 24 / 6 = 4, and 6 > 4 — the small factor is now bigger than the large factor, which means we have crossed the middle. All remaining pairs are repeats."

### Worked Examples
**Example A:** Find all factor pairs of 30.
- Test 1: 30 / 1 = 30. Pair: (1, 30)
- Test 2: 30 / 2 = 15. Pair: (2, 15)
- Test 3: 30 / 3 = 10. Pair: (3, 10)
- Test 4: 30 / 4 = 7 R2. Not a factor.
- Test 5: 30 / 5 = 6. Pair: (5, 6)
- Test 6: 30 / 6 = 5. But 6 > 5, so we have crossed the middle. Stop!
- Factor pairs of 30: (1, 30), (2, 15), (3, 10), (5, 6)
- All factors in order: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30

**Example B:** Find all factor pairs of 48.
- Test 1: 48 / 1 = 48. Pair: (1, 48)
- Test 2: 48 / 2 = 24. Pair: (2, 24)
- Test 3: 48 / 3 = 16. Pair: (3, 16)
- Test 4: 48 / 4 = 12. Pair: (4, 12)
- Test 5: 48 / 5 = 9 R3. Not a factor.
- Test 6: 48 / 6 = 8. Pair: (6, 8)
- Test 7: 48 / 7 = 6 R6. Not a factor.
- Test 8: 48 / 8 = 6. But 8 > 6, so we crossed the middle. Stop!
- Factor pairs of 48: (1, 48), (2, 24), (3, 16), (4, 12), (6, 8)
- All factors in order: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 48

**Example C:** Find all factor pairs of 23.
- Test 1: 23 / 1 = 23. Pair: (1, 23)
- Test 2: 23 / 2 = 11 R1. Not a factor.
- Test 3: 23 / 3 = 7 R2. Not a factor.
- Test 4: 23 / 4 = 5 R3. Not a factor.
- Test 5: 23 / 5 = 4 R3. Not a factor. And 5 > 4, so we crossed the middle. Stop!
- Factor pairs of 23: (1, 23)
- All factors: 1, 23
- Note: 23 has only one factor pair! Numbers like this are special — we will name them in Lesson 3.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Use the rainbow method (T-chart) to find all factor pairs. Students work on boards; facilitator circulates.

1. **Find all factor pairs of 20.**
   Facilitator prompt: "Start your T-chart. Test 1, 2, 3, 4... When do you stop?"
   Answer: (1, 20), (2, 10), (4, 5). Stop at 5 because 5 = 5 (equal to its partner, which means we hit the middle). Factors: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20.

2. **Find all factor pairs of 32.**
   Facilitator prompt: "Test each number carefully. Remember to divide and check for remainders."
   Answer: (1, 32), (2, 16), (4, 8). Factors: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32.

3. **Find all factor pairs of 40.**
   Answer: (1, 40), (2, 20), (4, 10), (5, 8). Factors: 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20, 40.

4. **Find all factor pairs of 17.**
   Answer: (1, 17). Factors: 1, 17.

5. **Find all factor pairs of 50.**
   Answer: (1, 50), (2, 25), (5, 10). Factors: 1, 2, 5, 10, 25, 50.

6. **Which number above had the most factor pairs? Which had the fewest?**
   Answer: 40 had the most (4 pairs). 17 had the fewest (1 pair).

**Facilitator tip:** Watch for students who stop testing too early. Encourage them to keep going until the test number exceeds the quotient.

## Assessment Check
1. Use the rainbow method to find all factor pairs of 36. Show your T-chart.
2. How do you know when you have found ALL the factor pairs?

**Target response:**
1. Factor pairs of 36: (1, 36), (2, 18), (3, 12), (4, 9), (6, 6). Factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36.
2. You know you have found all factor pairs when the number you are testing becomes larger than the quotient (or when you reach a factor paired with itself, like 6 x 6 = 36). After that point, all pairs would be repeats of pairs already found.

## Extension
- **Big number challenge:** Find all factor pairs of 96. (Answer: (1, 96), (2, 48), (3, 32), (4, 24), (6, 16), (8, 12) — 6 factor pairs, 12 total factors.)
- **Prediction:** Before finding the factor pairs, predict whether the number will have an odd or even number of total factors. Test your prediction for 16, 25, and 36. What do these numbers have in common? (They are all perfect squares and have an odd number of factors.)
