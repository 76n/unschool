# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 3, Lesson 9

## Topic
Pattern Relationships — Comparing Two Related Patterns

## Learning Objectives
- Generate two number patterns using two given rules and the same starting point
- Organize corresponding terms of two patterns in a table
- Identify and describe the relationship between corresponding terms in two patterns
- Express the relationship in words and as a mathematical operation

## Prerequisites
- Identifying and generating number patterns from a rule (Lesson 8)
- Understanding of multiplication as repeated addition
- Comfort comparing numbers and using division

## Materials Needed
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper
- Rulers (for drawing tables)
- Grid paper (optional)

**Standards:** 4.OA.5 — Generate a number or shape pattern that follows a given rule. Identify apparent features of the pattern that were not explicit in the rule itself.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review pattern generation and set up the idea of comparing two patterns.

1. Generate the first 6 terms of each pattern:
   - Pattern A: Start at 0. Rule: add 2. (0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10)
   - Pattern B: Start at 0. Rule: add 6. (0, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30)
2. Ask: "Look at the corresponding terms — the 1st term and the 1st term, the 2nd and the 2nd, and so on. What do you notice?"
3. Let students discuss. Guide them toward: "Each term in Pattern B is 3 times the corresponding term in Pattern A."
4. Say: "Today we will explore relationships like this. When two patterns share a starting point but use different rules, there is often a clear relationship between their corresponding terms."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
### Key Vocabulary
- **Corresponding terms:** Terms in the same position in two different patterns. In Pattern A: 2, 4, 6 and Pattern B: 10, 20, 30, the corresponding terms are 2 and 10 (both 1st), 4 and 20 (both 2nd), 6 and 30 (both 3rd).
- **Relationship:** A mathematical connection between corresponding terms, such as "multiply by 5" or "add 8."

### Instruction Notes
**Step 1 — Set up two patterns with a clear relationship.**
Say: "Let's generate two patterns that both start at 0."

Write on the board:
- Pattern A: Start at 0, rule: add 3. Terms: 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15
- Pattern B: Start at 0, rule: add 9. Terms: 0, 9, 18, 27, 36, 45

**Step 2 — Organize in a table.**
Say: "Let's put the corresponding terms side by side in a table."

| Term Number | Pattern A (add 3) | Pattern B (add 9) |
|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1st | 0 | 0 |
| 2nd | 3 | 9 |
| 3rd | 6 | 18 |
| 4th | 9 | 27 |
| 5th | 12 | 36 |
| 6th | 15 | 45 |

**Step 3 — Find the relationship.**
Say: "Now let's compare corresponding terms. Look at the 2nd terms: A is 3, B is 9. What operation takes me from 3 to 9?" (Multiply by 3.)

Say: "Check with the 3rd terms: A is 6, B is 18. Does 6 x 3 = 18?" (Yes!)

Say: "Check with the 4th terms: A is 9, B is 27. Does 9 x 3 = 27?" (Yes!)

Say: "The relationship is: each term in Pattern B is 3 times the corresponding term in Pattern A. We can write this as: B = A x 3."

**Step 4 — Explain why this makes sense.**
Say: "Why is it 3 times? Pattern A adds 3 each time. Pattern B adds 9 each time. Since 9 = 3 x 3, Pattern B is growing 3 times as fast as Pattern A. So at every step, B is 3 times A."

**Step 5 — Try a different type of relationship.**
Write:
- Pattern A: Start at 0, rule: add 2. Terms: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10
- Pattern B: Start at 0, rule: add 5. Terms: 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25

| Term Number | Pattern A (add 2) | Pattern B (add 5) |
|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1st | 0 | 0 |
| 2nd | 2 | 5 |
| 3rd | 4 | 10 |
| 4th | 6 | 15 |
| 5th | 8 | 20 |
| 6th | 10 | 25 |

Say: "Look at the 3rd terms: 4 and 10. Is 10 a whole-number multiple of 4?" (No, 10/4 = 2.5.)

Say: "This relationship is a bit different. But notice: 5/2 = 2.5. The ratio between the rules (5 and 2) gives us the relationship between corresponding terms. For now, we can describe this in words: each term in Pattern B is 2 and a half times the corresponding term in Pattern A. Or we can say: if we double Pattern A's term and add Pattern A's term divided by 2, we get Pattern B."

Say: "It is often easier to see the relationship when the rules divide evenly. Let's focus on examples where they do."

### Worked Examples
**Example A:** Compare these patterns.
- Pattern A: Start at 0, rule: add 4. Terms: 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20
- Pattern B: Start at 0, rule: add 12. Terms: 0, 12, 24, 36, 48, 60

| Term # | A | B |
|--------|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 4 | 12 |
| 3 | 8 | 24 |
| 4 | 12 | 36 |
| 5 | 16 | 48 |
| 6 | 20 | 60 |

Relationship: Each term in B is 3 times the corresponding term in A. (12/4=3, 24/8=3, 36/12=3, etc.)

**Example B:** Compare these patterns.
- Pattern A: Start at 0, rule: add 5. Terms: 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25
- Pattern B: Start at 0, rule: add 10. Terms: 0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50

| Term # | A | B |
|--------|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 5 | 10 |
| 3 | 10 | 20 |
| 4 | 15 | 30 |
| 5 | 20 | 40 |
| 6 | 25 | 50 |

Relationship: Each term in B is 2 times (double) the corresponding term in A. (10/5=2, 20/10=2, 30/15=2, etc.)

**Example C:** Compare these patterns.
- Pattern A: Start at 1, rule: add 3. Terms: 1, 4, 7, 10, 13, 16
- Pattern B: Start at 2, rule: add 6. Terms: 2, 8, 14, 20, 26, 32

| Term # | A | B |
|--------|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 4 | 8 |
| 3 | 7 | 14 |
| 4 | 10 | 20 |
| 5 | 13 | 26 |
| 6 | 16 | 32 |

Relationship: Each term in B is 2 times the corresponding term in A. (2/1=2, 8/4=2, 14/7=2, 20/10=2, etc.)
Note: This works because both the starting numbers AND the rules have the same "times 2" relationship (2 = 1 x 2 and 6 = 3 x 2).

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Work through these problems as a class.

1. **Generate both patterns and find the relationship.**
   Pattern A: Start at 0, rule: add 2. Pattern B: Start at 0, rule: add 8.
   Facilitator prompt: "Write the first 6 terms of each. Put them in a table. Divide each B term by its A term."
   Answer: A: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. B: 0, 8, 16, 24, 32, 40. Each term in B is 4 times the corresponding term in A.

2. **Generate both patterns and find the relationship.**
   Pattern A: Start at 0, rule: add 6. Pattern B: Start at 0, rule: add 3.
   Facilitator prompt: "This time, Pattern A grows faster. How does B relate to A?"
   Answer: A: 0, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30. B: 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15. Each term in B is half the corresponding term in A (or A is 2 times B).

3. **Given the table, describe the relationship.**

   | Term # | A | B |
   |--------|---|---|
   | 1 | 0 | 0 |
   | 2 | 1 | 5 |
   | 3 | 2 | 10 |
   | 4 | 3 | 15 |
   | 5 | 4 | 20 |

   Answer: Each term in B is 5 times the corresponding term in A. Rule for A: add 1. Rule for B: add 5.

4. **Predict the 8th term of each pattern.**
   Pattern A: Start at 0, rule: add 3. Pattern B: Start at 0, rule: add 12.
   Facilitator prompt: "You could extend the pattern to the 8th term, or use the relationship to predict."
   Answer: A's 8th term: 0 + 3 x 7 = 21. B's 8th term: 0 + 12 x 7 = 84. Check: 84 = 21 x 4. The relationship holds: B = 4 x A.

5. **True or false: If Pattern A uses "add 3" and Pattern B uses "add 9," then each term in B will be 3 times the corresponding term in A (assuming both start at 0).**
   Answer: True. Since 9 = 3 x 3, and both start at 0, each term in B is 3 times the corresponding term in A.

**Facilitator tip:** When students struggle to see the relationship, have them write the division B / A for each pair of corresponding terms (skipping the 0/0 pair). If the result is the same each time, that number IS the relationship.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)
### Part A: Generate and Compare
For each pair, write the first 6 terms, organize in a table, and describe the relationship.

1. Pattern A: Start at 0, rule: add 1. Pattern B: Start at 0, rule: add 7.
2. Pattern A: Start at 0, rule: add 4. Pattern B: Start at 0, rule: add 8.
3. Pattern A: Start at 0, rule: add 10. Pattern B: Start at 0, rule: add 5.

### Part B: Describe the Relationship

| Term # | A | B |
|--------|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 3 | 12 |
| 3 | 6 | 24 |
| 4 | 9 | 36 |

2.
| Term # | A | B |
|--------|---|---|

### Part C: Word Problems
1. Ava earns $4 each day for walking a neighbor's dog. Her friend Ben earns $12 each day for mowing lawns. Both start with $0. After 5 days, how much does each person have? What is the relationship between their earnings at each day?

2. A snail travels 2 inches per minute. A beetle travels 6 inches per minute. After 7 minutes, how far has each traveled? How many times farther has the beetle gone compared to the snail?

### Answer Key

**Part A:**
1. A: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. B: 0, 7, 14, 21, 28, 35. Relationship: Each term in B is 7 times the corresponding term in A.
2. A: 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20. B: 0, 8, 16, 24, 32, 40. Relationship: Each term in B is 2 times (double) the corresponding term in A.
3. A: 0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50. B: 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25. Relationship: Each term in B is half the corresponding term in A (or each term in A is 2 times B).

**Part B:**
1. B = 4 x A. Each term in B is 4 times the corresponding term in A. (12/3=4, 24/6=4, 36/9=4.)
2. B = A / 4. Each term in B is one-fourth the corresponding term in A. (2/8 = 1/4, 4/16 = 1/4, 6/24 = 1/4.) Or equivalently: A = 4 x B.

**Part C:**
1. Ava (add $4): $0, $4, $8, $12, $16, $20. Ben (add $12): $0, $12, $24, $36, $48, $60. After 5 days, Ava has $20 and Ben has $60. Relationship: Ben's total is always 3 times Ava's total ($12/$4 = 3).

2. Snail (add 2): 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 inches. Beetle (add 6): 0, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42 inches. After 7 minutes, the snail has traveled 14 inches and the beetle has traveled 42 inches. The beetle has gone 3 times as far as the snail (42/14 = 3), because the beetle's speed is 3 times the snail's speed (6/2 = 3).

## Assessment Check
1. Pattern A: Start at 0, rule: add 5. Pattern B: Start at 0, rule: add 15. What is the relationship between corresponding terms?
2. If the 10th term of Pattern A is 50, what is the 10th term of Pattern B?

**Target response:**
1. Each term in Pattern B is 3 times the corresponding term in Pattern A, because 15 / 5 = 3.
2. The 10th term of B = 50 x 3 = 150.

## Extension
- **Create your own:** Write two patterns that have a "times 5" relationship. Give them to a partner and see if they can find the relationship.
- **Going backward:** If corresponding terms in two patterns have a "times 4" relationship, and Pattern B uses "add 20," what rule does Pattern A use? (add 5, because 20 / 4 = 5.)
