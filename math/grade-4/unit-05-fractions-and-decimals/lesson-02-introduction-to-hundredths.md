# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 5, Lesson 2

## Topic
Introduction to Hundredths

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Divide a whole into 100 equal parts and name each part as one hundredth (1/100)
- Shade a 10×10 grid to represent any fraction with denominator 100
- Recognize that 10/100 equals 1/10 (ten hundredths make one tenth)
- Write the fraction form for a given number of hundredths

## Prerequisites
- Can represent fractions with denominator 10 using a 10×10 grid and number line (Lesson 1)
- Understands the numerator/denominator relationship
- Knows that 10 × 10 = 100

## Materials Needed
- 10×10 grid paper (at least 4 grids)
- Tenths strip from Lesson 1
- Colored pencils (two contrasting colors)
- Number line template: 0 to 1 with 100 marks (or 0 to 1 with 10 marks that the student subdivides)
- Pencil and paper
- (Optional) Play money: 10 dimes and 10 pennies

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review tenths and connect to today's finer partitioning.

1. "How many equal parts are in one whole on our 10×10 grid when we count rows?" (10 rows → 10 tenths)
2. "Shade 4/10 on a 10×10 grid." (Student shades 4 full rows)
3. "If I shade one complete row, what fraction of the whole grid is that?" (1/10)
4. "How many small squares are in one complete row?" (10)
5. "How many small squares are in the whole grid?" (100)

Tell the student: "Today we are going to zoom in on those small squares and give them their own fraction name."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build the meaning of hundredths as individual cells on the 10×10 grid, then establish the 10/100 = 1/10 relationship.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Hundredth:** One of one hundred equal parts of a whole. Written as 1/100.
- **10×10 grid:** A square divided into 100 equal small squares — each small square is 1/100 of the whole.
- **Ten hundredths (10/100):** Equal to one tenth (1/10). Ten small squares = one full row.

### Instruction Notes

**Concrete phase (10×10 grid as hundredths):**
Return to the 10×10 grid. "Last lesson we looked at whole rows — each row was 1/10. Today we look at each individual small square. How many small squares total? 100. So each small square is 1/100 of the whole grid."

Shade 1 small square: "This is 1/100." Shade 5 small squares in a scattered pattern: "This is 5/100." Ask the student to shade 25 individual cells: "What fraction is shaded?" (25/100)

**Discovering 10/100 = 1/10:**
Using two colors: Shade exactly 1 full row in Color A. Count the cells together: 10 cells.
"I shaded 1 full row. As a tenths fraction, that is 1/10. But each cell is 1/100. How many cells did I shade?" (10) "So 1/10 and 10/100 represent the same amount of shading — the same amount of the whole grid."

Write side by side: 1/10 = 10/100

Ask: "If 2 full rows are shaded, what fraction is that in tenths? In hundredths?" (2/10 = 20/100)
Ask: "If 7 full rows are shaded?" (7/10 = 70/100)

**Money connection (optional but recommended):**
"A dollar is our whole. A dime is worth 10 cents. There are 10 dimes in a dollar, so each dime = 1/10 of a dollar = 10/100. A penny is worth 1 cent. There are 100 pennies in a dollar, so each penny = 1/100 of a dollar."
- Show 3 dimes and 4 pennies: "How many pennies total?" (34) "So this is 34/100 of a dollar."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Shade a 10×10 grid to show 37/100. Write the fraction.
- Shade 3 complete rows (= 30/100) plus 7 additional individual cells (= 7/100).
- Total: 37 cells shaded. Fraction: 37/100.

**Example 2:** A 10×10 grid has 52 cells shaded. What fraction is shaded?
- Count: 52 cells out of 100 total.
- Fraction: 52/100.

**Example 3:** How many hundredths equal 4/10?
- 4 full rows = 4 × 10 = 40 individual cells.
- 4/10 = 40/100.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together:

1. Shade 15/100 on a 10×10 grid. (Tip: shade 1 full row = 10/100, then 5 more cells.)
2. A grid has 2 full rows and 3 extra cells shaded. What fraction is shaded? Write as a fraction with denominator 100.
3. Fill in the blank: 6/10 = ___/100
4. Fill in the blank: 50/100 = ___/10
5. There are 100 beads on a string. 43 beads are blue. What fraction of the beads are blue?

**Facilitation questions:**
- "How many cells are in one full row? So if I shade 5 rows, how many cells is that?"
- "6/10 means 6 rows. How many cells is 6 rows?"
- "50/100 means 50 cells. How many full rows is 50 cells?"

---

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Shade a 10×10 grid to show each fraction:
   a) 9/100     b) 40/100     c) 75/100     d) 100/100
2. Write the fraction with denominator 100 for each shaded grid:
   a) 1 row and 7 extra cells shaded
   b) 4 rows shaded
   c) 8 rows and 2 extra cells shaded
3. Fill in the blank:
   a) 3/10 = ___/100
   b) ___/10 = 80/100
   c) 5/10 = ___/100
4. A classroom has 100 tiles on the floor. 62 tiles are white. What fraction of the tiles are white?

**Stretch:**
5. Draw a 10×10 grid. Shade 30/100 in blue and 45/100 in red. What fraction of the grid is unshaded?
6. Order from least to greatest: 90/100, 9/100, 50/100, 1/100, 99/100
7. Marcus has 100 trading cards. 1/10 are rare. How many cards are rare? Write your answer as a fraction with denominator 100 as well.

**Challenge:**
8. A 10×10 grid is shaded. One third of the shaded cells are in the top half and two thirds are in the bottom half. Exactly 60 cells are shaded. How many are shaded in the top half? In the bottom half?
9. If I shade some cells so that exactly half the grid is shaded, what fraction is shaded? Write it two ways (as tenths and as hundredths).
10. What fraction is the same as 10/10? Can you write that as hundredths too?

### Interleaved Review
R1. *(Unit 4 — Comparing Fractions)* Which is larger: 3/8 or 3/5? Explain how you know.
R2. *(Unit 2 — Division)* 84 ÷ 4 = ___
R3. *(Unit 3 — Factors)* Is 36 prime or composite? How do you know?

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. Shade a 10×10 grid to show 48/100.
2. Fill in: 7/10 = ___/100
3. A 10×10 grid has 5 full rows shaded. Write the fraction two ways: as tenths and as hundredths.

**Mastery = all 3 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the physical action of shading: "Count the cells as you shade them. Stop when you reach 48. Now count all the cells in the grid — that's your denominator."
- For the 7/10 = ___/100: "How many cells in 7 rows? Count them: 7 × 10 = 70."
- Use dimes and pennies if available: "7 dimes = how many pennies?"

### If Mastery Is Met
- Ask: "What is 10/10 as a fraction with denominator 100? What does 100/100 mean?" (It equals 1 whole)
- Preview: "We know 3/10 = 30/100 and 3/10 can also be written as a decimal — we'll learn that in Lesson 4."

## Extension (Optional)
**Hundredths scavenger hunt:** Find something divided into 100 equal parts (a centimeter ruler has 100 millimeters in 10 cm, a dollar has 100 cents, graph paper). For each, write a fraction with denominator 100 to represent a portion.

**Estimation:** Shade a 10×10 grid with approximately 33/100 without counting every cell. Then count. How close were you? Can you develop a strategy for estimating?

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- The key conceptual leap today is that the 10×10 grid serves double duty: whole rows represent tenths, individual cells represent hundredths. Students need to internalize that the same grid can be viewed at both scales.
- The relationship 10/100 = 1/10 is foundational for everything in this unit. Do not rush past it.
- If using play money: the dime/penny connection is very powerful. A student who understands "10 pennies = 1 dime" already understands 10/100 = 1/10 in their bones.
- Some students may try to write 10/100 as "1/10" automatically — that is correct and should be praised. Ask them to explain why.
- Do not introduce decimal notation yet. Keep everything in fraction form.
