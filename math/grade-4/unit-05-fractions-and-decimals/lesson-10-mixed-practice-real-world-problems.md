# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 5, Lesson 10

## Topic
Mixed Practice and Real-World Decimal Problems

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Apply fraction-decimal knowledge to solve real-world problems involving money and measurement
- Choose the most efficient representation (fraction or decimal) based on context
- Demonstrate fluency with all unit skills: converting, comparing, ordering, placing on number line, and adding
- Explain reasoning clearly using mathematical vocabulary from the unit

## Prerequisites
- All skills from Lessons 1–9: tenths, hundredths, decimal notation, conversion, number line, comparison, addition of /10 and /100 fractions

## Materials Needed
- 10×10 grid paper (2–3 grids; mostly used for checking today)
- Number line (for reference)
- Play money or coin images (dimes and pennies)
- Ruler (centimeter side)
- Pencil and paper

**Purpose:** Quick review of all major unit skills before applying them in context.

**Rapid-fire oral review (1 minute each topic):**

2. "Write 0.34 as a fraction." (34/100)
3. "Compare: 0.6 ___ 0.59." (0.6 > 0.59)

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model problem-solving in money and measurement contexts; demonstrate how to select and apply the right tool for each problem type.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Price:** The cost of an item, usually expressed as a decimal (e.g., $0.75).
- **Change:** The amount returned after paying more than the price (difference between amount paid and price).
- **Measurement:** Length, mass, or capacity expressed with a unit; often uses decimals (e.g., 0.5 meters).
- **Estimate:** An approximate answer, useful for checking whether an exact answer is reasonable.

### Instruction Notes

**Money context — worked carefully:**
"Money is a perfect model for hundredths. The dollar is our whole (= 1). A dime is 1/10 = $0.10. A penny is 1/100 = $0.01."

Model problem: "I have $0.85. I spend $0.30. How much do I have left?"
- Represent: $0.85 = 85/100; $0.30 = 30/100
- Think: this is a subtraction problem. 85/100 − 30/100 = 55/100 = $0.55
- Verify: 8 dimes + 5 pennies; remove 3 dimes → 5 dimes + 5 pennies = $0.55 ✓

Model problem: "I have 3 dimes and 7 pennies. My friend has 4 dimes and 2 pennies. Together, how much do we have?"
- Me: 37/100 = $0.37
- Friend: 42/100 = $0.42
- Together: 37/100 + 42/100 = 79/100 = $0.79
- Note: this is same-denominator addition; no conversion needed.

**Measurement context:**
"A caterpillar is 0.7 cm long. Another caterpillar is 0.35 cm long. How long are they together?"
- 0.7 cm = 7/10 cm = 70/100 cm
- 0.35 cm = 35/100 cm
- Together: 70/100 + 35/100 = 105/100 cm = 1 and 5/100 cm = 1.05 cm
- Verify: more than 1 cm total makes sense since 0.7 + 0.35 > 1.

"A bookshelf is 0.9 m wide. A book is 0.08 m wide. Can we fit 10 such books on the shelf?"
- Width of 10 books: 0.08 × 10 = 0.80 m
- Shelf: 0.9 m. Is 0.80 < 0.90? Yes.
- Yes, 10 books will fit.

**Choosing the right representation:**
"When is decimal notation most useful?" (Money, measurement, comparison)
"When is fraction notation most useful?" (Emphasizing parts of a whole, explaining equal sharing, exact fractions like 3/10)
"Mathematicians switch freely between forms — both are correct."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** At a store, juice costs $0.60 and a snack costs $0.35. What is the total cost?
- $0.60 = 60/100; $0.35 = 35/100
- 60/100 + 35/100 = 95/100 = $0.95

**Example 2:** A worm is 0.45 dm long. Another worm is 3/10 dm long. Which is longer?
- Convert 3/10 = 0.30 dm. Compare: 0.45 > 0.30. The first worm is longer.

**Example 3:** A bucket holds 1 liter. You pour in 0.4 liters, then 0.35 liters more. How much is in the bucket? Is it more than half full?
- 0.4 + 0.35: convert 0.4 = 40/100; 40/100 + 35/100 = 75/100 = 0.75 liters
- Half full = 0.5 liters. Is 0.75 > 0.5? Yes. More than half full.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through together:

1. **Money:** I have $0.45. I earn $0.25 more. How much do I have now? Write the answer as a fraction and a decimal.
2. **Comparison:** A recipe needs 0.7 cups of flour and 0.25 cups of sugar. Which ingredient is more? How do you know?
3. **Measurement:** A ribbon is 0.9 meters long. I cut off 0.35 meters. How much is left? (Hint: this is subtraction — 90/100 − 35/100.)
4. **Number line:** The 100-meter dash finish line is at 1.0. A runner's halfway point is at ___ on the decimal number line.
5. **All representations:** Write $0.60 three ways: as a fraction with denominator 100, as a fraction with denominator 10, and as a decimal.

**Facilitation questions:**
- "Which representation makes this problem easiest to solve?"
- "Does your answer make sense? Is it reasonable for the situation?"
- "Can you estimate first, then calculate?"

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. **Money:** I buy a pencil for $0.35 and an eraser for $0.20. What is the total? I pay with $1.00 (= 100/100). How much change do I get?
2. **Comparison:** Three students measured a leaf. Leo says it is 0.6 cm; Maria says it is 0.57 cm; Ben says it is 0.65 cm. Order the measurements from smallest to largest.
3. **Fractions and decimals in context:** A rain gauge collected 4/10 of an inch of rain on Monday and 7/100 of an inch on Tuesday. How much total rain fell? Write the answer as a fraction and a decimal.
4. **Number line:** Draw a number line from 0 to 1. Mark and label these amounts from a jar being filled: empty (0), quarter full (0.25), half full (0.5), three-quarters full (0.75), full (1.0).
5. **Complete the table:**
   | Fraction | Decimal | Description |
   |----------|---------|-------------|
   | 5/10 | ___ | half a dollar |
   | ___ | 0.25 | a quarter (25 cents) |
   | 75/100 | ___ | three-quarters dollar |
   | ___ | 0.10 | a dime |

**Stretch:**
6. **Story problem:** Aria has 3 dimes, 2 nickels (1 nickel = 5/100), and 4 pennies. How much money does she have in total? Write as a fraction of a dollar and as a decimal.
7. **Measurement chain:** A bug walks 0.3 m, stops, then walks 0.45 m more, then walks 5/100 m. How far did the bug walk in total?
8. **Comparison and ordering:** Four packages weigh: 0.9 kg, 0.09 kg, 0.90 kg, 0.45 kg. Order them from lightest to heaviest. Note any ties.

**Challenge:**
9. **Multi-step:** I have $1.00. I buy item A for $0.35 and item B for 4/10 of a dollar. Do I have enough for item C that costs $0.30? Show all work.
10. **Reverse problem:** The sum of two amounts is $0.83. Both amounts are multiples of $0.01 (i.e., whole hundredths). One amount has a tenths digit of 4. Find all possible pairs.
11. **Design a problem:** Write a real-world problem that requires adding a fraction with denominator 10 and a fraction with denominator 100. Swap with a classmate (or save for self-checking). Include an answer key.

### Interleaved Review
R1. *(Unit 4 — Comparing Mixed Numbers)* Which is greater: 2 and 3/5 or 2 and 7/10? Show your work.
R2. *(Unit 2 — Multi-step Division)* A baker has 144 cookies to pack in boxes of 12. How many boxes does he fill? How many cookies are left over?
R3. *(Unit 3 — Prime Factorization)* What are the prime factors of 30?

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. Sam has $0.64. He finds 3 dimes. How much does he have now? Write the answer as a decimal.
2. A stick is 0.8 m long. A second stick is 0.15 m long. Together, how long are they? Show your work using fractions with denominator 100.
3. Write one thing you feel confident about from this unit and one thing you are still working on.

**Mastery = problems 1 and 2 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- For problem 1: "3 dimes = 30/100. $0.64 = 64/100. Add: 64/100 + 30/100 = 94/100 = $0.94."
- For problem 2: "0.8 m = 8/10 m = 80/100 m. 0.15 m = 15/100 m. Add: 80/100 + 15/100 = 95/100 = 0.95 m."
- If the student struggled, identify which specific skill failed: conversion? addition? decimal notation? Target that skill before the assessment.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Ask the student to explain the unit in their own words: "What are decimals? How are they connected to fractions?"
- Encourage them to tackle the challenge problem from independent practice.
- Preview: "The unit assessment will cover all ten lessons. Let's make sure you feel ready."

## Extension (Optional)
**Real-world project:** Design a "menu" for a small imaginary store. Write 6 items with prices using decimal notation. Then create three purchase scenarios (e.g., "Buy item A and item B; how much change from $1?") with answer keys. Present the menu to a family member or solve the problems yourself.

**Recipe scaling:** Find a simple recipe. Double all fractional measurements (expressed in tenths or hundredths). Write the new amounts as both fractions and decimals.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- This is the synthesis lesson. If any gaps have appeared in Lessons 1–9, today is the last chance to address them before the assessment.
- Observe the student throughout this lesson: Can they switch between fraction and decimal representations fluidly? Do they reach for the grid or number line when uncertain? These are signs of healthy conceptual understanding.
- The money context is almost always the most intuitive. If the student struggles with any problem, rephrase it in terms of dollars and cents.
- Watch for the student who can compute correctly but cannot explain their reasoning. Require at least one or two oral explanations during guided practice: "Tell me why you converted the 0.8 to 80/100 before adding."
- The self-reflection question on the exit ticket (question 3) is valuable — listen carefully to what the student identifies as a struggle and use it to guide assessment preparation.
- After this lesson, allow one review session before the summative assessment if the student needs it.
