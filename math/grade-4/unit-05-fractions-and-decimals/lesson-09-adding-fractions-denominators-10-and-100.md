# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 5, Lesson 9

## Topic
Adding Fractions with Denominators 10 and 100

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Convert a fraction with denominator 10 to an equivalent fraction with denominator 100 in order to add it to a fraction with denominator 100 (4.NF.5)
- Add two fractions that have denominators of 10 and 100 by finding equivalent forms
- Represent the addition visually using 10×10 grids
- Simplify results when possible and express them as both fractions and decimals

## Prerequisites
- Can convert fractions between denominators 10 and 100 (Lesson 3)
- Can add fractions with like denominators (Grade 4, Unit 4)
- Can convert fractions to decimals (Lessons 4–6)

## Materials Needed
- 10×10 grid paper (3–4 grids with TWO grids side by side or one grid per addend)
- Colored pencils (two colors)
- Pencil and paper
- Place value chart (optional reference)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review like-denominator addition and the /10 to /100 conversion.

1. "Add: 3/8 + 2/8." (5/8) "Why can we add these directly?" (Same denominator.)
2. "Convert 4/10 to a fraction with denominator 100." (40/100)
3. "Convert 3/10 to hundredths." (30/100)
4. "Can you add 3/10 + 25/100 directly? Why or why not?" (No — different denominators. This leads into the lesson.)

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show that adding fractions with denominators 10 and 100 requires a conversion step first; then the addition proceeds normally as a like-denominator addition.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Like denominators:** Fractions that share the same denominator; they can be added by adding numerators.
- **Unlike denominators:** Fractions with different denominators; they must be converted to a common denominator before adding.
- **Common denominator:** A denominator shared by two fractions. For /10 and /100, we always use 100 as the common denominator.

### Instruction Notes

**Why we can't add unlike denominators directly:**
"3/10 + 25/100. If I add the numerators: 3 + 25 = 28. If I add the denominators: 10 + 100 = 110. So 28/110 — does that make sense?"

Shade a 10×10 grid: shade 3 rows (=3/10) in blue, shade 25 individual cells (=25/100) in red — but make sure they don't overlap (shade 3 rows, then 25 new cells outside those rows). Count total shaded: 30 + 25 = 55 cells = 55/100. "The correct answer is 55/100, not 28/110."

"The grid tells us the answer. But we need a procedure to get there without drawing a grid every time."

**The procedure:**
Step 1: Convert the tenths fraction to hundredths.
Step 2: Add the two fractions with denominator 100.
Step 3: Simplify if possible.

Work through 3/10 + 25/100:
- Step 1: 3/10 = 30/100
- Step 2: 30/100 + 25/100 = 55/100
- Step 3: 55/100 — can we simplify? 55 and 100 share a factor of 5: 55÷5=11, 100÷5=20 → 11/20. (Note: this simplification goes beyond the standard; accept 55/100 as the answer.)
- Decimal: 0.55

**Visual model confirmation:**
On the 10×10 grid: shade 30 cells (3 rows) in blue = 3/10 = 30/100. Shade 25 more cells in red. Total = 55 cells = 55/100. ✓

**Second example — worked together:**
6/10 + 7/100:
- Convert: 6/10 = 60/100
- Add: 60/100 + 7/100 = 67/100
- Decimal: 0.67
- Grid: shade 6 rows (60 cells) + 7 individual cells = 67 cells. ✓

**What if both are already hundredths?**
"If both fractions already have denominator 100, just add the numerators: 23/100 + 45/100 = 68/100. No conversion needed."

**What if both are tenths?**
"If both have denominator 10, add directly: 3/10 + 4/10 = 7/10. Or convert both to hundredths first: 30/100 + 40/100 = 70/100 = 7/10. Same answer."

**Connection to decimals:**
"3/10 + 25/100 = 0.3 + 0.25 = 0.55. Notice: when you align the decimal places and add, you get the same answer!"
```
  0.30
+ 0.25
------
  0.55
```
"This is because we made the denominators match — same as aligning decimal places."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 5/10 + 3/100 = ?
- 5/10 = 50/100
- 50/100 + 3/100 = 53/100
- Decimal: 0.53

**Example 2:** 2/10 + 40/100 = ?
- 2/10 = 20/100
- 20/100 + 40/100 = 60/100
- Simplify: 60/100 = 6/10 = 3/5
- Decimal: 0.60 = 0.6

**Example 3:** A recipe calls for 3/10 liter of milk and 5/100 liter of cream. How much liquid is that in total?
- 3/10 = 30/100
- 30/100 + 5/100 = 35/100
- Answer: 35/100 liter = 0.35 liter

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Convert and add:
   a) 3/10 + 6/100 = ___
   b) 8/10 + 5/100 = ___
   c) 1/10 + 1/100 = ___
   d) 5/10 + 50/100 = ___
2. Write the sum as a fraction (denominator 100) AND as a decimal:
   a) 4/10 + 7/100
   b) 9/10 + 1/100
   c) 6/10 + 20/100
3. Fill in the blank: 3/10 + ___/100 = 35/100
4. A plant grew 5/10 of a centimeter in the morning and 3/100 of a centimeter in the afternoon. How much did it grow in total? Express as a fraction and decimal.

**Stretch:**
5. A student says: "4/10 + 3/100 = 7/110 because I added the numerators and the denominators." Show the student the correct method and explain the error.
6. Find the missing addend: ___ /10 + 7/100 = 37/100
7. Can the sum of a tenths fraction and a hundredths fraction ever be greater than 1? Give an example if yes, or explain why not if no.

**Challenge:**
8. Add: 3/10 + 4/100 + 7/100. Is this possible? Show your work.
9. The sum of two fractions is 75/100. One fraction has denominator 10. What are the two fractions? Find all possible answers where both fractions are less than 1.
10. A jar holds 1 liter. Sofia pours in 4/10 liter of juice and 35/100 liter of water. Does the total fit in the jar? Show your reasoning.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. Add: 6/10 + 8/100. Write the answer as a fraction with denominator 100 and as a decimal.
2. A path is marked in tenths and hundredths. One section is 2/10 km and another is 15/100 km. What is the total length? Write as a fraction and a decimal.

**Mastery = both correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Slow down at the conversion step: "Write 6/10 in the tens-to-hundredths box: 6/10 = 60/100. Now both fractions have the same denominator. Add the numerators."
- Use the grid: shade 6 rows (60 cells) + 8 more cells = 68 cells = 68/100. Check.
- Use the decimal connection: 0.6 + 0.08 = 0.68. If the student can do decimal addition, they can verify the fraction answer.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Ask: "What if you had to add three fractions: 2/10 + 3/100 + 4/10? How would you approach it?"
- Preview Lesson 10: "We'll use everything we've learned — fractions, decimals, comparisons, and addition — to solve real-world problems."

## Extension (Optional)
**Money addition:** Express coin combinations as fractions with denominator 100, then add them. For example: 3 dimes (30/100) + 7 pennies (7/100) = 37/100 = $0.37. Add various coin combinations and verify with play money.

**Decimal addition preview:** Try adding 0.3 + 0.25 by lining up the decimal points. Then verify by fraction method. Do they match? (They should: 0.30 + 0.25 = 0.55 = 55/100.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- The core skill is procedural: convert the tenths fraction to hundredths, then add. Students who mastered Lesson 3 should find this straightforward.
- The most common error is adding numerators AND denominators (3/10 + 5/100 = 8/110). Address this immediately with the grid demonstration.
- The connection to decimal column addition is powerful and worth showing: 0.30 + 0.05 = 0.35 mirrors 30/100 + 5/100 = 35/100. "Lining up decimal points" is the same as "finding a common denominator."
- The challenge of adding three fractions (extension/challenge) can be solved by converting all to hundredths first and then adding in one step.
- Sums greater than 100/100 (greater than 1) are possible and should not be dismissed — 90/100 + 20/100 = 110/100 = 1 and 10/100. This is a natural extension.
