# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 5, Lesson 6

## Topic
Fractions to Decimals and Decimals to Fractions

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Convert any fraction with denominator 10 or 100 to its decimal equivalent
- Convert any decimal with one or two decimal places to its fraction equivalent (denominator 10 or 100)
- Explain the connection between the two representations using place value language
- Identify which representation (fraction or decimal) is more useful in a given context

## Prerequisites
- Can write decimal notation for tenths (0.n) and hundredths (0.nn) (Lessons 4–5)
- Can convert between fractions with denominator 10 and 100 (Lesson 3)
- Understands the meaning of the tenths and hundredths decimal places

## Materials Needed
- 10×10 grid paper (2–3 grids)
- Place value chart (ones | . | tenths | hundredths)
- Number line template (0 to 1, with tenths labeled)
- Pencil and paper

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Warm up both conversion directions before consolidating them.

1. "Write 4/10 as a decimal." (0.4)
2. "Write 0.83 as a fraction." (83/100)
3. "Write 9/100 as a decimal." (0.09)
4. "Write 0.6 as a fraction." (6/10)

Tell the student: "Today we bring all of this together into one fluent skill — moving freely between fractions and decimals. By the end of today, you should be able to do these conversions quickly and without needing to draw a grid every time."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Systematize the conversion procedure; address the common conversion cases including those requiring equivalent fractions; build fluency and self-checking strategies.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Convert:** To change the form of a number while keeping its value the same (e.g., from fraction to decimal).
- **Decimal equivalent:** The decimal that represents the same value as a given fraction.
- **Fraction equivalent:** The fraction that represents the same value as a given decimal.

### Instruction Notes

**Organizing the four conversion cases:**
Present a two-by-two organizer on paper:

| Direction | Tenths | Hundredths |
|-----------|--------|------------|
| Fraction → Decimal | 3/10 → 0.3 | 47/100 → 0.47 |
| Decimal → Fraction | 0.8 → 8/10 | 0.65 → 65/100 |

"Every problem we'll see today fits into one of these four boxes. Let's work through each."

**Case 1 — Fraction with denominator 10 to decimal:**
"The numerator goes in the tenths place. 7/10 → 0.7. Simple."

**Case 2 — Fraction with denominator 100 to decimal:**
"The first digit of the numerator goes in the tenths place; the second digit goes in the hundredths place. 53/100 → 0.53."
"What about single-digit numerators? 9/100: tenths place gets 0, hundredths gets 9 → 0.09."

**Case 3 — Decimal with one decimal place to fraction:**
"One decimal place = tenths. The digit becomes the numerator; denominator is 10. 0.4 → 4/10."

**Case 4 — Decimal with two decimal places to fraction:**
"Two decimal places = hundredths. Both digits together form the numerator; denominator is 100. 0.37 → 37/100."
"What about 0.40? The numerator is 40; 40/100. We can simplify: 40/100 = 4/10 (divide by 10)."

**Self-checking with the grid:**
"Any time you're unsure, sketch a 10×10 grid. Shade the amount the fraction says. Count the shaded cells — that gives you the hundredths decimal. Count full rows — that gives you the tenths."

**Converting fractions that need equivalent fractions first:**
"What about a fraction like 1/5? That's not tenths or hundredths. If we want to convert it to a decimal, we first find an equivalent fraction with denominator 10 or 100."
"1/5 = 2/10 = 0.2. How? 5 × 2 = 10, so multiply numerator by 2 also: 1 × 2 = 2. So 1/5 = 2/10 = 0.2."
"What about 3/4? 4 × 25 = 100, so 3 × 25 = 75. 3/4 = 75/100 = 0.75."

Note: This extends the skill for students who are ready. The core standard (4.NF.6) only requires denominators of 10 or 100.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Convert 8/10 to a decimal.
- 8 in the tenths place: 0.8

**Example 2:** Convert 0.07 to a fraction.
- 0 tenths, 7 hundredths: 7/100

**Example 3:** Convert 29/100 to a decimal.
- Tens digit of 29 is 2 (tenths place), ones digit of 29 is 9 (hundredths place): 0.29

**Example 4:** Convert 0.90 to a fraction in simplest form.
- 0.90 = 90/100
- Simplify: 90 ÷ 10 = 9; 100 ÷ 10 = 10 → 9/10

**Example 5:** Convert 2/5 to a decimal. (Extension)
- Need denominator 10: 2/5 × (2/2) = 4/10 = 0.4

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through together:

1. Convert to decimals: a) 5/10  b) 62/100  c) 3/100  d) 10/10
2. Convert to fractions (denominator 10 or 100): a) 0.7  b) 0.58  c) 0.04  d) 0.20
3. Fill in the table:

| Fraction | Decimal |
|----------|---------|
| 9/10 | ___ |
| ___ | 0.35 |
| 20/100 | ___ |
| ___ | 0.06 |

4. True or false: 0.3 and 30/100 are the same number. Explain.
5. A recipe calls for 3/4 cup of sugar. Can you write this as a decimal? If yes, how?

**Facilitation questions:**
- "What does the denominator of the fraction tell you about where to look in the decimal? (10 → tenths place; 100 → two places)"
- "When going decimal to fraction, how do you know whether to use 10 or 100 as the denominator?"
- "Can every fraction be written as a decimal with one or two decimal places? Why or why not?"

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Convert each fraction to a decimal:
   a) 2/10     b) 84/100     c) 7/100     d) 30/100     e) 10/100
2. Convert each decimal to a fraction (simplify if you can):
   a) 0.6     b) 0.15     c) 0.80     d) 0.03     e) 0.50
3. Complete the table:

| Fraction | Decimal |
|----------|---------|
| 5/10 | ___ |
| ___ | 0.42 |
| 18/100 | ___ |
| ___ | 0.90 |
| 1/100 | ___ |

4. A student shaded 34 cells on a 10×10 grid. Write the fraction and the decimal.
5. Write three equivalent names for 0.70: as a decimal (different form), as a fraction with denominator 100, and as a fraction with denominator 10.

**Stretch:**
6. Convert these to decimals using equivalent fractions (change denominator to 10 or 100 first):
   a) 1/2     b) 4/5     c) 1/4     d) 3/20
7. A tile is 0.45 meters wide. Write this as a fraction of a meter. Is this greater or less than 1/2 meter?
8. Put in order from least to greatest and write each in both forms (fraction and decimal): 0.3, 17/100, 0.9, 55/100

**Challenge:**
9. Can you convert 1/3 to a decimal with one or two decimal places? Why or why not? What happens if you try to find 1/3 as tenths? As hundredths?
10. I am a decimal. My tenths digit is three times my hundredths digit. My value is less than 0.5. Write all possible decimals that match this description.
11. A fraction has denominator 100. When written as a decimal, both decimal digits are the same. List all possible fractions.

### Interleaved Review
R1. *(Unit 4 — Subtracting Fractions)* 7/8 − 3/8 = ___
R2. *(Unit 2 — Long Division)* 156 ÷ 6 = ___
R3. *(Unit 3 — Multiples)* What is the LCM of 5 and 8?

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. Convert 67/100 to a decimal.
2. Convert 0.08 to a fraction.
3. Ella says 0.4 and 40/100 are different because "40 is bigger than 4." Write a two-sentence explanation of why she is wrong.

**Mastery = all 3 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Use the place value chart for every conversion until the student is confident: fill in the chart, read the decimal, match to fraction.
- For the confusion between 0.4 and 40/100: draw the 10×10 grid. Shade 4 rows. Count cells (40). Write both: "4/10 = 40/100 = 0.4. Same shading!"
- Do a quick verbal drill: say a fraction, have the student immediately say the decimal and vice versa. Keep it oral before written.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Ask: "Can you think of a fraction that cannot easily become a decimal with just tenths or hundredths?" (1/3, 1/7, 2/9 — these will be explored in higher grades)
- Preview Lesson 7: "Now that you can convert between fractions and decimals, we'll place decimals on the number line — which means you'll need both skills."

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Conversion fluency game:** Create a set of 20 cards — 10 with fractions (mix of /10 and /100), 10 with their decimal equivalents. Shuffle and match them. Time yourself.

**Decimal detective:** Look at a price list, menu, or nutrition label. For each decimal you find, write the equivalent fraction. Identify whether it is a tenths or hundredths decimal.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- This is a consolidation lesson. The student has seen all four cases in Lessons 1–5; today formalizes the procedure and builds fluency.
- The most important outcome is automatic, confident conversion in both directions. If the student hesitates, praise the thinking and keep drilling.
- The extension involving 1/2, 1/4, 2/5 (denominators other than 10/100) is valuable but not required for Grade 4 standards. Use it for students who are ready.
- The challenge question about 1/3 is a genuine conceptual problem — 1/3 cannot be written exactly as a tenths or hundredths decimal. Do not force a resolution; instead treat it as a curiosity: "Some fractions become repeating decimals — you'll study those in middle school."
- Keep the place value chart available as a scaffold throughout the lesson. Gradually encourage students to internalize it rather than drawing it each time.

10. Tenths digit = 3 × hundredths digit; value < 0.5:
    - If hundredths = 1, tenths = 3 → 0.31 ✓
    - If hundredths = 0, tenths = 0 → 0.00 (equals 0, less than 0.5, but trivial)
    Valid answers: 0.31 (and 0.00 as a trivial case)
11. Both digits the same: 0.11 = 11/100; 0.22 = 22/100; 0.33 = 33/100; 0.44 = 44/100; 0.55 = 55/100; 0.66 = 66/100; 0.77 = 77/100; 0.88 = 88/100; 0.99 = 99/100. Nine fractions.
