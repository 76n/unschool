# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 6, Lesson 10

## Topic
Mixed Geometry Review and Problem Solving

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Apply skills from the entire unit to multi-step geometry problems
- Use geometric vocabulary precisely across all unit topics
- Connect multiple concepts (e.g., classify a shape AND find its area; measure an angle AND find a missing angle)
- Demonstrate mastery of unit concepts in preparation for the summative assessment

## Prerequisites
- All concepts from Lessons 1–9:
  - Points, lines, rays, segments (L1)
  - Angle classification (L2)
  - Angle measurement (L3)
  - Angle addition/subtraction (L4)
  - Triangle classification (L5)
  - Quadrilateral classification (L6)
  - Line symmetry (L7)
  - Area and perimeter of rectangles (L8)
  - Area of composite shapes (L9)

## Materials Needed
- Protractors (for angle problems)
- Rulers (for area/perimeter and drawing)
- Graph paper
- Colored pencils
- Review activity materials (see structure below)
- Index card vocabulary reference (optional: allow for review day)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Gauge retention across all unit topics with a rapid review circuit.

Post 6 questions around the room (or display in sequence). Students work quickly — 30 seconds per question:

1. Draw a ray and label it ray AB. (L1)
2. What angle type has a measure between 90° and 180°? (L2)
3. A protractor reads 65°. Classify this angle. (L3)
4. Two angles sum to 90°. One is 34°. What is the other? (L4)
5. A triangle has angles 30°, 60°, 90°. What type of triangle is it? (L5)
6. What shape has 2 pairs of parallel sides and 4 right angles but NOT necessarily 4 equal sides? (L6 — rectangle)

Review answers as a class. Use results to identify which topics need more practice during the lesson.

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect all unit concepts explicitly; model multi-step problem solving using geometry.

### Key Vocabulary Review

Rapid vocabulary review — ask students to give a definition or draw an example for each term:
- Line of symmetry (L7)
- Composite shape (L9)
- Perpendicular lines (L1/L6)
- Parallelogram (L6)
- Area vs. perimeter (L8)
- Equilateral triangle (L5)
- Right angle / acute angle / obtuse angle (L2)

### Instruction Notes

**Concept Connections:** Show students a single complex figure and ask multi-part questions about it:

Example figure: a rectangle ABCD with:
- Dimensions 12 cm × 8 cm
- A diagonal drawn from A to C
- The diagonal creates two triangles

Questions:
1. What shape is ABCD? (Rectangle — and also parallelogram, quadrilateral) (L6)
2. What is the area of rectangle ABCD? (96 cm²) (L8)
3. What is the perimeter of ABCD? (40 cm) (L8)
4. How many lines of symmetry does ABCD have? (2 — horizontal and vertical) (L7)
5. The diagonal AC creates two triangles. Classify each triangle by angles. (Each has one right angle → right triangles) (L5)
6. If angle BAC = 37°, what is angle BCA? (90° + 37° + n = 180°; n = 53°) (L4/L5)

**This models how geometry concepts connect to each other — a single figure can generate questions across all lesson topics.**

### Worked Examples

**Multi-step Problem 1:** An L-shaped park has the following dimensions: full rectangle 20 m × 15 m, with a 7 m × 9 m corner removed.
- Area: 20 × 15 − 7 × 9 = 300 − 63 = 237 m²
- A path runs along two full sides (the 20 m sides). What is the total path length? 20 + 20 = 40 m.
- Does the L-shape have any lines of symmetry? (Only if the cutout is centered on an axis — if corner, then no.)

**Multi-step Problem 2:** The angles of a triangle are in a 2:3:4 ratio.
- Total parts: 2 + 3 + 4 = 9 parts. Total degrees: 180°.
- One part = 180° ÷ 9 = 20°.
- Angles: 2×20 = 40°, 3×20 = 60°, 4×20 = 80°.
- All acute → acute scalene triangle.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Gallery Walk Format:** Post 8 problem stations around the room. Students rotate in pairs, spending about 1 minute per station. They record answers on their own paper.

**Station 1 (L1):** "Label these figures: two endpoints connected by a straight path; a straight path extending forever in both directions."
(Segment; Line)

**Station 2 (L2/L3):** "Measure this angle with your protractor. Classify it." (Pre-drawn angle — approximately 128°)
(Obtuse; approximately 128°)

**Station 3 (L4):** "A straight angle is divided by a ray. One part is 71°. What is the other?"
(109°)

**Station 4 (L5):** "A triangle has sides 7 cm, 7 cm, and 10 cm, and angles 66°, 66°, 48°. Classify it."
(Isosceles, acute)

**Station 5 (L6):** "Name the most specific quadrilateral: 4 equal sides, 4 right angles."
(Square)

**Station 6 (L7):** "How many lines of symmetry does a regular hexagon have?"
(6)

**Station 7 (L8):** "A rectangle is 14 m long and 9 m wide. Find area and perimeter."
(Area = 126 m²; Perimeter = 46 m)

**Station 8 (L9):** "L-shape: full rectangle 10 m × 8 m, notch 4 m × 3 m. Find area."
(10×8 − 4×3 = 80−12 = 68 m²)

Discuss answers as a class after the gallery walk.

## Independent Practice (13 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. A parallelogram has angles of 65° and 115°.
   a. Verify the angles sum to 360°. (65+115+65+115 = 360 ✓)
   b. What type of parallelogram is it, if any? (Not a rectangle or rhombus — just a parallelogram)
   c. Does it have a line of symmetry? (A non-rectangular parallelogram has no lines of symmetry)

2. Draw a right isosceles triangle. Label all three angles. Find the missing angle if one acute angle is 45°.
   (Third angle: 180° − 90° − 45° = 45°)

3. A rectangular floor is 18 m × 11 m. A square column (2 m × 2 m) stands in one corner. Find the floor area that can be tiled (excluding the column base).
   (18×11 − 2×2 = 198 − 4 = 194 m²)

4. A kite shape (informal) has a vertical line of symmetry. The top two sides each measure 5 cm and the bottom two sides each measure 8 cm. Classify the kite as specifically as possible. Does it have more than one line of symmetry?
   (A kite with perpendicular diagonals — it has 1 line of symmetry unless it is also a rhombus)

5. True or False. If false, correct the statement.
   a. Every rhombus is a square. (False — a rhombus has equal sides but does not need right angles)
   b. A right triangle can be isosceles. (True)
   c. A shape with no parallel sides can still be a quadrilateral. (True)
   d. Area is measured in square units. (True)
   e. A straight angle measures 90°. (False — a straight angle measures 180°)

**Stretch:**
6. Design a composite shape (made of two rectangles) with a total area of exactly 60 m². The shape must be L-shaped and have all whole-number dimensions. Draw and label your shape.

7. A triangular garden has angles 40°, 60°, and 80°. The gardener says it is "equilateral." Is she correct? What type is it actually?
   (No — equilateral has all 60° angles. This is an acute scalene triangle: all angles are different and all are less than 90°.)

8. An angle is twice as large as its complementary partner. (Two angles are complementary if they sum to 90°.) Find both angles.
   (n + 2n = 90°; 3n = 90°; n = 30°. Angles: 30° and 60°.)

**Challenge:**
9. A square has a perimeter of 48 cm. A second square has an area equal to the first square's area. What is the second square's perimeter? (They are the same square — same area = same side length = same perimeter. Perimeter = 48 cm, side = 12 cm, area = 144 cm².)
   Variation: If the area of the first square is 144 cm², what is the side? Perimeter? (Side = 12 cm; P = 48 cm.)

10. A regular polygon has interior angles that each measure 120°. How many sides does it have?
    (Each interior angle of a regular n-gon = (n−2)×180°/n. Setting equal to 120°: 120n = (n−2)×180; 120n = 180n−360; 360 = 60n; n = 6. It is a hexagon.)
    Note: This is enrichment beyond grade level. Provide for students who are ready.

### Interleaved Review
R1. What is 5/8 + 3/8? (Unit 4: adding fractions)
   Answer: 8/8 = 1

R2. Multiply: 203 × 14 (Unit 1: multiplication)
   Answer: 2,842

R3. Is 72 divisible by 9? How do you know? (Unit 2/3: divisibility)
   Answer: Yes — 72 ÷ 9 = 8, with no remainder; also, 7+2=9, which is divisible by 9.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

This exit ticket serves as a final self-assessment and unit assessment preview.

1. Name one concept from this unit you feel confident about.
2. Name one concept from this unit you want to review more.
3. Solve: A composite shape is made of a 9 m × 6 m rectangle and a 5 m × 4 m rectangle joined along one edge. What is the total area?
   (9×6 + 5×4 = 54 + 20 = 74 m²)

**Mastery = student correctly computes 74 m². The self-assessment items are informational — use them to plan any final targeted review before the unit assessment.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify the specific area(s) of weakness from exit ticket item 2 and the gallery walk results
- Provide targeted practice problems focusing on 2–3 highest-priority skills
- Consider delaying the unit assessment by one lesson and using the time for targeted small-group review

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for the unit assessment
- Encourage students to review their exit tickets from all 10 lessons as a self-study tool
- Pose open-ended: "Create your own geometry problem that uses at least 3 concepts from this unit. Write the problem, solve it, and create an answer key."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- This is a review lesson, but it should not feel like pure drill. The gallery walk format and multi-step problems keep it engaging.
- Use exit ticket items 1 and 2 to organize any final support before the assessment. Students who identify the same weak area can work together in a small group.
- The multi-step problems are a key feature of this lesson — they preview the problem-solving section of the unit assessment. Emphasize the process: read carefully, identify what is being asked, choose the appropriate tool (formula, angle equation, classification), show work.
- The gallery walk can be adapted: if space is limited, use a carousel format at desks (rotate papers rather than students), or work through stations as a class projected on a screen.
- The warm-up quick-fire results give you real data about which topics most need review. Adjust the guided practice emphasis accordingly.
- If the unit assessment will be given in the next session, this lesson serves as effective test preparation without feeling like "test prep" — because it reinforces genuine understanding rather than test-taking tricks.
- Encourage students to use their vocabulary reference cards during this review lesson (if you have been building these throughout the unit). For the assessment, determine in advance whether reference materials will be permitted.
