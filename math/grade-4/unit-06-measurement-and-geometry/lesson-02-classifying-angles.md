# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 6, Lesson 2

## Topic
Classifying Angles (Right, Acute, Obtuse, Straight)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define an angle as a figure formed by two rays sharing a common endpoint (vertex)
- Classify angles as right, acute, obtuse, or straight based on their measure
- Identify and draw each type of angle
- Recognize each angle type in 2D shapes and real-world contexts

## Prerequisites
- Points, lines, rays, and segments (Lesson 1)
- Understanding that rays extend infinitely in one direction
- Informal experience with "corners" in shapes from Grade 3

## Materials Needed
- Index cards (one per student for right angle reference)
- Rulers (one per student)
- Plain white paper
- Colored pencils
- Pattern blocks or 2D shape set
- Anchor chart: angle types with benchmark diagrams

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review rays from Lesson 1 and activate prior knowledge about corners/angles.

Draw two rays on the board that share an endpoint. Ask: "What do you notice about these two rays? What shape do they make?"

Then show students two physical objects: a book opened to different angles. Ask: "Which opening is wider? Which is narrower? How could we describe the difference?"

Tell students: "Today we are going to give these openings names and learn to classify them."

Quick review: On their paper, students draw one ray starting at a point. Then draw a second ray starting at the SAME point but going in a different direction. Circulate and check that students correctly draw two rays from one shared point.

---

## Direct Instruction (13 minutes)
**Purpose:** Formally define angle and introduce the four classifications using benchmark measures.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Angle:** A figure formed by two rays that share a common endpoint.
- **Vertex:** The common endpoint of the two rays that form an angle. The plural is vertices.
- **Degrees (°):** The unit used to measure angles. A full rotation = 360°.
- **Right angle:** An angle that measures exactly 90°. Looks like the corner of a square. Marked with a small square symbol in diagrams.
- **Acute angle:** An angle that measures less than 90°. "A cute little angle" — smaller than a right angle.
- **Obtuse angle:** An angle that measures more than 90° but less than 180°. Wider than a right angle but not a straight line.
- **Straight angle:** An angle that measures exactly 180°. The two rays form a straight line.

### Instruction Notes

**Concrete:** Use index cards.
- The corner of an index card is a right angle (90°). Students will use this all unit as a reference tool.
- Hold the card corner to various angles drawn on the board — does it fit exactly? (right) Is the angle smaller? (acute) Is the angle bigger? (obtuse)

Model each angle type by opening a book:
- Barely open = acute
- Open to a "standing" position (like an L) = right angle
- Open wider than L but not flat = obtuse
- Completely flat on the desk = straight angle

**Representational:** Draw each type on the board with labels:

```
Right angle:         Acute angle:         Obtuse angle:        Straight angle:
    |                  /                   \    /               ←————————————→
    |__ (small square)  / (less than 90°)    \  / (more than 90°,less than 180°)
                      /
```

Emphasize visual benchmarks:
- Right: exactly a square corner
- Acute: smaller than a square corner (think of a slice of pie that is narrow)
- Obtuse: larger than a square corner (think of a door opened very wide)
- Straight: completely flat, like a horizon line

**Abstract:** Introduce the degree ranges:
- Acute: 0° < angle < 90°
- Right: angle = 90°
- Obtuse: 90° < angle < 180°
- Straight: angle = 180°

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Classify the angle formed by the hands of a clock at 3:00.
- The minute hand points to 12 (straight up) and the hour hand points to 3 (to the right)
- These form a right angle (exactly 90°)

**Example 2:** A triangle has three angles. Looking at the corners:
- One corner matches the index card exactly → right angle
- The other two corners are smaller than the card → both are acute angles

**Example 3:** Look at the letter "V." The angle at the bottom of the V:
- It is narrower than a square corner → acute angle

Draw additional examples: a wide open angle (obtuse), a very narrow angle (acute), and verify each against the index card corner.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these together. Students classify, then justify using the index card test or degree range.

1. Classify each angle drawn on the board:
   - Angle A: looks like an open book at about 120° → obtuse
   - Angle B: looks like a perfect square corner → right
   - Angle C: two rays forming one straight line → straight
   - Angle D: a very narrow angle like a pizza slice → acute

2. Look at each shape:
   - A square: classify all its angles
   - An equilateral triangle: classify all its angles
   - A shape that looks like an arrow pointing right: identify any obtuse angles

3. Name a real-world object that contains:
   - A right angle
   - An acute angle
   - An obtuse angle
   - A straight angle

4. Sort these angle measures into the correct category: 45°, 90°, 135°, 180°, 27°, 91°
   - Acute: 45°, 27°
   - Right: 90°
   - Obtuse: 135°, 91°
   - Straight: 180°

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Draw one example of each angle type and label it:
   a. Right angle
   b. Acute angle
   c. Obtuse angle
   d. Straight angle

2. Classify each angle measure:
   a. 75° → ______
   b. 90° → ______
   c. 110° → ______
   d. 180° → ______
   e. 15° → ______
   f. 89° → ______

3. True or False:
   a. An obtuse angle is always less than 180°. (T)
   b. A right angle could also be described as acute. (F)
   c. Any angle greater than 90° is obtuse. (F — 180° is straight, angles over 180° are reflex)
   d. The corner of a sheet of paper is a right angle. (T)
   e. An acute angle could measure 89°. (T)

4. Look at the capital letters A, E, K, L, T, W, X, Y, Z. For each, identify whether its angles are acute, right, obtuse, or straight.

**Stretch:**
5. The minute hand of a clock points to 12. The hour hand points to each number in turn (1, 2, 3 ... 6). For each position, classify the angle between the hands.
   - 1: 30° → acute
   - 2: 60° → acute
   - 3: 90° → right
   - 4: 120° → obtuse
   - 5: 150° → obtuse
   - 6: 180° → straight

6. Can a triangle have more than one obtuse angle? Explain your reasoning. (No — the angles of a triangle add to 180°; if one angle is already more than 90°, the others must total less than 90°, so they cannot be obtuse.)

**Challenge:**
7. A straight angle (180°) is split into two parts. One part is a right angle. What type of angle is the other part? What is its measure?

8. An angle measures 3° more than a right angle. What type of angle is it? What is its measure?

### Interleaved Review
R1. What are all the factor pairs of 24? (Unit 3: factors and factor pairs)
   Answer: (1, 24), (2, 12), (3, 8), (4, 6)

R2. Maria has 3 pizzas. Each pizza is cut into 8 slices. She eats 5 slices. Write the fraction of all the slices she ate. (Unit 4: fractions)
   Answer: 5/24

R3. 368 ÷ 4 = _____ (Unit 2: division)
   Answer: 92

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Draw an acute angle and label it angle A.

2. Circle the correct classification: An angle that measures 145° is: acute / right / obtuse / straight

3. How is a right angle different from an obtuse angle? Write one sentence.

**Mastery = student draws a recognizably acute angle (clearly less than 90°), correctly identifies 145° as obtuse, and writes a correct distinction (right = exactly 90°; obtuse = more than 90° but less than 180°, or equivalent).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the index card concrete model: "Hold your index card corner on your drawing — is your angle smaller, equal to, or bigger than it?"
- Use the book opening demonstration again with physical manipulation
- Focus only on distinguishing right vs. not-right first, then acute vs. obtuse
- Provide a visual reference card with labeled angle diagrams

### If Mastery Is Met
- Preview Lesson 3: "Tomorrow we will learn to measure angles precisely with a tool called a protractor. Can you make a good guess about the measure of the acute angle you drew?"
- Explore: what happens to an angle if you make the rays longer? Does it change the type? (No — angle size is independent of ray length)

## Extension (Optional)
**Clock Angle Investigation:** Create a recording sheet with clock faces showing various times. For each time, students:
1. Draw the hands
2. Classify the angle between the hands
3. Estimate the degree measure

Times to investigate: 1:00, 2:00, 3:00, 4:00, 5:00, 6:00, 12:00, 9:00, 7:00

**Shape Angle Hunt:** Using pattern blocks, students identify and record all angles in each block type. Which blocks have all right angles? Which have all acute angles?

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- The index card right angle reference is invaluable — have students keep this card for Lessons 2 and 3. They can write the angle types on one side and use the corner as a physical reference tool.
- "Cute" memory hook for acute: "A-CUTE little angle is smaller than a right angle." Many students find this memorable.
- Watch for students who classify all angles with any resemblance to a corner as "right." The index card test helps: if the angle has a gap (too small), it is acute; if the angle overflows (too big), it is obtuse.
- Straight angle is often surprising to students who do not initially think of a straight line as an "angle." Reinforce: it is formed by two rays going in exactly opposite directions from the same endpoint.
- The lesson intentionally does NOT introduce protractors yet — students are building classification by visual benchmarks before they learn to measure precisely. This sequence ensures they estimate before measuring (an important habit).
- When analyzing letters and real-world objects, accept reasonable classifications and focus on the reasoning process rather than perfect precision.
