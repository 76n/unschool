# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 5

## Topic
Ordering Fractions

## Learning Objectives
- Order three or more fractions from least to greatest or greatest to least
- Use common denominators to order fractions precisely
- Use benchmark fractions to sort and order fractions efficiently
- Justify the ordering with visual models or numerical reasoning

## Prerequisites
- Comparing two fractions with unlike denominators (Lesson 4)
- Finding common denominators and equivalent fractions (Lessons 2-3)
- Using benchmark fractions, especially 1/2 (Lesson 4)

## Materials Needed
- Fraction strips
- Number line templates (0 to 1)
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper

**Standards:** 4.NF.2 — Compare two fractions with different numerators and different denominators.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review comparing pairs of fractions and set up the need for ordering multiple fractions.

1. Compare: 2/3 ___ 3/4. (2/3 < 3/4 — common denominator 12: 8/12 < 9/12)
2. Compare: 1/2 ___ 2/3. (1/2 < 2/3 — common denominator 6: 3/6 < 4/6)
3. Ask: "If 1/2 < 2/3 and 2/3 < 3/4, can you order all three from least to greatest?" (1/2, 2/3, 3/4)
4. Say: "Today we will learn how to order three or more fractions, even when the denominators are all different."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach systematic methods for ordering multiple fractions.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Order (from least to greatest):** Arrange numbers from the smallest value to the largest.
- **Order (from greatest to least):** Arrange numbers from the largest value to the smallest.
- **Common denominator:** A shared denominator used to rewrite fractions so they can be compared directly.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Strategy 1: Find a common denominator for all fractions.**
Say: "The most reliable method is to rewrite ALL the fractions with the same denominator, then simply compare the numerators."

Write: Order from least to greatest: 1/2, 2/3, 1/4.

Say: "I need a common denominator for 2, 3, and 4. The LCM of 2, 3, and 4 is 12."
- 1/2 = 6/12
- 2/3 = 8/12
- 1/4 = 3/12

Say: "Now I compare the numerators: 3, 6, 8. From least to greatest: 3/12, 6/12, 8/12."

Say: "In original form: 1/4, 1/2, 2/3."

**Step 2 — Strategy 2: Sort using benchmarks first.**
Say: "When there are many fractions, it can help to sort them into groups first using 1/2 as a benchmark."

Write: Order from least to greatest: 7/8, 1/3, 3/4, 2/10, 5/6.

Say: "First, let me sort into two groups."
- Less than 1/2: 1/3 (one-third is less than one-half), 2/10 (half of 10 is 5, and 2 < 5)
- Greater than 1/2: 7/8, 3/4, 5/6

Say: "Now I order within each group."
- Less-than-1/2 group: Common denominator for 3 and 10 is 30. 1/3 = 10/30, 2/10 = 6/30. Order: 2/10, 1/3.
- Greater-than-1/2 group: Common denominator for 8, 4, and 6 is 24. 7/8 = 21/24, 3/4 = 18/24, 5/6 = 20/24. Order: 3/4, 5/6, 7/8.

Say: "Final order: 2/10, 1/3, 3/4, 5/6, 7/8."

**Step 3 — Tips for finding a common denominator of three or more numbers.**
Say: "To find the LCM of several numbers, you can list multiples of the largest denominator and check which is divisible by the others. Or multiply all denominators together — this always works, but gives a bigger number."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Order from least to greatest: 3/8, 1/4, 1/2.
- Common denominator for 8, 4, and 2 is 8.
- 3/8 = 3/8
- 1/4 = 2/8
- 1/2 = 4/8
- Numerators in order: 2, 3, 4.
- Answer: 1/4, 3/8, 1/2

**Example B:** Order from greatest to least: 2/3, 5/12, 3/4.
- Common denominator for 3, 12, and 4 is 12.
- 2/3 = 8/12
- 5/12 = 5/12
- 3/4 = 9/12
- Numerators from greatest to least: 9, 8, 5.
- Answer: 3/4, 2/3, 5/12

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice ordering fractions with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Common Denominator Method**

1. Order from least to greatest: 1/3, 1/6, 1/2.
   Facilitator prompt: "What is the LCM of 3, 6, and 2?" (6) "Rewrite all three with denominator 6."
   Answer: 1/6 = 1/6, 1/3 = 2/6, 1/2 = 3/6. Order: 1/6, 1/3, 1/2.

2. Order from greatest to least: 2/5, 1/2, 3/10.
   Facilitator prompt: "Common denominator for 5, 2, and 10?" (10)
   Answer: 2/5 = 4/10, 1/2 = 5/10, 3/10 = 3/10. Greatest to least: 1/2, 2/5, 3/10.

**Activity 2: Benchmark + Common Denominator**

3. Order from least to greatest: 5/6, 1/4, 7/12, 1/8.
   Facilitator prompt: "Which are less than 1/2? Which are greater?"
   Less than 1/2: 1/4, 1/8. Greater than 1/2: 5/6, 7/12.
   Within less-than group: 1/8 = 2/8, 1/4 = 2/8... wait. 1/8 < 1/4 (eighths are smaller pieces).
   Within greater-than group: 5/6 = 10/12, 7/12 = 7/12. 7/12 < 10/12.
   Final order: 1/8, 1/4, 7/12, 5/6.

**Facilitator tip:** If students feel overwhelmed, remind them: "You already know how to compare two fractions. Ordering is just doing that several times in an organized way."

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1: On-Level
Order each set from least to greatest. Show your common denominator work.

1. 1/3, 1/4, 1/6 (Answer: 1/6, 1/4, 1/3 — using denominator 12: 4/12, 3/12, 2/12... wait. 1/6 = 2/12, 1/4 = 3/12, 1/3 = 4/12. Order: 1/6, 1/4, 1/3.)
2. 2/3, 3/4, 1/2 (Answer: 1/2, 2/3, 3/4 — denominator 12: 6/12, 8/12, 9/12)
3. 5/8, 1/4, 3/8 (Answer: 1/4, 3/8, 5/8 — denominator 8: 2/8, 3/8, 5/8)
4. 2/5, 1/3, 3/10 (Answer: 3/10, 1/3, 2/5 — denominator 30: 12/30, 10/30, 9/30... 1/3 = 10/30, 3/10 = 9/30, 2/5 = 12/30. Order: 3/10, 1/3, 2/5.)
5. Order from greatest to least: 7/12, 2/3, 1/4 (Answer: 2/3, 7/12, 1/4 — denominator 12: 8/12, 7/12, 3/12)

### Tier 2: Scaffolded
The common denominator is provided. Rewrite and order from least to greatest.

1. 1/2, 2/3, 1/6 (common denominator: 6). 1/2 = ?/6, 2/3 = ?/6, 1/6 = ?/6.
   (Answer: 3/6, 4/6, 1/6. Order: 1/6, 1/2, 2/3)
2. 3/4, 1/2, 5/8 (common denominator: 8). (Answer: 6/8, 4/8, 5/8. Order: 1/2, 5/8, 3/4)
3. 1/3, 1/4, 5/12 (common denominator: 12). (Answer: 4/12, 3/12, 5/12. Order: 1/4, 1/3, 5/12)

### Tier 3: Challenge
1. Order from least to greatest: 3/5, 7/10, 2/3, 1/2. (Answer: 1/2, 3/5, 2/3, 7/10 — denominator 30: 15/30, 18/30, 20/30, 21/30)
2. Order from greatest to least: 5/6, 11/12, 3/4, 7/8. (Answer: 11/12, 7/8, 5/6, 3/4 — denominator 24: 20/24, 22/24, 18/24, 21/24)
3. Place these fractions on a number line from 0 to 1: 1/4, 2/3, 1/2, 5/6. Mark each with its label.
4. Find a fraction that would come between 1/3 and 1/2 when ordering. Prove it belongs there. (Example: 5/12. 1/3 = 4/12, 1/2 = 6/12, and 4/12 < 5/12 < 6/12.)

## Exit Ticket

1. Order from least to greatest: 3/4, 1/3, 5/6. Show your work.
2. Which strategy did you use — common denominator, benchmarks, or both? Why?

**Mastery threshold:** Correct ordering with work shown.

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Common denominator of 4, 3, and 6 is 12. 3/4 = 9/12, 1/3 = 4/12, 5/6 = 10/12. Order: 1/3, 3/4, 5/6.
2. Answers will vary. Accept any reasonable explanation. Common denominator is the most reliable strategy for this set since all three fractions are not easily sorted by the 1/2 benchmark alone (1/3 < 1/2, but 3/4 and 5/6 are both > 1/2 and need a common denominator to order).

## Teacher Notes

- This lesson extends the pair-wise comparisons from Lesson 4 to ordering multiple fractions. The cognitive load is higher because students must manage several equivalent fractions at once. Allow extra time if needed.
- The benchmark-first strategy reduces cognitive load for larger sets by dividing the problem into smaller, more manageable parts. Encourage students to try it.
- Number lines are excellent for verifying ordering — they provide a physical layout of the fractions from least to greatest and catch errors that purely numerical work might miss.
- If students are confident with ordering three fractions, challenge them with four or five. If they are not yet confident, reduce to just two or three fractions and build up.
- This lesson prepares students for the fraction operations in Lessons 6-9, where finding common denominators is an essential skill.
