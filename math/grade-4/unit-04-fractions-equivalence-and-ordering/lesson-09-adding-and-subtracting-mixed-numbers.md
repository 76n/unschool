# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 9

## Topic
Adding and Subtracting Mixed Numbers

## Learning Objectives
- Add two mixed numbers with like denominators, including regrouping when the fraction sum exceeds one whole
- Subtract two mixed numbers with like denominators, including borrowing when the fraction being subtracted is larger
- Simplify the fraction part of the result
- Choose between working with mixed numbers or converting to improper fractions

## Prerequisites
- Adding and subtracting fractions with like denominators (Lessons 6-7)
- Converting between mixed numbers and improper fractions (Lesson 8)
- Understanding of regrouping from whole-number addition and subtraction

## Materials Needed
- Fraction strips (multiple sets to represent quantities greater than 1)
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper

**Standards:** 4.NF.3c — Add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators, e.g., by replacing each mixed number with an equivalent fraction and/or by using properties of operations and the relationship between addition and subtraction.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review adding fractions and converting improper fractions to mixed numbers.

1. Solve: 5/8 + 7/8 = ? (12/8 = 1 4/8 = 1 1/2)
2. Convert 3 2/5 to an improper fraction. (17/5)
3. Convert 19/6 to a mixed number. (3 1/6)
4. Say: "Today we will use everything we have learned to add and subtract mixed numbers. There are two ways to do it — you will learn both and choose the one you prefer."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach two methods for adding and subtracting mixed numbers with like denominators.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Regroup:** To exchange one whole for an equivalent fraction. For example, 1 whole = 4/4 = 6/6 = 8/8, depending on the denominator.
- **Borrow (in subtraction):** To take 1 from the whole-number part and convert it to a fraction so you can subtract.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Adding mixed numbers: Method 1 (add parts separately).**
Write: 2 3/8 + 1 5/8.

Say: "Method 1: Add the whole numbers and the fractions separately."
- Whole numbers: 2 + 1 = 3
- Fractions: 3/8 + 5/8 = 8/8 = 1

Say: "Now combine: 3 + 1 = 4. The answer is 4."

Write another example: 1 3/4 + 2 3/4.
- Whole numbers: 1 + 2 = 3
- Fractions: 3/4 + 3/4 = 6/4

Say: "6/4 is improper. Convert: 6/4 = 1 2/4 = 1 1/2. Now add that to the whole-number sum: 3 + 1 1/2 = 4 1/2."

Say: "This is called regrouping — when the fraction sum is more than one whole, we convert the extra into a whole number and add it in."

**Step 2 — Adding mixed numbers: Method 2 (convert to improper fractions).**
Write: 1 3/4 + 2 3/4.
- Convert: 1 3/4 = 7/4, 2 3/4 = 11/4.
- Add: 7/4 + 11/4 = 18/4.
- Convert back: 18/4 = 4 2/4 = 4 1/2.

Say: "Both methods give the same answer: 4 1/2. Use whichever one feels easier to you."

**Step 3 — Subtracting mixed numbers: No borrowing needed.**
Write: 3 5/6 - 1 2/6.
- Whole numbers: 3 - 1 = 2
- Fractions: 5/6 - 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2
- Answer: 2 1/2.

Say: "When the top fraction is bigger than the bottom fraction, subtraction is straightforward — just subtract parts separately."

**Step 4 — Subtracting mixed numbers: Borrowing needed.**
Write: 4 1/5 - 2 3/5.

Say: "I need to subtract 3/5 from 1/5, but 1/5 is smaller than 3/5. I need to borrow."

Say: "I borrow 1 whole from the 4, making it 3. That 1 whole becomes 5/5. Now I add 5/5 + 1/5 = 6/5."

Rewrite: 4 1/5 = 3 6/5.
- Whole numbers: 3 - 2 = 1
- Fractions: 6/5 - 3/5 = 3/5
- Answer: 1 3/5.

Say: "Borrowing in fraction subtraction is just like borrowing in whole-number subtraction — you exchange 1 from a bigger place value (the whole) into smaller pieces (fifths)."

**Step 5 — Subtraction Method 2 (convert to improper fractions).**
Write: 4 1/5 - 2 3/5.
- Convert: 4 1/5 = 21/5, 2 3/5 = 13/5.
- Subtract: 21/5 - 13/5 = 8/5.
- Convert back: 8/5 = 1 3/5.

Say: "Same answer. The improper fraction method avoids borrowing entirely."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 3 2/6 + 2 5/6.
Method 1 (add parts separately):
- Whole numbers: 3 + 2 = 5
- Fractions: 2/6 + 5/6 = 7/6 = 1 1/6
- Combine: 5 + 1 1/6 = 6 1/6
- Answer: 6 1/6.

**Example B:** 5 1/4 - 3 3/4.
Method 1 (borrow):
- Cannot subtract 3/4 from 1/4. Borrow: 5 1/4 = 4 5/4.
- Whole numbers: 4 - 3 = 1
- Fractions: 5/4 - 3/4 = 2/4 = 1/2
- Answer: 1 1/2.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice adding and subtracting mixed numbers with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Addition**

1. 1 2/5 + 3 1/5 = ?
   Facilitator prompt: "Add whole numbers: 1 + 3 = ?" (4) "Add fractions: 2/5 + 1/5 = ?" (3/5) "Combine." (4 3/5)

2. 2 5/8 + 1 7/8 = ?
   Facilitator prompt: "Fractions: 5/8 + 7/8 = 12/8. Is that improper?" (Yes.) "Convert 12/8." (1 4/8 = 1 1/2) "Now add to the whole-number sum: 3 + 1 1/2 = ?" (4 1/2)

**Activity 2: Subtraction**

3. 4 5/6 - 2 1/6 = ?
   Facilitator prompt: "Do you need to borrow?" (No — 5/6 > 1/6.) "Subtract parts." (2 4/6 = 2 2/3)

4. 3 1/8 - 1 5/8 = ?
   Facilitator prompt: "Can you subtract 5/8 from 1/8?" (No.) "Borrow 1 from 3 and convert to eighths. 3 1/8 = 2 ?/8." (2 9/8) "Now subtract." (2 9/8 - 1 5/8 = 1 4/8 = 1 1/2)

5. Try the improper fraction method for problem 4: Convert 3 1/8 to an improper fraction and 1 5/8 to an improper fraction, then subtract.
   (25/8 - 13/8 = 12/8 = 1 4/8 = 1 1/2. Same answer!)

**Facilitator tip:** Let students try both methods and choose their preferred one. Some students find borrowing confusing and prefer the improper fraction approach. Both are valid.

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1: On-Level
Solve. Simplify your answers.

1. 2 1/6 + 3 4/6 = ? (Answer: 5 5/6)
2. 4 3/8 + 1 7/8 = ? (Answer: 5 10/8 = 6 2/8 = 6 1/4)
3. 5 7/10 - 2 3/10 = ? (Answer: 3 4/10 = 3 2/5)
4. 6 1/4 - 2 3/4 = ? (Answer: borrow: 5 5/4 - 2 3/4 = 3 2/4 = 3 1/2)
5. 3 5/12 + 2 9/12 = ? (Answer: 5 14/12 = 6 2/12 = 6 1/6)

### Tier 2: Scaffolded
Fill in the blanks.

1. 1 2/5 + 2 1/5: Wholes: 1 + 2 = ___. Fractions: 2/5 + 1/5 = ___. Answer: ___ ___/5. (Answer: 3, 3/5, 3 3/5)
2. 3 5/8 + 2 5/8: Wholes: 3 + 2 = ___. Fractions: 5/8 + 5/8 = ___/8 = 1 ___/8. Combine: ___ + 1 ___/8 = ___. (Answer: 5, 10/8, 1 2/8, 5 + 1 2/8 = 6 2/8 = 6 1/4)
3. 4 5/6 - 1 2/6: Wholes: 4 - 1 = ___. Fractions: 5/6 - 2/6 = ___/6. Answer: ___ ___/6 = ___ ___/___. (Answer: 3, 3/6, 3 3/6 = 3 1/2)
4. 5 1/4 - 2 3/4: Need to borrow. 5 1/4 = 4 ___/4. Now subtract: 4 ___/4 - 2 3/4 = ___ ___/4 = ___ ___/___. (Answer: 4 5/4, 2 2/4, 2 1/2)

### Tier 3: Challenge
1. 7 3/10 + 4 9/10 = ? (Answer: 11 12/10 = 12 2/10 = 12 1/5)
2. 8 1/6 - 5 5/6 = ? (Answer: borrow: 7 7/6 - 5 5/6 = 2 2/6 = 2 1/3)
3. A carpenter has a board that is 6 1/4 feet long. She cuts off 2 3/4 feet. How long is the remaining piece? (Answer: 6 1/4 - 2 3/4 = 5 5/4 - 2 3/4 = 3 2/4 = 3 1/2 feet)
4. Jaden ran 2 5/8 miles on Monday and 3 7/8 miles on Tuesday. How far did he run in total? (Answer: 2 5/8 + 3 7/8 = 5 12/8 = 6 4/8 = 6 1/2 miles)
5. Show that 4 1/3 - 1 2/3 gives the same answer whether you use the borrowing method or the improper fraction method. (Borrowing: 3 4/3 - 1 2/3 = 2 2/3. Improper: 13/3 - 5/3 = 8/3 = 2 2/3. Same!)

## Exit Ticket

1. Solve: 2 3/8 + 1 7/8 = ?. Simplify.
2. Solve: 5 1/6 - 2 5/6 = ?. Simplify.

**Mastery threshold:** Both correct with simplified answers.

---

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Wholes: 2 + 1 = 3. Fractions: 3/8 + 7/8 = 10/8 = 1 2/8 = 1 1/4. Combine: 3 + 1 1/4 = 4 1/4.
2. Borrow: 5 1/6 = 4 7/6. Wholes: 4 - 2 = 2. Fractions: 7/6 - 5/6 = 2/6 = 1/3. Answer: 2 1/3.

## Teacher Notes

- This is one of the most challenging lessons in the unit. Allow two sessions if needed. The borrowing concept in subtraction is where most students struggle.
- Drawing a parallel to whole-number subtraction with borrowing is helpful: "When you subtract 47 - 29, you borrow a ten and make 17 ones. Here, you borrow a whole and make extra fraction pieces."
- Students who strongly prefer the improper fraction method should be allowed to use it consistently. The key is that they can solve the problems correctly, not that they use a specific method.
- Always require students to simplify their final answers. A common oversight is leaving answers like 6 2/8 instead of 6 1/4.
- The challenge problems include real-world contexts to preview Lesson 10 (problem solving). This helps students see why mixed number operations matter.
