# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 8

## Topic
Mixed Numbers and Improper Fractions

## Learning Objectives
- Define mixed numbers and improper fractions and explain how they are related
- Convert an improper fraction to a mixed number using division
- Convert a mixed number to an improper fraction using multiplication and addition
- Represent mixed numbers and improper fractions with visual models

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of fractions as parts of a whole
- Adding fractions with like denominators (Lesson 6)
- Division with remainders (Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- Fraction circles or fraction strip sets (multiple wholes)
- Grid paper
- Colored pencils
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper

**Standards:** 4.NF.3b — Decompose a fraction into a sum of fractions with the same denominator. 4.NF.3c — Add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Encounter a situation where a fraction answer is greater than 1.

1. Solve: 5/8 + 5/8 = ? (10/8)
2. Ask: "Is 10/8 less than 1, equal to 1, or greater than 1?" (Greater — because 8/8 = 1, and 10/8 is more than 8/8.)
3. Ask: "If you had 10 slices of pizza and each whole pizza has 8 slices, how many whole pizzas is that?" (1 whole pizza and 2 slices left over — 1 and 2/8.)
4. Say: "When the numerator is bigger than the denominator, we call it an improper fraction. Today we will learn to work with improper fractions and mixed numbers."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the relationship between mixed numbers and improper fractions and how to convert between them.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Improper fraction:** A fraction where the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator. Examples: 7/4, 5/3, 8/8. The fraction represents one whole or more.
- **Mixed number:** A number that has a whole-number part and a fraction part. Example: 1 3/4 means 1 whole and 3/4.
- **Convert:** To change from one form to another without changing the value.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce with a visual model.**
Draw 2 circles, each divided into 4 equal parts.
- Shade all 4 parts of the first circle and 3 parts of the second circle.

Say: "How many fourths are shaded in total?" (7 fourths, or 7/4.)
Say: "How many whole circles are completely shaded?" (1 whole.) "How many extra fourths?" (3 fourths.)
Say: "So 7/4 = 1 3/4. The improper fraction 7/4 and the mixed number 1 3/4 are two names for the same amount."

**Step 2 — Convert improper fraction to mixed number (division method).**
Say: "To convert an improper fraction to a mixed number, divide the numerator by the denominator."

Write: Convert 11/4 to a mixed number.
- 11 / 4 = 2 remainder 3
- The quotient (2) is the whole-number part.
- The remainder (3) is the numerator of the fraction part.
- The denominator stays the same (4).
- 11/4 = 2 3/4

Say: "Think of it this way: 11 fourths. How many groups of 4 fourths can I make? 2 groups (that is 2 wholes). How many fourths are left? 3. So 2 and 3/4."

**Step 3 — Convert mixed number to improper fraction (multiply and add).**
Say: "To convert a mixed number to an improper fraction, multiply the whole number by the denominator, then add the numerator. The denominator stays the same."

Write: Convert 3 2/5 to an improper fraction.
- Multiply: 3 x 5 = 15
- Add the numerator: 15 + 2 = 17
- Keep the denominator: 17/5
- 3 2/5 = 17/5

Say: "Why does this work? 3 wholes = 3 x 5 fifths = 15 fifths. Plus 2 more fifths = 17 fifths."

**Step 4 — Special case: fractions equal to whole numbers.**
Write: 8/4 = ?

Say: "8 divided by 4 = 2 with no remainder. So 8/4 = 2. When the numerator is a multiple of the denominator, the fraction equals a whole number."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Convert 9/6 to a mixed number.
- 9 / 6 = 1 remainder 3.
- 9/6 = 1 3/6.
- Simplify: 3/6 = 1/2. So 9/6 = 1 1/2.

**Example B:** Convert 2 3/8 to an improper fraction.
- Multiply: 2 x 8 = 16.
- Add: 16 + 3 = 19.
- Keep denominator: 19/8.
- 2 3/8 = 19/8.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice converting between mixed numbers and improper fractions with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Improper to Mixed**

1. Convert 7/3 to a mixed number.
   Facilitator prompt: "How many times does 3 go into 7?" (2 times, with 1 left over.) "So?" (2 1/3)

2. Convert 10/4 to a mixed number and simplify.
   Facilitator prompt: "10 / 4 = ?" (2 remainder 2.) "So 2 2/4. Can we simplify 2/4?" (Yes — 2/4 = 1/2. Answer: 2 1/2.)

3. Convert 15/6 to a mixed number and simplify.
   Facilitator prompt: "15 / 6 = ?" (2 remainder 3.) "So 2 3/6 = 2 1/2."

**Activity 2: Mixed to Improper**

4. Convert 1 5/8 to an improper fraction.
   Facilitator prompt: "Multiply: 1 x 8 = ?" (8) "Add: 8 + 5 = ?" (13) "So?" (13/8)

5. Convert 4 1/3 to an improper fraction.
   Facilitator prompt: "4 x 3 = ?" (12) "12 + 1 = ?" (13) "So?" (13/3)

**Facilitator tip:** Have students verify conversions with a visual model for the first few problems. Draw circles or rectangles to confirm that 7/3 really does equal 2 1/3.

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1: On-Level
Convert each improper fraction to a mixed number. Simplify when possible.

1. 9/4 (Answer: 2 1/4)
2. 11/3 (Answer: 3 2/3)
3. 14/6 (Answer: 2 2/6 = 2 1/3)
4. 8/5 (Answer: 1 3/5)

Convert each mixed number to an improper fraction.

5. 2 1/4 (Answer: 9/4)
6. 3 2/5 (Answer: 17/5)
7. 1 7/8 (Answer: 15/8)
8. 5 1/3 (Answer: 16/3)

### Tier 2: Scaffolded
Fill in the blanks.

1. 7/4: 7 / 4 = ___ remainder ___. So 7/4 = ___ ___/4. (Answer: 1 remainder 3. 1 3/4)
2. 10/3: 10 / 3 = ___ remainder ___. So 10/3 = ___ ___/3. (Answer: 3 remainder 1. 3 1/3)
3. 2 1/6: 2 x 6 = ___. ___ + 1 = ___. So 2 1/6 = ___/6. (Answer: 12, 13, 13/6)
4. Draw a picture of 5/3 and write it as a mixed number. (1 2/3)

### Tier 3: Challenge
1. Convert 25/8 to a mixed number. (Answer: 3 1/8)
2. Convert 7 3/4 to an improper fraction. (Answer: 31/4)
3. Which is greater: 13/5 or 2 1/2? Convert both to the same form and compare. (13/5 = 2 3/5. 2 1/2 = 2 5/10 = 2 1/2. Compare 2 3/5 and 2 1/2: same whole part, compare fractions. 3/5 = 6/10, 1/2 = 5/10. 6/10 > 5/10, so 13/5 > 2 1/2.)
4. Is 12/12 an improper fraction? Is it a mixed number? Explain. (12/12 = 1. It is technically an improper fraction because numerator equals denominator, but it simplifies to a whole number. It is not a mixed number.)
5. Explain in your own words why 3 x 4 + 2 gives the numerator of the improper fraction for 3 2/4. (Three wholes, each made of 4 fourths, gives 12 fourths. Plus 2 more fourths gives 14 fourths total. So 3 2/4 = 14/4.)

## Exit Ticket

1. Convert 11/6 to a mixed number. Simplify if possible.
2. Convert 3 1/4 to an improper fraction.

**Mastery threshold:** Both correct.

---

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. 11 / 6 = 1 remainder 5. So 11/6 = 1 5/6. (5 and 6 share no common factor other than 1, so this is already simplified.)
2. 3 x 4 = 12. 12 + 1 = 13. 3 1/4 = 13/4.

---

## Teacher Notes

- The pizza/circle model is very effective for this lesson. Students can physically count pieces across multiple wholes to build intuition before learning the division procedure.
- When converting improper fractions to mixed numbers, the most common error is writing the remainder as a whole number instead of a fraction. For example, writing 7/3 = 2 R1 instead of 2 1/3. Emphasize: "The remainder is 1 THIRD, not just 1."
- When converting mixed numbers to improper fractions, students sometimes forget to add the numerator (e.g., 3 2/5 becomes 15/5 instead of 17/5). Always prompt: "Did you add the numerator?"
- Remind students to simplify the fraction part of their mixed number answer. For example, 10/6 = 1 4/6 = 1 2/3.
- This lesson sets up Lesson 9, where students will add and subtract mixed numbers.
