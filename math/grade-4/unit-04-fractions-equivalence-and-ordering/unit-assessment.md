## Answer Key

### Section 1: Equivalence and Simplifying

**1.1** Any two correct equivalent fractions, such as: 4/6, 6/9, 8/12, 10/15, etc. [2 points]

**1.2** The denominator was multiplied by 3 (5 x 3 = 15), so multiply the numerator by 3: 3 x 3 = 9. Answer: 3/5 = 9/15. [2 points]

**1.3** The numerator was divided by 4 (8 / 4 = 2), so divide the denominator by 4: 12 / 4 = 3. Answer: 8/12 = 2/3. [2 points]

**1.4** [3 points]
- (a) 6/8: GCF of 6 and 8 is 2. 6/8 = 3/4.
- (b) 9/12: GCF of 9 and 12 is 3. 9/12 = 3/4.
- (c) 10/15: GCF of 10 and 15 is 5. 10/15 = 2/3.

**1.5** Mia is NOT correct. [3 points]
Explanation: To make equivalent fractions, you must MULTIPLY (or divide) both the numerator and denominator by the SAME number, not add. Check: 3/4 = 9/12 and 5/6 = 10/12. Since 9/12 is not equal to 10/12, the fractions 3/4 and 5/6 are NOT equivalent. Adding the same number to the top and bottom does not produce equivalent fractions.

### Section 2: Comparing and Ordering Fractions

**2.1** [4 points]
- (a) 3/4 > 2/3. (Common denominator 12: 9/12 > 8/12.)
- (b) 5/8 < 3/4. (Common denominator 8: 5/8 < 6/8.)
- (c) 4/6 = 2/3. (4/6 simplifies to 2/3, or common denominator 6: 4/6 = 4/6.)
- (d) 1/3 < 3/8. (Common denominator 24: 8/24 < 9/24.)

**2.2** [2 points]
3/10 < 1/2 (because half of 10 is 5, and 3 < 5). 7/12 > 1/2 (because half of 12 is 6, and 7 > 6). Since 3/10 is less than 1/2 and 7/12 is greater than 1/2, we know 3/10 < 7/12.

**2.3** [3 points]
Common denominator for 3, 4, and 2 is 12. 1/3 = 4/12, 3/4 = 9/12, 1/2 = 6/12. Order from least to greatest: 4/12, 6/12, 9/12 = **1/3, 1/2, 3/4**.

**2.4** [3 points]
Common denominator for 6, 3, and 12 is 12. 5/6 = 10/12, 2/3 = 8/12, 7/12 = 7/12. Order from greatest to least: 10/12, 8/12, 7/12 = **5/6, 2/3, 7/12**.

### Section 3: Adding Fractions with Like Denominators

**3.1** [4 points]
- (a) 1/5 + 2/5 = 3/5
- (b) 3/8 + 3/8 = 6/8 = 3/4
- (c) 5/12 + 1/12 = 6/12 = 1/2
- (d) 4/10 + 3/10 = 7/10

**3.2** 2/6 + 3/6 = 5/6. [2 points]

**3.3** [3 points]
Whole numbers: 2 + 1 = 3. Fractions: 3/4 + 3/4 = 6/4 = 1 2/4 = 1 1/2. Combine: 3 + 1 1/2 = 4 1/2.

**3.4** [3 points]
The student added the denominators (8 + 8 = 16) instead of keeping the denominator the same. When adding fractions with like denominators, you add only the numerators and keep the denominator the same. Correct answer: 2/8 + 3/8 = 5/8.

### Section 4: Subtracting Fractions with Like Denominators

**4.1** [4 points]
- (a) 4/5 - 1/5 = 3/5
- (b) 7/8 - 3/8 = 4/8 = 1/2
- (c) 9/12 - 3/12 = 6/12 = 1/2
- (d) 7/10 - 2/10 = 5/10 = 1/2

**4.2** 5/6 - 1/6 = 4/6 = 2/3. [2 points]

**4.3** [3 points]
Cannot subtract 5/8 from 1/8 directly. Borrow: 4 1/8 = 3 9/8. Now subtract: 3 9/8 - 2 5/8 = 1 4/8 = 1 1/2.

**4.4** [3 points]
1 gallon = 8/8 gallon. 8/8 - 3/8 = 5/8 gallon. There is 5/8 gallon of water left.

### Section 5: Mixed Numbers and Problem Solving

**5.1** 13/4: 13 / 4 = 3 remainder 1. So 13/4 = 3 1/4. [2 points]

**5.2** 3 2/5: 3 x 5 = 15. 15 + 2 = 17. So 3 2/5 = 17/5. [2 points]

**5.3** 3/8 + 5/8 = 8/8 = 1 cup. [2 points]

**5.4** [3 points]
5 3/10 - 2 7/10. Cannot subtract 7/10 from 3/10. Borrow: 5 3/10 = 4 13/10. Now subtract: 4 13/10 - 2 7/10 = 2 6/10 = 2 3/5 feet.

**5.5** [3 points]
Common denominator for 4, 8, and 3 is 24. Anna: 3/4 = 18/24. Ben: 5/8 = 15/24. Cara: 2/3 = 16/24. Order from least to greatest (closest to farthest): 15/24, 16/24, 18/24 = **Ben (5/8), Cara (2/3), Anna (3/4)**.

## Reteaching Recommendations

| If struggling in... | Focus on... |
|---|---|
| Section 1 (Equivalence/Simplifying) | Return to fraction strips and area models (Lesson 1). Reteach the multiply/divide rule with visual verification (Lesson 2). Practice finding GCF and connecting to simplifying (Lesson 3). |
| Section 2 (Comparing/Ordering) | Practice the common-denominator comparison method with visual confirmation using fraction strips (Lesson 4). Start with comparing two fractions before moving to ordering three (Lesson 5). Reinforce the benchmark strategy for quick checks. |
| Section 3 (Adding) | Return to the "counting like objects" analogy: eighths + eighths = eighths (Lesson 6). Use fraction strips to physically combine pieces. Emphasize that denominators do NOT get added. For mixed numbers, practice adding parts separately, then regrouping (Lesson 9). |
| Section 4 (Subtracting) | Mirror the addition approach — use "take away" with fraction strips (Lesson 7). For mixed number subtraction, practice the borrowing step in isolation before combining with subtraction (Lesson 9). |
| Section 5 (Mixed Numbers/Problem Solving) | Reteach conversion between mixed numbers and improper fractions with circle models (Lesson 8). For word problems, use the RSCV framework and practice identifying the operation before computing (Lesson 10). |

## Mastery Criteria

Students who achieve mastery demonstrate the ability to:

1. **Generate and recognize** equivalent fractions using both visual models and numerical methods
2. **Simplify** fractions to their simplest form using the GCF
3. **Compare** fractions with unlike denominators using common denominators or benchmarks
4. **Order** three or more fractions correctly
5. **Add and subtract** fractions and mixed numbers with like denominators, including simplifying results
6. **Convert** between mixed numbers and improper fractions
7. **Solve** word problems by choosing the correct operation and computing accurately

## Facilitator Observations

Use these prompts to record qualitative observations during the assessment:

- Does the student find common denominators efficiently, or does the process take a long time?
- Does the student simplify answers consistently, or do they leave fractions unsimplified?
- Can the student explain why a comparison is true, or do they just give an answer?
- For mixed number operations, does the student handle regrouping and borrowing confidently?
- In word problems, can the student identify the correct operation, or does the student guess?
- Does the student check their work or verify with a model?
