# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 2

## Topic
Generating Equivalent Fractions

## Learning Objectives
- Explain why multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same number produces an equivalent fraction
- Generate equivalent fractions by multiplying both parts by a chosen number
- Generate equivalent fractions by dividing both parts by a common factor
- Determine whether two given fractions are equivalent using multiplication or division

## Prerequisites
- Recognizing equivalent fractions using visual models (Lesson 1)
- Multiplication facts through 12
- Understanding of factor pairs (Unit 3)

## Materials Needed
- Fraction strips (from Lesson 1)
- Grid paper
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper

**Standards:** 4.NF.1 — Explain why a fraction a/b is equivalent to (n x a)/(n x b) using visual fraction models. Use this principle to recognize and generate equivalent fractions.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect visual equivalence from Lesson 1 to numerical patterns.

1. Write on the board: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8.
   Ask: "What is happening to the numerator as we go from left to right?" (It goes 1, 2, 3, 4 — it doubles, triples, quadruples.)
   Ask: "What is happening to the denominator?" (It goes 2, 4, 6, 8 — same thing!)
2. Ask: "Do you see a pattern? What is being done to both the top and bottom number each time?" (Both are multiplied by the same number.)
3. Say: "Today we will learn WHY this works and use it to create equivalent fractions without needing to draw pictures every time."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Formalize the rule for generating equivalent fractions using multiplication and division.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Equivalent fractions:** Fractions that represent the same amount (review from Lesson 1).
- **Generate:** To create or produce. When we generate equivalent fractions, we create new fractions that equal the original.
- **Identity property of multiplication:** Any number multiplied by 1 stays the same. Since 2/2 = 1, 3/3 = 1, etc., multiplying a fraction by 2/2 or 3/3 does not change its value.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect the visual to the numerical.**
Draw two rectangles side by side, both the same size.
- Rectangle 1: Divide into 3 columns, shade 1. Label: 1/3.
- Rectangle 2: Take the same rectangle and divide each column in half (now 6 columns total), shade 2. Label: 2/6.

Say: "When we cut each piece in half, we doubled the number of pieces AND doubled the number of shaded pieces. In fraction language, we multiplied both the numerator and the denominator by 2."

Write: 1/3 = (1 x 2)/(3 x 2) = 2/6

**Step 2 — State the rule.**
Say: "Here is the big idea: if you multiply the numerator and the denominator by the SAME number, you get an equivalent fraction. Why? Because multiplying by 2/2 is the same as multiplying by 1, and multiplying by 1 does not change the value."

Write on the board:
- 1/3 x 2/2 = 2/6 (multiplying by 1 in the form of 2/2)
- 1/3 x 3/3 = 3/9 (multiplying by 1 in the form of 3/3)
- 1/3 x 4/4 = 4/12 (multiplying by 1 in the form of 4/4)

Say: "All of these — 1/3, 2/6, 3/9, 4/12 — are equivalent fractions. They are all just 1/3 wearing different costumes."

**Step 3 — Going the other direction: dividing.**
Say: "We can also go backwards. If 4/12 is equivalent to 1/3, we can get from 4/12 to 1/3 by dividing both parts by 4."

Write: 4/12 = (4 / 4)/(12 / 4) = 1/3

Say: "Dividing both the numerator and denominator by the same number also gives an equivalent fraction. We will use this a lot in Lesson 3 when we learn to simplify."

**Step 4 — The critical rule.**
Write in a box on the board:

To generate an equivalent fraction:
- Multiply BOTH the numerator and denominator by the SAME nonzero number, OR
- Divide BOTH the numerator and denominator by the SAME nonzero number.

Say: "The key word is BOTH. Whatever you do to the top, you must do to the bottom. If you only multiply the top, you change the value of the fraction."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Generate three fractions equivalent to 2/5.
- 2/5 x 2/2 = 4/10
- 2/5 x 3/3 = 6/15
- 2/5 x 4/4 = 8/20
- Answer: 2/5 = 4/10 = 6/15 = 8/20

**Example B:** Fill in the missing number: 3/4 = ?/12.
- Ask: "What did we multiply the denominator by? 4 x ? = 12. The answer is 3."
- "So we also multiply the numerator by 3: 3 x 3 = 9."
- Answer: 3/4 = 9/12

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice generating equivalent fractions with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Multiply to Find Equivalents**

1. Generate two fractions equivalent to 1/4.
   Facilitator prompt: "Multiply both parts by 2 first. What do you get?" (2/8) "Now multiply both parts by 3." (3/12)

2. Fill in the blank: 2/3 = ?/9.
   Facilitator prompt: "The denominator went from 3 to 9. What did we multiply by?" (3) "So multiply the numerator by 3 too." (6/9)

3. Fill in the blank: 5/6 = 10/?.
   Facilitator prompt: "The numerator went from 5 to 10. What did we multiply by?" (2) "So multiply the denominator by 2 too." (10/12)

**Activity 2: Divide to Find Equivalents**

4. Fill in the blank: 6/8 = 3/?.
   Facilitator prompt: "The numerator went from 6 to 3. What did we divide by?" (2) "Divide the denominator by 2 too." (3/4)

5. Are 4/6 and 2/3 equivalent? How can you tell?
   Facilitator prompt: "Can you divide both parts of 4/6 by the same number to get 2/3?" (Yes — divide both by 2: 4/2 = 2, 6/2 = 3. So 4/6 = 2/3.)

**Facilitator tip:** If a student struggles with the missing-number problems, draw an arrow from the known pair showing the multiplier, and ask what that multiplier is before applying it to the unknown part.

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1: On-Level

1. Generate two equivalent fractions for 3/5. (Answers: 6/10, 9/15, 12/20, etc.)
2. Fill in the blank: 2/7 = ?/14 (Answer: 4/14)
3. Fill in the blank: 4/5 = 12/? (Answer: 12/15)
4. Fill in the blank: 8/12 = ?/3 (Answer: 2/3)
5. Are 3/8 and 6/16 equivalent? Show your work. (Yes — 3 x 2 = 6, 8 x 2 = 16.)

### Tier 2: Scaffolded

1. 1/3 x 2/2 = ?/? (Answer: 2/6)
2. 1/3 x 3/3 = ?/? (Answer: 3/9)
3. 2/5 x ?/? = 4/10. What did you multiply by? (Answer: 2/2)
4. Fill in the blank: 1/4 = ?/8 (Answer: 2/8)
5. Use fraction strips to verify that 2/4 = 1/2.

### Tier 3: Challenge

1. Generate four fractions equivalent to 5/8. (Answers: 10/16, 15/24, 20/32, 25/40)
2. Fill in both blanks: ?/6 = 10/? (One answer: 5/6 = 10/12; or 10/6 = 10/6 if numerator is 10. Most likely intended: 5/6 = 10/12.)
3. True or false: 7/12 = 14/24. Explain. (True — multiply both parts by 2.)
4. Find the missing numerator: ?/20 = 3/5. (Answer: 12/20)
5. Explain why multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same number does not change the value of the fraction. Use the idea of "multiplying by 1." (Answer: Because n/n = 1, and any number times 1 equals itself.)

---

## Exit Ticket

1. Generate one fraction equivalent to 3/4 by multiplying. Show your work.
2. Fill in the blank: 6/10 = 3/?. Show your work.
3. Are 2/3 and 8/12 equivalent? Explain how you know.

**Mastery threshold:** 2 out of 3 correct with work shown.

---

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Multiply both by 2: 3/4 = 6/8. (Or by 3: 9/12, etc.)
2. Divide both by 2: 6/10 = 3/5.
3. Yes. Multiply both parts of 2/3 by 4: (2 x 4)/(3 x 4) = 8/12. So 2/3 = 8/12.

---

## Teacher Notes

- The identity property of multiplication (multiplying by n/n = 1) is the mathematical justification for this procedure. While fourth graders do not need to know the formal property name, they should understand the reasoning: "Multiplying by 2/2 is the same as multiplying by 1, and 1 times anything is itself."
- Watch carefully for students who multiply only the numerator or only the denominator. If you see this, return to the area model: "If you only doubled the number of shaded pieces but did not double the total pieces, would the fraction stay the same?"
- This lesson transitions from the concrete (visual models in Lesson 1) to the representational/abstract (numerical rule). Some students may still need fraction strips to verify — encourage this as a checking strategy, not a crutch.
- Preview for Lesson 3: Generating equivalent fractions by dividing will be the foundation of simplifying fractions.
