# Facilitator Guide — Grade 4 Math, Unit 4: Fractions — Equivalence and Ordering

**Grade:** 4
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 4 — Fractions: Equivalence and Ordering
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 4-5 weeks at 2-3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (facilitator leads instruction; student works independently for practice)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit builds on the fraction foundations established in Grade 3 (fractions as parts of a whole, fraction notation, comparing fractions with the same numerator or denominator) and extends into equivalent fractions, comparing and ordering fractions with unlike denominators, and adding and subtracting fractions with like denominators. Students also learn to convert between mixed numbers and improper fractions. The unit emphasizes visual models throughout, following a concrete-to-representational-to-abstract progression with fraction strips, area models, and number lines before moving to purely numerical strategies.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Recognize and generate equivalent fractions using visual models (area models, fraction strips, number lines)
- Generate equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number
- Simplify fractions to their simplest form using the greatest common factor
- Compare two fractions with unlike denominators by finding a common denominator or using benchmark fractions
- Order three or more fractions from least to greatest (or greatest to least) using common denominators or benchmarks
- Add fractions with like denominators and simplify the result when needed
- Subtract fractions with like denominators and simplify the result when needed
- Convert between mixed numbers and improper fractions
- Add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators, including regrouping
- Solve real-world word problems involving fractions using multiple strategies

**Why this matters:** Fractions are one of the most critical topics in elementary mathematics. Research consistently shows that fraction understanding in Grade 4 is a strong predictor of later success in algebra and higher mathematics. This unit gives students the conceptual foundation and procedural fluency they need to work confidently with fractions in Grade 5 and beyond.

## Lesson Summaries and Facilitation Notes

### Lesson 1: Equivalent Fractions with Visual Models

**Goal:** Students discover that the same amount can be named by different fractions using area models, fraction strips, and number lines.

**Key moves:**
- Begin with concrete fraction strips — fold and compare to see that 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6
- Use area models (rectangles divided into equal parts) to shade equivalent amounts
- Transfer to number lines to see equivalent fractions as the same point

**Watch for:** Students who think bigger denominators always mean bigger fractions. Reinforce: "More pieces does not mean more pizza — it depends on how many pieces you have."

### Lesson 2: Generating Equivalent Fractions

**Goal:** Students learn the rule for generating equivalent fractions: multiply (or divide) both the numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number.

**Key moves:**
- Connect the visual discovery from Lesson 1 to the numerical pattern
- Show that multiplying both parts by the same number is the same as multiplying by 1 (e.g., 2/2 = 1)
- Practice generating families of equivalent fractions

**Watch for:** Students who multiply only the numerator or only the denominator. Emphasize: "Whatever you do to the top, you must also do to the bottom."

### Lesson 3: Simplifying Fractions

**Goal:** Students simplify fractions to their simplest form by dividing both the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor.

**Key moves:**
- Connect to GCF work from Unit 3 — finding the GCF is the key step
- Show that simplifying is the reverse of generating equivalent fractions
- Practice checking: "Is there any number other than 1 that divides evenly into both?"

**Watch for:** Students who divide numerator and denominator by different numbers. Students who stop before reaching simplest form (e.g., simplifying 8/12 to 4/6 but not to 2/3).

### Lesson 4: Comparing Fractions with Unlike Denominators

**Goal:** Students compare two fractions by finding a common denominator or by using benchmark fractions (especially 1/2).

**Key moves:**
- Review same-denominator comparison from Grade 3
- Introduce the common-denominator strategy: rewrite both fractions with the same denominator, then compare numerators
- Teach the benchmark strategy: compare each fraction to 1/2

**Watch for:** Students who compare numerators without finding a common denominator first. Students who think 3/8 > 2/5 "because 3 > 2" without considering the denominator.

### Lesson 5: Ordering Fractions

**Goal:** Students order three or more fractions from least to greatest using common denominators or benchmark reasoning.

**Key moves:**
- Extend the comparison strategy from Lesson 4 to multiple fractions
- Show how to find a common denominator for three or more fractions
- Practice with a mix of strategies (benchmarks for quick sorts, common denominator for precision)

**Watch for:** Students who get overwhelmed by finding a common denominator for many fractions. Suggest sorting into "less than 1/2" and "greater than 1/2" groups first, then ordering within each group.

### Lesson 6: Adding Fractions with Like Denominators

**Goal:** Students add fractions that share a denominator, understanding that they add the numerators and keep the denominator the same.

**Key moves:**
- Start with fraction strips: combine 1/8 and 3/8 by physically placing them together
- Connect to the idea of counting: "one eighth plus three eighths equals four eighths — just like one apple plus three apples equals four apples"
- Simplify results when possible

**Watch for:** Students who add both numerators AND denominators (e.g., 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/8). This is the most common error. Use visual models to disprove it.

### Lesson 7: Subtracting Fractions with Like Denominators

**Goal:** Students subtract fractions with the same denominator, subtract the numerators, keep the denominator, and simplify.

**Key moves:**
- Mirror the addition structure — start concrete, move to representational, then abstract
- Use "take away" language with fraction strips
- Practice simplifying results

**Watch for:** Same error as addition — subtracting denominators. Also watch for students who struggle when the result needs simplifying.

### Lesson 8: Mixed Numbers and Improper Fractions

**Goal:** Students understand mixed numbers and improper fractions as two ways to name the same amount, and convert between the two forms.

**Key moves:**
- Start with physical models: "If each pizza is cut into fourths and I have 7 slices, how many whole pizzas and leftover slices?"
- Teach conversion from improper to mixed: divide the numerator by the denominator
- Teach conversion from mixed to improper: multiply whole number by denominator, then add numerator

**Watch for:** Students who confuse the roles of the whole-number part and the fraction part. Students who forget to add the numerator when converting mixed to improper.

### Lesson 9: Adding and Subtracting Mixed Numbers

**Goal:** Students add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators, including regrouping when necessary.

**Key moves:**
- Start by adding whole parts and fraction parts separately
- Show what to do when the fraction sum is improper (regroup into an extra whole)
- For subtraction, show how to borrow from the whole number when needed

**Watch for:** Students who forget to regroup when the fraction sum exceeds one whole. Students who struggle with borrowing in subtraction (convert 1 whole into an equivalent fraction).

### Lesson 10: Fraction Problem Solving

**Goal:** Comprehensive review through multi-step word problems that require choosing and combining strategies from the entire unit.

**Key moves:**
- Use a variety of problem types: comparison, addition, subtraction, equivalence, mixed numbers
- Teach a problem-solving framework: read, identify the question, choose a strategy, solve, check
- Allow students to work collaboratively on some problems

**Watch for:** This lesson serves as a diagnostic before the unit assessment. Note which concept areas need additional review.

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Student adds denominators: 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/8 | Does not understand that the denominator names the size of the pieces | Return to fraction strips: "Put 1/4 and 2/4 together. Are the pieces eighths? No — they are still fourths." |
| Student thinks 3/8 > 1/2 because "3 is more than 1" | Compares numerators without considering denominators | Use visual models to compare: "Shade 3/8 and 1/2 on matching bars. Which shaded part is longer?" |
| Student says 2/3 and 4/5 are equivalent "because I added 2 to each" | Confuses additive and multiplicative relationships | "Let's check with fraction strips. Does 2/3 cover the same amount as 4/5? No! Equivalent fractions multiply both parts by the same number, not add." |
| Student simplifies 6/8 to 3/5 (subtracts 3 from each) | Subtracts instead of divides | "Let's check: is 3/5 the same as 6/8? Draw both. They are different amounts. We DIVIDE both parts by the same number." |
| Student writes 7/4 = 1 R3 instead of 1 3/4 | Applies whole-number division notation to fractions | "The remainder is 3 fourths — not just 3. Write it as a fraction: 1 and 3/4." |
| Student converts 2 1/3 to 3/3 (adds whole and numerator only) | Forgets to multiply the whole number by the denominator | Reteach: "2 wholes = 2 x 3 thirds = 6 thirds. Plus 1 third = 7 thirds. So 2 1/3 = 7/3." |

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Student-Friendly Definition |
|------|---------------------------|
| Equivalent fractions | Fractions that name the same amount (e.g., 1/2 and 2/4) |
| Simplify (reduce) | Rewrite a fraction using the smallest possible numbers by dividing both parts by their GCF |
| Simplest form | A fraction where the numerator and denominator have no common factor other than 1 |
| Common denominator | A shared denominator that allows you to compare, add, or subtract fractions |
| Benchmark fraction | A well-known fraction (like 1/2) used as a reference point for comparing |
| Improper fraction | A fraction where the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator (e.g., 7/4) |
| Mixed number | A number with a whole-number part and a fraction part (e.g., 1 3/4) |
| Numerator | The top number in a fraction — tells how many parts you have |
| Denominator | The bottom number in a fraction — tells how many equal parts make one whole |
| Greatest common factor (GCF) | The largest number that divides evenly into both the numerator and denominator |

## Conversation Starters

Use these questions throughout the unit to deepen understanding:

- "Can you show me that with a picture? What would it look like?"
- "How do you know those two fractions are equal? Can you prove it?"
- "Is this fraction more or less than one half? How can you tell without drawing?"
- "What does the denominator tell you? What does the numerator tell you?"
- "You simplified to 3/6 — is that the simplest form? How do you know?"
- "If I cut each piece in half, how many pieces do I have now? Does the amount change?"
- "Can you think of a real-life situation where you would need to compare fractions?"
- "Why do we keep the denominator the same when we add fractions with like denominators?"

## Differentiation Strategies

### Struggling Learners

- **Keep models longer:** Allow students to use fraction strips and area models through the entire unit — do not rush to purely abstract work.
- **Use smaller denominators:** Start with halves, thirds, and fourths before introducing sixths, eighths, and twelfths.
- **Provide reference sheets:** A fraction number line showing halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, and eighths helps students check their work.
- **Sentence frames:** "___/___  is equivalent to ___/___ because I multiplied both parts by ___."
- **Reduce the number of problems:** Focus on fewer problems done correctly with full understanding rather than many problems done mechanically.

### Advanced Learners

- **Extend to larger denominators:** Work with tenths, twelfths, and sixteenths.
- **Multiple strategies:** Ask students to compare fractions using both common denominators AND cross-multiplication, then explain why both work.
- **Create their own problems:** Have students write word problems that involve comparing or adding fractions, then swap with a partner.
- **Preview multiplication of fractions:** Explore what happens when you multiply a fraction by a whole number (preview of Unit 5 or later content).
- **Fraction puzzles:** Provide open-ended tasks such as "Find three different fractions between 1/3 and 1/2."

## Connection to Later Learning

The skills in this unit connect directly to:

- **Grade 4, later units:** Multiplying fractions by whole numbers; decimal-fraction connections (tenths and hundredths)
- **Grade 5:** Adding and subtracting fractions with unlike denominators; multiplying and dividing fractions and mixed numbers
- **Grade 6:** Ratio reasoning (which relies on equivalent fraction thinking); dividing fractions by fractions
- **Middle school and beyond:** Proportional reasoning, algebra, and real-world applications in science, cooking, construction, and finance

## Standards Alignment

- **4.NF.1** — Explain why a fraction a/b is equivalent to a fraction (n x a)/(n x b) by using visual fraction models, with attention to how the number and size of the parts differ even though the two fractions themselves are the same size. Use this principle to recognize and generate equivalent fractions.
- **4.NF.2** — Compare two fractions with different numerators and different denominators, e.g., by creating common denominators or numerators, or by comparing to a benchmark fraction such as 1/2. Recognize that comparisons are valid only when the two fractions refer to the same whole. Record the results of comparisons with symbols >, =, or <, and justify the conclusions.
- **4.NF.3** — Understand a fraction a/b with a > 1 as a sum of fractions 1/b.
  - 4.NF.3a — Understand addition and subtraction of fractions as joining and separating parts referring to the same whole.
  - 4.NF.3b — Decompose a fraction into a sum of fractions with the same denominator in more than one way, recording each decomposition by an equation.
  - 4.NF.3c — Add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators, e.g., by replacing each mixed number with an equivalent fraction, and/or by using properties of operations and the relationship between addition and subtraction.
  - 4.NF.3d — Solve word problems involving addition and subtraction of fractions referring to the same whole and having like denominators.
