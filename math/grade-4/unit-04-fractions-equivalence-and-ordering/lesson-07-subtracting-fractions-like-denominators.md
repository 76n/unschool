# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 7

## Topic
Subtracting Fractions with Like Denominators

## Learning Objectives
- Subtract two fractions with the same denominator
- Explain why we subtract numerators but keep the denominator the same
- Simplify the difference to its simplest form when needed
- Represent fraction subtraction using visual models and equations

## Prerequisites
- Adding fractions with like denominators (Lesson 6)
- Understanding of fractions as parts of a whole
- Simplifying fractions (Lesson 3)

## Materials Needed
- Fraction strips
- Grid paper or area model templates
- Colored pencils
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper

**Standards:** 4.NF.3a — Understand subtraction of fractions as separating parts referring to the same whole. 4.NF.3d — Solve word problems involving subtraction of fractions with like denominators.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect subtraction to addition and review prior skills.

1. Solve: 3/8 + 2/8 = ? (5/8)
2. Now solve: 5/8 - 2/8 = ? (3/8)
3. Ask: "What do you notice about problems 1 and 2?" (They are related — subtraction undoes addition.)
4. Solve: 7 apples - 3 apples = ? (4 apples)
   Then: 7 eighths - 3 eighths = ? (4 eighths = 4/8)
5. Say: "Just like addition, subtraction of fractions works by counting same-sized pieces. Today we will practice subtracting fractions with like denominators."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach subtraction of fractions with like denominators using concrete, representational, and abstract approaches.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Difference:** The result of subtracting one number from another.
- **Like denominators:** Fractions with the same denominator (same-sized pieces).
- **Separate:** To take away a part from a whole. Fraction subtraction means separating some pieces from a group of same-sized pieces.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete: Use fraction strips.**
Start with a 5/6 strip (five-sixths of a whole).

Say: "I have 5/6. If I take away 2/6, how much is left?"

Remove 2 of the sixth-sized pieces. Count what remains: 3 pieces, each one-sixth.

Say: "5/6 - 2/6 = 3/6. Can we simplify? GCF of 3 and 6 is 3. So 3/6 = 1/2."

Write: 5/6 - 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2.

**Step 2 — Representational: Use an area model.**
Draw a rectangle divided into 8 equal parts. Shade 7 parts.

Say: "This shows 7/8. Now I need to subtract 3/8. I will cross out 3 of the shaded parts."

Cross out 3 parts. Count remaining shaded parts: 4.

Say: "7/8 - 3/8 = 4/8. Simplify: GCF of 4 and 8 is 4. 4/8 = 1/2."

**Step 3 — Abstract: State the rule.**
Write on the board:

When subtracting fractions with the same denominator:
- Subtract the numerators.
- Keep the denominator the same.
- Simplify if possible.

a/d - b/d = (a - b)/d

Say: "This works exactly like addition, but we subtract the numerators instead of adding them. The denominator stays the same because the size of each piece does not change."

**Step 4 — Address the common mistake.**
Write: 5/8 - 2/8 = ?

Say: "The wrong answer would be 3/0 or 3/16 — subtracting or changing the denominators. The correct answer is 3/8. We only subtract the numerators."

**Step 5 — Connection to real life.**
Say: "If you had 7/8 of a granola bar and ate 3/8 of it, how much would be left? We subtract: 7/8 - 3/8 = 4/8 = 1/2. You would have half the bar left."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 4/5 - 1/5.
- Same denominator (fifths). Subtract numerators: 4 - 1 = 3.
- Keep denominator: 3/5.
- Simplify? GCF of 3 and 5 is 1. Already in simplest form.
- Answer: 4/5 - 1/5 = 3/5.

**Example B:** 7/12 - 1/12.
- Same denominator (twelfths). Subtract numerators: 7 - 1 = 6.
- Keep denominator: 6/12.
- Simplify? GCF of 6 and 12 is 6. 6/12 = 1/2.
- Answer: 7/12 - 1/12 = 6/12 = 1/2.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice fraction subtraction with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Subtract and Simplify**

1. 5/6 - 1/6 = ?
   Facilitator prompt: "Subtract the numerators. What do you get?" (4/6) "Can you simplify?" (Yes — GCF is 2. 4/6 = 2/3.)

2. 7/8 - 5/8 = ?
   Facilitator prompt: "Subtract the tops, keep the bottom." (2/8) "Simplify?" (2/8 = 1/4)

3. 9/10 - 3/10 = ?
   Facilitator prompt: "What is 9 - 3?" (6) "So 6/10. Simplify?" (6/10 = 3/5)

**Activity 2: Model It**

4. Draw an area model to show 3/4 - 1/4 = 2/4 = 1/2.
   Facilitator prompt: "Divide a rectangle into 4 parts. Shade 3 parts. Now cross out 1 shaded part. How many shaded parts remain?"

5. Word problem: A recipe calls for 5/8 cup of flour. You have already added 2/8 cup. How much more flour do you need?
   Facilitator prompt: "What operation do you need?" (Subtraction.) "Set it up." (5/8 - 2/8 = 3/8)

**Facilitator tip:** Students who mastered Lesson 6 often find this lesson straightforward. Use the extra time to reinforce simplifying, which is where most errors occur.

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1: On-Level
Subtract. Simplify your answer if possible.

1. 3/5 - 1/5 = ? (Answer: 2/5)
2. 5/8 - 3/8 = ? (Answer: 2/8 = 1/4)
3. 7/12 - 3/12 = ? (Answer: 4/12 = 1/3)
4. 8/10 - 2/10 = ? (Answer: 6/10 = 3/5)
5. 11/12 - 5/12 = ? (Answer: 6/12 = 1/2)

### Tier 2: Scaffolded
Fill in the blanks.

1. 4/6 - 1/6 = (4 - 1)/6 = ___/6 = ___/___ (simplified) (Answer: 3/6 = 1/2)
2. 5/8 - 1/8 = (5 - ___)/8 = ___/8 (Answer: 4/8 = 1/2)
3. 7/10 - 4/10 = ___/10 (Answer: 3/10)
4. Draw a picture to show 3/4 - 1/4 = 2/4 = 1/2.

### Tier 3: Challenge
1. 8/8 - 3/8 = ? (Answer: 5/8)
2. 1 - 2/5 = ? Hint: Rewrite 1 as 5/5. (Answer: 5/5 - 2/5 = 3/5)
3. 1 - 3/8 = ? (Answer: 8/8 - 3/8 = 5/8)
4. A ribbon is 7/12 of a yard long. If you cut off 5/12 of a yard, how much is left? Is the answer in simplest form? (7/12 - 5/12 = 2/12 = 1/6)
5. Write a word problem that could be solved with the equation 5/6 - 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2. (Answers vary. Example: "Sam had 5/6 of a pie. He gave 2/6 to his friend. How much pie does Sam have left?")

---

## Exit Ticket

1. Solve: 9/12 - 5/12 = ?. Simplify if possible.
2. A bottle was 7/10 full. Someone drank 4/10 of the bottle. How full is the bottle now?

**Mastery threshold:** Both correct with simplified answers.

---

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. 9/12 - 5/12 = 4/12 = 1/3.
2. 7/10 - 4/10 = 3/10. The bottle is 3/10 full.

---

## Teacher Notes

- This lesson mirrors Lesson 6 in structure but uses subtraction. Most students who understood addition will transfer easily. Spend your extra facilitation time on simplifying and word problems.
- Encourage students to check subtraction by adding back: "If 7/8 - 3/8 = 4/8, then 4/8 + 3/8 should equal 7/8. Does it? Yes!"
- The "subtract from 1 whole" problems in the challenge tier preview an important skill for Lesson 9 (borrowing in mixed number subtraction). Students who master it here will have an easier time later.
- Continue to reinforce the vocabulary: "We subtract the numerators because we are taking away some same-sized pieces. The denominator stays because the size of each piece does not change."
