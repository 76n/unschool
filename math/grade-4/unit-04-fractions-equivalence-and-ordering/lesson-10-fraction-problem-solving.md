# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Topic
Fraction Problem Solving

## Learning Objectives
- Solve real-world word problems involving equivalent fractions, comparing, ordering, adding, and subtracting fractions
- Choose the appropriate operation and strategy for each problem
- Explain and justify solution strategies using models, equations, or written reasoning
- Solve multi-step fraction problems

## Prerequisites
- All skills from Lessons 1-9 of this unit
- Ability to read and interpret word problems

## Materials Needed
- Fraction strips (available for reference)
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper

**Standards:** 4.NF.1, 4.NF.2, 4.NF.3d — Solve word problems involving addition and subtraction of fractions referring to the same whole and having like denominators.

**Purpose:** Quick review of key skills from the unit.

1. Simplify 6/8. (3/4)
2. Compare: 2/3 ___ 5/8. (2/3 > 5/8 — common denominator 24: 16/24 > 15/24)
3. Solve: 3/10 + 7/10. (10/10 = 1)
4. Convert 2 1/4 to an improper fraction. (9/4)
5. Say: "Today we put all of our fraction skills together to solve word problems. The most important step is figuring out WHAT the problem is asking you to do."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach a problem-solving framework for fraction word problems.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Multi-step problem:** A word problem that requires two or more operations to solve.
- **Reasonable answer:** An answer that makes sense in the context of the problem. For example, if you started with 3/4 of a pizza and ate some, your answer should be less than 3/4.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce the RSCV framework.**
Say: "When you see a fraction word problem, follow these four steps:"

Write on the board:
1. **R**ead — Read the problem carefully. What information is given?
2. **S**trategy — What does the problem ask? What operation do I need? (Compare? Add? Subtract? Find equivalents?)
3. **C**alculate — Do the math. Show your work.
4. **V**erify — Does my answer make sense? Did I answer the question that was asked?

**Step 2 — Model with a comparison problem.**
Read aloud: "Lena practiced piano for 3/4 of an hour. Marco practiced for 5/6 of an hour. Who practiced longer?"

Walk through:
- R: Lena = 3/4 hour. Marco = 5/6 hour. Question: who practiced more?
- S: This is a comparison problem. Find a common denominator.
- C: LCM of 4 and 6 is 12. 3/4 = 9/12. 5/6 = 10/12. Since 10/12 > 9/12, Marco practiced longer.
- V: Does this make sense? 3/4 = 0.75 and 5/6 is about 0.83, so yes, Marco practiced longer.

**Step 3 — Model with a multi-step problem.**
Read aloud: "A jar holds 1 cup of juice. Ava poured 3/8 cup into a glass, then her brother poured 2/8 cup more. How much juice is left in the jar?"

Walk through:
- R: Jar starts with 1 cup. Ava pours 3/8, brother pours 2/8. Question: how much is left?
- S: First, find how much was poured total (addition). Then subtract from 1 (subtraction).
- C: Total poured: 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8. Remaining: 1 - 5/8 = 8/8 - 5/8 = 3/8.
- V: 3/8 + 5/8 = 8/8 = 1. Check passes!

**Step 4 — Model with a mixed number problem.**
Read aloud: "Sarah walked 2 3/4 miles to school and 1 1/4 miles to the library after school. How far did she walk in all?"

Walk through:
- R: To school = 2 3/4. To library = 1 1/4. Question: total distance.
- S: Addition of mixed numbers.
- C: Wholes: 2 + 1 = 3. Fractions: 3/4 + 1/4 = 4/4 = 1. Total: 3 + 1 = 4 miles.
- V: 2 3/4 + 1 1/4 = 4. Makes sense — a little less than 3 plus a little more than 1.

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Diego has 5/6 yard of rope. He uses 2/6 yard for a project. Then he finds another piece that is 1/6 yard long. How much rope does Diego have now?
- Step 1: Start with 5/6. Subtract 2/6. Add 1/6.
- Step 2: 5/6 - 2/6 = 3/6. Then 3/6 + 1/6 = 4/6 = 2/3.
- Answer: Diego has 2/3 yard of rope.

**Example B:** Three friends share a large pizza equally. Each friend eats a different amount. Ana eats 2/8, Ben eats 3/8, and Chloe eats 1/8. How much of the pizza was eaten? How much is left?
- Total eaten: 2/8 + 3/8 + 1/8 = 6/8 = 3/4.
- Left over: 8/8 - 6/8 = 2/8 = 1/4.
- Answer: 3/4 was eaten. 1/4 is left.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Work through word problems together using the RSCV framework.

**Activity 1: Work Together**

1. A recipe calls for 3/4 cup of white flour and 1/4 cup of wheat flour. How much flour is needed in all?
   Facilitator prompt: "What operation?" (Addition.) "Set up the equation."
   Answer: 3/4 + 1/4 = 4/4 = 1 cup.

2. Emma ran 3/5 of a mile on Monday and 4/5 of a mile on Wednesday. How much farther did she run on Wednesday?
   Facilitator prompt: "The word 'farther' tells us to..." (Subtract.) "Set up the equation."
   Answer: 4/5 - 3/5 = 1/5 mile.

3. Two painters are painting a fence. Painter A has finished 3/10 of the fence. Painter B has finished 4/10. Have they painted more than half the fence?
   Facilitator prompt: "First, find how much they painted total. Then compare to 1/2."
   Answer: 3/10 + 4/10 = 7/10. Is 7/10 > 1/2? Yes (1/2 = 5/10, and 7/10 > 5/10). They painted more than half.

**Activity 2: Multi-Step**

4. Tom had 3 1/6 pounds of clay. He used 1 4/6 pounds for a sculpture. How much clay does he have left?
   Facilitator prompt: "Do you need to borrow?" (Yes — 1/6 < 4/6.) "Rewrite 3 1/6 with borrowing."
   Answer: 3 1/6 = 2 7/6. Then 2 7/6 - 1 4/6 = 1 3/6 = 1 1/2 pounds.

**Facilitator tip:** Encourage students to underline the question in each problem before they start calculating. Many errors come from answering a different question than what was asked.

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1: On-Level

1. A ribbon is 7/8 of a yard long. Marie cuts off 3/8 of a yard. How much ribbon is left? (Answer: 7/8 - 3/8 = 4/8 = 1/2 yard)

2. Jack ate 2/6 of a pie, and his sister ate 3/6. What fraction of the pie did they eat together? Simplify your answer. (Answer: 2/6 + 3/6 = 5/6)

3. Who has more stickers: Lily with 3/4 of a sheet, or Zoe with 2/3 of a sheet? By how much more? (Answer: Common denominator 12. 3/4 = 9/12, 2/3 = 8/12. Lily has more. Difference: 9/12 - 8/12 = 1/12)

4. A baker made 4 5/8 pounds of bread dough. He used 2 3/8 pounds for rolls. How much dough is left? (Answer: 4 5/8 - 2 3/8 = 2 2/8 = 2 1/4 pounds)

5. Mia walked 1 3/10 miles in the morning and 2 9/10 miles in the afternoon. How far did she walk in total? (Answer: 1 3/10 + 2 9/10 = 3 12/10 = 4 2/10 = 4 1/5 miles)

### Tier 2: Scaffolded

1. Dan has 5/8 cup of juice. He drinks 2/8 cup. How much is left?
   Operation: ___ (subtraction)
   Equation: 5/8 - 2/8 = ___/8
   Answer: ___/8 (Answer: 3/8)

2. Which is greater: 1/3 of a pizza or 2/5 of a pizza?
   Common denominator: ___
   1/3 = ___/___, 2/5 = ___/___
   ___ is greater. (Answer: 15. 1/3 = 5/15, 2/5 = 6/15. 2/5 is greater.)

3. A trail is 2 1/4 miles long. You have walked 1 2/4 miles. How far do you have left to walk?
   ___ ___/4 - ___ ___/4 = ___ ___/4
   (Answer: 2 1/4 - 1 2/4. Borrow: 1 5/4 - 1 2/4 = 0 3/4 = 3/4 mile)

### Tier 3: Challenge

1. A fruit salad recipe uses 3/8 cup of strawberries, 2/8 cup of blueberries, and 1/8 cup of raspberries. How many cups of berries are needed? If Kayla doubles the recipe, how many cups of berries does she need? (Answer: 3/8 + 2/8 + 1/8 = 6/8 = 3/4 cup. Doubled: 3/4 + 3/4 = 6/4 = 1 2/4 = 1 1/2 cups.)

2. Three students measured the length of a table. Aiden got 3 1/4 feet, Bella got 3 3/8 feet, and Carlos got 3 2/8 feet. Who got the longest measurement? Order all three from shortest to longest. (Answer: Convert to eighths. 3 1/4 = 3 2/8. Carlos = 3 2/8 = 3 1/4. Bella = 3 3/8. Order: Aiden = Carlos (3 2/8), then Bella (3 3/8). Bella measured longest.)

3. A water tank holds 5 gallons. In the morning, 1 3/4 gallons are used. In the afternoon, 2 1/4 gallons are used. How much water is left in the tank? (Answer: Total used: 1 3/4 + 2 1/4 = 3 4/4 = 4 gallons. Left: 5 - 4 = 1 gallon.)

4. Write your own fraction word problem that involves both addition and subtraction. Solve it and provide the answer key.

## Exit Ticket

1. Solve: A painter has 3 5/6 gallons of paint. She uses 1 2/6 gallons on Monday and 1 4/6 gallons on Tuesday. How much paint is left? Show all your work.

2. In your own words, explain what the most important step is when solving a fraction word problem and why.

**Mastery threshold:** Problem 1 correct with work shown. Problem 2 provides a reasonable explanation.

---

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Total used: 1 2/6 + 1 4/6 = 2 6/6 = 3 gallons. (Or: wholes 1+1=2, fractions 2/6 + 4/6 = 6/6 = 1, combine: 3.)
   Paint left: 3 5/6 - 3 = 5/6 gallon.
   (Alternate: 3 5/6 - 1 2/6 = 2 3/6 = 2 1/2. Then 2 1/2 - 1 4/6. Convert: 2 3/6 - 1 4/6. Borrow: 1 9/6 - 1 4/6 = 5/6.)

2. Answers will vary. Strong answers mention: understanding what the problem is asking (identifying the operation) before calculating. This is important because using the wrong operation gives a wrong answer even if the arithmetic is perfect.

## Teacher Notes

- This lesson is primarily a review and synthesis lesson. It is designed to reveal which students have gaps before the unit assessment. Take careful notes on which problem types cause difficulty.
- If a student gets the arithmetic right but sets up the wrong operation, the issue is reading comprehension, not fraction skills. Help them identify key words: "total" and "altogether" suggest addition; "left," "more than," and "difference" suggest subtraction; "which is greater" suggests comparison.
- Multi-step problems are the biggest challenge. Encourage students to write one equation at a time rather than trying to do everything in their heads.
- Allow students to use fraction strips as a checking tool if they wish. The goal is correct problem solving, and visual verification builds confidence.
- This is a good lesson for collaborative work. Pair students who struggle with reading comprehension with students who struggle with computation — they can support each other.
