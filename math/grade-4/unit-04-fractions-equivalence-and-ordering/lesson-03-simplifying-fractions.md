# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 3

## Topic
Simplifying Fractions

## Learning Objectives
- Define "simplest form" and explain what it means for a fraction
- Find the greatest common factor (GCF) of the numerator and denominator
- Simplify a fraction by dividing both parts by their GCF
- Determine whether a given fraction is already in simplest form

## Prerequisites
- Generating equivalent fractions by multiplying and dividing (Lesson 2)
- Finding factor pairs and GCF of two numbers (Unit 3, Lessons 1-2, 6)
- Recognizing equivalent fractions with visual models (Lesson 1)

## Materials Needed
- Fraction strips
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Pencil and paper

**Standards:** 4.NF.1 — Recognize and generate equivalent fractions.

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students to simplify fractions to their simplest form using the GCF.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Simplify (reduce):** To rewrite a fraction in an equivalent form using smaller numbers by dividing both the numerator and denominator by a common factor.
- **Simplest form (lowest terms):** A fraction is in simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor other than 1. For example, 3/4 is in simplest form, but 6/8 is not.
- **Greatest common factor (GCF):** The largest number that divides evenly into both the numerator and the denominator.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce the problem.**
Write on the board: 6/8.

Say: "Is 6/8 the simplest way to write this fraction? Can we find an equivalent fraction that uses smaller numbers?"

Draw two rectangles:
- Rectangle 1: Divide into 8 parts, shade 6. Label: 6/8.
- Rectangle 2: Divide into 4 parts, shade 3. Label: 3/4.

Say: "The shaded areas are the same! So 6/8 = 3/4. But 3/4 uses smaller numbers. We say 3/4 is the simplified form of 6/8."

**Step 2 — Show the method.**
Say: "To simplify, we divide BOTH the numerator and denominator by the same number. Let's use the GCF — that way we get to simplest form in one step."

Write:
- Numerator: 6, Denominator: 8
- Factors of 6: 1, 2, 3, 6
- Factors of 8: 1, 2, 4, 8
- Common factors: 1, 2
- GCF = 2
- 6/8 = (6 / 2)/(8 / 2) = 3/4

Say: "We divided both 6 and 8 by their GCF, which is 2. We got 3/4. Is 3/4 in simplest form? Let's check: the only common factor of 3 and 4 is 1. So yes, 3/4 is the simplest form."

**Step 3 — Show what happens if you do NOT use the GCF.**
Say: "What if we used a smaller common factor instead of the GCF?"

Write: 8/12. The GCF of 8 and 12 is 4.

"Suppose we divide by 2 instead of 4:"
- 8/12 = (8 / 2)/(12 / 2) = 4/6

"Is 4/6 in simplest form? No! 4 and 6 still share a common factor of 2. We have to simplify again:"
- 4/6 = (4 / 2)/(6 / 2) = 2/3

"Now 2/3 is in simplest form. We got there, but it took two steps instead of one. Using the GCF gets us there in one step."

**Step 4 — How to check simplest form.**
Say: "A fraction is in simplest form when the only common factor of the numerator and denominator is 1. Quick check: Can I divide both by 2? By 3? By any number? If the answer is no, it is in simplest form."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Simplify 9/12.
- Find the GCF of 9 and 12.
- Factors of 9: 1, 3, 9
- Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
- GCF = 3
- 9/12 = (9 / 3)/(12 / 3) = 3/4
- Check: GCF of 3 and 4 is 1. This is simplest form.

**Example B:** Simplify 10/15.
- Find the GCF of 10 and 15.
- Factors of 10: 1, 2, 5, 10
- Factors of 15: 1, 3, 5, 15
- GCF = 5
- 10/15 = (10 / 5)/(15 / 5) = 2/3
- Check: GCF of 2 and 3 is 1. This is simplest form.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice simplifying with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Simplify These Fractions**

1. Simplify 4/8.
   Facilitator prompt: "What is the GCF of 4 and 8?" (4) "Divide both by 4."
   Answer: 4/8 = 1/2

2. Simplify 6/9.
   Facilitator prompt: "List factors of 6 and 9. What is the GCF?" (3) "Divide both by 3."
   Answer: 6/9 = 2/3

3. Simplify 10/12.
   Facilitator prompt: "What numbers divide evenly into both 10 and 12?" (1 and 2. GCF = 2.)
   Answer: 10/12 = 5/6

**Activity 2: Is It Already in Simplest Form?**

4. Is 5/8 in simplest form?
   Facilitator prompt: "What factors do 5 and 8 share?" (Only 1.) "Then yes, 5/8 is already in simplest form."

5. Is 4/10 in simplest form?
   Facilitator prompt: "Both are even — can we divide by 2?" (Yes.) "So no, it is not in simplest form. Simplify it." (4/10 = 2/5)

**Facilitator tip:** Encourage students to always check whether both numbers are even — if they are, the fraction can definitely be simplified (divide both by 2 at minimum).

### Tier 2: Scaffolded
Fill in the blanks.

4. Circle the fractions that are already in simplest form: 2/5, 4/6, 7/8, 3/12, 5/9
   (Answer: 2/5, 7/8, 5/9 are in simplest form.)

### Tier 3: Challenge
1. Simplify 24/36. (Answer: 2/3, GCF = 12)
2. Simplify 15/45. (Answer: 1/3, GCF = 15)
3. A student simplified 12/18 to 4/6 and stopped. Is the student done? Explain. (No — 4/6 can be simplified further to 2/3. The student divided by 3 instead of the GCF of 6.)
4. Find a fraction with a denominator of 20 that simplifies to 3/4. (Answer: 15/20)
5. Explain why you cannot simplify 7/11. (The GCF of 7 and 11 is 1. Both are prime numbers with no shared factors other than 1.)

## Exit Ticket

1. Simplify 8/10 to simplest form. Show your work.
2. Is 5/12 in simplest form? How do you know?

**Mastery threshold:** Both correct.

---

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. GCF of 8 and 10 = 2. 8/10 = (8 / 2)/(10 / 2) = 4/5.
2. Yes. The factors of 5 are 1 and 5. The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. They share only 1 as a common factor, so 5/12 is in simplest form.

## Teacher Notes

- This lesson connects directly to Unit 3 (GCF). If a student struggles here, it may be because they are not fluent with finding the GCF. Consider a brief review of the factor-listing method from Unit 3, Lesson 6.
- Some students may prefer to simplify in multiple steps (divide by 2, then by 2 again) rather than finding the GCF first. This is acceptable as long as they reach simplest form. Over time, encourage the GCF method for efficiency.
- A common error: students who subtract the same number from both numerator and denominator instead of dividing. For example, simplifying 6/8 as (6-2)/(8-2) = 4/6. If you see this, use a visual model to show that 4/6 is NOT equal to 6/8 via subtraction — the correct method is division.
- From this point forward, students should simplify all fraction answers throughout the unit. Reinforce this expectation consistently.
