# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 2, Lesson 4

## Topic

Introduction to Long Division (2-Digit ÷ 1-Digit, No Remainder)

## Learning Objectives

- Students will use the long division algorithm to divide a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number (no remainder).
- Students will connect each step of the algorithm to place value (dividing tens, then ones).
- Students will verify their answers using multiplication.

## Prerequisites

- Division as inverse of multiplication (Lesson 1)
- Dividing multiples of 10 (Lesson 2)
- Estimating quotients (Lesson 3)

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Student scratch paper or grid paper (for aligning digits)
- Base-ten blocks (tens rods and ones cubes)
- Place-value chart handout

**Standards:** 4.NBT.6 — Find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to four-digit dividends and one-digit divisors.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate division facts and estimation skills that support long division.

1. 60 ÷ 3 = ? (20)
2. 80 ÷ 4 = ? (20)
3. Estimate: 84 ÷ 4 ≈ ? (About 20, since 80 ÷ 4 = 20)
4. Ask: "Do you think 84 ÷ 4 is exactly 20? Why or why not?" (No — 84 is more than 80, so the answer should be a bit more than 20.)

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Long division:** A step-by-step method for dividing multi-digit numbers.
- **Division bracket:** The symbol ⟌ used to set up a long division problem.
- **Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring down (DMSB):** The four repeating steps in the long division algorithm.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Set up the problem concretely.**
Problem: 84 ÷ 4
Display 84 with base-ten blocks: 8 tens rods, 4 ones cubes.
Say: "We need to share 84 equally into 4 groups."

**Step 2 — Divide the tens.**
Say: "Start with the tens. We have 8 tens. How many tens go into each of the 4 groups?"
8 tens ÷ 4 = 2 tens per group.
Place 2 tens rods in each of 4 groups.
"We've used all 8 tens (4 × 2 tens = 8 tens). Nothing left over in the tens."

**Step 3 — Divide the ones.**
Say: "Now the ones. We have 4 ones. How many ones go into each of the 4 groups?"
4 ones ÷ 4 = 1 one per group.
"Each group now has 2 tens and 1 one = 21."

Say: "So 84 ÷ 4 = 21. Check: 4 × 21 = 84. ✓"

**Step 4 — Connect to the written algorithm.**
Write on the board:

```
    2 1
   -----
4 ) 8 4
  - 8       ← 4 × 2 = 8 (tens)
    0 4     ← bring down the 4
  -   4     ← 4 × 1 = 4 (ones)
      0     ← nothing left over
```

Walk through each step:
1. **Divide:** How many 4s in 8? → 2. Write 2 above the 8.
2. **Multiply:** 4 × 2 = 8. Write 8 below the 8.
3. **Subtract:** 8 − 8 = 0.
4. **Bring down:** Bring down the 4 to make 04.
5. **Divide:** How many 4s in 4? → 1. Write 1 above the 4.
6. **Multiply:** 4 × 1 = 4. Write 4 below.
7. **Subtract:** 4 − 4 = 0. No remainder.

Say: "The cycle is always the same: Divide → Multiply → Subtract → Bring down. Then repeat."

**Step 5 — Another example with the algorithm.**
Problem: 96 ÷ 3

```
    3 2
   -----
3 ) 9 6
  - 9       ← 3 × 3 = 9
    0 6     ← bring down 6
  -   6     ← 3 × 2 = 6
      0
```

Walk through: "How many 3s in 9? → 3. Write 3. Multiply: 3 × 3 = 9. Subtract: 9 − 9 = 0. Bring down 6. How many 3s in 6? → 2. Write 2. Multiply: 3 × 2 = 6. Subtract: 6 − 6 = 0."

Check: 3 × 32 = 96. ✓

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 78 ÷ 6

```
    1 3
   -----
6 ) 7 8
  - 6       ← 6 × 1 = 6
    1 8     ← bring down 8
  - 1 8     ← 6 × 3 = 18
      0
```

- Divide: 7 ÷ 6 = 1 (6 goes into 7 once). Write 1.
- Multiply: 6 × 1 = 6. Subtract: 7 − 6 = 1.
- Bring down 8 → 18.
- Divide: 18 ÷ 6 = 3. Write 3.
- Multiply: 6 × 3 = 18. Subtract: 18 − 18 = 0.
- Answer: **13**. Check: 6 × 13 = 78 ✓

**Example B:** 95 ÷ 5

```
    1 9
   -----
5 ) 9 5
  - 5       ← 5 × 1 = 5
    4 5     ← bring down 5
  - 4 5     ← 5 × 9 = 45
      0
```

- Answer: **19**. Check: 5 × 19 = 95 ✓

**Example C:** 72 ÷ 4

```
    1 8
   -----
4 ) 7 2
  - 4       ← 4 × 1 = 4
    3 2     ← bring down 2
  - 3 2     ← 4 × 8 = 32
      0
```

- Answer: **18**. Check: 4 × 18 = 72 ✓

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Complete these together. The student should set up the division bracket, work through each step, and check with multiplication.

1. 68 ÷ 4 = ? → **17** (Check: 4 × 17 = 68)
2. 84 ÷ 7 = ? → **12** (Check: 7 × 12 = 84)
3. 56 ÷ 4 = ? → **14** (Check: 4 × 14 = 56)
4. 96 ÷ 8 = ? → **12** (Check: 8 × 12 = 96)

**Facilitator tip:** If the student is unsure how many times the divisor goes into a digit, redirect to multiplication: "What times 4 is close to 7 but not more than 7?"

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Long Division (No Remainder)

Set up and solve each problem using the long division algorithm. Check each answer with multiplication.

1. 48 ÷ 3 = ___
2. 75 ÷ 5 = ___
3. 84 ÷ 6 = ___
4. 91 ÷ 7 = ___
5. 72 ÷ 3 = ___
6. 96 ÷ 6 = ___

### Part B: Matching

Match each division problem to its quotient.

| Problem | Quotients |
|---------|-----------|
| 52 ÷ 4 | 14 |
| 78 ÷ 3 | 13 |
| 98 ÷ 7 | 26 |

### Part C: Word Problem

A baker made 76 muffins and wants to put them equally into 4 boxes. How many muffins go in each box? Show your long division work and check with multiplication.

### Answer Key

**Part A:**
1. 16 (3 × 16 = 48 ✓)
2. 15 (5 × 15 = 75 ✓)
3. 14 (6 × 14 = 84 ✓)
4. 13 (7 × 13 = 91 ✓)
5. 24 (3 × 24 = 72 ✓)
6. 16 (6 × 16 = 96 ✓)

**Part B:**
- 52 ÷ 4 = 13
- 78 ÷ 3 = 26
- 98 ÷ 7 = 14

**Part C:**
76 ÷ 4 = 19 muffins per box. Check: 4 × 19 = 76 ✓

## Assessment Check

Ask the student to solve 85 ÷ 5 using the long division algorithm and explain each step aloud.

**Target:** Student correctly sets up the bracket, works through D-M-S-B for each place value, arrives at 17, and checks: 5 × 17 = 85.

## Extension

- Challenge: 96 ÷ 4 (= 24) — notice the tens digit divides evenly but gives a larger quotient digit.
- Preview: "What if 7 doesn't divide into the tens digit evenly? What if we had 85 ÷ 7? We'll explore remainders in the next lesson."
