# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 2, Lesson 7

## Topic

Long Division: 3-Digit ÷ 1-Digit (With Remainder)

## Learning Objectives

- Students will perform long division of a 3-digit number by a 1-digit number when there is a remainder.
- Students will handle dividends containing zeros (e.g., 305 ÷ 4).
- Students will verify all answers using the check: quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend.

## Prerequisites

- Long division of 3-digit numbers without remainders (Lesson 6)
- Understanding of remainders (Lesson 5)
- Estimation (Lesson 3)

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Grid paper (for alignment)
- Student scratch paper

**Standards:** 4.NBT.6 — Find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to four-digit dividends and one-digit divisors.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review 3-digit division and set up for remainders.

1. Solve: 468 ÷ 4 = ? (117)
2. Estimate: 470 ÷ 4 ≈ ? (About 117-118)
3. Ask: "Will 470 ÷ 4 divide evenly? How do you know?" (No — 4 × 117 = 468, and 470 is 2 more, so there will be a remainder of 2.)

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Standard 3-digit problem with remainder.**
Problem: 547 ÷ 3

Estimate first: 3 × 180 = 540, so the answer is about 180.

```
    1 8 2  R1
   -------
3 ) 5 4 7
  - 3         ← 3 × 1 = 3
    2 4       ← bring down 4
  - 2 4       ← 3 × 8 = 24
    0 0 7     ← bring down 7
  -     6     ← 3 × 2 = 6
        1     ← remainder
```

Cycle 1 — Hundreds: 5 ÷ 3 = 1. Multiply: 3. Subtract: 5 − 3 = 2.
Cycle 2 — Tens: Bring down 4 → 24. 24 ÷ 3 = 8. Multiply: 24. Subtract: 24 − 24 = 0.
Cycle 3 — Ones: Bring down 7. 7 ÷ 3 = 2. Multiply: 6. Subtract: 7 − 6 = 1. Remainder: 1.

Answer: 182 R1. Check: 182 × 3 + 1 = 546 + 1 = 547 ✓

**Step 2 — Dividend with a zero in the middle.**
Problem: 305 ÷ 4

Estimate: 4 × 75 = 300, so about 75.

```
    7 6  R1
   -----
4 ) 3 0 5
  - 2 8       ← 4 × 7 = 28
    0 2 5     ← bring down 5
  -   2 4     ← 4 × 6 = 24
        1     ← remainder
```

- 4 doesn't go into 3, so look at 30. 30 ÷ 4 = 7. Write 7 above the 0.
- Multiply: 4 × 7 = 28. Subtract: 30 − 28 = 2. Bring down 5 → 25.
- 25 ÷ 4 = 6. Multiply: 24. Subtract: 25 − 24 = 1.

Answer: 76 R1. Check: 76 × 4 + 1 = 304 + 1 = 305 ✓

**Step 3 — Larger quotient with remainder.**
Problem: 893 ÷ 7

Estimate: 7 × 127 ≈ 889, so about 127.

```
    1 2 7  R4
   -------
7 ) 8 9 3
  - 7         ← 7 × 1 = 7
    1 9       ← bring down 9
  - 1 4       ← 7 × 2 = 14
    0 5 3     ← bring down 3
  -   4 9     ← 7 × 7 = 49
        4     ← remainder
```

Answer: 127 R4. Check: 127 × 7 + 4 = 889 + 4 = 893 ✓

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 415 ÷ 6

```
    6 9  R1
   -----
6 ) 4 1 5
  - 4 1 4 ...
```

Wait — let me redo this properly:

- 6 doesn't go into 4, so look at 41. 41 ÷ 6 = 6. Write 6.
- Multiply: 6 × 6 = 36. Subtract: 41 − 36 = 5. Bring down 5 → 55.
- 55 ÷ 6 = 9. Multiply: 6 × 9 = 54. Subtract: 55 − 54 = 1.

```
    6 9  R1
   -----
6 ) 4 1 5
  - 3 6       ← 6 × 6 = 36
    0 5 5     ← bring down 5
  -   5 4     ← 6 × 9 = 54
        1     ← remainder
```

- Answer: **69 R1**. Check: 69 × 6 + 1 = 414 + 1 = 415 ✓

**Example B:** 740 ÷ 9

```
    8 2  R2
   -----
9 ) 7 4 0
  - 7 2       ← 9 × 8 = 72
    0 2 0     ← bring down 0
  -   1 8     ← 9 × 2 = 18
        2     ← remainder
```

- Answer: **82 R2**. Check: 82 × 9 + 2 = 738 + 2 = 740 ✓

**Example C:** 503 ÷ 8

```
    6 2  R7
   -----
8 ) 5 0 3
  - 4 8       ← 8 × 6 = 48
    0 2 3     ← bring down 3
  -   1 6     ← 8 × 2 = 16
        7     ← remainder
```

- Answer: **62 R7**. Check: 62 × 8 + 7 = 496 + 7 = 503 ✓

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these together.

1. 359 ÷ 5 = ? → **71 R4** (Check: 71 × 5 + 4 = 359)
2. 623 ÷ 8 = ? → **77 R7** (Check: 77 × 8 + 7 = 623)
3. 407 ÷ 6 = ? → **67 R5** (Check: 67 × 6 + 5 = 407)
4. 950 ÷ 7 = ? → **135 R5** (Check: 135 × 7 + 5 = 950)

**Facilitator tip:** For problem #3, watch for the zero in the dividend — students may skip it or mishandle it. Ask: "When you look at 40, how many 6s fit?"

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: 3-Digit Long Division with Remainders

Solve each problem. Show all work and verify with the check formula.

1. 237 ÷ 4 = ___
2. 518 ÷ 6 = ___
3. 675 ÷ 8 = ___
4. 829 ÷ 9 = ___
5. 403 ÷ 7 = ___
6. 560 ÷ 3 = ___

### Part B: Estimate First, Then Solve

For each problem, write an estimate first, then solve exactly. Was your estimate close?

1. 347 ÷ 5 = ___
2. 761 ÷ 8 = ___

### Part C: Word Problem

A farmer has 485 eggs. He packs them into cartons of 6 eggs each. How many full cartons can he fill? How many eggs are left over?

### Answer Key

**Part A:**
1. 59 R1 (59 × 4 + 1 = 237 ✓)
2. 86 R2 (86 × 6 + 2 = 518 ✓)
3. 84 R3 (84 × 8 + 3 = 675 ✓)
4. 92 R1 (92 × 9 + 1 = 829 ✓)
5. 57 R4 (57 × 7 + 4 = 403 ✓)
6. 186 R2 (186 × 3 + 2 = 560 ✓)

**Part B:**
1. Estimate ≈ 70 (350 ÷ 5 = 70). Exact: 69 R2 (69 × 5 + 2 = 347). Close!
2. Estimate ≈ 95 (760 ÷ 8 = 95). Exact: 95 R1 (95 × 8 + 1 = 761). Very close!

**Part C:**
485 ÷ 6 = 80 R5. He can fill 80 full cartons with 5 eggs left over. Check: 80 × 6 + 5 = 485 ✓

## Assessment Check

Have the student solve 502 ÷ 7 and explain their thinking at each step.

**Target:** 71 R5. Check: 71 × 7 + 5 = 497 + 5 = 502. Student should handle the zero in 502 correctly and explain why 7 doesn't go into 5 initially, so they use 50.

## Extension

- Challenge: 999 ÷ 7 = ? (142 R5)
- Explore: "What's the biggest possible remainder when dividing by 7?" (6 — the remainder is always less than the divisor.)
