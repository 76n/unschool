# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 2, Lesson 6

## Topic

Long Division: 3-Digit ÷ 1-Digit (No Remainder)

## Learning Objectives

- Students will perform long division of a 3-digit number by a 1-digit number (no remainder).
- Students will handle the full D-M-S-B cycle three times (hundreds, tens, ones).
- Students will correctly place zeros in the quotient when needed.

## Prerequisites

- Long division of 2-digit numbers (Lessons 4-5)
- Place value through hundreds
- Multiplication of multi-digit numbers (Unit 1)

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Grid paper (for aligning digits in the algorithm)
- Student scratch paper
- Base-ten blocks (optional — hundreds flats, tens rods, ones cubes)

**Standards:** 4.NBT.6 — Find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to four-digit dividends and one-digit divisors.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review 2-digit long division and estimate a 3-digit quotient.

1. Solve: 96 ÷ 4 = ? (24)
2. Estimate: 396 ÷ 4 ≈ ? (About 100 — since 400 ÷ 4 = 100)
3. Ask: "How is dividing 396 ÷ 4 different from 96 ÷ 4?" (We have one more place value — hundreds — to deal with first.)

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Hundreds place:** The third digit from the right. In long division, we start here for 3-digit dividends.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Set up and estimate.**
Problem: 396 ÷ 3
Estimate: 3 × 100 = 300, 3 × 130 = 390, 3 × 132 = 396. So the answer is about 130.

**Step 2 — Walk through the algorithm.**

```
    1 3 2
   -------
3 ) 3 9 6
  - 3         ← 3 × 1 = 3 (hundreds)
    0 9       ← bring down 9
  -   9       ← 3 × 3 = 9 (tens)
    0 0 6     ← bring down 6
  -     6     ← 3 × 2 = 6 (ones)
        0
```

Walk through each cycle:

**Cycle 1 — Hundreds:**
- Divide: 3 ÷ 3 = 1. Write 1 in the hundreds place of the quotient.
- Multiply: 3 × 1 = 3.
- Subtract: 3 − 3 = 0.
- Bring down: the 9.

**Cycle 2 — Tens:**
- Divide: 9 ÷ 3 = 3. Write 3 in the tens place.
- Multiply: 3 × 3 = 9.
- Subtract: 9 − 9 = 0.
- Bring down: the 6.

**Cycle 3 — Ones:**
- Divide: 6 ÷ 3 = 2. Write 2 in the ones place.
- Multiply: 3 × 2 = 6.
- Subtract: 6 − 6 = 0. Done.

Answer: 132. Check: 3 × 132 = 396 ✓

**Step 3 — Example where the divisor doesn't go into the hundreds digit evenly.**
Problem: 258 ÷ 6

```
    4 3
   -----
6 ) 2 5 8
  - 2 4       ← 6 × 4 = 24
    0 1 8     ← bring down 8
  -   1 8     ← 6 × 3 = 18
        0
```

Important: 6 doesn't go into 2, so we look at 25 (the first two digits).
- Divide: 25 ÷ 6 = 4. Write 4 above the 5 (tens place).
- Multiply: 6 × 4 = 24. Subtract: 25 − 24 = 1.
- Bring down: 8 → 18.
- Divide: 18 ÷ 6 = 3. Write 3.
- Multiply: 6 × 3 = 18. Subtract: 18 − 18 = 0.

Answer: 43. Check: 6 × 43 = 258 ✓

Say: "Notice the quotient is only 2 digits even though the dividend was 3 digits. That's because 6 is too big to go into 2, so the quotient starts in the tens place."

**Step 4 — Zero in the quotient.**
Problem: 612 ÷ 6

```
    1 0 2
   -------
6 ) 6 1 2
  - 6         ← 6 × 1 = 6
    0 1       ← bring down 1
  -   0       ← 6 × 0 = 0 (6 doesn't go into 1)
    0 1 2     ← bring down 2
  -   1 2     ← 6 × 2 = 12
        0
```

- Divide: 6 ÷ 6 = 1. Write 1.
- Multiply: 6 × 1 = 6. Subtract: 6 − 6 = 0. Bring down 1.
- Divide: 1 ÷ 6 = 0. **Write 0.** This is critical — the zero holds the tens place!
- Multiply: 6 × 0 = 0. Subtract: 1 − 0 = 1. Bring down 2 → 12.
- Divide: 12 ÷ 6 = 2. Write 2.
- Multiply: 6 × 2 = 12. Subtract: 12 − 12 = 0.

Answer: 102. Check: 6 × 102 = 612 ✓

Say: "Don't skip the zero! If you wrote 12 instead of 102, your answer would be way too small. The zero holds the place value."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 468 ÷ 4

```
    1 1 7
   -------
4 ) 4 6 8
  - 4         ← 4 × 1 = 4
    0 6       ← bring down 6
  -   4       ← 4 × 1 = 4
    0 2 8     ← bring down 8
  -   2 8     ← 4 × 7 = 28
        0
```

- Answer: **117**. Check: 4 × 117 = 468 ✓

**Example B:** 945 ÷ 9

```
    1 0 5
   -------
9 ) 9 4 5
  - 9         ← 9 × 1 = 9
    0 4       ← bring down 4
  -   0       ← 9 × 0 = 0
    0 4 5     ← bring down 5
  -   4 5     ← 9 × 5 = 45
        0
```

- Answer: **105**. Check: 9 × 105 = 945 ✓

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these together. Student sets up the bracket, works through all cycles, and checks.

1. 372 ÷ 3 = ? → **124** (Check: 3 × 124 = 372)
2. 584 ÷ 8 = ? → **73** (Check: 8 × 73 = 584)
3. 510 ÷ 5 = ? → **102** (Check: 5 × 102 = 510)

**Facilitator tip:** For #3, emphasize that the zero in the quotient (102) is essential. Ask: "What does the 0 in 102 represent?" (0 tens — there are no full groups of 5 in the 1 left after dividing the hundreds.)

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Long Division (3-Digit, No Remainder)

Solve each problem. Show your work and check with multiplication.

1. 246 ÷ 6 = ___
2. 396 ÷ 4 = ___
3. 575 ÷ 5 = ___
4. 378 ÷ 9 = ___
5. 424 ÷ 8 = ___
6. 721 ÷ 7 = ___

### Part B: Zero in the Quotient

Solve carefully — watch for zeros!

1. 315 ÷ 3 = ___
2. 832 ÷ 8 = ___
3. 918 ÷ 9 = ___

### Part C: Word Problem

A school ordered 456 pencils to be divided equally among 8 classrooms. How many pencils does each classroom get?

### Answer Key

**Part A:**
1. 41 (6 × 41 = 246 ✓)
2. 99 (4 × 99 = 396 ✓)
3. 115 (5 × 115 = 575 ✓)
4. 42 (9 × 42 = 378 ✓)
5. 53 (8 × 53 = 424 ✓)
6. 103 (7 × 103 = 721 ✓)

**Part B:**
1. 105 (3 × 105 = 315 ✓)
2. 104 (8 × 104 = 832 ✓)
3. 102 (9 × 102 = 918 ✓)

**Part C:**
456 ÷ 8 = 57 pencils per classroom. Check: 8 × 57 = 456 ✓

## Assessment Check

Have the student solve 612 ÷ 6 and explain why there is a zero in the quotient.

**Target response:** 612 ÷ 6 = 102. After dividing the hundreds (6 ÷ 6 = 1) and bringing down the 1, 6 doesn't go into 1, so we write 0 in the tens place. Then we bring down the 2 to get 12, and 12 ÷ 6 = 2.
