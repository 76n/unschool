# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 2, Lesson 2

## Topic

Dividing Multiples of 10 and 100

## Learning Objectives

- Students will use place-value patterns to divide a multiple of 10 by a one-digit number (e.g., 240 ÷ 6 = 40).
- Students will use place-value patterns to divide a multiple of 100 by a one-digit number (e.g., 3,600 ÷ 9 = 400).
- Students will explain why the "basic fact plus zeros" strategy works using place value.

## Prerequisites

- Division as inverse of multiplication (Lesson 1)
- Multiplying by multiples of 10 and 100 (Unit 1, Lesson 1)
- Understanding of place value through thousands

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Base-ten blocks (optional, for concrete modeling)
- Place-value chart (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands)

**Standards:** 4.NBT.6 — Find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to four-digit dividends and one-digit divisors, using strategies based on place value.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Connect multiplication of multiples of 10/100 to the division they'll learn today.

1. Solve these multiplication problems:
   - 6 × 40 = ? (240)
   - 8 × 50 = ? (400)
   - 3 × 200 = ? (600)
2. Ask: "If 6 × 40 = 240, what is 240 ÷ 6?" (40)
3. Ask: "If 3 × 200 = 600, what is 600 ÷ 3?" (200)
4. Say: "Today we'll use these patterns to divide multiples of 10 and 100 quickly."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Multiple of 10:** A number divisible by 10 (10, 20, 30, 40, ...).
- **Multiple of 100:** A number divisible by 100 (100, 200, 300, ...).
- **Basic fact:** The related single-digit division fact (e.g., for 240 ÷ 6, the basic fact is 24 ÷ 6 = 4).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Start with a concrete model.**
Display 180 using base-ten blocks: 1 hundred flat, 8 ten rods.
Say: "We want to divide 180 into 6 equal groups. Let's first think of 180 as 18 tens."
Distribute: 18 tens ÷ 6 = 3 tens per group = 30.
So 180 ÷ 6 = 30.

**Step 2 — Connect to the basic fact.**
Write:
- Basic fact: 18 ÷ 6 = 3
- 180 ÷ 6 = 30

Say: "180 is 18 × 10. When we divide 180 by 6, we're really dividing 18 tens by 6, which gives 3 tens. The basic fact (18 ÷ 6 = 3) gives us the digits, and the place value tells us those digits represent tens."

**Step 3 — Practice with more multiples of 10.**
- 240 ÷ 8 = ? → Basic fact: 24 ÷ 8 = 3 → Answer: 30
- 350 ÷ 5 = ? → Basic fact: 35 ÷ 5 = 7 → Answer: 70
- 420 ÷ 7 = ? → Basic fact: 42 ÷ 7 = 6 → Answer: 60

**Step 4 — Extend to multiples of 100.**
Write: 2,400 ÷ 6 = ?
Say: "2,400 is 24 hundreds. 24 hundreds ÷ 6 = 4 hundreds = 400."
Or: "Basic fact: 24 ÷ 6 = 4. The dividend had two trailing zeros, so the quotient has two trailing zeros: 400."

**Step 5 — More examples with multiples of 100.**
- 3,600 ÷ 9 = ? → Basic fact: 36 ÷ 9 = 4 → Answer: 400
- 4,500 ÷ 5 = ? → Basic fact: 45 ÷ 5 = 9 → Answer: 900
- 5,600 ÷ 8 = ? → Basic fact: 56 ÷ 8 = 7 → Answer: 700

**Step 6 — State the pattern.**
"To divide a multiple of 10 or 100 by a one-digit number:
1. Identify the basic fact (ignore the trailing zeros).
2. Divide using the basic fact.
3. Attach the same number of trailing zeros to the quotient."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 270 ÷ 3
- Basic fact: 27 ÷ 3 = 9
- Trailing zeros: 1 (from 270)
- Answer: **90**
- Check: 3 × 90 = 270 ✓

**Example B:** 4,800 ÷ 6
- Basic fact: 48 ÷ 6 = 8
- Trailing zeros: 2 (from 4,800)
- Answer: **800**
- Check: 6 × 800 = 4,800 ✓

**Example C:** 1,500 ÷ 5
- Basic fact: 15 ÷ 5 = 3
- Trailing zeros: 2 (from 1,500)
- Answer: **300**
- Check: 5 × 300 = 1,500 ✓

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. Have the student identify the basic fact first.

1. 160 ÷ 4 = ? → Basic fact: 16 ÷ 4 = 4 → Answer: **40**
2. 360 ÷ 6 = ? → Basic fact: 36 ÷ 6 = 6 → Answer: **60**
3. 450 ÷ 9 = ? → Basic fact: 45 ÷ 9 = 5 → Answer: **50**
4. 2,100 ÷ 7 = ? → Basic fact: 21 ÷ 7 = 3 → Answer: **300**
5. 3,200 ÷ 8 = ? → Basic fact: 32 ÷ 8 = 4 → Answer: **400**

For each, ask: "How can you check your answer?" (Multiply the quotient by the divisor.)

## Assessment Check

Ask: "Why does the 'basic fact plus trailing zeros' strategy work for division?"

**Target response:** Because dividing a multiple of 10 is really dividing groups of tens (or hundreds). The basic fact tells us how many groups, and the trailing zeros tell us the size of each group — tens or hundreds.

## Extension

- Try: 6,000 ÷ 8 = ? (Basic fact: 60 ÷ 8 ... this doesn't divide evenly! 6,000 ÷ 8 = 750. This previews the need for the long division algorithm.)
- Explore: "Does this strategy always give a whole number? When might it not?" (When the basic fact doesn't divide evenly — preview of Lesson 3 on estimation.)
