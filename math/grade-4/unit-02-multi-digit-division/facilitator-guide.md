# Facilitator Guide — Grade 4 Math, Unit 2: Multi-Digit Division

**Grade:** 4
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 2 — Multi-Digit Division
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (facilitator leads instruction; student works independently for practice)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit introduces multi-digit division with one-digit divisors. Students connect division to multiplication, build from dividing multiples of 10 and 100 to the standard long division algorithm, and learn to interpret remainders in context. The progression moves from place-value reasoning and estimation to the procedural steps of long division, always grounded in the relationship between multiplication and division.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Explain division as the inverse of multiplication
- Divide multiples of 10 and 100 by a one-digit number using place-value patterns
- Estimate quotients using compatible numbers and rounding
- Perform long division of 2-, 3-, and 4-digit dividends by a 1-digit divisor
- Interpret remainders (as leftover, as a fraction, or by rounding up) based on the problem context
- Solve multi-step word problems involving division

**Why this matters:** Division is essential for equal sharing, measurement, fractions, and proportional reasoning. Understanding the long division algorithm through place value (rather than rote steps) allows students to self-check using multiplication and to extend to larger dividends independently.

## Lesson Summaries and Facilitation Notes

### Lesson 1: Division as the Inverse of Multiplication
**Goal:** Reactivate the multiplication-division connection from Grade 3 and extend it to larger numbers.
**Key moves:** Use fact families (e.g., 6 × 8 = 48 → 48 ÷ 8 = 6). Show students that "48 ÷ 8 = ?" is really asking "8 × ? = 48." This "think multiplication" strategy is the foundation for all division work in this unit.
**Watch for:** Students who see division as a separate, unrelated operation. Reinforce the inverse relationship consistently.

### Lesson 2: Dividing Multiples of 10 and 100
**Goal:** Use place-value patterns to divide multiples of 10 and 100 (e.g., 240 ÷ 6 = 40).
**Key moves:** Connect to the "append zeros" pattern from multiplication. If 6 × 4 = 24, then 6 × 40 = 240, so 240 ÷ 6 = 40. Work with base-ten blocks: 240 as 24 tens divided into 6 groups gives 4 tens per group.
**Watch for:** Students confused by zeros. Ground every example in place value, not just a procedural "remove and add zeros" trick.

### Lesson 3: Estimating Quotients
**Goal:** Use compatible numbers and rounding to estimate quotients before computing.
**Key moves:** Teach students to ask "What nearby number is easy to divide by this divisor?" For 153 ÷ 7, think "7 × 20 = 140 and 7 × 25 = 175, so the answer is between 20 and 25." Estimation builds number sense and provides a reasonableness check for long division.
**Watch for:** Over-reliance on rounding rules without connecting to multiplication facts. Encourage flexible thinking about compatible numbers.

### Lesson 4: Introduction to Long Division (2-Digit ÷ 1-Digit, No Remainder)
**Goal:** Introduce the long division algorithm with simple 2-digit dividends that divide evenly.
**Key moves:** Use the "place-value partitioning" approach: break 84 ÷ 4 into "How many 4s in 8 tens?" (2 tens) and "How many 4s in 4 ones?" (1 one), giving 21. Connect each step to base-ten blocks. Introduce the standard notation (division bracket) alongside the place-value reasoning.
**Watch for:** Students skipping the estimation step or not understanding what each digit in the quotient represents. Always ask "What does the 2 in the quotient mean?" (2 tens).

### Lesson 5: Long Division with Remainders (2-Digit ÷ 1-Digit)
**Goal:** Extend long division to problems that do not divide evenly.
**Key moves:** Start with a concrete model — 47 blocks shared among 3 groups gives 15 in each group with 2 left over. Introduce the remainder notation (R2). Verify: 3 × 15 = 45, 45 + 2 = 47. The remainder must always be less than the divisor.
**Watch for:** Remainders larger than the divisor (means the quotient is too small). Students forgetting to check their work with multiplication.

### Lesson 6: Long Division: 3-Digit ÷ 1-Digit (No Remainder)
**Goal:** Extend long division to 3-digit dividends that divide evenly.
**Key moves:** Model 396 ÷ 3 step by step: divide hundreds, then tens, then ones. Emphasize the repeated cycle: Divide → Multiply → Subtract → Bring down. Show alignment with place value at every step.
**Watch for:** Errors in subtraction within the algorithm. Students losing track of place value when the quotient has a zero in a middle position (e.g., 612 ÷ 6 = 102).

### Lesson 7: Long Division: 3-Digit ÷ 1-Digit (With Remainder)
**Goal:** Practice 3-digit long division where the result includes a remainder.
**Key moves:** Combine estimation ("about how many?") with the full algorithm. Emphasize the check: quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend. Increase the variety of problems, including dividends with zeros (e.g., 305 ÷ 4).
**Watch for:** Students who forget to bring down the next digit or who handle zeros in the dividend incorrectly.

### Lesson 8: Long Division: 4-Digit ÷ 1-Digit
**Goal:** Extend long division to 4-digit dividends.
**Key moves:** The procedure is identical — just one more place-value step. Reinforce estimation first ("The answer is around ____"), then execute the algorithm. Include problems with and without remainders.
**Watch for:** Fatigue with multi-step computation. Allow multiplication charts as a reference tool so the cognitive load is on the division process, not fact recall.

### Lesson 9: Interpreting Remainders in Word Problems
**Goal:** Decide what to do with a remainder based on the real-world context.
**Key moves:** Present three types: (a) ignore the remainder (e.g., "How many full teams of 5 from 23 students?" → 4 teams), (b) round up (e.g., "How many buses for 23 students if each holds 5?" → 5 buses), (c) the remainder is the answer (e.g., "23 students, teams of 5 — how many students are left over?" → 3). Students must read carefully and justify their interpretation.
**Watch for:** Students who mechanically write "R3" without considering what the remainder means in context.

### Lesson 10: Multi-Step Word Problems with Division
**Goal:** Apply division (and multiplication) in multi-step real-world problems.
**Key moves:** Problems require two or more operations. Students identify what information is needed, choose operations, and solve step by step. Include problems combining multiplication and division, addition and division, etc.
**Watch for:** Students who rush to compute without planning. Encourage underlining key information and writing a plan before calculating.

## Differentiation Strategies

### Struggling Learners
- Continue using base-ten blocks alongside the algorithm through Lessons 4-6
- Provide a "long division steps" checklist: Divide → Multiply → Subtract → Check (is the difference less than the divisor?) → Bring down
- Allow a multiplication chart for reference
- Reduce digit count: if struggling with 3-digit dividends, provide additional 2-digit practice first

### Advanced Learners
- Introduce 2-digit divisors informally (e.g., 144 ÷ 12)
- Challenge problems with larger dividends (5-digit ÷ 1-digit)
- Explore the connection between remainders and fractions (e.g., 17 ÷ 5 = 3 R2 = 3 2/5)
- Multi-step problems with irrelevant information that students must filter

---

## Common Errors and How to Address Them

| Error | Likely Cause | Remediation |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Remainder ≥ divisor | Quotient digit is too small | Ask: "Can you make one more group?" Have student check with multiplication. |
| Forgetting to bring down | Procedural lapse | Use grid paper with place-value columns. Practice the cycle: D-M-S-B. |
| Zero in the quotient skipped | Student thinks "0 doesn't matter" | Show with blocks: "We have 0 tens to put in each group." The zero holds the place. |
| Wrong subtraction inside the algorithm | Computation error | Have student verify each subtraction independently before proceeding. |
| Ignoring the remainder context | Not reading the problem carefully | Practice sorting word problems into "ignore," "round up," or "remainder is the answer." |

## Assessment Guidance

The **Unit Assessment** covers all major skills. Administer after Lesson 10.

**Mastery criteria (80% or above):**
- Correctly computes long division with 1-digit divisors for 2-, 3-, and 4-digit dividends
- Correctly identifies and records remainders
- Verifies at least one answer using multiplication
- Interprets a remainder correctly in a word problem context

**If below mastery:** Reteach Lessons 4-7 (the core algorithm) with additional concrete modeling before reassessing.
