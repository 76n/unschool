# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 2, Lesson 1

## Topic

Division as the Inverse of Multiplication

## Learning Objectives

- Students will explain that division "undoes" multiplication and vice versa.
- Students will use multiplication to find quotients (think: "? × divisor = dividend").
- Students will write fact families that connect multiplication and division for multi-digit numbers.

## Prerequisites

- Fluent single-digit multiplication facts (1-10)
- Understanding of fact families from Grade 3 (e.g., 3 × 7 = 21, 7 × 3 = 21, 21 ÷ 3 = 7, 21 ÷ 7 = 3)
- Completed Unit 1: Multi-Digit Multiplication

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Counters or base-ten blocks (optional, for concrete modeling)

**Standards:** 4.NBT.6 — Find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to four-digit dividends and one-digit divisors, using strategies based on place value, the properties of operations, and/or the relationship between multiplication and division.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review basic multiplication facts and introduce the "thinking backward" idea.

1. Write on the board:
   - 6 × 7 = ?
   - 8 × 5 = ?
   - 9 × 4 = ?
2. Students solve on dry-erase boards.
3. Then write: "42 ÷ 7 = ?" Ask: "What multiplication fact helps you solve this?"
4. Discuss: Division asks "What number times 7 equals 42?"

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Dividend:** The number being divided (the total amount).
- **Divisor:** The number you divide by (how many groups or size of each group).
- **Quotient:** The answer to a division problem.
- **Inverse operations:** Operations that undo each other (multiplication and division are inverse operations, just like addition and subtraction).
- **Fact family:** A set of related multiplication and division facts using the same three numbers.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Review the inverse relationship with basic facts.**
Write on the board:
- 5 × 8 = 40
- 40 ÷ 8 = 5
- 40 ÷ 5 = 8

Say: "These three facts use the same numbers: 5, 8, and 40. Multiplication puts groups together, division breaks them apart. They are inverse operations — each one undoes the other."

**Step 2 — Extend to larger numbers.**
Write: 12 × 4 = 48
Ask: "If 12 × 4 = 48, what are the two division facts?"
- 48 ÷ 4 = 12
- 48 ÷ 12 = 4

Say: "This works for any multiplication fact, no matter how large. Division always 'undoes' multiplication."

**Step 3 — Introduce the "Think Multiplication" strategy.**
Write: 72 ÷ 8 = ?
Say: "Instead of thinking about division directly, ask yourself: '8 times what equals 72?' You know 8 × 9 = 72, so 72 ÷ 8 = 9."

Write: 96 ÷ 8 = ?
Say: "Ask: '8 times what equals 96?' You might know 8 × 12 = 96, so 96 ÷ 8 = 12. Even if you don't know this fact immediately, you can build up: 8 × 10 = 80, 8 × 11 = 88, 8 × 12 = 96. There it is!"

**Step 4 — Connect to real-world meaning.**
Say: "Suppose you have 84 stickers and want to share them equally among 4 friends. You're asking: 4 × ? = 84. That's the same as 84 ÷ 4."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 56 ÷ 7
- Think multiplication: 7 × ? = 56
- 7 × 8 = 56
- So 56 ÷ 7 = **8**
- Check: 8 × 7 = 56 ✓

**Example B:** 63 ÷ 9
- Think multiplication: 9 × ? = 63
- 9 × 7 = 63
- So 63 ÷ 9 = **7**
- Check: 7 × 9 = 63 ✓

**Example C:** 84 ÷ 7
- Think multiplication: 7 × ? = 84
- 7 × 10 = 70, 7 × 11 = 77, 7 × 12 = 84
- So 84 ÷ 7 = **12**
- Check: 12 × 7 = 84 ✓

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. For each problem, have the student state the multiplication fact that helps them solve it.

1. 45 ÷ 5 = ? → Think: 5 × **9** = 45 → Answer: **9**
2. 54 ÷ 6 = ? → Think: 6 × **9** = 54 → Answer: **9**
3. 72 ÷ 9 = ? → Think: 9 × **8** = 72 → Answer: **8**
4. 48 ÷ 4 = ? → Think: 4 × **12** = 48 → Answer: **12**
5. Write the complete fact family for 6, 9, and 54:
   - 6 × 9 = 54
   - 9 × 6 = 54
   - 54 ÷ 6 = 9
   - 54 ÷ 9 = 6

**Facilitator tip:** If the student hesitates on a division fact, always redirect to multiplication: "What would you multiply ___ by to get ___?"

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Think Multiplication to Divide

Solve each division problem. Write the multiplication fact you used.

1. 36 ÷ 4 = ___
2. 81 ÷ 9 = ___
3. 48 ÷ 6 = ___
4. 35 ÷ 7 = ___
5. 64 ÷ 8 = ___
6. 72 ÷ 6 = ___
7. 96 ÷ 8 = ___
8. 60 ÷ 5 = ___

### Part B: Fact Families

Write the complete fact family (2 multiplication and 2 division facts) for each set of numbers.

1. 7, 8, 56
2. 4, 11, 44
3. 6, 12, 72

### Part C: Word Problems

1. There are 63 books arranged equally on 7 shelves. How many books are on each shelf? Write the multiplication fact that helps you solve this.

2. A teacher has 84 pencils. She gives each student 6 pencils. How many students receive pencils? What multiplication fact helps?

### Answer Key

**Part A:**
1. 9 (4 × 9 = 36)
2. 9 (9 × 9 = 81)
3. 8 (6 × 8 = 48)
4. 5 (7 × 5 = 35)
5. 8 (8 × 8 = 64)
6. 12 (6 × 12 = 72)
7. 12 (8 × 12 = 96)
8. 12 (5 × 12 = 60)

**Part B:**
1. 7 × 8 = 56, 8 × 7 = 56, 56 ÷ 7 = 8, 56 ÷ 8 = 7
2. 4 × 11 = 44, 11 × 4 = 44, 44 ÷ 4 = 11, 44 ÷ 11 = 4
3. 6 × 12 = 72, 12 × 6 = 72, 72 ÷ 6 = 12, 72 ÷ 12 = 6

**Part C:**
1. 63 ÷ 7 = 9 books per shelf. (7 × 9 = 63)
2. 84 ÷ 6 = 14 students. (6 × 14 = 84)

## Assessment Check

Ask the student to explain in their own words: "How does knowing your multiplication facts help you divide?"

**Target response:** Division asks "what times the divisor equals the dividend?" so if you know your multiplication facts, you can find the quotient by thinking of the related multiplication.

## Extension

- Challenge: 144 ÷ 12 = ? (Think: 12 × ? = 144 → 12 × 12 = 144 → Answer: 12)
- Explore: "Can you always find an exact answer when dividing? What happens with 50 ÷ 7?" (Preview of remainders, Lesson 5)
