# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 2, Lesson 3

## Topic

Estimating Quotients

## Learning Objectives

- Students will use compatible numbers to estimate quotients.
- Students will determine whether an estimate is reasonable by comparing to the actual dividend.
- Students will explain why estimation is useful before performing exact division.

## Prerequisites

- Division as inverse of multiplication (Lesson 1)
- Dividing multiples of 10 and 100 (Lesson 2)
- Rounding to the nearest 10 and 100

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Multiplication chart (reference)

**Standards:** 4.NBT.6, 4.OA.3 — Use estimation strategies to assess the reasonableness of answers.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Practice dividing multiples of 10 quickly (Lesson 2 review) as a foundation for estimation.

1. 180 ÷ 6 = ? (30)
2. 350 ÷ 7 = ? (50)
3. 240 ÷ 8 = ? (30)
4. 450 ÷ 5 = ? (90)
5. Ask: "What if the dividend were 185 instead of 180? Could you still divide by 6 easily?" (No — it's not an exact multiple. But we know the answer is close to 30.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Estimate:** An answer that is close to the exact answer, found using simpler numbers.
- **Compatible numbers:** Numbers that are easy to compute with mentally, especially numbers that divide evenly. They don't have to be the rounded version — just "friendly" numbers.
- **Reasonable:** An answer that makes sense and is close to what you'd expect.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Why estimate?**
Say: "Before you solve a long division problem, it helps to estimate the answer. Estimation tells you roughly what to expect, so you can check if your exact answer makes sense. If you estimate 'about 40' and your long division gives you 400, you know something went wrong."

**Step 2 — Introduce compatible numbers.**
Write: 153 ÷ 7 = ?
Say: "We want a number close to 153 that divides easily by 7. What multiples of 7 are close to 153?"
- 7 × 20 = 140
- 7 × 25 = 175

"140 is close to 153 and divides by 7 easily. So we can estimate: 153 ÷ 7 ≈ 140 ÷ 7 = 20. The actual answer is a bit more than 20."

**Step 3 — Show that compatible numbers are flexible.**
Write: 258 ÷ 5 = ?
Say: "What number close to 258 divides easily by 5? 250 ÷ 5 = 50, or 300 ÷ 5 = 60. So the answer is between 50 and 60, probably closer to 50."

Another approach: "You could also think: 5 × 50 = 250, which is very close to 258. So the answer is about 50."

**Step 4 — Estimation with larger dividends.**
Write: 4,318 ÷ 7 = ?
Say: "Think: 7 × 600 = 4,200. That's close to 4,318. So the answer is about 600."
Or: "Round 4,318 to 4,200 (a compatible number for 7). 4,200 ÷ 7 = 600."

**Step 5 — Using estimation to bracket the answer.**
Write: 345 ÷ 8 = ?
Say: "Find two multiples of 8 that 345 falls between."
- 8 × 40 = 320
- 8 × 50 = 400

"345 is between 320 and 400, so the answer is between 40 and 50. Since 345 is closer to 320, the answer is closer to 40 — probably around 43."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 195 ÷ 4
- Compatible number: 200 (close to 195, easy to divide by 4)
- 200 ÷ 4 = 50
- Estimate: **about 50** (actual: 48 R3)

**Example B:** 472 ÷ 9
- Think: 9 × 50 = 450, 9 × 55 = 495
- 472 is between 450 and 495
- Estimate: **about 50** (actual: 52 R4)

**Example C:** 2,750 ÷ 6
- Compatible number: 2,400 (6 × 400) or 3,000 (6 × 500)
- 2,750 is between 2,400 and 3,000
- Closer estimate: 6 × 450 = 2,700, which is very close
- Estimate: **about 450** (actual: 458 R2)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

For each problem, have the student find compatible numbers and estimate before solving exactly.

1. 162 ÷ 4 ≈ ?
   - Compatible: 160 ÷ 4 = **40** (actual: 40 R2)

2. 247 ÷ 5 ≈ ?
   - Compatible: 250 ÷ 5 = **50** (actual: 49 R2)

3. 335 ÷ 7 ≈ ?
   - Think: 7 × 50 = 350, so about **50** (actual: 47 R6)

4. 518 ÷ 6 ≈ ?
   - Think: 6 × 80 = 480, 6 × 90 = 540, so about **85** (actual: 86 R2)

5. 1,845 ÷ 9 ≈ ?
   - Think: 9 × 200 = 1,800, so about **200** (actual: 205)

**Facilitator tip:** There's no single "right" compatible number. Praise any sensible choice and check that the estimate is reasonable.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Estimate Using Compatible Numbers

For each problem, write your compatible numbers and your estimate. You do NOT need to find the exact answer.

1. 178 ÷ 3 ≈ ___
2. 325 ÷ 8 ≈ ___
3. 438 ÷ 7 ≈ ___
4. 561 ÷ 6 ≈ ___
5. 2,190 ÷ 4 ≈ ___
6. 3,572 ÷ 9 ≈ ___

### Part B: Is the Answer Reasonable?

For each problem, a student's exact answer is given. Use estimation to decide if the answer is reasonable. Write "reasonable" or "not reasonable" and explain.

1. 252 ÷ 6 = 42 → Reasonable or not?
2. 475 ÷ 5 = 95 → Reasonable or not?
3. 312 ÷ 4 = 28 → Reasonable or not?
4. 1,624 ÷ 8 = 23 → Reasonable or not?

### Part C: Word Problem

A farmer harvested 892 apples and wants to pack them into crates of 9. About how many crates will he need? Explain how you estimated.

### Answer Key

**Part A:** (Estimates will vary; accept any reasonable response.)
1. ≈ 60 (180 ÷ 3 = 60)
2. ≈ 40 (320 ÷ 8 = 40)
3. ≈ 63 (420 ÷ 7 = 60 or 490 ÷ 7 = 70; between 60 and 70)
4. ≈ 90 (540 ÷ 6 = 90)
5. ≈ 550 (2,200 ÷ 4 = 550)
6. ≈ 400 (3,600 ÷ 9 = 400)

**Part B:**
1. Reasonable. Estimate: 240 ÷ 6 = 40, so 42 is close. ✓
2. Reasonable. Estimate: 500 ÷ 5 = 100, so 95 is close. ✓
3. Not reasonable. Estimate: 320 ÷ 4 = 80, but the answer says 28. That's way too low. (Correct answer: 78)
4. Not reasonable. Estimate: 1,600 ÷ 8 = 200, but the answer says 23. Missing a digit. (Correct answer: 203)

**Part C:**
About 100 crates. Think: 9 × 100 = 900, and 892 is close to 900. So approximately 100 crates. (Exact: 99 R1, so 100 crates needed since you can't have a partial crate.)

## Assessment Check

Ask: "Why is it important to estimate before you do long division?"

**Target response:** Estimation gives you a ballpark answer so you can check if your exact answer makes sense. If your long division answer is very different from your estimate, you probably made an error.

## Extension

- Challenge: Estimate 7,891 ÷ 8. (About 1,000. Think: 8 × 1,000 = 8,000 ≈ 7,891.)
- Discuss: "When might estimation be good enough and you don't need the exact answer?" (Budgeting, planning, quick mental math at the store.)
