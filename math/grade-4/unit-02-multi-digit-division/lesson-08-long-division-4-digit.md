# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 2, Lesson 8

## Topic

Long Division: 4-Digit ÷ 1-Digit

## Learning Objectives

- Students will perform long division of a 4-digit number by a 1-digit number (with or without remainder).
- Students will estimate quotients for 4-digit dividends to check reasonableness.
- Students will manage the D-M-S-B cycle across four place values, including zeros in the quotient.

## Prerequisites

- Long division of 3-digit numbers (Lessons 6-7)
- Estimation with larger dividends (Lesson 3)
- Place value through thousands

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Grid paper (for alignment — highly recommended for 4-digit problems)
- Student scratch paper
- Multiplication chart (optional reference)

**Standards:** 4.NBT.6 — Find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to four-digit dividends and one-digit divisors.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Build confidence that the same algorithm works for any number of digits.

1. Solve: 738 ÷ 6 = ? (123)
2. Ask: "If I added a digit and made it 7,380 ÷ 6, what would change?" (One more D-M-S-B cycle. The process is the same — just longer.)
3. Estimate: 7,380 ÷ 6 ≈ ? (About 1,200. Think: 6 × 1,200 = 7,200.)

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Emphasize: the process is identical.**
Say: "Long division works the same way no matter how many digits the dividend has. We just repeat the D-M-S-B cycle for each digit. Today we'll practice with 4-digit dividends."

**Step 2 — Full walkthrough.**
Problem: 5,748 ÷ 4

Estimate: 4 × 1,400 = 5,600 and 4 × 1,500 = 6,000. So the answer is between 1,400 and 1,500.

```
    1 4 3 7
   ---------
4 ) 5 7 4 8
  - 4           ← 4 × 1 = 4
    1 7         ← bring down 7
  - 1 6         ← 4 × 4 = 16
    0 1 4       ← bring down 4
  -   1 2       ← 4 × 3 = 12
    0 0 2 8     ← bring down 8
  -     2 8     ← 4 × 7 = 28
          0
```

Cycle 1 — Thousands: 5 ÷ 4 = 1. Multiply: 4. Subtract: 5 − 4 = 1.
Cycle 2 — Hundreds: Bring down 7 → 17. 17 ÷ 4 = 4. Multiply: 16. Subtract: 17 − 16 = 1.
Cycle 3 — Tens: Bring down 4 → 14. 14 ÷ 4 = 3. Multiply: 12. Subtract: 14 − 12 = 2.
Cycle 4 — Ones: Bring down 8 → 28. 28 ÷ 4 = 7. Multiply: 28. Subtract: 28 − 28 = 0.

Answer: 1,437. Check: 4 × 1,437 = 5,748 ✓
Compare to estimate (1,400-1,500): ✓ Reasonable.

**Step 3 — Example with a remainder.**
Problem: 8,531 ÷ 6

Estimate: 6 × 1,400 = 8,400. So about 1,400.

```
    1 4 2 1  R5
   -----------
6 ) 8 5 3 1
  - 6           ← 6 × 1 = 6
    2 5         ← bring down 5
  - 2 4         ← 6 × 4 = 24
    0 1 3       ← bring down 3
  -   1 2       ← 6 × 2 = 12
    0 0 1 1     ← bring down 1
  -       6     ← 6 × 1 = 6
          5     ← remainder
```

Answer: 1,421 R5. Check: 1,421 × 6 + 5 = 8,526 + 5 = 8,531 ✓

**Step 4 — Example with a zero in the quotient.**
Problem: 3,024 ÷ 3

```
    1 0 0 8
   ---------
3 ) 3 0 2 4
  - 3           ← 3 × 1 = 3
    0 0         ← bring down 0
  -   0         ← 3 × 0 = 0 (3 doesn't go into 0)
    0 0 2       ← bring down 2
  -     0       ← 3 × 0 = 0 (3 doesn't go into 2)
    0 0 2 4     ← bring down 4
  -     2 4     ← 3 × 8 = 24
          0
```

Answer: 1,008. Check: 3 × 1,008 = 3,024 ✓

Say: "Two zeros in the quotient! Every one of them matters. If you wrote 18 instead of 1,008, that's way off. Always write a 0 when the divisor doesn't go into the current number."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 2,856 ÷ 7

```
    4 0 8
   -------
7 ) 2 8 5 6
  - 2 8         ← 7 × 4 = 28
    0 0 5       ← bring down 5
  -     0       ← 7 × 0 = 0
    0 0 5 6     ← bring down 6
  -   5 6       ← 7 × 8 = 56
        0
```

- Answer: **408**. Check: 7 × 408 = 2,856 ✓

**Example B:** 6,295 ÷ 5

```
    1 2 5 9
   ---------
5 ) 6 2 9 5
  - 5           ← 5 × 1 = 5
    1 2         ← bring down 2
  - 1 0         ← 5 × 2 = 10
    0 2 9       ← bring down 9
  -   2 5       ← 5 × 5 = 25
    0 0 4 5     ← bring down 5
  -     4 5     ← 5 × 9 = 45
          0
```

- Answer: **1,259**. Check: 5 × 1,259 = 6,295 ✓

**Example C:** 4,573 ÷ 8

```
    5 7 1  R5
   ---------
8 ) 4 5 7 3
  - 4 0         ← 8 × 5 = 40
    0 5 7       ← bring down 7
  -   5 6       ← 8 × 7 = 56
    0 0 1 3     ← bring down 3
  -       8     ← 8 × 1 = 8
          5     ← remainder
```

- Answer: **571 R5**. Check: 571 × 8 + 5 = 4,568 + 5 = 4,573 ✓

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. 7,236 ÷ 6 = ? → **1,206** (Check: 6 × 1,206 = 7,236)
2. 9,451 ÷ 7 = ? → **1,350 R1** (Check: 1,350 × 7 + 1 = 9,451)
3. 4,032 ÷ 4 = ? → **1,008** (Check: 4 × 1,008 = 4,032)

**Facilitator tip:** Encourage students to estimate first and compare. For #3, ask: "How many zeros do you expect in the quotient?" Grid paper is very helpful for keeping digits aligned.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: 4-Digit Long Division

Estimate first, then solve. Check with multiplication.

1. 3,648 ÷ 4 = ___
2. 5,670 ÷ 9 = ___
3. 7,344 ÷ 6 = ___
4. 8,195 ÷ 5 = ___
5. 2,917 ÷ 3 = ___
6. 6,503 ÷ 8 = ___

### Part B: Find the Error

A student solved 4,218 ÷ 6 and got 73. Use estimation to explain why this can't be right. Then find the correct answer.

### Part C: Word Problem

A factory produced 7,560 widgets in one week (5 working days). How many widgets were produced each day, if production was equal each day?

### Answer Key

**Part A:**
1. 912 (4 × 912 = 3,648 ✓)
2. 630 (9 × 630 = 5,670 ✓)
3. 1,224 (6 × 1,224 = 7,344 ✓)
4. 1,639 (5 × 1,639 = 8,195 ✓)
5. 972 R1 (972 × 3 + 1 = 2,917 ✓)
6. 812 R7 (812 × 8 + 7 = 6,503 ✓)

**Part B:**
Estimate: 4,200 ÷ 6 = 700. The answer should be around 700, not 73. The student is missing a digit. Correct answer: 4,218 ÷ 6 = 703 (6 × 703 = 4,218 ✓).

**Part C:**
7,560 ÷ 5 = 1,512 widgets per day. Check: 5 × 1,512 = 7,560 ✓

## Assessment Check

Have the student solve 5,016 ÷ 6 and explain what happens at each step, especially the zeros.

**Target:** 836. Check: 6 × 836 = 5,016. Student should correctly handle 5 ÷ 6 → look at 50, and manage the bring-down sequence smoothly.

## Extension

- Challenge: 9,999 ÷ 9 = ? (1,111) — satisfying pattern!
- Challenge with remainder: 9,999 ÷ 7 = ? (1,428 R3)
- Discuss: "What is the largest 4-digit number you can divide by 8 with no remainder?" (9,992, since 8 × 1,249 = 9,992)
