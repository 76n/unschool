# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 2, Lesson 10

## Topic

Multi-Step Word Problems with Division

## Learning Objectives

- Students will solve word problems requiring two or more operations, at least one of which is division.
- Students will identify relevant information, plan a solution strategy, and execute it step by step.
- Students will check the reasonableness of their answers using estimation.

## Prerequisites

- All prior lessons in this unit (Lessons 1-9)
- Multi-digit multiplication (Unit 1)
- Addition and subtraction of multi-digit numbers

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Student scratch paper
- Grid paper (optional, for long division work)

**Standards:** 4.OA.3 — Solve multistep word problems posed with whole numbers and having whole-number answers using the four operations, including problems in which remainders must be interpreted.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Practice identifying operations in a word problem.

Read this problem aloud (do NOT solve yet):
"A store received 3 shipments of 48 books each. The books are displayed equally on 6 shelves. How many books are on each shelf?"

Ask:
1. "What operation do you need first?" (Multiplication: 3 × 48)
2. "What operation comes second?" (Division: total ÷ 6)
3. "How can you tell this is a multi-step problem?" (You can't answer the question with just one calculation.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Multi-step problem:** A word problem that requires two or more calculations to solve.
- **Plan:** Before computing, identify what information you have, what you need to find, and what steps will get you there.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Model the problem-solving process.**
Problem: "A store received 3 shipments of 48 books each. The books are displayed equally on 6 shelves. How many books are on each shelf?"

**Plan:**
1. Find the total number of books: 3 × 48
2. Divide equally among 6 shelves: total ÷ 6

**Solve:**
Step 1: 3 × 48 = 144 books total
Step 2: 144 ÷ 6 = 24 books per shelf

**Check:** Is 24 reasonable? About 150 books on 6 shelves ≈ 25 per shelf. Yes, 24 is reasonable. ✓

**Step 2 — Multi-step with division first.**
Problem: "A farmer has 972 apples. He divides them equally into 4 crates, then eats 3 apples from one crate. How many apples are left in that crate?"

**Plan:**
1. Divide apples into crates: 972 ÷ 4
2. Subtract 3 from one crate's amount

**Solve:**
Step 1: 972 ÷ 4 = 243 apples per crate
Step 2: 243 − 3 = 240 apples left in that crate

**Step 3 — Multi-step with remainder interpretation.**
Problem: "A school has 350 students. They sit in rows of 8 in the auditorium. After all rows are full, extra chairs are removed. How many chairs are in the auditorium?"

**Plan:**
1. Divide to find rows: 350 ÷ 8
2. The full rows determine the chairs (ignore remainder for rows, but multiply back for chairs)

**Solve:**
Step 1: 350 ÷ 8 = 43 R6 → 43 full rows, plus 6 students need partial row → 44 rows needed
Step 2: But wait — "extra chairs are removed." So we count only the chairs actually used: 350 chairs (one per student).

Actually, re-read: The question asks how many chairs are in the auditorium. If chairs are in rows of 8 and extra chairs are removed:
Step 1: 350 ÷ 8 = 43 R6 → 43 full rows (8 chairs each) + 1 partial row (6 chairs)
Step 2: Total chairs = (43 × 8) + 6 = 344 + 6 = 350

Hmm — let me pose a cleaner version:

Problem: "A school has 350 students. They sit in rows of 8. The school only sets up complete rows of 8 chairs. How many students don't get a chair?"

**Solve:**
Step 1: 350 ÷ 8 = 43 R6
Step 2: 43 complete rows seat 43 × 8 = 344 students.
Step 3: 350 − 344 = 6 students without chairs.
Answer: **6 students.** (The remainder tells us directly.)

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** "Marcus earns $8 per hour. He worked 5 hours on Saturday and 3 hours on Sunday. He wants to buy books that cost $6 each. How many books can he buy?"

Plan:
1. Total hours: 5 + 3 = 8 hours
2. Total earnings: 8 × $8 = $64
3. Books he can buy: $64 ÷ $6

Solve:
- 8 hours × $8 = $64
- 64 ÷ 6 = 10 R4
- He can buy **10 books** (ignore remainder — he can't buy a partial book). He'll have $4 left over.

**Example B:** "A bakery made 1,250 muffins on Monday and 750 on Tuesday. All muffins are packed into boxes of 6. How many boxes are needed?"

Plan:
1. Total muffins: 1,250 + 750
2. Boxes needed: total ÷ 6

Solve:
- 1,250 + 750 = 2,000 muffins
- 2,000 ÷ 6 = 333 R2
- **334 boxes** needed (round up — the 2 remaining muffins need a box)

**Example C:** "A school ordered 8 packs of paper with 500 sheets each. The paper is shared equally among 5 grades. How many sheets does each grade get?"

Plan:
1. Total sheets: 8 × 500
2. Per grade: total ÷ 5

Solve:
- 8 × 500 = 4,000 sheets
- 4,000 ÷ 5 = 800 sheets per grade

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

For each problem, have the student write a plan before computing.

1. "A garden has 4 rows with 36 plants each. The gardener divides all the plants equally into 6 pots. How many plants go in each pot?"
   - Plan: 4 × 36 = 144, then 144 ÷ 6 = **24 plants per pot**

2. "A store had 500 oranges. They sold 128 on Monday. The remaining oranges are packed into bags of 8. How many bags can be filled?"
   - Plan: 500 − 128 = 372, then 372 ÷ 8 = **46 R4, so 46 full bags**

3. "Three friends each earned $75 mowing lawns. They combined their money and split it equally among 5 charity jars. How much went into each jar?"
   - Plan: 3 × $75 = $225, then $225 ÷ 5 = **$45 per jar**

**Facilitator tip:** If the student jumps straight to computing, stop them and say: "What are we finding first? Write a plan." Building the habit of planning prevents errors in multi-step problems.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Multi-Step Problems

Write a plan, then solve. Show all work.

1. A farmer collected 248 eggs on Monday and 312 eggs on Tuesday. He packs them into cartons of 6. How many full cartons can he fill?

2. A school bought 9 boxes of markers with 24 markers in each box. The markers are shared equally among 6 classrooms. How many markers does each classroom get?

3. Mrs. Chen had $500. She spent $185 on supplies. She wants to buy books that cost $7 each with the remaining money. How many books can she buy?

4. A movie theater has 8 rows with 45 seats each. A sold-out show had 350 people. How many empty seats were there?

### Part B: Write Your Own

Write a two-step word problem that uses both multiplication and division. Then solve it. (Swap with a partner if working in a group.)

### Answer Key

**Part A:**
1. Total eggs: 248 + 312 = 560. Cartons: 560 ÷ 6 = 93 R2. **93 full cartons** (2 eggs left over).
2. Total markers: 9 × 24 = 216. Per classroom: 216 ÷ 6 = **36 markers**.
3. Remaining money: $500 − $185 = $315. Books: $315 ÷ $7 = **45 books**.
4. Total seats: 8 × 45 = 360. Empty seats: 360 − 350 = **10 empty seats**.

**Part B:** Answers will vary. Check that the problem requires two steps and the solution is correct.

## Assessment Check

Present: "A store received 5 shipments of 126 T-shirts each. The T-shirts are divided equally among 9 displays. How many T-shirts are on each display?"

**Target:** 5 × 126 = 630. Then 630 ÷ 9 = 70 T-shirts per display.

Student should identify the two operations, solve in order, and check reasonableness.

## Extension

- Three-step challenge: "A school fundraiser earned $840. Half was spent on supplies. The rest was shared equally among 7 clubs. Each club used $25 for materials. How much does each club have left?"
  - Step 1: $840 ÷ 2 = $420 remaining
  - Step 2: $420 ÷ 7 = $60 per club
  - Step 3: $60 − $25 = $35 left per club
