# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 2, Lesson 5

## Topic

Long Division with Remainders (2-Digit ÷ 1-Digit)

## Learning Objectives

- Students will perform long division of a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number when there is a remainder.
- Students will record the remainder correctly using "R" notation.
- Students will verify their answer: quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend.

## Prerequisites

- Long division algorithm (Lesson 4)
- Estimating quotients (Lesson 3)
- Understanding that division doesn't always "come out even"

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Student scratch paper or grid paper
- Counters or base-ten blocks (for concrete modeling)

**Standards:** 4.NBT.6 — Find whole-number quotients and remainders with up to four-digit dividends and one-digit divisors.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Show that some division problems don't divide evenly.

1. Distribute 15 counters into 3 equal groups. How many in each group? (5, no leftovers)
2. Now try 17 counters into 3 equal groups. How many in each group? (5 in each group, with 2 left over)
3. Ask: "What do we call the leftovers?" (The remainder)
4. Ask: "Could the remainder ever be 3 or more if we're dividing by 3?" (No — if we had 3 more, we could make another full group.)

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Remainder (R):** The amount left over after dividing as evenly as possible. It is always less than the divisor.
- **Check formula:** Quotient × Divisor + Remainder = Dividend.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete model.**
Problem: 47 ÷ 3
Use base-ten blocks: 4 tens and 7 ones.
Divide into 3 groups:
- Tens: 4 tens ÷ 3 = 1 ten per group, with 1 ten left over.
- Trade the leftover ten for 10 ones: now we have 17 ones.
- Ones: 17 ones ÷ 3 = 5 ones per group, with 2 left over.
- Each group: 1 ten + 5 ones = 15. Remainder: 2.
- 47 ÷ 3 = 15 R2.

**Step 2 — Written algorithm.**

```
    1 5  R2
   -----
3 ) 4 7
  - 3       ← 3 × 1 = 3
    1 7     ← bring down 7
  - 1 5     ← 3 × 5 = 15
      2     ← remainder
```

Walk through:
1. **Divide:** 4 ÷ 3 = 1. Write 1 above.
2. **Multiply:** 3 × 1 = 3. Write 3 below.
3. **Subtract:** 4 − 3 = 1.
4. **Bring down:** the 7 → makes 17.
5. **Divide:** 17 ÷ 3 = 5. Write 5 above.
6. **Multiply:** 3 × 5 = 15. Write 15 below.
7. **Subtract:** 17 − 15 = 2. This is less than 3, so we're done. Write R2.

**Step 3 — Emphasize the check.**
Check: 15 × 3 + 2 = 45 + 2 = 47 ✓

Say: "Always check! Multiply the quotient by the divisor, then add the remainder. You should get back to the dividend."

**Step 4 — Critical rule about remainders.**
Say: "The remainder MUST be less than the divisor. If your remainder is 3 or more (when dividing by 3), your quotient is too small — you could have made one more group."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 53 ÷ 4

```
    1 3  R1
   -----
4 ) 5 3
  - 4       ← 4 × 1 = 4
    1 3     ← bring down 3
  - 1 2     ← 4 × 3 = 12
      1     ← remainder
```

- Answer: **13 R1**
- Check: 13 × 4 + 1 = 52 + 1 = 53 ✓

**Example B:** 67 ÷ 5

```
    1 3  R2
   -----
5 ) 6 7
  - 5       ← 5 × 1 = 5
    1 7     ← bring down 7
  - 1 5     ← 5 × 3 = 15
      2     ← remainder
```

- Answer: **13 R2**
- Check: 13 × 5 + 2 = 65 + 2 = 67 ✓

**Example C:** 89 ÷ 6

```
    1 4  R5
   -----
6 ) 8 9
  - 6       ← 6 × 1 = 6
    2 9     ← bring down 9
  - 2 4     ← 6 × 4 = 24
      5     ← remainder
```

- Answer: **14 R5**
- Check: 14 × 6 + 5 = 84 + 5 = 89 ✓

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Complete these together. Student works through the algorithm and checks with multiplication.

1. 59 ÷ 4 = ? → **14 R3** (Check: 14 × 4 + 3 = 59)
2. 38 ÷ 5 = ? → **7 R3** (Check: 7 × 5 + 3 = 38)
3. 74 ÷ 9 = ? → **8 R2** (Check: 8 × 9 + 2 = 74)
4. 85 ÷ 6 = ? → **14 R1** (Check: 14 × 6 + 1 = 85)

**Facilitator tip:** If the student gets a remainder equal to or larger than the divisor, ask: "Is that remainder less than the divisor? Can you make one more group?"

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Long Division with Remainders

Solve each problem. Write the quotient and remainder. Check your answer.

1. 29 ÷ 4 = ___
2. 43 ÷ 6 = ___
3. 55 ÷ 8 = ___
4. 67 ÷ 7 = ___
5. 79 ÷ 3 = ___
6. 94 ÷ 8 = ___

### Part B: Find and Fix the Error

A student solved 58 ÷ 7 and wrote "7 R9." What went wrong? What is the correct answer?

### Part C: Word Problems

1. A teacher has 47 markers and wants to give each student 5 markers. How many students get a full set of 5? How many markers are left over?

2. There are 83 students going on a field trip. Each bus holds 9 students. How many buses are completely full? How many students are on the last bus?

### Answer Key

**Part A:**
1. 7 R1 (7 × 4 + 1 = 29 ✓)
2. 7 R1 (7 × 6 + 1 = 43 ✓)
3. 6 R7 (6 × 8 + 7 = 55 ✓)
4. 9 R4 (9 × 7 + 4 = 67 ✓)
5. 26 R1 (26 × 3 + 1 = 79 ✓)
6. 11 R6 (11 × 8 + 6 = 94 ✓)

**Part B:**
The remainder (9) is bigger than the divisor (7). That means the student didn't divide enough. Correct answer: 58 ÷ 7 = 8 R2 (8 × 7 + 2 = 58).

**Part C:**
1. 47 ÷ 5 = 9 R2. Nine students get a full set. Two markers are left over.
2. 83 ÷ 9 = 9 R2. Nine buses are completely full. Two students are on the last bus.

## Assessment Check

Ask the student: "How do you know when you're done with a long division problem that has a remainder?"

**Target response:** You're done when the number left after the last subtraction is less than the divisor. That leftover number is the remainder.

## Extension

- Challenge: 99 ÷ 7 = ? (14 R1)
- Discuss: "In real life, what do we do with the remainder? Does it always just get written as 'R'?" (Preview of Lesson 9: sometimes we ignore it, round up, or report it as the answer.)
