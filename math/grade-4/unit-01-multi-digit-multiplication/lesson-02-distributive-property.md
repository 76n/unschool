# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 1, Lesson 2

## Topic

The Distributive Property

## Learning Objectives

- Students will decompose a two-digit factor into tens and ones to make multiplication easier.
- Students will apply the distributive property to solve two-digit by one-digit multiplication problems (e.g., 34 x 5 = (30 x 5) + (4 x 5) = 170).
- Students will explain why breaking apart a number and multiplying each part works.

## Prerequisites

- Fluency with single-digit multiplication facts (3.OA.7)
- Multiplying by multiples of 10 and 100 (Lesson 1)
- Understanding of place value — tens and ones (2.NBT.1)

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Base-ten blocks (optional, for concrete modeling)
- Area-model grid paper (optional handout)

**Standards:** 4.NBT.5 — Multiply a whole number of up to four digits by a one-digit whole number using strategies based on place value and the properties of operations. 3.OA.5 — Apply properties of operations as strategies to multiply and divide.

2. Decomposition warm-up — write the tens and ones:

   - 83 = ___ tens + ___ ones

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Distributive property:** A rule that says you can break apart one factor, multiply each part separately, then add the products. a x (b + c) = (a x b) + (a x c).
- **Decompose:** To break a number into parts (usually by place value).
- **Partial product:** The result of multiplying one part of a decomposed number.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Pose a problem students cannot easily solve with a basic fact.**
Write on the board: 34 x 5 = ?
Say: "We don't have a basic fact for 34 x 5. But we know how to multiply smaller pieces. Let's break 34 apart."

**Step 2 — Decompose the two-digit number.**
Write: 34 = 30 + 4
Say: "34 is the same as 30 + 4. Now we can multiply each part by 5."

**Step 3 — Multiply each part (partial products).**
- 30 x 5 = 150 (use the Lesson 1 strategy!)
- 4 x 5 = 20

**Step 4 — Add the partial products.**
150 + 20 = 170
So 34 x 5 = 170.

**Step 5 — Name the property.**
Say: "What we just did has a name: the distributive property. We distributed the 5 to both the 30 and the 4. This works every single time."

**Step 6 — Show an area model (visual).**
Draw a rectangle split into two sections:
- Left section labeled 30 wide x 5 tall = 150
- Right section labeled 4 wide x 5 tall = 20
- Total area = 150 + 20 = 170

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 26 x 3
- Decompose: 26 = 20 + 6
- Partial products: 20 x 3 = 60 and 6 x 3 = 18
- Add: 60 + 18 = 78
- Answer: 26 x 3 = 78

**Example B:** 45 x 4
- Decompose: 45 = 40 + 5
- Partial products: 40 x 4 = 160 and 5 x 4 = 20
- Add: 160 + 20 = 180
- Answer: 45 x 4 = 180

**Example C:** 58 x 7
- Decompose: 58 = 50 + 8
- Partial products: 50 x 7 = 350 and 8 x 7 = 56
- Add: 350 + 56 = 406
- Answer: 58 x 7 = 406

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems as a class. Students solve on their boards; teacher circulates.

1. **23 x 4 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "How do you decompose 23?" (20 + 3) "Now multiply each part by 4 and add." (80 + 12 = 92)

2. **15 x 6 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "Break 15 into tens and ones." (10 + 5) "Find each partial product." (60 + 30 = 90)

3. **37 x 5 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "What are your two partial products?" (30 x 5 = 150 and 7 x 5 = 35) "Add them." (185)

4. **62 x 8 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "This one has bigger numbers. Take it step by step. 60 x 8 = ?" (480) "2 x 8 = ?" (16) "Total?" (496)

5. **49 x 3 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "Decompose 49." (40 + 9) "Partial products?" (120 + 27 = 147)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

Use the distributive property to solve. Show the two partial products and the final answer.

1. 27 x 6 = ___
2. 53 x 4 = ___

**Scoring:** Each problem is worth 2 points — 1 point for showing correct partial products, 1 point for the correct final answer (4 points total). Mastery = 3 out of 4 points.

### If Mastery Is Not Met

- Use base-ten blocks to physically model the decomposition: build 27 as 2 tens-rods and 7 unit cubes, then make 6 copies of that set.
- Have the student practice decomposing numbers on a place-value chart before multiplying.
- Assign three additional distributive-property problems with scaffolded blanks (e.g., 35 x 4 = (___ x 4) + (___ x 4) = ___ + ___ = ___).

### If Mastery Is Met

- Students move to the Extension activity below.
- Students may preview Lesson 3 by attempting 23 x 3 in vertical format.

## Extension (Optional)

- **Two Ways to Break Apart:** Challenge students to decompose the one-digit factor instead. For example, 34 x 6: break 6 into 3 + 3, so 34 x 6 = (34 x 3) + (34 x 3). Compare with the standard decomposition. Do they get the same answer?
- **Area Model Art:** Students draw and color area models for three problems of their choice, labeling all dimensions and partial products.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Common Errors:**
- Students decompose correctly but forget to multiply both parts (e.g., for 34 x 5 they compute 30 x 5 = 150 but forget 4 x 5, writing 150 as the answer).
- Students add instead of multiply in the partial-product step (e.g., 30 + 5 = 35 instead of 30 x 5 = 150).
- When the partial products require adding with regrouping (e.g., 350 + 56), students make addition errors. Encourage them to line up place values carefully.

**Pacing Advice:**
- Spend extra time on Steps 2-4 if students seem unsure about decomposing two-digit numbers. The concept of breaking apart is the foundation for the entire unit.
- The area model (Step 6) is very powerful for visual learners. If time is short, you may skip it during class but offer it as a resource for struggling students later.
- Guided Practice problems are sequenced from easier (23 x 4) to harder (62 x 8). If students struggle on problems 4-5, do not rush — return to modeling.
