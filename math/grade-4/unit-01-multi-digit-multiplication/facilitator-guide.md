# Facilitator Guide — Grade 4 Math, Unit 1: Multi-Digit Multiplication

**Grade:** 4
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 1 — Multi-Digit Multiplication
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (facilitator leads instruction; student works independently for practice)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit extends multiplication from single-digit facts (Grade 3) to multi-digit problems. Students learn to multiply a multi-digit number by a one-digit number (e.g., 34 × 7) and to multiply two two-digit numbers (e.g., 23 × 15). The progression moves from place-value-based strategies (area models, partial products) to the standard algorithm.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Multiply by multiples of 10 and 100 (30 × 4, 200 × 5)
- Use the distributive property to break apart multiplication problems
- Multiply a 2- or 3-digit number by a 1-digit number using place value strategies and the standard algorithm
- Multiply two 2-digit numbers using area models and partial products
- Estimate products using rounding
- Solve multi-step word problems involving multiplication

**Why this matters:** Multi-digit multiplication is foundational for division, fractions, measurement, and real-world applications (area, money, scaling). Students who understand the place-value reasoning behind the algorithm can self-correct errors and extend to larger numbers.

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm:

- [ ] Fluent single-digit multiplication facts (1-10)
- [ ] Understanding of place value through thousands
- [ ] Ability to multiply by 10 and 100 (pattern: append zeros)
- [ ] Understanding of the distributive property informally (break-apart strategy)
- [ ] Addition of multi-digit numbers with regrouping

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| 30 × 4 = 34 (treats × as concatenation) | Doesn't understand multiplying tens | Use groups: "30 × 4 means 4 groups of 30." Or: 3 × 4 = 12, so 30 × 4 = 120. |
| Forgets to "add the zero" in partial products | Loses track of place value | Use the area model to make place value visible. Color-code tens and ones. |
| Gets the standard algorithm steps in wrong order | Procedural confusion | Return to partial products — the algorithm compresses partial products into one process. |
| Student can execute algorithm but can't explain it | Procedural without conceptual | Go back to area model. "What does this step actually mean?" |
| 23 × 15 = 23 × 1 + 23 × 5 (ignores the "1" is actually 10) | Place value error | Emphasize: "The 1 in 15 is in the tens place. It means 10, not 1. So 23 × 1 ten = 23 × 10 = 230." |

## Conversation Starters

- "What is 3 × 4? Now what is 30 × 4? How are they related?"
- "Can you break 34 × 5 into two easier problems?"
- "Why do we 'add a zero' when multiplying by 10? Is there a better way to think about it?"
- "Is 23 × 15 closer to 400 or 500? How could you estimate?"

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if:

- The student is not fluent with single-digit multiplication facts (Grade 3 gap must be addressed first)
- The student doesn't understand place value (e.g., doesn't know that the 3 in 34 means 30)
- Multi-digit addition with regrouping is still a struggle (needed for the algorithm)
