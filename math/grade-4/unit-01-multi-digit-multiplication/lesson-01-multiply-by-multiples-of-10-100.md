# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 1, Lesson 1

## Topic

Multiplying by Multiples of 10 and 100

## Learning Objectives

- Students will use place-value patterns to multiply a single-digit number by a multiple of 10 (e.g., 30 x 4 = 120).
- Students will use place-value patterns to multiply a single-digit number by a multiple of 100 (e.g., 200 x 5 = 1,000).
- Students will explain the "append zeros" shortcut and connect it to place value.

## Prerequisites

- Fluency with single-digit multiplication facts (3.OA.7)
- Understanding of place value through thousands (3.NBT.1)

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Place-value chart handout (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands)
- Base-ten blocks (optional, for concrete modeling)

**Standards:** 4.NBT.5 — Multiply a whole number of up to four digits by a one-digit whole number using strategies based on place value and the properties of operations.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of basic multiplication facts and place-value understanding.

1. Flash five quick multiplication facts on the board. Students write answers on their dry-erase boards:
   - 3 x 4 = ?
   - 5 x 6 = ?
   - 7 x 8 = ?
   - 9 x 2 = ?
   - 4 x 5 = ?
2. Ask: "What is the value of the digit 3 in the number 300?" (300, or 3 hundreds)
3. Ask: "What is the value of the digit 5 in the number 50?" (50, or 5 tens)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Multiple of 10:** A number you get when you multiply any whole number by 10 (10, 20, 30, 40, ...).
- **Multiple of 100:** A number you get when you multiply any whole number by 100 (100, 200, 300, ...).
- **Place value:** The value of a digit based on its position in a number.
- **Factor:** A number being multiplied.
- **Product:** The answer to a multiplication problem.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a known fact.**
Write on the board: 3 x 4 = 12. Ask students to confirm the product.

**Step 2 — Introduce the pattern with multiples of 10.**
Write: 30 x 4 = ?
Say: "30 is the same as 3 tens. If we have 4 groups of 3 tens, how many tens do we have?" (12 tens)
"12 tens equals 120. Notice that we can take the basic fact 3 x 4 = 12 and append one zero because one of our factors had one trailing zero."

**Step 3 — Show more examples with multiples of 10.**
- 50 x 6 = ? Think: 5 x 6 = 30, append one zero -> 300
- 70 x 8 = ? Think: 7 x 8 = 56, append one zero -> 560

**Step 4 — Extend to multiples of 100.**
Write: 200 x 5 = ?
Say: "200 is the same as 2 hundreds. If we have 5 groups of 2 hundreds, we have 10 hundreds, which equals 1,000. We can also think: 2 x 5 = 10, then append two zeros -> 1,000."

**Step 5 — Show more examples with multiples of 100.**
- 400 x 3 = ? Think: 4 x 3 = 12, append two zeros -> 1,200
- 600 x 7 = ? Think: 6 x 7 = 42, append two zeros -> 4,200

**Step 6 — State the pattern clearly.**
"Count the trailing zeros in the multiple-of-10 or multiple-of-100 factor. Multiply the non-zero digits. Then attach all the trailing zeros to the product of the non-zero digits."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 40 x 7
- Basic fact: 4 x 7 = 28
- Trailing zeros in the factors: one zero (from 40)
- Append one zero: 280
- Answer: 40 x 7 = 280

**Example B:** 300 x 9
- Basic fact: 3 x 9 = 27
- Trailing zeros in the factors: two zeros (from 300)
- Append two zeros: 2,700
- Answer: 300 x 9 = 2,700

**Example C:** 60 x 5
- Basic fact: 6 x 5 = 30
- Trailing zeros in the factors: one zero (from 60)
- Append one zero: 300
- Note: The basic fact already ends in a zero, so we get 30 + one appended zero = 300.
- Answer: 60 x 5 = 300

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems as a class. Students solve on their boards; teacher circulates.

1. **20 x 8 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "What basic fact do you start with?" (2 x 8 = 16) "How many zeros do you append?" (one) "So what's the product?" (160)

2. **50 x 3 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "Tell your partner the basic fact first." (5 x 3 = 15) "Now append the zero." (150)

3. **400 x 6 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "How many trailing zeros does 400 have?" (two) "What is 4 x 6?" (24) "Attach the zeros." (2,400)

4. **700 x 5 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "Careful — what is 7 x 5?" (35) "Now append two zeros." (3,500)

5. **90 x 9 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "9 x 9 = ?" (81) "Append one zero." (810)

6. **500 x 8 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "5 x 8 = ?" (40) "Now attach two zeros to 40. How many zeros total?" (4,000 — the zero from 40 plus the two appended zeros)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

Solve the following two problems. Show or explain your thinking.

1. 40 x 9 = ___
2. 600 x 4 = ___

**Scoring:** Each problem is worth 1 point (2 points total). Students must get both correct to demonstrate mastery.

### If Mastery Is Not Met

- Provide a place-value chart and have the student model the problem with base-ten blocks.
- Pair the student with a peer tutor to talk through two additional problems (e.g., 30 x 6, 400 x 5).
- Assign targeted practice: five additional multiples-of-10 problems for homework.

### If Mastery Is Met

- Students move to the Extension activity below.
- Students may also begin the Lesson 2 preview warm-up if time permits.

## Extension (Optional)

- **Pattern Hunt:** Give students a chart with rows for x1, x10, x100 across the top and single-digit factors down the side. Have them fill in the products and describe the patterns they see in writing.
- **Story Problem Creation:** Students write their own word problem that uses multiplication by a multiple of 10 or 100, swap with a partner, and solve each other's problems.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Common Errors:**
- Students forget to append the zero(s) and just write the basic-fact product (e.g., writing 12 instead of 120 for 30 x 4).
- When the basic fact itself ends in zero (e.g., 6 x 5 = 30), students may lose track of total zeros. Stress counting all trailing zeros carefully.
- Students may confuse "append zeros" with "add zeros to the basic fact answer" (e.g., writing 1,200 instead of 120 for 30 x 4 because they append two zeros). Clarify: count only the trailing zeros from the factors.

**Pacing Advice:**
- If the warm-up takes longer than 5 minutes, shorten Direct Instruction by combining Steps 2-3 into one quick demonstration.
- If students grasp the multiples-of-10 pattern quickly, spend more time on multiples of 100 and the tricky cases where the basic fact ends in zero.
- The Guided Practice is the heart of the lesson — do not cut it short. If time is tight, reduce Independent Practice to problems 1-5 and assign the rest as homework.
