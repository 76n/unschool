# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 1, Lesson 6

## Topic

Estimating Products

## Learning Objectives

- Round a factor to the nearest ten or hundred before multiplying to estimate a product.
- Use estimation to predict whether an exact answer is reasonable.
- Explain why an estimate is greater than or less than the exact product based on how the factors were rounded.

## Prerequisites

- Rounding whole numbers to the nearest ten and hundred (Grade 3).
- Multiplication facts through 9 x 9.
- Multiplying a multi-digit number by a one-digit number (Lessons 1-5).

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher).
- Personal whiteboards or scratch paper (students).
- Number line (0-100) displayed on the board.
- Estimation Recording Sheet (one per student).

**Standards:** 4.NBT.5, 4.OA.3

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate rounding skills and build fluency with mental multiplication of multiples of ten.

1. Flash the following numbers one at a time. Students write each number rounded to the nearest ten on their whiteboards:
   - 28 (30), 43 (40), 67 (70), 85 (90), 52 (50)
2. Then flash these mental-math multiplication problems:
   - 30 x 4 (120), 50 x 6 (300), 70 x 3 (210), 20 x 8 (160)
3. Briefly discuss: "What makes multiplying by a multiple of ten easier?"

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Estimate** — a number close to the exact answer, found by using simpler numbers.
- **Round** — to replace a number with the nearest ten, hundred, or other place value.
- **Reasonable** — an answer that makes sense and is close to the estimate.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce the idea of estimation.**
Tell students: "Sometimes we don't need an exact answer. If you want to know roughly how much 28 packs of 6 stickers cost, an estimate is enough. Estimation also helps us check whether our exact answers make sense."

**Step 2 — Demonstrate rounding-then-multiplying.**
Write on the board: 28 x 6.
- "Which factor should we round? 28 is close to 30. Let's round 28 to 30."
- "Now multiply: 30 x 6 = 180."
- "So 28 x 6 is approximately 180."
- Calculate the exact answer together: 28 x 6 = 168.
- "Is 168 close to our estimate of 180? Yes! Our estimate tells us 168 is reasonable."

**Step 3 — Show when to round to the nearest hundred.**
Write: 312 x 4.
- "312 rounds to 300. So 300 x 4 = 1,200."
- Exact answer: 312 x 4 = 1,248.
- "1,248 is close to 1,200 — reasonable!"

**Step 4 — Discuss over-estimates vs. under-estimates.**
- "When we rounded 28 up to 30, our estimate (180) was a little more than the exact answer (168). Rounding up gives an over-estimate."
- "If we had a problem like 32 x 5 and rounded 32 down to 30, our estimate (150) would be a little less than the exact answer (160). Rounding down gives an under-estimate."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Estimate 47 x 3.
- Round 47 to 50.
- 50 x 3 = 150.
- Exact: 47 x 3 = 141. Estimate is reasonable.

**Example B:** Estimate 283 x 5.
- Round 283 to 300.
- 300 x 5 = 1,500.
- Exact: 283 x 5 = 1,415. Estimate is reasonable.

**Example C:** Estimate 68 x 7.
- Round 68 to 70.
- 70 x 7 = 490.
- Exact: 68 x 7 = 476. Estimate is reasonable.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems as a class. Students try each on their whiteboards first, then share.

1. Estimate 34 x 8.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What does 34 round to? Now multiply that rounded number by 8."

2. Estimate 72 x 6.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Round 72 to the nearest ten. What is your estimate? Is it an over-estimate or an under-estimate?"

3. Estimate 418 x 3.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Should you round to the nearest ten or the nearest hundred? Try both — which is easier to compute mentally?"

4. Marcus says 53 x 4 is about 250. Is his estimate reasonable? Explain.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What would you round 53 to? What estimate do you get? Compare it to Marcus's."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-level

1. Estimate 26 x 5 by rounding. Then find the exact product. Is your exact answer reasonable?
2. Estimate 63 x 4 by rounding. Then find the exact product.
3. Estimate 187 x 6 by rounding to the nearest hundred.

### Stretch

4. Estimate 45 x 8. Then find the exact product. What is the difference between your estimate and the exact answer?
5. A school orders 78 notebooks for each of 4 classrooms. Estimate the total. Then find the exact total.

### Challenge

6. Without finding the exact answer, determine: Is 37 x 9 greater than or less than 350? Use estimation to explain your reasoning.
7. Jamie estimated 54 x 7 as 400. What mistake might Jamie have made? What is a better estimate?

### Interleaved Review

8. (From Lesson 3) Solve using any strategy: 46 x 3 = ?
9. (From Lesson 5) Solve: 215 x 4 = ?

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Estimate 38 x 7 by rounding. Show your work.

**Problem 2:** Lena multiplied 42 x 6 and got 352. Use estimation to explain whether her answer is reasonable.

**Scoring:**
- 2/2 correct with clear reasoning = Mastery.
- 1/2 correct or incomplete reasoning = Approaching.
- 0/2 correct = Not yet.

### If Mastery Is Not Met

- Pair the student with a peer for 3 additional estimation problems during the next session's warm-up.
- Provide a number-line visual to support rounding before multiplying.
- Revisit rounding to the nearest ten as a stand-alone mini-lesson if the difficulty is with rounding itself.

### If Mastery Is Met

- Student moves to Extension activity.
- Student may serve as a "checking partner" for classmates during Independent Practice in the next lesson.

## Extension (Optional)

**Estimation Scavenger Hunt:** Give students a catalog or grocery flyer. They choose 3 items and estimate the cost of buying multiple quantities (e.g., "About how much would 6 boxes of cereal at $3.89 each cost?"). Students record their rounded calculation and then find the exact cost with a calculator to compare.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors

- **Rounding the wrong digit.** Some students round to the nearest ten when the number has three digits and a nearest-hundred estimate would be simpler. Encourage students to think about which rounding makes the multiplication easiest.
- **Forgetting the zero.** When multiplying 30 x 6, some students write 18 instead of 180. Remind them: "Multiply 3 x 6 = 18, then attach the zero back."
- **Thinking estimates must be exact.** Emphasize that an estimate is meant to be close, not perfect. The goal is a quick check, not a precise calculation.
- **Rounding both factors when only one is multi-digit.** In problems like 28 x 6, students may try to round 6 to 10. Clarify that single-digit numbers are already easy to multiply with, so we round the larger factor.

### Pacing Advice

- Spend extra time on Step 2 if students struggle with rounding. A brief detour to practice rounding on a number line is worthwhile.
- Guided Practice problem 4 (Marcus's estimate) is a good discussion point — do not rush it.
- If time is short, assign Stretch and Challenge problems as homework rather than skipping the Exit Ticket.

## Answer Key

### Guided Practice

1. 34 rounds to 30. 30 x 8 = 240. (Exact: 34 x 8 = 272.)
2. 72 rounds to 70. 70 x 6 = 420. Under-estimate (exact: 72 x 6 = 432).
3. Rounding to nearest hundred: 400 x 3 = 1,200. Rounding to nearest ten: 420 x 3 = 1,260. (Exact: 418 x 3 = 1,254.) Either estimate is acceptable.
4. 53 rounds to 50. 50 x 4 = 200. Marcus said 250, which is too high — he may have rounded 53 to 60 instead of 50, giving 60 x 4 = 240 (close to his 250) or made another error. A better estimate is 200. (Exact: 53 x 4 = 212.)

### Independent Practice

1. 26 rounds to 30. Estimate: 30 x 5 = 150. Exact: 26 x 5 = 130. The exact answer is reasonable (close to 150).
2. 63 rounds to 60. Estimate: 60 x 4 = 240. Exact: 63 x 4 = 252. Reasonable.
3. 187 rounds to 200. Estimate: 200 x 6 = 1,200. (Exact: 187 x 6 = 1,122.)
4. 45 rounds to 50. Estimate: 50 x 8 = 400. Exact: 45 x 8 = 360. Difference: 400 - 360 = 40.
5. 78 rounds to 80. Estimate: 80 x 4 = 320 notebooks. Exact: 78 x 4 = 312 notebooks.
6. 37 rounds to 40. 40 x 9 = 360. Since we rounded up, the exact product is less than 360. But is it less than 350? 37 x 9 = 333. Yes, 37 x 9 is less than 350.
7. Jamie may have rounded 54 to 50 and then multiplied 50 x 8 = 400 (wrong factor), or rounded 54 to 40 (incorrect rounding). The correct estimate: 54 rounds to 50, and 50 x 7 = 350. (Exact: 54 x 7 = 378.)

### Interleaved Review

8. 46 x 3 = 138.
9. 215 x 4 = 860.

### Exit Ticket

1. 38 rounds to 40. 40 x 7 = 280. Estimated product: approximately 280. (Exact: 38 x 7 = 266.)
2. Estimate: 42 rounds to 40. 40 x 6 = 240. Lena's answer of 352 is much larger than 240 — it is not reasonable. (Exact: 42 x 6 = 252, so Lena's answer of 352 is incorrect.)
