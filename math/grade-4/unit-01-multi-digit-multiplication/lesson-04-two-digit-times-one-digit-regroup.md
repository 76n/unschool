# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 1, Lesson 4

## Topic

Multiplying 2-Digit x 1-Digit (With Regrouping)

## Learning Objectives

- Students will multiply a two-digit number by a one-digit number when the product of the ones digits is 10 or greater (requiring regrouping/carrying).
- Students will use the standard algorithm with carrying to solve these problems.
- Students will verify their answers using the distributive property (partial products).

## Prerequisites

- Fluency with single-digit multiplication facts (3.OA.7)
- Multiplying by multiples of 10 (Lesson 1)
- The distributive property (Lesson 2)
- Multiplying 2-digit x 1-digit without regrouping in vertical format (Lesson 3)

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher)
- Student dry-erase boards or scratch paper
- Grid paper (helps keep digits aligned)
- Base-ten blocks (optional, for concrete modeling of regrouping)

**Standards:** 4.NBT.5 — Multiply a whole number of up to four digits by a one-digit whole number using strategies based on place value and the properties of operations.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review no-regrouping vertical multiplication (Lesson 3) and surface the need for a new strategy when products exceed 9.

1. Solve using the vertical format (no regrouping):
   - 21 x 4 = ?
   - 13 x 3 = ?
2. Now try this one:
   - 16 x 4 = ? (Ones: 6 x 4 = 24 — that's more than 9! What do we do?)

Say: "Today we'll learn what to do when a column product is 10 or more."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Regrouping (carrying):** When the product in a column is 10 or more, we write the ones digit of that product in the column and carry the tens digit to the next column.
- **Standard algorithm:** The step-by-step procedure for multiplying numbers using place value and regrouping.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Start with a problem that requires regrouping.**
Write on the board vertically:
```
    4 7
  x   6
  -----
```

**Step 2 — Multiply the ones.**
- 7 x 6 = 42
- 42 has 4 tens and 2 ones.
- Write the 2 in the ones column of the answer.
- Write the small 4 above the tens column (this is the carry).
```
      4
    4 7
  x   6
  -----
      2
```

**Step 3 — Multiply the tens.**
- 4 x 6 = 24
- Now add the carried 4: 24 + 4 = 28
- Write 28 to the left of the 2.
```
      4
    4 7
  x   6
  -----
  2 8 2
```

**Step 4 — Read the answer: 282.**

**Step 5 — Verify with the distributive property.**
- 47 x 6 = (40 x 6) + (7 x 6) = 240 + 42 = 282. It matches!

**Step 6 — Explain why regrouping works.**
Say: "When 7 x 6 gives us 42, the 42 means 4 tens and 2 ones. We put the 2 ones in the ones place and move the 4 tens up to join the other tens. Then when we multiply 4 tens x 6 = 24 tens, we add those 4 extra tens to get 28 tens, which is 280. So 280 + 2 = 282."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 36 x 4
```
      2
    3 6
  x   4
  -----
  1 4 4
```
- Ones: 6 x 4 = 24. Write 4, carry 2.
- Tens: 3 x 4 = 12, plus carried 2 = 14. Write 14.
- Answer: 36 x 4 = 144
- Check: (30 x 4) + (6 x 4) = 120 + 24 = 144

**Example B:** 58 x 3
```
      2
    5 8
  x   3
  -----
  1 7 4
```
- Ones: 8 x 3 = 24. Write 4, carry 2.
- Tens: 5 x 3 = 15, plus carried 2 = 17. Write 17.
- Answer: 58 x 3 = 174
- Check: (50 x 3) + (8 x 3) = 150 + 24 = 174

**Example C:** 75 x 8
```
      4
    7 5
  x   8
  -----
  6 0 0
```
- Ones: 5 x 8 = 40. Write 0, carry 4.
- Tens: 7 x 8 = 56, plus carried 4 = 60. Write 60.
- Answer: 75 x 8 = 600
- Check: (70 x 8) + (5 x 8) = 560 + 40 = 600

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems as a class. Students solve on their boards using the standard algorithm.

1. **29 x 3 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "Ones: 9 x 3 = ?" (27) "Write 7, carry ?" (2) "Tens: 2 x 3 = 6, plus 2 = ?" (8) "Answer?" (87)

2. **45 x 6 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "Ones: 5 x 6 = ?" (30) "Write 0, carry ?" (3) "Tens: 4 x 6 = 24, plus 3 = ?" (27) "Answer?" (270)

3. **64 x 7 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "Ones: 4 x 7 = ?" (28) "Write 8, carry ?" (2) "Tens: 6 x 7 = 42, plus 2 = ?" (44) "Answer?" (448)

4. **83 x 5 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "Try this one on your own. Then check with partial products." (Ones: 3 x 5 = 15, write 5, carry 1. Tens: 8 x 5 = 40, plus 1 = 41. Answer: 415)

5. **19 x 9 = ?**
   *Facilitator prompt:* "This one has a big carry. 9 x 9 = ?" (81) "Write 1, carry ?" (8) "1 x 9 = 9, plus 8 = ?" (17) "Answer?" (171)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

Solve using the standard algorithm. Show the carry.

1. 38 x 6 = ___
2. 54 x 7 = ___

**Scoring:** Each problem is worth 2 points — 1 point for correct regrouping/carrying, 1 point for the correct final answer (4 points total). Mastery = 3 out of 4 points.

### If Mastery Is Not Met

- Model the regrouping with base-ten blocks: physically trade 10 ones for 1 ten to show what carrying means.
- Have the student solve the same problem using partial products (distributive property) first, then translate each step into the standard algorithm side-by-side.
- Assign three scaffolded problems where the carry digit is pre-written for the student, and they only need to complete the tens-column computation.

### If Mastery Is Met

- Students move to the Extension activity below.
- Students may preview Lesson 5 by attempting 123 x 4.

## Extension (Optional)

- **Regrouping Detective:** Give students completed problems with intentional carrying errors. Students must find and correct the mistake in each problem.
- **Create Your Own:** Students write three multiplication problems that require regrouping and three that do not, then swap with a partner to solve and verify.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Common Errors:**
- Forgetting to add the carry: Students multiply the tens digit correctly but forget to add the small carried number (e.g., for 47 x 6, they write 4 x 6 = 24 instead of 24 + 4 = 28).
- Carrying the wrong digit: Students sometimes carry the ones digit and write the tens digit in the answer, reversing the regrouping (e.g., for 7 x 6 = 42, they write 4 and carry 2 instead of writing 2 and carrying 4).
- Not erasing the carry when moving to the next problem, leading to confusion on subsequent problems.

**Pacing Advice:**
- The warm-up problem (16 x 4) is designed to create productive struggle — let students try it and discover the issue before you teach the solution.
- Spend extra time on Step 6 (explaining why regrouping works) if students seem to be following the procedure without understanding. Connect each step back to place value.
- For Guided Practice, problems 1-3 have a single regrouping. Problems 4-5 are slightly harder. If students struggle on 1-3, do not proceed to 4-5 — do more examples at the easier level.
- Example C (75 x 8 = 600) is included deliberately because it produces a product ending in 00, which surprises some students. Draw attention to this.
