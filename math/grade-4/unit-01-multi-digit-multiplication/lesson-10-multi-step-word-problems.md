# Math — Grade 4 — Unit 1, Lesson 10

## Topic

Multi-Step Word Problems

## Learning Objectives

- Solve word problems that require two or more operations, including multiplication combined with addition or subtraction.
- Use the Read-Plan-Solve-Check strategy to organize thinking for multi-step problems.
- Write an equation or set of equations to represent a word problem before computing.
- Use estimation to verify that final answers are reasonable.

## Prerequisites

- Multiplying two-digit by one-digit and two-digit by two-digit numbers (Lessons 3-9).
- Addition and subtraction of multi-digit whole numbers (Grade 3 and ongoing).
- Estimating products by rounding (Lesson 6).
- Familiarity with word-problem vocabulary (total, each, how many more, how many left, altogether).

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers (teacher).
- Personal whiteboards or scratch paper (students).
- Read-Plan-Solve-Check anchor chart (displayed on the wall).
- Word-problem strip cards (one set per pair, for Guided Practice).
- Colored pencils (optional, for underlining key information in problems).

**Standards:** 4.OA.3

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of multiplication and reinforce the habit of reading carefully before computing.

Read this problem aloud (do NOT show the numbers yet):

"A baker makes muffins every morning. She puts them in boxes. How many muffins does she make in a week?"

1. "Can you solve this? No! Why not?" (We are missing information — how many muffins per day? How many days?)
2. Now reveal the full problem: "A baker makes 24 muffins every morning. She bakes 5 days a week. How many muffins does she make in a week?"
3. Students solve: 24 x 5 = 120 muffins.
4. "Today we will solve problems that need more than one step. Reading carefully and planning before solving will be more important than ever."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Multi-step problem** — a word problem that requires two or more operations (e.g., multiply then add) to find the answer.
- **Equation** — a number sentence that shows the relationship between quantities (e.g., 24 x 5 + 36 = 156).
- **Reasonable** — an answer that makes sense when compared to an estimate.
- **Operation** — a mathematical process: addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce the Read-Plan-Solve-Check strategy.**
Post the anchor chart:
- **Read:** Read the problem twice. Underline the question. Circle important numbers.
- **Plan:** Decide which operations to use and in what order. Write an equation or draw a diagram.
- **Solve:** Do the computation carefully, one step at a time.
- **Check:** Estimate to see if the answer is reasonable. Re-read the question to make sure you answered what was asked.

**Step 2 — Model with a two-step problem.**
"A school orders 18 boxes of crayons. Each box has 24 crayons. The school already had 150 crayons in the supply closet. How many crayons does the school have now?"

- **Read:** Question = "How many crayons does the school have now?" Key numbers: 18 boxes, 24 per box, 150 already.
- **Plan:** First multiply to find the new crayons (18 x 24), then add the 150 crayons already there.
- **Solve:**
  - Step 1: 18 x 24. Using the standard algorithm: 18 x 4 = 72, 18 x 20 = 360. Total: 72 + 360 = 432 new crayons.
  - Step 2: 432 + 150 = 582 crayons total.
- **Check:** Estimate: 20 x 24 = 480, plus 150 = 630. Our answer 582 is close to 630 — reasonable!

**Step 3 — Model with a problem requiring subtraction after multiplication.**
"Maria earns $15 each week for doing chores. After 12 weeks, she buys a skateboard that costs $125. How much money does she have left?"

- **Read:** Question = "How much money does she have left?" Key numbers: $15/week, 12 weeks, $125 skateboard.
- **Plan:** Multiply to find total earnings (15 x 12), then subtract the cost of the skateboard.
- **Solve:**
  - Step 1: 15 x 12 = 180. (15 x 2 = 30, 15 x 10 = 150. Total: 30 + 150 = 180.)
  - Step 2: 180 - 125 = 55.
- **Check:** Estimate: 15 x 10 = 150, minus 125 = 25. Our answer is $55, and using the more precise calculation (12 weeks, not 10), it makes sense that she has more. Reasonable!
- Answer: Maria has $55 left.

**Step 4 — Emphasize re-reading the question.**
"A common mistake is to stop too soon. If the question asks 'how much is left,' you must subtract. If it asks 'how many altogether,' you must add. Always re-read the question before writing your final answer."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** "A farmer plants 34 rows of corn with 26 plants in each row. He also plants 48 rows of beans with 15 plants in each row. How many plants did the farmer plant altogether?"

- Plan: Find corn plants (34 x 26), find bean plants (48 x 15), then add.
- Corn: 34 x 26. Partial products: 30x20=600, 30x6=180, 4x20=80, 4x6=24. Total: 884.
- Beans: 48 x 15. Partial products: 40x10=400, 40x5=200, 8x10=80, 8x5=40. Total: 720.
- Altogether: 884 + 720 = 1,604 plants.
- Estimate check: 30x25=750 + 50x15=750 = 1,500. Answer 1,604 is reasonable.

**Example B:** "A toy store received 24 boxes of action figures with 12 figures in each box. They put 175 figures on the shelves and stored the rest in the back. How many figures are stored in the back?"

- Plan: Find total figures (24 x 12), then subtract 175.
- 24 x 12 = 288. (24x2=48, 24x10=240. Total: 288.)
- 288 - 175 = 113 figures stored in the back.
- Estimate check: 25 x 12 = 300, minus 175 = 125. Answer 113 is reasonable.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Students work in pairs on these problems. After each, one pair shares their Read-Plan-Solve-Check work with the class.

1. A bookstore sells 16 copies of a book each day for 14 days. Then they receive a new shipment of 85 books. How many books do they have from sales and the new shipment combined? (Note: re-read carefully — "from sales and the new shipment" means total sold plus new shipment.)
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Read the problem again. What are you being asked to find? First find how many books were sold, then figure out what to do with the 85."

2. Ms. Chen buys 15 packs of markers for her classroom. Each pack has 12 markers. She gives 45 markers to another teacher. How many markers does Ms. Chen have left?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What operation do you use first? What operation finishes the problem?"

3. The school cafeteria serves lunch to 26 students per table. There are 14 tables in the cafeteria. If 312 students need lunch, does the cafeteria have enough seats for everyone? Explain.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find the total seats first. Then compare to 312. The answer is not just a number — you need to say yes or no and explain."

4. Create your own two-step word problem using the numbers 35, 12, and 200. Trade with your partner and solve each other's problem.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Think of a real-world situation. What could 35 and 12 represent? Where does 200 fit in?"

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-level

1. A library has 23 shelves with 18 books on each shelf. The librarian adds 64 new books to the library. How many books are in the library now?
2. Ryan earns $14 per hour. He works 22 hours. He spends $195 on a bicycle. How much money does he have left?
3. A pet store has 12 fish tanks. Each tank holds 15 fish. The store sells 87 fish. How many fish remain?

### Stretch

4. Two classes are collecting cans for a food drive. Class A collects 28 cans per day for 15 days. Class B collects 32 cans per day for 12 days. Which class collected more cans, and by how many?
5. A garden center sells flower pots for $13 each. On Monday they sold 24 pots, and on Tuesday they sold 18 pots. How much money did the garden center earn from flower-pot sales on both days combined?

### Challenge

6. A theater has 25 rows on the ground floor with 32 seats each, and 15 rows in the balcony with 18 seats each. Are there more than 1,000 seats total? How many seats are there altogether?
7. Emma and Noah both save money each week. Emma saves $18 per week for 14 weeks. Noah saves $22 per week for 11 weeks. Who saved more, and can they combine their savings to buy a game system that costs $500? Explain.

### Interleaved Review

8. (From Lesson 9) Use the standard algorithm to solve 37 x 16.
9. (From Lesson 6) Estimate 78 x 42 by rounding both factors. Is the exact product greater or less than your estimate?

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A school orders 16 boxes of pencils with 25 pencils in each box. The school gives 128 pencils to the fourth graders and keeps the rest for other grades. How many pencils are left for other grades? Show your Read-Plan-Solve-Check work.

**Problem 2:** What operations did you use, and in what order? Why?

**Scoring:**
- Problem 1: Correct answer (272) with clear multi-step work shown = Mastery of computation.
- Problem 2: Identifies "multiply first, then subtract" and explains that you must find the total before you can take away = Mastery of reasoning.
- Both mastery = Full mastery.
- One of two = Approaching.
- 0 of 2 = Not yet.

### If Mastery Is Not Met

- Provide the student with a structured Read-Plan-Solve-Check graphic organizer with labeled boxes for each step, so they practice the strategy with scaffolding.
- Work through one additional problem one-on-one, asking the student to verbalize each step before writing.
- If the difficulty is with the multiplication itself (not the problem-solving), revisit Lessons 8-9 for computation practice.

### If Mastery Is Met

- Student moves to Extension activity.
- Student may write two-step problems for classmates to solve as a "Problem of the Day" during future warm-ups.

## Extension (Optional)

**Real-World Project — Field Trip Budget:** Students plan a field trip for their class. Given information:
- Bus rental: $225 per bus. Each bus holds 45 students.
- Admission tickets: $12 per student.
- Lunch: $8 per student.
- There are 28 students in the class plus 4 adults (who also need tickets and lunch).

Students must determine:
1. How many buses are needed?
2. What is the total cost for buses?
3. What is the total cost for admission?
4. What is the total cost for lunch?
5. What is the grand total cost for the field trip?

This multi-step project integrates multiplication, addition, and real-world reasoning.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors

- **Stopping after one step.** Students find the product and write it as their final answer without completing the addition or subtraction. Teach them to re-read the question and ask: "Did I answer what was asked?"
- **Using the wrong operation for the second step.** Students may add when they should subtract, or vice versa. Key words help, but context comprehension is more reliable. Encourage students to act out or visualize the scenario.
- **Computation errors carrying over.** A small multiplication mistake in Step 1 leads to an incorrect final answer. Encourage estimation after each step, not just at the end.
- **Not showing work.** Multi-step problems require clear organization. Insist that students label each step (e.g., "Step 1: Find total muffins. Step 2: Add the extra muffins.") so their thinking is visible and checkable.
- **Difficulty identifying the hidden question.** In a problem like "How much is left?", the hidden first question is "How much was there to begin with?" Explicitly teach students to find the hidden question.

### Pacing Advice

- The Read-Plan-Solve-Check strategy introduction (Step 1) can be brief if students have encountered similar frameworks before. If this is new, spend a full 3 minutes on the anchor chart.
- Guided Practice problem 3 (cafeteria seats) is especially valuable because the answer is a comparison, not just a number. Give students time to write a complete sentence.
- Guided Practice problem 4 (create your own) is engaging but can take longer than expected. If time is short, assign it as homework.
- The Challenge problems require careful reading and multiple steps. Do not expect all students to finish them in class; they work well as take-home enrichment.

### Interleaved Review

8. 37 x 16: 37x6=222. (6x7=42, write 2 carry 4; 6x3=18, +4=22.) 37x10=370. Total: 222+370 = 592.
9. 78 rounds to 80, 42 rounds to 40. 80 x 40 = 3,200. Since 78 < 80 and 42 > 40, the rounding effects partially cancel. Exact: 78 x 42 = 3,276. The exact product (3,276) is greater than the estimate (3,200).

### Exit Ticket

1. **Read:** 16 boxes, 25 pencils per box, give away 128 to fourth graders. Question: How many left for other grades?
   **Plan:** Multiply (16 x 25), then subtract 128.
   **Solve:** 16 x 25 = 400. (16x5=80, 16x20=320. Total: 400.) Then 400 - 128 = 272 pencils.
   **Check:** Estimate: 16 x 25 = 400 (exact, since 25 is easy to multiply). 400 - 128 is about 400 - 130 = 270. Answer 272 is reasonable.
   Answer: 272 pencils are left for other grades.

2. Multiplication first (to find the total number of pencils), then subtraction (to find how many are left after giving some away). You must know the total before you can subtract from it.
