## Section C: Estimating Products (2 items)

**C1.** Estimate the product of 48 × 6 by rounding.

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Rounds 48 to 50 → 50 × 6 = 300 (or similar reasonable estimate) | 1 point |
| Exact answer (288) without estimation shown | 0.5 points |

**C2.** A student says 37 × 8 = 2,960. Without calculating the exact answer, is this reasonable? Explain.

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| No — 40 × 8 = 320, so the answer should be near 300, not 2,960. The student likely made a place value error. | 1 point |
| Says "no" without explanation | 0.5 points |

## Section E: Word Problems (3 items)

**E1.** "A theater has 28 rows of seats. Each row has 15 seats. How many seats are in the theater?"

Equation: _______________
Answer: ___ seats

(28 × 15 = 420 seats)

**E2.** "A school orders 36 boxes of pencils. Each box has 12 pencils. After giving 100 pencils to the art room, how many pencils does the school have left?"

Show your work.
Answer: ___

(36 × 12 = 432; 432 − 100 = 332 pencils)

**E3.** "Maria buys 4 packs of stickers with 25 stickers each. Jake buys 3 packs with 30 stickers each. Who has more stickers, and by how many?"

Show your work.
Answer: ___

(Maria: 4 × 25 = 100. Jake: 3 × 30 = 90. Maria has 10 more.)

## Rubric and Mastery Determination

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Next Steps |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Mastered** | 13-16 (80-100%) with ≥50% per section | Solid multi-digit multiplication | Advance to Unit 2 (Multi-Digit Division). |
| **Approaching** | 10-12 (63-79%) | Partial fluency; specific strategy gaps | Identify weak areas. If multiples of 10/100 are weak → reteach Lesson 1. If algorithm errors → reteach Lessons 4-5. If 2×2-digit → reteach Lessons 7-9 with area models. |
| **Developing** | Below 10 (<63%) | Significant gaps | Check prerequisites: single-digit facts fluent? Place value solid? Multi-digit addition? Address root cause. |

### Section-Specific Reteaching Guide

| If struggling in... | Focus on... |
|---|---|
| Section A | Patterns with zeros: 3×4=12, 30×4=120, 300×4=1200. Use place value charts. |
| Section B | Return to partial products (distributive property). If regrouping errors → practice carrying with smaller problems first. |
| Section C | Practice estimating before computing for 5-10 problems. Build the habit of checking reasonableness. |
| Section D | Return to the area model (Lesson 7). The visual decomposition must be solid before moving to partial products and the algorithm. |
| Section E | If computation is correct but setup is wrong → practice identifying what to multiply. If computation is wrong → fix the computation first. |

---

## Facilitator Observations

- Which method does the student prefer for 2×2-digit: area model, partial products, or standard algorithm?
- Does the student estimate before or after computing?
- Are single-digit fact errors causing multi-digit errors? (If so, address fact fluency.)
- Does the student show organized work or make place-value alignment errors?
