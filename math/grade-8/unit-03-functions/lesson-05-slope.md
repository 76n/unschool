# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 3, Lesson 5

## Topic
Slope

## Learning Objectives
- Define slope as a measure of steepness and as the rate of change of a linear function.
- Calculate slope from two points using the formula: slope = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1).
- Classify slopes as positive, negative, zero, or undefined.
- Interpret slope in real-world contexts as a rate of change.

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of linear functions and constant rate of change (Lesson 4)
- Ability to subtract integers, including negative numbers (Grade 7)
- Ability to read coordinates from a graph (Grade 6-7)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Graph paper or pre-printed coordinate planes.
- Ruler or straightedge.
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 8.F.4 — Construct a function to model a linear relationship between two quantities. Determine the rate of change and initial value of the function from a description of a relationship or from two (x, y) values, including reading these from a table or from a graph.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build intuition about steepness before introducing the formal definition.

1. Imagine three hills: Hill A rises gently over a long distance. Hill B rises steeply over a short distance. Hill C is perfectly flat. Which hill is hardest to climb? (Hill B)
2. Look at this table:

   | x | y |
   |---|---|
   | 0 | 1 |
   | 1 | 4 |
   | 2 | 7 |
   | 3 | 10 |

   How much does y increase each time x increases by 1? (+3)

3. Ask: "That number — the +3 — is called the SLOPE. Today we learn how to calculate it, what it means, and why it matters."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Slope** — a measure of the steepness of a line; the ratio of the vertical change (rise) to the horizontal change (run) between any two points on the line.
- **Rise** — the vertical change between two points (change in y).
- **Run** — the horizontal change between two points (change in x).
- **Positive slope** — a line that goes UP from left to right.
- **Negative slope** — a line that goes DOWN from left to right.
- **Zero slope** — a horizontal line (no vertical change).
- **Undefined slope** — a vertical line (no horizontal change; division by zero).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Define slope as rise over run.**

Write on the board: slope = rise / run = (change in y) / (change in x)

Say: "Slope tells you how steep a line is and in which direction it tilts. Imagine walking along a line from left to right. The RISE is how far you go up or down. The RUN is how far you go to the right. Slope is the ratio of rise to run."

Draw or describe: "If you move 1 step to the right and go up 3 steps, the slope is 3/1 = 3. If you move 1 step to the right and go down 2 steps, the slope is -2/1 = -2. The negative sign tells you the line goes downward."

**Step 2 — Introduce the slope formula.**

Write on the board: Given two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2):

slope = m = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1)

Say: "We use the letter m for slope. To find the slope, pick any two points on the line, subtract the y-values (that gives the rise), and subtract the x-values in the SAME ORDER (that gives the run)."

Say: "The order matters — you must subtract in the same direction for both. If you start with y2 on top, you must start with x2 on the bottom. Switching the order of subtraction in just one part will flip the sign."

**Step 3 — Demonstrate with two points.**

Find the slope of the line through (1, 2) and (4, 8).

- m = (8 - 2) / (4 - 1) = 6 / 3 = 2.
- Rise: 6 (go up 6). Run: 3 (go right 3). Slope = 2.

Say: "A slope of 2 means for every 1 unit you move to the right, you go up 2 units. The line is fairly steep and goes upward."

**Step 4 — Demonstrate with a negative slope.**

Find the slope of the line through (1, 7) and (3, 1).

- m = (1 - 7) / (3 - 1) = -6 / 2 = -3.
- Rise: -6 (go down 6). Run: 2 (go right 2). Slope = -3.

Say: "A slope of -3 means for every 1 unit you move to the right, you go DOWN 3 units. The line goes downward from left to right."

**Step 5 — Show the four types of slope.**

Draw or describe four lines:

1. **Positive slope:** A line going upward from left to right, like climbing a hill. Example: the line through (0, 0) and (3, 6) has slope 2.
2. **Negative slope:** A line going downward from left to right, like going down a slide. Example: the line through (0, 4) and (4, 0) has slope -1.
3. **Zero slope:** A horizontal line, like a flat road. Example: the line y = 3 passes through (0, 3) and (5, 3). m = (3 - 3)/(5 - 0) = 0/5 = 0.
4. **Undefined slope:** A vertical line, like an elevator. Example: the line x = 2 passes through (2, 0) and (2, 5). m = (5 - 0)/(2 - 2) = 5/0 = undefined (division by zero).

Say: "Remember: horizontal lines have zero slope (the numerator is 0). Vertical lines have undefined slope (the denominator is 0). A common mnemonic is: 'Zero slope is like a zero on its side — flat. Undefined is like standing a number up — straight vertical.'"

**Step 6 — Interpret slope as rate of change.**

Say: "In real life, slope IS the rate of change. If a car drives at a constant speed, the slope of the distance-time graph is the speed. If a bank account earns $50 per month, the slope of the balance-time graph is 50."

Example: A plumber charges $40 per hour. The slope of the cost-vs-hours graph is 40. This means the cost increases by $40 for each additional hour.

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Positive slope from two points**
Find the slope of the line through (2, 3) and (5, 12).

- m = (12 - 3) / (5 - 2) = 9 / 3 = 3.
- The slope is 3. The line rises 3 units for every 1 unit to the right.

**Example B: Negative slope from two points**
Find the slope of the line through (-1, 5) and (3, -3).

- m = (-3 - 5) / (3 - (-1)) = -8 / 4 = -2.
- The slope is -2. The line falls 2 units for every 1 unit to the right.

**Example C: Slope from a table**
| x | y |
|---|---|
| 0 | 6 |
| 2 | 10 |
| 4 | 14 |
| 6 | 18 |

Pick any two points, say (0, 6) and (2, 10):
- m = (10 - 6) / (2 - 0) = 4 / 2 = 2.

Verify with (4, 14) and (6, 18):
- m = (18 - 14) / (6 - 4) = 4 / 2 = 2.

The slope is 2. It is the same between any two points because this is a linear function.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. The student attempts each problem first, then discusses.

1. Find the slope of the line through (1, 4) and (5, 16).
   - *Answer:* m = (16 - 4) / (5 - 1) = 12 / 4 = 3.

2. Find the slope of the line through (2, 9) and (6, 1).
   - *Answer:* m = (1 - 9) / (6 - 2) = -8 / 4 = -2.

3. Find the slope of the line through (0, 5) and (4, 5).
   - *Answer:* m = (5 - 5) / (4 - 0) = 0 / 4 = 0. This is a horizontal line.

4. Find the slope of the line through (3, -2) and (3, 7).
   - *Answer:* m = (7 - (-2)) / (3 - 3) = 9 / 0 = undefined. This is a vertical line.

5. Find the slope from this table:

   | x | y |
   |---|---|
   | 1 | 10 |
   | 3 | 4 |
   | 5 | -2 |

   - *Answer:* Using (1, 10) and (3, 4): m = (4 - 10) / (3 - 1) = -6 / 2 = -3.

6. A hiker climbs 800 feet in elevation over a horizontal distance of 2 miles. What is the slope of the trail?
   - *Answer:* slope = 800 / 2 = 400 feet per mile. (In context: the trail rises 400 feet for every mile of horizontal distance.)

**Facilitator tip:** When students get a negative slope, make sure they understand that the negative sign means the line goes downward. A common error is computing the rise and run both as positive numbers and forgetting the direction. Emphasize: always subtract in the SAME order for both y and x.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Calculating Slope from Two Points

Find the slope of the line through each pair of points.

1. (2, 5) and (6, 13)
2. (0, 7) and (3, 1)
3. (-2, -3) and (4, 9)
4. (1, 8) and (5, 8)
5. (-3, 4) and (-3, -2)
6. (0, 0) and (6, -18)
7. (-4, -1) and (2, 11)
8. (3, 10) and (7, 2)

### Part B: Slope from Tables

Find the slope from each table.

9. | x | y |
   |---|---|
   | 0 | -3 |
   | 1 | 1 |
   | 2 | 5 |
   | 3 | 9 |

10. | x | y |
    |---|---|
    | 2 | 20 |
    | 4 | 14 |
    | 6 | 8 |
    | 8 | 2 |

11. | x | y |
    |---|---|
    | 0 | 0 |
    | 3 | 6 |
    | 6 | 12 |
    | 9 | 18 |

### Part C: Word Problems

12. A candle is 15 inches tall and burns at a rate of 0.5 inches per hour. What is the slope of the height-vs-time graph? Is it positive or negative? Explain what the slope means.
13. A phone company charges $0.05 per text message. What is the slope of the cost-vs-texts graph? If you send 200 texts, what is the cost?

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. m = (13 - 5) / (6 - 2) = 8 / 4 = 2.
2. m = (1 - 7) / (3 - 0) = -6 / 3 = -2.
3. m = (9 - (-3)) / (4 - (-2)) = 12 / 6 = 2.
4. m = (8 - 8) / (5 - 1) = 0 / 4 = 0. (Horizontal line.)
5. m = (-2 - 4) / (-3 - (-3)) = -6 / 0 = undefined. (Vertical line.)
6. m = (-18 - 0) / (6 - 0) = -18 / 6 = -3.
7. m = (11 - (-1)) / (2 - (-4)) = 12 / 6 = 2.
8. m = (2 - 10) / (7 - 3) = -8 / 4 = -2.

**Part B**
9. Using (0, -3) and (1, 1): m = (1 - (-3)) / (1 - 0) = 4 / 1 = 4.
10. Using (2, 20) and (4, 14): m = (14 - 20) / (4 - 2) = -6 / 2 = -3.
11. Using (0, 0) and (3, 6): m = (6 - 0) / (3 - 0) = 6 / 3 = 2.

**Part C**
12. The slope is -0.5. It is negative because the candle gets shorter over time (the height decreases). The slope means the candle loses 0.5 inches of height for each hour of burning.
13. The slope is 0.05 (dollars per text). For 200 texts: cost = 0.05 * 200 = $10.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** Find the slope of the line through (3, -1) and (7, 11).
**Problem 2:** A line passes through (0, 8) and (4, 8). What is the slope? Is this line horizontal or vertical?

**Target response:**
1. m = (11 - (-1)) / (7 - 3) = 12 / 4 = 3.
2. m = (8 - 8) / (4 - 0) = 0 / 4 = 0. The line is horizontal.

## Extension

- **Slope Scavenger Hunt:** Find three objects or surfaces in the real world that have different slopes (e.g., a ramp, a staircase, a roof). Estimate the rise and run for each and calculate the approximate slope. Rank them from least steep to most steep.
- **Same Slope, Different Lines:** Plot three different lines that all have a slope of 2 but different y-intercepts. How are the lines related? (They are parallel — they never intersect.) Now plot three lines that all pass through the point (0, 3) but have different slopes. What do you notice? (They all cross at the same point on the y-axis but fan out in different directions.)
