# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Topic
Geometry Problem Solving

## Learning Objectives
- Apply transformations (translations, reflections, rotations, dilations) in multi-step problems.
- Determine congruence and similarity using transformations.
- Apply the Pythagorean Theorem in geometric and real-world contexts.
- Integrate multiple unit concepts to solve complex problems.

## Prerequisites
- All transformation types and their coordinate rules (Lessons 1-5)
- Congruence and similarity (Lessons 6-7)
- Pythagorean Theorem and its applications (Lessons 8-9)

## Materials Needed
- Graph paper
- Ruler or straightedge
- Protractor
- Calculator (for square root computations)
- Colored pencils
- Index card or half-sheet for Exit Ticket

**Standards:** 8.G.1-8.G.8 (comprehensive review of all geometry standards)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick review of key concepts from each major topic in the unit.

1. Translate the point (3, -2) by (x - 4, y + 6). Result: (-1, 4).
2. Reflect the point (5, -3) over the x-axis. Result: (5, 3).
3. A triangle has sides 5, 12, and 13. Is it a right triangle? (5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13^2. Yes.)
4. Two similar triangles have a scale factor of 3. One side of the smaller triangle is 7. What is the corresponding side of the larger? (21.)

Say: "Today we put everything together. You will solve problems that combine transformations, congruence, similarity, and the Pythagorean Theorem."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- All vocabulary from Lessons 1-9 (review as needed during problem solving).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Problem-solving strategies for geometry.**

Say: "When you face a geometry problem that combines multiple concepts, use this strategy:"

Write on the board:
1. **Read carefully.** What is the problem asking for?
2. **Draw a diagram.** Label all known information.
3. **Identify the concept.** Is this a transformation problem? A congruence/similarity problem? A Pythagorean Theorem problem? It might be more than one.
4. **Apply the appropriate tool.** Use the coordinate rule, set up a proportion, or apply a^2 + b^2 = c^2.
5. **Check your answer.** Does it make sense? Is the hypotenuse the longest side? Is the scale factor consistent?

**Step 2 — Walk through a multi-concept problem.**

Problem: Triangle ABC has vertices A(0, 0), B(6, 0), C(0, 8).
(a) Find the length of side BC.
(b) Reflect triangle ABC over the y-axis to get triangle A'B'C'. List the image vertices.
(c) Is triangle A'B'C' congruent to triangle ABC? Explain.
(d) Dilate triangle ABC by scale factor k = 1/2 to get triangle DEF. List the image vertices.
(e) Is triangle DEF similar to triangle ABC? Congruent? Explain.

Solution:
(a) BC: from (6, 0) to (0, 8). Horizontal: 6, Vertical: 8. BC = sqrt(36 + 64) = sqrt(100) = **10**.
(b) Reflect over y-axis: A'(0, 0), B'(-6, 0), C'(0, 8).
(c) **Yes, congruent.** Reflection is a rigid transformation that preserves all distances and angles.
(d) Dilate by 1/2: D(0, 0), E(3, 0), F(0, 4).
(e) **Similar but not congruent.** The dilation preserves shape (same angles) but changes size (sides are half as long). Scale factor = 1/2.

**Step 3 — Walk through a real-world multi-step problem.**

Problem: A park has a rectangular playground that is 30 meters long and 40 meters wide. A diagonal walking path cuts across the playground.
(a) How long is the diagonal path?
(b) A scale model of the playground is built with a scale factor of 1:100. What are the dimensions of the model?
(c) How long is the diagonal path on the model?

Solution:
(a) 30^2 + 40^2 = 900 + 1600 = 2500. Diagonal = sqrt(2500) = **50 meters**.
(b) Model: 30/100 = 0.3 m and 40/100 = 0.4 m. So **0.3 m by 0.4 m** (or 30 cm by 40 cm).
(c) Model diagonal: 50/100 = **0.5 m** (or 50 cm). Check: 0.3^2 + 0.4^2 = 0.09 + 0.16 = 0.25. sqrt(0.25) = 0.5. Confirmed.

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Point P(2, 3) is rotated 90 degrees CCW to get P'. Then P' is translated by (x + 1, y - 2) to get P''. Find the distance from P to P''.
- Rotate 90 CCW: P(2, 3) -> P'(-3, 2).
- Translate: P'(-3, 2) -> P''(-3 + 1, 2 - 2) = P''(-2, 0).
- Distance P to P'': horizontal = |-2 - 2| = 4, vertical = |0 - 3| = 3. d = sqrt(16 + 9) = sqrt(25) = **5**.

**Example B:** Two triangles are shown. Triangle 1 has sides 5, 12, 13. Triangle 2 has sides 10, 24, 26. Are they similar? Are they congruent? Is Triangle 1 a right triangle?
- Ratios: 10/5 = 2, 24/12 = 2, 26/13 = 2. **Similar, scale factor 2.** Not congruent (different sizes).
- Check Triangle 1: 5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13^2. **Yes, right triangle.** Since Triangle 2 is similar, it is also a right triangle.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Mixed Transformation Problems**

1. Triangle with vertices (1, 1), (4, 1), (1, 5). Reflect over the y-axis, then dilate by k = 2.
   - Reflect: (-1, 1), (-4, 1), (-1, 5).
   - Dilate: (-2, 2), (-8, 2), (-2, 10).
   - Ask: "Is the final triangle congruent to the original? Similar?" (Similar with scale factor 2, not congruent.)

2. Point A(3, 4). Rotate 180 degrees. Call the image A'. Find the distance from A to A'.
   - A'(-3, -4).
   - Distance: horizontal = |3 - (-3)| = 6, vertical = |4 - (-4)| = 8.
   - d = sqrt(36 + 64) = sqrt(100) = **10**.
   - Ask: "Is it a coincidence that this distance is exactly twice the distance from A to the origin?" (No — the origin is the midpoint of A and A' in a 180-degree rotation. Distance from A to origin: sqrt(9 + 16) = 5. Distance A to A' = 2 x 5 = 10.)

**Activity 2: Real-World Application**

3. A flagpole casts a shadow 15 feet long. At the same time, a 6-foot-tall person standing nearby casts a shadow 4.5 feet long. How tall is the flagpole?
   - The triangles formed by the flagpole and its shadow and by the person and their shadow are similar (same sun angle).
   - Scale factor: 15/4.5 = 10/3.
   - Flagpole height = 6 x (10/3) = **20 feet**.

4. A rectangular swimming pool is 24 feet by 10 feet. A lifeguard at one corner needs to swim diagonally to the opposite corner. How far must the lifeguard swim?
   - 24^2 + 10^2 = 576 + 100 = 676. d = sqrt(676) = **26 feet**.

**Facilitator tip:** Encourage the student to classify each problem before solving: "Is this a transformation problem, a similarity problem, a Pythagorean Theorem problem, or a combination?" This metacognitive step builds problem-solving independence.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. Translate (2, -3) by (x + 4, y + 1), then reflect over the x-axis. What is the final image?
2. Triangle A has sides 3, 4, 5. Triangle B has sides 9, 12, 15. Are they similar? Are they congruent? Is Triangle A a right triangle?
3. Find the distance between (-3, 4) and (5, -2).
4. A 20-foot rope is attached to the top of a vertical pole and staked to the ground 12 feet from the base of the pole. How tall is the pole?
5. Name the four types of transformations studied in this unit. Which are rigid? Which is not?

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

6. Triangle PQR: P(0, 0), Q(5, 0), R(0, 12). Find the length of each side. Verify that PQR is a right triangle. Then dilate by k = 2 and verify that the image is also a right triangle.
7. A figure with vertices (1, 2), (4, 2), (4, 6) is reflected over the line y = 0 (the x-axis) and then translated by (x - 6, y). Is the resulting figure congruent to the original? Similar? Justify your answer.
8. Two cell phone towers are 5 miles apart. A drone flies from the top of one tower (100 feet tall) directly to the top of the other tower (100 feet tall). How far does the drone fly? (Hint: The towers are the same height, so the path is horizontal. But if one tower were 100 feet and the other 180 feet, the path would not be horizontal — work out that version too.)

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

9. A triangle has vertices at A(1, 1), B(5, 4), and C(2, 8). Find the perimeter of the triangle. Is it a right triangle?
10. A square has vertices at (0, 0), (4, 0), (4, 4), (0, 4). It is rotated 90 degrees CCW about the origin, then dilated by k = 1.5 from the origin. List the final vertices. Is the final figure similar to the original? What is the scale factor?

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A right triangle has legs 9 and 12. Find the hypotenuse. A similar triangle has a hypotenuse of 45. What is the scale factor? What are the legs of the similar triangle?

**Problem 2:** Triangle DEF has vertices D(0, 0), E(3, 0), F(0, 4). Triangle D'E'F' has vertices D'(0, 0), E'(0, 3), F'(-4, 0). Are these triangles congruent? If so, describe the transformation.

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 correct.**

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. Translate: (2 + 4, -3 + 1) = (6, -2). Reflect over x-axis: **(6, 2)**.
2. Ratios: 9/3 = 3, 12/4 = 3, 15/5 = 3. **Similar, scale factor 3.** Not congruent (different sizes). Triangle A: 3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5^2. **Yes, right triangle.**
3. Horizontal: 5 - (-3) = 8. Vertical: -2 - 4 = -6, so 6. d^2 = 64 + 36 = 100. d = **10**.
4. 12^2 + h^2 = 20^2 -> 144 + h^2 = 400 -> h^2 = 256 -> h = **16 feet**.
5. Translation, reflection, rotation, dilation. **Rigid:** translation, reflection, rotation. **Not rigid:** dilation (changes size).
6. PQ = 5, PR = 12. QR: horizontal 5, vertical 12. QR = sqrt(25 + 144) = sqrt(169) = 13. Check: 5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13^2. **Right triangle.** Dilate by 2: P'(0,0), Q'(10,0), R'(0,24). Sides: 10, 24, 26. Check: 10^2 + 24^2 = 100 + 576 = 676 = 26^2. **Also a right triangle.** (Dilation preserves angles, so right angles are preserved.)
7. Reflect over x-axis: (1, -2), (4, -2), (4, -6). Translate (x - 6): (-5, -2), (-2, -2), (-2, -6). Only rigid transformations were used (reflection + translation). **Congruent** (same shape and size) **and similar** (all congruent figures are similar, scale factor 1).
8. Same height: the path is horizontal = 5 miles = 26,400 feet. For different heights (100 and 180 feet): the vertical difference is 80 feet, horizontal = 26,400 feet. d = sqrt(26400^2 + 80^2) = sqrt(696,960,000 + 6,400) = sqrt(696,966,400). This is approximately **26,400.12 feet**, barely longer than 5 miles. The height difference is negligible compared to the horizontal distance.
9. AB: sqrt((5-1)^2 + (4-1)^2) = sqrt(16 + 9) = sqrt(25) = 5. BC: sqrt((2-5)^2 + (8-4)^2) = sqrt(9 + 16) = sqrt(25) = 5. AC: sqrt((2-1)^2 + (8-1)^2) = sqrt(1 + 49) = sqrt(50) = 5*sqrt(2). Perimeter = 5 + 5 + 5*sqrt(2) = **10 + 5*sqrt(2)** (approximately 17.07). Check right triangle: 5^2 + 5^2 = 50 = (5*sqrt(2))^2. **Yes, right triangle** (isosceles right triangle).
10. Rotate 90 CCW: (0,0)->((0,0), (4,0)->((0,4), (4,4)->((-4,4), (0,4)->((-4,0). Dilate by 1.5: (0,0), (0,6), (-6,6), (-6,0). **Similar** to original. Scale factor = **1.5**. (Shape preserved, angles all 90 degrees, but side length changed from 4 to 6.)

### Exit Ticket

1. 9^2 + 12^2 = 81 + 144 = 225. Hypotenuse = sqrt(225) = **15**. Scale factor = 45/15 = **3**. Legs of similar triangle: 9 x 3 = **27** and 12 x 3 = **36**. Check: 27^2 + 36^2 = 729 + 1296 = 2025 = 45^2. Confirmed.
2. Sides of DEF: DE = 3, DF = 4, EF = sqrt(9 + 16) = 5. Sides of D'E'F': D'E' = 3, D'F' = 4, E'F' = sqrt(9 + 16) = 5. Same side lengths. Transformation: (0,0)->(0,0), (3,0)->(0,3), (0,4)->(-4,0). This is a **90-degree counterclockwise rotation** about the origin. Check: (3,0) -> (-0,3) = (0,3). (0,4) -> (-4,0). Confirmed. **Yes, congruent.**

## Teacher Notes

- This is a review and synthesis lesson. The goal is not to introduce new content but to build fluency in applying the right tool to the right problem. Resist the urge to teach new material — instead, guide the student to connect concepts they have already learned.
- If the student struggles with a particular problem type, redirect to the specific lesson for targeted review: Lessons 1-3 for transformations, Lesson 5 for dilations, Lessons 6-7 for congruence/similarity, Lessons 8-9 for Pythagorean Theorem.
- The most common meta-error at this stage is misidentifying the problem type — applying the Pythagorean Theorem when a transformation is needed, or attempting a transformation when proportional reasoning would work. The "classify before you solve" strategy in Direct Instruction Step 1 addresses this.
- The flagpole/shadow problem (Guided Practice #3) is a classic similar triangles application. It connects geometry to measurement and science (sun angles). Students enjoy this because it feels practical.
- The cell tower problem (Tier 2, #8) teaches an important lesson: when one dimension is vastly larger than the others, small differences are negligible. This is a real insight used in physics and engineering.
- Use this lesson to identify which concepts need additional review before the unit assessment. If the student consistently struggles with one topic, plan an extra session on that topic before administering the assessment.
