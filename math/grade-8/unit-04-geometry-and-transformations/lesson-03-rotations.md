# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 4, Lesson 3

## Topic
Rotations

## Learning Objectives
- Define a rotation as a transformation that turns a figure around a fixed point by a specified angle.
- Apply coordinate rules for 90-degree, 180-degree, and 270-degree counterclockwise rotations about the origin.
- Verify that rotations preserve distance, angle measure, and shape (rigid transformation).
- Distinguish between clockwise and counterclockwise rotations.

## Prerequisites
- Plotting and reading ordered pairs in all four quadrants (Grade 6-7)
- Understanding of translations and reflections (Lessons 1-2)
- Familiarity with angle measurement: 90, 180, 270, 360 degrees (Grade 7)
- Computation with positive and negative integers (Grade 7)

## Materials Needed
- Graph paper (at least two sheets per student)
- Ruler or straightedge
- Protractor (for verifying angles)
- Colored pencils (two colors: one for pre-image, one for image)
- Tracing paper or patty paper (highly recommended)
- Index card or half-sheet for Exit Ticket

**Standards:** 8.G.1 — Verify experimentally the properties of rotations, reflections, and translations; 8.G.3 — Describe the effect of dilations, translations, rotations, and reflections on two-dimensional figures using coordinates.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review reflections and activate spatial reasoning.

1. Reflect the point (3, -5) over the x-axis. (3, 5)
2. Reflect the point (3, -5) over the y-axis. (-3, -5)
3. Now do BOTH: reflect (3, -5) over the x-axis, then reflect the result over the y-axis. What do you get?
   - First: (3, 5). Then: (-3, 5).
4. Ask: "What single operation would take (3, -5) to (-3, 5)?" (This previews a 180-degree rotation.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Rotation** — a transformation that turns a figure around a fixed point by a specified angle.
- **Center of rotation** — the fixed point around which the figure turns. In this lesson, the center is always the origin (0, 0).
- **Angle of rotation** — the number of degrees the figure turns.
- **Counterclockwise (CCW)** — the standard direction of rotation in mathematics (opposite of clock hands).
- **Clockwise (CW)** — the direction clock hands move. A 90-degree clockwise rotation is the same as a 270-degree counterclockwise rotation.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce rotations with a physical demonstration.**

Say: "Imagine pushing a revolving door. The door spins around a center point. Every part of the door moves, but the center stays fixed. A rotation works the same way — every point in the figure turns around the center of rotation by the same angle."

Say: "In math, we measure rotations counterclockwise by default. Counterclockwise means the opposite direction of clock hands — from the positive x-axis up toward the positive y-axis."

**Step 2 — The three key rotation rules (counterclockwise about the origin).**

Write on the board:

| Rotation | Rule |
|----------|------|
| 90 degrees CCW | (x, y) -> (-y, x) |
| 180 degrees | (x, y) -> (-x, -y) |
| 270 degrees CCW (or 90 degrees CW) | (x, y) -> (y, -x) |

Say: "These rules might look strange at first. Let us build them one at a time."

**Step 3 — 90-degree rotation.**

Say: "Start with point A(3, 1). We want to rotate it 90 degrees counterclockwise about the origin."

Use tracing paper: "Trace the point and the axes. Put your pencil on the origin and turn the paper 90 degrees counterclockwise (a quarter turn to the left). Where is the point now?"

Result: A'(-1, 3).

Say: "The rule is (x, y) -> (-y, x). The original point was (3, 1). Applying the rule: (-1, 3). It matches!"

Try another: B(4, -2). Apply rule: (-(-2), 4) = (2, 4). B'(2, 4).

**Step 4 — 180-degree rotation.**

Say: "A 180-degree rotation is a half turn. The rule is (x, y) -> (-x, -y). Both coordinates change sign."

Example: C(5, 3) rotated 180 degrees: C'(-5, -3).
Example: D(-2, -4) rotated 180 degrees: D'(2, 4).

Say: "Notice that a 180-degree rotation gives the same result as reflecting over both axes. That connects to the warm-up!"

**Step 5 — 270-degree counterclockwise rotation (= 90-degree clockwise).**

Say: "A 270-degree counterclockwise rotation is the same as a 90-degree clockwise rotation — three-quarter turn one way equals quarter turn the other way. The rule is (x, y) -> (y, -x)."

Example: E(1, 4) rotated 270 degrees CCW: E'(4, -1).

**Step 6 — Rotate an entire figure.**

Triangle PQR: P(1, 1), Q(4, 1), R(2, 3). Rotate 90 degrees CCW.
- P(1, 1) -> P'(-1, 1)
- Q(4, 1) -> Q'(-1, 4)
- R(2, 3) -> R'(-3, 2)

Say: "Plot both triangles. The image has rotated a quarter turn counterclockwise. Same shape, same size — rotation is a rigid transformation."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Rotate the point (5, -3) by 90 degrees CCW about the origin.
- Rule: (x, y) -> (-y, x)
- (-(-3), 5) = **(3, 5)**

**Example B:** Rotate the point (-2, 6) by 180 degrees about the origin.
- Rule: (x, y) -> (-x, -y)
- (-(-2), -6) = **(2, -6)**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Rotating Individual Points**

1. Rotate (4, 2) by 90 degrees CCW.
   - "Apply the rule (x, y) -> (-y, x)."
   - Image: **(-2, 4)**

2. Rotate (4, 2) by 180 degrees.
   - "Apply the rule (x, y) -> (-x, -y)."
   - Image: **(-4, -2)**

3. Rotate (4, 2) by 270 degrees CCW.
   - "Apply the rule (x, y) -> (y, -x)."
   - Image: **(2, -4)**

4. Ask: "What would happen if we rotate (4, 2) by 360 degrees?" (It returns to (4, 2) — a full circle.)

**Activity 2: Rotating a Figure**

5. Triangle with vertices A(0, 0), B(3, 0), C(3, 2). Rotate 90 degrees CCW about the origin.
   - A(0, 0) -> A'(0, 0) — the origin does not move!
   - B(3, 0) -> B'(0, 3)
   - C(3, 2) -> C'(-2, 3)
   - Ask: "Check: Is AB the same length as A'B'?" AB = 3. A'B' goes from (0, 0) to (0, 3), length = 3. Yes, preserved.

6. Same triangle, rotated 180 degrees.
   - A(0, 0) -> A'(0, 0)
   - B(3, 0) -> B'(-3, 0)
   - C(3, 2) -> C'(-3, -2)
   - Ask: "Where is the image relative to the original?" (Opposite side of the origin — flipped upside down and left-right.)

**Facilitator tip:** If the student confuses the 90-degree and 270-degree rules, have them use tracing paper for every problem until the rules become automatic. The physical turning motion cements which direction is which. Also remind them: a 90-degree CCW rotation moves a point in Quadrant I to Quadrant II; a 270-degree CCW rotation moves it to Quadrant IV.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. Rotate (6, 1) by 90 degrees CCW about the origin.
2. Rotate (-3, 5) by 180 degrees about the origin.
3. Rotate (2, -4) by 270 degrees CCW about the origin.
4. Rotate (-1, -3) by 90 degrees CCW about the origin.
5. Triangle with vertices D(1, 0), E(4, 0), F(2, 3). Rotate 180 degrees about the origin. List the image vertices.

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

6. A point P(a, b) is in Quadrant I (both a and b are positive). After a 90-degree CCW rotation, which quadrant is P' in? After 180 degrees? After 270 degrees?
7. A point Q(3, 5) is rotated and its image is Q'(-3, -5). What was the angle of rotation?
8. A point R(2, -1) is rotated 90 degrees CLOCKWISE. What is the image? (Hint: 90 degrees clockwise = 270 degrees counterclockwise.)

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

9. A triangle has vertices at (1, 2), (4, 2), and (3, 5). Rotate the triangle 90 degrees CCW. Then rotate the IMAGE another 90 degrees CCW. Where is the final triangle? What single rotation would give the same result?
10. Explain why a 180-degree rotation produces the same result as reflecting over the x-axis followed by reflecting over the y-axis. Use a specific point to demonstrate.

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Rotate the point (-2, 5) by 90 degrees counterclockwise about the origin. State the image.

**Problem 2:** Rotate the point (4, -1) by 180 degrees about the origin. State the image.

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 correct.**

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. (6, 1) rotated 90 CCW: (-y, x) = **(-1, 6)**
2. (-3, 5) rotated 180: (-x, -y) = **(3, -5)**
3. (2, -4) rotated 270 CCW: (y, -x) = **(-4, -2)**
4. (-1, -3) rotated 90 CCW: (-(-3), -1) = **(3, -1)**
5. D(1, 0) -> D'(-1, 0); E(4, 0) -> E'(-4, 0); F(2, 3) -> F'(-2, -3). Image: **(-1, 0), (-4, 0), (-2, -3)**.
6. Quadrant I: a > 0, b > 0. After 90 CCW: (-b, a) — negative x, positive y = **Quadrant II**. After 180: (-a, -b) = **Quadrant III**. After 270 CCW: (b, -a) = **Quadrant IV**.
7. (3, 5) -> (-3, -5). Both coordinates negated. This is a **180-degree rotation**.
8. 90 CW = 270 CCW. Rule: (y, -x). R(2, -1) -> (-1, -2). Image: **(-1, -2)**.
9. First 90 CCW: (1, 2) -> (-2, 1); (4, 2) -> (-2, 4); (3, 5) -> (-5, 3). Second 90 CCW: (-2, 1) -> (-1, -2); (-2, 4) -> (-4, -2); (-5, 3) -> (-3, -5). Final triangle: **(-1, -2), (-4, -2), (-3, -5)**. This is the same as a single **180-degree rotation** of the original triangle. Check: (1, 2) -> (-1, -2); (4, 2) -> (-4, -2); (3, 5) -> (-3, -5). Confirmed.
10. Start with point (a, b). Reflect over x-axis: (a, -b). Then reflect over y-axis: (-a, -b). A 180-degree rotation gives (-a, -b). Same result. Example: Start with (3, 2). Reflect over x-axis: (3, -2). Reflect over y-axis: (-3, -2). Rotate 180: (-3, -2). **Identical results** because both processes negate both coordinates.

### Exit Ticket

1. (-2, 5) rotated 90 CCW: (-y, x) = (-5, -2). Image: **(-5, -2)**.
2. (4, -1) rotated 180: (-x, -y) = (-4, 1). Image: **(-4, 1)**.

## Teacher Notes

- Rotation rules are the hardest coordinate rules in the transformation sequence. Expect the student to need more time and practice than with translations or reflections. Using tracing paper to physically rotate figures is not optional — it is essential for building intuition.
- The most common errors: (1) forgetting to negate a coordinate (e.g., writing (y, x) instead of (-y, x) for 90 CCW), and (2) confusing 90 CCW with 270 CCW. A helpful pattern: after a 90 CCW rotation, a point in Quadrant I moves to Quadrant II; after 270 CCW, it moves to Quadrant IV. If the student gets a Quadrant IV answer for what should be a 90 CCW rotation, they likely used the wrong rule.
- Clarify the clockwise/counterclockwise convention early. In mathematics, counterclockwise is positive (the default direction). Students see clocks daily and may default to thinking clockwise. Explicitly connect: 90 CW = 270 CCW, and vice versa.
- The connection between 180-degree rotation and reflecting over both axes (explored in the warm-up and Challenge problem 10) is a deep structural insight. It previews Lesson 4 on combining transformations.
- A 360-degree rotation maps every point back to itself — it is the "do-nothing" transformation. This is worth mentioning but does not need extensive practice.
