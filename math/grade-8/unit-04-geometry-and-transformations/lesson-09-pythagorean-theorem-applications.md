# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 4, Lesson 9

## Topic
Pythagorean Theorem Applications

## Learning Objectives
- Use the Pythagorean Theorem to find the distance between two points on the coordinate plane.
- Apply the Pythagorean Theorem to solve real-world problems involving right triangles.
- Use the converse of the Pythagorean Theorem to determine whether a triangle is a right triangle.

## Prerequisites
- The Pythagorean Theorem: a^2 + b^2 = c^2 (Lesson 8)
- Plotting ordered pairs on the coordinate plane (Grade 6-7)
- Computing squares and square roots (Grade 8, Unit 1)

## Materials Needed
- Graph paper
- Ruler or straightedge
- Calculator (recommended for non-perfect-square roots)
- Colored pencils
- Index card or half-sheet for Exit Ticket

**Standards:** 8.G.7 — Apply the Pythagorean Theorem to determine unknown side lengths in right triangles in real-world and mathematical problems; 8.G.8 — Apply the Pythagorean Theorem to find the distance between two points in a coordinate system; 8.G.6 — Explain a proof of the Pythagorean Theorem and its converse.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review the Pythagorean Theorem and prepare for applications.

1. A right triangle has legs 5 and 12. Find the hypotenuse. (13.)
2. A right triangle has hypotenuse 10 and one leg 8. Find the other leg. (6.)
3. Plot the points A(1, 1) and B(4, 5) on graph paper. If you draw a horizontal line from A and a vertical line from B, they form a right angle. What are the lengths of the horizontal and vertical segments? (Horizontal: 3 units. Vertical: 4 units.)
4. Using your answer from Problem 3, what is the straight-line distance from A to B? (3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25. Distance = 5 units.)

Say: "You just used the Pythagorean Theorem to find the distance between two points. Today we will master this technique and apply it to real-world problems."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Distance between two points** — the length of the straight line connecting them. On the coordinate plane, this can be found using the Pythagorean Theorem.
- **Converse of the Pythagorean Theorem** — if a^2 + b^2 = c^2 for the three sides of a triangle (where c is the longest side), then the triangle is a right triangle.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Distance on the coordinate plane.**

Say: "To find the distance between two points on the coordinate plane, we create a right triangle. The two points are connected by the hypotenuse. The horizontal distance is one leg, and the vertical distance is the other leg."

Write the method:
1. Find the horizontal distance: |x2 - x1|
2. Find the vertical distance: |y2 - y1|
3. Use the Pythagorean Theorem: d^2 = (x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2
4. Take the square root: d = sqrt((x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2)

Example: Find the distance between (2, 3) and (7, 15).
- Horizontal: 7 - 2 = 5
- Vertical: 15 - 3 = 12
- d^2 = 5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169
- d = sqrt(169) = **13 units**

Say: "This method works because the horizontal and vertical distances form the legs of a right triangle, and the straight-line distance is the hypotenuse."

**Step 2 — Real-world applications.**

Say: "The Pythagorean Theorem appears everywhere in real life, whenever you have a situation with a right angle."

**Ladder problem:** A 13-foot ladder leans against a wall. The base of the ladder is 5 feet from the wall. How high up the wall does the ladder reach?

Say: "Draw a picture: the wall is vertical, the ground is horizontal, and the ladder is the hypotenuse. The ground, wall, and ladder form a right triangle."
- Hypotenuse (ladder) = 13
- One leg (ground) = 5
- Other leg (wall height) = ?
- 5^2 + h^2 = 13^2
- 25 + h^2 = 169
- h^2 = 144
- h = 12 feet

**Diagonal of a rectangle:** A rectangular TV screen is 40 inches wide and 30 inches tall. What is the diagonal measurement?

Say: "The width and height are legs of a right triangle, and the diagonal is the hypotenuse."
- 40^2 + 30^2 = d^2
- 1600 + 900 = d^2
- 2500 = d^2
- d = 50 inches

**Step 3 — The converse of the Pythagorean Theorem.**

Say: "The Pythagorean Theorem says: if a triangle IS a right triangle, then a^2 + b^2 = c^2. The converse goes the other direction: if a^2 + b^2 = c^2 for the three sides of a triangle, then the triangle IS a right triangle."

Example: A triangle has sides 9, 12, and 15. Is it a right triangle?
- Longest side (potential hypotenuse): 15
- Check: 9^2 + 12^2 = 81 + 144 = 225 = 15^2
- **Yes, it is a right triangle.**

Example: A triangle has sides 7, 10, and 12. Is it a right triangle?
- Longest side: 12
- Check: 7^2 + 10^2 = 49 + 100 = 149. But 12^2 = 144. 149 ≠ 144.
- **No, it is not a right triangle.**

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Find the distance between (-3, 2) and (5, -4).
- Horizontal: 5 - (-3) = 8
- Vertical: -4 - 2 = -6, so |vertical| = 6
- d^2 = 8^2 + 6^2 = 64 + 36 = 100
- d = sqrt(100) = **10 units**

**Example B:** A kite is flying 40 feet directly above a point on the ground. The person holding the string is standing 30 feet away from that point. How long is the string?
- The height (40) and the ground distance (30) are legs. The string is the hypotenuse.
- 40^2 + 30^2 = s^2 -> 1600 + 900 = s^2 -> 2500 = s^2 -> s = **50 feet**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Distance on the Coordinate Plane**

1. Find the distance between (0, 0) and (6, 8).
   - "What is the horizontal distance?" (6) "Vertical?" (8)
   - d^2 = 36 + 64 = 100 -> d = **10**

2. Find the distance between (1, -2) and (4, 2).
   - Horizontal: 4 - 1 = 3. Vertical: 2 - (-2) = 4.
   - d^2 = 9 + 16 = 25 -> d = **5**

3. Find the distance between (-5, 3) and (7, 8).
   - Horizontal: 7 - (-5) = 12. Vertical: 8 - 3 = 5.
   - d^2 = 144 + 25 = 169 -> d = **13**

**Activity 2: Real-World Problems**

4. A baseball diamond is a square with sides of 90 feet. The distance from home plate to second base is the diagonal of the square. Find this distance.
   - The diagonal forms a right triangle with two sides of 90 feet.
   - d^2 = 90^2 + 90^2 = 8100 + 8100 = 16200
   - d = sqrt(16200) = 90*sqrt(2) = approximately **127.3 feet**

5. A triangle has sides 11, 60, and 61. Is it a right triangle?
   - 11^2 + 60^2 = 121 + 3600 = 3721 = 61^2. **Yes, right triangle.**

**Facilitator tip:** For distance problems, encourage the student to sketch the two points on graph paper and draw the right triangle. Seeing the horizontal and vertical legs makes the problem concrete. For real-world problems, always draw a diagram and label which side is the hypotenuse.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. Find the distance between (3, 1) and (7, 4).
2. Find the distance between (-2, -3) and (4, 5).
3. A 10-foot ladder leans against a wall. The base is 6 feet from the wall. How high up the wall does the ladder reach?
4. A rectangular garden is 24 feet long and 10 feet wide. What is the length of the diagonal path across the garden?
5. A triangle has sides 8, 15, and 17. Is it a right triangle?

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

6. Find the distance between (-1, 5) and (2, -1). Leave your answer in simplified square root form.
7. A ship sails 12 miles north and then 16 miles east. How far is the ship from its starting point in a straight line?
8. The sides of a triangle are 20, 21, and 29. Is it a right triangle? What about 20, 21, and 28?

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

9. Three points on the coordinate plane are A(0, 0), B(3, 4), and C(8, 0). Find the perimeter of triangle ABC. (Hint: Find each side length using the Pythagorean Theorem or direct distance.)
10. A rectangular box is 3 feet long, 4 feet wide, and 12 feet tall. A spider at one corner wants to reach the opposite corner by crawling along the inside of the box. What is the shortest path along the box surfaces? (Hint: Unfold the box into a flat net and use the Pythagorean Theorem.)

**Problem 1:** Find the distance between the points (2, -1) and (10, 5).

**Problem 2:** A right triangle has one leg of 15 and a hypotenuse of 17. Find the other leg. Then verify your answer using the Pythagorean Theorem.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. Horizontal: 7 - 3 = 4. Vertical: 4 - 1 = 3. d^2 = 16 + 9 = 25. d = **5**.
2. Horizontal: 4 - (-2) = 6. Vertical: 5 - (-3) = 8. d^2 = 36 + 64 = 100. d = **10**.
3. 6^2 + h^2 = 10^2 -> 36 + h^2 = 100 -> h^2 = 64 -> h = **8 feet**.
4. 24^2 + 10^2 = d^2 -> 576 + 100 = d^2 -> 676 = d^2 -> d = **26 feet**.
5. 8^2 + 15^2 = 64 + 225 = 289 = 17^2. **Yes, right triangle.**
6. Horizontal: 2 - (-1) = 3. Vertical: -1 - 5 = -6, so 6. d^2 = 9 + 36 = 45. d = sqrt(45) = **3*sqrt(5)** (approximately 6.71).
7. 12^2 + 16^2 = s^2 -> 144 + 256 = 400 -> s = **20 miles**.
8. For 20, 21, 29: 20^2 + 21^2 = 400 + 441 = 841 = 29^2. **Yes, right triangle.** For 20, 21, 28: 20^2 + 21^2 = 841, but 28^2 = 784. 841 ≠ 784. **Not a right triangle.**
9. AB: horizontal 3, vertical 4. AB = sqrt(9 + 16) = 5. BC: horizontal 8 - 3 = 5, vertical 0 - 4 = -4. BC = sqrt(25 + 16) = sqrt(41). AC: from (0,0) to (8,0) = 8. Perimeter = 5 + sqrt(41) + 8 = **13 + sqrt(41)** (approximately 19.4).
10. Unfold the box: place the 3 x 12 face flat and unfold the 4-wide face adjacent. The spider path becomes a straight line on the flat net from one corner to the diagonally opposite corner. The flat dimensions are (3 + 4) = 7 by 12, or (3 + 12) = 15 by 4, or (4 + 12) = 16 by 3. Try each: 7^2 + 12^2 = 49 + 144 = 193, sqrt(193) = 13.89. 15^2 + 4^2 = 225 + 16 = 241, sqrt(241) = 15.52. 16^2 + 3^2 = 256 + 9 = 265, sqrt(265) = 16.28. The shortest is **sqrt(193) ≈ 13.89 feet** (crawling across the 3-wide face and up the 12-tall face, for a total flat dimension of 7 by 12).

### Exit Ticket

1. Horizontal: 10 - 2 = 8. Vertical: 5 - (-1) = 6. d^2 = 64 + 36 = 100. d = **10 units**.
2. 15^2 + b^2 = 17^2 -> 225 + b^2 = 289 -> b^2 = 64 -> b = **8**. Check: 15^2 + 8^2 = 225 + 64 = 289 = 17^2. Confirmed.

## Teacher Notes

- This lesson is application-heavy. The student should already be comfortable with the mechanics of a^2 + b^2 = c^2 from Lesson 8. Today's focus is on WHEN and WHERE to use it.
- For distance on the coordinate plane, the most common error is computing horizontal and vertical distances incorrectly — especially when one or both coordinates are negative. Remind students: horizontal distance = |x2 - x1|, vertical distance = |y2 - y1|. The absolute value (or squaring) eliminates sign issues.
- For real-world problems, always insist on a diagram before any computation. The diagram makes it clear which sides are legs and which is the hypotenuse. Without a diagram, students frequently assign the wrong value to the wrong variable.
- The converse of the Pythagorean Theorem is a powerful tool that students sometimes overlook. It allows us to test whether any triangle is a right triangle, which has applications in construction (checking if corners are square), navigation, and more.
- The baseball diamond problem is a classic and highly engaging for students who enjoy sports. The exact answer (90*sqrt(2)) introduces irrational numbers in a natural context.
- The spider-on-a-box problem (Tier 3) is a famous puzzle that requires creative thinking — "unfolding" a 3D surface into 2D. It is challenging but rewarding. Do not expect most students to solve it independently; use it as a discussion piece or extension activity.
