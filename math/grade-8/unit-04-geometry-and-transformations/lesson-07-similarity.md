# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 4, Lesson 7

## Topic
Similarity

## Learning Objectives
- Define similar figures as figures that have the same shape but possibly different sizes.
- Explain that two figures are similar if one can be mapped onto the other by a sequence of rigid transformations and/or dilations.
- Identify that similar figures have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.
- Compute the scale factor between two similar figures and use it to find missing measurements.

## Prerequisites
- Dilations and the rule (x, y) -> (kx, ky) (Lesson 5)
- Congruence and rigid transformations (Lesson 6)
- Proportional reasoning: setting up and solving proportions (Grade 7)

## Materials Needed
- Graph paper
- Ruler or straightedge
- Protractor
- Colored pencils (two colors)
- Calculator (optional)
- Index card or half-sheet for Exit Ticket

**Standards:** 8.G.4 — Understand that a two-dimensional figure is similar to another if the second can be obtained from the first by a sequence of rotations, reflections, translations, and dilations; given two similar two-dimensional figures, describe a sequence that exhibits the similarity between them.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review dilations and connect to proportional thinking.

1. Dilate the point (4, 6) by scale factor k = 2. Result: (8, 12).
2. Dilate the point (4, 6) by scale factor k = 1/2. Result: (2, 3).
3. A triangle has sides of length 3, 4, and 5. After a dilation with k = 3, what are the new side lengths? (9, 12, 15.)
4. Do the original and dilated triangles have the same angles? (Yes — dilation preserves angles.)

Say: "The original and dilated triangles have the same shape but different sizes. Today we learn the word for this relationship: similarity."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Similar** — two figures are similar if they have the same shape but not necessarily the same size. Written with the symbol ~.
- **Scale factor** — the ratio of corresponding side lengths between two similar figures.
- **Corresponding angles** — angles that occupy the same position in two similar figures. They are always equal.
- **Corresponding sides** — sides that occupy the same position in two similar figures. They are always proportional.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Define similarity through transformations.**

Say: "In Lesson 6, we learned that two figures are congruent if you can map one onto the other using only rigid transformations — translations, reflections, and rotations. Today we expand the toolbox: two figures are SIMILAR if you can map one onto the other using rigid transformations AND dilations."

Write on the board: "Similar = same shape, possibly different size = connected by rigid transformations + dilations."

Say: "Every congruent pair of figures is also similar — think of it as similarity with scale factor 1. But similar figures do not have to be congruent. A small triangle and a big triangle with the same angles are similar but not congruent."

**Step 2 — Properties of similar figures.**

Say: "Similar figures have two key properties:"

Write:
1. Corresponding angles are equal.
2. Corresponding sides are proportional (all in the same ratio).

Say: "If triangle ABC is similar to triangle DEF with a scale factor of 2, then every side of DEF is exactly 2 times the corresponding side of ABC. And every angle of DEF equals the corresponding angle of ABC."

**Step 3 — Work through an example.**

Triangle ABC: A(0, 0), B(3, 0), C(0, 4). Side lengths: AB = 3, AC = 4, BC = 5.
Triangle DEF: D(0, 0), E(6, 0), F(0, 8). Side lengths: DE = 6, DF = 8, EF = 10.

Say: "Are these similar? Check the ratios of corresponding sides."
- DE/AB = 6/3 = 2
- DF/AC = 8/4 = 2
- EF/BC = 10/5 = 2

Say: "All ratios are equal to 2. The scale factor is 2. Now check angles: triangle ABC is a right triangle (right angle at A). Triangle DEF is also a right triangle (right angle at D). The corresponding angles match."

Say: "The transformation: this is a dilation with k = 2 centered at the origin. D = 2A, E = 2B, F = 2C."

**Step 4 — Finding missing measurements using similarity.**

Say: "If we know two figures are similar and we know the scale factor, we can find missing side lengths."

Example: Triangle PQR ~ Triangle XYZ. PQ = 5, QR = 8, PR = 6. XY = 15. Find YZ and XZ.
- Scale factor: XY/PQ = 15/5 = 3.
- YZ = QR x 3 = 8 x 3 = 24.
- XZ = PR x 3 = 6 x 3 = 18.

**Step 5 — Similarity vs. congruence.**

Say: "Let us be crystal clear about the difference."

| | Congruent | Similar |
|---|---|---|
| Same shape? | Yes | Yes |
| Same size? | Yes | Not necessarily |
| Corresponding angles? | Equal | Equal |
| Corresponding sides? | Equal | Proportional |
| Transformations used? | Rigid only | Rigid + dilations |

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Triangle ABC has sides 4, 5, 7. Triangle DEF has sides 8, 10, 14. Are they similar?
- Ratios: 8/4 = 2, 10/5 = 2, 14/7 = 2. All equal.
- **Yes, they are similar with scale factor 2.**

**Example B:** Triangle PQR ~ Triangle STU. PQ = 6, QR = 9, PR = 12. ST = 4. Find TU and SU.
- Scale factor: ST/PQ = 4/6 = 2/3.
- TU = QR x (2/3) = 9 x (2/3) = 6.
- SU = PR x (2/3) = 12 x (2/3) = 8.
- **TU = 6, SU = 8.**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Identifying Similar Figures**

1. Triangle A has sides 3, 4, 5. Triangle B has sides 6, 8, 10. Similar?
   - Ratios: 6/3 = 2, 8/4 = 2, 10/5 = 2. **Yes, similar. Scale factor = 2.**

2. Triangle A has sides 3, 4, 5. Triangle B has sides 5, 6, 7. Similar?
   - Ratios: 5/3, 6/4 = 1.5, 7/5 = 1.4. Not all equal.
   - **Not similar.** The ratios are not consistent.

3. Rectangle A is 4 x 6. Rectangle B is 6 x 9. Similar?
   - Ratios: 6/4 = 1.5, 9/6 = 1.5. **Yes, similar. Scale factor = 1.5.**

4. Rectangle A is 4 x 6. Rectangle B is 4 x 9. Similar?
   - Ratios: 4/4 = 1, 9/6 = 1.5. Not equal. **Not similar.**

**Activity 2: Finding Missing Sides**

5. Triangle ABC ~ Triangle DEF. AB = 5, BC = 8, CA = 10. DE = 15. Find EF and FD.
   - "What is the scale factor?" (DE/AB = 15/5 = 3.)
   - EF = 8 x 3 = **24**. FD = 10 x 3 = **30**.

6. Two similar triangles have a scale factor of 1/2. The larger triangle has sides 12, 16, 20. What are the sides of the smaller triangle?
   - 12 x 1/2 = **6**, 16 x 1/2 = **8**, 20 x 1/2 = **10**.

**Facilitator tip:** If the student struggles with finding the scale factor, remind them: "Pick any pair of corresponding sides and divide. The ratio you get should work for ALL pairs. If it does not work for all pairs, the figures are not similar."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. Triangle A has sides 5, 12, 13. Triangle B has sides 10, 24, 26. Are they similar? If so, state the scale factor.
2. Triangle PQR ~ Triangle XYZ. PQ = 7, QR = 10, PR = 8. XY = 21. Find YZ and XZ.
3. A rectangle is 3 cm by 5 cm. A similar rectangle has a short side of 9 cm. What is the long side?
4. True or false: All squares are similar to each other. Explain.
5. True or false: All rectangles are similar to each other. Explain.

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

6. Triangle ABC: A(0, 0), B(4, 0), C(0, 3). Triangle DEF: D(0, 0), E(8, 0), F(0, 6). Describe a transformation (or sequence) that maps ABC to DEF. Are they similar? Congruent?
7. Two similar triangles have corresponding sides in the ratio 3:5. The perimeter of the smaller triangle is 36 cm. What is the perimeter of the larger triangle?
8. In similar triangles ABC and DEF, angle A = 40 degrees and angle B = 75 degrees. What is angle E in triangle DEF?

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

9. Two similar rectangles have areas of 18 cm^2 and 72 cm^2. What is the scale factor of their corresponding sides?
10. A figure on the coordinate plane has vertices (1, 1), (3, 1), (3, 4), (1, 4). After a dilation with k = 2 centered at the origin followed by a translation (x + 1, y - 3), what are the image vertices? Is the image similar to the original? Congruent?

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Triangle ABC has sides 6, 8, 10. Triangle DEF has sides 9, 12, 15. Are these triangles similar? If so, what is the scale factor?

**Problem 2:** Two similar triangles have a scale factor of 4. One side of the smaller triangle is 5 cm. What is the length of the corresponding side of the larger triangle?

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 correct.**

---

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. Ratios: 10/5 = 2, 24/12 = 2, 26/13 = 2. **Yes, similar. Scale factor = 2.**
2. Scale factor = 21/7 = 3. YZ = 10 x 3 = **30**. XZ = 8 x 3 = **24**.
3. Scale factor = 9/3 = 3. Long side = 5 x 3 = **15 cm**.
4. **True.** All squares have four right angles and four equal sides. Any square can be mapped to any other square by a dilation (and possibly a rigid transformation). The scale factor equals the ratio of side lengths.
5. **False.** Not all rectangles are similar. A 2 x 5 rectangle and a 2 x 10 rectangle have different proportions (ratios 2:5 vs. 2:10), so they are not similar.
6. Dilation with k = 2: A(0,0)->D(0,0), B(4,0)->E(8,0), C(0,3)->F(0,6). **Similar with scale factor 2. Not congruent** (different sizes).
7. If sides are in ratio 3:5, perimeters are also in ratio 3:5. Larger perimeter = 36 x (5/3) = **60 cm**.
8. In similar triangles, corresponding angles are equal. Angle A corresponds to angle D, angle B corresponds to angle E. So **angle E = 75 degrees**.
9. Area ratio = 72/18 = 4. Area scales by k^2, so k^2 = 4, k = **2**.
10. Dilate by k = 2: (2, 2), (6, 2), (6, 8), (2, 8). Translate (x + 1, y - 3): (3, -1), (7, -1), (7, 5), (3, 5). **Similar** (same shape, different size because of the dilation). **Not congruent** (scale factor is 2, not 1).

### Exit Ticket

1. Ratios: 9/6 = 1.5, 12/8 = 1.5, 15/10 = 1.5. **Yes, similar. Scale factor = 3/2 (or 1.5).**
2. Corresponding side = 5 x 4 = **20 cm**.

## Teacher Notes

- The key conceptual shift in this lesson is expanding from congruence (same shape, same size) to similarity (same shape, possibly different size). Use the Venn diagram relationship: all congruent figures are similar, but not all similar figures are congruent.
- Students often confuse "same shape" with "same type of shape." All rectangles are the same type of shape, but not all rectangles are similar — a 1 x 10 rectangle has a very different shape from a 5 x 6 rectangle. "Same shape" in the mathematical sense means equal angles AND proportional sides.
- The question "Are all squares similar?" (yes) vs. "Are all rectangles similar?" (no) is a powerful distinction. Use it to test understanding.
- Proportional reasoning from Grade 7 is essential here. If the student struggles with computing scale factors or setting up proportions, this is a sign to revisit those skills.
- Connect to real life: maps and scale models, enlarging or reducing photographs, architectural blueprints, model cars and airplanes. In each case, the shape is preserved but the size differs — that is similarity.
- The relationship between scale factor and area (area scales by k^2) appears in the challenge problems. This is not a required Grade 8 standard but is a natural extension for strong students.
