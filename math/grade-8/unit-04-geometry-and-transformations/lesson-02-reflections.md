# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 4, Lesson 2

## Topic
Reflections

## Learning Objectives
- Define a reflection as a transformation that flips a figure over a line of reflection, creating a mirror image.
- Apply coordinate rules for reflecting over the x-axis: (x, y) -> (x, -y).
- Apply coordinate rules for reflecting over the y-axis: (x, y) -> (-x, y).
- Verify that reflections preserve distance and angle measure but reverse orientation.

## Prerequisites
- Plotting and reading ordered pairs in all four quadrants (Grade 6-7)
- Understanding of translations and prime notation (Lesson 1)
- Computation with positive and negative integers (Grade 7)

## Materials Needed
- Graph paper (at least two sheets per student)
- Ruler or straightedge
- Colored pencils (two colors: one for pre-image, one for image)
- Tracing paper or patty paper (recommended for physical demonstration)
- Index card or half-sheet for Exit Ticket

**Standards:** 8.G.1 — Verify experimentally the properties of rotations, reflections, and translations; 8.G.3 — Describe the effect of dilations, translations, rotations, and reflections on two-dimensional figures using coordinates.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review translations from Lesson 1 and activate prior knowledge of symmetry.

1. Translate the point (-1, 4) using the rule (x, y) -> (x + 3, y - 5). What is the image? (2, -1)
2. Look at this pair of points: (3, 2) and (3, -2). What do you notice about their coordinates? (Same x, opposite y.) Plot them. What line is exactly halfway between them? (The x-axis.)
3. Now look at (4, 1) and (-4, 1). Same y, opposite x. What line is exactly halfway between them? (The y-axis.)

Say: "Today we will study a transformation that creates these mirror-image pairs."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Reflection** — a transformation that flips a figure over a line, creating a mirror image.
- **Line of reflection** — the line over which a figure is reflected; every point and its image are the same distance from this line.
- **Orientation** — the order in which vertices are listed (clockwise or counterclockwise). Reflections reverse orientation.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce reflections with a physical analogy.**

Say: "Think of a mirror sitting on a table. When you hold an object in front of the mirror, you see a mirror image — it is the same shape and size, but it is flipped. If you raise your right hand, the mirror image raises its left hand. A reflection in math works the same way: it flips a figure over a line, like folding a piece of paper along a crease."

**Step 2 — Reflection over the x-axis.**

Write on the board: "Reflection over the x-axis: (x, y) -> (x, -y)"

Say: "When we reflect over the x-axis, the x-coordinate stays the same but the y-coordinate changes sign. A point above the x-axis moves to the same position below the x-axis, and vice versa."

Example: Reflect A(2, 5) over the x-axis.
- A'(2, -5)
- "The point was 5 units above the x-axis. Its image is 5 units below the x-axis. Both are at x = 2."

Example: Reflect B(-3, -1) over the x-axis.
- B'(-3, 1)
- "The point was 1 unit below the x-axis. Its image is 1 unit above."

**Step 3 — Reflection over the y-axis.**

Write on the board: "Reflection over the y-axis: (x, y) -> (-x, y)"

Say: "When we reflect over the y-axis, the y-coordinate stays the same but the x-coordinate changes sign. A point to the right of the y-axis moves to the same position on the left, and vice versa."

Example: Reflect C(4, 3) over the y-axis.
- C'(-4, 3)

Example: Reflect D(-2, -6) over the y-axis.
- D'(2, -6)

**Step 4 — Reflect an entire figure.**

Say: "Let us reflect triangle PQR over the y-axis."

Triangle PQR: P(1, 1), Q(4, 1), R(3, 4).
- P'(-1, 1), Q'(-4, 1), R'(-3, 4)

Say: "Plot both triangles. Notice that the image is a mirror image — it looks like you flipped the original triangle across the y-axis. The shape and size are identical, but the triangle is now facing the opposite direction."

**Step 5 — Verify preservation and note orientation change.**

Say: "Check the side lengths. PQ has length 3 (from x = 1 to x = 4). P'Q' has length 3 (from x = -4 to x = -1). Same length — distance is preserved. But notice: if the original vertices go counterclockwise (P, Q, R), the image vertices go clockwise (P', Q', R'). Reflections reverse orientation. This is different from translations, which preserve orientation."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Reflect the point (6, -2) over the x-axis.
- Rule: (x, y) -> (x, -y)
- (6, -2) -> (6, -(-2)) = **(6, 2)**

**Example B:** Reflect triangle with vertices A(-1, 2), B(3, 2), C(1, 5) over the y-axis.
- A(-1, 2) -> A'(1, 2)
- B(3, 2) -> B'(-3, 2)
- C(1, 5) -> C'(-1, 5)

Check: AB has length 3 - (-1) = 4. A'B' has length 1 - (-3) = 4. Preserved.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity 1: Reflecting Points**

1. Reflect (5, 3) over the x-axis.
   - "Which coordinate changes?" (The y-coordinate changes sign.)
   - Image: **(5, -3)**

2. Reflect (-4, -7) over the y-axis.
   - "Which coordinate changes?" (The x-coordinate changes sign.)
   - Image: **(4, -7)**

3. Reflect (0, 6) over the x-axis.
   - Image: **(0, -6)**
   - Ask: "What if we reflect (0, 6) over the y-axis instead?" (0, 6) — the point is on the y-axis, so it does not move!

4. A point P(3, -2) is reflected to P'(3, 2). Over which axis was it reflected?
   - "The x-coordinate stayed the same, but the y-coordinate changed sign. It was reflected over the **x-axis**."

**Activity 2: Reflecting Figures**

5. Plot quadrilateral ABCD: A(1, 1), B(4, 1), C(5, 3), D(2, 3). Reflect over the x-axis. Plot and label A'B'C'D'.
   - A'(1, -1), B'(4, -1), C'(5, -3), D'(2, -3)
   - Ask: "Is the image the same shape and size as the original?" (Yes.) "Did the orientation change?" (Yes — the original goes counterclockwise, the image goes clockwise.)

6. Reflect the same quadrilateral ABCD over the y-axis instead.
   - A'(-1, 1), B'(-4, 1), C'(-5, 3), D'(-2, 3)
   - Ask: "Where is the image relative to the original?" (On the opposite side of the y-axis, like a mirror image.)

**Facilitator tip:** If the student has difficulty remembering which coordinate changes, use this mnemonic: "Reflect over the x-axis? The x stays. Reflect over the y-axis? The y stays." The coordinate matching the axis name is the one that does NOT change.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

1. Reflect (7, 2) over the x-axis.
2. Reflect (-3, 5) over the y-axis.
3. Reflect (-1, -4) over the x-axis.
4. Reflect (0, -8) over the y-axis.
5. Triangle DEF has vertices D(2, 0), E(5, 0), F(4, 3). Reflect over the x-axis. List the image vertices and plot both triangles.

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

6. A point Q is reflected over the x-axis to get Q'(-5, 3). What are the coordinates of Q?
7. Triangle GHI has vertices G(1, 2), H(3, 5), I(5, 1). Reflect over the y-axis. Then check: is side GH the same length as side G'H'?
8. A figure has a vertex at (a, b). After reflection over the x-axis, where is this vertex? After reflection over the y-axis instead, where is it? What if you reflect over the x-axis AND THEN the y-axis?

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

9. Describe the coordinate rule for reflecting over the line y = x. (Hint: Try reflecting the points (1, 3), (4, 2), and (0, 5) over the line y = x and look for a pattern.)
10. A point is reflected over the x-axis and then reflected over the x-axis again. Where does it end up? Explain why. What about reflecting over the y-axis twice?

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Reflect the point (-4, 3) over the y-axis. State the image coordinates.

**Problem 2:** Triangle ABC has vertices A(1, 2), B(4, 2), C(3, 5). It is reflected over the x-axis. What are the coordinates of A', B', and C'?

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 correct (all coordinates must be accurate).**

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. (7, 2) over x-axis: **(7, -2)**
2. (-3, 5) over y-axis: **(3, 5)**
3. (-1, -4) over x-axis: **(-1, 4)**
4. (0, -8) over y-axis: **(0, -8)** — the point is on the y-axis, so it stays.
5. D'(2, 0), E'(5, 0), F'(4, -3). Note: D and E are on the x-axis, so they stay in place.
6. Q' is (-5, 3), which is the reflection of Q over the x-axis. To reverse: change the sign of y. **Q = (-5, -3)**.
7. G'(-1, 2), H'(-3, 5), I'(-5, 1). GH: distance from (1, 2) to (3, 5). Using distance formula or counting: horizontal change = 2, vertical change = 3. G'H': distance from (-1, 2) to (-3, 5). Horizontal change = 2, vertical change = 3. **Same length.** (Length = sqrt(4 + 9) = sqrt(13).)
8. Reflection over x-axis: **(a, -b)**. Reflection over y-axis: **(-a, b)**. Reflection over x-axis then y-axis: first (a, b) -> (a, -b), then (a, -b) -> (-a, -b). Result: **(-a, -b)**.
9. Reflecting over y = x swaps the coordinates: **(x, y) -> (y, x)**. Check: (1, 3) -> (3, 1), (4, 2) -> (2, 4), (0, 5) -> (5, 0). Each image point is the same distance from the line y = x as the original, on the opposite side.
10. Reflecting over the x-axis twice: (x, y) -> (x, -y) -> (x, -(-y)) = (x, y). **The point returns to its original position.** Same for the y-axis: (x, y) -> (-x, y) -> (-(-x), y) = (x, y). Two reflections over the same line always return to the start.

### Exit Ticket

1. (-4, 3) over y-axis: change the sign of x. **(4, 3)**
2. A(1, 2) -> A'(1, -2); B(4, 2) -> B'(4, -2); C(3, 5) -> C'(3, -5). **A'(1, -2), B'(4, -2), C'(3, -5)**.

## Teacher Notes

- The most common error is changing the wrong coordinate: reflecting over the x-axis but changing x instead of y. The mnemonic "the axis name tells you what STAYS" is simple and effective.
- Points on the line of reflection do not move. This is worth emphasizing — students are often surprised that D(2, 0) stays at D'(2, 0) when reflected over the x-axis. A point on the mirror does not move in the mirror.
- Orientation reversal is a key conceptual point. Have the student label vertices in order and check whether the order reverses after reflection. This is the defining difference between reflections and translations.
- Tracing paper is especially valuable here. Have the student trace the figure, fold the tracing paper along the line of reflection, and see where the figure lands. This physical experience is more memorable than any rule.
- The Challenge problem about reflecting over y = x is a preview — this transformation rule is used extensively in high school. If the student discovers the rule (x, y) -> (y, x), praise the discovery and file it away for later reference.
- Connect to real life: mirrors, symmetry in nature (butterfly wings, human faces), and reflections in water.
