# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 1, Lesson 4

## Topic
Square Roots

## Learning Objectives
- Evaluate square roots of perfect squares through 15^2 = 225.
- Simplify square roots of non-perfect squares by factoring out perfect square factors (e.g., sqrt(50) = 5*sqrt(2)).
- Estimate the value of non-perfect square roots using knowledge of perfect squares.

## Prerequisites
- Knowledge of perfect squares from 1^2 through 12^2 (Lesson 3 Warm-Up)
- Ability to find factor pairs of a number
- Understanding of rational and irrational numbers (Lessons 1-2)
- Successive approximation of square roots (Lesson 3)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- List of perfect squares from 1^2 to 15^2 (posted or handout).
- Graph paper (for area models).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 8.EE.2 — Use square root and cube root symbols to represent solutions to equations of the form x^2 = p and x^3 = p, where p is a positive rational number. Evaluate square roots of small perfect squares and cube roots of small perfect cubes. Know that sqrt(2) is irrational.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Build automaticity with perfect squares and connect square roots to area.

1. Rapid recall: What is sqrt(64)? sqrt(121)? sqrt(144)? sqrt(196)? (8, 11, 12, 14)
2. Draw a square with area 49 square units. Ask: "What is the side length?" (7 units, because sqrt(49) = 7)
3. Ask: "If a square has area 50 square units, can the side length be a whole number?" (No, because 50 is not a perfect square. The side length is sqrt(50), which is irrational.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Square root (sqrt)** — a value that, when multiplied by itself, produces the given number. sqrt(25) = 5 because 5 x 5 = 25.
- **Perfect square** — the square of a whole number: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, 169, 196, 225.
- **Radical sign** — the symbol sqrt( ) used to denote a square root.
- **Radicand** — the number under the radical sign. In sqrt(50), the radicand is 50.
- **Simplified radical form** — a square root expression where no perfect square factor (other than 1) remains under the radical.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Extend the perfect squares table.**
Write the complete list on the board:

| n | n^2 |
|---|-----|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 4 |
| 3 | 9 |
| 4 | 16 |
| 5 | 25 |
| 6 | 36 |
| 7 | 49 |
| 8 | 64 |
| 9 | 81 |
| 10 | 100 |
| 11 | 121 |
| 12 | 144 |
| 13 | 169 |
| 14 | 196 |
| 15 | 225 |

Say: "You should memorize these. They are as important in algebra as times tables were in elementary school."

**Step 2 — Introduce simplifying square roots.**
Say: "When the radicand is NOT a perfect square, we can often simplify by pulling out the largest perfect square factor."

Write the key property on the board:
sqrt(a x b) = sqrt(a) x sqrt(b), as long as a and b are non-negative.

Example: sqrt(50).
- Factor 50: 50 = 25 x 2.
- 25 is a perfect square!
- sqrt(50) = sqrt(25 x 2) = sqrt(25) x sqrt(2) = 5 x sqrt(2) = 5*sqrt(2).

**Step 3 — Show the process step by step.**
To simplify sqrt(N):
1. Find the largest perfect square that divides N.
2. Rewrite N as (perfect square) x (remaining factor).
3. Take the square root of the perfect square and bring it outside the radical.
4. Leave the remaining factor under the radical.

Demonstrate with sqrt(72):
1. Perfect square factors of 72: 4 (72 = 4 x 18), 9 (72 = 9 x 8), 36 (72 = 36 x 2). The largest is 36.
2. sqrt(72) = sqrt(36 x 2) = sqrt(36) x sqrt(2) = 6*sqrt(2).

Note: If you use a smaller perfect square factor, you still get the right answer but need additional steps. sqrt(72) = sqrt(4 x 18) = 2*sqrt(18) = 2*sqrt(9 x 2) = 2 x 3 x sqrt(2) = 6*sqrt(2). Same result, more work.

**Step 4 — Connect back to estimation.**
Say: "We can check our simplification. sqrt(72) = 6*sqrt(2). We know sqrt(2) is approximately 1.414. So 6 x 1.414 = 8.485. Let's verify: 8.485^2 is approximately 72. It checks out."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Simplifying sqrt(18)**
Find the largest perfect square factor of 18.
- 18 = 9 x 2, and 9 is a perfect square.
- sqrt(18) = sqrt(9 x 2) = sqrt(9) x sqrt(2) = 3*sqrt(2).
- Check: 3 x 1.414 = 4.243. And 4.243^2 is approximately 18. Correct.

**Example B — Simplifying sqrt(200)**
Find the largest perfect square factor of 200.
- 200 = 100 x 2, and 100 is a perfect square.
- sqrt(200) = sqrt(100 x 2) = sqrt(100) x sqrt(2) = 10*sqrt(2).
- Check: 10 x 1.414 = 14.14. And 14.14^2 is approximately 200. Correct.

**Example C — Simplifying sqrt(48)**
Find the largest perfect square factor of 48.
- 48 = 16 x 3, and 16 is a perfect square.
- sqrt(48) = sqrt(16 x 3) = sqrt(16) x sqrt(3) = 4*sqrt(3).
- Check: 4 x 1.732 = 6.928. And 6.928^2 is approximately 48. Correct.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. The student attempts each problem first, then discusses.

1. Evaluate: sqrt(169).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Which number squared gives 169? Check the perfect squares table."

2. Simplify: sqrt(32).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What is the largest perfect square that divides 32? Is it 4 or 16?"

3. Simplify: sqrt(75).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Factor 75. Does 25 divide into 75 evenly?"

4. Simplify: sqrt(98).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "98 = 49 x 2. Is 49 a perfect square?"

5. A square tile has an area of 128 square centimeters. Express the side length in simplified radical form.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Side = sqrt(128). What is the largest perfect square factor of 128?"

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** Simplify sqrt(80).
**Problem 2:** Evaluate sqrt(225).

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the perfect squares list. If the student does not recognize perfect square factors, practice by listing factor pairs of the radicand and circling any that are perfect squares.
- Provide a step-by-step checklist: (1) List factor pairs, (2) Identify perfect square factors, (3) Choose the largest, (4) Apply sqrt(a x b) = sqrt(a) x sqrt(b).
- Practice three more simplification problems together before moving to Lesson 5.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Challenge: Simplify sqrt(500), sqrt(363), and sqrt(1000).
- Preview: "Can we do something similar with cube roots? That is what Lesson 5 is about."

## Extension (Optional)
- **Area Model Investigation:** Draw rectangles on graph paper whose area is a given number (e.g., 12, 18, 27). For each, find dimensions that are both whole numbers. Then find the side of a square with that area. How does factoring relate to simplifying the square root?
- **Spiral Back:** "We simplified sqrt(72) to 6*sqrt(2). Suppose you need to add sqrt(72) + sqrt(8). Can you simplify both first and then combine?" (6*sqrt(2) + 2*sqrt(2) = 8*sqrt(2) — a preview of combining like radicals.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Not finding the LARGEST perfect square factor.** A student may write sqrt(72) = sqrt(4 x 18) = 2*sqrt(18) and stop, not realizing 18 can be simplified further. Encourage finding the largest perfect square factor from the start, or continuing until no perfect squares remain under the radical.
- **Confusing sqrt(a x b) with sqrt(a + b).** A student may think sqrt(9 + 16) = sqrt(9) + sqrt(16) = 3 + 4 = 7. But sqrt(25) = 5, not 7. Reinforce: the product rule (sqrt(a x b) = sqrt(a) x sqrt(b)) works for multiplication, NOT addition.
- **Forgetting perfect squares above 100.** Students may not recognize 121, 144, 169, or 196 as perfect squares. Have the table visible throughout the lesson.

### Pacing Advice
- Spend most of the Direct Instruction time on Step 2 (the simplification process). Steps 1 and 4 can be brief.
- If the student picks up simplification quickly, use extra time for more Guided Practice with harder radicands (e.g., 150, 245).
- If the student struggles with identifying factors, pause and do a brief review of factor pairs. Being able to factor is critical for this skill.
- Consider splitting this lesson into two sessions: one for perfect square evaluation and one for simplification.
