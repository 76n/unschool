# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 1, Lesson 5

## Topic
Cube Roots

## Learning Objectives
- Evaluate cube roots of perfect cubes through 5^3 = 125 and common perfect cubes (including negative values).
- Understand the connection between cube roots and volume.
- Simplify cube roots by factoring out perfect cube factors (e.g., cbrt(40) = 2*cbrt(5)).

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of square roots and the simplification process (Lesson 4)
- Knowledge of volume of a cube (V = s^3)
- Ability to find factor pairs and factor triples
- Familiarity with exponent notation (Grade 6)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Unit cubes or blocks (if available) for building physical models.
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 8.EE.2 — Use square root and cube root symbols to represent solutions to equations of the form x^2 = p and x^3 = p, where p is a positive rational number. Evaluate square roots of small perfect squares and cube roots of small perfect cubes. Know that sqrt(2) is irrational.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review cubing numbers and connect to volume.

1. Compute: 1^3, 2^3, 3^3, 4^3, 5^3. (1, 8, 27, 64, 125)
2. A cube-shaped box has a volume of 27 cubic inches. What is the side length? (3 inches, because 3^3 = 27)
3. Ask: "If squaring means multiplying a number by itself twice, and the square root undoes squaring, what operation undoes cubing?" (The cube root.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Cube root (cbrt)** — a value that, when multiplied by itself three times, produces the given number. cbrt(27) = 3 because 3 x 3 x 3 = 27. Written with the radical symbol and a small 3.
- **Perfect cube** — the cube of an integer: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000.
- **Radicand** — the number under the radical sign. In cbrt(64), the radicand is 64.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Build the perfect cubes table.**
Write on the board:

| n | n^3 |
|---|-----|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 8 |
| 3 | 27 |
| 4 | 64 |
| 5 | 125 |
| 6 | 216 |
| 7 | 343 |
| 8 | 512 |
| 9 | 729 |
| 10 | 1000 |

Say: "Just like we memorized perfect squares, you should know at least the first five perfect cubes (through 125) by heart."

**Step 2 — Connect cube roots to volume.**
Draw a cube on the board. Say: "If a cube has volume V, its side length is cbrt(V)."

Example: A storage container is a perfect cube with a volume of 64 cubic feet. What is the side length?
- Side = cbrt(64) = 4 feet, because 4 x 4 x 4 = 64.

**Step 3 — Cube roots of negative numbers.**
Say: "Here is something interesting. Unlike square roots, cube roots CAN be negative."
- cbrt(-8) = -2, because (-2) x (-2) x (-2) = -8.
- cbrt(-27) = -3, because (-3) x (-3) x (-3) = -27.

Explain: "A negative times a negative is positive, but a negative times a negative times a negative is negative. So cubing a negative number gives a negative result, and the cube root of a negative number is negative."

**Step 4 — Simplify cube roots by factoring out perfect cube factors.**
Say: "Just like with square roots, we can simplify cube roots. The rule is similar:"

cbrt(a x b) = cbrt(a) x cbrt(b)

Example: Simplify cbrt(40).
- Factor 40: 40 = 8 x 5. And 8 is a perfect cube (2^3 = 8).
- cbrt(40) = cbrt(8 x 5) = cbrt(8) x cbrt(5) = 2*cbrt(5).

Example: Simplify cbrt(250).
- Factor 250: 250 = 125 x 2. And 125 is a perfect cube (5^3 = 125).
- cbrt(250) = cbrt(125 x 2) = cbrt(125) x cbrt(2) = 5*cbrt(2).

**Step 5 — When the radicand has no perfect cube factor.**
Say: "Not every cube root can be simplified. For example, cbrt(10). The factors of 10 are 1, 2, 5, and 10. None of these (except 1) is a perfect cube. So cbrt(10) is already in simplified form."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Evaluating a perfect cube root**
Evaluate cbrt(216).
- Check the table: 6^3 = 216.
- cbrt(216) = 6.

**Example B — Cube root of a negative number**
Evaluate cbrt(-125).
- 5^3 = 125, so cbrt(125) = 5.
- Since the radicand is negative: cbrt(-125) = -5.
- Check: (-5) x (-5) x (-5) = 25 x (-5) = -125. Correct.

**Example C — Simplifying a cube root**
Simplify cbrt(54).
- Factor 54: 54 = 27 x 2. And 27 is a perfect cube (3^3 = 27).
- cbrt(54) = cbrt(27 x 2) = cbrt(27) x cbrt(2) = 3*cbrt(2).

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. The student attempts each problem first, then discusses.

1. Evaluate: cbrt(343).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Check the perfect cubes table. Which number cubed gives 343?"

2. Evaluate: cbrt(-64).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "First find cbrt(64). Then think about what negative number, cubed, gives -64."

3. Simplify: cbrt(16).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Factor 16 as 8 x 2. Is 8 a perfect cube?"

4. Simplify: cbrt(192).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "192 = 64 x 3. Is 64 a perfect cube?"

5. A cube-shaped aquarium holds 343 cubic inches of water. What is the length of one edge?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Set up the equation: s^3 = 343. Solve for s."

3. Simplify: cbrt(24).
4. Simplify: cbrt(81).

### ★★ Stretch

6. A cube has a volume of 216 cubic centimeters. A second cube has a volume of 1000 cubic centimeters. What is the difference in their edge lengths?
7. Simplify: cbrt(-128).

### ★★★ Challenge
8. Is cbrt(2) rational or irrational? Explain your reasoning.
9. Simplify cbrt(72) x cbrt(3) by first multiplying the radicands. Write the result in simplest form.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** Evaluate cbrt(-27).
**Problem 2:** Simplify cbrt(56).

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Drill the first five perfect cubes (1, 8, 27, 64, 125) using flashcards or rapid oral questioning until the student can recall them instantly.
- For simplification, use the same structured approach as for square roots: list all factor pairs, circle the perfect cube factors, choose the largest.
- Provide three additional simplification problems with scaffolding (the perfect cube factor is highlighted).

### If Mastery Is Met
- Challenge: "Can cube roots be simplified like square roots when adding? Is cbrt(16) + cbrt(54) the same as cbrt(70)?" (No. cbrt(16) + cbrt(54) = 2*cbrt(2) + 3*cbrt(2) = 5*cbrt(2), which is not cbrt(70).)
- Preview Lesson 6: "We have been working with roots. Now we shift to the other direction — exponents — and learn the rules that make working with them efficient."

## Extension (Optional)
- **Volume Challenge:** A cube has a volume of 2 cubic meters. What is the exact edge length? What is the approximate edge length to the nearest hundredth? (cbrt(2) meters, approximately 1.26 meters.)
- **Comparison:** Compare the behaviors of square roots and cube roots in a two-column table. Include: Can the radicand be negative? Is the result always positive? Can the result be irrational?

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Confusing square roots with cube roots.** A student may write cbrt(64) = 8 (thinking sqrt(64) = 8). Remind them: cube root means three factors, not two. 4 x 4 x 4 = 64, so cbrt(64) = 4.
- **Thinking cube roots of negative numbers are impossible.** Students familiar with square roots (where negatives under the radical are not allowed in real numbers) may assume the same for cube roots. Demonstrate with (-3)^3 = -27 to show that negative cube roots are valid.
- **Not finding the largest perfect cube factor.** A student may simplify cbrt(192) as cbrt(8 x 24) = 2*cbrt(24), then stop. But cbrt(24) = cbrt(8 x 3) = 2*cbrt(3). The full simplification is 2 x 2*cbrt(3) = 4*cbrt(3). Alternatively, recognize 192 = 64 x 3 from the start.

### Pacing Advice
- If the student grasped square root simplification easily in Lesson 4, cube root simplification should follow naturally. In that case, spend more time on the conceptual aspects (volume connection, negative cube roots).
- If the student struggled with square root simplification, slow down on Step 4 and work through extra examples together.
- The perfect cubes table should remain visible throughout the lesson and ideally throughout the rest of the unit.

## Answer Key

### Guided Practice
1. cbrt(343) = 7 (because 7^3 = 343)
2. cbrt(-64) = -4 (because (-4)^3 = -64)
3. cbrt(16) = cbrt(8 x 2) = 2*cbrt(2)
4. cbrt(192) = cbrt(64 x 3) = 4*cbrt(3)
5. s = cbrt(343) = 7 inches

### Independent Practice

**★ On-Level**
1. cbrt(729) = 9 (because 9^3 = 729)
2. cbrt(-8) = -2
3. cbrt(24) = cbrt(8 x 3) = 2*cbrt(3)
4. cbrt(81) = cbrt(27 x 3) = 3*cbrt(3)

**★★ Stretch**
5. cbrt(500) = cbrt(125 x 4) = 5*cbrt(4)
6. Edge of first cube: cbrt(216) = 6 cm. Edge of second cube: cbrt(1000) = 10 cm. Difference: 10 - 6 = 4 cm.
7. cbrt(-128) = cbrt(-1 x 128) = -cbrt(128) = -cbrt(64 x 2) = -4*cbrt(2)

**★★★ Challenge**
8. cbrt(2) is irrational. If cbrt(2) = p/q for integers p and q, then (p/q)^3 = 2, meaning p^3 = 2q^3. By an argument similar to the proof that sqrt(2) is irrational, this leads to a contradiction. Since 2 is not a perfect cube, its cube root is irrational.
9. cbrt(72) x cbrt(3) = cbrt(72 x 3) = cbrt(216) = 6.

### Interleaved Review
10. 3/5 = x/20. Cross-multiply: 5x = 60. x = 12.
11. 7/x = 21/15. Cross-multiply: 21x = 105. x = 5.

### Exit Ticket
1. cbrt(-27) = -3
2. cbrt(56) = cbrt(8 x 7) = 2*cbrt(7)
