# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 5, Lesson 7

## Topic
Two-Way Tables: Organizing Categorical Data

## Learning Objectives
- Explain what a two-way frequency table is and what type of data it displays.
- Identify the rows, columns, joint frequencies, and marginal totals in a two-way table.
- Construct a two-way frequency table from a verbal description of categorical data.
- Read and interpret individual cells in a two-way table.

## Prerequisites
- Understands the difference between categorical and numerical data (Grade 7 statistics)
- Can read and interpret data tables (Grade 6-7)
- Can perform basic addition and verify row/column totals (Grade 5-6)

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and lined paper (for constructing tables)
- Graph paper or blank paper (tables can be drawn or provided as templates)
- Index card or half-sheet for Exit Ticket
- Calculator (optional)

**Standards:** 8.SP.4 — Understand that patterns of association can also be seen in bivariate categorical data by displaying frequencies and relative frequencies in a two-way table. Construct and interpret a two-way table summarizing data on two categorical variables collected from the same subjects.

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Categorical data** — data that places individuals into groups or categories (e.g., yes/no, color, grade level, sport).
- **Two-way frequency table** — a table that organizes data by two categorical variables simultaneously, with one variable as rows and one as columns.
- **Joint frequency** — the count in a single cell of a two-way table; the number of individuals who belong to both a specific row category and a specific column category.
- **Marginal frequency** — a row total or column total; the total count for one category regardless of the other variable.
- **Grand total** — the total number of individuals in the entire data set; located in the bottom-right cell of the table.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — What is a two-way table?**

Say: "When we worked with scatter plots, we were studying two numerical variables — things like height, scores, or time. Now we turn to two categorical variables — things like yes/no, type of activity, grade level, or preference."

Say: "A two-way frequency table lets us look at both categorical variables at the same time. Each person in the data set falls into exactly one cell of the table — the cell where their row category and column category intersect."

**Step 2 — Anatomy of a two-way table.**

Draw this table structure:

```
                   | Variable B: Cat 1 | Variable B: Cat 2 | ROW TOTAL |
Variable A: Cat 1  |   (joint)         |   (joint)         | (marginal)|
Variable A: Cat 2  |   (joint)         |   (joint)         | (marginal)|
COLUMN TOTAL       |   (marginal)      |   (marginal)      | (grand)   |
```

Label each part:
- **Joint frequencies:** the four interior cells — count of individuals in both specific categories.
- **Marginal frequencies (row totals):** count of all individuals in that row (regardless of which column they're in).
- **Marginal frequencies (column totals):** count of all individuals in that column (regardless of which row they're in).
- **Grand total:** total number of individuals in the study.

**Step 3 — Worked example: Constructing a table.**

A school surveys 120 students about whether they participate in a sport (Yes/No) and what grade they are in (Grade 7 or Grade 8). The results:
- 35 Grade 7 students play a sport.
- 15 Grade 7 students do not play a sport.
- 40 Grade 8 students play a sport.
- 30 Grade 8 students do not play a sport.

Build the table step by step:

|                   | Plays Sport | Does Not Play Sport | Row Total |
|-------------------|-------------|---------------------|-----------|
| **Grade 7**       | 35          | 15                  | 50        |
| **Grade 8**       | 40          | 30                  | 70        |
| **Column Total**  | 75          | 45                  | 120       |

Walk through: "The joint frequency 35 means '35 Grade 7 students who play a sport.' The row total 50 means 'there are 50 Grade 7 students in the survey.' The column total 75 means '75 students in total play a sport.' The grand total 120 confirms that 50 + 70 = 120 and 75 + 45 = 120."

**Step 4 — Reading the table.**

Ask questions about the completed table:
- "How many Grade 8 students do not play a sport?" (30)
- "How many total students play a sport?" (75)
- "How many Grade 7 students are in the survey?" (50)
- "What does the cell (Grade 8, Does Not Play Sport) tell us?" (30 Grade 8 students do not play a sport.)
- "Do the row totals add up to the grand total?" (50 + 70 = 120. Yes.)
- "Do the column totals add up to the grand total?" (75 + 45 = 120. Yes.)

Say: "Always check: row totals must add to the grand total, and column totals must also add to the grand total. If they don't, you made an arithmetic error."

**Step 5 — Constructing a table from raw survey data.**

Present a list of survey responses and show how to tally them into a table:

A class of 20 students answered two questions:
- Do you prefer watching movies or playing video games?
- Are you in the morning or afternoon session?

Responses (each entry is one student: session/preference):
Morning/Movies, Morning/Movies, Morning/Games, Afternoon/Movies, Morning/Movies, Afternoon/Games, Morning/Games, Afternoon/Movies, Afternoon/Games, Morning/Movies, Afternoon/Games, Morning/Games, Afternoon/Movies, Morning/Movies, Afternoon/Games, Morning/Games, Afternoon/Movies, Afternoon/Games, Morning/Movies, Afternoon/Movies

Tally:
- Morning/Movies: 6
- Morning/Games: 5
- Afternoon/Movies: 6
- Afternoon/Games: 3 (check: wait — 6+5+6+3 = 20. ✓)

Wait, recount for accuracy:
Morning/Movies: responses 1, 2, 5, 10, 14, 19 = **6**
Morning/Games: responses 3, 7, 12, 16 = **4** (correcting the count)
Afternoon/Movies: responses 4, 8, 13, 17, 20 = **5**
Afternoon/Games: responses 6, 9, 11, 15, 18 = **5**
Total: 6 + 4 + 5 + 5 = 20. ✓

Table:
|                  | Movies | Video Games | Row Total |
|------------------|--------|-------------|-----------|
| **Morning**      | 6      | 4           | 10        |
| **Afternoon**    | 5      | 5           | 10        |
| **Column Total** | 11     | 9           | 20        |

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity: Construct and read a two-way table.**

A researcher surveys 80 teenagers about two things:
- Do they have a smartphone? (Yes / No)
- Do they get 8 or more hours of sleep? (Yes / No)

The researcher collects this information:
- 42 teens have a smartphone and get enough sleep.
- 18 teens have a smartphone but do not get enough sleep.
- 10 teens do not have a smartphone but get enough sleep.
- 10 teens do not have a smartphone and do not get enough sleep.

1. Set up a two-way table with "Smartphone" (Yes/No) as rows and "8+ Hours Sleep" (Yes/No) as columns.

2. Fill in the four joint frequencies.

3. Calculate and fill in all row totals and column totals.

4. Calculate and confirm the grand total.

5. Answer these questions by reading the table:
   a. How many teenagers in the survey have a smartphone?
   b. How many teenagers in the survey do not get enough sleep?
   c. How many teenagers do not have a smartphone AND get enough sleep?
   d. What is the grand total? Does it match the problem statement?

**Expected table:**

|                    | 8+ Hours Sleep | Less than 8 Hours Sleep | Row Total |
|--------------------|----------------|--------------------------|-----------|
| **Has Smartphone** | 42             | 18                       | 60        |
| **No Smartphone**  | 10             | 10                       | 20        |
| **Column Total**   | 52             | 28                       | 80        |

Answers: (a) 60, (b) 28, (c) 10, (d) 80. ✓

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

A sports organization surveys 150 youth athletes about two things:
- Their main sport: Soccer or Basketball
- Whether they play on a travel team: Yes or No

Survey results:
- 55 soccer players are on a travel team.
- 25 soccer players are not on a travel team.
- 45 basketball players are on a travel team.
- 25 basketball players are not on a travel team.

1. Construct a complete two-way frequency table. Label rows and columns clearly and include all totals.
2. How many athletes in the survey play soccer?
3. How many athletes in the survey are on a travel team?
4. What is the grand total? Verify it matches in both directions (rows summing to grand total, and columns summing to grand total).
5. Which sport has more athletes on a travel team? By how many?
6. A researcher made an error and says there are 160 total athletes. Where might the error be? How would you catch this?

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

7. Using the same athletics table: A new category is discovered — 10 athletes listed as "Basketball" should have been listed as "Other sport." How does the table change? Rebuild the table with a third row for "Other."
   (Hint: assume all 10 came from the basketball travel-team players, so travel team basketball becomes 35, and other/travel becomes 10.)

8. Could a two-way table have more than two rows or two columns? Give a real-world example of data that would need a 3-row × 3-column table.

9. (Interleaved — Unit 2 linear equations) A two-way table has row totals of 60 and 90. The column totals are listed as 45, x, and 45 (three columns). What is the value of x? What is the grand total?

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

10. A student tries to construct a two-way table but makes an error. The table looks like this:

|                  | Likes Math | Doesn't Like Math | Row Total |
|------------------|------------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Grade 7**      | 18         | 12                | 32        |
| **Grade 8**      | 22         | 16                | 38        |
| **Column Total** | 40         | 28                | 70        |

Identify at least two errors in this table. Explain each error.

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A survey of 60 students records their grade (Grade 8 or Grade 9) and their preferred reading format (Print or Digital). Here is what was found: 15 Grade 8 students prefer print, 20 Grade 8 students prefer digital, 10 Grade 9 students prefer print, and 15 Grade 9 students prefer digital. Construct the complete two-way frequency table, including all totals.

**Problem 2:** In the table from Problem 1, what does the marginal frequency of 35 (row total for Grade 8) tell you?

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 correct.**

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. Complete two-way frequency table:

|                    | Travel Team: Yes | Travel Team: No | Row Total |
|--------------------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Soccer**         | 55              | 25              | 80        |
| **Basketball**     | 45              | 25              | 70        |
| **Column Total**   | 100             | 50              | 150       |

2. **80** athletes play soccer (row total for Soccer).
3. **100** athletes are on a travel team (column total for Travel Team: Yes).
4. Grand total = **150**. Check: 80 + 70 = 150. ✓ And 100 + 50 = 150. ✓
5. Soccer has more athletes on a travel team: 55 vs. 45. **Soccer has 10 more travel team athletes.**
6. The correct grand total is 150, not 160. The researcher may have accidentally added an extra row or column value, or made an arithmetic error when computing totals. To catch this: check that all row totals add to the claimed grand total (80 + 70 = 150, not 160) and that all column totals also add to the claimed grand total.

7. Revised table (10 taken from Basketball/Travel Team):

|                   | Travel Team: Yes | Travel Team: No | Row Total |
|-------------------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Soccer**        | 55              | 25              | 80        |
| **Basketball**    | 35              | 25              | 60        |
| **Other**         | 10              | 0               | 10        |
| **Column Total**  | 100             | 50              | 150       |

8. Yes — a two-way table can have any number of rows or columns. Example: a survey asking favorite school subject (Math, Science, English) and grade level (Grade 6, Grade 7, Grade 8) would need a 3 × 3 table.

9. Grand total = 60 + 90 = **150**. Column totals: 45 + x + 45 = 150 → x + 90 = 150 → **x = 60**.

10. **Error 1:** The row total for Grade 7 is 18 + 12 = 30, not 32. The sum of the joint frequencies in Row 1 does not match the listed row total.
    **Error 2:** The grand total is inconsistent. Adding row totals: 32 + 38 = 70. But adding column totals: 40 + 28 = 68. These do not match (70 ≠ 68). The table has an internal inconsistency — at least one cell or total is wrong.
    *(Additional error: if we use the correct row totals of 30 and 38, grand total would be 68, which matches column totals. The error is in Row 1's total — it should be 30.)*

### Exit Ticket

1. Complete two-way frequency table:

|                   | Print | Digital | Row Total |
|-------------------|-------|---------|-----------|
| **Grade 8**       | 15    | 20      | 35        |
| **Grade 9**       | 10    | 15      | 25        |
| **Column Total**  | 25    | 35      | 60        |

2. The marginal frequency of 35 (row total for Grade 8) means that **35 out of the 60 students surveyed are in Grade 8**, regardless of their reading preference.

## Teacher Notes

- The most common error is confusing joint and marginal frequencies. Use the visual scaffold suggested in the Facilitator Guide: shade joint frequency cells (interior) one color, row totals another color, column totals a third color, and the grand total a fourth. This physical distinction supports accurate reading.
- Always require students to verify that row totals add to the grand total AND that column totals also add to the grand total. This double-check catches arithmetic errors before they propagate into Lessons 8-9.
- The Challenge problem (Tier 3 #10) requires error-checking and careful arithmetic — excellent critical thinking practice.
- Lesson 7 is deliberately lower in difficulty to allow students to build the table structure fluently. Lesson 8 will add the layer of relative frequencies. If a student rushes through Lesson 7, confirm understanding of what each cell represents before advancing.
- Interleaved review: Tier 2 #9 requires solving a simple linear equation (Unit 2 skill) to find a missing column total.
