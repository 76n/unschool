# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 5, Lesson 3

## Topic
Drawing Lines of Best Fit

## Learning Objectives
- Explain what a line of best fit is and what it models.
- Draw an informal line of best fit that approximately balances data points above and below the line.
- Identify two points on a drawn line of best fit to calculate approximate slope.
- Explain why a line of best fit is only drawn for data that shows a linear association.

## Prerequisites
- Can construct and describe a scatter plot (Unit 5, Lessons 1-2)
- Understands positive, negative, and no association (Lesson 2)
- Can calculate slope from two points: m = (y2 - y1)/(x2 - x1) (Grade 8, Unit 3)

## Materials Needed
- Graph paper (2-3 sheets)
- Ruler or straightedge (essential for this lesson)
- Pencil (recommended — students will adjust lines)
- Index card or half-sheet for Exit Ticket

**Standards:** 8.SP.2 — Know that straight lines are widely used to model relationships between two quantitative variables. For scatter plots that suggest a linear association, informally fit a straight line, and informally assess the model fit by judging the closeness of the data points to the line.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect the scatter plot pattern vocabulary from Lesson 2 to the concept of modeling with a line.

1. A scatter plot shows a strong positive linear association. Describe what the cloud of points looks like. *(Points drift from lower-left to upper-right, clustered fairly tightly around an invisible straight line.)*

2. Interleaved review (Unit 3 — slope): Calculate the slope of the line passing through (2, 10) and (6, 30).
   *(m = (30 - 10)/(6 - 2) = 20/4 = 5)*

3. If a scatter plot shows NO association, would you try to draw a straight line through it to model the data? Explain. *(No — a line assumes a relationship exists. If there is no association, a line would be meaningless and misleading.)*

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Line of best fit** — a straight line drawn through a scatter plot that best models the linear trend in the data. Also called a trend line.
- **Residual** — the vertical distance from a data point to the line of best fit; a measure of how far the point is from the model.
- **Balance point** — roughly speaking, the line of best fit should have about equal numbers of data points above and below it.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Why a line?**

Say: "When a scatter plot shows a linear association, we can model that association with a straight line. The line does not connect the data points — it is a model that captures the overall trend. Think of it like this: if you squeezed all the points into a single line as best you could, where would that line go?"

Say: "The line of best fit is the mathematician's answer to: 'What straight line fits this data cloud most closely?' In high school and college, you will learn a precise formula for this. For now, we will draw an informal line by eye — and we can get surprisingly close."

**Step 2 — Rules for drawing a line of best fit.**

Write these rules on the board:

1. The data must show a linear association. Do not draw a line of best fit on data with no association or clearly nonlinear data.
2. Use a ruler — the line must be straight.
3. The line should pass through the middle of the data cloud, not just through the first and last points.
4. Aim for roughly equal numbers of data points above and below the line.
5. The line does not need to pass through any actual data point (though it might).
6. The line should extend to cover the full range of the data and slightly beyond.

**Step 3 — Demonstration with data.**

Use this data set: Average study time per day (hours) vs. GPA at end of term for 10 students.

| Student | Daily Study (hrs) | GPA |
|---------|------------------|-----|
| A       | 0.5              | 1.8 |
| B       | 1.0              | 2.2 |
| C       | 1.5              | 2.5 |
| D       | 2.0              | 2.8 |
| E       | 2.5              | 3.0 |
| F       | 3.0              | 3.2 |
| G       | 3.5              | 3.5 |
| H       | 4.0              | 3.7 |
| I       | 0.8              | 2.0 |
| J       | 2.8              | 3.4 |

Plot on graph paper: x-axis from 0 to 5 (hours), y-axis from 1.5 to 4.0 (GPA).

Say: "I can see a clear positive linear association. Now I'll draw my line of best fit."

Model the process:
- "I'll place my ruler so it passes through the middle of the data cloud — not hugging the top or bottom."
- "I'll count: how many points are above my line? How many below? I want roughly equal numbers. Let me adjust."
- Draw the line and verify: approximately 4-5 points above and 4-5 below.

Say: "Now I can pick two points ON my line (not necessarily data points) to find the slope. The line appears to pass through about (0.5, 1.9) and (4.0, 3.7)."

Calculate: m = (3.7 - 1.9)/(4.0 - 0.5) = 1.8/3.5 ≈ 0.51.

Say: "This means for each additional hour of daily study, GPA increases by about 0.51 points."

**Step 4 — What makes a line "bad"?**

Show two incorrect versions of the line:
- A line that only passes through the top cluster of points (too high — too many points below it).
- A line that connects just the leftmost and rightmost data points (ignores all the middle data).

Say: "Both of these would give us a line that poorly represents the data. The goal is always to minimize the overall distance from the points to the line — not to hit specific points."

### Worked Example

**Example:** Draw a line of best fit for this data about daily temperature and number of park visitors.

| Day | High Temp (°F) | Visitors |
|-----|---------------|----------|
| 1   | 62            | 120      |
| 2   | 70            | 180      |
| 3   | 75            | 220      |
| 4   | 80            | 265      |
| 5   | 85            | 300      |
| 6   | 68            | 160      |
| 7   | 73            | 200      |
| 8   | 78            | 245      |
| 9   | 82            | 280      |
| 10  | 90            | 340      |

1. Plot on graph paper (x: 60 to 95 by 5s; y: 100 to 360 by 20s).
2. Confirm: positive linear association? Yes.
3. Draw line of best fit using a ruler.
4. Check balance: about 5 points above, 5 below. Adjust if needed.
5. Pick two points on the line (not necessarily data points). Suggested: approximately (62, 125) and (90, 340).
6. Slope: m ≈ (340 - 125)/(90 - 62) = 215/28 ≈ 7.7.
   Interpretation: "For every 1°F increase in temperature, the park gets about 8 more visitors."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Activity:** Draw a line of best fit and assess the fit.

The data below shows a school track team's weekly training distance (miles) and their average 1-mile race time (minutes) at the end of the season.

| Runner | Weekly Mileage | Race Time (min) |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| 1      | 10            | 7.2             |
| 2      | 15            | 6.8             |
| 3      | 20            | 6.3             |
| 4      | 25            | 5.9             |
| 5      | 30            | 5.6             |
| 6      | 35            | 5.3             |
| 7      | 40            | 5.1             |
| 8      | 12            | 7.0             |
| 9      | 22            | 6.1             |
| 10     | 38            | 5.2             |

1. Plot this data on graph paper. Label: x = "Weekly Mileage", y = "Race Time (min)."
   *(x: 0 to 45 by 5s; y: 4.5 to 7.5 by 0.5s recommended)*

2. What type of association does this scatter plot show? *(Strong negative linear association.)*

3. Draw a line of best fit using a ruler. Check: roughly equal points above and below?

4. Count the data points above your line and below your line. *(Should be approximately 5 and 5, though exact balance depends on the student's line.)*

5. Choose two points ON your line (not from the data table) and calculate the slope.
   *(Approximate: line through (10, 7.1) and (40, 5.1) → slope = (5.1 - 7.1)/(40 - 10) = -2.0/30 ≈ -0.067. Answers will vary based on individual line placement.)*

6. Interpret the slope: "For each additional mile of weekly training, the race time changes by about ___ minute(s)."
   *(Decreases by about 0.067 minutes, or roughly 4 seconds, per additional mile.)*

**Facilitator tip:** Students often select points that are far apart for better slope accuracy. If a student picks two nearby points with very similar y-values, the slope will be inaccurate. Suggest: "Use two points that are as far apart as possible along the line — one near each end."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Tier 1 (On-level)

A researcher records daily screen time (hours) and sleep duration (hours) for 8 teenagers:

| Teen | Screen Time (hrs) | Sleep (hrs) |
|------|------------------|-------------|
| 1    | 2                | 9.0         |
| 2    | 4                | 8.0         |
| 3    | 6                | 7.2         |
| 4    | 8                | 6.5         |
| 5    | 10               | 5.8         |
| 6    | 3                | 8.5         |
| 7    | 7                | 6.8         |
| 8    | 9                | 6.2         |

1. Plot the scatter plot on graph paper. Label axes with variable names and units.
2. Describe the association: direction, form (linear/nonlinear), any outliers.
3. Draw a line of best fit. Check that you used a ruler.
4. Identify two points on your line of best fit (not from the data table) and record their coordinates.
5. Calculate the slope of your line of best fit. Show your work.
6. Interpret the slope in the context of screen time and sleep.

### Tier 2 (Stretch)

Use the same screen time data for problems 7-9.

7. The y-intercept of your line of best fit is the value of y when x = 0. Read or estimate the y-intercept from your graph. What does it mean in the context of this study?

8. A student draws their line of best fit so that it passes through points (2, 9.0) and (10, 5.8), which are actual data points. Is this necessarily a good line of best fit? Explain.

9. Two students draw lines of best fit for the same data. Student A's line has 3 points above it and 5 below. Student B's line has 4 points above and 4 below. Which line is likely better? Are there cases where the "less balanced" line might still be better?

### Tier 3 (Challenge)

10. (Interleaved — Unit 4 Pythagorean Theorem) A rectangular sports field has a length of 80 meters and a width of 60 meters. A groundskeeper walks diagonally from one corner to the opposite corner. How far do they walk? Show your work.

11. A scatter plot has 12 data points and a strong positive linear association. After drawing a line of best fit, you notice that one data point is 15 units above the line while all other points are within 3 units. What should you consider before deciding whether to adjust your line or keep it?

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A student draws a line of best fit that passes through 9 of the 10 data points on a scatter plot. Is this a good line of best fit? Explain.

**Problem 2:** From this data, would you draw a line of best fit? Why or why not?

| Day | Lucky number of student | Temperature (°F) |
|-----|------------------------|-----------------|
| 1   | 7                      | 58              |
| 2   | 3                      | 72              |
| 3   | 12                     | 65              |
| 4   | 1                      | 80              |
| 5   | 9                      | 47              |

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 correct.**

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

1. Plot: x-axis "Screen Time (hours)", 0 to 12 by 1s or 2s; y-axis "Sleep (hours)", 5 to 10 by 0.5s. Points: (2, 9.0), (4, 8.0), (6, 7.2), (8, 6.5), (10, 5.8), (3, 8.5), (7, 6.8), (9, 6.2). Check that each point is plotted accurately.

2. **Negative linear association.** As screen time increases, sleep decreases. Points cluster closely around a downward-sloping line. No clear outliers.

3. Line should slope downward from left to right, with roughly 4 points above and 4 below. Award credit if the student's line passes through the middle of the data cloud and is straight.

4. Accept any two points that lie on the student's line. Sample: (2, 9.0) and (10, 5.7), or (0, 9.5) and (12, 5.0), depending on the student's line placement.

5. Sample calculation using (2, 9.0) and (10, 5.7): m = (5.7 - 9.0)/(10 - 2) = -3.3/8 ≈ **-0.41**. Accept any slope in the range -0.30 to -0.50 based on the student's own line.

6. Interpretation: "For each additional hour of daily screen time, average sleep duration decreases by about 0.41 hours (approximately 25 minutes)."

7. The y-intercept represents the predicted sleep duration for a teenager with 0 hours of screen time. Sample: if the line appears to pass through approximately (0, 9.7), then the y-intercept is about 9.7 hours — meaning teenagers who use screens not at all might average about 9.7 hours of sleep. Note: this extrapolates beyond the data range (minimum x in data is 2), so this interpretation has uncertainty.

8. Passing through two actual data points does not guarantee a good line of best fit. The goal is to balance all data points, not to hit specific ones. If those two points are near the extremes of the data and the rest of the data aligns well, it might happen to work — but it might also ignore the middle data. The test is: how well does the line balance all 8 points?

9. **Student B's line (4 above, 4 below) is likely better** for this data since it is more balanced. However, balance is not the only criterion — a line could be perfectly balanced but still poorly fitting if some residuals are very large. If all of Student A's below-line points are very close to the line but the above-line points are far away, the line is still poorly fitted. True best-fit minimizes the sum of squared residuals, not just the count.

10. The diagonal is the hypotenuse: 80^2 + 60^2 = c^2 → 6400 + 3600 = 10000 → c = sqrt(10000) = **100 meters**.

11. Before adjusting the line or treating the point as an outlier, consider: (1) Was there a data entry error for that point? (2) Does the point represent a genuinely unusual observation (e.g., a person with an unusual combination of the two variables)? (3) Does excluding it change the line significantly? (4) Is this point important to the story the data is telling? Do not automatically remove outliers — investigate them first.

### Exit Ticket

1. **No, this is not necessarily a good line of best fit.** A line passing through 9 of 10 data points may be "overfit" to specific points rather than capturing the overall trend. More importantly, a line that passes through 9 points is not the same as a line that minimizes the overall distance from all points. A good line of best fit balances points above and below — it does not need to pass through any of the data points.

2. **No, you would not draw a line of best fit** for this data. The two variables are a student's lucky number (which is arbitrary and random) and the daily temperature (which is determined by weather). These two variables have no meaningful relationship — the scatter plot would show no association. Drawing a line of best fit on data with no association is meaningless.

## Teacher Notes

- Ruler use is non-negotiable in this lesson. A freehand curved or wobbly line defeats the purpose of a "straight-line model."
- The most common error is drawing the line through the first and last data points instead of through the middle of the cloud. Address this by asking: "How many points are above your line? How many below?" and having the student adjust until the balance is approximately equal.
- The slope calculation from the line of best fit should use two points ON THE LINE, not two data points from the original table. Students frequently calculate slope from data table values, which may not match their line. Reinforce: "Put your pencil on two spots on YOUR line — points that are not necessarily in the data table."
- Interleaved review: Tier 3 Problem 10 brings back the Pythagorean Theorem from Unit 4 in a sports context.
- This lesson sets up Lesson 4 (predictions) and Lesson 5 (writing the equation). The slope calculation practiced here is exactly what students will need in Lesson 5.
