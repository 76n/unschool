# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 2, Lesson 1

## Topic
Solving Two-Step Equations

## Learning Objectives
- Review solving one-step equations using inverse operations.
- Solve two-step equations of the form ax + b = c by undoing operations in reverse order.
- Check solutions by substituting back into the original equation.
- Translate simple verbal descriptions into two-step equations and solve.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to solve one-step equations (e.g., x + 5 = 12, 4x = 20) (Grade 6-7)
- Understanding of inverse operations (addition/subtraction, multiplication/division)
- Fluency with integer operations, including negatives
- Ability to substitute values into expressions

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 8.EE.7 — Solve linear equations in one variable. 8.EE.7a — Give examples of linear equations in one variable with one solution, infinitely many solutions, or no solution.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of one-step equations and inverse operations.

1. Solve: x + 9 = 14. (x = 5)
2. Solve: x - 3 = 10. (x = 13)
3. Solve: 5x = 35. (x = 7)
4. Solve: x/4 = 6. (x = 24)
5. Ask: "What operation did you use to solve each one? How did you decide which operation to use?"

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Equation** — a mathematical statement that two expressions are equal, connected by an equals sign.
- **Inverse operations** — operations that undo each other. Addition and subtraction are inverses. Multiplication and division are inverses.
- **Solution** — the value of the variable that makes the equation true.
- **Two-step equation** — an equation that requires exactly two inverse operations to isolate the variable.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect one-step equations to two-step equations.**
Write on the board: 2x + 5 = 13.

Say: "In a one-step equation like x + 5 = 8, we only need to subtract 5. But here, two things have happened to x: first it was multiplied by 2, and then 5 was added. To undo these, we work in reverse order — undo the LAST thing first."

Draw the analogy: "Think of putting on socks and shoes. You put socks on first, then shoes. To take them off, you remove shoes first, then socks. The same reverse-order logic applies to equations."

**Step 2 — Demonstrate the solving process.**
Solve 2x + 5 = 13:
- Step A: Undo the addition. Subtract 5 from both sides.
  - 2x + 5 - 5 = 13 - 5
  - 2x = 8
- Step B: Undo the multiplication. Divide both sides by 2.
  - 2x / 2 = 8 / 2
  - x = 4

**Step 3 — Check the solution.**
Say: "Always check by substituting back into the ORIGINAL equation."
- 2(4) + 5 = 8 + 5 = 13. Correct.

Write on the board: "Check: substitute your answer into the original equation. Both sides should be equal."

**Step 4 — Work through a subtraction example.**
Solve 3x - 7 = 14:
- Add 7 to both sides: 3x = 21
- Divide both sides by 3: x = 7
- Check: 3(7) - 7 = 21 - 7 = 14. Correct.

**Step 5 — Work through a division example.**
Solve x/5 + 3 = 10:
- Subtract 3 from both sides: x/5 = 7
- Multiply both sides by 5: x = 35
- Check: 35/5 + 3 = 7 + 3 = 10. Correct.

**Step 6 — Connect to real-world language.**
Say: "A plumber charges $40 per hour plus a $75 service fee. The total bill was $235. How many hours did the plumber work?"
- Write the equation: 40h + 75 = 235
- Subtract 75: 40h = 160
- Divide by 40: h = 4 hours
- Check: 40(4) + 75 = 160 + 75 = 235. Correct.

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Basic two-step equation**
Solve: 4x + 9 = 25.
- Subtract 9 from both sides: 4x = 16.
- Divide both sides by 4: x = 4.
- Check: 4(4) + 9 = 16 + 9 = 25. Correct.

**Example B — Two-step equation with subtraction**
Solve: 6x - 11 = 31.
- Add 11 to both sides: 6x = 42.
- Divide both sides by 6: x = 7.
- Check: 6(7) - 11 = 42 - 11 = 31. Correct.

**Example C — Two-step equation with negative result**
Solve: 5x + 8 = -12.
- Subtract 8 from both sides: 5x = -20.
- Divide both sides by 5: x = -4.
- Check: 5(-4) + 8 = -20 + 8 = -12. Correct.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. The student attempts each problem first, then discusses.

1. Solve: 3x + 4 = 19.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What is the last operation done to x? Undo that first."

2. Solve: 7x - 5 = 23.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What do you add to both sides to undo the subtraction?"

3. Solve: x/3 + 8 = 15.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "After you subtract 8, how do you undo division by 3?"

4. Solve: 2x + 11 = 3.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "The solution will be negative. That's okay — work the same process."

5. A movie streaming service charges $12 per month plus a one-time sign-up fee of $20. If you have spent $80 total, how many months have you been subscribed?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Write an equation: 12m + 20 = 80. Now solve."

**Facilitator tip:** Watch for students who try to divide first instead of subtracting. Reinforce the reverse-order principle: undo the addition/subtraction (the "outer" operation) before the multiplication/division (the "inner" operation).

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A — Core Practice
Solve each equation and check your solution.

1. 5x + 3 = 28
2. 4x - 9 = 15
3. x/2 + 7 = 13
4. 8x - 4 = 36
5. 3x + 14 = 2

### Part B — Negative Solutions and Larger Numbers
Solve each equation and check your solution.

6. 6x + 15 = -3
7. 9x - 20 = 52
8. x/4 - 5 = -2

### Part C — Word Problems
Write an equation and solve.

9. A taxi ride costs $3.50 per mile plus a base fare of $5.00. If the total fare was $26.00, how many miles was the ride?
10. Marcus has $45. He spends $8 on lunch and then buys notebooks that cost $5 each. If he has no money left, how many notebooks did he buy? (Hint: be careful about the order of operations here.)

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. 5x + 3 = 28 → 5x = 25 → x = 5. Check: 5(5) + 3 = 28. Correct.
2. 4x - 9 = 15 → 4x = 24 → x = 6. Check: 4(6) - 9 = 15. Correct.
3. x/2 + 7 = 13 → x/2 = 6 → x = 12. Check: 12/2 + 7 = 13. Correct.
4. 8x - 4 = 36 → 8x = 40 → x = 5. Check: 8(5) - 4 = 36. Correct.
5. 3x + 14 = 2 → 3x = -12 → x = -4. Check: 3(-4) + 14 = -12 + 14 = 2. Correct.

**Part B**
6. 6x + 15 = -3 → 6x = -18 → x = -3. Check: 6(-3) + 15 = -18 + 15 = -3. Correct.
7. 9x - 20 = 52 → 9x = 72 → x = 8. Check: 9(8) - 20 = 72 - 20 = 52. Correct.
8. x/4 - 5 = -2 → x/4 = 3 → x = 12. Check: 12/4 - 5 = 3 - 5 = -2. Correct.

**Part C**
9. 3.50m + 5 = 26 → 3.50m = 21 → m = 6 miles. Check: 3.50(6) + 5 = 21 + 5 = 26. Correct.
10. 5n + 8 = 45 → 5n = 37 → n = 7.4. Since you cannot buy 0.4 notebooks, Marcus can buy 7 notebooks and would have $2 left. (Accept either the pure algebraic answer n = 7.4 with explanation, or the practical answer of 7 notebooks with $2 remaining.)

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** Solve 4x + 7 = 31. Show your work and check your answer.
**Problem 2:** Solve 6x - 10 = -4. Show your work and check your answer.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct (with check) = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review one-step equations to confirm prerequisite skills are solid.
- Use a balance scale diagram: draw the equation as objects on a scale, then show how removing the same amount from both sides keeps the scale balanced.
- Provide three additional two-step equations with scaffolding (the first inverse operation is indicated).

### If Mastery Is Met
- Challenge: Solve 2x + 3 = 5x - 9 (preview of Lesson 3 — variables on both sides).
- Ask: "Can a two-step equation ever have no solution? Why or why not?" (Not in the standard form ax + b = c where a is not 0 — that always has exactly one solution.)

## Extension

- **Create Your Own:** Write three real-world situations that lead to two-step equations. Solve each one and verify your answer makes sense in context.
- **Backward Equations:** Start with the solution x = 7 and create five different two-step equations that all have this solution. What patterns do you notice?
