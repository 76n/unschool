# Facilitator Guide — Grade 8 Math, Unit 2: Linear Equations and Systems

**Grade:** 8
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 2 — Linear Equations and Systems
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (facilitator leads instruction; student works independently for practice)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit develops the student's ability to solve increasingly complex equations and introduces the concept of systems of equations. The unit begins with a review of two-step equations, then progresses to multi-step equations requiring the distributive property and combining like terms. Students learn to solve equations with variables on both sides and encounter the special cases of no solution and infinitely many solutions. The second half of the unit introduces systems of two linear equations in two variables, teaching three solution methods: graphing, substitution, and elimination. The unit culminates with applying systems to real-world word problems.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Solve two-step and multi-step linear equations in one variable, including equations requiring the distributive property and combining like terms
- Solve equations with variables on both sides by collecting variable terms on one side
- Recognize and explain the special cases: no solution (contradiction) and infinitely many solutions (identity)
- Understand that a system of equations is a set of two equations with two unknowns, and that the solution is an ordered pair satisfying both equations
- Solve systems of equations by graphing and identify the intersection point as the solution
- Solve systems of equations using the substitution method
- Solve systems of equations using the elimination (addition/subtraction) method
- Choose the most efficient method for solving a given system
- Translate real-world situations into systems of equations, solve them, and interpret the results

**Why this matters:** Linear equations are the backbone of algebra. The ability to set up and solve equations — and systems of equations — is essential for high school mathematics, science, economics, and everyday problem-solving. This unit bridges arithmetic thinking ("find the number") with algebraic reasoning ("express relationships symbolically and solve"), a critical shift that prepares students for functions and graphing in Unit 3 and beyond.

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm:

- [ ] Can evaluate expressions by substituting values for variables (Grade 7)
- [ ] Understands and applies the distributive property: a(b + c) = ab + ac (Grade 7)
- [ ] Can combine like terms in expressions (e.g., 3x + 5x = 8x) (Grade 7)
- [ ] Can solve one-step equations (e.g., x + 7 = 12, 3x = 15) (Grade 6-7)
- [ ] Can plot points on a coordinate plane and graph simple lines (Grade 6-7)
- [ ] Understands the concept of slope and y-intercept at an introductory level (Grade 7-8 Unit 1)
- [ ] Can perform operations with integers and rational numbers fluently (Grade 7)

| — | Unit Assessment | Separate session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lessons 1-2 review and extend Grade 7 equation-solving. If the student demonstrates strong mastery of two-step equations, condense Lesson 1 and use the saved time for Lesson 3 (variables on both sides), which is often the first genuinely new challenge.
- Lessons 7-8 (substitution and elimination) are the densest part of the unit. If the student needs more time, split each across two sessions rather than rushing. These methods require procedural fluency and careful attention to sign errors.
- Lesson 4 (special cases) is conceptually challenging but procedurally straightforward. One session is typically sufficient, but allow extra discussion time if the student struggles with the idea of "no solution."
- Lesson 9 (choosing a method) is a synthesis lesson. If time is tight, integrate its content into the practice sections of Lessons 7-8 rather than skipping it entirely.
- Lesson 10 (word problems) often requires two sessions: one for setting up systems from context, and one for solving and interpreting.

## Lesson Summaries

| Lesson | Summary |
|--------|---------|
| 1 | Reviews one-step equations, then introduces two-step equations solved by inverse operations. Emphasizes checking solutions by substitution. |
| 2 | Extends to multi-step equations requiring the distributive property and combining like terms before solving. |
| 3 | Introduces equations with variables on both sides. Students learn to collect variable terms on one side by adding or subtracting. |
| 4 | Explores special cases: equations that simplify to contradictions (no solution) and identities (infinitely many solutions). Students learn to recognize each case. |
| 5 | Introduces the concept of a system of equations: two equations with two unknowns. Students verify whether a given ordered pair is a solution to a system. |
| 6 | Teaches solving systems by graphing both equations on the same coordinate plane. The intersection point is the solution. |
| 7 | Teaches the substitution method: isolate one variable in one equation, substitute into the other, solve, and back-substitute. |
| 8 | Teaches the elimination method: add or subtract equations to eliminate one variable. Includes multiplying equations to align coefficients. |
| 9 | Synthesis lesson: students analyze systems and choose the most efficient method (graphing, substitution, or elimination). Compares methods on the same system. |
| 10 | Applies systems of equations to real-world word problems involving mixtures, rates, costs, and comparisons. Emphasizes defining variables and interpreting solutions. |

## Differentiation Strategies

### If the Student Needs More Support
- **Equation balance model:** Use the metaphor of a balance scale throughout Lessons 1-4. Whatever operation is applied to one side must be applied to the other to keep the equation "balanced." Physical manipulatives (algebra tiles or a simple balance) can reinforce this.
- **Color-coding:** In equations with variables on both sides (Lesson 3), have the student underline variable terms in one color and constant terms in another before moving terms.
- **Structured templates:** Provide a step-by-step checklist for substitution (Lesson 7) and elimination (Lesson 8) that the student follows until the procedure becomes automatic.
- **Reduce cognitive load:** When introducing systems (Lessons 5-6), start with systems that have small positive integer solutions (e.g., intersection at (2, 3) rather than (-1, 4.5)).

### If the Student Needs More Challenge
- **Equations with rational coefficients:** Introduce equations with fractions and decimals in Lessons 2-3 (e.g., 0.5x + 1.2 = 3.7, or (2/3)x - 4 = 8).
- **Three-variable preview:** After Lesson 8, show a simple three-variable system and ask how the elimination method could be extended.
- **Real-world modeling:** Ask the student to create their own word problems (Lesson 10) and trade with the facilitator for solving.
- **Proof and reasoning:** In Lesson 4, ask the student to explain algebraically WHY the equation a*x + b = a*x + c has no solution when b is not equal to c.

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Student subtracts the variable term from only one side when solving equations with variables on both sides | Does not consistently apply the balance principle | Return to the balance scale model. Emphasize: whatever you do to one side, you MUST do to the other. Write each step on both sides explicitly. |
| Student writes 3(x + 4) = 3x + 4 | Incomplete distribution — forgot to distribute to the second term | Rewrite the distributive property explicitly: 3 * x + 3 * 4 = 3x + 12. Use arrows to connect the multiplier to each term inside the parentheses. |
| Student gets 0 = 0 and writes "x = 0" as the solution | Confuses the identity result with a specific solution | Explain: 0 = 0 is ALWAYS true — it does not depend on x. This means every value of x works. The equation has infinitely many solutions, not the solution x = 0. |
| Student gets 0 = 5 and writes "x = 5" | Misinterprets a contradiction as a solution | Explain: 0 = 5 is NEVER true — no value of x can make this happen. The equation has no solution. |
| Student checks a point in only one equation and declares it a solution to the system | Does not understand that a system's solution must satisfy BOTH equations | Reinforce: a solution to a system must work in BOTH equations. Always check the point in both. |
| Student graphs lines but reads the wrong intersection point | Imprecise graphing or misreading coordinates | Emphasize careful plotting. After reading the intersection, always verify by substituting back into both original equations. |
| Student forgets to back-substitute after finding one variable | Procedure is incomplete | Remind: a system has two unknowns, so the answer must have two values. Finding x is only half the work — plug it back in to find y. |
| Student distributes the negative sign incorrectly during elimination | Sign errors when subtracting an entire equation | Teach the alternative: instead of subtracting, multiply one equation by -1 and then ADD. This avoids distributing a negative across multiple terms. |

## Assessment Guidance

### Formative Assessment (During the Unit)
- Each lesson includes an Exit Ticket (2 problems). Use these to gauge daily understanding.
- During Guided Practice, observe whether the student can set up each step independently or needs prompting at every stage.
- Pay particular attention to the transition from single equations (Lessons 1-4) to systems (Lessons 5-10). This is a conceptual leap, not just a procedural one.

### Summative Assessment (End of Unit)
The unit assessment contains 5 sections totaling 60 points:
- Section A: Multi-Step Equations (12 points)
- Section B: Variables on Both Sides and Special Cases (10 points)
- Section C: Solving Systems by Graphing (12 points)
- Section D: Solving Systems by Substitution and Elimination (14 points)
- Section E: Word Problems (12 points)

**Mastery threshold:** 48 of 60 points (80%) AND at least 50% in each section.

### What to Do If the Student Does Not Meet Mastery
- Identify the weakest section(s) and reteach using the section-specific guidance in the unit assessment file.
- Reassess the weak section(s) with parallel problems before moving to Unit 3.
- If the student scores below 50% across three or more sections, revisit prerequisite skills (one-step equations, distributive property, graphing points) before reteaching.

## Conversation Starters

- "If I tell you that 2x + 3 = 2x + 7, can you find a value of x that works? Why or why not?"
- "How is solving a system of equations different from solving a single equation? What makes it harder — or more interesting?"
- "When would graphing be the best way to solve a system? When might it be a bad choice?"
- "If you and a friend each have a different phone plan, how could you figure out when both plans cost the same? What would that look like on a graph?"
- "Why do we need to check our solutions? Can't we just trust the algebra?"

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if:

- The student cannot solve one-step equations (e.g., x + 4 = 9, 3x = 12) even with guidance — this indicates a Grade 6-7 gap that will prevent progress through the entire unit
- The student does not understand what "equals" means in an equation — they may need work on the concept of equivalence before manipulating equations
- The student cannot plot ordered pairs on a coordinate plane — this will block Lessons 5-6 entirely
- The student shows persistent sign errors (positive/negative confusion) that do not improve with targeted practice — this may indicate a need for focused integer operations review
- After reteaching, the student scores below 50% on the unit assessment in more than two sections
