# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 2, Lesson 3

## Topic
Equations with Variables on Both Sides

## Learning Objectives
- Recognize equations with variable terms on both sides.
- Collect variable terms on one side of the equation by adding or subtracting.
- Solve equations with variables on both sides, including those requiring distribution and combining like terms first.
- Determine which side to collect variables on and explain the reasoning.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to solve multi-step equations (Lessons 1-2)
- Fluency with the distributive property and combining like terms
- Understanding of inverse operations and the balance principle
- Comfort with integer and rational number operations

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 8.EE.7 — Solve linear equations in one variable. 8.EE.7b — Solve linear equations with rational number coefficients, including equations whose solutions require expanding expressions using the distributive property and collecting like terms.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review multi-step equation solving and set up the challenge of variables on both sides.

1. Solve: 4x + 7 = 31. (x = 6)
2. Solve: 3(x - 2) = 15. (x = 7)
3. Now look at this equation: 5x + 3 = 3x + 11. Ask: "How is this different from the equations you just solved?" (There are x-terms on BOTH sides.)
4. Ask: "Can you still isolate x? What would you need to do first?"

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Variables on both sides** — an equation where the variable appears in terms on the left side AND the right side (e.g., 5x + 3 = 3x + 11).
- **Collect variable terms** — move all variable terms to one side of the equation by adding or subtracting.
- **Collect constant terms** — move all constant terms to the other side of the equation by adding or subtracting.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce the new challenge.**
Write on the board: 5x + 3 = 3x + 11.

Say: "In previous lessons, the variable was only on one side. Now x appears on both sides. Our goal is still to isolate x, so we need to get all the x-terms on one side and all the number terms on the other."

**Step 2 — Strategy: subtract the smaller variable term from both sides.**
Say: "Look at the two variable terms: 5x on the left and 3x on the right. Which is smaller? 3x. Subtract 3x from both sides."

- 5x + 3 - 3x = 3x + 11 - 3x
- 2x + 3 = 11

Say: "Now we have a two-step equation that we already know how to solve."
- Subtract 3: 2x = 8.
- Divide by 2: x = 4.
- Check: Left side: 5(4) + 3 = 23. Right side: 3(4) + 11 = 23. Both sides equal 23. Correct.

**Step 3 — Why subtract the smaller term?**
Say: "We subtracted the smaller variable term (3x) so that the remaining variable term (2x) would be positive. You can subtract the larger term instead, but then you will get a negative coefficient, and that is one more thing to deal with. Either approach works — subtracting the smaller term is just a strategy to keep things simpler."

Demonstrate the alternative: subtract 5x from both sides.
- 5x + 3 - 5x = 3x + 11 - 5x
- 3 = -2x + 11
- -8 = -2x
- 4 = x (same answer)

**Step 4 — Example with distribution required first.**
Write on the board: 2(x + 5) = 4x - 6.

Say: "Before we can collect variable terms, we need to distribute."
- Distribute: 2x + 10 = 4x - 6.
- Subtract 2x from both sides: 10 = 2x - 6.
- Add 6: 16 = 2x.
- Divide by 2: 8 = x, or x = 8.
- Check: Left: 2(8 + 5) = 2(13) = 26. Right: 4(8) - 6 = 32 - 6 = 26. Correct.

**Step 5 — Summarize the extended process.**
Write on the board:
1. Distribute (if there are parentheses).
2. Combine like terms on each side (if needed).
3. Move all variable terms to one side (subtract the smaller variable term).
4. Move all constant terms to the other side.
5. Divide to isolate the variable.
6. Check your solution in the ORIGINAL equation.

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Basic variables on both sides**
Solve: 7x - 4 = 3x + 12.
- Subtract 3x: 4x - 4 = 12.
- Add 4: 4x = 16.
- Divide by 4: x = 4.
- Check: 7(4) - 4 = 24. 3(4) + 12 = 24. Correct.

**Example B — Requires distribution first**
Solve: 3(x + 2) = x + 14.
- Distribute: 3x + 6 = x + 14.
- Subtract x: 2x + 6 = 14.
- Subtract 6: 2x = 8.
- Divide by 2: x = 4.
- Check: 3(4 + 2) = 18. 4 + 14 = 18. Correct.

**Example C — Negative coefficients**
Solve: 2x - 9 = 8x + 15.
- Subtract 2x: -9 = 6x + 15.
- Subtract 15: -24 = 6x.
- Divide by 6: -4 = x.
- Check: 2(-4) - 9 = -8 - 9 = -17. 8(-4) + 15 = -32 + 15 = -17. Correct.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. The student attempts each problem first, then discusses.

1. Solve: 6x + 5 = 2x + 21.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Which side has the smaller variable term? Subtract it from both sides."

2. Solve: 9x - 7 = 4x + 18.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "After collecting variable terms, what two-step equation do you have?"

3. Solve: 3(x + 4) = 5x + 2.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Distribute first. Then collect variable terms."

4. Solve: 8x + 1 = 3x - 14.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "The solution will be negative. Work the same process."

5. Solve: 2(3x - 1) = 4(x + 3).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Distribute on BOTH sides first. Then collect variable terms."

**Facilitator tip:** Students often try to move variable terms and constant terms in the same step, leading to errors. Encourage them to do one type of move at a time: first collect all variable terms on one side, THEN collect all constant terms on the other side.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A — Core Practice
Solve each equation and check your solution.

1. 4x + 9 = 2x + 17
2. 7x - 3 = 4x + 15
3. 10x + 2 = 6x + 18

### Part B — Distribution Required
Solve each equation and check your solution.

4. 5(x - 1) = 2x + 13
5. 3(x + 7) = 5x + 9
6. 4(2x + 3) = 6x + 22

### Part C — Mixed Challenges
Solve each equation and check your solution.

7. 2(x + 4) = 3(x - 1)
8. 9x - 5 = 12x + 7
9. 5(x - 2) - 3 = 2(x + 4) - 1
10. A phone plan charges $25 per month plus $0.10 per text. A second plan charges $15 per month plus $0.20 per text. After how many texts do both plans cost the same?

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. 4x + 9 = 2x + 17 → 2x + 9 = 17 → 2x = 8 → x = 4. Check: 4(4) + 9 = 25, 2(4) + 17 = 25. Correct.
2. 7x - 3 = 4x + 15 → 3x - 3 = 15 → 3x = 18 → x = 6. Check: 7(6) - 3 = 39, 4(6) + 15 = 39. Correct.
3. 10x + 2 = 6x + 18 → 4x + 2 = 18 → 4x = 16 → x = 4. Check: 10(4) + 2 = 42, 6(4) + 18 = 42. Correct.

**Part B**
4. 5(x - 1) = 2x + 13 → 5x - 5 = 2x + 13 → 3x - 5 = 13 → 3x = 18 → x = 6. Check: 5(5) = 25, 2(6) + 13 = 25. Correct.
5. 3(x + 7) = 5x + 9 → 3x + 21 = 5x + 9 → 21 = 2x + 9 → 12 = 2x → x = 6. Check: 3(13) = 39, 5(6) + 9 = 39. Correct.
6. 4(2x + 3) = 6x + 22 → 8x + 12 = 6x + 22 → 2x + 12 = 22 → 2x = 10 → x = 5. Check: 4(13) = 52, 6(5) + 22 = 52. Correct.

**Part C**
7. 2(x + 4) = 3(x - 1) → 2x + 8 = 3x - 3 → 8 = x - 3 → 11 = x. Check: 2(15) = 30, 3(10) = 30. Correct.
8. 9x - 5 = 12x + 7 → -5 = 3x + 7 → -12 = 3x → x = -4. Check: 9(-4) - 5 = -41, 12(-4) + 7 = -41. Correct.
9. 5(x - 2) - 3 = 2(x + 4) - 1 → 5x - 10 - 3 = 2x + 8 - 1 → 5x - 13 = 2x + 7 → 3x = 20 → x = 20/3. Check: 5(20/3 - 2) - 3 = 5(14/3) - 3 = 70/3 - 9/3 = 61/3. 2(20/3 + 4) - 1 = 2(32/3) - 1 = 64/3 - 3/3 = 61/3. Correct.
10. Set the two costs equal: 25 + 0.10t = 15 + 0.20t → 10 = 0.10t → t = 100 texts. Check: Plan 1: 25 + 0.10(100) = 35. Plan 2: 15 + 0.20(100) = 35. Both cost $35 at 100 texts. Correct.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** Solve 8x + 3 = 5x + 18. Show your work and check.
**Problem 2:** Solve 4(x - 2) = 2x + 10. Show your work and check.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct (with check) = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

**Exit Ticket Answer Key:**
1. 8x + 3 = 5x + 18 → 3x + 3 = 18 → 3x = 15 → x = 5. Check: 8(5) + 3 = 43, 5(5) + 18 = 43. Correct.
2. 4(x - 2) = 2x + 10 → 4x - 8 = 2x + 10 → 2x - 8 = 10 → 2x = 18 → x = 9. Check: 4(7) = 28, 2(9) + 10 = 28. Correct.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Revisit the concept using a visual model: draw a balance scale with variable terms and constants on both sides. Show how removing the same number of x-blocks from both sides keeps the scale balanced.
- Have the student use color-coding: underline all variable terms in one color and all constant terms in another, then physically rewrite the equation with all variable terms on the left and all constant terms on the right.
- Provide three additional equations with the first step (variable collection) already completed.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Challenge: "What happens if, after collecting variable terms, you get 0x = 5? Or 0x = 0? What would these results mean?" (Preview of Lesson 4 — special cases.)
- Ask the student to write an equation with variables on both sides that has the solution x = -2.

## Extension

- **Real-World Comparison:** Research two competing services (e.g., streaming plans, gym memberships) that have different fee structures. Write an equation with variables on both sides to find when the costs are equal. Solve and interpret.
- **Always, Sometimes, Never:** Decide if these statements are always true, sometimes true, or never true, and explain:
  - "An equation with variables on both sides has exactly one solution." (Sometimes — it could have no solution or infinitely many.)
  - "The variable terms can be collected on either side and you will get the same answer." (Always.)
