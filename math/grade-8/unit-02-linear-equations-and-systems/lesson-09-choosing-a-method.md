# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 2, Lesson 9

## Topic
Choosing a Method for Solving Systems

## Learning Objectives
- Analyze a system of equations and decide which method (graphing, substitution, or elimination) is most efficient.
- Articulate reasons for choosing one method over another based on the structure of the equations.
- Solve the same system using two different methods and confirm the results match.
- Develop flexibility in approaching systems of equations.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to solve systems by graphing (Lesson 6)
- Ability to solve systems by substitution (Lesson 7)
- Ability to solve systems by elimination (Lesson 8)
- Comfort with all prerequisite algebra skills (distribution, combining like terms, integer operations)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Graph paper (for systems where graphing is chosen).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 8.EE.8b — Solve systems of two linear equations in two variables algebraically, and estimate solutions by graphing the equations. Solve simple cases by inspection. 8.EE.8c — Solve real-world and mathematical problems leading to two linear equations in two variables.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review all three methods and prompt strategic thinking.

Present this system: y = x + 3 and x + y = 9.

1. Ask: "Could you solve this by graphing?" (Yes, graph both lines.)
2. Ask: "Could you solve this by substitution?" (Yes, the first equation already gives y = x + 3.)
3. Ask: "Could you solve this by elimination?" (Yes, rewrite the first as -x + y = 3, then add to x + y = 9.)
4. Ask: "Which method seems fastest here? Why?" (Substitution — because y is already isolated.)
5. Solve it using the fastest method. (Substitute: x + (x + 3) = 9 → 2x = 6 → x = 3, y = 6. Solution: (3, 6).)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Efficiency** — in this context, choosing the method that requires the fewest steps and minimizes the chance of errors.
- **Structure** — the arrangement of the equations (which variables are isolated, what the coefficients look like) that makes one method more natural than another.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — When graphing is the best choice.**
Say: "Graphing is the best choice when:"
- Both equations are in slope-intercept form (y = mx + b) with simple slopes.
- You want a visual understanding of the solution (seeing where lines cross).
- You want to estimate a solution or check if the solution is reasonable.

Say: "Graphing is NOT the best choice when:"
- The solution involves fractions or decimals (hard to read from a graph).
- You need an exact answer.
- The equations are not easily graphed.

Example where graphing works well:
- y = 2x - 1 and y = -x + 5. (Simple slopes, integer intersection expected.)

**Step 2 — When substitution is the best choice.**
Say: "Substitution is the best choice when:"
- One equation is already solved for a variable (y = ... or x = ...).
- One variable has a coefficient of 1 or -1, making it easy to isolate.
- The system involves simple expressions.

Example where substitution works well:
- y = 3x - 4 and 2x + y = 16. (y is already isolated in equation 1.)

Example where substitution works well:
- x + 3y = 15 and 2x - y = 1. (x in equation 1 has coefficient 1 — easy to isolate as x = 15 - 3y.)

**Step 3 — When elimination is the best choice.**
Say: "Elimination is the best choice when:"
- The coefficients of one variable are already the same or opposite.
- Neither variable is easily isolated (both have coefficients other than 1 or -1).
- The equations are in standard form (ax + by = c).

Example where elimination works well:
- 3x + 2y = 14 and 5x - 2y = 10. (The y-coefficients are +2 and -2 — just add.)

Example where elimination works well:
- 4x + 3y = 22 and 6x + 5y = 34. (Neither variable is easy to isolate, but multiplying to align coefficients is straightforward.)

**Step 4 — The decision guide.**
Write on the board:

| Look for... | Choose... |
|---|---|
| One variable already isolated (y = ... or x = ...) | Substitution |
| One variable with coefficient 1 or -1 | Substitution (isolate first) |
| Coefficients of one variable are same or opposite | Elimination (add or subtract) |
| Both equations in standard form with no easy isolation | Elimination (multiply and add) |
| Both in slope-intercept form and you want a visual | Graphing |
| You need an estimate or a visual check | Graphing |

Say: "This is a guide, not a rule. All three methods give the same answer. The question is which one gets you there with the least work."

**Step 5 — Demonstrate solving one system two ways.**
System: 2x + y = 10 and x - y = 2.

Method 1 — Elimination:
- Add: 3x = 12 → x = 4. y = 10 - 2(4) = 2. Solution: (4, 2).

Method 2 — Substitution:
- From equation 2: x = y + 2.
- Substitute: 2(y + 2) + y = 10 → 2y + 4 + y = 10 → 3y = 6 → y = 2. x = 4.
- Solution: (4, 2).

Say: "Both methods give the same answer. Elimination was slightly faster here because the y-coefficients were already opposites. But substitution was also very manageable."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Choose and justify**
System: y = 5x - 2 and 3x + y = 22.
- Best method: Substitution — y is already isolated.
- Solve: 3x + (5x - 2) = 22 → 8x = 24 → x = 3, y = 13.
- Solution: (3, 13). Check: 13 = 15 - 2, 9 + 13 = 22. Correct.

**Example B — Choose and justify**
System: 4x + 5y = 23 and 4x - y = 5.
- Best method: Elimination — x-coefficients are the same (4 and 4). Subtract.
- Subtract: 6y = 18 → y = 3. 4x - 3 = 5 → x = 2.
- Solution: (2, 3). Check: 8 + 15 = 23, 8 - 3 = 5. Correct.

**Example C — Two methods compared**
System: x + 2y = 11 and 3x - 2y = 5.

Elimination: Add: 4x = 16 → x = 4. 4 + 2y = 11 → y = 3.5.
Substitution: From eq 1: x = 11 - 2y. Sub: 3(11 - 2y) - 2y = 5 → 33 - 6y - 2y = 5 → -8y = -28 → y = 3.5. x = 4.
Solution: (4, 3.5). Both methods agree. Elimination was faster (fewer steps).

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

For each system, (a) state which method you would choose and why, then (b) solve it.

1. y = -2x + 7 and y = 3x - 8.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Both equations are solved for y. Which method does that suggest?"

2. 5x + 3y = 21 and 5x - y = 9.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Look at the x-coefficients. Are they the same or opposite?"

3. x = 4y - 1 and 3x + 2y = 25.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "x is already isolated. Which method takes advantage of that?"

4. 2x + 7y = 24 and 3x + 7y = 31.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "The y-coefficients are the same. What is the quickest move?"

5. y = x + 1 and 2x + 3y = 23.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Choose your method, solve, and verify."

**Facilitator tip:** Some students will gravitate to one method and resist using others. Encourage flexibility by occasionally requiring them to solve with a specified method, even if it is not their first choice. The goal is that they CAN use all three, even though they may prefer one.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A — Choose and Solve
For each system, state which method you would use and why. Then solve.

1. y = 4x - 9 and y = -x + 6
2. 6x + y = 17 and 2x + y = 9
3. x + y = 8 and y = 2x + 2

### Part B — Solve Using Two Methods
Solve each system using two different methods. Confirm you get the same answer.

4. x + y = 10 and 2x - y = 5
5. y = x - 3 and 3x + 2y = 19

### Part C — Application and Reasoning

6. 3x + 4y = 28 and 5x - 2y = 6. Choose a method and solve.
7. y = (1/2)x + 3 and y = -(1/2)x + 7. Choose a method and solve.
8. A student says: "I always use substitution because it works every time." Is the student correct that substitution always works? Why might it not always be the BEST choice?
9. Write a system of equations that would be most efficiently solved by elimination. Solve it.
10. Write a system of equations that would be most efficiently solved by substitution. Solve it.

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. Best method: Substitution (both solved for y). Set equal: 4x - 9 = -x + 6 → 5x = 15 → x = 3, y = 3. **Solution: (3, 3).** Check: 3 = 12 - 9, 3 = -3 + 6. Correct.
2. Best method: Elimination (y-coefficients are the same). Subtract eq 2 from eq 1: 4x = 8 → x = 2. 2(2) + y = 9 → y = 5. **Solution: (2, 5).** Check: 12 + 5 = 17, 4 + 5 = 9. Correct.
3. Best method: Substitution (y is isolated in eq 2). Substitute: x + (2x + 2) = 8 → 3x + 2 = 8 → 3x = 6 → x = 2, y = 6. **Solution: (2, 6).** Check: 2 + 6 = 8, 6 = 4 + 2. Correct.

**Part B**
4. Elimination: Add: 3x = 15 → x = 5. y = 5. Solution: (5, 5). Substitution: From eq 1: y = 10 - x. Sub: 2x - (10 - x) = 5 → 3x = 15 → x = 5, y = 5. **Both give (5, 5).** Check: 5 + 5 = 10, 10 - 5 = 5. Correct.
5. Substitution: 3x + 2(x - 3) = 19 → 5x - 6 = 19 → 5x = 25 → x = 5, y = 2. Elimination: Rewrite eq 1 as -x + y = -3. Multiply by 3: -3x + 3y = -9. Eq 2: 3x + 2y = 19. Add: 5y = 10 → y = 2, x = 5. **Both give (5, 2).** Check: 2 = 5 - 3, 15 + 4 = 19. Correct.

**Part C**
6. Elimination: Multiply eq 2 by 2: 10x - 4y = 12. Add to eq 1: 13x = 40 → x = 40/13. This gives fractions. Alternative: multiply eq 1 by 1, eq 2 by 2 to eliminate y: 3x + 4y = 28 and 10x - 4y = 12. Add: 13x = 40 → x = 40/13, y = (28 - 120/13)/4 = (364/13 - 120/13)/4 = (244/13)/4 = 61/13. **Solution: (40/13, 61/13).** Check: 120/13 + 244/13 = 364/13 = 28. 200/13 - 122/13 = 78/13 = 6. Correct.
7. Substitution (both solved for y): (1/2)x + 3 = -(1/2)x + 7 → x = 4, y = 5. **Solution: (4, 5).** Check: 2 + 3 = 5, -2 + 7 = 5. Correct.
8. The student is correct that substitution always works in theory — you can always isolate a variable and substitute. However, if neither variable has a coefficient of 1 or -1, isolating creates fractions, which makes the algebra more complex. Elimination may be more efficient in those cases because you can work with integer coefficients throughout.
9. Answers will vary. Example: 4x + 3y = 22 and 4x - y = 10. Subtract: 4y = 12 → y = 3. 4x - 3 = 10 → x = 13/4. (Or choose coefficients that yield integer answers.)
10. Answers will vary. Example: y = 3x + 2 and x + y = 14. Substitute: x + 3x + 2 = 14 → 4x = 12 → x = 3, y = 11. Solution: (3, 11).

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** For the system y = 2x + 1 and 5x - y = 8, which method would you choose? Justify your choice and then solve.
**Problem 2:** For the system 3x + y = 13 and 3x - 2y = 4, which method would you choose? Justify your choice and then solve.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct (with justified method choice) = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

**Exit Ticket Answer Key:**
1. Substitution (y is isolated). Substitute: 5x - (2x + 1) = 8 → 3x - 1 = 8 → 3x = 9 → x = 3, y = 7. **Solution: (3, 7).** Check: 7 = 7, 15 - 7 = 8. Correct.
2. Elimination (x-coefficients are the same). Subtract eq 2 from eq 1: 3y = 9 → y = 3. 3x + 3 = 13 → x = 10/3. Hmm, let me verify: 3(10/3) - 2(3) = 10 - 6 = 4. Correct. **Solution: (10/3, 3).** Alternatively, substitution from eq 1: y = 13 - 3x → 3x - 2(13 - 3x) = 4 → 9x - 26 = 4 → 9x = 30 → x = 10/3. Same answer.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the individual method that the student struggles with most (graphing, substitution, or elimination) and practice that method exclusively for 3-5 problems.
- Provide a decision flowchart: "Is a variable already isolated? → Yes → Use substitution. No → Are coefficients the same or opposite? → Yes → Use elimination. No → Can you easily isolate a variable? → Yes → Use substitution. No → Multiply and use elimination."
- Have the student solve the same system two different ways to build confidence that all methods yield the same answer.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Challenge: Present a system where all three methods are approximately equally efficient and have the student explain their reasoning for whichever method they choose.
- Preview Lesson 10: "Now that you can solve systems efficiently, we will apply these skills to real-world problems where you must first SET UP the system from a word problem."

## Extension

- **Speed Challenge:** Time yourself solving the system 2x + 3y = 13 and x - y = 1 using each of the three methods. Which was fastest? Does speed correlate with the "best method" guidelines from the lesson?
- **Debate:** Prepare a short argument for why one method is the "best overall." Consider: ease of use, reliability, speed, and applicability to different types of systems. (There is no single right answer — the point is to articulate reasoning.)
