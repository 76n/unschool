# Math — Grade 8 — Unit 2, Lesson 10

## Topic
Systems of Equations Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
- Translate real-world situations into systems of two linear equations.
- Define variables clearly and write two equations that represent the constraints in a problem.
- Solve the system using an appropriate method (substitution or elimination).
- Interpret the solution in the context of the original problem and check that it makes sense.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to solve systems by substitution (Lesson 7) and elimination (Lesson 8)
- Ability to choose an efficient method (Lesson 9)
- Experience writing simple equations from word problems (Lessons 1-3)
- Comfort with real-world contexts involving money, quantities, rates, and mixtures

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 8.EE.8c — Solve real-world and mathematical problems leading to two linear equations in two variables. For example, given coordinates for two pairs of points, determine whether the line through the first pair of points intersects the line through the second pair.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Practice translating verbal descriptions into equations.

1. Write an equation: "The total number of cats and dogs is 15." (c + d = 15)
2. Write an equation: "Cats cost $12 each and dogs cost $20 each, and the total cost is $220." (12c + 20d = 220)
3. Ask: "Now we have two equations and two unknowns. What do we call this?" (A system of equations.)
4. Ask: "What do c and d represent? Why is it important to define your variables before writing equations?"

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Define variables** — state clearly what each variable represents, including units (e.g., "Let x = the number of adult tickets sold").
- **Constraint** — a condition or restriction in the problem that can be expressed as an equation (e.g., "the total is 100" becomes x + y = 100).
- **Interpret** — explain what the solution means in the real-world context of the problem.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — The process for setting up a system from a word problem.**
Write on the board:
1. **Read** the problem carefully. Identify what is unknown.
2. **Define** variables. Write "Let x = ..." and "Let y = ..." with clear descriptions.
3. **Write** two equations based on two different relationships (constraints) in the problem.
4. **Solve** the system using substitution or elimination.
5. **Interpret** the answer in context. Use complete sentences.
6. **Check** the answer against the original problem (not just the equations).

**Step 2 — Example: Ticket sales.**
Problem: A school sold a total of 150 tickets for a concert. Adult tickets cost $10 and student tickets cost $6. The total revenue was $1,260. How many adult tickets and how many student tickets were sold?

Define variables:
- Let a = number of adult tickets sold.
- Let s = number of student tickets sold.

Write two equations:
- Total tickets: a + s = 150
- Total revenue: 10a + 6s = 1,260

Solve (elimination):
- Multiply equation 1 by -6: -6a - 6s = -900.
- Add to equation 2: 4a = 360 → a = 90.
- Back-substitute: 90 + s = 150 → s = 60.

Interpret: 90 adult tickets and 60 student tickets were sold.

Check: 90 + 60 = 150 tickets. 10(90) + 6(60) = 900 + 360 = 1,260. Correct.

**Step 3 — Example: Mixture problem.**
Problem: A coffee shop mixes premium beans at $12 per pound with regular beans at $8 per pound. They want to make 20 pounds of a blend that costs $9.50 per pound. How many pounds of each type should they use?

Define variables:
- Let p = pounds of premium beans.
- Let r = pounds of regular beans.

Write two equations:
- Total weight: p + r = 20
- Total cost: 12p + 8r = 9.50 x 20 = 190

Solve (substitution — from eq 1, r = 20 - p):
- 12p + 8(20 - p) = 190
- 12p + 160 - 8p = 190
- 4p = 30
- p = 7.5

Back-substitute: r = 20 - 7.5 = 12.5.

Interpret: Use 7.5 pounds of premium beans and 12.5 pounds of regular beans.

Check: 7.5 + 12.5 = 20 pounds. 12(7.5) + 8(12.5) = 90 + 100 = 190. And 190/20 = $9.50. Correct.

**Step 4 — Example: Rate/distance problem.**
Problem: Two friends leave the same point walking in opposite directions. One walks at 4 mph and the other at 3 mph. After how many hours are they 21 miles apart?

Define variables:
- Let t = time in hours (both walk for the same time).
- Distance person 1: d1 = 4t
- Distance person 2: d2 = 3t
- Combined distance: 4t + 3t = 21

This is actually a single equation: 7t = 21, so t = 3 hours.

Say: "Not every word problem requires a system. This one has only one unknown (time), so one equation is enough. A system is needed when you have TWO unknowns."

Revised rate problem: Two trains leave a station. Train A travels at 60 mph and Train B travels at 80 mph. Train B leaves 1 hour after Train A. When does Train B catch Train A?

Define variables:
- Let t = time (hours) that Train A has been traveling.
- Distance Train A: 60t
- Distance Train B: 80(t - 1) (it started 1 hour later)

Set distances equal: 60t = 80(t - 1) → 60t = 80t - 80 → -20t = -80 → t = 4.

Interpret: Train B catches Train A after Train A has been traveling for 4 hours (Train B traveled for 3 hours). Distance: 60(4) = 240 miles. Check: 80(3) = 240 miles. Correct.

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Total and value**
A store sells pens for $2 and notebooks for $5. A customer buys 11 items and spends $40. How many pens and notebooks were purchased?
- Let p = pens, n = notebooks.
- p + n = 11 and 2p + 5n = 40.
- From eq 1: p = 11 - n. Sub: 2(11 - n) + 5n = 40 → 22 - 2n + 5n = 40 → 3n = 18 → n = 6. p = 5.
- **5 pens and 6 notebooks.** Check: 5 + 6 = 11, 10 + 30 = 40. Correct.

**Example B — Comparison/break-even**
Company A charges $200 plus $15 per hour. Company B charges $50 plus $25 per hour. When do they cost the same?
- Let h = hours, c = total cost.
- c = 15h + 200 and c = 25h + 50.
- Set equal: 15h + 200 = 25h + 50 → 150 = 10h → h = 15.
- c = 15(15) + 200 = 425.
- **After 15 hours, both cost $425.** Check: 25(15) + 50 = 425. Correct.

**Example C — Age problem**
The sum of Maria's and Tom's ages is 40. Maria is 6 years older than Tom. How old is each person?
- Let m = Maria's age, t = Tom's age.
- m + t = 40 and m = t + 6.
- Sub: (t + 6) + t = 40 → 2t + 6 = 40 → 2t = 34 → t = 17. m = 23.
- **Maria is 23, Tom is 17.** Check: 23 + 17 = 40, 23 - 17 = 6. Correct.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. The student attempts each problem first, then discusses.

1. A farmer has chickens and cows. There are 30 animals total and 82 legs total. How many chickens and how many cows? (Chickens have 2 legs, cows have 4.)
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What are the two unknowns? What are the two constraints (total animals, total legs)?"

2. Two numbers add up to 25. One number is 7 more than the other. Find both numbers.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Let x and y be the two numbers. Write two equations."

3. A phone plan costs $40 per month plus $0.05 per text. Another plan costs $30 per month plus $0.10 per text. At how many texts per month do the plans cost the same?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Write an equation for each plan's monthly cost, then set them equal."

4. A chemistry class mixes a 10% acid solution with a 30% acid solution to make 50 mL of a 22% acid solution. How much of each should be used?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "The two constraints are total volume and total amount of acid."

5. Solve Problem 4: Define variables, write the system, solve, and interpret.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Let x = mL of 10% solution, y = mL of 30% solution. Total volume: x + y = 50. Total acid: 0.10x + 0.30y = 0.22(50) = 11."

**Facilitator tip:** The hardest part of word problems is setting up the equations, not solving them. Spend more time helping the student identify the two constraints and translate them into equations. Once the system is written, solving is a skill they already have.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A — Total and Value Problems

1. A school store sells erasers for $0.50 and markers for $1.25. A student buys 10 items and spends $8.75. How many erasers and markers were purchased?
2. A vending machine collected a total of 50 coins, all quarters and dimes. The total value is $9.80. How many quarters and how many dimes are there?

### Part B — Comparison and Break-Even

3. Gym A charges a $100 enrollment fee and $30 per month. Gym B charges no enrollment fee but $45 per month. After how many months will both gyms have cost the same total?
4. Two friends are saving money. Ava has $120 and saves $25 per week. Ben has $45 and saves $40 per week. After how many weeks will they have the same amount? How much will each have?

### Part C — Mixed Problem Types

5. The perimeter of a rectangle is 56 cm. The length is 4 cm more than the width. Find the length and width.
6. A boat travels 30 miles upstream in 3 hours and 30 miles downstream in 2 hours. Find the speed of the boat in still water and the speed of the current. (Hint: upstream speed = boat speed - current; downstream speed = boat speed + current.)
7. On a test with 25 questions, each correct answer earns 4 points and each incorrect answer loses 1 point. A student scores 75 points. How many questions did the student answer correctly?
8. A grocer mixes cashews at $9 per pound with peanuts at $4 per pound to make 10 pounds of mixed nuts costing $5.50 per pound. How many pounds of each type of nut should be used?

### Part D — Challenge

9. Maria is twice as old as her daughter. In 10 years, Maria will be 1.5 times as old as her daughter. How old is each person now?
10. A car traveling at 55 mph leaves a city. Two hours later, a second car traveling at 70 mph leaves the same city in the same direction. How long after the second car leaves will it catch the first car?

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. Let e = erasers, m = markers. e + m = 10 and 0.50e + 1.25m = 8.75. From eq 1: e = 10 - m. Sub: 0.50(10 - m) + 1.25m = 8.75 → 5 - 0.50m + 1.25m = 8.75 → 0.75m = 3.75 → m = 5. e = 5. **5 erasers and 5 markers.** Check: 5 + 5 = 10, 2.50 + 6.25 = 8.75. Correct.
2. Let q = quarters, d = dimes. q + d = 50 and 0.25q + 0.10d = 9.80. Multiply eq 2 by 100: 25q + 10d = 980. From eq 1: d = 50 - q. Sub: 25q + 10(50 - q) = 980 → 25q + 500 - 10q = 980 → 15q = 480 → q = 32. d = 18. **32 quarters, 18 dimes.** Check: 32 + 18 = 50, 8.00 + 1.80 = 9.80. Correct.

**Part B**
3. Let m = months. Gym A: 100 + 30m. Gym B: 45m. Set equal: 100 + 30m = 45m → 100 = 15m → m = 20/3 ≈ 6.67. Since months are whole numbers, they will not cost exactly the same at a whole month. At month 6: A costs 280, B costs 270. At month 7: A costs 310, B costs 315. **After approximately 6.67 months (between months 6 and 7), both plans cost the same.** Exact equal point: 100 + 30(20/3) = 300 = 45(20/3). Both equal $300.
4. Let w = weeks. Ava: 120 + 25w. Ben: 45 + 40w. Set equal: 120 + 25w = 45 + 40w → 75 = 15w → w = 5. Amount: 120 + 25(5) = 245. **After 5 weeks, both have $245.** Check: 45 + 40(5) = 245. Correct.

**Part C**
5. Let l = length, w = width. 2l + 2w = 56 and l = w + 4. Sub: 2(w + 4) + 2w = 56 → 2w + 8 + 2w = 56 → 4w = 48 → w = 12. l = 16. **Length = 16 cm, width = 12 cm.** Check: 2(16) + 2(12) = 56, 16 = 12 + 4. Correct.
6. Let b = boat speed, c = current speed. Upstream: b - c = 30/3 = 10. Downstream: b + c = 30/2 = 15. Add: 2b = 25 → b = 12.5. c = 2.5. **Boat speed: 12.5 mph, current: 2.5 mph.** Check: 12.5 - 2.5 = 10 mph, 10 * 3 = 30 mi. 12.5 + 2.5 = 15 mph, 15 * 2 = 30 mi. Correct.
7. Let c = correct, w = wrong. c + w = 25 and 4c - w = 75. Add: 5c = 100 → c = 20. w = 5. **20 correct, 5 incorrect.** Check: 4(20) - 5 = 75. Correct.
8. Let c = pounds of cashews, p = pounds of peanuts. c + p = 10 and 9c + 4p = 55 (since 5.50 x 10 = 55). From eq 1: p = 10 - c. Sub: 9c + 4(10 - c) = 55 → 9c + 40 - 4c = 55 → 5c = 15 → c = 3. p = 7. **3 pounds cashews, 7 pounds peanuts.** Check: 27 + 28 = 55. 55/10 = 5.50. Correct.

**Part D**
9. Let m = Maria's age, d = daughter's age. m = 2d and m + 10 = 1.5(d + 10). Sub: 2d + 10 = 1.5d + 15 → 0.5d = 5 → d = 10. m = 20. **Maria is 20, daughter is 10.** Check: In 10 years: 30 and 20, and 30 = 1.5(20). Correct.
10. Let t = hours after the second car leaves. First car distance: 55(t + 2) (it had a 2-hour head start). Second car distance: 70t. Set equal: 55(t + 2) = 70t → 55t + 110 = 70t → 110 = 15t → t = 22/3 ≈ 7.33 hours. **The second car catches the first after 7 hours and 20 minutes.** Check: First car: 55(22/3 + 2) = 55(28/3) = 1540/3 ≈ 513.3 mi. Second car: 70(22/3) = 1540/3. Correct.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
**Problem 1:** A theater sold 200 tickets. Adult tickets were $12 and child tickets were $7. Total revenue was $2,050. How many adult tickets and how many child tickets were sold? Define variables, write a system, and solve.
**Problem 2:** The sum of two numbers is 28 and their difference is 6. Find the two numbers.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct (with variables defined and work shown) = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

**Exit Ticket Answer Key:**
1. Let a = adult, c = child. a + c = 200 and 12a + 7c = 2050. From eq 1: c = 200 - a. Sub: 12a + 7(200 - a) = 2050 → 12a + 1400 - 7a = 2050 → 5a = 650 → a = 130. c = 70. **130 adult, 70 child.** Check: 130 + 70 = 200, 1560 + 490 = 2050. Correct.
2. Let x and y be the numbers. x + y = 28 and x - y = 6. Add: 2x = 34 → x = 17. y = 11. **The numbers are 17 and 11.** Check: 17 + 11 = 28, 17 - 11 = 6. Correct.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Separate the two skills: setting up systems and solving systems. If the student can solve but struggles to set up, focus exclusively on translating word problems into equations (without solving them) for 5-10 problems.
- Provide a template: "What are the two unknowns?" → "What is the first relationship?" → "What is the second relationship?" → "Write the equations."
- Start with simpler contexts (two numbers with a sum and a difference) before progressing to ticket, mixture, and rate problems.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Challenge: Create a word problem that leads to a system with no solution. What would that mean in real-world terms? (Example: "A store sells 20 items for $100. Pens cost $5 and pencils cost $5 each. How many of each?" → p + n = 20 and 5p + 5n = 100. Since both equations say the same thing scaled, there are infinitely many combinations. To make it "no solution," change the total to $110: 5p + 5n = 110 → p + n = 22, contradicting p + n = 20.)
- Preview the unit assessment: "You now have all the skills needed for the unit assessment. Review your notes and practice any type of problem you found challenging."
