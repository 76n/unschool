# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 4

## Topic
Near-Doubles (Doubles Plus 1, Doubles Plus 2)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Recognize a near-double as a problem where the addends differ by 1 or 2
- Use a known double to solve a near-double (e.g., 6 + 7 = 6 + 6 + 1 = 13)
- Decide when to use the near-doubles strategy versus counting on

## Prerequisites
- Doubles facts 1+1 through 10+10 (Lesson 3)
- Counting on (Lesson 2)

## Materials Needed
- 20 counting objects in two colors
- Double ten-frame
- Paper and pencil
- Doubles chart (from Lesson 3)

**Purpose:** Review doubles and counting on.

1. "Double 6?" (12) *(Lesson 3)*
2. "Double 8?" (16) *(Lesson 3)*
3. "Count on: 9 + 3?" (12) *(Lesson 2)*
4. "What makes 10 with 7?" (3)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show how doubles can solve "almost-doubles" problems.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Near-double:** An addition problem where the two numbers are close together (differ by 1 or 2)
- **Doubles plus 1:** A near-double where one addend is 1 more than the other (e.g., 5 + 6)
- **Doubles plus 2:** A near-double where one addend is 2 more than the other (e.g., 5 + 7)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: From doubles to near-doubles.**
Build 6 + 6 with two rows of 6: "6 + 6 = 12. I know this."

Now add 1 more to the bottom row to make 6 + 7: "Look — this is almost double 6. It's 6 + 6 = 12, plus 1 more = 13."

"So 6 + 7 = 13. I used my doubles fact and added 1."

**Step 2: More examples.**
- 7 + 8: "That's near double 7. 7 + 7 = 14, plus 1 more = 15."
- 5 + 6: "Near double 5. 5 + 5 = 10, plus 1 more = 11."
- 4 + 5: "Near double 4. 4 + 4 = 8, plus 1 = 9."

Build each with objects to show the extra 1.

**Step 3: Doubles plus 2.**
- 6 + 8: "These are 2 apart. Near double 6: 6 + 6 = 12, plus 2 = 14." Or: "Near double 7: 7 + 7 = 14. Take away... hmm, that's harder. Let's use 6 + 6 + 2 = 14."

Show with objects: row of 6, row of 8. Point to the extra 2 in the longer row.

**Step 4: How to spot a near-double.**
"Look at the two numbers. If they're right next to each other (like 6 and 7) or very close (like 5 and 7), try the near-doubles strategy. Use the smaller number as your double, then add the extra."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 8 + 9 = ? Think: 8 + 8 = 16, plus 1 more = 17.

**Example 2:** 7 + 5 = ? Think: 5 + 5 = 10, plus 2 more = 12.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student uses near-doubles with support.

**Activity 1: Identify the double (3 minutes)**
For each problem, student identifies the nearby double first:
- 5 + 6 → "What's the double?" (5+5=10) "Plus how many more?" (1) "So?" (11)
- 7 + 8 → Double? (7+7=14) Plus? (1) Answer? (15)
- 9 + 8 → Double? (8+8=16) Plus? (1) Answer? (17)

**Activity 2: Build it (4 minutes)**
Student builds each near-double with objects:
- 4 + 5: Build 4 and 4, then add 1 more → 9
- 6 + 8: Build 6 and 6, then add 2 more → 14
- 3 + 4: Build 3 and 3, then add 1 → 7

**Activity 3: Which strategy? (3 minutes)**
Present problems. Student decides: count on, or near-doubles?
- 9 + 2 → Count on (small addend) → 11
- 7 + 8 → Near-doubles (close numbers) → 15
- 6 + 1 → Count on → 7
- 5 + 6 → Near-doubles → 11

*Facilitator prompt: "If one number is small (1, 2, or 3), count on. If both numbers are big and close together, use near-doubles."*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Apply near-doubles independently.

**★ On-level:**
1. 5 + 6 = ___ (11)
2. 7 + 8 = ___ (15)
3. 4 + 5 = ___ (9)
4. 6 + 7 = ___ (13)
5. 8 + 9 = ___ (17)

**★★ Stretch:**
6. 9 + 7 = ___ (16) *(Doubles +2: 8+8=16, or 7+7+2=16)*
7. 6 + 8 = ___ (14)
8. "What double did you use to solve 7 + 8?" (7+7=14, plus 1)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "Can you solve 8 + 9 using two different strategies?" (Near-double: 8+8+1=17. Count on: start at 9, count 8... less efficient but works.)
10. "What is 10 + 9? Can you use near-doubles?" (10+10=20, minus 1=19. Or 9+9=18, plus 1=19.)

### Interleaved Review
- "Double 5?" (10) *(Lesson 3)*
- "7 + 3 = ___?" (10) *(Make-10 review)*
- "10 − 4 = ___?" (6) *(Subtraction review)*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess near-doubles strategy.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "6 + 7 = ___? What double did you use?" (13; double 6 = 12, plus 1)
2. "8 + 9 = ___?" (17)
3. "Is 5 + 6 a near-double? How do you know?" (Yes — 5 and 6 are 1 apart)

**Mastery = At least 2 of 3 correct, with ability to identify the related double.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Ensure doubles facts are solid first — near-doubles won't work if the underlying double is unknown.
- Use objects to make the "plus 1 more" visible. Build the double, then physically add the extra.
- Practice only doubles-plus-1 before introducing doubles-plus-2.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 5 (Making 10 to Add).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Near-doubles expand the power of doubles.** Once a student knows 10 doubles facts, near-doubles give them 18 more facts (each double unlocks two near-doubles: e.g., knowing 7+7=14 gives you 7+8=15 and 6+7=13). That's 28 facts from 10 memorized facts. This is the power of strategies over memorization.

**Students may prefer different strategies.** Some students will love near-doubles. Others will find counting-on easier for certain problems. Both are valid. The goal is having multiple strategies available, not mandating one. Let the student choose what makes sense for each problem.

**Doubles plus 2 is optional at this stage.** Doubles-plus-1 is the core strategy. Doubles-plus-2 is introduced here but may be too abstract for some students. If the student handles doubles-plus-1 well but struggles with plus-2, that's fine — revisit plus-2 later.
