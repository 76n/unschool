# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 5

## Topic
Making 10 to Add

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Decompose one addend to make a 10 with the other addend (e.g., 8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13)
- Use the ten-frame to visualize making 10
- Apply the Make 10 strategy to addition problems with sums between 11 and 20

## Prerequisites
- Fluent with make-10 pairs (1+9, 2+8, 3+7, 4+6, 5+5) — Lesson 1
- Addition within 10 (Lesson 1)
- Counting on (Lesson 2)

## Materials Needed
- 20 counting objects in two colors
- Two ten-frames (double ten-frame)
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Make-10 pairs must be automatic for this strategy.

1. "What makes 10 with 6?" (4) "With 8?" (2) "With 3?" (7) "With 9?" (1)
2. "Double 7?" (14) *(Lesson 3)*
3. "6 + 7?" (13, using near-doubles) *(Lesson 4)*

If the student hesitates on make-10 pairs, spend extra time on this before proceeding.

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the Make 10 strategy step by step using the ten-frame.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Make 10:** A strategy where you break apart one number to fill the other up to 10, then add the rest
- **Decompose:** Break a number into smaller parts (e.g., 5 = 2 + 3)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Set up the problem visually.**
Write: 8 + 5 = ?

Place 8 red counters on the first ten-frame (top row full, 3 on bottom). Place 5 blue counters next to it (not on a frame yet).

"I have 8, and I need to add 5. The ten-frame has 2 empty spaces. If I fill those 2 spaces, I'll have 10!"

**Step 2: Move objects to make 10.**
Take 2 of the 5 blue counters and place them on the first ten-frame, filling it to 10.

"I took 2 from the 5 to make 10. How many blue counters are left?" (3.)

"So now I have 10 + 3. That's easy: 13!"

Write: 8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13.

**Step 3: Repeat with another problem.**
Write: 9 + 4 = ?

Place 9 on the ten-frame. "How many empty spaces?" (1.)
Take 1 from the 4. "Now I have 10. How many left?" (3.)
"10 + 3 = 13."

Write: 9 + 4 = 9 + 1 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13.

**Step 4: One more, student-guided.**
Write: 7 + 5 = ?

"7 is on the ten-frame. How many empty spaces?" (3.)
"Take 3 from the 5. How many left?" (2.)
"10 + 2 = 12!"

**Step 5: Summarize the steps.**
"Make 10 has three steps:
1. Look at the bigger number. How many more to make 10?
2. Break the other number apart to get that many.
3. 10 + the leftover = your answer."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 6 + 7. Ten-frame shows 7. Need 3 more for 10. Take 3 from 6, leaving 3. 10 + 3 = 13.

**Example 2:** 9 + 6. Need 1 more for 10. Take 1 from 6, leaving 5. 10 + 5 = 15.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices Make 10 with the ten-frame.

**Activity 1: Ten-frame Make 10 (6 minutes)**
For each problem, student uses the double ten-frame:

- 8 + 4: "How many to make 10?" (2) "Break 4 into 2 + 2. 10 + 2 = 12." ✓
- 9 + 5: "How many to make 10?" (1) "Break 5 into 1 + 4. 10 + 4 = 14." ✓
- 7 + 6: "How many to make 10?" (3) "Break 6 into 3 + 3. 10 + 3 = 13." ✓
- 8 + 7: (2) Break 7 into 2 + 5. 10 + 5 = 15. ✓
- 6 + 5: (4) Break 5 into 4 + 1. 10 + 1 = 11. ✓

*Facilitator prompt: Always start by filling the bigger number on the ten-frame. Ask "How many empty spaces?" every time. This becomes the key question.*

**Activity 2: Make 10 without the frame (4 minutes)**
Student tries solving mentally (ten-frame available for checking):

- 9 + 3: 9 + 1 + 2 = 10 + 2 = 12 ✓
- 8 + 6: 8 + 2 + 4 = 10 + 4 = 14 ✓

*Facilitator prompt: If the student gets stuck, go back to the ten-frame. No shame in that — it's the learning tool.*

---

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Apply Make 10 independently.

**★ On-level:**
1. 9 + 4 = ___ (13) Show your thinking: 9 + ___ + ___ = 10 + ___ = ___
2. 8 + 5 = ___ (13)
3. 7 + 4 = ___ (11)
4. 9 + 6 = ___ (15)
5. 8 + 3 = ___ (11)

**★★ Stretch:**
6. 6 + 6 = ___ (12) "Can you use Make 10 for this?" (6 + 4 + 2 = 12. Or just use doubles: 6+6=12. Both valid.)
7. 7 + 8 = ___ (15) "Use Make 10: 8 + 2 + 5 = 15."
8. 9 + 9 = ___ (18) "Is doubles or Make 10 easier here?" (Doubles: 9+9=18.)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "Solve 5 + 8 using Make 10. Write ALL the steps." (8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13)
10. "Which is easier for 9 + 2: counting on or Make 10?" (Counting on — addend is small) "What about 9 + 7?" (Make 10 — addend is large.)

### Interleaved Review
- "Double 9?" (18) *(Lesson 3)*
- "5 + 6?" (11, near-doubles) *(Lesson 4)*
- "Count on: 12 + 3?" (15) *(Lesson 2)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess Make 10 strategy.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "Use Make 10 to solve 8 + 6. Show your steps." (8 + 2 + 4 = 10 + 4 = 14)
2. "9 + 5 = ___?" (14)
3. "How many more does 7 need to make 10?" (3)

**Mastery = All 3 correct, with evidence of Make 10 reasoning (not just counting on to find the answer).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If make-10 pairs aren't fluent, go back to Lesson 1 Activity 3 and practice daily.
- If decomposing is the problem (e.g., student can't break 5 into 2+3), practice number decomposition as a standalone activity: "How many ways can you break 5 into two parts?"
- Budget a second session on this lesson. Make 10 is the hardest strategy but also the most powerful.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 6 (Addition Within 20 — Mixed Strategies).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is the hardest strategy in the unit — and the most important.** Make 10 is the foundation of mental math for the rest of elementary school. It relies on three sub-skills: knowing make-10 pairs, decomposing numbers, and adding to 10. If any of these is shaky, the strategy won't work. Diagnose which sub-skill is the problem and address it.

**The ten-frame is essential here.** It provides the visual structure that makes "how many more to 10" obvious. Don't skip it. Even when the student starts to work mentally, having the ten-frame available for verification builds confidence.

**It's okay to budget two sessions.** If the student grasps the idea but is slow or inconsistent, a second session of practice is well worth it. Make 10 pays dividends for years.

**Strategy choice matters.** Not every problem is best solved with Make 10. For 9+2, counting on is faster. For 7+7, doubles is faster. The goal is a toolkit, not a single strategy. Lesson 6 focuses on strategy selection.
