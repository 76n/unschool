# Facilitator Guide — Grade 1 Math, Unit 1: Addition and Subtraction Within 20

**Grade:** 1
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 1 — Addition and Subtraction Within 20
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Fully facilitated (adult present and actively guiding)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit extends the addition and subtraction work from kindergarten into the range of 20. Students move beyond counting-all to more efficient strategies: counting on, making 10, and using doubles. They deepen their understanding of the equal sign and begin to see the relationship between addition and subtraction.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Add and subtract within 20 using objects, drawings, and strategies
- Count on from a larger number to add (e.g., for 8 + 3, start at 8 and count "9, 10, 11")
- Use the "make 10" strategy to simplify addition (e.g., 8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13)
- Use doubles and near-doubles as addition strategies (e.g., 6 + 7 = 6 + 6 + 1 = 13)
- Understand the relationship between addition and subtraction (fact families)
- Solve addition and subtraction word problems within 20
- Understand the meaning of the equal sign as "the same as"

**Why this matters:** Fluency within 20 is one of the most critical milestones in early math. Students who can add and subtract within 20 confidently and flexibly are prepared for multi-digit operations, measurement, and early multiplication. The strategies taught here — making 10, doubles, counting on — become mental math tools used throughout life.

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm:

- [ ] Adds within 10 using objects or drawings (Kindergarten Unit 2)
- [ ] Subtracts within 10 using objects or drawings (Kindergarten Unit 2)
- [ ] Reads and writes number sentences with +, −, and = (Kindergarten Unit 2)
- [ ] Knows pairs that make 10 (e.g., 7 + 3, 6 + 4) — not necessarily from memory, but can find them
- [ ] Counts to 20 orally and recognizes numerals to 20
- [ ] Understands place value basics: teen numbers are "ten and some ones" (Kindergarten Unit 3)

## Materials Needed for This Unit

- **Counting objects (20+):** Blocks, counters, beans — two colors are essential
- **Two ten-frames** (side by side to show numbers 11-20)
- **Number line 0-20** (hand-drawn or printed strip)
- **Number cards 0-20**
- **Paper and pencil**
- **Dominos** (optional but excellent for doubles and addition practice)

| 5 | Making 10 to Add | 1-2 |

| 10 | Word Problems Within 20 | 1 |
| — | Unit Assessment | Separate session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lesson 5 (Making 10) is the most complex strategy. Budget 2 sessions if needed.
- If the student is already fluent with Kindergarten addition/subtraction, Lesson 1 can be a quick warm-up rather than a full session.
- The equal sign lesson (Lesson 9) is conceptually important but quick if the student already understands it.

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Student still counts all from 1 (e.g., for 8+3, counts "1,2,3...11") | Counting-on hasn't developed | Model counting-on explicitly. Use the phrase "Start at 8, count on 3 more: 9, 10, 11." Fold fingers to track how many they've counted on. |
| Student adds but puts the larger number in the "counted on" position (e.g., for 3+8, starts at 3 and counts on 8) | Doesn't know the commutative property or "start from the bigger number" strategy | Teach: "We can start from the bigger number. 3+8 is the same as 8+3. Start at 8, count on 3." |
| Student struggles with Make 10 (e.g., can't decompose 5 into 2+3 to make 8+5 into 10+3) | Decomposition is a new skill; "make 10" pairs aren't fluent | Go back to "pairs that make 10" practice. Use the ten-frame to show visually: fill 8, see 2 empty spaces, take 2 from the 5 to fill the frame. |
| Student thinks = means "the answer is" (e.g., says 5 = 3 + 2 is wrong) | Misconception about the equal sign | Explicitly teach: "= means the same as. Both sides must be the same. 5 IS the same as 3 + 2." Use balance scale language. |
| Student can add 8+5 but can't do 13−5 | Doesn't see the addition-subtraction relationship | Use fact family triangles: 8, 5, 13. "If 8+5=13, then 13−5=8 and 13−8=5." Always show with objects first. |

## Conversation Starters

- "What's double 7? How do you know?"
- "I need to add 9 + 4. What's a fast way to do it?" (Make 10: 9+1+3 = 13)
- "If 6 + 8 = 14, what's 14 − 8?"
- "How is adding 8 + 5 similar to adding 8 + 2?"
- "Can you think of a doubles fact? Can you find a near-double that uses it?"

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if:

- After 4+ weeks, the student cannot add within 10 reliably (this indicates a Kindergarten-level gap)
- The student shows no progress from counting-all to any more efficient strategy after explicit instruction and practice
- Math anxiety increases significantly (avoidance, tears, refusal) despite patient facilitation
- The student consistently confuses addition and subtraction (adds when should subtract and vice versa)

## What Comes Next

After this unit, students move to **Unit 2: Place Value — Tens and Ones**, where they deepen understanding of two-digit numbers. The Make 10 strategy from this unit directly supports place value understanding.
