# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 6

## Topic
Addition Within 20 — Mixed Strategies

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Choose an appropriate strategy for a given addition problem (counting on, doubles, near-doubles, or Make 10)
- Explain WHY they chose a particular strategy
- Solve addition problems within 20 fluently using any strategy

## Prerequisites
- Counting on (Lesson 2), Doubles (Lesson 3), Near-doubles (Lesson 4), Make 10 (Lesson 5)

## Materials Needed
- Double ten-frame (available for reference)
- Number line 0-20
- Paper and pencil
- Doubles chart (from Lesson 3)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick-fire review of each strategy.

1. "Count on: 14 + 2?" (16)
2. "Doubles: 7 + 7?" (14)
3. "Near-doubles: 6 + 7?" (13)
4. "Make 10: 8 + 5?" (13)

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach strategy selection — matching the right tool to the problem.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Strategy:** A plan or method for solving a problem
- **Efficient:** A strategy that gets the answer quickly with fewer steps

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Review the strategy toolkit.**
Write on paper or board:

| Strategy | Best for | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| **Count on** | One addend is 1, 2, or 3 | 9 + 2, 14 + 3 |
| **Doubles** | Both addends are the same | 6 + 6, 8 + 8 |
| **Near-doubles** | Addends differ by 1 or 2 | 7 + 8, 6 + 8 |
| **Make 10** | One addend is close to 10 (7, 8, 9) | 8 + 5, 9 + 6 |

"You now have FOUR strategies. The question is: which one fits each problem?"

**Step 2: Model strategy choice.**
Present problems and think aloud about strategy selection:

"9 + 2... One addend is 2 — that's small. I'll count on. 10, 11. Answer: 11."

"8 + 8... Both numbers are 8. That's a double. 16."

"7 + 8... These are 1 apart. Near-double. 7 + 7 = 14, plus 1 = 15."

"8 + 6... 8 is close to 10. Make 10: 8 + 2 + 4 = 14."

**Step 3: Note that multiple strategies can work.**
"For 7 + 5, I could use Make 10 (7 + 3 + 2 = 12) or count on (7... 8, 9, 10, 11, 12). Both give 12. Choose what feels fastest for you."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 9 + 7. Options: Make 10 (9+1+6=16) or near-doubles (8+8=16). Both work!

**Example 2:** 5 + 3. Count on from 5: 6, 7, 8. Quick and easy.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student picks strategies and solves.

For each problem, ask: "What strategy would you use? Solve it."

1. 6 + 3 = ___ → (Count on → 9)
2. 9 + 9 = ___ → (Doubles → 18)
3. 8 + 7 = ___ → (Make 10 or near-doubles → 15)
4. 5 + 6 = ___ → (Near-doubles → 11)
5. 9 + 4 = ___ → (Make 10 → 13)
6. 13 + 2 = ___ → (Count on → 15)
7. 7 + 7 = ___ → (Doubles → 14)
8. 8 + 4 = ___ → (Make 10 → 12)

*Facilitator prompt: Accept any valid strategy. If the student picks a less efficient one but gets the right answer, acknowledge it, then ask: "Can you think of a faster way?" Don't insist — just plant the seed.*

## Independent Practice (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Fluency practice with strategy choice.

**★ On-level:**
1. 7 + 3 = ___ (10)
2. 6 + 6 = ___ (12)
3. 9 + 5 = ___ (14)
4. 8 + 9 = ___ (17)
5. 5 + 2 = ___ (7)
6. 7 + 6 = ___ (13)
7. 4 + 8 = ___ (12)
8. 9 + 3 = ___ (12)

**★★ Stretch:**
9. "Solve 7 + 8 two different ways. Show both." (Near-doubles: 7+7+1=15. Make 10: 8+2+5=15.)
10. For each problem, write which strategy you used:
    - 6 + 5 = ___ Strategy: ___
    - 8 + 8 = ___ Strategy: ___
    - 9 + 2 = ___ Strategy: ___

**★★★ Challenge:**
11. "What's the fastest strategy for 6 + 9? Why?" (Make 10: 9+1+5=15. Because 9 needs only 1 to make 10.)
12. "Solve: 7 + 4 + 3 = ___" (14. Look for Make 10: 7+3=10, 10+4=14.)

### Interleaved Review
- "10 − 6 = ___?" (4)
- "What makes 10 with 4?" (6)
- "Write: seven plus eight equals fifteen." (7 + 8 = 15)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess fluent addition within 20 with strategy awareness.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 8 + 6 = ___ (14)
2. 9 + 9 = ___ (18)
3. 7 + 5 = ___ (12)
4. "What strategy did you use for problem 1?" (Accept any valid strategy name)

**Mastery = All 3 computation items correct, with a named strategy for at least one.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which strategy is weakest and revisit that lesson.
- Create a strategy reference card for the student to use during practice.
- The most common gap: Make 10. If Make 10 is still shaky, revisit Lesson 5 before pushing further.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (Subtraction as "Undoing" Addition).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Strategy flexibility is the goal, not one "correct" strategy.** Research in mathematics education consistently shows that students who know multiple strategies and can choose flexibly are more successful than those who rely on a single method. Honor the student's choice while gently expanding their toolkit.

**Speed is NOT the goal yet.** Fluency means accuracy + flexibility + reasonable speed. At this stage, taking 5-10 seconds to think through a strategy is perfectly acceptable. Timed drills are not recommended at this point.

**Some problems have a "natural" strategy.** 9 + anything → Make 10 (because 9 needs only 1 more). Doubles → instant if memorized. Small addend → count on. But students may find their own shortcuts, and that's great.
