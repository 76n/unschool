# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 8

## Topic
Subtraction Within 20 — Counting Back and Thinking Addition

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract within 20 by counting back (for subtracting 1, 2, or 3)
- Subtract within 20 by thinking addition (for larger differences)
- Choose between counting back and thinking addition based on the problem

## Prerequisites
- Fact families and the inverse relationship (Lesson 7)
- Counting on and Make 10 (Lessons 2, 5)
- Subtraction within 10 (Kindergarten)

## Materials Needed
- Number line 0-20
- Double ten-frame
- Counting objects (available for support)
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review fact families and addition strategies.

1. "If 8 + 7 = 15, what is 15 − 7?" (8) *(Lesson 7)*
2. "14 − 7 = ___?" (7 — doubles family) *(Lesson 7)*
3. "Make 10: 9 + 6 = ___?" (15) *(Lesson 5)*
4. "Count backward from 15 to 10." (15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 10) *(Counting practice)*

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach two subtraction strategies and when to use each.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Count back:** Start at the bigger number and count backward to subtract (like counting on, but in reverse)
- **Think addition:** Ask "what plus ___ equals ___?" to solve a subtraction problem

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Counting back for small subtrahends.**
"14 − 2. I start at 14 and count back 2: 13, 12. The answer is 12."

Use the number line: point to 14, hop back to 13, hop back to 12.

"Counting back is fast when you subtract 1, 2, or 3."

More examples:
- 17 − 1 = 16 (hop back 1)
- 13 − 3 = 10 (hop back 3: 12, 11, 10)
- 20 − 2 = 18 (hop back 2: 19, 18)

**Step 2: Think addition for larger subtrahends.**
"15 − 8. Counting back 8 is hard and slow. Instead, I THINK ADDITION: 8 + ___ = 15?"

"I know 8 + 7 = 15 (Make 10: 8+2+5=15). So 15 − 8 = 7."

More examples:
- 13 − 9: Think 9 + ___ = 13. 9 + 4 = 13. So 13 − 9 = 4.
- 16 − 7: Think 7 + ___ = 16. 7 + 9 = 16. So 16 − 7 = 9.
- 14 − 6: Think 6 + ___ = 14. 6 + 8 = 14. So 14 − 6 = 8.

**Step 3: Strategy selection.**
"Two strategies for subtraction:
1. **Count back** — when subtracting 1, 2, or 3. Fast and easy.
2. **Think addition** — when subtracting a bigger number. Uses your addition skills."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 18 − 3. Count back: 17, 16, 15. Answer: 15.

**Example 2:** 17 − 9. Think addition: 9 + ___ = 17. 9 + 8 = 17. Answer: 8.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices both strategies.

**Activity 1: Count back (3 minutes)**
- 15 − 2 = ___ (13)
- 19 − 3 = ___ (16)
- 11 − 1 = ___ (10)
- 20 − 3 = ___ (17)

**Activity 2: Think addition (4 minutes)**
- 13 − 8 = ___? Think: 8 + ___ = 13. (5) ✓
- 15 − 6 = ___? Think: 6 + ___ = 15. (9) ✓
- 17 − 8 = ___? Think: 8 + ___ = 17. (9) ✓
- 12 − 7 = ___? Think: 7 + ___ = 12. (5) ✓

*Facilitator prompt: If "think addition" is hard, the student can use Make 10 to figure out the addition: "8 + what = 13? 8 + 2 = 10, 10 + 3 = 13, so 2 + 3 = 5."*

**Activity 3: Choose your strategy (3 minutes)**
- 16 − 2 = ___? (14, count back)
- 14 − 9 = ___? (5, think addition)
- 18 − 3 = ___? (15, count back)
- 11 − 6 = ___? (5, think addition)

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Mixed subtraction practice within 20.

**★ On-level:**
1. 13 − 2 = ___ (11)
2. 15 − 9 = ___ (6)
3. 17 − 3 = ___ (14)
4. 12 − 5 = ___ (7)
5. 16 − 8 = ___ (8)
6. 20 − 1 = ___ (19)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. 18 − 9 = ___ (9)
8. 14 − 7 = ___ (7) "What strategy did you use?"
9. "Fill in: 16 − ___ = 9" (7)

**★★★ Challenge:**
10. "Solve 15 − 7 two different ways." (Count back 7 from 15: 14,13,12,11,10,9,8. Think addition: 7+8=15, so 15−7=8.)
11. "Is 13 − 5 the same as 5 − 13?" (No! You can't take away more than you have — at this level. The answer to 13−5 is 8; 5−13 doesn't work with the numbers we know.)

### Interleaved Review
- "8 + 7 = ___?" (15, near-doubles or Make 10) *(Lessons 4/5)*
- "Double 9?" (18) *(Lesson 3)*
- "Write the fact family for 6, 9, 15." *(Lesson 7)*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess subtraction within 20.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "18 − 2 = ___?" (16, count back)
2. "13 − 7 = ___?" (6, think addition)
3. "How did you solve problem 2?" (Student explains think-addition or other valid strategy)

**Mastery = Both computation items correct with a described strategy.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- For count-back struggles: practice counting backward from 20 to 0 daily. Use the number line physically.
- For think-addition struggles: the bottleneck is usually addition fluency. Return to Lessons 5-6 for more addition practice before pushing subtraction further.
- Objects and the ten-frame are always available as fallbacks.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 9 (The Equal Sign).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**"Think addition" is the most important subtraction strategy.** Students who master "think addition" can derive any subtraction fact from their addition knowledge. This is much more reliable than counting backward, which becomes error-prone for larger numbers.

**Counting back is limited but useful.** It works well for subtracting 1, 2, or 3. Beyond that, it's slow and error-prone (students lose track). Don't push counting back for larger subtrahends.

**Some students will use objects for a long time.** That's okay. The ten-frame remains a valid tool throughout this unit. Mental strategies are the goal but not the requirement. Understanding comes first; speed comes later.
