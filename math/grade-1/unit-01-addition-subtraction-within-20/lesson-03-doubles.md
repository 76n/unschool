# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 3

## Topic
Doubles (1+1 Through 10+10)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Recognize and solve doubles facts (1+1 through 10+10)
- Use visual models (pairs, two-row arrangements) to understand why doubles work
- Begin to recall common doubles from memory (especially 5+5 and 6+6)

## Prerequisites
- Addition within 10 (Lesson 1)
- Counting on (Lesson 2)
- Counts to 20

## Materials Needed
- 20 counting objects in a single color
- Double ten-frame (two ten-frames side by side)
- Paper and crayons
- Dominos (optional — excellent for doubles)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Counting-on review and make-10 practice.

1. "Use counting on: 9 + 2 = ___?" (11) *(Lesson 2 review)*
2. "Use counting on: 7 + 3 = ___?" (10) *(Lesson 2 review)*
3. "I say 6, you say what makes 10." (4) *(Make-10 review)*
4. "I say 8, you say what makes 10." (2)

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce doubles as a memorable set of addition facts.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Double:** Adding a number to itself (e.g., 4 + 4 is "double 4")
- **Doubles fact:** A number sentence where both addends are the same

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Build doubles with objects.**
Place objects in two equal rows:

"Let's see what happens when we add a number to itself."

1 + 1: ○ / ○ → 2
2 + 2: ○○ / ○○ → 4
3 + 3: ○○○ / ○○○ → 6

"See the pattern? Two equal rows. We call these DOUBLES."

Continue building:
4 + 4 → 8
5 + 5 → 10

"Double 5 is 10! That's our old friend, make 10."

6 + 6 → 12
7 + 7 → 14

**Step 2: Show on the double ten-frame.**
Use two ten-frames side by side. For 6 + 6:
- Fill 6 on the left frame.
- Fill 6 on the right frame.
- Count all: 12.

For 8 + 8:
- Fill 8 on the left, 8 on the right.
- Count: 16.

**Step 3: Doubles are "anchor facts."**
"Doubles are easy to remember because both numbers are the same. If you can remember your doubles, they'll help you solve LOTS of other problems. We'll see how in the next lesson."

**Step 4: Build the doubles chart together.**
Write on paper as you go:
```
1 + 1 = 2
2 + 2 = 4
3 + 3 = 6
4 + 4 = 8
5 + 5 = 10
6 + 6 = 12
7 + 7 = 14
8 + 8 = 16
9 + 9 = 18
10 + 10 = 20
```

"Notice: the answers are all EVEN numbers. And they go up by 2 each time."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "What is double 7?" Build two rows of 7. Count: 14. 7 + 7 = 14.

**Example 2:** "What is 5 + 5?" Student can build or recall: 10.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice doubles facts toward fluency.

**Activity 1: Build and solve (4 minutes)**
Student builds each double with objects in two rows and states the answer:
- Double 3 → 6
- Double 6 → 12
- Double 8 → 16
- Double 4 → 8
- Double 9 → 18

**Activity 2: Doubles flash (3 minutes)**
Say a number. Student says the double.
"Double 5!" (10) "Double 7!" (14) "Double 2!" (4) "Double 10!" (20) "Double 6!" (12)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student can't recall, they can build with objects or count on. The goal is to MOVE TOWARD recall, not demand it immediately.*

**Activity 3: Real-world doubles (3 minutes)**
"Where do we see doubles in real life?"
- 2 hands × 5 fingers = 10 fingers (double 5)
- 2 eyes = double 1
- A carton of eggs: 2 rows of 6 = 12 (double 6)
- A spider: 2 rows of 4 legs = 8 (double 4)

*Facilitator prompt: Ask the student to come up with their own doubles example.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build toward doubles fluency.

**★ On-level:**
1. 3 + 3 = ___ (6)
2. 5 + 5 = ___ (10)
3. 7 + 7 = ___ (14)
4. 4 + 4 = ___ (8)
5. 6 + 6 = ___ (12)

**★★ Stretch:**
6. 8 + 8 = ___ (16)
7. 9 + 9 = ___ (18)
8. "What number, doubled, gives 10?" (5)
9. "What number, doubled, gives 14?" (7)

**★★★ Challenge:**
10. "Is 7 + 7 more or less than 6 + 6? By how much?" (More, by 2)
11. "Double 10 is 20. What is double 11?" (22 — preview of extending the pattern)

### Interleaved Review
- "Count on: 8 + 3 = ___?" (11) *(Lesson 2 review)*
- "10 − 6 = ___?" (4) *(Subtraction review)*
- "What makes 10 with 3?" (7) *(Make-10 review)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess doubles fact knowledge.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What is 6 + 6?" (12)
2. "What is double 8?" (16)
3. "What is 5 + 5?" (10)

**Mastery = All 3 correct (objects/counting allowed, but ideally with minimal counting).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Create a doubles reference chart and post it where the student works. Practice reading it daily.
- Use the two-row object model for every double until the visual is internalized.
- Focus on doubles through 5+5 first; extend to larger doubles once those are solid.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Near-Doubles). Encourage daily doubles practice to build toward automaticity.

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Doubles dominoes:** If you have dominoes, find all the "doubles" dominoes (1-1, 2-2, etc.) and state the doubles fact for each.
- **Doubles poem or song:** Create a rhyme or song for doubles facts. (Example: "6 + 6 = 12, I can count them by myself!")
- **Explore even numbers:** "All doubles answers are even numbers. Can you see why?" (Two equal groups always make an even total.)

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Doubles are "anchor facts."** Research shows that doubles are among the first facts children commit to memory because the symmetry makes them memorable. Once doubles are known, the "near-doubles" strategy (next lesson) opens up many other facts.

**Don't demand memorization yet.** The goal is familiarity and emerging recall, not instant recall. Automaticity develops over weeks and months of practice. If the student can build a double with objects or count on to find it, they understand the concept. Speed will come.

**The even-number pattern is a bonus insight.** All doubles produce even numbers. This is because doubling means "two groups of the same size," which by definition is an even number. If the student notices this pattern, celebrate it — it shows mathematical thinking.
