# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 7

## Topic
Subtraction as "Undoing" Addition (Fact Families)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Understand that subtraction "undoes" addition (the inverse relationship)
- Given an addition fact, derive the related subtraction facts (e.g., 8+5=13 → 13−5=8, 13−8=5)
- Identify all four facts in a fact family

## Prerequisites
- Addition within 20 using strategies (Lessons 2-6)
- Subtraction within 10 (Kindergarten)

## Materials Needed
- 20 counting objects in two colors
- Paper and pencil
- Fact family triangle cards (optional — can be made from paper)

---

4. "10 − 4 = ___?" (6) *(Kindergarten review)*

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show that addition and subtraction are connected — knowing one gives you the other.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Fact family:** A group of related addition and subtraction facts using the same three numbers
- **Inverse:** The opposite operation — subtraction is the inverse of addition
- **Related facts:** Facts that use the same numbers (e.g., 3+5=8 and 8−5=3)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Build an addition fact and reverse it.**
Place 8 red blocks and 5 blue blocks. Push them together. "8 + 5 = 13."

"Now watch." Separate the blue blocks back out. "I started with 13. I took the 5 blue blocks away. What's left? 8. So 13 − 5 = 8."

"Adding put them together. Subtracting took them apart again. Subtraction UNDOES addition."

**Step 2: Find all four facts.**
"With these three numbers — 8, 5, and 13 — I can write FOUR facts:"

```
8 + 5 = 13
5 + 8 = 13
13 − 5 = 8
13 − 8 = 5
```

Show each with objects. "These four facts are a FACT FAMILY. They all use the same three numbers."

**Step 3: Fact family triangle.**
Draw a triangle with 13 at the top and 8, 5 at the bottom corners.

"The big number goes at the top. The two parts go at the bottom. From this triangle, I can read all four facts."

**Step 4: Try another family.**
Build with objects: 6 + 9 = 15.

"What's the fact family?"
- 6 + 9 = 15
- 9 + 6 = 15
- 15 − 9 = 6
- 15 − 6 = 9

Draw the triangle: 15 on top, 6 and 9 on bottom.

**Step 5: The subtraction shortcut.**
"Here's the powerful idea: If you KNOW that 7 + 8 = 15, you AUTOMATICALLY know that 15 − 8 = 7 and 15 − 7 = 8. You don't need to count backward or use objects. Addition gives you subtraction for free!"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 4 + 9 = 13. Fact family: 4+9=13, 9+4=13, 13−4=9, 13−9=4.

**Example 2:** If 7 + 6 = 13, what is 13 − 6? → 7. (Think addition: 6 + ___ = 13.)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student derives subtraction facts from known addition facts.

**Activity 1: Build the fact family (5 minutes)**
For each addition fact, student writes all four family members:

- 5 + 7 = 12 → 7+5=12, 12−5=7, 12−7=5 ✓
- 8 + 4 = 12 → 4+8=12, 12−8=4, 12−4=8 ✓
- 9 + 6 = 15 → 6+9=15, 15−9=6, 15−6=9 ✓

**Activity 2: Subtraction via addition (5 minutes)**
Student solves subtraction by thinking of the related addition:

- 14 − 8 = ___? Think: 8 + ___ = 14. Answer: 6. ✓
- 11 − 7 = ___? Think: 7 + ___ = 11. Answer: 4. ✓
- 16 − 9 = ___? Think: 9 + ___ = 16. Answer: 7. ✓

*Facilitator prompt: If the student doesn't spontaneously think addition, guide them: "What plus 8 makes 14?" This "think addition" approach is the key insight of this lesson.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Apply fact family reasoning.

**★ On-level:**
1. Write the fact family for 3, 8, 11.
2. 13 − 7 = ___? (6) Think: 7 + ___ = 13.
3. 15 − 8 = ___? (7)
4. 11 − 5 = ___? (6)

**★★ Stretch:**
5. Write the fact family for 7, 9, 16.
6. "If 6 + 8 = 14, what is 14 − 6?" (8) "What is 14 − 8?" (6)
7. "Is there a fact family with only TWO different numbers? When?" (Yes: doubles! e.g., 7+7=14 → only 14−7=7. The family has 3 members, not 4.)

**★★★ Challenge:**
8. "I know 9 + 7 = 16. Without calculating, what are 16 − 9 and 16 − 7?" (7 and 9)
9. "Fill in the blank: ___ − 5 = 8. Use a fact family to help." (13)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess inverse relationship understanding.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Write the fact family for 6, 7, 13. (6+7=13, 7+6=13, 13−6=7, 13−7=6)
2. "If 9 + 5 = 14, what is 14 − 9?" (5)
3. "12 − 8 = ___? Use 'think addition.'" (4, because 8+4=12)

**Mastery = All 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Use objects to physically show the connection: build 8+5=13, then separate to show 13−5=8.
- Practice with well-known facts (doubles are easiest: 7+7=14, so 14−7=7).
- The "think addition" strategy may take several sessions to become natural. Don't rush it.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 8 (Subtraction Within 20).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**"Think addition" is the most powerful subtraction strategy.** Rather than counting backward (which is slow and error-prone), students learn to ask "What plus ___ makes ___?" This leverages their growing addition fluency and creates a bridge between the operations.

**Doubles families are simpler.** 7+7=14 produces only 3 unique facts (since both addition sentences are identical). Start with doubles families if the student needs an easier entry point.

**The fact family triangle is a helpful visual.** Some students really benefit from drawing the triangle with the sum on top and the addends on the bottom. The triangle makes it clear that you can "read" all four facts from three numbers.
