# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 4, Lesson 7

## Topic
Telling Time to the Half-Hour

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Understand that "half past" means the minute hand is pointing to the 6 (30 minutes)
- Tell and write time to the half-hour using both forms (e.g., 2:30 and "half past 2")
- Recognize that the hour hand is BETWEEN two numbers at the half-hour
- Distinguish between o'clock times and half-past times on an analog clock

## Prerequisites
- Tell and write time to the hour on analog and digital clocks (Lesson 6)
- Identify the hour hand (short) and minute hand (long) on a clock (Lesson 5)
- Count by 5s to 30 (helpful; introduced in Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- Analog teaching clock with movable hands
- Paper plate clock (from Lesson 5) — or make a second one for folding
- Paper and pencil
- Blank clock-face worksheets
- Sorting cards labeled with times (mix of o'clock and half-past)
- Scissors (for folding activity)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review telling time to the hour from Lesson 6.

1. Set the teaching clock to 4:00. "What time is it?" (4 o'clock / 4:00.)
2. Set the teaching clock to 10:00. "Write this time in digital form." (10:00.)
3. "Draw hands on a blank clock to show 7 o'clock." (Hour hand on 7, minute hand on 12.)

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the concept of "half past" the hour and connect it to the :30 digital format.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Half past:** A time when the minute hand points to the 6 — exactly halfway around the clock
- **Half-hour:** 30 minutes — half of one full hour (60 minutes)
- **Between:** The hour hand sits between two numbers at the half-hour, because it is partway through moving from one hour to the next

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Fold the clock to discover "half."**
Take a paper plate clock (or draw a large clock on paper).

"Let's fold our clock right down the middle — from the 12 to the 6."

Fold the paper plate in half so that 12 is at the top and 6 is at the bottom crease.

"See? The clock is split into two equal halves. The top half goes from 12 to 6 on one side. When the minute hand starts at 12 and goes all the way down to 6, it has traveled HALF of the circle. That is why we call it 'half past.'"

"Half of the 60 minutes in an hour is 30 minutes. So when the minute hand is on the 6, 30 minutes have passed."

**Step 2: Demonstrate half-past times on the teaching clock.**
Set the clock to 2:30.

"Look at the minute hand — it is on the 6. That tells us it is 'half past' or '30 minutes.' Now look at the hour hand. It is NOT right on the 2. It is between the 2 and the 3. That is because we are HALFWAY through the 2 o'clock hour — we left 2 o'clock but we have not reached 3 o'clock yet."

"So we say: 'It is half past 2' or 'It is 2:30.'"

Move to 8:30.

"The minute hand is on the 6 — half past. The hour hand is between the 8 and the 9. It is half past 8, or 8:30."

"Notice the pattern: At half past, the hour hand is always BETWEEN two numbers, and the minute hand is always on the 6."

**Step 3: Write half-hour times in digital format.**
Write on paper:
- Half past 2 = 2:30
- Half past 8 = 8:30
- Half past 11 = 11:30

"In digital form, the number before the colon is still the hour. But instead of :00, we write :30 — because 30 minutes have gone by."

"Compare: 2:00 means 2 o'clock — the very start. 2:30 means half past 2 — we are in the middle of the 2 o'clock hour."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** The teaching clock shows the minute hand on 6, and the hour hand between 5 and 6.
"The minute hand is on the 6 — that means it is 'half past.' The hour hand is between 5 and 6, so we are in the 5 o'clock hour. It is half past 5, or 5:30."

**Example B:** A digital clock reads 10:30.
"The number before the colon is 10, so the hour is 10. The 30 tells us it is half past. Let me set the teaching clock to match: minute hand on 6, hour hand between 10 and 11. This is half past 10."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads, writes, and sets half-hour times with support.

**Activity 1: Read the clock.**
Set the teaching clock to each time. Student says the time in two ways and writes the digital form.

1. Clock shows 3:30 → "Half past 3" / "3:30" → writes 3:30
2. Clock shows 7:30 → "Half past 7" / "7:30" → writes 7:30
3. Clock shows 12:30 → "Half past 12" / "12:30" → writes 12:30
4. Clock shows 1:30 → "Half past 1" / "1:30" → writes 1:30

*Facilitator prompt: If the student reads the hour as the NEXT number (e.g., says "half past 4" for 3:30), say: "The hour hand is between 3 and 4. It has not reached 4 yet — so we are still in the 3 o'clock hour. It is half past 3."*

**Activity 2: Sort the cards.**
Prepare cards with times written in digital format (mix of o'clock and half-past): 2:00, 5:30, 9:00, 6:30, 11:00, 4:30, 8:00, 1:30.

"Sort these cards into two groups: 'o'clock' times and 'half past' times."

O'clock pile: 2:00, 9:00, 11:00, 8:00
Half past pile: 5:30, 6:30, 4:30, 1:30

"How can you tell which pile a time goes in?" (O'clock times end in :00. Half-past times end in :30.)

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
Read each clock and write the time in digital format. (Clocks are drawn/provided with hands.)

1. Minute hand on 6, hour hand between 4 and 5. → ___:___ (4:30)
2. Minute hand on 6, hour hand between 9 and 10. → ___:___ (9:30)
3. Minute hand on 12, hour hand on 6. → ___:___ (6:00)
4. Minute hand on 6, hour hand between 2 and 3. → ___:___ (2:30)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
1. Clock shows minute hand on 6, hour hand between 6 and 7. Write the time. (6:30)
2. Clock shows minute hand on 12, hour hand on 3. Write the time. (3:00)
3. Draw hands on a blank clock to show 5:30. (Minute hand on 6, hour hand between 5 and 6.)
4. Draw hands on a blank clock to show 10:30. (Minute hand on 6, hour hand between 10 and 11.)
5. Match each analog clock to its digital time:
   - Clock A (half past 1) → 1:30
   - Clock B (8 o'clock) → 8:00
   - Clock C (half past 11) → 11:30

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
1. Draw two clocks side by side: one showing 4:00 and one showing 4:30. Write a sentence explaining what is DIFFERENT about the two clocks. (At 4:00, the minute hand is on 12 and the hour hand is right on 4. At 4:30, the minute hand is on 6 and the hour hand is between 4 and 5.)
2. "Maya says this clock shows 6:30." (Clock shows minute hand on 6, hour hand between 5 and 6.) "Is Maya correct? Explain." (No. The hour hand is between 5 and 6, so it is 5:30, not 6:30.)
3. "Write three half-past times using digital format." (Any three times ending in :30, e.g., 2:30, 7:30, 12:30.)
4. "You eat a snack at half past 3. You start homework at 4 o'clock. Draw both clocks and write both digital times." (3:30 and 4:00)
5. "What is the next o'clock time after 9:30?" (10:00)

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. The clock shows the minute hand on 6 and the hour hand between 7 and 8. What time is it? Write it in digital form. (7:30)
2. Draw hands on a blank clock to show half past 5. (Minute hand on 6, hour hand between 5 and 6.)
3. Is 3:30 an "o'clock" time or a "half past" time? (Half past.)

## Teacher Notes

**Common Errors:**
- **Reading the wrong hour at half past:** Students frequently look at the hour hand sitting between two numbers and pick the higher number. For example, at 3:30 the hour hand is between 3 and 4, and the student says "4:30." Reinforce: "The hour hand has not reached 4 yet. It left 3 but is still in the 3 o'clock hour."
- **Drawing the hour hand directly on the number for half-past times:** Students may draw the hour hand pointing exactly at the 5 for 5:30. Gently correct: "At half past, the hour hand is BETWEEN the numbers — it is traveling."
- **Forgetting the minute hand on 6:** Some students draw the minute hand on 12 for half-past times (confusing with o'clock). Reinforce: "O'clock = minute hand on 12. Half past = minute hand on 6."

**Tips:**
- The paper-plate folding activity is powerful. Let the student fold the plate themselves — the physical action of folding makes "half" concrete and memorable.
- Use the phrase "half past" consistently alongside the digital format. Some students will prefer one form; exposure to both is important.
- If counting by 5s to 30 is shaky, spend a minute practicing: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30. Touch each number on the clock face as you count.

**Look-Fors:**
- Does the student correctly identify that the minute hand is on the 6 for half-past times?
- Does the student recognize the hour hand is between two numbers at the half-hour?
- Can the student distinguish between o'clock and half-past times when sorting or reading?
- Does the student write :30 (not :06 or :60) for half-hour times?
