# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 4, Lesson 6

## Topic
Telling Time to the Hour

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Tell and write time to the hour on an analog clock (e.g., "It is 3 o'clock")
- Match an analog clock display to the correct digital time (e.g., 3:00)
- Write time in digital format using __:00
- Relate daily activities to clock times (e.g., "We eat lunch at 12 o'clock")

## Prerequisites
- Identify the hour hand and minute hand on an analog clock (Lesson 5)
- Know that the short hand is the hour hand and the long hand is the minute hand (Lesson 5)
- Count to 12 fluently (Unit 1)

## Materials Needed
- Analog teaching clock with movable hands (or paper plate clock from Lesson 5)
- Digital clock display (phone, microwave, or drawn on paper)
- Paper and pencil
- Blank clock-face worksheets (circles with numbers 1-12 marked, no hands)
- Daily schedule cards (pictures of activities: waking up, eating breakfast, school, lunch, dinner, bedtime)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review the parts of the clock from Lesson 5.

1. Hold up the teaching clock. "Point to the hour hand." (Student points to the short hand.) "Point to the minute hand." (Student points to the long hand.)
2. "Which hand is longer — the hour hand or the minute hand?" (The minute hand is longer.)
3. Set the clock to any position. "Where is the hour hand pointing? Where is the minute hand pointing?" (Student identifies the numbers each hand points to.)

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach how to read and write "o'clock" times on analog and digital clocks.

### Key Vocabulary
- **O'clock:** A time when the minute hand points straight up to the 12 — the very start of an hour
- **Digital clock:** A clock that shows time using only numbers, with a colon in the middle (like 3:00)
- **Analog clock:** A clock with a face, numbers in a circle, and two hands that move

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce "o'clock" on the analog clock.**
Set the teaching clock to 3:00.

"Look at the clock. The minute hand is pointing straight up to the 12. That means it is exactly the start of an hour — we say 'o'clock.' Now, where is the hour hand? It is pointing to the 3. So this clock says 3 o'clock."

Move the clock to 7:00.

"Now the minute hand is still pointing to the 12. The hour hand moved — it points to the 7. What time is it?" (7 o'clock.)

"Here is the rule: When the minute hand points to the 12, look at the hour hand. The number the hour hand points to is the hour."

**Step 2: Connect analog time to digital format.**
Write on paper: **3:00**

"This is how we write 3 o'clock using numbers. The first number is the hour — 3. Then we write a colon — those two dots. Then we write 00, because the minutes are zero. The minute hand is at the top, at zero minutes."

Write several examples:
- 3 o'clock = 3:00
- 7 o'clock = 7:00
- 10 o'clock = 10:00

"When we write an o'clock time, it is always ___:00. The two zeros mean 'zero extra minutes.'"

**Step 3: Connect times to the daily routine.**
Show daily schedule cards or draw simple pictures.

"Let's think about YOUR day. You might wake up at 7 o'clock in the morning."

Set the clock to 7:00. Write 7:00.

"You eat lunch at 12 o'clock." Set the clock to 12:00. Write 12:00.

"You eat dinner at 6 o'clock." Set the clock to 6:00. Write 6:00.

"You go to bed at 8 o'clock." Set the clock to 8:00. Write 8:00.

"Clocks help us know when things happen during our day!"

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** The teaching clock shows the minute hand on 12 and the hour hand on 5.
"The minute hand is on the 12, so it is 'o'clock.' The hour hand points to 5. It is 5 o'clock. In digital form, we write 5:00."

**Example B:** A digital clock shows 9:00.
"The number before the colon is 9 — that is the hour. The two zeros mean 'o'clock.' So this says 9 o'clock. Let me set the teaching clock to match — hour hand on 9, minute hand on 12."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads, writes, and sets o'clock times with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Read the clock.**
Set the teaching clock to each time. Student says the time and writes it in digital format.

1. Clock shows 2:00 → "2 o'clock" → writes 2:00
2. Clock shows 11:00 → "11 o'clock" → writes 11:00
3. Clock shows 6:00 → "6 o'clock" → writes 6:00
4. Clock shows 1:00 → "1 o'clock" → writes 1:00

*Facilitator prompt: If the student looks at the minute hand instead of the hour hand, say: "Remember, the SHORT hand tells us the hour. Which number is the short hand pointing to?"*

**Activity 2: Draw the hands.**
Give the student blank clock faces. Call out a time; student draws the hour hand (short) and minute hand (long).

1. "Show me 4 o'clock." (Hour hand on 4, minute hand on 12.)
2. "Show me 9 o'clock." (Hour hand on 9, minute hand on 12.)
3. "Show me 12 o'clock." (Hour hand on 12, minute hand on 12 — both point up!)

*Facilitator prompt: "Where does the minute hand ALWAYS go for an o'clock time?" (On the 12.)*

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
Read each clock and write the time in digital format. (Clocks are drawn/provided with hands.)

1. Clock shows hour hand on 3, minute hand on 12. → ___:___ (3:00)
2. Clock shows hour hand on 8, minute hand on 12. → ___:___ (8:00)
3. Clock shows hour hand on 10, minute hand on 12. → ___:___ (10:00)
4. Clock shows hour hand on 1, minute hand on 12. → ___:___ (1:00)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
Read the clock and write the time. Then draw hands on a blank clock for the given time.

1. Clock shows hour hand on 5, minute hand on 12. → ___:___ (5:00)
2. Clock shows hour hand on 11, minute hand on 12. → ___:___ (11:00)
3. Draw hands on a blank clock to show 7:00. (Hour hand on 7, minute hand on 12.)
4. Draw hands on a blank clock to show 2:00. (Hour hand on 2, minute hand on 12.)
5. Match: Draw a line from each analog clock to its digital time.
   - Clock A (hour hand on 4) → 4:00
   - Clock B (hour hand on 9) → 9:00
   - Clock C (hour hand on 12) → 12:00

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
1. Draw hands on a blank clock to show 6:00. Write the digital time. (6:00)
2. "Sam eats breakfast at 7 o'clock and dinner at 6 o'clock. Draw both clocks and write both digital times." (7:00 and 6:00)
3. "The clock shows 10:00. What might you be doing at 10 o'clock in the morning?" (Answers vary — e.g., "doing schoolwork," "playing outside")
4. "A clock shows the hour hand on 3 and the minute hand on 12. Carlos says it is 12 o'clock. Is Carlos correct? Explain." (No. The hour hand is on 3, so it is 3 o'clock. Carlos looked at the minute hand instead of the hour hand.)
5. "Write the digital time for each: 1 o'clock, 5 o'clock, 12 o'clock." (1:00, 5:00, 12:00)

---

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. The clock shows the hour hand on 6 and the minute hand on 12. What time is it? Write it in digital form. (6:00)
2. Draw hands on a blank clock to show 3 o'clock. (Hour hand on 3, minute hand on 12.)
3. What does 8:00 mean in words? (8 o'clock)

## Teacher Notes

**Common Errors:**
- **Confusing the hands:** The most frequent error at this age is reading the minute hand as the hour. Consistently reinforce: "SHORT hand = hour." Consider color-coding the hands on the teaching clock (e.g., red for hour, blue for minute).
- **12 o'clock confusion:** When both hands point to 12, some students say "0 o'clock." Remind them: "There is no 0 on the clock. When the short hand is on 12, it is 12 o'clock."
- **Writing the colon:** Students may forget the colon or write it as a period. Model the colon carefully: "Two dots, one on top of the other."

**Tips:**
- Throughout the day, stop and ask: "What time is it now?" This real-world practice is more valuable than any worksheet.
- Use the student's actual daily schedule to make the lesson personally meaningful.
- If a geared teaching clock is available (where moving the minute hand automatically moves the hour hand), use it — this helps the student see that both hands work together.

**Look-Fors:**
- Does the student consistently identify the short hand as the hour hand?
- Can the student write the digital format correctly with the colon and two zeros?
- Does the student draw the minute hand pointing to 12 for every o'clock time?
- Can the student connect clock times to real-life events without prompting?
