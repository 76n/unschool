# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 4, Lesson 2

## Topic
Measuring with Consistent Units

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain why using the same-size unit is important for accurate measurement
- Discover that measuring the same object with different-size units gives different numbers
- Describe the relationship: smaller units produce a larger number, bigger units produce a smaller number
- Measure the same object with two different non-standard units and compare results

## Prerequisites
- Measuring length with non-standard units placed end-to-end (Unit 4, Lesson 1)
- Counting with one-to-one correspondence up to 30 (Kindergarten)
- Comparing numbers — which is more, which is less (Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- 20 small paper clips (standard size)
- 10 large blocks or jumbo paper clips (noticeably bigger than the small clips)
- 20 snap cubes (same size)
- A book, a desk edge, a shoe (objects to measure twice)
- Recording sheet with columns: Object / Small Units / Large Units
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review Lesson 1 measurement skills and set up the investigation.

1. "Let's measure this book with paper clips. Remember our three rules — what are they?" (Start at the end, no gaps, no overlaps.) Student measures the book. Record the answer.
2. "How many paper clips long is the book?" (Student states the measurement.)
3. "What if I used something bigger to measure? Do you think I'd need more or fewer of them?" (Accept predictions — do not correct yet. This is the question we investigate today.)

"Great prediction! Let's find out."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show that the size of the unit changes the measurement number, and build the key insight: smaller unit means more units.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Consistent units:** Using the same unit for the whole measurement — don't mix sizes
- **Non-standard unit:** Something we use to measure that is not a ruler (paper clips, blocks, cubes)
- **Compare:** To look at two things and see how they are the same or different

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Concrete — measure with small paper clips.**
Place a desk edge (or a large book) in front of the student. "Let's measure this with our small paper clips."

Line up small paper clips end-to-end along the desk edge. Count together: "1, 2, 3, ... 12. The desk is 12 small paper clips long." Record the number.

**Step 2: Concrete — measure the same object with large blocks.**
"Now let's measure the same desk with these big blocks." Hold up a large block next to a small paper clip. "Look at how much bigger the block is."

Line up large blocks end-to-end along the same desk edge. Count: "1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. The desk is 6 large blocks long." Record the number.

**Step 3: Compare and discuss.**
"Wait — the desk didn't change size. It's the same desk! But we got two different numbers: 12 and 6. Why?"

Guide the student to see:
- "The big blocks are bigger, so fewer of them fit along the desk."
- "The small paper clips are smaller, so more of them fit along the desk."

Write on paper:
- Small paper clips: 12
- Large blocks: 6

"The small unit gave us a BIGGER number. The big unit gave us a SMALLER number. That's an important pattern!"

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Measure a shoe with small paper clips, then with large blocks.
Small paper clips: "1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8. The shoe is 8 small paper clips long."
Large blocks: "1, 2, 3, 4. The shoe is 4 large blocks long."

"Which number is bigger — 8 or 4?" (8.) "And which unit is smaller — the paper clip or the block?" (The paper clip.) "Smaller unit, bigger number. The pattern works again!"

**Example B:** Measure a crayon with snap cubes, then with large blocks.
Snap cubes: "1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. The crayon is 6 snap cubes long."
Large blocks: "1, 2, 3. The crayon is 3 large blocks long."

"6 cubes or 3 blocks. Same crayon, different numbers, because the units are different sizes."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student measures the same object with two different units and compares.

**Activity 1: Measure a book two ways**
"Measure the long side of this book with small paper clips." Student measures and records. (Example: 10 paper clips.)

"Now measure the same side with large blocks." Student measures and records. (Example: 5 blocks.)

"Which number is bigger?" (10.) "Which unit is smaller?" (The paper clip.) "Does our pattern hold?" (Yes — smaller unit, bigger number.)

**Activity 2: Predict, then check**
Hold up a marker. "I want you to predict: Will you need more paper clips or more blocks to measure this marker?"

Student predicts: "More paper clips." (Because paper clips are smaller.)

"Let's check!" Student measures with both units and confirms.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student's prediction is wrong, that is a wonderful learning moment. Say: "Interesting! Let's see what really happens." After measuring, revisit: "The smaller unit gave us the bigger number. That surprised us, but now we know the rule!"*

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
Use paper clips and large blocks to measure each object. Write both numbers.
1. Measure a pencil with small paper clips: ___ paper clips. Now with large blocks: ___ blocks.
2. Measure a crayon with small paper clips: ___ paper clips. Now with large blocks: ___ blocks.
3. Measure a book with small paper clips: ___ paper clips. Now with large blocks: ___ blocks.
4. Circle the unit that always gives the bigger number: paper clips / blocks.

### Tier 2 (On Level)
Use paper clips and large blocks to measure each object. Write both numbers. Answer the questions.
1. Measure a pencil with small paper clips: ___ paper clips. Now with large blocks: ___ blocks.
2. Measure a shoe with small paper clips: ___ paper clips. Now with large blocks: ___ blocks.
3. Measure a book with small paper clips: ___ paper clips. Now with large blocks: ___ blocks.
4. Which unit always gives the bigger number? Why? (Paper clips, because they are smaller so you need more of them.)
5. Sam measured a table with big blocks and got 8. Mia measured the same table with small cubes and got 15. Who is wrong? (Neither — they used different units.)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
Use paper clips and large blocks to measure each object. Write both numbers. Solve the problems.
1. Measure a pencil with small paper clips: ___ paper clips. Now with large blocks: ___ blocks.
2. Measure a book with small paper clips: ___ paper clips. Now with large blocks: ___ blocks.
3. Measure a desk edge with small paper clips: ___ paper clips. Now with large blocks: ___ blocks.
4. A ribbon is 10 small paper clips long. Would it be more or fewer than 10 large blocks long? Explain. (Fewer — large blocks are bigger so you need fewer.)
5. Leo says a table is 6 long and Rosa says it is 12 long. Can they both be right? How? (Yes — they used different-size units. Leo used bigger units and Rosa used smaller units.)

---

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. You measure a desk with big blocks and get 5. You measure the same desk with small cubes and get 14. Why are the numbers different? (The cubes are smaller than the blocks, so more cubes fit along the desk.)
2. Smaller units give a ___ (bigger/smaller) number. (bigger)
3. When you measure, should you mix big and small units together? (No — use the same-size unit for the whole measurement.)

## Teacher Notes

**Common errors:**
- Student mixes unit sizes during a single measurement (uses some big blocks and some small ones). Remind: "We pick ONE unit and use only that unit for the whole measurement. That's what consistent means."
- Student is confused that the same object gives two different numbers. Reassure: "The desk didn't change. The UNITS changed. Different units give different numbers, and that's okay."
- Student thinks the person with the bigger number measured a longer object. Clarify: "The number depends on the size of the unit. You can't compare measurements unless the same unit was used."

**Tips:**
- Use dramatically different-sized units to make the effect obvious. A large block that is roughly twice the size of a paper clip works well.
- Have the student hold a big block and a small clip side by side before measuring. Ask: "Which one will I need more of? Why?"
- This lesson plants an early seed for the need for standard units (Lesson 4). When summarizing, say: "This is why grown-ups invented inches and centimeters — so everyone uses the same unit and gets the same number."

**Look-fors:**
- Does the student use only one type of unit per measurement (no mixing)?
- Can the student explain why the numbers are different?
- Does the student correctly state the pattern: smaller unit gives a bigger number?
