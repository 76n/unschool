# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 4, Lesson 1

## Topic
Measuring Length with Non-Standard Units

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Measure the length of objects using non-standard units (paper clips, cubes, or blocks) placed end-to-end
- Understand that measurement means finding how many units fit along the length of an object with no gaps and no overlaps
- Express measurements using the sentence frame "This ___ is ___ paper clips long"
- Count the units accurately and record the measurement as a number

## Prerequisites
- Counting objects to at least 30 with one-to-one correspondence (Kindergarten)
- Adding and subtracting within 20 (Unit 1)

## Materials Needed
- 20 same-size paper clips
- 20 snap cubes (all the same color/size)
- A pencil, a book, a crayon, a shoe, and a marker (objects to measure)
- Recording sheet (paper with two columns: Object / How Many Paper Clips)
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate counting skills and introduce the idea of comparing lengths.

1. "Count by 1s from 1 to 50." (Student counts aloud.)
2. Quick addition review: "4 + 3 = ?" (7) "5 + 5 = ?" (10) "8 + 2 = ?" (10) "6 + 4 = ?" (10)
3. Hold up a pencil and a crayon side by side. "Which one is longer? How can you tell?" (Accept any reasonable answer — the pencil sticks out farther, it is bigger, etc.)

"Today we are going to learn how to find out exactly HOW MUCH longer something is. We're going to measure!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the concept of measurement as laying units end-to-end and counting them.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Measure:** To find out how long something is by using units
- **Unit:** The object we use over and over to measure (today: paper clips)
- **Length:** How long something is from one end to the other
- **End-to-end:** Placing units so each one touches the next — no gaps and no overlaps

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Concrete — demonstrate measuring with paper clips.**
Place a pencil flat on the table. "I want to find out how long this pencil is. I'm going to use paper clips to measure it."

Line up paper clips along the pencil, starting at one end. Place each clip so it touches the previous one — no gaps and no overlaps. Think aloud:

"I start at this end of the pencil. I put down one paper clip. Then I put the next one right next to it — touching, but not on top of it. I keep going until I reach the other end."

Count the paper clips: "1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. The pencil is 7 paper clips long."

**Step 2: Emphasize the rules of measuring.**
"There are three important rules when we measure:"
- "Rule 1: Start at one end of the object."
- "Rule 2: No gaps — the units must touch each other."
- "Rule 3: No overlaps — don't stack them or let them cross over each other."

Demonstrate a wrong way: leave a gap between two paper clips. "Oops! See this space? That's a gap. If I have a gap, my measurement won't be right. Let me fix it." Push the clips together.

Demonstrate another wrong way: overlap two clips. "See how this clip is on top of the other one? That's an overlap. That's not right either." Adjust so they are end-to-end.

**Step 3: Representational — draw the measurement.**
On paper, draw a long rectangle (the pencil). Below it, draw 7 small rectangles in a row, touching, representing the paper clips. Write "7 paper clips" underneath.

"See? I can draw a picture of my measurement too. The pencil is 7 paper clips long."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Measure a crayon with paper clips.
Place the crayon on the table. Line up paper clips end-to-end along its length. Count: "1, 2, 3, 4, 5. The crayon is 5 paper clips long."

"Let's say it together: 'The crayon is 5 paper clips long.'"

**Example B:** Measure a marker with paper clips.
"Now let's try the marker." Line up paper clips. Count: "1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8. The marker is 8 paper clips long."

"Which is longer, the crayon or the marker?" (The marker.) "How do you know?" (8 is more than 5, so the marker is longer.)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student measures objects with support and records results.

**Activity 1: Measure together (facilitator assists)**
Give the student a book. "Measure the long side of this book using paper clips. Remember our three rules: start at one end, no gaps, no overlaps."

Watch as the student places paper clips. Coach as needed:
- "Make sure you start right at the edge."
- "Push that clip a little closer — no gaps!"
- "Good — now count them all."

Student counts and states: "The book is ___ paper clips long." Record on the sheet.

**Activity 2: Measure with less support**
Give the student a shoe. "Now measure the shoe on your own. I'll watch."

Let the student work. Only intervene if there is a gap, an overlap, or the student doesn't start at the end. When done, the student counts, states the measurement, and records it.

"Great work! You just measured two objects like a real scientist."

*Facilitator prompt: If the object length doesn't come out to an exact number of paper clips, tell the student to use the nearest whole number. Say: "It's close to ___ paper clips. We'll say it is about ___ paper clips long."*

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
Measure each object with paper clips. Write the number.
1. Measure your pencil. The pencil is ___ paper clips long.
2. Measure the long side of a piece of paper. The paper is ___ paper clips long.
3. Measure a crayon. The crayon is ___ paper clips long.
4. Measure a glue stick. The glue stick is ___ paper clips long.

### Tier 2 (On Level)
Measure each object with paper clips. Write the number. Then answer the question.
1. Measure your pencil. The pencil is ___ paper clips long.
2. Measure the long side of a piece of paper. The paper is ___ paper clips long.
3. Measure a crayon. The crayon is ___ paper clips long.
4. Measure a glue stick. The glue stick is ___ paper clips long.
5. Which object was the longest? How do you know?

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
Measure each object with paper clips. Write the number. Then solve the problems.
1. Measure your pencil. The pencil is ___ paper clips long.
2. Measure the long side of a piece of paper. The paper is ___ paper clips long.
3. Measure a crayon. The crayon is ___ paper clips long.
4. Measure a glue stick. The glue stick is ___ paper clips long.
5. How much longer is the longest object than the shortest object? Write a number sentence to show your thinking.

---

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. When we measure with paper clips, we place them ___-to-___ with no ___ and no ___. (end-to-end, gaps, overlaps)
2. A ribbon is 9 paper clips long. A string is 6 paper clips long. Which is longer? (The ribbon.)
3. You measure a marker and count 8 paper clips. How long is the marker? (8 paper clips long.)

---

## Answer Key

**Independent Practice — Tier 1, 2, and 3:**
Problems 1-4: Answers will vary depending on the actual objects measured. Accept any reasonable whole-number measurement that the student can demonstrate by lining up paper clips correctly.

**Tier 2, Problem 5:** The student should identify the object with the largest number and explain that a bigger number means a longer length.

**Tier 3, Problem 5:** The student should subtract the smallest measurement from the largest. For example, if the paper was 14 paper clips and the crayon was 5 paper clips: 14 - 5 = 9 paper clips longer.

**Exit Ticket:**
1. end-to-end, gaps, overlaps
2. The ribbon (9 is more than 6)
3. 8 paper clips long

## Teacher Notes

**Common errors:**
- Student leaves gaps between paper clips. This leads to a measurement that is too large. Remind: "Scoot them together so they touch."
- Student overlaps paper clips. This leads to a measurement that is too small. Remind: "Side by side, not on top."
- Student does not start at the end of the object. The first paper clip should be aligned with the edge. Show the student how to line up the first clip.
- Student loses count. Encourage the student to touch each clip as they count, or number them with a finger tap.

**Tips:**
- If the student is struggling, start with fewer clips and shorter objects (e.g., a crayon that is only 4-5 clips long).
- The CRA progression in this lesson: Concrete (physical paper clips) then Representational (drawing the clips in a row). Abstract comes in later lessons when students record just the number.
- This is the student's first formal measurement lesson. Expect it to feel slow. Accuracy and understanding the rules matter more than speed.

**Look-fors:**
- Does the student line up clips carefully with no gaps and no overlaps?
- Does the student start at the edge of the object?
- Can the student count the clips accurately and state the measurement in a full sentence?
