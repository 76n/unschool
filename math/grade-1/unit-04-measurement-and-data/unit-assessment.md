# Unit Assessment — Grade 1 Math, Unit 4: Measurement and Data

**Grade:** 1
**Unit:** 4 — Measurement and Data
**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** One-on-one; facilitator reads all items aloud; student writes answers, draws on provided clock faces, or measures real objects
**Estimated Duration:** 30-40 minutes
**Total Items:** 17
**Total Points:** 40

---

## Overview

This assessment measures student mastery of three core Grade 1 measurement and data skills: measuring length with non-standard and standard units, telling time to the hour and half-hour, and organizing and interpreting simple data displays. It also includes a problem-solving section that requires students to apply these skills in context.

**How to administer:** Read every item aloud exactly as scripted. The student writes or points to their answer. For measurement items, provide real objects and measuring tools. For time items, provide printed clock faces. For data items, display the tally chart and bar graph where the student can see them. Do not provide strategy hints during the assessment. If a student asks for help, say: "Try your best. Show me what you know."

## Materials Needed

- Pencil and eraser
- Student answer sheet (or blank paper)
- 6 printed blank clock faces (for items 8, 9, and 10; extras for mistakes)
- 5-6 paper clips (uniform size, for non-standard measurement)
- A small ruler marked in inches (for standard measurement)
- A crayon or marker (approximately 4 inches long)
- A piece of string or ribbon (approximately 7 inches long)
- A new unsharpened pencil (approximately 8 inches long)
- Printed tally chart (see Section 3, provided below)
- Printed bar graph (see Section 3, provided below)

## Section 1: Measuring Length

**Items 1-5 | 10 points**

*This section assesses whether the student can measure objects using non-standard units (paper clips) and standard units (inches), and compare lengths.*

### Item 1 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Place the crayon and the paper clips in front of the student.*

"Here is a crayon and some paper clips. I want you to measure how long the crayon is using paper clips. Line the paper clips up end-to-end along the crayon, starting at one end. How many paper clips long is the crayon?"

**Expected answer:** Approximately 4 paper clips (accept the student's accurate count based on the actual crayon length).

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: Correctly lines up paper clips without gaps or overlaps and states the correct count.
- 1 point: Lines up paper clips with minor gaps or overlaps but states a reasonable count (within 1 of the correct answer).
- 0 points: Does not demonstrate understanding of how to measure with paper clips, or count is off by more than 1.

### Item 2 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Place the ruler and the piece of string in front of the student.*

"Now use this ruler to measure how long this piece of string is. Put one end of the string at the zero mark. How many inches long is the string?"

**Expected answer:** Approximately 7 inches (accept the student's accurate reading based on the actual string length).

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: Aligns string to zero and reads the correct measurement.
- 1 point: Aligns string to zero but reads the measurement incorrectly by 1 inch, OR reads correctly but does not start at zero (starts at 1).
- 0 points: Cannot use the ruler to measure, or answer is off by more than 1 inch.

### Item 3 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Place the crayon and the piece of string side by side in front of the student.*

"Look at the crayon and the string. Which one is longer? How do you know?"

**Expected answer:** The string is longer. The student should explain by referencing measurement (e.g., "The string is 7 inches and the crayon is 4 inches, so the string is longer") or by direct comparison (e.g., "I can see the string sticks out past the crayon").

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: Correctly identifies the longer object AND gives a valid explanation.
- 1 point: Correctly identifies the longer object but cannot explain why, or gives a vague explanation.
- 0 points: Identifies the wrong object as longer, or does not respond.

### Item 4 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Place the unsharpened pencil and the ruler in front of the student.*

"Measure this pencil with the ruler. How many inches long is it?"

**Expected answer:** Approximately 8 inches (accept the student's accurate reading based on the actual pencil).

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: Aligns pencil to zero and reads the correct measurement.
- 1 point: Answer is within 1 inch of the correct measurement.
- 0 points: Answer is off by more than 1 inch, or student cannot use the ruler.

### Item 5 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:**

"You measured the crayon, the string, and the pencil. Can you put them in order from shortest to longest?"

**Expected answer:** Crayon (shortest), string (middle), pencil (longest) — based on the actual objects provided.

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: All three objects in the correct order.
- 1 point: Two out of three objects in the correct relative position.
- 0 points: Incorrect order or no attempt.

## Section 2: Telling Time

**Items 6-10 | 10 points**

*This section assesses whether the student can tell time to the hour and half-hour on analog clocks, write time in digital format, and draw clock hands.*

### Item 6 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Draw or show a clock face with the hour hand pointing to 3 and the minute hand pointing to 12.*

"Look at this clock. What time does it show?"

**Expected answer:** 3 o'clock (accept "3:00" or "three o'clock").

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: Correctly states 3:00 or 3 o'clock.
- 0 points: Incorrect answer.

### Item 7 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Draw or show a clock face with the hour hand pointing between 7 and 8, and the minute hand pointing to 6.*

"Look at this clock. What time does it show?"

**Expected answer:** 7:30 or half past 7 (accept "seven thirty" or "half past seven").

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: Correctly states 7:30 or half past 7.
- 0 points: Incorrect answer.

### Item 8 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Give the student a blank clock face.*

"I am going to say a time, and I want you to draw the hands on the clock to show that time. Draw the clock hands to show 10 o'clock."

**Expected answer:** Hour hand pointing to 10, minute hand pointing to 12.

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: Both hands are correctly placed (hour hand at 10, minute hand at 12), AND the hour hand is visibly shorter than the minute hand (or the student distinguishes them).
- 1 point: Hands point to the correct numbers, but the student does not distinguish the hour and minute hand (both same length, no labels).
- 0 points: One or both hands point to incorrect numbers.

### Item 9 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Give the student another blank clock face.*

"Now draw the clock hands to show 4:30."

**Expected answer:** Hour hand pointing between 4 and 5, minute hand pointing to 6.

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: Minute hand at 6 AND hour hand between 4 and 5 (or pointing at 4 is acceptable for Grade 1).
- 1 point: Minute hand at 6 but hour hand exactly at 4 or exactly at 5 (partially correct placement), OR hour hand is correctly placed but minute hand is at 3 instead of 6.
- 0 points: Both hands are incorrectly placed, or no attempt.

### Item 10 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:**

"If a clock shows the time is 6 o'clock, how would you write that with numbers? Write it on your paper."

**Expected answer:** 6:00

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: Writes 6:00.
- 1 point: Writes "6" without the :00, or writes "600" without the colon.
- 0 points: Writes an incorrect number, or no attempt.

### Item 11 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Place the tally chart in front of the student.*

"This tally chart shows the favorite fruit of some students in a class. Look at the chart. How many students chose grapes as their favorite fruit?"

**Expected answer:** 9

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: Correctly answers 9.
- 0 points: Incorrect answer.

### Item 12 (2 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Continue with the tally chart.*

"Which fruit was chosen by the fewest students?"

**Expected answer:** Bananas (3 students).

**Scoring:**
- 2 points: Correctly answers bananas.
- 0 points: Incorrect answer.

### Item 13 (3 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Place the bar graph in front of the student.*

"This bar graph shows how many books four children read this week. Look at the graph. How many more books did Mia read than Leo?"

**Expected answer:** 5 more books (Mia read 8, Leo read 3; 8 - 3 = 5).

**Scoring:**
- 3 points: Correctly answers 5 (or "5 more").
- 2 points: Correctly identifies Mia's total (8) and Leo's total (3) but makes a subtraction error.
- 1 point: Identifies one of the two values correctly but cannot complete the comparison.
- 0 points: Cannot read the graph or gives an unrelated answer.

### Item 14 (3 points)

**Facilitator script:** *Continue with the bar graph.*

"Who read the most books? Who read the fewest books? How many books did they read altogether — the person who read the most and the person who read the fewest, combined?"

**Expected answer:** Mia read the most (8). Leo read the fewest (3). Together: 8 + 3 = 11.

**Scoring:**
- 1 point for correctly identifying Mia as reading the most.
- 1 point for correctly identifying Leo as reading the fewest.
- 1 point for correctly computing 8 + 3 = 11.

## Section 4: Problem Solving

**Items 15-17 | 10 points**

*This section assesses whether the student can apply measurement, time, and data skills to solve word problems in context.*

### Item 15 (3 points) — Measurement Comparison

**Facilitator script:**

"Listen carefully to this story. Emma has a ribbon that is 6 inches long. Her friend Jake has a ribbon that is 9 inches long. How much longer is Jake's ribbon than Emma's ribbon? You can use your fingers or draw a picture to help."

**Expected answer:** 3 inches longer (9 - 6 = 3).

**Scoring:**
- 3 points: Correct answer (3 inches) with any valid strategy (drawing, subtraction, counting up).
- 2 points: Sets up the correct comparison (9 - 6) but makes a computation error.
- 1 point: Identifies the two lengths but does not attempt or complete the comparison.
- 0 points: Does not understand the problem or gives an unrelated answer.

### Item 16 (3 points) — Time Word Problem

**Facilitator script:**

"Listen carefully. Maria starts eating lunch at 12 o'clock. She finishes eating at 12:30. How long did it take Maria to eat lunch?"

**Expected answer:** 30 minutes (or "half an hour" or "a half hour").

**Scoring:**
- 3 points: Correctly answers 30 minutes (or half an hour).
- 2 points: Says "half past" or demonstrates understanding that it is between 12:00 and 12:30 but does not state 30 minutes explicitly.
- 1 point: Identifies both times correctly (12:00 and 12:30) but cannot determine the elapsed time.
- 0 points: Does not understand the problem or gives an unrelated answer.

### Item 17 (4 points) — Data Interpretation

**Facilitator script:** *Place the tally chart (Favorite Fruit) back in front of the student.*

"Look at the tally chart again. How many students voted in all? Count up all the students who chose a favorite fruit. Then tell me: did more students choose apples or oranges, and how many more?"

**Expected answer:**
- Total: 6 + 3 + 9 + 4 = 22 students.
- More students chose apples (6) than oranges (4). Apples received 2 more votes than oranges (6 - 4 = 2).

**Scoring:**
- 2 points for correct total (22).
- 1 point for correctly identifying that apples received more votes than oranges.
- 1 point for correctly stating "2 more."

| Level | Score Range | Percentage | Meaning |
|-------|-----------|------------|---------|
| **Mastered** | 34-40 points | 85-100% | Student demonstrates strong understanding of measurement, time, and data skills. Ready to advance. |
| **Approaching** | 28-33 points | 70-84% | Student shows partial understanding with specific gaps. Targeted reteaching needed before advancing. |
| **Developing** | Below 28 points | Below 70% | Student has significant gaps in one or more domains. Return to specific lessons for reteaching. |

**Additional mastery requirement:** The student must earn at least 4 out of 10 points in each section to demonstrate baseline competence across all domains. A student who scores 36/40 overall but earns 0/10 in Telling Time has not mastered the unit.

## Reteaching Recommendations

### If Struggling in Section 1: Measuring Length

**Common errors:**
- Leaves gaps or overlaps when measuring with paper clips.
- Starts measuring at 1 on the ruler instead of 0.
- Cannot compare or order objects by length.

**Reteaching strategies:**
- Return to lessons on non-standard measurement. Have the student practice measuring with cubes, paper clips, and other uniform objects. Emphasize: "No gaps, no overlaps — line them up end-to-end."
- For ruler errors, use a number line on the floor. Have the student walk from 0 to the target number while counting steps. Then connect this to the ruler: "The ruler is like a tiny number line. We always start at 0."
- For comparison, place two objects side by side with ends aligned. Ask: "Which one sticks out farther? That one is longer." Practice with many pairs before asking the student to order three objects.

### If Struggling in Section 2: Telling Time

**Common errors:**
- Confuses the hour hand and the minute hand.
- Reads 7:30 as "6:30" (reads the number the hour hand is closest to going forward instead of backward).
- Cannot draw hands on a clock or does not distinguish hand lengths.

**Reteaching strategies:**
- Return to lessons on telling time. Use a demonstration clock with moveable hands. Color-code the hands (e.g., short red hand = hour, long blue hand = minute).
- For half-hour confusion, practice with the demonstration clock. Move the minute hand from 12 to 6 slowly. Say: "When the minute hand is on the 6, it is half past the hour. The hour hand is between two numbers — it has gone PAST the smaller number."
- For digital notation, practice writing times on a whiteboard. Show the pattern: "The number before the two dots tells us the hour. The number after tells us the minutes. O'clock means zero-zero."

### If Struggling in Section 3: Organizing Data

**Common errors:**
- Cannot count tallies correctly (especially groups of 5).
- Cannot read bar graph values (does not align bar end with the number scale).
- Does not understand "how many more" as a subtraction comparison.

**Reteaching strategies:**
- For tally counting, practice making tallies in groups of 5. Use bundling sticks or craft sticks to make the connection physical. Count: "5, 10, 15 ... and 2 more makes 17."
- For bar graphs, use a ruler or straight edge to line up the end of the bar with the number axis. Have the student trace a line from the bar to the number. Practice with several bar graphs using different scales.
- For "how many more" problems, use connecting cubes. Build two towers of different heights and ask: "How many more cubes does the tall tower have?" Have the student physically remove cubes until the towers match, then count the removed cubes. This makes subtraction-as-comparison concrete.

### If Struggling in Section 4: Problem Solving

**Common errors:**
- Cannot identify what the problem is asking.
- Chooses the wrong operation (adds instead of subtracts for comparison problems).
- Cannot connect real-world context to mathematical operations.

**Reteaching strategies:**
- For measurement word problems, act them out with real objects. Cut two pieces of string to the lengths in the problem. Ask: "Which is longer? How can we find out how much longer?" Let the student discover that lining them up and counting the difference works.
- For time word problems, use the demonstration clock. Move the hands from the start time to the end time. Ask: "How far did the minute hand move?" Count by fives around the clock.
- For data word problems, have the student retell the problem in their own words before solving. Ask: "What is the question asking you to find?" Then point to the data display: "Where can you find that information?" Build the habit of reading the question, finding the data, and then computing.
