## Facilitator Administration Notes

- Read all questions aloud to the student. This is not a reading test.
- Allow the student to use physical shape manipulatives (pattern blocks, 3D solids, paper for folding) for any problem — this is consistent with the hands-on instruction throughout the unit.
- Do not prompt the student with correct vocabulary or answers. You may re-read a question if needed.
- Record responses on this sheet or on a separate answer sheet.
- Score immediately after completion using the Answer Key and Scoring Guide at the end.

## Section A: Identifying and Describing 2D Shapes (1.G.1)
**8 items — 16 points total (2 points each)**

---

**Item 1** (2 points)

Describe a shape to the student:

"I am a flat shape. I have three sides and three corners. What shape am I?"

Student answer: ___________________________

---

**Item 2** (2 points)

Describe a shape to the student:

"I am a flat shape. I have four sides and four corners. All four of my sides are the same length. What shape am I?"

Student answer: ___________________________

---

**Item 3** (2 points)

Describe a shape to the student:

"I am a flat shape. I have four sides and four corners. Two of my sides are longer and two of my sides are shorter. What shape am I?"

Student answer: ___________________________

---

**Item 4** (2 points)

Describe a shape to the student:

"I am a flat shape. I have zero sides and zero corners. I am perfectly round. What shape am I?"

Student answer: ___________________________

---

**Item 5** (2 points)

Ask the student:

"Name two shapes that both have four corners."

Student answer: ___________________________ and ___________________________

*Award 1 point for each correct shape named. Award full 2 points only if both are correct.*

---

**Item 6** (2 points)

Ask the student:

"I have a triangle. If I turn it upside down, does it stop being a triangle? Tell me why or why not."

Student answer: ___________________________

*Award 2 points for: No, it is still a triangle, because it still has three sides and three corners. Orientation does not change the shape's identity.*
*Award 1 point for: No, it is still a triangle (without explanation).*
*Award 0 points for: Yes, it changes.*

---

**Item 7** (2 points)

Ask the student to complete a drawing task:

"Draw a shape that has three sides and three corners."

*Award 2 points for a valid closed shape with three sides. Accept any proportions or orientation.*
*Award 1 point if the student draws three sides but the shape is not fully closed.*
*Award 0 points if the shape does not have three sides.*

---

**Item 8** (2 points)

Ask the student:

"How is a square different from a rectangle?"

Student answer: ___________________________

*Award 2 points for: A square has four equal (same-length) sides; a rectangle may have two longer sides and two shorter sides.*
*Award 1 point for a partially correct answer (e.g., "the square has equal sides" without mentioning the rectangle's sides).*
*Award 0 points for an incorrect answer.*

## Section B: Identifying 3D Shapes and Comparing 2D / 3D Shapes (1.G.1)
**6 items — 14 points total**

---

**Item 9** (2 points)

Hold up or describe a cube. Ask the student:

"What is the name of this 3D shape? How many flat faces does it have?"

Student answer: ___________________________ / ___ faces

*Award 1 point for naming cube. Award 1 point for answering 6 faces.*

---

**Item 10** (2 points)

Describe the shape:

"I am a solid 3D shape. I am perfectly round. I have zero flat faces, zero edges, and zero vertices. What am I?"

Student answer: ___________________________

*Award 2 points for: sphere.*

---

**Item 11** (2 points)

Describe the shape:

"I am a solid 3D shape. I have two flat circular faces and one curved surface. I look like a can. What am I?"

Student answer: ___________________________

*Award 2 points for: cylinder.*

---

**Item 12** (2 points)

Describe the shape:

"I am a solid 3D shape. I have one flat circular face and one pointy vertex at the top. I look like an ice cream cone. What am I?"

Student answer: ___________________________

*Award 2 points for: cone.*

---

**Item 13** (3 points)

Ask the student:

"Look at this square cutout and this cube. Tell me one way they are the same and one way they are different."

Same: ___________________________
Different: ___________________________

*Award 1 point for a valid "same" comparison (e.g., both are square, both are shapes, the cube's face is a square).*
*Award 1 point for a valid "different" comparison (e.g., the square is flat/2D; the cube is solid/3D).*
*Award 1 bonus point if the student specifically uses the word "face" correctly (e.g., "the cube's faces are squares").*

---

**Item 14** (3 points)

Ask the student:

"What 2D shape would you trace if you pressed one face of a cube against paper? What 2D shape would you trace if you pressed one flat end of a cylinder against paper?"

Cube face trace: ___________________________
Cylinder face trace: ___________________________

*Award 1 point for: square (cube face).*
*Award 1 point for: circle (cylinder face).*
*Award 1 bonus point if the student spontaneously explains why the sphere cannot be traced (no flat face).*

## Section C: Composing Shapes (1.G.2)
**3 items — 8 points total**

---

**Item 15** (2 points)

Ask the student (use physical pattern blocks or describe):

"I have two triangles. I push their long sides together so they touch. What new shape do I make?"

Student answer: ___________________________

*Award 2 points for: rectangle (or parallelogram/rhombus, depending on triangle type — accept any correct composite shape the student can justify).*
*Award 1 point if the student says "a bigger shape" without a specific name but demonstrates understanding of composing.*

---

**Item 16** (3 points)

Ask the student to build and describe:

"Use three shapes from the pattern blocks (or shape cutouts) to make a new composite shape. Tell me: what three shapes did you use? What new shape did you make?"

Shapes used: ___________________________
New shape: ___________________________
Description: ___________________________

*Award 1 point for naming three shapes correctly.*
*Award 1 point for describing the composite shape.*
*Award 1 point for any additional correct observation (e.g., "The small shapes are still inside the big shape.").*

---

**Item 17** (3 points)

Describe to the student:

"I built a 3D composite structure. A cylinder is on the bottom. A cone is on the very top of the cylinder. How many 3D shapes make up this structure? Name them. Which shape is on top?"

Number of shapes: ___________________________
Names: ___________________________
Shape on top: ___________________________

*Award 1 point for: 2 shapes.*
*Award 1 point for: cylinder and cone.*
*Award 1 point for: cone is on top.*

## Section D: Partitioning into Halves and Fourths (1.G.3)
**9 items — 18 points total (2 points each)**

---

**Item 18** (2 points)

Give the student a paper rectangle. Ask:

"Fold this rectangle into two equal pieces. What is each piece called?"

Student demonstrates fold and answers: ___________________________

*Award 1 point for correctly folding into two equal pieces (check by overlapping).*
*Award 1 point for saying: one half (or halves).*

---

**Item 19** (2 points)

Give the student a paper circle. Ask:

"Fold this circle into four equal pieces. What is each piece called?"

Student demonstrates fold and answers: ___________________________

*Award 1 point for correctly folding into four equal pieces.*
*Award 1 point for saying: one fourth (or one quarter).*

---

**Item 20** (2 points)

Ask the student:

"How many halves make one whole?"

Student answer: ___________________________

*Award 2 points for: two (2).*

---

**Item 21** (2 points)

Ask the student:

"How many fourths make one whole?"

Student answer: ___________________________

*Award 2 points for: four (4).*

---

**Item 22** (2 points)

Ask the student:

"Which is bigger — one half of a circle or one fourth of the same circle? Tell me why."

Student answer: ___________________________

*Award 1 point for: one half is bigger.*
*Award 1 point for explanation: when you cut into fewer pieces, each piece is bigger. (Accept any reasonable explanation that conveys this relationship.)*

---

**Item 23** (2 points)

Present a drawing task. Draw a circle on paper. Ask the student:

"Draw a line on this circle to split it into two equal halves."

*Award 2 points if the line passes through (or approximately through) the center, creating two approximately equal halves.*
*Award 1 point if the student clearly intends to draw through the center but is inaccurate by a wide margin.*
*Award 0 points if the line clearly does not create two equal halves.*

---

**Item 24** (2 points)

Present a drawing task. Draw a rectangle on paper. Ask the student:

"Draw lines on this rectangle to split it into four equal fourths."

*Award 2 points for two lines that create four equal sections (one horizontal and one vertical, or two horizontal lines, etc.).*
*Award 1 point if the student creates four sections but they are clearly unequal.*
*Award 0 points if the student creates fewer than four sections.*

---

**Item 25** (2 points)

Read the story problem:

"Four children want to share a round pizza equally. What should each child's piece be called?"

Student answer: ___________________________

*Award 2 points for: one fourth (or one quarter).*

---

**Item 26** (2 points)

Read the story problem:

"A rectangle is cut into four equal fourths. One child eats two fourths. How many fourths are left?"

Student answer: ___________________________

*Award 2 points for: two fourths.*
*Award 1 point if the student says "one half" with an explanation that two fourths equals one half — this shows advanced understanding and should receive full credit.*

## Answer Key

**Section A — 2D Shapes**
1. Triangle
2. Square
3. Rectangle
4. Circle
5. Any two of: square, rectangle, triangle does not qualify — must be two shapes with 4 corners. Accept: square and rectangle.
6. No — a triangle is still a triangle because it still has three sides and three corners. Orientation does not change a shape.
7. Student draws a valid closed 3-sided shape.
8. A square has four equal (same-length) sides. A rectangle may have two longer sides and two shorter sides.

**Section B — 3D Shapes**
9. Cube; 6 faces.
10. Sphere.
11. Cylinder.
12. Cone.
13. Same: the cube's flat faces are squares (or both have square-related shapes). Different: the square cutout is flat/2D; the cube is solid/3D.
14. Cube face → square. Cylinder face → circle. (Bonus: sphere cannot be traced because it has no flat face.)

**Section C — Composing**
15. Rectangle (or parallelogram/rhombus — accept with explanation).
16. Accept any valid combination of three shapes with a named or described composite shape.
17. Two shapes; cylinder and cone; cone is on top.

**Section D — Partitioning**
18. Student folds correctly; one half (halves).
19. Student folds correctly; one fourth (one quarter).
20. Two (2).
21. Four (4).
22. One half is bigger; because fewer pieces means each piece is larger.
23. Line approximately through center, creating two equal halves.
24. Two lines creating four equal sections.
