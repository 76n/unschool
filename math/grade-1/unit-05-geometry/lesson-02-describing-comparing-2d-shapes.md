# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 5, Lesson 2

## Topic
Describing and Comparing 2D Shapes — Same and Different, Sorting by Attributes

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Describe 2D shapes using the words sides and corners.
- Sort shapes into groups based on a chosen attribute (number of sides, number of corners).
- Compare two shapes and explain at least one way they are the same and one way they are different.
- Identify that shape identity depends on sides and corners, not color, size, or orientation.

## Prerequisites
- Knows the names circle, triangle, rectangle, and square (Lesson 1)
- Understands the terms sides and corners
- Can count to at least 10

## Materials Needed
- 2D shape cutouts in mixed colors, sizes, and orientations (at least 3 copies of each shape type)
- Two sorting mats (sheets of paper labeled "4 Corners" and "Not 4 Corners," or "3 Sides" and "4 Sides")
- Pattern blocks
- Whiteboard and dry-erase marker
- Sticky note for exit ticket

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review shape attributes from Lesson 1 and prime sorting thinking.

Place five shape cutouts on the table: a circle, two triangles of different sizes, and two rectangles of different colors. Say: "Look at these shapes. Without picking them up, what do you notice?"

After the student responds, ask: "If I asked you to put shapes that are alike into a group, how might you do it? What would go together?"

Accept any sorting idea (by color, by number of sides, by roundness, etc.). Say: "Today we are going to sort shapes using what we know about sides and corners."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model how to describe shapes with precise language and how to sort by attributes.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Attribute:** A quality or feature of a shape, like number of sides, number of corners, or whether the sides are straight or curved.
- **Sort:** To put things into groups based on what they have in common.
- **Compare:** To look at two things and find what is the same and what is different.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete.**
Pick up a triangle and a rectangle. Hold them side by side. Say: "Let's compare these two shapes. I am going to think out loud."

"First, what is the same? Both shapes have straight sides. Both shapes have corners."

"Now, what is different? The triangle has three sides and three corners. The rectangle has four sides and four corners. The triangle has three corners; the rectangle has four."

Set both down. Pick up a circle and a square. Ask the student: "Can you tell me one way these are the same and one way they are different?" (Accept: both are shapes, both are flat; different because circle has no sides/corners, square has four of each.)

**Step 2 — Pictorial.**
Draw two columns on the whiteboard. Label one column "3 sides" and one column "4 sides." Draw one triangle in the first column and one square in the second. Ask the student to tell you where to draw each new shape as you draw them: another triangle (rotated), a rectangle, a very small triangle, a tall rectangle.

Ask: "What would happen to the circle?" Let the student reason. (It goes in neither column because it has zero sides.)

**Step 3 — Abstract.**
Say: "If I have a shape with three sides, it must be a ___." (Triangle.) "If it has four sides and all sides are the same, it must be a ___." (Square.) "What about a shape with four sides but two sides are longer? It must be a ___." (Rectangle.)

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "I have a red triangle and a blue triangle. Are they the same shape? How do you know?"
- Count the sides and corners on both: 3 and 3. Same shape, different color. Color is not a defining attribute.
- Answer: Yes, both are triangles. Color does not change the shape.

**Example 2:** "Shape A has 4 sides and 4 corners. Shape B has 4 sides and 4 corners, but all sides are the same length. What is each shape?"
- Shape A: rectangle. Shape B: square.
- Same: both have 4 sides and 4 corners. Different: square has all equal sides, rectangle does not (necessarily).

**Example 3:** "I am sorting shapes. My rule is: shapes with 4 corners go on the left, all others go on the right. Where does a circle go? A triangle? A rectangle?"
- Circle → right (0 corners). Triangle → right (3 corners). Rectangle → left (4 corners).

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Sort and compare shapes together with guided discussion.

**Activity: Attribute Sort**
Place 10-12 shape cutouts (a mix of circles, triangles, rectangles, squares — different sizes and colors) on the table. Set out two sorting mats labeled "Has corners" and "No corners."

Work together:
1. Pick up each shape, count corners together, and place it on the correct mat.
2. After all shapes are sorted, count how many are in each group.
3. Ask: "Are all the shapes on the 'Has corners' mat the same shape?" (No — they can be triangles, rectangles, or squares.) "What is the same about all of them?" (They all have corners.)

**Follow-up Questions:**
- "Can you pick two shapes from the 'Has corners' pile that are different from each other? How are they different?"
- "Can we re-sort the 'Has corners' shapes into two smaller groups? What rule could we use?" (Example: 3 corners vs. 4 corners.)

---

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Apply shape comparison and sorting skills independently.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Name two ways a square and a rectangle are the same.
2. Name one way a square and a rectangle are different.
3. Name one way a triangle and a circle are the same. (Accept: both are flat, both are shapes.)
4. Name one way a triangle and a circle are different.
5. Sort these shapes by number of sides. Write the shape names in the right column.

   | 0 sides | 3 sides | 4 sides |
   |---------|---------|---------|
   |  ___    |  ___    |  ___    |

   Shapes to sort: circle, square, triangle, rectangle, triangle, circle, rectangle.

6. True or false: A triangle is still a triangle even if it is upside down.
7. True or false: A blue rectangle and a red rectangle are different shapes.
8. I sort shapes by number of corners. I have groups of 0, 3, and 4. Which shapes are in each group?

**Challenge:**
9. Draw a shape with 4 corners where all sides are the same length. Draw another shape with 4 corners where two sides are longer. Name both shapes.
10. Make up your own sorting rule for these shapes: circle, triangle, rectangle, square. Tell what rule you used.

### Interleaved Review
11. (From Unit 1 — number order) Write the numbers 1 through 5 in order from smallest to biggest.
12. (From Unit 2 — addition) I had 4 red shapes and 3 blue shapes. How many shapes were there in all?
13. (From Unit 4 — measurement/data) I counted shapes: 2 circles, 5 triangles, 1 square. Which shape did I count the most of?

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. Name two shapes that have four corners. (Square and rectangle.)
2. How is a square different from a rectangle? (All four sides of a square are the same length.)
3. Circle, triangle, rectangle — which one has zero sides? (Circle.)

**Expected Answers:**
1. Square and rectangle.
2. A square has four equal sides; a rectangle has two longer sides and two shorter sides.
3. Circle.

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Return to the concrete step: hold a square and a rectangle side by side, physically touch and count the sides together. For the circle question, trace around a circle and confirm no straight sides. Revisit at the Lesson 3 warm-up.

### If Mastery Is Met
Say: "You can describe and compare flat shapes using their attributes. Next, we will explore solid 3D shapes — shapes you can pick up and hold."

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Shape Sort Challenge:** Create a new sorting rule — such as "shapes where all sides are the same length" vs. "shapes where sides are different lengths." Sort all available shape cutouts. Be ready to explain the rule.
2. **My Sorting Book:** Draw six shapes. Under each one, write how many sides and how many corners it has. Then put them in groups of your choice and write your sorting rule.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Many students will sort by color when first asked to sort. Gently redirect: "Let's sort by something we learned about shapes — sides or corners."
- The square-versus-rectangle distinction is a common point of confusion. Reinforce that a square is a special type of rectangle (all four sides equal). If a student insists they are completely different, do not force the issue yet — return to it in the review lesson.
- Watch for students who think orientation changes shape identity. If this comes up, rotate a triangle cutout and ask: "Is it still a triangle? Count the sides and corners."
- This lesson is a good opportunity to build vocabulary in context. When a student says "pointy part," affirm it and then model the word corner: "Yes! That pointy part is called a corner."

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice:** Shapes with corners: triangles, rectangles, squares. Shapes with no corners: circles. The "has corners" pile can be re-sorted into 3-corner group (triangles) and 4-corner group (rectangles, squares).

**Independent Practice:**
1. Both have four sides and four corners; both have straight sides.
2. A square has four equal sides; a rectangle may have two long sides and two short sides.
3. Accept: both are flat shapes, both are closed shapes.
4. A triangle has three sides and three corners; a circle has zero sides and zero corners.
5. 0 sides: circle, circle. 3 sides: triangle, triangle. 4 sides: square, rectangle, rectangle.
6. True.
7. False — they are both rectangles. Color does not change the shape name.
8. 0 corners: circles. 3 corners: triangles. 4 corners: squares, rectangles.
9. First shape: square (equal sides). Second shape: rectangle (longer sides). Accept valid drawings.
10. Accept any valid sorting rule the student can explain.

**Interleaved Review:**
11. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.
12. 4 + 3 = 7 shapes.
13. Triangles (5 — the most).

**Exit Ticket:** 1. Square and rectangle. 2. All four sides of a square are equal. 3. Circle.
