# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 5, Lesson 3

## Topic
Identifying 3D Shapes: Cubes, Cones, Cylinders, and Spheres

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Name four 3D shapes: cube, cone, cylinder, and sphere.
- Describe each 3D shape using the words face, edge, and vertex (vertices).
- Identify 3D shapes using real-world objects.
- Distinguish 3D solid shapes from 2D flat shapes.

## Prerequisites
- Knows the four 2D shapes: circle, triangle, rectangle, square (Lessons 1-2)
- Understands the terms side, corner, flat
- Can count to at least 10

## Materials Needed
- 3D solid models or real-world objects: cube (block or die), cone (party hat or model), cylinder (toilet paper roll or soup can), sphere (ball or orange)
- 2D shape cutouts (circle, square, rectangle) for comparison
- Whiteboard and dry-erase marker
- Small sticky notes or labels
- Sticky note for exit ticket

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect prior knowledge of 2D shapes to the new world of 3D shapes.

Place a square 2D cutout and a cube (or small block) on the table side by side. Ask: "Look at these two things. How are they the same? How are they different?"

Guide the student toward noticing: "The flat shape is two-dimensional — you can't pick it up and feel the depth. The block is three-dimensional — it has length, width, and height. You can hold it."

Say: "Today we are going to learn about four 3D shapes — shapes that are solid and three-dimensional. You can find them all around your home and classroom."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce each 3D shape using hands-on objects, then define faces, edges, and vertices.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Face:** A flat side of a 3D shape. ("The flat part you could put down on a table.")
- **Edge:** A line where two faces meet. ("Think of the edge of a table — where two flat parts come together.")
- **Vertex (vertices):** A corner where edges meet. ("A vertex is a pointy corner of a 3D shape. Vertex is one corner; vertices is more than one corner.")
- **3D shape:** A solid shape that has length, width, and height — it takes up space.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete.**
Hold up the cube. Say: "This is a cube. It looks like a box. Let's explore it together."

Run a finger along one flat side: "This flat part is called a face. Let's count all the faces on the cube." Count each flat square face: "One, two, three, four, five, six. A cube has six faces. Every face is a square!"

Run a finger along one edge where two faces meet: "This line where two faces meet is called an edge." Count edges together (12 edges). "A cube has twelve edges."

Touch one corner point: "This point where edges come together is called a vertex. More than one is called vertices." Count the corners (8). "A cube has eight vertices."

Hold up the sphere (ball). Say: "This is a sphere. Run your hands all around it." Ask: "Are there any flat parts? Any edges? Any pointy corners?" (No.) "A sphere is completely round. It has zero faces, zero edges, and zero vertices."

Hold up the cylinder (roll). Say: "This is a cylinder. It looks like a can." Point to the circular ends: "These two flat ends are circular faces. This curved middle is not a flat face — it curves all the way around." Count: "A cylinder has two flat faces, one curved surface, and no vertices."

Hold up the cone. Say: "This is a cone. It looks like an ice cream cone." Point to the bottom circle: "One flat circular face on the bottom." Point to the tip: "One vertex at the top. The rest is curved — no edges."

**Step 2 — Pictorial.**
Draw a quick sketch of each 3D shape on the whiteboard (simple outlines — a square outline for cube, a circle for sphere, a rectangle with circles on each end for cylinder, a triangle shape with a circle base for cone). Label each with its name and write the key attribute count next to it.

| Shape | Faces | Edges | Vertices |
|-------|-------|-------|----------|
| Cube | 6 (squares) | 12 | 8 |
| Sphere | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Cylinder | 2 (circles) + 1 curved | 0 | 0 |
| Cone | 1 (circle) + 1 curved | 0 | 1 |

**Step 3 — Abstract.**
Say: "If a 3D shape has six square faces, what shape is it?" (Cube.) "If it is perfectly round with no faces, edges, or vertices?" (Sphere.) "If it has one pointy vertex and one circular face?" (Cone.) "If it has two circular faces and rolls on its side?" (Cylinder.)

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "I am holding a shape that looks like a can. It has two flat circular ends and a curved side. It has zero vertices. What 3D shape is it?"
- Clue: two circular faces, curved middle, no vertices → cylinder.
- Answer: Cylinder.

**Example 2:** "A shape has six faces. Every face is a square. All edges are the same length. What is the shape?"
- Clue: 6 square faces → cube.
- Answer: Cube.

**Example 3:** "This shape rolls and rolls. It has no flat faces, no edges, and no vertices. What is it?"
- Clue: no faces, rolls perfectly → sphere.
- Answer: Sphere.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice identifying and describing 3D shapes using objects.

**Activity: Feely Bag**
Place the four 3D objects in a bag or behind the student's back (or under the table). The student reaches in, feels the shape without looking, and describes it. The facilitator guesses the shape from the description, or the student names it directly.

Prompts if the student is stuck:
- "Are there any flat parts? How many?"
- "Is any part curved?"
- "Are there any pointy corners — vertices?"
- "Does it roll? Does it slide?"

Work through at least two rounds per shape. Then have the student hold each shape and say its name and one fact about it.

Follow up with three comparison questions:
1. "How is a cube different from a sphere?" (Cube has flat faces, edges, and vertices; sphere has none of those.)
2. "How is a cone different from a cylinder?" (Cone has one vertex and one circular face; cylinder has two circular faces and no vertex.)
3. "Which shapes can roll? Which shapes can slide?" (Sphere rolls; cylinder rolls on its curved side; cube slides; cone rolls in a circle on its curved side.)

---

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Apply knowledge of 3D shape names and attributes independently.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Name the shape: "I have six square faces, twelve edges, and eight vertices."
2. Name the shape: "I am perfectly round. I have zero faces, zero edges, and zero vertices."
3. Name the shape: "I have two circular faces and one curved surface. I look like a can."
4. Name the shape: "I have one circular face and one vertex at the top. I look like an ice cream cone."
5. True or false: A cube has more vertices than a cone.
6. True or false: A sphere can roll across a table.
7. Which 3D shape would a soup can look like?
8. Which 3D shape would a block look like?

**Challenge:**
9. Name something in your home or classroom for each 3D shape: a cube-shaped object, a sphere-shaped object, a cylinder-shaped object, and a cone-shaped object.
10. Compare the cube and the cylinder. Write two ways they are the same and one way they are different.

### Interleaved Review
11. (From Unit 1 — counting) Count the faces on a cube. Write a number sentence: ___ + ___ = 6 (to show two groups of 3 faces). (Accept: 3 + 3 = 6.)
12. (From Unit 2 — addition) There are 2 cubes and 4 spheres in a basket. How many 3D shapes are there in all?
13. (From Unit 3 — subtraction) I had 5 cones. I used 3 for a game. How many cones are left?

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. What 3D shape has six square faces?
2. What 3D shape is perfectly round with no flat faces?
3. What do we call the pointy corner of a 3D shape?

**Expected Answers:**
1. Cube.
2. Sphere.
3. Vertex.

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Return to the physical objects. Touch and name each shape. Focus on the shape with the error. If the student mixed up cone and cylinder, hold both up and contrast: one has a pointy top (cone), one does not (cylinder). Review at the start of Lesson 4.

### If Mastery Is Met
Say: "You know the four 3D shapes and how to describe them. In our next lesson, we will compare flat 2D shapes and solid 3D shapes and discover how they are connected."

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. **3D Shape Hunt:** Walk around the home or classroom and find two objects for each of the four 3D shapes. List or draw each one and label the shape name.
2. **Shape Journal:** Draw each 3D shape in a simple way and write one fact about it. Example: "A cylinder has 2 flat circles and 1 curved surface."

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Students often call a sphere a "circle" — gently redirect: "A circle is flat. We can't pick up a circle and hold it. A sphere is a solid ball shape. You can hold it in your hand."
- The vocabulary face, edge, and vertex is new and may take several lessons to stick. Review these terms at the start of Lesson 4 and Lesson 6.
- A cone can be tricky because it rolls in a circular path, not a straight line. If time permits, roll a cone on a flat table and watch it curve. This is a memorable demonstration.
- Do not worry about the technical term "curved surface" — use "curved part" or "curved side" and accept either phrasing from students.

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice:** Feely bag answers: cube (6 flat faces, no curve), sphere (no faces, all round), cylinder (two flat circular ends, curved middle), cone (one flat circular end, one pointy top, curved side). Comparison: 1. Cube has flat faces, edges, vertices; sphere is round, no flat parts. 2. Cone has one vertex and one circular face; cylinder has two circular faces, no vertex. 3. Sphere rolls; cylinder rolls on curved side; cube slides; cone rolls in a circle.

**Independent Practice:**
1. Cube.
2. Sphere.
3. Cylinder.
4. Cone.
5. True (cube has 8 vertices; cone has 1).
6. True.
7. Cylinder.
8. Cube.
9. Accept any valid real-world examples.
10. Same: both have faces, both are 3D shapes. Different: cube has square faces and flat sides only; cylinder has curved surface.

**Interleaved Review:**
11. 3 + 3 = 6 (or other valid combinations).
12. 2 + 4 = 6 shapes.
13. 5 − 3 = 2 cones.

**Exit Ticket:** 1. Cube. 2. Sphere. 3. Vertex.
