# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 5, Lesson 1

## Topic
Identifying 2D Shapes: Circles, Triangles, Rectangles, and Squares

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Name four 2D shapes: circle, triangle, rectangle, and square.
- Count the number of sides and corners on triangles, rectangles, and squares.
- Identify these shapes in different sizes, colors, and orientations.
- Distinguish a circle from the other shapes (no sides, no corners).

## Prerequisites
- Can name basic colors
- Understands the words "same" and "different"
- Can count objects up to at least 10

## Materials Needed
- 2D shape cutouts: several circles, triangles, rectangles, and squares in different sizes and colors
- Pattern blocks (triangles and squares from the set)
- Whiteboard and dry-erase marker
- Crayons and blank paper for tracing
- Sticky note for exit ticket

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior shape knowledge and build curiosity about shapes in the world.

Show the student a circle cutout and a triangle cutout. Say: "Look at these two shapes. Without using the names, can you tell me one way they are the same? One way they are different?"

Accept any answer (both are flat, different colors, different numbers of pointy parts, etc.). Do not correct shape-name usage yet — just listen.

Say: "Today we are going to learn the names for four special shapes and learn what makes each one unique."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the four shapes using physical models, then introduce defining attributes.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Side:** A straight line that forms part of a shape's outline. ("The edge of a shape — like the edge of a piece of paper.")
- **Corner:** A point where two sides of a shape meet. ("A corner is where two sides come together, like the corner of a table.")
- **Circle:** A perfectly round shape with no sides and no corners.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete.**
Pick up the circle cutout. Run your finger all the way around the edge. Say: "This is a circle. Feel how it curves all the way around with no straight parts. Count the sides with me: one, two... there are none! A circle has zero sides and zero corners."

Pick up the triangle cutout. Run your finger along each side and tap each corner. Say: "This is a triangle. Let's count the sides together." Touch each side: "One, two, three. A triangle has three sides. Now let's count the corners." Touch each corner: "One, two, three. A triangle has three corners. Three sides and three corners — that is what makes a triangle."

Pick up the rectangle. Say: "This is a rectangle." Count the sides: "One, two, three, four — four sides." Count the corners: "One, two, three, four — four corners."

Pick up the square. Say: "This is a square. Let's count sides and corners." Count together: "Four sides, four corners. A square looks like a rectangle, but all four sides are exactly the same length."

**Step 2 — Pictorial.**
Draw each shape on the whiteboard. As you draw, narrate: "I am drawing three sides to make a triangle." Label each drawing with its name and write "sides: 3, corners: 3" next to the triangle, and so on.

**Step 3 — Abstract.**
Say: "If a shape has three sides and three corners, what shape is it?" (Triangle.) "If a shape has four equal sides and four corners?" (Square.) "If a shape has no sides and no corners?" (Circle.)

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "I see a shape. It has four sides and four corners. Two sides are long and two sides are short. What shape is it?"
- Think: four sides, four corners, long sides and short sides → rectangle.
- Answer: Rectangle.

**Example 2:** "I see a shape. It has three sides and three corners. What shape is it?"
- Think: three sides, three corners → triangle.
- Answer: Triangle.

**Example 3:** "I see a shape. It has no sides and no corners. It is perfectly round. What shape is it?"
- Think: no sides, no corners, round → circle.
- Answer: Circle.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice identifying shapes using attribute clues, together.

Place several shape cutouts face-up on the table in a mixed pile. Work through each problem together, letting the student answer but offering prompts as needed.

1. Pick up any triangle. Ask: "How many sides does this have? How many corners? What shape is this?" (3 sides, 3 corners, triangle.)

2. Pick up a small circle. Ask: "How many sides? How many corners? What shape is this?" (0 sides, 0 corners, circle.)

3. Pick up a square. Ask: "Let's count sides and corners. What shape is this?" (4 sides, 4 corners, square.)

4. Pick up a rectangle. Ask: "Let's count. What shape is this?" (4 sides, 4 corners, rectangle.) Then ask: "How is this different from the square?" (The sides are not all the same length.)

5. Hold up a triangle that is rotated so one side is on top (looks like an upside-down triangle). Ask: "Is this still a triangle? How do you know?" (Yes — count the sides and corners. Three sides, three corners.)

Prompt the student: "We count sides and corners to find out the shape's name. The color and how big it is do not matter."

---

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice identifying shapes independently using attributes.

Ask the student to complete problems on paper or respond verbally. Write or dictate the problems.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Draw a shape that has three sides and three corners. What is its name?
2. Draw a shape with four equal sides and four corners. What is its name?
3. A shape is perfectly round. It has zero sides and zero corners. What shape is it?
4. A shape has four corners. Two sides are short and two sides are long. What shape is it?
5. Look at a triangle cutout turned sideways. How many sides does it have? How many corners? Does it change what shape it is?
6. True or false: A big triangle and a small triangle are both triangles.
7. Circle the shapes that have exactly 4 corners: (show or describe: circle, triangle, square, rectangle) — student circles square and rectangle.
8. Which shape has no corners at all?

**Challenge:**
9. Can you find two shapes in this room that look like a circle? Draw them.
10. Draw a triangle that looks different from the one I drew. (Encourage a very flat triangle or very tall triangle.)

### Interleaved Review
11. (From Unit 1 — counting) Count these shapes and write the number: [describe a group of 7 triangles]. How many triangles?
12. (From Unit 2 — addition) There are 3 squares on the left and 2 squares on the right. How many squares are there in all?
13. (From Unit 3 — subtraction) There were 5 circles. Two rolled away. How many are left?

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
Ask the student to answer on a sticky note or aloud.

1. What shape has three sides and three corners?
2. What shape has four equal sides and four corners?
3. I have a shape with zero sides and zero corners. What shape am I?

**Expected Answers:**
1. Triangle
2. Square
3. Circle

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Review the shape with the error. Use a physical cutout. Count sides and corners together again. Draw the shape on the whiteboard and label the sides and corners by number. Revisit at the start of Lesson 2.

### If Mastery Is Met
Tell the student: "You know the four shapes and their attributes. Next time, we will sort and compare shapes to find what makes them the same and different."

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Shape Hunt:** Walk around the room (or house) and find one real object that looks like each shape: something round like a circle, something with three sides like a triangle, a rectangle, and a square. Draw or list what you found.
2. **Shape Book:** Fold two pieces of paper in half and staple to make a small book. Label one page for each shape. Draw three different versions of each shape — big, small, tilted, etc.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Emphasize orientation: students frequently believe a tilted triangle is "not really a triangle." Always count sides and corners to confirm identity.
- If the student struggles to distinguish squares from rectangles, hold two shapes side by side and physically measure or compare the sides with a finger span.
- Do not introduce the word "vertex" (vertices) yet — save that for Lesson 3 when 3D shapes are introduced. Use "corner" consistently in this lesson.
- Keep the vocabulary wall or shape reference card visible throughout the lesson and for the rest of the unit.

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice:** 1. Triangle. 2. Circle. 3. Square. 4. Rectangle. 5. Still a triangle — 3 sides, 3 corners.

**Independent Practice:**
1. Student draws a three-sided shape; triangle.
2. Student draws a four-equal-sided shape; square.
3. Circle.
4. Rectangle.
5. 3 sides, 3 corners; no, it does not change.
6. True.
7. Square and rectangle (both have 4 corners).
8. Circle.
9. Accept any two round real-world objects (clock face, coin, plate).
10. Accept any valid triangle with different proportions from the teacher's drawing.

**Interleaved Review:**
11. 7 triangles.
12. 3 + 2 = 5 squares.
13. 5 − 2 = 3 circles.

**Exit Ticket:** 1. Triangle. 2. Square. 3. Circle.
