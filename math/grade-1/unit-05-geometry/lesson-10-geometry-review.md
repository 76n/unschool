# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 5, Lesson 10

## Topic
Geometry Review and Problem Solving — Mixed Practice Across All Geometry Skills

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify and describe 2D shapes (circle, triangle, rectangle, square) using attributes.
- Identify and describe 3D shapes (cube, cone, cylinder, sphere) using attributes.
- Compare 2D and 3D shapes and explain the relationship between them.
- Compose new shapes from two or more 2D or 3D shapes.
- Partition circles and rectangles into halves and fourths and use correct vocabulary.
- Solve multi-step geometry word problems using all unit skills.

## Prerequisites
- All skills from Lessons 1-9
- Vocabulary: side, corner, face, edge, vertex/vertices, compose, partition, half/halves, fourth/quarter

## Materials Needed
- Pattern blocks (for composing review)
- 3D solid models (cube, cone, cylinder, sphere)
- Paper circles and rectangles (for partitioning review)
- Crayons
- Whiteboard and dry-erase marker
- Index cards or sticky notes for vocabulary game
- Sticky note for exit ticket

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Rapid vocabulary review to prime all unit concepts.

Play "What Shape Am I?" — a fast-paced oral riddle game.

Read each clue and have the student answer as quickly as possible. Do not wait long between clues — the goal is to build fluency.

1. "I have three sides and three corners. What shape am I?" (Triangle.)
2. "I am a solid shape. I have six square faces. What shape am I?" (Cube.)
3. "I have zero sides and zero corners. I am flat and round. What shape am I?" (Circle.)
4. "I have two equal pieces. Each piece is one half. How many pieces did the whole have?" (Two.)
5. "I am a solid shape. I am perfectly round. No flat faces. What shape am I?" (Sphere.)
6. "I split a shape into four equal pieces. Each piece is one ___." (Fourth or quarter.)

If the student hesitates on any clue, that is a signal to spend extra time on that topic during guided practice.

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Re-teach and synthesize the unit's big ideas through a structured mini-review.

### Key Vocabulary (Full Unit Review)
- **2D shapes:** flat shapes with sides and corners — circle (0 sides, 0 corners), triangle (3 sides, 3 corners), rectangle (4 sides, 4 corners), square (4 equal sides, 4 corners)
- **3D shapes:** solid shapes with faces, edges, and vertices — cube (6 square faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices), cone (1 circular face, 1 vertex), cylinder (2 circular faces, 0 vertices), sphere (0 faces, 0 edges, 0 vertices)
- **Compose:** Put shapes together to make a new shape.
- **Partition:** Split a shape into equal shares.
- **Halves:** Two equal shares of a whole.
- **Fourths / Quarters:** Four equal shares of a whole.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — 2D and 3D Shape Review (Concrete).**
Place the four 3D solids on the table. Hold up each one. For each shape, ask: "What shape is this? Can you tell me one thing about it?"

Then quickly hold up pattern blocks or shape cutouts and ask students to name them and say the number of sides and corners.

Set one 3D solid next to one matching 2D shape (cube next to a square cutout). Ask: "How are these connected?" (A square is the shape of a cube's face. The cube has six square faces.)

**Step 2 — Composing Review (Concrete).**
Place two triangles on the table. Ask: "What shape can we make with these two triangles?" Push them together to confirm: rectangle (or rhombus with pattern blocks). "We composed a rectangle from two triangles."

Stack a cylinder and place a cone on top. Ask: "What 3D shapes make up this structure? Which is on top? Which is on bottom?"

**Step 3 — Partitioning Review (Pictorial).**
Draw a circle on the whiteboard. Ask: "How do I split this into halves?" Have the student instruct you where to draw the line. Color one half. Ask: "What do we call this colored part?"

Draw a rectangle. Ask: "How do I split this into fourths?" Have the student direct you. Draw both lines. Color two fourths. Ask: "What do we call the colored part? What do we call all four parts together?"

Ask: "Which is smaller — one half or one fourth of the same shape?" (One fourth.) "Why?" (When you cut into four pieces instead of two, each piece is smaller.)

**Step 4 — Synthesis Question (Abstract).**
Ask: "I have a circle. I want to share it with three friends, and I also want a piece for myself. That means I need four equal pieces. What are the four equal pieces called?" (Fourths or quarters.) "What will each person get?" (One fourth of the circle.)

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (Multi-step):** "I have a rectangle. I split it into four equal pieces. Then I eat two of the pieces. What fraction of the rectangle did I eat? What fraction is left?"
- Ate: two fourths. Left: two fourths. (Two fourths are the same amount as one half.)
- Answer: I ate two fourths; two fourths are left.

**Example 2 (Composing):** "I put two triangles together at their long sides and made a new shape. Then I put another identical shape next to it. What large shape might I have made?"
- Two triangles → rectangle. Two rectangles next to each other → larger rectangle.
- Answer: A large rectangle.

**Example 3 (3D):** "Name a 3D shape that can roll. Name a 3D shape that can only slide. Explain why."
- Can roll: sphere (all curved), cylinder (curved side), cone (curved side rolls in a circle).
- Can only slide: cube (all flat faces — no curved parts to roll on).
- Answer: Sphere, cylinder, or cone can roll. Cube can only slide.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Apply all unit skills together in a review game format.

**Activity: Geometry Challenge Stations**
Move through three short challenges. Set a timer (about 3 minutes each):

**Challenge 1 — Shape Sort**
Place a mix of 2D shape cutouts and 3D solid models on the table. Student sorts them into two groups: "Flat (2D)" and "Solid (3D)." After sorting, pick one from each group and ask: "What do these two shapes have in common?" (May share a face shape, e.g., cube and square.)

**Challenge 2 — Compose a Picture**
Use pattern blocks. Challenge: build a "house" using exactly 5 shapes. Student names each shape used and describes the composite structure.

**Challenge 3 — Partition and Tell**
Give the student a paper circle and a paper rectangle. Instruct:
- Fold the circle into halves. Color one half red.
- Fold the rectangle into fourths. Color two fourths blue.
- Ask: "On the circle, what part is red? On the rectangle, what part is blue? Which shape has more colored — the circle or the rectangle? Are they actually the same amount?" (Circle: one half colored. Rectangle: two fourths colored. They are equal amounts — one half = two fourths.)

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Demonstrate mastery of all geometry skills through mixed problem set.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Name a 2D shape with four sides where all sides are the same length.
2. Name a 3D shape with six flat square faces.
3. How many sides does a circle have?
4. A cube and a sphere — which one has flat faces? Which one has zero flat faces?
5. Two triangles are pushed together at their long sides. What new shape do they make?
6. A cone is placed on top of a cylinder. Name both shapes in this composite structure.
7. A rectangle is folded in half. What is each piece called?
8. A circle is folded into fourths. How many equal pieces are there?
9. True or false: One fourth is bigger than one half.
10. I ate two fourths of a circle. My friend ate one half of the same circle. Did we each eat the same amount?

**Challenge:**
11. Describe a 3D composite structure using at least three shapes. Name each shape and describe where it is positioned.
12. Draw a shape. Split it into fourths in two different ways. Show that both ways result in equal pieces.

### Interleaved Review
13. (From Unit 2 — addition) A cube has 6 faces. A cylinder has 2 faces. How many faces do they have in all?
14. (From Unit 3 — subtraction) I have 4 fourths of a pie. I eat 1 fourth. How many fourths are left?
15. (From Unit 4 — place value/counting to 100) How many sides do ten triangles have? (10 × 3 = 30 sides.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
This exit ticket covers the three standards of the unit.

1. (1.G.1) Name a 2D shape with exactly three sides and three corners.
2. (1.G.2) I put two squares side by side. What new shape do they make?
3. (1.G.3) I split a circle into four equal pieces. What is each piece called?

**Expected Answers:**
1. Triangle.
2. Rectangle.
3. One fourth (or one quarter).

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Identify which standard the student missed. Return to the relevant lesson content:
- Shape attributes (Lessons 1-2): review sides and corners.
- Composing (Lessons 5-6): use physical blocks to rebuild composite shapes.
- Partitioning (Lessons 7-8): refold paper circles or rectangles.
Administer the Unit Assessment only after the student demonstrates mastery on the exit ticket concepts.

### If Mastery Is Met
Say: "You have reviewed all of the geometry ideas from this unit. You are ready to take the Unit 5 Assessment. Great work!"

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Geometry Storybook:** Create a small book (6 pages) with one page for each major concept: 2D shapes, 3D shapes, 2D vs. 3D, composing, halves, fourths. Draw and label an example on each page.
2. **Geometry Scavenger Hunt:** Walk through the home and photograph (or draw) one example of each: a 2D circle, a 2D rectangle, a 3D sphere, a 3D cylinder, a composite structure (e.g., a can with a lid = cylinder + circle), something divided into halves, something divided into fourths.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson is intentionally review-heavy. Let the student lead as much as possible. Ask questions rather than explaining first — find out what the student remembers before re-teaching.
- Use the Warm-Up riddles diagnostically. Hesitation or errors on any particular concept tell you where to focus during Guided Practice.
- The insight that "two fourths = one half" is worth reinforcing if it came up in Lesson 8 or 9. It does not need to be formally stated, but affirming it builds proportional reasoning.
- Students who are ready for the assessment after this lesson can take it in the next session. Students who need more review can revisit specific lessons or do targeted reteaching before assessment.
