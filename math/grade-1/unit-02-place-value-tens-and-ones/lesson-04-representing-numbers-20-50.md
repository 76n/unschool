# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 2, Lesson 4

## Topic
Representing Numbers 20–50

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Represent numbers 20–50 using tens and ones
- Identify the tens digit and ones digit for any number 20–50
- Build numbers with bundles/blocks and record them on a place value chart

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of tens and ones (Lessons 1–3)
- Ability to bundle objects into groups of 10 (Lesson 2)
- Place value chart reading for numbers 10–19 (Lesson 3)

## Materials Needed
- 50+ counting objects (cubes, beans, or craft sticks)
- Rubber bands (for bundling)
- Place value mat (tens | ones columns)
- Base-ten blocks (tens rods and ones cubes)
- Hundred chart

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review tens and ones for numbers 10–19 and reinforce bundling.

1. "How many tens and ones in 14?" (1 ten, 4 ones) *(Lesson 3)*
2. "How many tens and ones in 10?" (1 ten, 0 ones) *(Lesson 2)*
3. "How many tens and ones in 19?" (1 ten, 9 ones) *(Lesson 3)*
4. "If I have 2 bundles of 10, how many objects do I have?" (20) *(Lesson 2)*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Extend place value understanding from the teens into the 20–50 range, showing that the same tens-and-ones structure continues.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Two-digit number:** A number with a tens digit and a ones digit (e.g., 23, 40)
- **Twenty:** 2 tens (20)
- **Thirty:** 3 tens (30)
- **Forty:** 4 tens (40)
- **Fifty:** 5 tens (50)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Build 23.**
Count out 23 objects. Bundle them into 2 groups of 10 with 3 left over: "2 tens and 3 ones." Place 2 tens rods and 3 ones cubes on the place value mat. Write on the chart: Tens = 2, Ones = 3. "2 tens and 3 ones = twenty-three."

**Step 2: Build 30.**
Count out 30 objects. Bundle into 3 groups of 10: "3 tens and 0 ones." Place 3 tens rods and 0 ones cubes on the mat. Write: Tens = 3, Ones = 0. "3 tens and 0 ones = thirty. The zero means no extra ones — every object is in a bundle."

**Step 3: Build 45.**
Count out 45 objects. Bundle into 4 groups of 10 with 5 left over. Place on mat: 4 tens rods, 5 ones cubes. "4 tens and 5 ones = forty-five."

**Step 4: Decade numbers pattern.**
Line up the tens rods for 20, 30, 40, 50. "What do you notice? 20, 30, 40, 50 — they all end in 0. That zero tells us there are no extra ones." Introduce the hundred chart. Find 20, 30, 40, 50. "See how each decade starts a new row? The tens digit goes up by one each row."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 34 = ? tens and ? ones. Think: 3 tens and 4 ones. On the chart: Tens = 3, Ones = 4.

**Example 2:** What number is 5 tens and 0 ones? Think: 5 tens = 50, 0 ones = 0. The number is 50.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student builds numbers 20–50 with hands-on materials and records on a place value chart.

**Activity 1: Build and record (5 minutes)**
For each number, student builds with base-ten blocks, places on the mat, and writes the tens and ones on a chart:
- 27 → (2 tens, 7 ones)
- 34 → (3 tens, 4 ones)
- 41 → (4 tens, 1 one)

*Facilitator prompt: "How many bundles of ten? How many left over? Write it on your chart."*

**Activity 2: Build and record continued (5 minutes)**
Continue with:
- 50 → (5 tens, 0 ones)
- 22 → (2 tens, 2 ones)
- 39 → (3 tens, 9 ones)

*Facilitator prompt: "Point to the tens digit. Now point to the ones digit. Do the digits match your blocks?"*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Apply tens-and-ones understanding to numbers 20–50 without physical support.

**★ On-level:**
1. 28 = ___ tens and ___ ones (2, 8)
2. 33 = ___ tens and ___ ones (3, 3)
3. 40 = ___ tens and ___ ones (4, 0)
4. 45 = ___ tens and ___ ones (4, 5)
5. 21 = ___ tens and ___ ones (2, 1)
6. 36 = ___ tens and ___ ones (3, 6)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. "I have ___ tens and 5 ones. My number is 25. How many tens?" (2)
8. "What is 3 tens and 0 ones?" (30)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "I am a number between 30 and 40. My ones digit is 7. What number am I?" (37)
10. "I have the same digit in my tens place and my ones place. I am between 20 and 50. What could I be?" (22, 33, 44)

### Interleaved Review
- "How many tens and ones in 16?" (1 ten, 6 ones) *(Lesson 3)*
- "2 + 9 = ___?" (11) *(Unit 1 review)*
- "Double 7?" (14) *(Unit 1 review)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Verify the student can decompose numbers 20–50 into tens and ones and compose a number from its place value description.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Build 38 with blocks. How many tens? (3) How many ones? (8)
2. What number is 4 tens and 6 ones? (46)

**Mastery = Both questions correct, with the student able to explain the connection between the blocks/digits and the number.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to building numbers physically. Have the student count out loose objects, bundle into tens, and count the bundles. The transition from concrete (objects) to representational (place value chart) may need more time.
- Focus on decade numbers first (20, 30, 40, 50) before adding in ones. If the student can reliably say "3 tens = 30," then add "3 tens and 2 ones = 32."
- Use the hundred chart as a visual anchor — point to the number and count the tens rows.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 5 (Representing Numbers 51–99).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Keep the hundred chart visible.** This lesson is where the hundred chart starts to become a powerful tool. Students should begin noticing that each row represents a decade, that the tens digit increases going down, and that the ones digit cycles 0–9 in every row. Don't force these observations — just make the chart available and let students start seeing the patterns.

**The zero in decade numbers is important.** When building 30 or 40, emphasize that the 0 in the ones place means "no extra ones." Students sometimes want to skip 30 and go to 31, or they think 30 "doesn't have" a ones digit. Reinforce: "Every two-digit number has a tens digit AND a ones digit. Sometimes the ones digit is zero."

**Watch for reversal errors.** Some students may write 32 as 23 (reversing the digits). If this happens, go back to the place value mat: "Which column do the bundles go in? Tens. Which column do the loose ones go in? Ones. Tens is on the left. Ones is on the right."
