# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 2, Lesson 3

## Topic
Representing Teen Numbers (10 + Ones)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Represent teen numbers (11-19) as 1 ten and some ones
- Connect teen numbers to the Make 10 strategy from Unit 1
- Write teen numbers in tens-and-ones form

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-2 (grouping into tens, place value chart)
- Kindergarten Unit 3 (teen numbers as "ten and some more")
- Unit 1, Lesson 5 (Make 10 to Add)

## Materials Needed
- Counting objects (beans, cubes, buttons, etc.)
- Place value mat (two-column: Tens | Ones)
- Ten-frame
- Number cards 10-19
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review place value chart from Lesson 2 and activate teen-number recognition.

1. "How many tens in 35?" (3) "How many ones?" (5)
2. "How many tens in 12?" (1) "How many ones?" (2)
3. "What is 10 + 4?" (14)
4. "What is 10 + 8?" (18)

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show that every teen number is exactly 1 ten and some ones, connecting the ten-frame, the place value chart, and the Make 10 strategy.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Teen number:** A number from 11 to 19 (eleven through nineteen)
- **Ten and some more:** A way to describe teen numbers — they are all "10 and some more ones"

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Fill a ten-frame completely.**
Place 10 objects on the ten-frame so every space is filled. "This is 10. The ten-frame is completely full — that means I have exactly 1 ten."

Now place 3 more objects beside the ten-frame (not on it). "Ten and 3 more. What number is that?" (13.) "Thir-teen. Listen to the word: thir-TEEN. The 'teen' part tells you there's a ten in there."

**Step 2: Connect to the place value chart.**
Move the full ten-frame to the Tens column (or represent it with 1 bundle/rod). Place the 3 extra objects in the Ones column.

"On the chart: 1 in the tens place, 3 in the ones place. That's 13."

| Tens | Ones |
|------|------|
| 1    | 3    |

**Step 3: Repeat with more teen numbers.**
- **11:** Fill the ten-frame (10), add 1 more. 1 ten and 1 one = 11. "E-LEV-en is a funny word, but it still means 10 + 1."
- **15:** Fill the ten-frame, add 5 more. 1 ten and 5 ones = 15. "Fif-TEEN."
- **17:** Fill the ten-frame, add 7 more. 1 ten and 7 ones = 17. "Seven-TEEN."
- **19:** Fill the ten-frame, add 9 more. 1 ten and 9 ones = 19. "Nine-TEEN. This is the biggest teen number — one more and we'd need a second ten!"

**Step 4: Point out the pattern.**
"What do all teen numbers have in common on the place value chart?" (They all have 1 in the tens place.) "That's because every teen number has exactly 1 ten. The only thing that changes is the ones digit."

**Step 5: Connect to Unit 1 Make 10.**
"Remember the Make 10 strategy? When you added 8 + 5, you made 10 + 3 = 13. That's 1 ten and 3 ones! The Make 10 strategy was really showing you place value all along."

Write: 8 + 5 = 10 + 3 = 13 → 1 ten, 3 ones.

"And 9 + 7 = 10 + 6 = 16 → 1 ten, 6 ones."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 14. Fill the ten-frame (10), add 4 beside it. Place on chart: 1 ten, 4 ones. Write: 14 = 10 + 4. The digit 1 is in the tens place, the digit 4 is in the ones place.

**Example 2:** 10. Fill the ten-frame (10), add 0 beside it. Place on chart: 1 ten, 0 ones. Write: 10 = 10 + 0. "Ten itself is a teen number in a way — it's the one with 0 extra ones."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student builds each teen number on both the ten-frame AND the place value chart.

**Activity 1: Build and record teen numbers (6 minutes)**
For each number, the student fills the ten-frame, places extras beside it, then records on the place value chart.

- 12: ten-frame full + 2 extras → 1 ten, 2 ones (12)
- 16: ten-frame full + 6 extras → 1 ten, 6 ones (16)
- 14: ten-frame full + 4 extras → 1 ten, 4 ones (14)
- 19: ten-frame full + 9 extras → 1 ten, 9 ones (19)
- 11: ten-frame full + 1 extra → 1 ten, 1 one (11)

*Facilitator prompt: For each number, ask: "How many tens?" (Always 1.) "How many ones?" (Varies.) The repetition of "always 1 ten" is the whole point.*

**Activity 2: Match teen numbers to 10 + ___ (4 minutes)**
Facilitator says a teen number. Student writes it as 10 + ___.

- "13" → 10 + 3 (3)
- "18" → 10 + 8 (8)
- "11" → 10 + 1 (1)
- "15" → 10 + 5 (5)
- "10" → 10 + 0 (0)

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Apply understanding of teen numbers as 1 ten and some ones.

Write the number of tens and ones for each teen number.

**★ On-level:**
1. 13 = ___ ten(s) and ___ one(s) (1 ten, 3 ones)
2. 18 = ___ ten(s) and ___ one(s) (1 ten, 8 ones)
3. 11 = ___ ten(s) and ___ one(s) (1 ten, 1 one)
4. 15 = ___ ten(s) and ___ one(s) (1 ten, 5 ones)
5. 17 = ___ ten(s) and ___ one(s) (1 ten, 7 ones)
6. 10 = ___ ten(s) and ___ one(s) (1 ten, 0 ones)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. "What teen number has 1 ten and 6 ones? Write it." (16)
8. "Is 20 a teen number? Why or why not?" (No. 20 has 2 tens, not 1 ten. Teen numbers only have 1 ten.)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "10 + ___ = 14" (4). "10 + ___ = 19" (9).
10. "10 + 10 = ___" (20). "Is 20 a teen number?" (No — it has 2 tens. This bridges to the next lesson on larger two-digit numbers.)

### Interleaved Review
- "Group 27 into tens and ones." (2 tens, 7 ones) *(Lesson 1)*
- "What digit is in the tens place in 53?" (5) *(Lesson 2)*
- "6 + 9 = ___" (15) *(Unit 1 — Make 10)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student understands teen numbers as 1 ten and some ones.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. How many tens in 16? (1) How many ones? (6)
2. 10 + 7 = ___ (17)

**Mastery = Both correct: the student identifies 1 ten and 6 ones in 16, and knows that 10 + 7 = 17.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student says 16 has "16 tens" or "6 tens," return to the ten-frame. Build 16 physically: fill the ten-frame (1 ten), count the extras (6 ones). Repeat with 3-4 more teen numbers.
- If the student struggles with 10 + ___ equations, practice filling the ten-frame and adding more objects. The visual of "full ten-frame plus extras" should make the connection clear.
- Reteach this lesson before moving on. Teen numbers are the simplest case of place value — if this is not solid, larger numbers will be much harder.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4. If the student found this lesson very easy (answered everything quickly and correctly), consider accelerating through the next lesson.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson bridges Kindergarten understanding with formal place value.** In Kindergarten, students learned teen numbers as "ten and some more" in a concrete, informal way. This lesson formalizes that understanding by connecting it to the place value chart and digit positions. For some students, this will feel like review — and that is fine. The goal is to make the connection explicit.

**If the student already gets this, move quickly to Lesson 4.** Not every student needs 40 minutes on teen numbers. If the student breezes through Guided Practice and Independent Practice with no errors, shorten the lesson and move on. The real challenge of Unit 2 comes with larger numbers and comparing.

**The Unit 1 connection is powerful.** Pointing out that Make 10 was "secretly" place value gives the student a sense of continuity. "You already know this — you just didn't know what it was called." This builds confidence.

**Language note:** The English words for 11 and 12 ("eleven" and "twelve") do not follow the "-teen" pattern. Acknowledge this: "Eleven and twelve have weird names, but they still follow the same rule: 11 is 1 ten and 1 one, 12 is 1 ten and 2 ones." Some students find it helpful to say "oneteen" and "twoteen" informally before switching to the proper names.
