# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 2, Lesson 8

## Topic
Comparing Two-Digit Numbers

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Compare two two-digit numbers using >, <, and = symbols
- Use place value reasoning to compare: examine the tens digit first, then the ones digit
- Explain why one two-digit number is greater than, less than, or equal to another

## Prerequisites
- Grouping objects into tens (Lesson 1)
- Tens and ones on a place value chart (Lesson 2)
- Representing two-digit numbers with base-ten blocks (Lessons 3-5)
- Reading and writing numbers to 100 (Lesson 6)
- Expanded form (Lesson 7)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (tens rods and ones cubes)
- Place value mat (tens | ones columns)
- Comparison symbol cards: >, <, = (cut from index cards)
- Paper and pencil

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students how to compare two-digit numbers by examining the tens place first, then the ones place, and how to record comparisons using >, <, and = symbols.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Greater than (>):** A symbol that means the first number is bigger. The open end faces the bigger number. (e.g., 54 > 32)
- **Less than (<):** A symbol that means the first number is smaller. The pointed end faces the smaller number. (e.g., 32 < 54)
- **Equal to (=):** A symbol that means both numbers are the same. (e.g., 47 = 47)
- **Compare:** To decide which number is greater, which is less, or if they are equal.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Build two numbers side by side.**
Build 34 and 52 on two place value mats, one for each number.

"Let's compare 34 and 52. Look at the tens first. 34 has 3 tens. 52 has 5 tens. Which has more tens? 52 has more tens. So 52 is greater than 34. We don't even need to look at the ones — 5 tens will always be more than 3 tens, no matter how many ones there are."

Write: **34 < 52** and **52 > 34**.

**Step 2: Introduce the symbols.**
Hold up the comparison symbol cards.

"This is the greater-than sign: >. The open, wide end always faces the BIGGER number. Think of it as a hungry alligator — it always opens its mouth toward the bigger meal!"

"This is the less-than sign: <. The pointy end faces the SMALLER number."

"This is the equal sign: =. We use it when both numbers are the same."

Practice the "alligator" memory trick: draw a simple alligator mouth around the > symbol opening toward the bigger number.

**Step 3: What happens when the tens are the same?**
Build 47 and 43 on the mats.

"Both numbers have 4 tens. The tens are the same! Now we need to look at the ones. 47 has 7 ones. 43 has 3 ones. 7 ones is more than 3 ones. So 47 is greater than 43."

Write: **47 > 43**.

**Step 4: What about equal numbers?**
Build 56 on both mats.

"56 and 56. The tens are the same — both have 5 tens. The ones are the same — both have 6 ones. These numbers are equal!"

Write: **56 = 56**.

**Step 5: Summarize the comparison strategy.**
"Here is our rule:
1. Compare the tens first.
2. If the tens are different, the number with more tens is greater. Done!
3. If the tens are the same, compare the ones. The number with more ones is greater.
4. If the tens AND ones are the same, the numbers are equal."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Compare 71 and 68.
Tens: 7 tens vs. 6 tens. 7 > 6. So 71 > 68.

**Example B:** Compare 35 and 39.
Tens: 3 tens vs. 3 tens — same! Ones: 5 ones vs. 9 ones. 9 > 5. So 35 < 39.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student compares numbers with support, building fluency with the symbols.

Compare each pair. Write >, <, or = in the blank.

1. 42 ___ 57
   *Facilitator prompt: "Look at the tens first. How many tens does 42 have? How many tens does 57 have?"*
   (42 < 57)

2. 83 ___ 79
   *Facilitator prompt: "Which has more tens?"*
   (83 > 79)

3. 65 ___ 65
   *Facilitator prompt: "The tens are the same. The ones are the same. What symbol do we use?"*
   (65 = 65)

4. 28 ___ 31
   *Facilitator prompt: "2 tens versus 3 tens — which is more?"*
   (28 < 31)

5. 54 ___ 58
   *Facilitator prompt: "The tens are the same! What do we check next?"*
   (54 < 58)

*If the student places the symbol incorrectly, have them build both numbers with blocks on the mats. Seeing 5 tens rods versus 4 tens rods makes the comparison concrete.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### On-Level
Write >, <, or = in each blank.

1. 36 ___ 63 (36 < 63)
2. 72 ___ 48 (72 > 48)
3. 55 ___ 55 (55 = 55)
4. 17 ___ 71 (17 < 71)
5. 89 ___ 92 (89 < 92)

### Stretch
6. "Write a number that is greater than 45 but less than 50." (Any of 46, 47, 48, or 49)
7. "True or false: 39 > 41." (False — 3 tens < 4 tens, so 39 < 41)
8. Fill in the blank with a digit that makes this true: 5__ > 56. (Any digit 7, 8, or 9 in the ones place: 57, 58, or 59)

### Challenge
9. "Sam says 29 > 31 because 9 is bigger than 1. Is Sam correct? Explain why or why not." (Sam is incorrect. We compare tens first: 29 has 2 tens, 31 has 3 tens. 2 tens < 3 tens, so 29 < 31. The ones digit does not matter when the tens are different.)
10. "I have two numbers. Both have 7 in the tens place. One number is greater than the other. What could the two numbers be?" (Any two numbers in the 70s with different ones digits, e.g., 73 and 78, where 73 < 78)

### Interleaved Review
*(From prior lessons)*
- "Write 74 in expanded form." (70 + 4) *(Lesson 7)*
- "8 + 7 = ___?" (15) *(Unit 1 review)*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. Compare: 46 ___ 64. Write the correct symbol. (46 < 64)
2. Compare: 53 ___ 51. Write the correct symbol. (53 > 51)

**Scoring:**
- 2/2 correct = Mastery
- 1/2 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0/2 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the place value mats. Have the student build both numbers with blocks and count the tens rods on each side. Ask: "Which side has more tens? That number is greater."
- If the student gets the comparison right but writes the wrong symbol, focus on the symbol specifically. Use the alligator trick: "The alligator's mouth opens toward the bigger number." Practice writing the symbol between 5 pairs of numbers.
- Drill 3-5 more comparisons where the tens digit is clearly different (e.g., 25 vs. 73, 81 vs. 14) before moving to comparisons where the tens are the same.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Try three-way comparisons: "Put these numbers in order from least to greatest: 42, 17, 83." (17, 42, 83) This previews Lesson 9.
- Ask the student to create their own comparison problems and quiz the facilitator.

## Extension (Optional)
**Comparison War (card game):** Each player draws two number cards (0-9) and arranges them to make a two-digit number. Both players reveal their numbers at the same time. The player with the greater number wins the round. If the numbers are equal, it is a tie and each player draws again. Play 10 rounds. This game reinforces comparison skills and adds a strategic element — the student must decide which digit to put in the tens place to make the biggest number.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors

1. **Comparing ones instead of tens.** A student may say 29 > 31 because 9 > 1. This is the single most common error. Always redirect: "Start with the tens. How many tens in 29? How many in 31?" Build both numbers to make the tens visually obvious.

2. **Reversing the symbol.** The student knows which number is bigger but writes < when they mean >. The alligator trick helps: "The open mouth eats the bigger number." Alternatively, teach: "The pointy end always points to the smaller number."

3. **Ignoring the ones when tens are equal.** When comparing 43 and 47, the student says they are equal because both have 4 tens. Prompt: "The tens are tied! Now we need a tiebreaker — look at the ones."

4. **Confusing "greater than" and "less than" language.** The student may understand the symbols but mix up the English words. Practice reading comparisons aloud both ways: "46 is less than 64" and "64 is greater than 46."

### Pacing Advice
This lesson can take 1-2 sessions. If the student grasps the tens-first comparison quickly, complete it in one session. If the student struggles with the symbols or keeps comparing ones first, split it: Session 1 covers Steps 1-3 (concept and symbols) with guided practice; Session 2 covers independent practice and the exit ticket.
