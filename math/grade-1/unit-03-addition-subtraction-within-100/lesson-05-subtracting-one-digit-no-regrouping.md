# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 3, Lesson 5

## Topic
Subtracting a One-Digit Number from a Two-Digit Number (No Regrouping)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract a one-digit number from a two-digit number when the ones digit is large enough (e.g., 47 − 3 = 44)
- Explain that only the ones digit changes when subtracting ones
- Use base-ten blocks and place value reasoning to solve

## Prerequisites
- Adding a one-digit number (no regrouping) — Lesson 4
- Subtraction facts within 10 (Unit 1)
- Place value: tens and ones (Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (ten-rods and unit cubes)
- Place value mat
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate subtraction facts and place value.

1. "8 − 3 = ?" (5) "9 − 4 = ?" (5) "7 − 2 = ?" (5)
2. "Break apart 58: tens? ones?" (5 tens, 8 ones)
3. "45 + 3 = ?" (48) — review Lesson 4

"Yesterday you added ones to a two-digit number. Today you'll take ones away."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show that subtracting a one-digit number changes only the ones place (when ones are sufficient).

### Key Vocabulary
- **Subtract ones:** Remove single units from the ones column
- **No regrouping:** The ones digit is big enough to subtract from directly

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Build and subtract with blocks.**
Build 47 on the place value mat: 4 ten-rods, 7 unit cubes. "I want to subtract 3." Remove 3 unit cubes from the ones column. "How many tens?" (4 — unchanged) "How many ones?" (4) "What number?" (44)

"47 − 3 = 44. The tens stayed at 4. Only the ones changed: 7 − 3 = 4."

**Step 2: Place value reasoning.**
"47 is 40 + 7. I'm taking away 3 ones. 7 − 3 = 4. The tens are still 40. So 40 + 4 = 44."

**Step 3: Multiple examples.**
- 58 − 5: Ones: 8 − 5 = 3. Tens: 5. Answer: 53.
- 36 − 4: Ones: 6 − 4 = 2. Tens: 3. Answer: 32.
- 79 − 9: Ones: 9 − 9 = 0. Tens: 7. Answer: 70. "Zero ones! That's fine — we get a multiple of 10."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 65 − 2. Tens: 6 (stays). Ones: 5 − 2 = 3. Answer: 63.

**Example 2:** 84 − 4. Tens: 8 (stays). Ones: 4 − 4 = 0. Answer: 80.

**Example 3:** 93 − 1. Tens: 9 (stays). Ones: 3 − 1 = 2. Answer: 92.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student subtracts with decreasing support.

**Activity 1: Build and solve (with blocks)**
- 56 − 4 = ? Student builds, removes cubes. (52)
- 38 − 6 = ? Build and solve. (32)
- 75 − 5 = ? Build and solve. (70)

**Activity 2: Think aloud (no blocks)**
- 49 − 7 = ? "Tens?" (4) "Ones: 9 − 7 = ?" (2) "Answer?" (42)
- 67 − 3 = ? (64)
- 81 − 1 = ? (80)

*Facilitator prompt: "Do the tens change?" (No.) "Could you do 42 − 7 this way?" (No, because 2 − 7 doesn't work. That's a different lesson!)*

**Purpose:** Practice subtracting one-digit numbers without regrouping.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm accurate subtraction of one-digit from two-digit (no regrouping).

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 76 − 4 = ___ (72)
2. 59 − 7 = ___ (52)

**Mastery = Both correct. Student subtracts ones digits and keeps tens unchanged.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to blocks. Build, remove, count.
- If the subtraction fact itself is the issue (e.g., 9 − 7), review Unit 1 facts.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 6 (Adding a One-Digit Number With Regrouping).

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Parallel structure with Lesson 4.** Addition and subtraction of ones without regrouping are mirror operations. The student should see the symmetry: adding ones makes the ones digit go up; subtracting ones makes it go down. Tens stay the same in both cases.

**Setting up regrouping:** At the end of this lesson, the student should intuitively feel that "something different happens" when the ones digit isn't big enough. This sets up the motivating question for Lesson 6: "What do we do when we can't subtract the ones?"

**Common errors:**
- Student reverses when ones digit is too small: 53 − 5, they do 5 − 3 = 2, answer 52. This is incorrect (the answer is 48, but that requires regrouping). For this lesson, only give problems where no regrouping is needed. Lesson 7 addresses this.
