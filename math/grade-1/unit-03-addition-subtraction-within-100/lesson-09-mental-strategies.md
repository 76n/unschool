# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 3, Lesson 9

## Topic
Mental Strategies: Counting On and Making Ten

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Use counting on by tens and ones to add within 100 (e.g., 46 + 23: start at 46, count on 20 → 66, count on 3 → 69)
- Use the Make 10 / break-apart strategy for addition with regrouping (e.g., 57 + 6: break 6 into 3 + 3, so 57 + 3 = 60, 60 + 3 = 63)
- Choose an efficient strategy and explain their reasoning

## Prerequisites
- All prior lessons in this unit (Lessons 1-8)
- Make 10 strategy (Unit 1)

## Materials Needed
- Hundred chart
- Number line 0-100
- Paper and pencil
- No blocks (this lesson focuses on mental and written strategies)

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Name and demonstrate two powerful mental strategies.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Counting on:** Starting at one number and adding by tens, then ones
- **Break apart (Make 10):** Splitting a number to reach the next ten, then adding the rest

### Instruction Notes

**Strategy 1: Counting On by Tens and Ones**

Problem: 46 + 23.
"Start at 46. Add 23 — that's 2 tens and 3 ones."
- Count on 2 tens: 46 → 56 → 66.
- Count on 3 ones: 66 → 67 → 68 → 69.
- Answer: 69.

Show on the hundred chart: start at 46, jump down 2 rows (56, 66), then move right 3 (67, 68, 69).

Another example: 52 + 34.
- Start at 52. Add 3 tens: 62, 72, 82. Add 4 ones: 83, 84, 85, 86.
- Answer: 86.

**Strategy 2: Break Apart to Make 10**

Problem: 57 + 6.
"57 is close to 60. How much do I need to get to 60?" (3) "So break 6 into 3 + 3."
- 57 + 3 = 60.
- 60 + 3 = 63.
- Answer: 63.

Another example: 38 + 5.
"38 is close to 40. I need 2 more to reach 40. Break 5 into 2 + 3."
- 38 + 2 = 40.
- 40 + 3 = 43.
- Answer: 43.

**When to use which?**
- Counting on works well for any addition — especially when adding a two-digit number.
- Break apart / Make 10 works well when one number is close to a multiple of 10 and you're adding a small number.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (Counting on):** 35 + 42. Start at 35. Add 4 tens: 45, 55, 65, 75. Add 2 ones: 76, 77. Answer: 77.

**Example 2 (Break apart):** 64 + 8. Need 6 to reach 70. Break 8 into 6 + 2. 64 + 6 = 70. 70 + 2 = 72. Answer: 72.

**Example 3 (Student's choice):** 27 + 5. Break apart: 27 + 3 = 30, 30 + 2 = 32. Or count on: 27, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32. Both work! Answer: 32.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices choosing and applying strategies.

**Activity 1: Try counting on**
- 53 + 21 = ? "Start at 53, add 2 tens (63, 73), add 1 one (74)." (74)
- 41 + 35 = ? (76)

**Activity 2: Try break apart**
- 48 + 4 = ? "48 + 2 = 50, 50 + 2 = 52." (52)
- 76 + 7 = ? "76 + 4 = 80, 80 + 3 = 83." (83)

**Activity 3: Student chooses**
- 36 + 30 = ? (66) — just add tens
- 29 + 5 = ? (34) — break apart works great (29 + 1 = 30, + 4 = 34)
- 44 + 23 = ? (67) — counting on works well

*Facilitator prompt: After each problem, ask: "What strategy did you use? Why?" There is no single right strategy — the goal is that the student can explain their thinking.*

**★★★ Challenge:**

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can use at least one mental strategy effectively.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 47 + 5 = ___ (52) — explain your strategy
2. 35 + 23 = ___ (58) — explain your strategy

**Mastery = Both correct with a coherent explanation of strategy (doesn't have to be a specific strategy — any valid reasoning counts).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- The student may need more practice with the component skills before combining strategies. Revisit whichever lesson corresponds to the error pattern:
  - Can't add tens? → Lesson 1
  - Can't regroup? → Lesson 6
  - Can't break apart? → Revisit Make 10 from Unit 1
- Let the student use the hundred chart as a scaffold.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 10 (Mixed Practice and Problem Solving).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson is about flexibility, not speed.** The goal is for the student to have multiple tools and know when to use each one. A student who uses break-apart for everything, or counting on for everything, is doing fine — as long as they can explain their thinking and get correct answers.

**The hundred chart is a strategy, not a crutch.** If the student wants to use it to verify, that's healthy. Over time, they'll internalize the jumps and not need the chart.

**Don't force one strategy.** If a student naturally counts on and gets the right answer, don't insist they break apart (or vice versa). Expose them to both, but honor what works.
