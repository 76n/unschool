# Facilitator Guide — Grade 1 Math, Unit 3: Addition and Subtraction Within 100 (Introduction)

**Grade:** 1
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 3 — Addition and Subtraction Within 100 (Introduction)
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Fully facilitated (adult present and actively guiding)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit extends students' addition and subtraction skills from working within 20 (Unit 1) to working with two-digit numbers up to 100. Students apply their place value understanding from Unit 2 to add and subtract tens, combine two-digit and one-digit numbers, and develop mental strategies for efficient computation.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Add multiples of 10 (e.g., 30 + 40 = 70)
- Subtract multiples of 10 (e.g., 80 − 30 = 50)
- Mentally find 10 more and 10 less than any two-digit number
- Add a one-digit number to a two-digit number without regrouping (e.g., 34 + 5 = 39)
- Subtract a one-digit number from a two-digit number without regrouping (e.g., 47 − 3 = 44)
- Add a one-digit number to a two-digit number with regrouping (e.g., 36 + 7 = 43)
- Subtract a one-digit number from a two-digit number with regrouping (e.g., 43 − 7 = 36)
- Add a two-digit number and a multiple of 10 (e.g., 34 + 20 = 54)
- Solve one-step word problems involving addition and subtraction within 100

**Why this matters:** This unit is the bridge between basic fact fluency and multi-digit arithmetic. Students who can fluently add and subtract within 100 using place value reasoning (rather than counting by ones) are set up for the multi-digit algorithms they will encounter in Grade 2 and beyond. Every strategy here — adding tens to tens, making a new ten, counting on by tens — becomes a building block for more complex computation.

---

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm:

- [ ] Adds and subtracts within 20 fluently (Unit 1 mastery)
- [ ] Understands place value: decomposes two-digit numbers into tens and ones (Unit 2 mastery)
- [ ] Reads and writes numbers to 100
- [ ] Skip counts by 10s from any decade number (10, 20, 30 ... 100)
- [ ] Understands expanded form (e.g., 47 = 40 + 7)
- [ ] Compares two-digit numbers using place value reasoning

## Materials Needed for This Unit

- **Base-ten blocks** (or bundled objects from Unit 2): Rods of 10 and unit cubes
- **Place value mat:** Two-column chart labeled "Tens" and "Ones"
- **Hundred chart** (10×10 grid numbered 1-100)
- **Number line 0-100**
- **Counting objects** (for regrouping lessons)
- **Paper and pencil**
- **Ten-frames** (two per student — for making a new ten in regrouping lessons)

| 2 | Subtracting Tens from Tens | 1 |

| 5 | Subtracting a One-Digit Number (No Regrouping) | 1 |
| 6 | Adding a One-Digit Number (With Regrouping) | 1-2 |
| 7 | Subtracting a One-Digit Number (With Regrouping) | 1-2 |

| 9 | Mental Strategies: Counting On and Making Ten | 1 |
| 10 | Mixed Practice and Problem Solving | 1 |
| — | Unit Assessment | Separate session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lessons 6-7 (regrouping) are the hardest concepts in this unit. Budget 2 sessions each if the student struggles with the idea of "making a new ten."
- If the student has strong skip-counting and place value, Lessons 1-3 may move quickly.
- Lesson 9 consolidates strategies — if the student is already using them naturally, this can be brief.

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Student counts by ones to add 30 + 40 (counts 31, 32, ... 70) | Not using tens as units | Go back to base-ten blocks. "30 is 3 tens. 40 is 4 tens. 3 tens + 4 tens = 7 tens = 70." Reinforce that tens can be counted just like ones. |
| Student says 10 more than 47 is 48 | Confuses "1 more" with "10 more" | Use base-ten blocks: add 1 unit cube (48) vs. add 1 ten-rod (57). "See the difference? 1 more means 1 one. 10 more means 1 ten." Use the hundred chart to show the visual pattern (10 more = one row down). |
| Student adds 36 + 7 and gets 313 (writes 3 tens, 1 ten, 3 ones) | Regrouping error — doesn't combine the new ten with existing tens | Build with blocks: 6 ones + 7 ones = 13 ones. Trade 10 ones for 1 ten. Now: 3 tens + 1 new ten = 4 tens, 3 ones left = 43. |
| Student writes 43 − 7 = 44 (subtracts from tens: 4 − 0 = 4, 3 − 7 ... can't, so does 7 − 3 = 4) | Reverses subtraction when ones digit is smaller | This is a classic error. Use blocks: "We have 43. We need to take away 7, but we only have 3 ones. We have to break open a ten." Physically unbundle a ten-rod into 10 units. |
| Student adds 34 + 20 and gets 36 | Treats 20 as 2 (ones) instead of 2 tens | Build with blocks: "20 is 2 tens, not 2 ones. 34 + 20: we add 2 more tens. 3 tens + 2 tens = 5 tens. 54." |

## Conversation Starters

- "What is 10 more than 63? How do you know without counting?"
- "If you have 50 and I give you 30 more, how many? Which part of the number changes?"
- "24 + 5 — what happens to the tens? What happens to the ones?"
- "36 + 7 — do you have enough ones, or do you need to make a new ten?"
- "I had 72 stickers and gave away 30. How many do I have left?"

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if:

- After repeated practice, the student cannot add or subtract multiples of 10 (e.g., 40 + 30) without counting by ones
- The student cannot find 10 more or 10 less than a given number after multiple lessons
- Place value decomposition (Unit 2) is still not solid — the student cannot tell how many tens and ones are in a number
- The student becomes extremely frustrated with regrouping — it may indicate the Unit 1 and Unit 2 foundations need more time

---

## What Comes Next

After this unit, students move to **Unit 4: Measurement and Data**, where they apply their number skills to real-world contexts — measuring lengths, telling time to the hour and half-hour, and organizing data. The addition and subtraction skills from this unit will be used throughout the rest of Grade 1 and reinforced heavily in Grade 2.
