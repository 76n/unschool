# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 3, Lesson 6

## Topic
Adding a One-Digit Number to a Two-Digit Number (With Regrouping)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Add a one-digit number to a two-digit number when the ones sum exceeds 9 (e.g., 36 + 7 = 43)
- Explain that when ones add up to 10 or more, you "make a new ten"
- Trade 10 ones for 1 ten using base-ten blocks

## Prerequisites
- Adding a one-digit number without regrouping (Lesson 4)
- Making 10 strategy (Unit 1)
- Place value: tens and ones (Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (ten-rods and unit cubes)
- Place value mat
- Two ten-frames (for visualizing the "make a ten" moment)
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review the Make 10 strategy from Unit 1 and set up regrouping.

1. "8 + 5 = ?" (13) "How did you figure that out?" (Make 10: 8 + 2 = 10, then 3 more = 13)
2. "7 + 6 = ?" (13) "9 + 4 = ?" (13)
3. "45 + 3 = ?" (48) — no regrouping review
4. "What do you think happens when we try 45 + 7? The ones would be 5 + 7 = 12. But can we have 12 in the ones column?" (Let the student wonder — this is the lesson's question.)

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach that when ones exceed 9, we trade 10 ones for 1 ten (regrouping).

### Key Vocabulary
- **Regroup:** Trade 10 ones for 1 ten (or 1 ten for 10 ones)
- **Make a new ten:** When ones add up to 10 or more, group 10 of them into a ten

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Build the problem that breaks.**
Build 36 on the place value mat: 3 ten-rods, 6 unit cubes. "I want to add 7." Place 7 unit cubes into the ones column. "How many ones do we have now?" (13) "Can we write 13 in the ones column? The ones column can only hold a single digit — 0 through 9."

**Step 2: Make a new ten.**
"Watch — when we have 10 or more ones, we trade." Count out 10 of the 13 unit cubes. Exchange them for 1 ten-rod. Move the ten-rod to the tens column. "Now how many tens?" (4) "How many ones?" (3) "What number?" (43)

"36 + 7 = 43. We made a new ten from the ones!"

**Step 3: Show with a ten-frame.**
Draw two ten-frames. Fill the first with 6 dots (the ones in 36). Add 7 more dots — fill the remaining 4 spots in the first frame, then put 3 in the second frame. "See? 6 + 7 fills up one ten-frame (that's the new ten) with 3 left over."

**Step 4: Step-by-step strategy.**
Write: 36 + 7
1. Add the ones: 6 + 7 = 13
2. 13 ones = 1 ten and 3 ones
3. Add the new ten to the tens: 3 + 1 = 4 tens
4. Answer: 43

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 28 + 5. Ones: 8 + 5 = 13 = 1 ten and 3 ones. Tens: 2 + 1 = 3. Answer: 33.

**Example 2:** 47 + 6. Ones: 7 + 6 = 13 = 1 ten and 3 ones. Tens: 4 + 1 = 5. Answer: 53.

**Example 3:** 54 + 9. Ones: 4 + 9 = 13 = 1 ten and 3 ones. Tens: 5 + 1 = 6. Answer: 63.

**Example 4:** 85 + 8. Ones: 5 + 8 = 13 = 1 ten and 3 ones. Tens: 8 + 1 = 9. Answer: 93.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student regroups with blocks, then transitions to mental strategies.

**Activity 1: Build with blocks (full support)**
- 29 + 4 = ? Student builds 29, adds 4 cubes to ones (13 ones), trades 10 for a ten-rod. (33)
- 46 + 8 = ? Build and trade. (54)

**Activity 2: Ten-frame strategy (visual support)**
- 37 + 5 = ? "How many ones? 7 + 5 = 12. That's 1 new ten and 2 ones. Tens: 3 + 1 = 4. Answer?" (42)
- 68 + 4 = ? (72)

**Activity 3: Mental (no blocks)**
- 55 + 7 = ? "5 + 7 = 12. New ten. 5 + 1 = 6 tens, 2 ones." (62)
- 79 + 3 = ? (82)

*Facilitator prompt: After each problem, ask: "Did you have to make a new ten? How do you know?" (Yes, because the ones added up to more than 9.)*

**★★ Stretch:**

8. "Lee had 38 toy cars. He got 6 more for his birthday. How many does he have now?" (44)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "Which of these need regrouping? Circle them: 42 + 5, 37 + 8, 61 + 3, 58 + 6, 44 + 4." (37 + 8 and 58 + 6 need regrouping because 7+8=15 and 8+6=14)
10. "What is 96 + 7?" (103) "Hmm — that's bigger than 100! Is that OK?" (Yes! 9 tens + 1 new ten = 10 tens = 100, plus 3 ones = 103.)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can add with regrouping.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 47 + 6 = ___ (53)
2. 68 + 5 = ___ (73)

**Mastery = Both correct. Student recognizes when regrouping is needed and correctly forms the new ten.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to blocks. Every problem should be built physically: add ones cubes, count total ones, trade 10 for a rod, count tens and ones.
- Use ten-frames to make the "fill up to 10" visual concrete. If the student's Make 10 strategy from Unit 1 is shaky, spend extra time on that before continuing.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (Subtracting a One-Digit Number With Regrouping).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is often the hardest lesson in the unit.** Regrouping is a conceptual leap. Some students will need 2 sessions here. That is perfectly fine.

**The Make 10 connection is critical.** Regrouping in addition IS the Make 10 strategy applied to place value. A student who can fluently "make 10" (from Unit 1) should see the connection: "6 + 7 — I need 4 more to make 10, then 3 left over. So 36 becomes 40 + 3 = 43."

**Common errors:**
- Student writes both digits of the ones sum: 36 + 7 = 313 (3 tens, 13 ones written as-is). Reteach the trade: "You can't have 13 ones. Trade 10 of them for a ten."
- Student forgets to add the new ten to the tens: 36 + 7 = 33 (3 tens, 3 ones — forgot the extra ten). "You made a new ten! Where does it go? In the tens column."
- Student adds the ones correctly but then also adds the one-digit number to the tens: 36 + 7 = 113. Reteach: "The 7 goes only to the ones. The tens get the new ten from regrouping, not the 7 itself."
