# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 3, Lesson 8

## Topic
Adding a Two-Digit Number and a Multiple of 10

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Add a multiple of 10 to any two-digit number (e.g., 34 + 20 = 54)
- Explain that only the tens digit changes; the ones digit stays the same
- Connect this to the "10 more" concept from Lesson 3, extended to 20 more, 30 more, etc.

## Prerequisites
- Adding tens to tens (Lesson 1)
- Finding 10 more and 10 less (Lesson 3)
- Place value: tens and ones (Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (ten-rods and unit cubes)
- Place value mat
- Hundred chart
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate 10 more and adding tens.

1. "10 more than 34 = ?" (44) "10 more than 44 = ?" (54) "So 20 more than 34 = ?" (54)
2. "30 + 40 = ?" (70) — Lesson 1 review
3. "47 + 5 = ?" (52) — Lesson 6 review (regrouping)

"Today we put two ideas together: adding tens to a number that already has ones."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show that adding a multiple of 10 changes only the tens digit.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Multiple of 10:** A number made entirely of tens — 10, 20, 30, 40, ...

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Build with blocks.**
Build 34 on the place value mat: 3 ten-rods, 4 unit cubes. "I want to add 20." Add 2 ten-rods to the tens column. "How many tens now?" (5) "How many ones?" (4 — unchanged) "What number?" (54)

"34 + 20 = 54. The ones stayed at 4. Only the tens changed: 3 + 2 = 5."

**Step 2: Connect to adding tens (Lesson 1).**
"Remember adding tens to tens? 30 + 20 = 50. This is the same — the tens add — but now we carry along the 4 ones."

**Step 3: Show on the hundred chart.**
Point to 34. "Adding 20 means going down 2 rows: 44, 54." The ones column (4) doesn't change.

**Step 4: Multiple examples.**
- 47 + 30: Tens: 4 + 3 = 7. Ones: 7. Answer: 77.
- 25 + 40: Tens: 2 + 4 = 6. Ones: 5. Answer: 65.
- 53 + 10: Tens: 5 + 1 = 6. Ones: 3. Answer: 63. (Same as "10 more.")
- 18 + 50: Tens: 1 + 5 = 6. Ones: 8. Answer: 68.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 42 + 30. Tens: 4 + 3 = 7. Ones: 2. Answer: 72.

**Example 2:** 65 + 20. Tens: 6 + 2 = 8. Ones: 5. Answer: 85.

**Example 3:** 37 + 50. Tens: 3 + 5 = 8. Ones: 7. Answer: 87.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student adds multiples of 10 to two-digit numbers with decreasing support.

**Activity 1: Build and solve (with blocks)**
- 56 + 20 = ? Build 56, add 2 ten-rods. (76)
- 23 + 40 = ? Build and solve. (63)
- 41 + 50 = ? Build and solve. (91)

**Activity 2: Mental (no blocks)**
- 67 + 20 = ? "Tens: 6 + 2 = 8. Ones: 7. Answer?" (87)
- 38 + 30 = ? (68)
- 14 + 60 = ? (74)
- 72 + 10 = ? (82)

*Facilitator prompt: "Which digit changes? Which stays the same?" The student should consistently say: "The tens change. The ones stay."*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency with this type of addition.

**★ On-level:**
1. 35 + 40 = ___ (75)
2. 52 + 30 = ___ (82)
3. 48 + 20 = ___ (68)
4. 61 + 30 = ___ (91)
5. 27 + 50 = ___ (77)
6. 13 + 70 = ___ (83)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. Fill in the blank: 43 + ___ = 73 (30)
8. Fill in the blank: ___ + 40 = 86 (46)
9. "Emma walked 24 minutes in the morning and 30 minutes in the afternoon. How many minutes total?" (54)

**★★★ Challenge:**
10. "Start at 15. Add 20 three times: 15 → ___ → ___ → ___." (35, 55, 75) "What pattern do you see?" (The tens go up by 2 each time, the ones stay at 5.)

### Interleaved Review
- "15 − 9 = ___" (6) *(Unit 1)*
- "52 − 7 = ___" (45) *(Lesson 7)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm accurate addition of multiples of 10 to two-digit numbers.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 46 + 30 = ___ (76)
2. 58 + 20 = ___ (78)

**Mastery = Both correct. Student adds the tens and keeps the ones unchanged.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to base-ten blocks and the place value mat. Adding ten-rods to the tens column while the ones cubes stay put is very visual.
- Use the hundred chart: start at the number, jump down by the appropriate number of rows.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 9 (Mental Strategies: Counting On and Making Ten).

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson feels easy if Lessons 1-3 are solid.** It's essentially the same idea — adding tens — but now the starting number has ones too. If a student breezes through this, that's great. Use the extra time for word problems.

**Important note on sums exceeding 100:** Some problems (like 72 + 30 = 102) go past 100. For this lesson, keep sums at or below 100 to avoid confusion. Sums past 100 are a Grade 2 topic.

**Common errors:**
- Student adds the multiple of 10 to both the tens AND ones: 34 + 20 = 56 (added 2 to both). Reteach: "20 is 2 tens, 0 ones. Add the 2 to the tens only."
- Student treats 20 as 2: 34 + 20 = 36. "20 is twenty — that's 2 tens, not 2 ones."
