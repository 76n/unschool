# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 3, Lesson 7

## Topic
Subtracting a One-Digit Number from a Two-Digit Number (With Regrouping)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract a one-digit number from a two-digit number when the ones digit is too small (e.g., 43 − 7 = 36)
- Explain that you "break open a ten" to get more ones when needed
- Trade 1 ten for 10 ones using base-ten blocks

## Prerequisites
- Subtracting a one-digit number without regrouping (Lesson 5)
- Adding with regrouping (Lesson 6 — understands the concept of trading)
- Subtraction facts within 20 (Unit 1)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (ten-rods and unit cubes)
- Place value mat
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review subtraction facts and the regrouping idea from Lesson 6.

1. "13 − 7 = ?" (6) "15 − 8 = ?" (7) "12 − 5 = ?" (7)
2. "58 − 3 = ?" (55) — no regrouping review
3. "What about 52 − 7? The ones are 2 and we need to take away 7. Is 2 big enough?" (No!) "So what do we do?"

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach that when there aren't enough ones to subtract, we break a ten into 10 ones.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Break a ten / unbundle:** Trade 1 ten for 10 ones to make subtraction possible
- **Regroup:** Rearrange tens and ones (in subtraction: break a ten into ones)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Build the problem that doesn't work directly.**
Build 43 on the place value mat: 4 ten-rods, 3 unit cubes. "I want to subtract 7. Can I take 7 from 3 ones?" (No — there aren't enough.) "We're stuck. We need more ones."

**Step 2: Break a ten.**
"Watch — I'm going to break one of the tens." Take 1 ten-rod from the tens column, replace it with 10 unit cubes in the ones column. "How many tens now?" (3) "How many ones now?" (13) "Did the total change?" (No — 3 tens and 13 ones is still 43. We just rearranged.)

**Step 3: Now subtract.**
"Now I can take away 7 ones from 13 ones. 13 − 7 = 6." Remove 7 cubes. "How many tens?" (3) "How many ones?" (6) "What number?" (36)

"43 − 7 = 36."

**Step 4: Step-by-step strategy.**
Write: 43 − 7
1. Can I take 7 from 3? No (3 < 7).
2. Break a ten: 4 tens → 3 tens, 3 ones → 13 ones.
3. Subtract ones: 13 − 7 = 6.
4. Tens stay at 3.
5. Answer: 36.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 52 − 8. Can I take 8 from 2? No. Break a ten: 4 tens, 12 ones. 12 − 8 = 4. Answer: 44.

**Example 2:** 31 − 5. Can I take 5 from 1? No. Break a ten: 2 tens, 11 ones. 11 − 5 = 6. Answer: 26.

**Example 3:** 60 − 3. Can I take 3 from 0? No. Break a ten: 5 tens, 10 ones. 10 − 3 = 7. Answer: 57.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student regroups with blocks, then transitions to mental.

**Activity 1: Build with blocks (full support)**
- 42 − 6 = ? Build 42, try to remove 6 ones (only 2), break a ten (3 tens, 12 ones), remove 6. (36)
- 55 − 8 = ? Build and break. (47)

**Activity 2: Think through the steps**
- 34 − 9 = ? "3 ones, taking away 9. Need to break a ten. 2 tens, 13 ones. 13 − 9 = 4. Answer?" (24) *(Note: that's wrong — let me recalculate. 34: 3 tens 4 ones. Break a ten: 2 tens, 14 ones. 14 − 9 = 5. Answer: 25.)*

Let me redo this properly:
- 34 − 9 = ? "4 ones, taking away 9. Can't do it. Break a ten: 2 tens, 14 ones. 14 − 9 = 5." (25)
- 61 − 4 = ? "1 one, taking away 4. Break a ten: 5 tens, 11 ones. 11 − 4 = 7." (57)

*Facilitator prompt: Always start by asking: "Do you have enough ones?" This is the decision point. If yes → subtract directly (Lesson 5). If no → break a ten first.*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can subtract with regrouping.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 53 − 6 = ___ (47)
2. 40 − 7 = ___ (33)

**Mastery = Both correct. Student correctly breaks a ten and subtracts.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to blocks for every problem. The physical act of unbundling a ten-rod into 10 cubes must be repeated until automatic.
- If the student does 53 − 6 = 53 (doesn't subtract) or 53 − 6 = 57 (adds instead), revisit the meaning of subtraction.
- If the student reverses digits (53 − 6 → does 6 − 3 = 3, answers 50 + 3 = 53), this is the classic "bigger minus smaller" error. Emphasize: "You must take 6 from the ones you have, not the other way around."

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 8 (Adding a Two-Digit Number and a Multiple of 10).

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson may need 2 sessions.** Regrouping in subtraction is typically harder than regrouping in addition. In addition, you're combining ones until they overflow into a new ten — a somewhat natural process. In subtraction, you're deliberately breaking something apart before you can take away, which feels less intuitive.

**The decision question is key:** "Do I have enough ones?" Train the student to ask this before every subtraction problem. Over time, it becomes automatic.

**Common errors:**
- Student does 6 − 3 instead of breaking a ten for 53 − 6. "You have 3 ones and need to subtract 6. You can't turn it around."
- Student breaks a ten but forgets to reduce the tens digit: 43 − 7, they get 13 ones, subtract 7 = 6, but keep 4 tens → 46. "When you break a ten, you lose a ten. 4 tens became 3 tens."
- Student subtracts from 10 instead of from the full ones: 43 − 7, they break a ten to get 10 ones, do 10 − 7 = 3, forget the original 3 ones → 33. "You had 3 ones already plus 10 more = 13 ones. 13 − 7 = 6."
