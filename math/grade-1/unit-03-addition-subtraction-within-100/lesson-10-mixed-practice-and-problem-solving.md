# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 3, Lesson 10

## Topic
Mixed Practice and Problem Solving

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve a variety of addition and subtraction problems within 100
- Choose appropriate strategies for different problem types
- Solve one-step word problems involving addition and subtraction within 100

## Prerequisites
- All prior lessons in this unit (Lessons 1-9)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (available for reference, not required)
- Hundred chart (available for reference)
- Paper and pencil

"Today you're going to use EVERYTHING you've learned this unit. Some problems add tens, some add ones, some need regrouping, some don't. You decide what to do."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model how to read a problem, identify the type, and choose a strategy.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Read and understand.**
Present: "There are 47 fish in the pond. 20 more swim in. How many fish are there now?"

"What's happening? Fish are being added. So I add. 47 + 20."

**Step 2: Choose a strategy.**
"47 + 20. I'm adding a multiple of 10. The ones stay at 7, the tens go up by 2: 4 + 2 = 6 tens. Answer: 67."

**Step 3: Verify.**
"Does 67 make sense? I started with 47 and added 20. 67 is 20 more than 47. Yes."

**Model Problem 2:** "There are 52 birds on a tree. 8 fly away. How many are left?"

"Birds fly away — I subtract. 52 − 8. Do I have enough ones? 2 < 8 — no. Break a ten: 4 tens, 12 ones. 12 − 8 = 4. Answer: 44."

**Model Problem 3:** "A farmer has 35 chickens and 24 cows. How many animals altogether?"

"Altogether — I add. 35 + 24. Count on: start at 35, add 2 tens (45, 55), add 4 ones (56, 57, 58, 59). Answer: 59."

### Key Decision Flowchart
Present to the student:
1. **Am I adding or subtracting?** (Look for clues: "more," "altogether," "in all" = add. "Fewer," "left," "gave away" = subtract.)
2. **What kind of numbers?** (Tens only? One-digit to two-digit? Two-digit + two-digit?)
3. **Do I need to regroup?** (Check if ones add past 9 or if ones aren't enough to subtract.)
4. **Solve and check.** (Does my answer make sense?)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student solves mixed problems with facilitator coaching on strategy selection.

**Problem 1:** 64 + 30 = ? "What type?" (Two-digit + multiple of 10) "Strategy?" (Add tens) Answer: 94.

**Problem 2:** 71 − 5 = ? "Enough ones?" (Yes, 1 ≥ ... wait, 1 < 5. No.) "Break a ten." 6 tens, 11 ones. 11 − 5 = 6. Answer: 66.

**Problem 3:** "There are 28 red apples and 15 green apples. How many apples are there?" Add: 28 + 15. Count on: 28 → 38 → 43. Answer: 43.

**Problem 4:** "Sam had 53 stickers. He gave 20 to his sister. How many does he have now?" Subtract tens: 53 − 20 = 33.

**Problem 5:** 46 + 7 = ? Regrouping: 6 + 7 = 13. 4 tens + 1 ten = 5 tens, 3 ones. Answer: 53.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Final unit readiness check before the assessment.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 46 + 30 = ___ (76)
2. 53 − 7 = ___ (46)
3. "There are 67 flowers. 5 more bloom. How many flowers now?" (72)

**Mastery = All three correct with appropriate strategies.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which problem type caused the error and revisit the specific lesson:
  - Adding tens → Lesson 1 or 8
  - Subtracting with regrouping → Lesson 7
  - Word problems → Re-read with the student, identify operation, then solve
- Provide additional mixed practice before administering the unit assessment.

### If Mastery Is Met
Proceed to the Unit Assessment.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson is a consolidation day.** The goal is not to introduce new concepts but to give the student practice switching between strategies. Watch for:
- Can the student correctly identify whether to add or subtract?
- Can the student recognize when regrouping is needed?
- Does the student choose reasonable strategies, or does every problem become counting by ones?

**If time is short,** prioritize the word problems (★★ section). The ability to interpret a story and choose the right operation is the most important skill being assessed.

**Common errors in word problems:**
- Student adds when they should subtract (or vice versa). Help them identify key words, but more importantly, have them act out the story: "There are 73 books. Some get checked out. Are there MORE books or FEWER books now?" (Fewer → subtract.)
