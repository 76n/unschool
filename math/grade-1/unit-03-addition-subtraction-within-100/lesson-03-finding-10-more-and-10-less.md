# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 3, Lesson 3

## Topic
Finding 10 More and 10 Less

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Mentally find 10 more than any two-digit number
- Mentally find 10 less than any two-digit number
- Explain that adding or subtracting 10 changes only the tens digit

## Prerequisites
- Adding and subtracting multiples of 10 (Lessons 1-2)
- Place value: tens and ones (Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (ten-rods and unit cubes)
- Hundred chart
- Place value mat
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review adding/subtracting tens and activate place value.

1. "40 + 30 = ?" (70) "80 − 50 = ?" (30)
2. "Break apart 53 for me: how many tens and how many ones?" (5 tens, 3 ones)
3. "Break apart 27." (2 tens, 7 ones) "Break apart 61." (6 tens, 1 one)

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show that adding 10 changes only the tens digit, and the ones digit stays the same.

### Key Vocabulary
- **10 more:** The number you get by adding one ten
- **10 less:** The number you get by subtracting one ten

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Build and add 10.**
Build 34 on the place value mat: 3 ten-rods in the tens column, 4 unit cubes in the ones column. "This is 34. Now I'm going to add 10 more." Add 1 ten-rod. "How many tens now?" (4) "How many ones?" (4) "What number?" (44)

"The ones didn't change! Only the tens went up by 1. 10 more than 34 is 44."

**Step 2: Build and subtract 10.**
Start with 34 again. Remove 1 ten-rod. "How many tens now?" (2) "How many ones?" (4) "What number?" (24)

"Again, the ones stayed at 4. Only the tens went down by 1. 10 less than 34 is 24."

**Step 3: Show on the hundred chart.**
Point to 34. "10 more — go down one row — 44. 10 less — go up one row — 24. See? The ones digit stays the same. You just move one row."

**Step 4: Test with several numbers.**
- 10 more than 52 → 62 (ones stay at 2, tens go from 5 to 6)
- 10 less than 78 → 68 (ones stay at 8, tens go from 7 to 6)
- 10 more than 90 → 100 (ones stay at 0, tens go from 9 to 10)
- 10 less than 15 → 5 (ones stay at 5, tens go from 1 to 0)

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 10 more than 47. Tens: 4 → 5. Ones: 7 → 7. Answer: 57.

**Example 2:** 10 less than 63. Tens: 6 → 5. Ones: 3 → 3. Answer: 53.

**Example 3:** 10 less than 10. Tens: 1 → 0. Ones: 0 → 0. Answer: 0.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student finds 10 more and 10 less with decreasing scaffold.

**Activity 1: With blocks and hundred chart**
- 10 more than 25 = ? (35) — student builds, adds a ten-rod, reads new number
- 10 less than 58 = ? (48) — student builds, removes a ten-rod
- 10 more than 71 = ? (81) — student uses hundred chart (move down one row)

**Activity 2: Mental — say it fast**
- 10 more than 33 = ? (43)
- 10 less than 49 = ? (39)
- 10 more than 86 = ? (96)
- 10 less than 20 = ? (10)
- 10 more than 55 = ? (65)
- 10 less than 72 = ? (62)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student hesitates, ask: "What's the tens digit? Make it one more (or one less). Does the ones digit change?" (No)*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency with 10 more and 10 less.

**★ On-level:**
1. 10 more than 42 = ___ (52)
2. 10 less than 67 = ___ (57)
3. 10 more than 18 = ___ (28)
4. 10 less than 35 = ___ (25)
5. 10 more than 74 = ___ (84)
6. 10 less than 90 = ___ (80)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. Fill in the table:

| Number | 10 Less | 10 More |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 53 | ___ (43) | ___ (63) |
| 29 | ___ (19) | ___ (39) |
| 80 | ___ (70) | ___ (90) |

8. "I have 46 crayons. My friend gives me 10 more. How many do I have now?" (56)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "Start at 14. Keep adding 10: 14, ___, ___, ___, ___, ___, ___, ___, ___." (24, 34, 44, 54, 64, 74, 84, 94)
10. "What do you notice about the ones digit when you keep adding 10?" (It stays the same — always 4.)

### Interleaved Review
- "16 − 9 = ___" (7) *(Unit 1)*
- "60 + 20 = ___" (80) *(Lesson 1)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm quick, accurate 10 more / 10 less.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 10 more than 56 = ___ (66)
2. 10 less than 41 = ___ (31)

**Mastery = Both correct, answered within a few seconds. The student recognizes that only the tens digit changes.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Use the hundred chart as the primary tool. "Point to 56. Move down one row. What number?" (66) Repeat with many examples until the pattern (ones digit stays, tens digit changes) clicks.
- If the student is still counting by ones, slow down. Build the number with blocks, add/remove exactly one ten-rod, and ask: "Which column changed? Which stayed the same?"

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Adding a One-Digit Number Without Regrouping).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The hundred chart is your best friend for this lesson.** The visual pattern — 10 more is one row down, 10 less is one row up — makes the concept almost self-evident. Let the student discover the pattern rather than telling them.

**Why this matters so much:** "10 more / 10 less" becomes the basis for adding any multiple of 10 to any number (Lesson 8), and later for mental math strategies throughout elementary school. A student who can instantly say "10 more than 47 is 57" is a student who understands how our number system works.

**Edge cases:**
- 10 more than 90 = 100 (the student might not have written 100 before — treat it as "10 tens")
- 10 less than a teen number (e.g., 10 less than 13 = 3) — the tens digit goes from 1 to 0
