# Math — Grade 1 — Unit 3, Lesson 2

## Topic
Subtracting Tens from Tens

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract multiples of 10 using place value reasoning (e.g., 80 − 30 = 50)
- Connect subtraction of tens to basic subtraction facts ("8 tens − 3 tens = 5 tens")
- Model subtraction of tens with base-ten blocks and a hundred chart

## Prerequisites
- Adding multiples of 10 (Lesson 1 mastery)
- Subtraction facts within 10 (Unit 1)
- Place value: tens and ones (Unit 2)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (ten-rods)
- Hundred chart
- Number line 0-100
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect to Lesson 1 and activate subtraction facts.

1. "50 + 30 = ?" (80) "How did you figure that out?" (5 + 3 = 8, so 50 + 30 = 80)
2. Quick subtraction facts: "7 − 3 = ?" (4) "9 − 5 = ?" (4) "8 − 2 = ?" (6)
3. "If 3 + 4 = 7, what is 7 − 4?" (3) "If 5 + 2 = 7, what is 7 − 2?" (5)

"Yesterday we added tens. Today we subtract tens — and the same trick works."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show that subtracting multiples of 10 reduces to subtracting the tens digits.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Subtract:** Take away
- **Difference:** The answer when you subtract

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Concrete — build and remove.**
Place 7 ten-rods on the table. "How much is this?" (70) "I'm going to take away 30. How many tens is 30?" (3) Remove 3 ten-rods. "How many tens are left?" (4) "4 tens is?" (40)

"70 − 30 = 40."

**Step 2: Connect to basic facts.**
"I had 7 tens and took away 3 tens. 7 − 3 = 4. So 7 tens − 3 tens = 4 tens = 40."

**Step 3: Show on the hundred chart.**
Point to 70. "Start at 70. We're subtracting 30, which is 3 tens. Jump up 3 rows: 60, 50, 40." Circle 40.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 90 − 40. Build: 9 ten-rods, remove 4. 5 left = 50. Think: 9 − 4 = 5, so 90 − 40 = 50.

**Example 2:** 60 − 20. Think: 6 − 2 = 4, so 60 − 20 = 40.

**Example 3:** 50 − 50. Think: 5 − 5 = 0, so 50 − 50 = 0. "Zero tens! Nothing left."

**Example 4:** 80 − 10. Think: 8 − 1 = 7, so 80 − 10 = 70.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student subtracts tens with decreasing support.

**Activity 1: Build and solve (with blocks)**
- 60 − 30 = ? Build 6 ten-rods, remove 3. (30)
- 90 − 50 = ? Build and remove. (40)
- 80 − 80 = ? Build and remove. (0)

**Activity 2: Think and solve (mentally)**
- 70 − 40 = ? "7 tens minus 4 tens = 3 tens = ?" (30)
- 50 − 20 = ? (30)
- 100 − 60 = ? "10 tens minus 6 tens = ?" (40) *(Note: 100 = 10 tens. This is a stretch but connects nicely.)*

*Facilitator prompt: Always ask the student to say it in tens: "8 tens minus 3 tens equals 5 tens equals 50."*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency with subtracting multiples of 10.

**★ On-level:**
1. 80 − 30 = ___ (50)
2. 70 − 20 = ___ (50)
3. 60 − 40 = ___ (20)
4. 90 − 60 = ___ (30)
5. 50 − 10 = ___ (40)
6. 100 − 70 = ___ (30)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. Fill in the blank: 80 − ___ = 50 (30)
8. Fill in the blank: ___ − 40 = 20 (60)
9. "There were 70 birds on a wire. 30 flew away. How many are left?" (40)

**★★★ Challenge:**
10. "I'm thinking of a number. When I subtract 50 from it, I get 30. What's my number?" (80) "How do you know?" (Because 30 + 50 = 80)

### Interleaved Review
- "12 + 6 = ___" (18) *(Unit 1 — addition within 20)*
- "40 + 50 = ___" (90) *(Lesson 1 — adding tens)*

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can subtract multiples of 10.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 90 − 30 = ___ (60)
2. 70 − 50 = ___ (20)

**Mastery = Both correct using tens-based reasoning.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to blocks. Have the student build the starting number, physically remove tens, and count what remains.
- Use the hundred chart: "Start on 90. Jump up 3 rows (subtract 3 tens). Where do you land?" (60)

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (Finding 10 More and 10 Less).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Connection to addition:** Reinforce the inverse relationship. "If 30 + 40 = 70, then 70 − 40 = ?" (30) and "70 − 30 = ?" (40). This fact family thinking strengthens both operations.

**Common errors:**
- Student says 70 − 30 = 73 or 37 (concatenation). Reteach the meaning of 30 as "3 tens."
- Student counts backward by ones. Redirect: "You don't need to count by ones. Count backward by tens: 70, 60, 50, 40."
