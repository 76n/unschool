# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 6, Lesson 5

## Topic
Additive Volume — Finding Volume of Composite 3D Shapes by Decomposing

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Recognize that a composite solid figure can be split into two or more rectangular prisms
- Find the total volume of a composite solid by calculating the volume of each part and adding them together
- Apply the additive volume strategy to solve real-world problems
- Explain and justify their decomposition strategy

## Prerequisites
- V = l × w × h and V = B × h (Lesson 3)
- Additive area strategies from Grade 4 (composite rectangles)
- Multi-digit multiplication and addition

## Materials Needed
- Unit cubes (for building composite shapes)
- Student notebooks
- Whiteboard
- Rulers
- Printed diagrams of composite shapes (or teacher-drawn on board)
- Colored pencils (to shade the two component prisms)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of additive area (composite rectangles) as an analogy for additive volume.

Draw a composite rectangle on the board (an L-shape) with dimensions labeled. Ask: "How would you find the area of this shape?"

Expected: "Split it into two rectangles, find the area of each, and add them."

Say: "Today we do the exact same thing, but in 3D. Instead of splitting a flat shape into rectangles, we split a 3D shape into rectangular prisms."

---

## Direct Instruction (13 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model the decompose-and-add strategy for composite 3D figures; show that the split can be made in multiple places.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Composite solid:** a three-dimensional figure made of two or more rectangular prisms joined together
- **Decompose:** to split a shape into two or more simpler shapes
- **Additive volume:** the principle that the volume of a composite shape equals the sum of the volumes of its non-overlapping parts

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Visual setup**
Draw (or show) an L-shaped prism on the board. Label all visible dimensions. Say: "This isn't one simple rectangular prism. But we can imagine cutting it into two rectangular prisms."

Draw a dotted line showing one possible cut. Color one part blue, one part red.

**Step 2 — Calculate each part**
Suppose the L-shape is made of:
- Left prism: 4 × 2 × 3 = 24 cubic units
- Right prism: 2 × 2 × 3 = 12 cubic units
- Total: 24 + 12 = 36 cubic units

**Step 3 — Show alternative decomposition**
Redraw the same L-shape. Suggest a different cut. Show it also gives the same total volume. Emphasize: "There is no single 'right' way to decompose — any correct split gives the same total."

**Step 4 — Real-world connection**
Say: "Architects and engineers do this all the time. If you're designing a building with an unusual shape, you figure out the volume of each part and add them. Let's practice."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** An L-shaped block has the following dimensions:
- Total footprint: 6 cm × 4 cm, but the top-right corner (2 × 2) is cut away
- Height of whole shape: 5 cm

Strategy: Calculate the full prism, then subtract the missing piece.
- Full: 6 × 4 × 5 = 120 cm³
- Missing corner: 2 × 2 × 5 = 20 cm³
- Volume = 120 − 20 = 100 cm³

*OR* decompose into two parts:
- Left part: 4 × 4 × 5 = 80 cm³
- Right part: 2 × 2 × 5 = 20 cm³ — wait, let's be careful:
  The shape has dimensions needing careful labeling. Use a teacher-drawn diagram with explicit labels.
  For this lesson, diagrams on the board are essential. Show the labels clearly.

**Example 2 (step-shaped prism):**
A shape looks like two steps:
- Bottom step: 6 ft long, 3 ft wide, 2 ft tall
- Top step (smaller, sitting on top-left): 3 ft long, 3 ft wide, 2 ft tall

Volume of bottom step: 6 × 3 × 2 = 36 ft³
Volume of top step: 3 × 3 × 2 = 18 ft³
Total volume: 36 + 18 = 54 ft³

**Example 3 (real-world):** A concrete curb is 10 ft long. Its cross-section is an L-shape that can be split into:
- Rectangle A: 1 ft × 0.5 ft
- Rectangle B: 0.5 ft × 1 ft
(Actually it's made of two pieces: bottom base 1 ft wide, 0.5 ft tall; side wall 0.5 ft wide, 0.5 ft tall)
- B_A = 1 × 0.5 = 0.5 ft²; V_A = 0.5 × 10 = 5 ft³
- B_B = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 ft²; V_B = 0.25 × 10 = 2.5 ft³
- Total: 7.5 ft³

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Students use colored pencils to shade the two parts before calculating.

**Problem 1:** A step-shaped block (show on board):
- Back part: 8 cm × 4 cm × 3 cm
- Front step: 4 cm × 2 cm × 3 cm

Students find: 8 × 4 × 3 + 4 × 2 × 3 = 96 + 24 = 120 cm³

**Problem 2:** An office building has two sections:
- Section A: 20 m × 15 m × 10 m
- Section B (attached): 10 m × 10 m × 20 m
Find the total volume.
- V_A = 20 × 15 × 10 = 3,000 m³
- V_B = 10 × 10 × 20 = 2,000 m³
- Total = 5,000 m³

**Problem 3:** A shape can be decomposed two ways. Way 1 gives volumes of 30 cm³ and 18 cm³. Way 2 gives volumes of 24 cm³ and 24 cm³. What is the total volume both ways? Are both ways correct?
- Way 1: 30 + 18 = 48 cm³; Way 2: 24 + 24 = 48 cm³. Both are correct — additive volume works regardless of decomposition.

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. A composite shape is made of:
   - Prism A: 5 in × 3 in × 4 in
   - Prism B (attached): 2 in × 3 in × 4 in
   Find the total volume.
   - 60 + 24 = 84 in³

2. A shape is made of two prisms:
   - Bottom prism: 8 cm × 6 cm × 2 cm
   - Top prism (sitting on part of the bottom): 4 cm × 6 cm × 3 cm
   Find the total volume.
   - 96 + 72 = 168 cm³

3. A garden planter has two sections:
   - Left: 3 ft × 2 ft × 1 ft
   - Right: 1 ft × 2 ft × 1 ft
   What is the total volume of soil it can hold?
   - 6 + 2 = 8 ft³

**Stretch:**
4. A house-shaped solid (box for the walls + box for the roof section):
   - Walls: 10 ft × 8 ft × 8 ft
   - Roof section (rectangular, sitting on top): 10 ft × 8 ft × 3 ft
   What is the total volume?
   - 640 + 240 = 880 ft³

5. An L-shaped swimming pool:
   - Part A: 12 m × 4 m × 2 m
   - Part B: 6 m × 4 m × 2 m
   What is the total volume of water the pool holds?
   - 96 + 48 = 144 m³

6. The diagram shows a shape where a small prism has been removed from a larger prism:
   - Large prism: 10 cm × 8 cm × 6 cm
   - Removed prism: 4 cm × 3 cm × 6 cm
   What is the remaining volume?
   - 480 − 72 = 408 cm³

**Challenge:**
7. A storage unit has a floor plan shaped like a large rectangle with a small rectangular notch cut out of one corner:
   - Full floor plan: 20 ft × 15 ft
   - Notch: 5 ft × 4 ft (corner removed)
   - Height throughout: 10 ft
   Strategy: Find full volume minus notch volume.
   - Full: 20 × 15 × 10 = 3,000 ft³
   - Notch: 5 × 4 × 10 = 200 ft³
   - Remaining: 2,800 ft³

8. A model consists of 3 rectangular prisms stacked in stair-step formation:
   - Step 1 (bottom): 6 in × 4 in × 2 in
   - Step 2 (middle): 4 in × 4 in × 2 in
   - Step 3 (top): 2 in × 4 in × 2 in
   Find the total volume.
   - 48 + 32 + 16 = 96 in³

### Interleaved Review
R1. Find the volume of a rectangular prism: l = 9 ft, w = 4 ft, h = 3 ft. *(Lesson 3 review)*
   - 108 ft³

R2. A bag of apples weighs 2 3/4 pounds. You buy 4 bags. How many pounds total? *(Unit 2 — mixed number multiplication)*
   - 2 3/4 × 4 = 11/4 × 4 = 11 pounds

R3. Order these decimals from least to greatest: 0.6, 0.06, 0.62, 0.602. *(Unit 4 — decimal comparison)*
   - 0.06, 0.6, 0.602, 0.62

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)

**Exit Ticket:**
A shape is made of two rectangular prisms joined together:
- Prism A: 6 cm × 4 cm × 3 cm
- Prism B: 2 cm × 4 cm × 5 cm

1. What is the volume of Prism A?
2. What is the volume of Prism B?
3. What is the total volume of the composite shape?

**Mastery = All three answers correct (72 cm³, 40 cm³, 112 cm³).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Check whether the student understands how to decompose the shape (show a clear diagram)
- Ensure student is applying the formula separately to each part before adding
- Use unit cubes to build each part separately, count, then combine

### If Mastery Is Met
- Ask: "Could you always find composite volume by subtracting the missing part from the full rectangle instead of adding parts? When would you use each strategy?"

## Extension (Optional)
**Architecture Challenge:** Design a building using exactly two or three rectangular prism sections. Assign your own dimensions. Calculate the total volume. Draw your building from the front and the side. Label all dimensions.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Diagrams are essential in this lesson. Every problem should be drawn on the board with labeled dimensions. Students should sketch every composite shape before calculating.
- Color-coding (shade Prism A in blue, Prism B in red) significantly reduces errors.
- Show both strategies: adding parts AND subtracting the missing piece from the whole. Both are valid; both appear on assessments.
- Students who decompose incorrectly (overlapping pieces or leaving gaps) will get wrong answers. Watch for this during guided practice.
- The "two ways to decompose" problem (Problem 3 in Guided Practice) builds mathematical flexibility — there is no single right cut, and students benefit from seeing this explicitly.
