# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 6, Lesson 7

## Topic
Plotting and Reading Ordered Pairs in the First Quadrant

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Accurately plot ordered pairs in the first quadrant of the coordinate plane
- Read (identify) the ordered pair of any point plotted in the first quadrant
- Identify points that share a common x-coordinate or y-coordinate
- Use coordinate notation to describe locations in context

## Prerequisites
- Definition of x-axis, y-axis, origin, and ordered pair (Lesson 6)
- The "right then up" procedure for plotting (Lesson 6)

## Materials Needed
- Graph paper (2 sheets per student, 10 × 10 first-quadrant grid)
- Rulers
- Colored pencils or markers
- Whiteboard or projected grid for class discussion

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Reinforce previous lesson's procedure and activate readiness for today's fluency practice.

Quick-fire oral review: teacher calls out an ordered pair, students trace the path in the air (right hand movement = x direction, then upward = y direction).
- (3, 4) — "3 right, 4 up"
- (7, 0) — "7 right, 0 up — on the x-axis!"
- (0, 5) — "0 right, 5 up — on the y-axis!"
- (2, 8) — "2 right, 8 up"

Then: show a single point on the board grid (already plotted). Students call out the ordered pair. Do 3–4 examples.

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Deepen precision in plotting and reading, and introduce patterns among sets of points.

### Key Vocabulary
(Review from Lesson 6; add one new concept)
- **Collinear points:** points that lie on the same straight line (informal usage; notice when plotted points form a line)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Precise plotting review**
Model plotting (4, 7) on the board. Use a ruler to draw a light vertical line at x = 4 and a horizontal line at y = 7 — their intersection is the point. Say: "A point lives at an intersection of two grid lines — not in a space."

**Step 2 — Common coordinate patterns**
Plot three points: (2, 1), (2, 4), (2, 7). Ask: "What do you notice?" (All have the same x-coordinate: 2.) "Where do they all fall?" (On the vertical line x = 2.)

Plot three more: (1, 5), (4, 5), (8, 5). Ask: "What do you notice?" (All have y-coordinate 5.) "Where do they all fall?" (On the horizontal line y = 5.)

Say: "Points with the same x-coordinate are in a vertical line. Points with the same y-coordinate are in a horizontal line."

**Step 3 — Coordinate context**
Say: "Ordered pairs don't just describe math problems — they describe real locations. Architects use grid coordinates to describe where features go on a floor plan. We'll practice that today."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Plot these points and describe their pattern:
(1, 2), (3, 2), (5, 2), (7, 2)
- All share y = 2; they form a horizontal line

**Example 2:** A drawing has landmarks at these positions:
- Library: (3, 6)
- School: (7, 6)
- Park: (7, 2)
- Home: (3, 2)

Plot all four. What shape do they form?
- A rectangle (all corners are right angles on a grid)

**Example 3:** Which point is further from the y-axis: (5, 3) or (5, 8)?
- Both are the same distance from the y-axis (x = 5 for both). The y-coordinate tells how far from the x-axis.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Use a shared class grid on the board AND students' individual graph paper.

**Task 1:** Plot and label all 6 points:
- A = (1, 3)
- B = (4, 3)
- C = (8, 3)
- D = (4, 7)
- E = (4, 1)
- F = (4, 9)

After plotting: "Which points are on the same horizontal line?" (A, B, C — all y = 3)
"Which points are on the same vertical line?" (B, D, E, F — all x = 4)

**Task 2:** The teacher plots 8 unlabeled points on the board grid. Students write the ordered pair for each. (Teacher uses: (2, 5), (6, 1), (0, 7), (9, 3), (5, 5), (3, 0), (8, 8), (1, 6))

**Task 3 — Partner activity:** Student A closes their eyes while Student B plots 3 points on their paper and tells Student A only the ordered pairs (verbally). Student A plots the same 3 points from the verbal description. Compare results — do the grids match?

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
Use a 10 × 10 first-quadrant grid on graph paper.

1. Plot and label each point:
   - W = (2, 4)
   - X = (5, 9)
   - Y = (8, 1)
   - Z = (0, 6)
   - V = (7, 7)

2. Write the ordered pair for each point on the provided grid:
   - Point A: 3 right, 5 up → (3, 5)
   - Point B: 9 right, 0 up → (9, 0)
   - Point C: 0 right, 4 up → (0, 4)
   - Point D: 6 right, 6 up → (6, 6)

3. Circle the point that is on the y-axis: (0, 5) / (5, 0) / (3, 3)
   - (0, 5) — on the y-axis because x = 0

4. Circle the point that is on the x-axis: (0, 4) / (4, 0) / (4, 4)
   - (4, 0) — on the x-axis because y = 0

**Stretch:**
5. Plot these four points and connect them in order. What shape is formed?
   - (1, 1), (6, 1), (6, 5), (1, 5)
   - Rectangle (sides parallel to axes)

6. Point J = (3, 7). Point K is directly to the right of J, 4 units away. What are K's coordinates?
   - (7, 7) — y stays the same, x increases by 4

7. Three corners of a square are: (2, 2), (6, 2), (6, 6). What is the fourth corner?
   - (2, 6) — completing the square

8. All of these points have the same y-coordinate. What do they have in common when plotted: (1, 4), (3, 4), (5, 4), (7, 4), (9, 4)?
   - They all fall on the horizontal line y = 4; they form a horizontal line

**Challenge:**
9. The points (1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 6), (4, 8) are plotted. Describe the pattern you see. What would the next point be?
   - The y-coordinate is always 2 times the x-coordinate. Next point: (5, 10) — but note (5,10) may fall outside a 0–10 grid if only plotted to 9 on each axis; adjust as needed.

10. Point M is equidistant (same distance) from the x-axis and the y-axis. If M is at (k, k) and is 6 units from the origin along both axes, what is M?
   - (6, 6)

### Interleaved Review
R1. A box is 6 cm × 4 cm × h. The volume is 96 cm³. Find h. *(Lesson 3 — working backwards)*
   - B = 6 × 4 = 24; 96 ÷ 24 = 4; h = 4 cm

R2. Subtract: 7/8 − 1/4. *(Unit 1 — fraction subtraction)*
   - 7/8 − 2/8 = 5/8

R3. What is 0.03 × 100? *(Unit 5 — multiply by powers of 10)*
   - 3

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)

**Exit Ticket:**
Use the mini-grid provided (or draw a small 5 × 5 grid).

1. Plot and label point A at (4, 2) and point B at (1, 5).
2. Write the ordered pair for the point that is 3 units to the right and 6 units up from the origin.
3. Do (5, 3) and (3, 5) represent the same location? Explain.

**Mastery = Points A and B correctly plotted, (3, 6) written for problem 2, and student explains that (5,3) ≠ (3,5) because x and y are different positions.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Remind the student of the "right, then up" rule
- Use finger tracing: start at (0,0), count right, then up
- Practice reading coordinates from pre-plotted points before expecting students to plot from scratch

### If Mastery Is Met
- Challenge: "If I wanted to plot a point that's exactly the same distance from the x-axis as from the y-axis, what would its ordered pair look like?"

## Extension (Optional)
**Coordinate Art:** Create a design or picture on a coordinate plane by plotting at least 10 points and connecting them in a specific order. Write the list of ordered pairs as instructions so another student can recreate your drawing without seeing the original.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Fluency is the goal of this lesson. Students should be able to plot and read points quickly and confidently.
- The most common error remains the reversal of (x, y). Any time you see a reversed point, stop and correct — "Remember: x tells us how far right. Y tells us how far up. Say it with me."
- The partner talking activity in Guided Practice Task 3 is highly effective — verbal description requires students to internalize the meaning of each coordinate.
- Stretch problems 5 and 7 introduce the idea of using coordinates to define geometric shapes — a preview of Grade 6 geometry work.
- Challenge problem 9 begins to develop the concept of linear relationships — students don't need to formalize this, but noticing the pattern is excellent pre-algebra preparation.
