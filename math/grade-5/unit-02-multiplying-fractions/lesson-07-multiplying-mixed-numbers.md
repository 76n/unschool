# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 2, Lesson 7

## Topic
Multiplying with Mixed Numbers

## Learning Objectives
- Students will convert mixed numbers to improper fractions as the first step in multiplication.
- Students will multiply a mixed number by a whole number (e.g., 2 1/3 x 4).
- Students will multiply a mixed number by a fraction (e.g., 1 3/4 x 2/5).
- Students will convert improper fraction products back to mixed numbers in simplest form.
- Students will use estimation (rounding mixed numbers to the nearest whole number) to check the reasonableness of their answers.

## Prerequisites
- Fluency with the fraction multiplication algorithm: multiply numerators, multiply denominators (Lessons 4-5).
- Ability to convert between mixed numbers and improper fractions (Grade 4 review and Lesson 1).
- Ability to simplify fractions to lowest terms (Unit 1, Lesson 6).
- Comfort multiplying a fraction by a whole number (Lesson 1) and a fraction by a fraction (Lessons 3-5).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student reference sheet: "Converting Mixed Numbers to Improper Fractions" (optional, for students who need the reminder).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.
- Fraction strips or number lines (optional, for visual verification).

**Standards:** 5.NF.6 — Solve real world problems involving multiplication of fractions and mixed numbers, e.g., by using visual fraction models or equations to represent the problem.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review converting mixed numbers to improper fractions and activate estimation skills that students will use throughout the lesson.

1. Write on the board: "Convert each mixed number to an improper fraction."
   - 1 2/3 = ?
   - 2 1/4 = ?
   - 3 1/2 = ?
2. Give students 1 minute to convert all three. Review answers: 1 2/3 = 5/3, 2 1/4 = 9/4, 3 1/2 = 7/2.
3. Remind students of the process: "Multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and keep the same denominator." Show: 2 1/4 = (2 x 4 + 1)/4 = 9/4.
4. Now write: "Estimate: 2 1/3 x 4."
5. Ask: "If we round 2 1/3 to the nearest whole number, what do we get?" (2.) "So a good estimate is 2 x 4 = 8. Our exact answer should be close to 8."
6. Tell students: "Today we are going to multiply with mixed numbers. The key strategy is to convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first, then use the multiplication skills you already have."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Mixed number** — a number made up of a whole number and a proper fraction (e.g., 2 1/3).
- **Improper fraction** — a fraction where the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator (e.g., 7/3).
- **Convert** — to change a number from one form to another (mixed number to improper fraction, or vice versa).
- **Estimate** — an approximate answer found by rounding; used to check whether an exact answer is reasonable.
- **Simplify** — to reduce a fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Establish the method: convert first, then multiply.**

Write on the board: **2 1/3 x 4**

Say: "Can we multiply a mixed number directly using our fraction algorithm? Not easily. The algorithm says 'multiply numerators, multiply denominators,' but a mixed number is not written as a single fraction. So our first step is always to convert the mixed number to an improper fraction."

Convert: 2 1/3 = (2 x 3 + 1)/3 = 7/3.

"Now we also need the whole number as a fraction." Write: 4 = 4/1.

"Now we have two fractions: 7/3 x 4/1."

**Step 2 — Multiply using the standard algorithm.**

Write: 7/3 x 4/1 = (7 x 4)/(3 x 1) = 28/3.

"We multiplied numerators (7 x 4 = 28) and multiplied denominators (3 x 1 = 3)."

**Step 3 — Convert the result back to a mixed number.**

"Is 28/3 an improper fraction? Yes, because 28 > 3. Divide: 28 / 3 = 9 remainder 1. So 28/3 = 9 1/3."

**Step 4 — Check with estimation.**

"Earlier we estimated 2 1/3 x 4 is about 2 x 4 = 8. Our exact answer is 9 1/3. That is close to 8 — a little more, which makes sense because 2 1/3 is a little more than 2. Our answer is reasonable."

Write the final answer: **2 1/3 x 4 = 9 1/3.**

**Step 5 — Model a mixed number times a fraction: 1 3/4 x 2/5.**

Say: "Now let's multiply a mixed number by a fraction. The process is the same: convert the mixed number first."

Convert: 1 3/4 = (1 x 4 + 3)/4 = 7/4.

Write: 7/4 x 2/5 = (7 x 2)/(4 x 5) = 14/20.

"Can we simplify? The GCF of 14 and 20 is 2. So 14/20 = 7/10."

"Is 7/10 improper? No — 7 < 10. So our answer stays as a fraction: 7/10."

Estimate check: "1 3/4 is close to 2. And 2/5 is close to 1/2. So the estimate is 2 x 1/2 = 1. Our exact answer, 7/10, is close to 1. Reasonable."

Write: **1 3/4 x 2/5 = 7/10.**

**Step 6 — Real-world connection.**

"Where do we see mixed number multiplication in real life? Recipes! Suppose a recipe calls for 1 3/4 cups of sugar, but you only want to make 2/5 of the recipe. You need 1 3/4 x 2/5 = 7/10 cup of sugar."

Write another scenario: "A trail is 2 1/3 miles long. You walk 3/4 of the trail. How far did you walk?"

Convert: 2 1/3 = 7/3. Multiply: 7/3 x 3/4 = 21/12 = 7/4 = 1 3/4 miles.

"You walked 1 3/4 miles. Does that make sense? 3/4 of 2 1/3 should be a little less than 2 1/3. And 1 3/4 < 2 1/3. Yes, reasonable."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Mixed Number x Whole Number: 2 1/3 x 4**
1. Convert: 2 1/3 = 7/3; write 4 as 4/1.
2. Multiply: 7/3 x 4/1 = 28/3.
3. Convert back: 28 / 3 = 9 R 1, so 28/3 = 9 1/3.
4. Estimate check: 2 x 4 = 8. Answer of 9 1/3 is close. Reasonable.
**Answer: 2 1/3 x 4 = 28/3 = 9 1/3**

**Example B — Mixed Number x Fraction: 1 3/4 x 2/5**
1. Convert: 1 3/4 = 7/4.
2. Multiply: 7/4 x 2/5 = 14/20.
3. Simplify: 14/20 = 7/10 (divided by GCF of 2).
4. 7/10 is already a proper fraction; no further conversion needed.
5. Estimate check: 2 x 1/2 = 1. Answer of 7/10 is close. Reasonable.
**Answer: 1 3/4 x 2/5 = 14/20 = 7/10**

**Example C — Mixed Number x Fraction: 2 1/2 x 3/4**
1. Convert: 2 1/2 = 5/2.
2. Multiply: 5/2 x 3/4 = 15/8.
3. Convert back: 15 / 8 = 1 R 7, so 15/8 = 1 7/8.
4. Estimate check: 3 x 1 = 3. More precisely, 2 1/2 x 3/4 — about 2.5 x 0.75 — should be close to 2. Answer of 1 7/8 is close to 2. Reasonable.
**Answer: 2 1/2 x 3/4 = 15/8 = 1 7/8**

**Example D — Mixed Number x Whole Number: 3 2/5 x 3**
1. Convert: 3 2/5 = 17/5; write 3 as 3/1.
2. Multiply: 17/5 x 3/1 = 51/5.
3. Convert back: 51 / 5 = 10 R 1, so 51/5 = 10 1/5.
4. Estimate check: 3 x 3 = 9. Answer of 10 1/5 is close. Reasonable (slightly more, since 3 2/5 > 3).
**Answer: 3 2/5 x 3 = 51/5 = 10 1/5**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. Students attempt each problem first, then discuss.

1. 1 1/2 x 6 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Convert 1 1/2 to an improper fraction first. What is 1 1/2 as an improper fraction? Now write 6 as 6/1 and multiply across."

2. 2 2/3 x 3 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Convert 2 2/3 to an improper fraction. Multiply by 3/1. Convert the result back to a mixed number. Estimate first: about 3 x 3 = 9."

3. 1 1/4 x 2/3 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Convert the mixed number. Now you have two fractions — multiply numerators and denominators. Can you simplify the result?"

4. 3 1/3 x 1/2 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Convert first. What is 3 1/3 as an improper fraction? Multiply by 1/2. Is the answer more or less than 3 1/3? Why?"

5. A recipe calls for 2 1/4 cups of oats. You are making 2/3 of the recipe. How many cups of oats do you need?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Identify the multiplication: 2 1/4 x 2/3. Convert the mixed number. Multiply. Simplify. Does the answer make sense — should it be less than 2 1/4?"

> **Facilitator Tip:** Emphasize the three-step process: (1) Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions, (2) Multiply using the fraction algorithm, (3) Convert back and simplify. Have students write these steps on a sticky note or at the top of their worksheet as a reference. The most common mistake at this stage is forgetting to convert the mixed number first and attempting to multiply the whole-number part and fraction part separately.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A — Mixed Number x Whole Number (On-Level)
Solve each problem. Show conversion steps and express your answer as a mixed number in simplest form.

1. 1 2/5 x 5 = ?
2. 3 1/4 x 2 = ?
3. 2 3/8 x 4 = ?
4. 4 1/6 x 3 = ?

### Part B — Mixed Number x Fraction and Word Problems (Stretch)
5. 1 1/3 x 3/4 = ?
6. 2 1/2 x 1/5 = ?
7. A ribbon is 1 2/3 yards long. Maria uses 3/4 of the ribbon to wrap a gift. How many yards of ribbon does she use?
8. A cookie recipe calls for 2 1/4 cups of flour. Deshawn is making half the recipe. How many cups of flour does he need? Express your answer as a mixed number.

### Part C — Challenge
9. A jogger runs 3 1/2 miles each day. She runs 5 days this week but only runs 2/3 of her usual distance on 2 of those days. How many total miles does she run this week? (Hint: calculate full days and partial days separately, then add.)
10. True or false: When you multiply a mixed number by a fraction that is less than 1, the product is always less than the mixed number. Explain your reasoning and give an example.
11. Without computing, determine whether 1 3/4 x 2/5 is greater than, less than, or equal to 1. Explain your reasoning, then verify by computing.

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. 1 2/5 x 5: Convert 1 2/5 = 7/5. Multiply: 7/5 x 5/1 = 35/5 = 7. **Answer: 7.**
2. 3 1/4 x 2: Convert 3 1/4 = 13/4. Multiply: 13/4 x 2/1 = 26/4 = 13/2 = 6 1/2. **Answer: 6 1/2.**
3. 2 3/8 x 4: Convert 2 3/8 = 19/8. Multiply: 19/8 x 4/1 = 76/8 = 19/2 = 9 1/2. **Answer: 9 1/2.**
4. 4 1/6 x 3: Convert 4 1/6 = 25/6. Multiply: 25/6 x 3/1 = 75/6 = 25/2 = 12 1/2. **Answer: 12 1/2.**

**Part B**
5. 1 1/3 x 3/4: Convert 1 1/3 = 4/3. Multiply: 4/3 x 3/4 = 12/12 = 1. **Answer: 1.**
6. 2 1/2 x 1/5: Convert 2 1/2 = 5/2. Multiply: 5/2 x 1/5 = 5/10 = 1/2. **Answer: 1/2.**
7. 1 2/3 x 3/4: Convert 1 2/3 = 5/3. Multiply: 5/3 x 3/4 = 15/12 = 5/4 = 1 1/4. **Answer: Maria uses 1 1/4 yards of ribbon.**
8. 2 1/4 x 1/2: Convert 2 1/4 = 9/4. Multiply: 9/4 x 1/2 = 9/8 = 1 1/8. **Answer: Deshawn needs 1 1/8 cups of flour.**

**Part C**
9. Full days: 3 days x 3 1/2 miles = 3 x 7/2 = 21/2 = 10 1/2 miles. Partial days: 2 days x (2/3 x 3 1/2) = 2 x (2/3 x 7/2) = 2 x 14/6 = 2 x 7/3 = 14/3 = 4 2/3 miles. Total: 10 1/2 + 4 2/3 = 21/2 + 14/3 = 63/6 + 28/6 = 91/6 = 15 1/6 miles. **Answer: 15 1/6 miles.**
10. True. When you multiply any positive number by a fraction less than 1, you are taking a part of that number, so the product must be smaller. Example: 2 1/2 x 1/5 = 5/2 x 1/5 = 5/10 = 1/2, and 1/2 < 2 1/2. **Answer: True.**
11. 1 3/4 x 2/5 is less than 1. Reasoning: 2/5 is less than 1/2. Half of 1 3/4 would be 7/8, which is less than 1. Since 2/5 < 1/2, the product is even smaller than 7/8, so it must be less than 1. Verification: 1 3/4 = 7/4. Multiply: 7/4 x 2/5 = 14/20 = 7/10. Since 7/10 < 1, the prediction is correct. **Answer: Less than 1.**

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
Solve the following two problems. Show all your work, including the conversion to improper fractions.

**Problem 1:** 1 2/3 x 6 = ? (express as a mixed number or whole number)
**Problem 2:** 2 3/4 x 2/5 = ? (express in simplest form)

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct with conversion work shown = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct or correct answer but work missing = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

**Exit Ticket Answers:**
1. 1 2/3 x 6: Convert 1 2/3 = 5/3. Multiply: 5/3 x 6/1 = 30/3 = 10. **Answer: 10.**
2. 2 3/4 x 2/5: Convert 2 3/4 = 11/4. Multiply: 11/4 x 2/5 = 22/20 = 11/10 = 1 1/10. **Answer: 1 1/10.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Pull students into a small group and review the conversion process with additional examples (e.g., converting 1 1/2, 2 3/4, 3 1/5 to improper fractions and back).
- Provide a step-by-step reference card: (1) Convert every mixed number to an improper fraction, (2) Write any whole number as n/1, (3) Multiply numerators, (4) Multiply denominators, (5) Simplify, (6) Convert back to a mixed number if improper.
- Use estimation as a scaffold: before computing, have the student estimate. This helps them catch major errors (e.g., "I got 1/3, but my estimate was about 9 — something went wrong").
- Pair the student with a peer tutor for additional guided practice before Lesson 8.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Students may begin the Extension activity below.
- Students may write their own recipe-based word problems involving mixed number multiplication for a classmate to solve.

## Extension

- **Recipe Scaling:** Give students a simple recipe (e.g., pancakes: 1 1/2 cups flour, 2/3 cup milk, 1 1/4 cups sugar). Ask them to calculate the ingredient amounts needed to make 3 batches and 1/2 batch. Compare the two sets of results and discuss how scaling up and scaling down work.
- **Distance Challenge:** A hiking trail has three sections. Section A is 1 1/4 miles, Section B is 2 1/3 miles, and Section C is 1 1/2 miles. If a hiker completes 3/4 of each section before turning back, how far does the hiker walk in total (going out only, not counting the return trip)?
  - Solution: 3/4 x 1 1/4 + 3/4 x 2 1/3 + 3/4 x 1 1/2 = 3/4 x 5/4 + 3/4 x 7/3 + 3/4 x 3/2 = 15/16 + 21/12 + 9/8 = 15/16 + 7/4 + 9/8 = 15/16 + 28/16 + 18/16 = 61/16 = 3 13/16 miles.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Multiplying the whole number and fraction parts separately.** For example, computing 2 1/3 x 4 as (2 x 4) + (1/3 x 4) = 8 + 4/3 = 9 1/3. While this distributive approach actually does yield the correct answer, it is error-prone and breaks down in more complex cases (Lesson 8). Teach students to always convert to improper fractions first for a reliable, consistent method.
- **Forgetting to convert the mixed number before multiplying.** Students may write 2 1/3 x 4 = 2 4/12 or make other nonsensical computations. Reinforce: "Step 1 is always convert."
- **Conversion errors.** The most common conversion mistake is multiplying the whole number by the numerator instead of the denominator. For example, writing 2 1/3 = (2 x 1 + 3)/3 = 5/3 (correct) vs. 2 1/3 = (2 x 1)/3 = 2/3 (incorrect). Provide extra conversion practice for students who struggle here.
- **Not converting back to a mixed number.** Students may leave 28/3 as their final answer. Remind them that unless directed otherwise, improper fractions should be expressed as mixed numbers.
- **Simplification oversights.** After multiplying, students may not simplify (e.g., leaving 14/20 instead of writing 7/10). Encourage checking for common factors as a final step.

### Pacing Advice
- The Warm-Up conversion review is essential. If many students are rusty on converting mixed numbers, spend an extra 2-3 minutes here — this skill is the foundation for the entire lesson.
- Examples A and B (mixed number x whole number, and mixed number x fraction) are the core. Make sure both are thoroughly modeled before moving to Guided Practice.
- The estimation step is not just a "nice to have" — it is a critical metacognitive check. Model it consistently and require students to estimate before computing on at least the first three problems.
- For students who finish Independent Practice early, direct them to the Extension activity or have them create recipe word problems for a classmate.
- This lesson covers the simpler cases of mixed number multiplication (one mixed number). Lesson 8 will tackle multiplying two mixed numbers together, so ensure students are solid on this foundation before moving on.
