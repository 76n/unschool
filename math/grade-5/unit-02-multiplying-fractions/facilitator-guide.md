# Facilitator Guide — Grade 5 Math, Unit 2: Multiplying Fractions

**Grade:** 5
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 2 — Multiplying Fractions
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (facilitator leads instruction; student works independently for practice)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit develops the concept of fraction multiplication from its foundations in repeated addition and scaling. Students begin by multiplying a fraction by a whole number (as repeated addition), then progress to multiplying a whole number by a fraction (as "a part of"), and finally multiply fraction by fraction using area models. The unit culminates with multiplying mixed numbers by converting to improper fractions.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Multiply a fraction by a whole number (e.g., 3/4 × 5)
- Multiply a whole number by a fraction (e.g., 6 × 2/3)
- Multiply a fraction by a fraction using area models and the algorithm (e.g., 2/3 × 3/4)
- Explain why multiplying by a fraction less than 1 produces a smaller result
- Simplify products and express in lowest terms
- Multiply mixed numbers by converting to improper fractions
- Solve real-world problems involving fraction multiplication

**Why this matters:** Fraction multiplication is essential for proportional reasoning, scaling, area calculations, and probability. Understanding that "2/3 of 3/4" means multiplication (not addition) is a conceptual leap that underpins all future work with ratios, rates, and algebra.

## Lesson Summaries and Facilitation Notes

### Lesson 1: Fraction × Whole Number (Repeated Addition)
**Goal:** Understand fraction × whole number as repeated addition (e.g., 3 × 2/5 = 2/5 + 2/5 + 2/5).
**Key moves:** Start with physical fraction strips. "3 groups of 2/5" is a natural extension of whole-number multiplication. Then show the shortcut: multiply the numerator by the whole number, keep the denominator.
**Watch for:** Students who try to multiply both numerator and denominator by the whole number.

### Lesson 2: Whole Number × Fraction (Part of a Group)
**Goal:** Understand "2/3 of 12" as multiplication (2/3 × 12).
**Key moves:** "2/3 of 12" means divide 12 into 3 equal parts, then take 2 of those parts. Use discrete objects (e.g., 12 counters). Show this is the same as (12 × 2) ÷ 3 = 8, or equivalently 12/1 × 2/3 = 24/3 = 8.
**Watch for:** Students who confuse "2/3 of 12" with "12 ÷ 2/3" (division by a fraction comes in Unit 3).

### Lesson 3: Fraction × Fraction: Conceptual Introduction
**Goal:** Build intuition for fraction × fraction using paper folding and shading.
**Key moves:** Fold a paper into thirds horizontally and shade 2/3. Then fold into fourths vertically and shade 3/4. The overlap (double-shaded region) represents 2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2. This concretely demonstrates that multiplying two fractions less than 1 gives a smaller result.
**Watch for:** Students who expect the product to be larger (whole-number intuition). Address this directly.

### Lesson 4: Fraction × Fraction: Area Model
**Goal:** Use a grid-based area model to multiply fractions systematically.
**Key moves:** Draw a unit square on grid paper. Shade one fraction horizontally, the other vertically. Count the overlap squares out of the total squares. This visually confirms numerator × numerator / denominator × denominator.
**Watch for:** Miscounting grid squares. Use consistent shading colors (one direction per fraction).

### Lesson 5: Fraction × Fraction: The Algorithm
**Goal:** Formalize the multiplication algorithm: multiply numerators, multiply denominators.
**Key moves:** After extensive visual work, state the rule: a/b × c/d = (a×c)/(b×d). Connect every algorithmic step back to the area model. Practice with a variety of fractions, always simplifying the answer.
**Watch for:** Students who add instead of multiply. Reinforce: addition requires common denominators, multiplication does not.

### Lesson 6: Simplifying Before Multiplying (Cross-Canceling)
**Goal:** Simplify fractions before multiplying to keep numbers manageable.
**Key moves:** Show that 3/8 × 4/9 can be simplified by canceling the 3 with the 9 (both divisible by 3) and the 4 with the 8 (both divisible by 4) before multiplying: 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6. Emphasize this is optional but makes computation easier.
**Watch for:** Students canceling across an addition or subtraction sign (only valid in multiplication). Also watch for canceling numerator with its own denominator (must be cross-diagonal).

### Lesson 7: Multiplying with Mixed Numbers
**Goal:** Multiply a mixed number by a whole number or by a fraction.
**Key moves:** Convert mixed number to improper fraction, then multiply normally. For example: 2 1/3 × 4 = 7/3 × 4 = 28/3 = 9 1/3. Always convert the result back to a mixed number.
**Watch for:** Students who try to multiply the whole part and fraction part separately without adding correctly (the distributive property works but is error-prone for beginners).

### Lesson 8: Multiplying Mixed Numbers (Continued)
**Goal:** Multiply two mixed numbers.
**Key moves:** Both mixed numbers → improper fractions → multiply → simplify → convert back. For example: 1 2/3 × 2 1/4 = 5/3 × 9/4 = 45/12 = 3 3/4. Use estimation to check (about 2 × 2 = 4, so 3 3/4 is reasonable).
**Watch for:** Arithmetic errors with larger numbers. Encourage cross-canceling first.

### Lesson 9: Scaling and Comparison
**Goal:** Predict whether a product will be greater than, less than, or equal to a given number.
**Key moves:** Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 shrinks the number. Multiplying by a fraction greater than 1 (improper fraction or mixed number) grows it. Multiplying by exactly 1 (e.g., 3/3) leaves it unchanged. Practice without computing: "Is 5/6 × 24 more or less than 24?"
**Watch for:** Students relying on computation rather than reasoning. Push for explanations before calculating.

### Lesson 10: Word Problems with Fraction Multiplication
**Goal:** Apply fraction multiplication to real-world contexts (recipes, area, scaling).
**Key moves:** Identify key phrases that signal multiplication: "of," "each," "times as much." Multi-step problems may combine fraction multiplication with addition or subtraction.
**Watch for:** Students who set up division when they should multiply (or vice versa). Read the problem carefully and model the operation with a diagram first.

## Differentiation Strategies

### Struggling Learners
- Continue using area models alongside the algorithm through Lessons 5-8
- Provide fraction strip manipulatives for fraction × whole number
- Offer a reference card: "To multiply fractions: multiply tops, multiply bottoms, simplify"
- Break mixed-number problems into explicit steps: (1) convert, (2) multiply, (3) simplify, (4) convert back

### Advanced Learners
- Explore multiplying three fractions (e.g., 1/2 × 2/3 × 3/4)
- Connect fraction multiplication to probability (chance of two independent events)
- Area problems: "What is the area of a rectangle that is 3/4 ft by 2/3 ft?"
- Introduction to fraction division as a preview: "If 2/3 × ? = 1/2, what is the unknown?"

## Common Errors and How to Address Them

| Error | Likely Cause | Remediation |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Adding numerators and denominators (1/2 × 1/3 = 2/5) | Confusing multiplication with addition | Side-by-side comparison: show 1/2 + 1/3 vs. 1/2 × 1/3 using area models |
| Not simplifying the product | Doesn't recognize common factors | Practice GCF and build the habit of checking after every multiplication |
| Multiplying whole number by both numerator and denominator | Misunderstanding the algorithm | Show: 3 × 2/5 = 3/1 × 2/5 = 6/5 (write the whole number as n/1 first) |
| Forgetting to convert mixed numbers | Skipping the conversion step | Enforce a written step: "Step 1: Convert to improper fractions" |
| Incorrect cross-canceling | Canceling non-diagonal pairs | Use color coding: circle the cross-diagonal pair that shares a common factor |

## Assessment Guidance

The **Unit Assessment** covers all major skills. Administer after Lesson 10.

**Mastery criteria (80% or above):**
- Correctly multiplies fraction × whole number, fraction × fraction, and mixed numbers
- Uses area models to represent fraction multiplication
- Simplifies products to lowest terms
- Predicts whether a product is greater or less than a given factor
- Solves at least one multi-step word problem involving fraction multiplication

**If below mastery:** Reteach Lessons 3-5 (fraction × fraction with area models) and Lesson 7 (mixed numbers) with additional visual support before reassessing.
