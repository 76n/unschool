# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 2, Lesson 8

## Topic
Multiplying Mixed Numbers by Mixed Numbers

## Learning Objectives
- Students will multiply two mixed numbers together by converting both to improper fractions first (e.g., 1 2/3 x 2 1/4).
- Students will apply cross-cancellation (simplifying before multiplying) to reduce computation difficulty.
- Students will convert improper fraction products back to mixed numbers in simplest form.
- Students will estimate products by rounding each mixed number to the nearest whole number and use the estimate to check reasonableness.
- Students will solve real-world area problems involving the multiplication of two mixed numbers.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to convert mixed numbers to improper fractions and back (Lesson 7 and Grade 4 review).
- Fluency with the fraction multiplication algorithm: multiply numerators, multiply denominators (Lessons 4-5).
- Experience multiplying a mixed number by a whole number or fraction (Lesson 7).
- Understanding of area as length x width (Lesson 4 area model work).
- Ability to find the GCF of two numbers and simplify fractions.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Grid paper (optional, for students who want to sketch area models for verification).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.
- Colored pencils (optional, for area model sketches).

**Standards:** 5.NF.6 — Solve real world problems involving multiplication of fractions and mixed numbers, e.g., by using visual fraction models or equations to represent the problem.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review the Lesson 7 process with a quick mixed-number-times-fraction problem, then prime students for today's extension — multiplying two mixed numbers.

1. Write on the board: "Quick Review — Solve: 1 1/2 x 2/3."
2. Give students 1 minute to solve. Review: Convert 1 1/2 = 3/2. Multiply: 3/2 x 2/3 = 6/6 = 1. "Nice — the answer is exactly 1."
3. Now write: "What about 1 1/2 x 2 1/3?"
4. Ask: "How is this different from what we just did?" (Both numbers are mixed numbers.) "Do we know how to multiply two mixed numbers yet?" Pause for responses.
5. Say: "The method is the same as Lesson 7, but now we have to convert BOTH mixed numbers to improper fractions before we multiply. Let's estimate first: 1 1/2 is about 2, and 2 1/3 is about 2. So the answer should be close to 2 x 2 = 4. Let's find out the exact answer today."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Cross-cancellation** — simplifying before multiplying by dividing a numerator and a denominator that share a common factor (e.g., in 5/3 x 3/4, the 3 in the denominator and the 3 in the numerator can be divided by 3 before multiplying).
- **Improper fraction** — a fraction where the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator (e.g., 5/3).
- **Mixed number** — a number made up of a whole number and a proper fraction (e.g., 2 1/4).
- **Estimate** — an approximate answer found by rounding; used to verify the exact answer is reasonable.
- **Area** — the measure of the surface inside a two-dimensional shape, calculated as length x width for rectangles.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce the full process: 1 2/3 x 2 1/4.**

Write on the board: **1 2/3 x 2 1/4**

Say: "In Lesson 7, we only had one mixed number to convert. Today, BOTH factors are mixed numbers. The method is the same — we just convert both."

**Step 2 — Convert both mixed numbers to improper fractions.**

Convert 1 2/3: "1 x 3 + 2 = 5. So 1 2/3 = 5/3."

Convert 2 1/4: "2 x 4 + 1 = 9. So 2 1/4 = 9/4."

Write: 5/3 x 9/4.

**Step 3 — Look for cross-cancellation opportunities.**

"Before we multiply, let's check: do any numerator and denominator across the multiplication sign share a common factor?"

"Look at the numerator 5 and the denominator 4 — no common factor other than 1. Look at the numerator 9 and the denominator 3 — yes! 9 and 3 are both divisible by 3."

"Divide 9 by 3 = 3, and divide 3 by 3 = 1."

Write: 5/3 x 9/4 = 5/1 x 3/4 (after cross-cancelling the 3 and 9).

"Now multiply: 5/1 x 3/4 = (5 x 3)/(1 x 4) = 15/4."

"Cross-cancellation makes the numbers smaller and easier to work with. It does not change the answer — it just simplifies before you multiply instead of after."

**Step 4 — Convert back to a mixed number.**

"15/4 is improper. Divide: 15 / 4 = 3 remainder 3. So 15/4 = 3 3/4."

**Step 5 — Estimate check.**

"We estimated at the start: about 2 x 2 = 4. Our exact answer is 3 3/4, which is close to 4. Reasonable."

Write: **1 2/3 x 2 1/4 = 15/4 = 3 3/4.**

**Step 6 — Model a second example: 2 1/2 x 1 3/5.**

Convert: 2 1/2 = 5/2. Convert: 1 3/5 = 8/5.

Write: 5/2 x 8/5.

"Cross-cancel: the numerator 5 and the denominator 5 share a factor of 5. Divide both by 5: 5/5 = 1 and 5/5 = 1."

"Also check: numerator 8 and denominator 2 — both divisible by 2. Divide: 8/2 = 4, 2/2 = 1."

Write: 1/1 x 4/1 = 4/1 = 4.

"The answer is exactly 4! Estimate: 3 x 2 = 6. Hmm — the estimate was 6, but the exact answer is 4. Why? Because 2 1/2 rounds up to 3 and 1 3/5 rounds up to 2, so the estimate overshot. The estimate tells us we should be in the ballpark of a small whole number, and 4 is reasonable."

Write: **2 1/2 x 1 3/5 = 4.**

**Step 7 — Real-world area problem.**

Write on the board: "A garden is 3 1/2 feet long and 2 1/4 feet wide. What is the area of the garden?"

"Area = length x width = 3 1/2 x 2 1/4."

Convert: 3 1/2 = 7/2. Convert: 2 1/4 = 9/4.

Write: 7/2 x 9/4 = 63/8.

"Cross-cancellation check: 7 and 4 — no common factor. 9 and 2 — no common factor. So we multiply straight: (7 x 9)/(2 x 4) = 63/8."

Convert: 63 / 8 = 7 remainder 7. So 63/8 = 7 7/8.

Estimate: 4 x 2 = 8. Answer of 7 7/8 is very close to 8. Reasonable.

Write: **Area = 3 1/2 x 2 1/4 = 63/8 = 7 7/8 square feet.**

"Notice the label: square feet. When we compute area, the answer is always in square units."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — 1 2/3 x 2 1/4**
1. Convert: 1 2/3 = 5/3; 2 1/4 = 9/4.
2. Cross-cancel: 9 and 3 share factor 3. Simplify to 5/1 x 3/4.
3. Multiply: (5 x 3)/(1 x 4) = 15/4.
4. Convert: 15/4 = 3 3/4.
5. Estimate: 2 x 2 = 4. Answer of 3 3/4 is close. Reasonable.
**Answer: 1 2/3 x 2 1/4 = 15/4 = 3 3/4**

**Example B — 2 1/2 x 1 3/5**
1. Convert: 2 1/2 = 5/2; 1 3/5 = 8/5.
2. Cross-cancel: 5 and 5 share factor 5 (giving 1/2 x 8/1). Then 8 and 2 share factor 2 (giving 1/1 x 4/1).
3. Multiply: 4/1 = 4.
4. Estimate: 3 x 2 = 6. Answer of 4 is in a reasonable range.
**Answer: 2 1/2 x 1 3/5 = 4**

**Example C — 3 1/2 x 2 1/4 (Area Problem)**
1. Convert: 3 1/2 = 7/2; 2 1/4 = 9/4.
2. Cross-cancel: no common factors between numerators and denominators across the sign.
3. Multiply: (7 x 9)/(2 x 4) = 63/8.
4. Convert: 63/8 = 7 7/8.
5. Estimate: 4 x 2 = 8. Answer of 7 7/8 is close. Reasonable.
**Answer: 3 1/2 x 2 1/4 = 63/8 = 7 7/8 square feet**

**Example D — 1 1/3 x 3 3/4**
1. Convert: 1 1/3 = 4/3; 3 3/4 = 15/4.
2. Cross-cancel: 4 and 4 share factor 4. Simplify to 1/3 x 15/1. Then 15 and 3 share factor 3. Simplify to 1/1 x 5/1.
3. Multiply: 5/1 = 5.
4. Estimate: 1 x 4 = 4. Answer of 5 is close. Reasonable.
**Answer: 1 1/3 x 3 3/4 = 5**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. Students attempt each problem first, then discuss.

1. 1 1/2 x 2 2/3 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Convert both: 1 1/2 = 3/2 and 2 2/3 = 8/3. Before multiplying, check — can you cross-cancel? The 3 in the denominator of the first fraction and the 3 in the denominator of the second... wait, cross-cancellation works between a numerator and a denominator, not two denominators. Check: numerator 8 and denominator 2 — yes, both divisible by 2."

2. 2 1/3 x 1 1/2 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Estimate first: about 2 x 2 = 4. Now convert and multiply. Is your answer close to 4?"

3. 1 3/4 x 2 2/5 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Convert both mixed numbers. Look for cross-cancellation opportunities. Then multiply, convert back, and simplify."

4. A rectangular flag is 1 1/2 feet tall and 2 1/3 feet wide. What is the area of the flag?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Set up the multiplication: 1 1/2 x 2 1/3. Convert, multiply, and don't forget to include 'square feet' in your answer."

5. A sidewalk is 4 1/2 feet wide and 3 1/3 feet long. What is the area of this section of sidewalk?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Estimate first: about 5 x 3 = 15. Convert both mixed numbers, multiply, and check — is your answer close to 15?"

> **Facilitator Tip:** Cross-cancellation is a powerful shortcut but can be confusing for students who try to cancel two numerators or two denominators with each other. Remind students: "Cross-cancellation means one number comes from the top and the other from the bottom — they must be across from each other diagonally." If a student is unsure, it is perfectly fine to skip cross-cancellation and simplify at the end instead — both approaches yield the same result.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A — Multiplying Two Mixed Numbers (On-Level)
Solve each problem. Show all conversion steps and express your answer as a mixed number in simplest form.

1. 1 1/4 x 2 2/3 = ?
2. 2 1/3 x 1 1/5 = ?
3. 1 3/4 x 1 1/3 = ?
4. 3 1/2 x 1 1/7 = ?

### Part B — Area and Word Problems (Stretch)
5. A picture frame is 2 1/2 inches wide and 3 1/3 inches tall. What is the area of the picture frame?
6. A piece of fabric is 1 3/4 yards long and 1 1/3 yards wide. What is the area of the fabric?
7. Carlos is tiling a small section of his kitchen floor. The section is 2 1/4 feet by 1 2/3 feet. What is the area he needs to tile?
8. A recipe makes 1 1/2 batches of muffins. Each batch requires 2 1/3 cups of mix. How many cups of mix does Carlos need in total?

### Part C — Challenge
9. Estimate, then compute: 4 2/3 x 3 3/4. How close was your estimate to the exact answer? Explain why the estimate was higher or lower.
10. A rectangular garden has an area of 8 3/4 square feet. The length of the garden is 2 1/2 feet. What is the width? (Hint: work backward — divide the area by the length. Can you set up a multiplication equation with a missing factor?)
11. Kenji claims that 2 1/2 x 3 1/2 = 6 1/4 because "you just multiply the whole numbers (2 x 3 = 6) and multiply the fractions (1/2 x 1/2 = 1/4)." Is Kenji correct? Compute the actual answer and explain why his method does or does not work.

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. 1 1/4 x 2 2/3: Convert: 5/4 x 8/3. Cross-cancel: 8 and 4 share factor 4 (giving 5/1 x 2/3). Multiply: 10/3 = 3 1/3. **Answer: 3 1/3.**
2. 2 1/3 x 1 1/5: Convert: 7/3 x 6/5. Cross-cancel: 6 and 3 share factor 3 (giving 7/1 x 2/5). Multiply: 14/5 = 2 4/5. **Answer: 2 4/5.**
3. 1 3/4 x 1 1/3: Convert: 7/4 x 4/3. Cross-cancel: 4 and 4 share factor 4 (giving 7/1 x 1/3). Multiply: 7/3 = 2 1/3. **Answer: 2 1/3.**
4. 3 1/2 x 1 1/7: Convert: 7/2 x 8/7. Cross-cancel: 7 and 7 share factor 7 (giving 1/2 x 8/1). Then 8 and 2 share factor 2 (giving 1/1 x 4/1). Multiply: 4. **Answer: 4.**

**Part B**
5. 2 1/2 x 3 1/3: Convert: 5/2 x 10/3. Cross-cancel: 10 and 2 share factor 2 (giving 5/1 x 5/3). Multiply: 25/3 = 8 1/3. **Answer: 8 1/3 square inches.**
6. 1 3/4 x 1 1/3: Convert: 7/4 x 4/3. Cross-cancel: 4 and 4 (giving 7/1 x 1/3). Multiply: 7/3 = 2 1/3. **Answer: 2 1/3 square yards.**
7. 2 1/4 x 1 2/3: Convert: 9/4 x 5/3. Cross-cancel: 9 and 3 share factor 3 (giving 3/4 x 5/1). Multiply: 15/4 = 3 3/4. **Answer: 3 3/4 square feet.**
8. 1 1/2 x 2 1/3: Convert: 3/2 x 7/3. Cross-cancel: 3 and 3 share factor 3 (giving 1/2 x 7/1). Multiply: 7/2 = 3 1/2. **Answer: 3 1/2 cups of mix.**

**Part C**
9. Estimate: 5 x 4 = 20. Compute: 4 2/3 x 3 3/4. Convert: 14/3 x 15/4. Cross-cancel: 14 and 4 share factor 2 (giving 7/3 x 15/2). Then 15 and 3 share factor 3 (giving 7/1 x 5/2). Multiply: 35/2 = 17 1/2. The estimate of 20 was higher because both mixed numbers were rounded up (4 2/3 to 5, and 3 3/4 to 4). **Answer: 17 1/2. The estimate of 20 was higher because both factors were rounded up.**
10. If area = length x width, then width = area / length = 8 3/4 / 2 1/2. Convert: 35/4 / 5/2 = 35/4 x 2/5 = 70/20 = 7/2 = 3 1/2. Check: 2 1/2 x 3 1/2 = 5/2 x 7/2 = 35/4 = 8 3/4. Confirmed. **Answer: The width is 3 1/2 feet.**
11. Kenji is incorrect. Actual computation: 2 1/2 x 3 1/2 = 5/2 x 7/2 = 35/4 = 8 3/4. Kenji got 6 1/4, which is not the same as 8 3/4. His method fails because he did not account for the "cross terms." When you multiply (2 + 1/2)(3 + 1/2), you get 2 x 3 + 2 x 1/2 + 1/2 x 3 + 1/2 x 1/2 = 6 + 1 + 1 1/2 + 1/4 = 8 3/4. Kenji only computed the first term (2 x 3) and the last term (1/2 x 1/2), missing the middle terms. **Answer: Kenji is incorrect. The correct answer is 8 3/4.**

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
Solve the following two problems. Show all your work, including conversion to improper fractions and any cross-cancellation.

**Problem 1:** 2 1/3 x 1 1/2 = ? (express as a mixed number)
**Problem 2:** A rectangular rug is 2 3/4 feet long and 1 1/3 feet wide. What is the area of the rug?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct with work shown = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct or correct answer but work missing = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

**Exit Ticket Answers:**
1. 2 1/3 x 1 1/2: Convert: 7/3 x 3/2. Cross-cancel: 3 and 3 (giving 7/1 x 1/2). Multiply: 7/2 = 3 1/2. **Answer: 3 1/2.**
2. 2 3/4 x 1 1/3: Convert: 11/4 x 4/3. Cross-cancel: 4 and 4 (giving 11/1 x 1/3). Multiply: 11/3 = 3 2/3. **Answer: The area of the rug is 3 2/3 square feet.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review Lesson 7 skills first — if a student cannot multiply a single mixed number by a fraction, they are not ready for two mixed numbers. Provide targeted practice on single-mixed-number problems before re-attempting.
- Walk through the full process with the student using color coding: write each step in a different color (conversion in blue, cross-cancellation in green, multiplication in red, final conversion in purple) so the student can see each distinct phase.
- Provide a step-by-step reference card: (1) Estimate by rounding both mixed numbers, (2) Convert BOTH mixed numbers to improper fractions, (3) Look for cross-cancellation, (4) Multiply numerators and denominators, (5) Simplify, (6) Convert back to a mixed number, (7) Compare to your estimate.
- Pair the student with a peer tutor for 3-4 additional problems before the unit assessment.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Students may begin the Extension activity below.
- Students may design their own "dream room" floor plan with mixed-number dimensions and calculate the area of each section.

## Extension

- **Room Design Project:** Students draw a floor plan for a small room with at least three rectangular sections (e.g., a reading nook that is 2 1/2 ft by 3 1/4 ft, a desk area that is 1 3/4 ft by 2 1/2 ft, and a rug space that is 4 1/3 ft by 2 2/3 ft). They calculate the area of each section and the total floor area. Students present their designs and explain their calculations.
- **Estimation Game:** Students work in pairs. One student writes a mixed number multiplication problem (e.g., 3 1/4 x 2 2/3). The other student has 10 seconds to estimate the answer by rounding. Then both students compute the exact answer. The estimator earns a point if their estimate is within 2 of the exact answer. Play 5 rounds and switch roles.
- **Fraction Detective:** Present the equation: ? x 1 1/2 = 5 1/4. Students must find the missing mixed number. (Solution: 5 1/4 / 1 1/2 = 21/4 / 3/2 = 21/4 x 2/3 = 42/12 = 7/2 = 3 1/2.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Converting only one mixed number.** Students may convert one factor to an improper fraction and leave the other as a mixed number, then try to multiply. Remind them: "Check — are BOTH factors written as fractions before you multiply?"
- **Cross-cancellation mistakes.** Students may try to cancel two numerators with each other or two denominators with each other. Emphasize: "Cross-cancellation means one from the top, one from the bottom — they go diagonally across the multiplication sign." Students who are confused should skip cross-cancellation and simplify at the end.
- **Using Kenji's method (multiplying whole parts and fraction parts separately).** This is a persistent misconception because it feels intuitive. Use Problem 11 from Part C as a teaching moment. Show the distributive property expansion to explain why the "cross terms" matter.
- **Arithmetic errors with larger numbers.** When both mixed numbers are converted, the numerators and denominators can be larger than students are used to (e.g., 14/3 x 15/4). Encourage cross-cancellation to keep numbers manageable, and remind students to double-check their multiplication.
- **Forgetting square units in area problems.** Students may write "7 7/8 feet" instead of "7 7/8 square feet." Reinforce: "When you multiply length times width, the units are squared."

### Pacing Advice
- If students mastered Lesson 7 quickly, you can move through Steps 1-4 of Direct Instruction at a brisk pace and spend more time on the real-world area problem (Step 7) and on cross-cancellation (Step 3).
- If many students are still shaky on single-mixed-number multiplication, spend the first 5 minutes of Direct Instruction reviewing a Lesson 7 problem before introducing two mixed numbers.
- Cross-cancellation is a useful tool but not a required one. If introducing it slows the class down significantly, teach it as an optional shortcut and focus on ensuring students can get correct answers by simplifying at the end. Cross-cancellation can be reinforced in later lessons and practice.
- The area word problems are essential for connecting to 5.NF.6. Make sure at least one area problem is completed during class, whether in Guided Practice or Independent Practice.
- For students who finish early, the Extension activities — especially the Room Design Project — provide rich, open-ended practice that reinforces the lesson's skills in a creative context.
