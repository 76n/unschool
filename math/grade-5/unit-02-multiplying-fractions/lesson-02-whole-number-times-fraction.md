# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 2, Lesson 2

## Topic
Whole Number x Fraction (Part of a Group)

## Learning Objectives
- Students will interpret "a fraction of a whole number" (e.g., 2/3 of 12) as fraction multiplication (2/3 x 12).
- Students will model "fraction of a group" using discrete objects (counters) by dividing into equal groups and selecting the appropriate number of groups.
- Students will compute whole number x fraction using the algorithm: (whole number x numerator) / denominator.
- Students will connect the concrete model to the fraction algorithm: n/1 x a/b = (n x a) / (1 x b).
- Students will distinguish "fraction of a number" (multiplication) from "division by a fraction" (a different operation introduced later).

## Prerequisites
- Multiplication of a fraction by a whole number using repeated addition (Lesson 1).
- Understanding of fractions as parts of a whole.
- Whole-number multiplication and division fluency.
- Ability to write a whole number as a fraction with denominator 1 (e.g., 12 = 12/1).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Counters, cubes, or small objects (at least 30 per student or pair).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Grid paper or blank paper for drawing models.
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 5.NF.4a — Interpret the product (a/b) x q as a parts of a partition of q into b equal parts; equivalently, as the result of a sequence of operations a x q / b.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of equal sharing and connect everyday language ("fraction of") to multiplication.

1. Display the following on the board: "There are 20 apples. You get 1/4 of them. How many apples do you get?"
2. Ask: "What does '1/4 of 20' mean in your own words?" (Divide 20 into 4 equal groups and take 1 group.)
3. Have students use counters: arrange 20 counters into 4 equal groups. Ask: "How many are in each group?" (5.) "So 1/4 of 20 = 5."
4. Now ask: "What if you got 3/4 of the 20 apples? How many would that be?" (Take 3 of the 4 groups: 3 x 5 = 15.)
5. Tell students: "Today we are going to learn that '3/4 of 20' is the same as 3/4 x 20. The word 'of' tells us to multiply."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **"Of" means multiply** — in fraction problems, the word "of" signals multiplication (e.g., 2/3 of 12 = 2/3 x 12).
- **Partition** — dividing a set or quantity into equal groups.
- **Discrete model** — using individual objects (like counters) to represent a whole number, then grouping them to find a fraction of the total.
- **Algorithm** — a step-by-step procedure for computing; here: (whole number x numerator) / denominator.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Establish the meaning of "fraction of a group" with a concrete model.**
Place 12 counters on the table or draw 12 circles on the board.
"Let's find 2/3 of 12."
"The denominator is 3, so divide the 12 counters into 3 equal groups."
Arrange: Group 1 = 4 counters, Group 2 = 4 counters, Group 3 = 4 counters.
"The numerator is 2, so take 2 of those 3 groups."
Circle 2 groups: 4 + 4 = 8 counters.
"So 2/3 of 12 = 8."

**Step 2 — Connect the concrete model to the algorithm.**
"Here is what we just did, step by step:"
- We divided 12 by 3 (the denominator) to find the size of each group: 12 / 3 = 4.
- We multiplied by 2 (the numerator) to find how many counters in 2 groups: 2 x 4 = 8.

Write the general algorithm:
- **To find a/b of n: compute (n x a) / b, or equivalently, (n / b) x a.**

"Both paths give the same answer. Let's check: (12 x 2) / 3 = 24 / 3 = 8. And (12 / 3) x 2 = 4 x 2 = 8."

**Step 3 — Show the fraction multiplication form.**
"We can also write this as a fraction problem by expressing 12 as 12/1."
Write: 12/1 x 2/3 = (12 x 2) / (1 x 3) = 24/3 = 8.
"This is the same fraction multiplication algorithm we will use all unit: multiply numerators, multiply denominators, then simplify."

**Step 4 — Model a second example: 3/5 of 20.**
Concrete: 20 counters divided into 5 groups of 4. Take 3 groups: 3 x 4 = 12.
Algorithm: (20 x 3) / 5 = 60 / 5 = 12.
Fraction form: 20/1 x 3/5 = 60/5 = 12.
"Notice the answer is a whole number. This happens when the denominator divides evenly into the product of the whole number and numerator."

**Step 5 — Model a problem where the answer is not a whole number: 2/3 of 10.**
Concrete: 10 counters divided into 3 groups — but 10 does not divide evenly by 3. This is where the algorithm helps.
Algorithm: (10 x 2) / 3 = 20/3 = 6 2/3.
Fraction form: 10/1 x 2/3 = 20/3 = 6 2/3.
"When the denominator does not divide evenly into the whole number, we get a mixed number. The algorithm still works perfectly."

**Step 6 — Distinguish from division by a fraction.**
"Be careful: '2/3 of 12' means 2/3 x 12 = 8. This is NOT the same as 12 / 2/3 (dividing 12 by two-thirds), which is a different operation we will study in a later unit. The word 'of' tells us to multiply."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — 2/3 of 12**
Concrete: 12 counters / 3 groups = 4 per group. Take 2 groups = 8.
Algorithm: (12 x 2) / 3 = 24 / 3 = 8
Fraction form: 12/1 x 2/3 = 24/3 = 8
**Answer: 8**

**Example B — 3/4 of 24**
Concrete: 24 counters / 4 groups = 6 per group. Take 3 groups = 18.
Algorithm: (24 x 3) / 4 = 72 / 4 = 18
Fraction form: 24/1 x 3/4 = 72/4 = 18
**Answer: 18**

**Example C — 2/5 of 15**
Concrete: 15 counters / 5 groups = 3 per group. Take 2 groups = 6.
Algorithm: (15 x 2) / 5 = 30 / 5 = 6
Fraction form: 15/1 x 2/5 = 30/5 = 6
**Answer: 6**

**Example D — 3/4 of 10**
Algorithm: (10 x 3) / 4 = 30 / 4 = 7 1/2
Fraction form: 10/1 x 3/4 = 30/4 = 15/2 = 7 1/2
**Answer: 30/4 = 7 1/2**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. Students attempt each problem first (using counters if desired), then discuss.

1. Find 1/3 of 18.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Divide 18 counters into 3 equal groups. How many in each group? Since the numerator is 1, you just take 1 group."

2. Find 3/4 of 16.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "How many groups? How many in each group? How many groups do you take?"

3. Find 2/5 of 30.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Try the algorithm: (30 x 2) / 5. Can you also verify with counters?"

4. Find 5/6 of 18.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Write this as a fraction multiplication: 18/1 x 5/6. Multiply across and simplify."

5. Find 3/8 of 20.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Does 20 divide evenly by 8? Use the algorithm: (20 x 3) / 8. Express your answer as a mixed number."

> **Facilitator Tip:** Encourage students to use both the concrete model (counters) and the algorithm side by side for Problems 1-3. For Problems 4-5, push students toward the algorithm since the numbers are less friendly for physical grouping. This mirrors the concrete-to-abstract progression.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A — Computation (On-Level)
Find each product. Express your answer as a whole number or a mixed number in simplest form.

1. 1/4 of 28 = ?
2. 2/3 of 15 = ?
3. 3/5 of 35 = ?
4. 5/8 of 24 = ?

### Part B — Word Problems (Stretch)
5. There are 24 students in Mr. Rivera's class. 3/4 of the students brought their lunch from home. How many students brought their lunch?
6. A bakery made 30 muffins. They sold 2/5 of them in the morning. How many muffins did they sell in the morning? How many are left?
7. A bookshelf holds 36 books. 5/6 of the books are fiction. How many fiction books are on the shelf?
8. Jamal has 40 trading cards. He gives 3/8 of them to his friend. How many cards does he give away? How many does he keep?

### Part C — Challenge
9. A school has 150 students. 2/5 of the students play a sport, and 3/4 of the students who play a sport play soccer. How many students play soccer? (Hint: this is a two-step problem.)
10. Which is greater: 2/3 of 18 or 3/4 of 16? Show your work for both and compare.
11. Marcus says that 3/5 of 25 is 10. Keisha says it is 15. Who is correct? Explain the error the other person made.

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. 1/4 of 28 = (28 x 1) / 4 = 28/4 = 7
2. 2/3 of 15 = (15 x 2) / 3 = 30/3 = 10
3. 3/5 of 35 = (35 x 3) / 5 = 105/5 = 21
4. 5/8 of 24 = (24 x 5) / 8 = 120/8 = 15

**Part B**
5. 3/4 of 24 = (24 x 3) / 4 = 72/4 = 18 students brought their lunch.
6. 2/5 of 30 = (30 x 2) / 5 = 60/5 = 12 muffins sold. 30 - 12 = 18 muffins remaining.
7. 5/6 of 36 = (36 x 5) / 6 = 180/6 = 30 fiction books.
8. 3/8 of 40 = (40 x 3) / 8 = 120/8 = 15 cards given away. 40 - 15 = 25 cards remaining.

**Part C**
9. Step 1: 2/5 of 150 = (150 x 2) / 5 = 300/5 = 60 students play a sport. Step 2: 3/4 of 60 = (60 x 3) / 4 = 180/4 = 45 students play soccer.
10. 2/3 of 18 = (18 x 2) / 3 = 36/3 = 12. And 3/4 of 16 = (16 x 3) / 4 = 48/4 = 12. They are equal; both equal 12.
11. Keisha is correct: 3/5 of 25 = (25 x 3) / 5 = 75/5 = 15. Marcus likely divided 25 by 5 to get 5 and then multiplied by 2 instead of 3 (computing 2/5 of 25 = 10 by mistake), or he forgot to multiply by the numerator and simply computed 25 / 5 x 2 = 10. The correct numerator to use is 3, not 2.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket
Solve the following two problems. Show your work.

**Problem 1:** Find 3/4 of 20.
**Problem 2:** There are 36 crayons in a box. 2/3 of them are broken. How many crayons are broken?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct with work shown = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct or correct answer but work missing = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the concrete model with counters. Have the student physically partition 12 counters into groups of 3 (for 1/3 of 12) and groups of 4 (for 1/4 of 12) until the process is automatic.
- Provide a step-by-step reference card: (1) Identify the fraction and the whole number, (2) Divide the whole number by the denominator, (3) Multiply by the numerator. Or: (1) Write the whole number as n/1, (2) Multiply across, (3) Simplify.
- Assign 3 additional practice problems at the On-Level tier before moving to Lesson 3.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Students may begin the Extension activity below.
- Students may serve as peer tutors for classmates who need additional support with the counter model.

## Extension

- **Fraction of a Fraction Preview:** If 2/3 of a class of 24 students wear glasses, and 1/2 of those students wear blue glasses, how many students wear blue glasses? (This previews fraction x fraction in Lesson 3.)
- **Real-World Investigation:** Survey the class and collect a data set (e.g., number of siblings). Then compute fractions of the class total: "What is 1/4 of our class?" "What is 3/5 of the total number of siblings reported?" Discuss whether fractional answers make sense in context (can you have 2/3 of a person?).
- **Create Your Own:** Write three word problems that use "fraction of a whole number." Include the answer key. Trade with a partner and solve.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Confusing "fraction of" (multiplication) with "divided by a fraction."** For example, interpreting "2/3 of 12" as 12 / (2/3) = 18 instead of 2/3 x 12 = 8. Emphasize: "The word 'of' means multiply. Division by a fraction is a different operation that makes numbers larger, not smaller."
- **Dividing by the numerator instead of the denominator.** For example, computing 2/3 of 12 as 12 / 2 = 6 instead of 12 / 3 = 4, then 4 x 2 = 8. Redirect: "The denominator tells you how many equal groups. The numerator tells you how many groups to take."
- **Forgetting to multiply by the numerator.** Students may compute 1/3 of 12 correctly as 4 but then give 4 as the answer for 2/3 of 12. Remind: "You found the size of one group. Now how many groups does the numerator tell you to take?"
- **Difficulty when the whole number does not divide evenly by the denominator.** For example, 3/4 of 10 yields 30/4 = 7 1/2. Students may think they made an error because they expect whole-number answers. Reassure them that fractional answers are valid and show how the algorithm handles these cases cleanly.

### Pacing Advice
- Spend ample time on Step 1 (concrete model with counters). This is the conceptual anchor for the entire lesson. Students who internalize "divide into groups, then count groups" will have strong intuition for fraction multiplication going forward.
- If students can quickly set up counter models, transition to the algorithm by Step 2 and use the saved time for more word problems in Independent Practice.
- The distinction between "fraction of" (multiplication) and "divided by a fraction" (Step 6) is important but should be brief at this point. A full treatment of fraction division comes in Unit 3. Simply plant the seed here so students are aware the operations are different.
- Word problems are critical in this lesson because "fraction of a group" arises naturally in real-world contexts. Prioritize Part B of Independent Practice over Part C if time is limited.
