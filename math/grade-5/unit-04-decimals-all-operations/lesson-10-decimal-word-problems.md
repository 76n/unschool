# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Topic
Decimal Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve multi-step word problems involving decimal operations in real-world contexts (money, measurement, science).
- Use a structured problem-solving process: understand, plan, solve, check.
- Identify the correct operations and sequence of steps in a multi-step problem.
- Communicate solutions clearly with proper units and labels.

## Prerequisites
- All four decimal operations (Lessons 2-3, 5-8).
- Mixed practice with operation identification (Lesson 9).
- Estimating with decimals (Lesson 4).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers.
- Scratch paper.
- Index cards for exit ticket.

**Standards:** 5.NBT.7

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick review of all four operations to activate skills before word problems.

Solve each problem mentally or on paper.

1. 4.5 + 3.75 = ?
   - **Answer: 8.25**
2. 10 - 6.48 = ?
   - **Answer: 3.52**
3. 0.6 x 7 = ?
   - **Answer: 4.2**
4. 8 / 0.4 = ?
   - **Answer: 20**

Say: "Today you will use all of these skills to solve real-world problems. The hardest part is not the computation -- it is reading carefully, planning your steps, and making sure your answer makes sense."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Multi-step problem:** A problem that requires two or more operations to solve.
- **Reasonable answer:** An answer that makes sense in the context of the problem.
- **Units:** Labels that tell what the numbers represent (meters, dollars, kilograms, etc.).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — The 4-step problem-solving process.**

Write on the board:
1. **Understand:** What is the problem asking? What information is given?
2. **Plan:** What operations do I need? In what order?
3. **Solve:** Do the computation. Show your work.
4. **Check:** Does my answer make sense? Is it reasonable? Did I include units?

Say: "We will use these four steps for every problem today. Let me model with an example."

**Step 2 — Model Problem: Money Context.**

Read aloud: "Maya goes to the store. She buys 3 binders at $4.25 each and 2 packs of paper at $2.50 each. She pays with a $20 bill. How much change does she receive?"

**Understand:** We need to find the change Maya gets back. We know the cost of binders, the cost of paper, the number of each, and how much she paid.

**Plan:** Step 1 -- find the cost of binders (multiply). Step 2 -- find the cost of paper (multiply). Step 3 -- find the total cost (add). Step 4 -- find the change (subtract from $20).

**Solve:**
- Binders: 3 x $4.25 = $12.75
- Paper: 2 x $2.50 = $5.00
- Total: $12.75 + $5.00 = $17.75
- Change: $20.00 - $17.75 = $2.25

**Check:** Estimate -- 3 x $4 = $12, 2 x $3 = $6, total about $18, change about $2. Our answer of $2.25 is close. The answer has proper units (dollars). It makes sense.

**Answer: Maya receives $2.25 in change.**

**Step 3 — Model Problem: Measurement Context.**

Read aloud: "A piece of wood is 3.6 meters long. Aiden cuts off a piece that is 1.45 meters long. He then cuts the remaining piece into 5 equal sections. How long is each section?"

**Understand:** Find the length of each section after cutting.

**Plan:** Step 1 -- subtract the first cut. Step 2 -- divide the remaining length by 5.

**Solve:**
- Remaining: 3.60 - 1.45 = 2.15 meters
- Each section: 2.15 / 5 = 0.43 meters

**Check:** 0.43 x 5 = 2.15. And 2.15 + 1.45 = 3.60. Everything checks out.

**Answer: Each section is 0.43 meters long.**

**Step 4 — Model Problem: Science Context.**

Read aloud: "In a science experiment, a beaker holds 2.5 liters of water. The student pours out 0.75 liters each time she runs a test. After 3 tests, how much water remains?"

**Understand:** Find the water left after 3 tests.

**Plan:** Step 1 -- find the total water used (multiply). Step 2 -- subtract from the starting amount.

**Solve:**
- Used: 3 x 0.75 = 2.25 liters
- Remaining: 2.50 - 2.25 = 0.25 liters

**Check:** 0.25 + 2.25 = 2.50. Correct.

**Answer: 0.25 liters of water remain.**

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** "A family drives 125.4 km on Monday and 98.7 km on Tuesday. How much farther did they drive on Monday?"
- Subtraction: 125.4 - 98.7 = 26.7 km

**Example B:** "Bananas cost $0.65 per pound. How much do 4 pounds cost?"
- Multiplication: 4 x $0.65 = $2.60

**Example C:** "A 7.5-meter ribbon is cut into pieces that are each 0.5 meters long. How many pieces are there?"
- Division: 7.5 / 0.5. Multiply both by 10: 75 / 5 = 15 pieces.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Solve together using the 4-step process. Identify the operation(s) before computing.

1. Carlos has $15.00. He buys a sandwich for $6.75 and a drink for $2.50. How much money does he have left?
   - Add: $6.75 + $2.50 = $9.25. Subtract: $15.00 - $9.25 = **$5.75**

2. A package of meat weighs 1.36 kg. A package of cheese weighs 0.45 kg. If you buy 2 packages of meat and 1 package of cheese, what is the total weight?
   - Multiply: 2 x 1.36 = 2.72 kg. Add: 2.72 + 0.45 = **3.17 kg**

3. A trail is 4.8 km long. Sara has walked 2.95 km so far. If she walks the remaining distance in 2 equal parts, how long is each part?
   - Subtract: 4.80 - 2.95 = 1.85 km. Divide: 1.85 / 2 = **0.925 km**

4. Each tile is 0.3 meters wide. How many tiles are needed to cover a floor that is 6 meters wide?
   - Divide: 6 / 0.3 = 60 / 3 = **20 tiles**

5. The temperature at 6 AM was 4.8 degrees Celsius. By noon, it had risen by 7.5 degrees. By 6 PM, it had dropped by 3.2 degrees from the noon reading. What was the temperature at 6 PM?
   - Noon: 4.8 + 7.5 = 12.3 degrees. At 6 PM: 12.3 - 3.2 = **9.1 degrees Celsius**

**Facilitator tip:** For each problem, have the student tell you the plan before they start computing. This builds the habit of thinking before calculating.

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (On Level)
1. A farmer harvests 24.6 kg of apples on Saturday and 18.35 kg on Sunday. How many more kilograms were harvested on Saturday?
2. A pack of 8 water bottles costs $6.00. How much does each bottle cost?
3. A jogger runs 2.75 km each day for 6 days. What is the total distance?
4. A board is 10 meters long. Pieces of 0.25 meters are cut off. How many pieces can be cut?
5. Li has $25.00. She buys 2 books at $7.95 each. Does she have enough money left to buy a $10 poster?

### Tier 2 (Scaffolded)
1. Read the problem: "A bag of flour weighs 2.5 kg. You use 0.75 kg for one recipe. How much flour is left?"
   What operation? ___. Solve: ___
2. Read: "A pen costs $1.35. How much do 4 pens cost?"
   Step 1: operation = ___. Computation: ___. Answer with units: ___
3. Two-step: "A rope is 8 meters long. You cut off 2.4 meters. You divide the remaining rope into 4 equal pieces."
   Step 1: ___ - ___ = ___ meters remaining.
   Step 2: ___ / ___ = ___ meters per piece.
4. Check this answer: "3 books at $5.49 each cost $15.47." Is this correct? Estimate to decide, then compute.

### Tier 3 (Challenge)
1. A recipe calls for 1.5 cups of flour for 12 cookies. How much flour is needed for 36 cookies? For 8 cookies?
2. Jake ran 3.2 km on Monday, 4.15 km on Tuesday, and 2.8 km on Wednesday. His goal for the week (Mon-Fri) is 20 km. How much does he need to run on Thursday and Friday combined to meet his goal?
3. A store sells juice boxes in packs of 6 for $4.50 or individually for $0.89 each. Which is the better deal for 6 juice boxes? How much do you save?
4. A scientist measures the mass of 5 identical samples. The total mass is 3.75 grams. She then needs 12 of these samples for an experiment. What is the total mass of 12 samples?
5. The gas tank in a car holds 45 liters. The car uses 6.5 liters per 100 km. The car drives 300 km. How many liters of gas are used? How many liters remain in the tank?

## Exit Ticket

1. A plumber has a pipe that is 5.4 meters long. She cuts off a piece that is 1.85 meters. How much pipe is left?
2. Each tile weighs 0.35 kg. A box contains 8 tiles. What is the total weight of the tiles?
3. Tyrone buys 3 notebooks at $2.45 each and a pen for $1.89. What is the total cost?

**Mastery: 3 out of 3 correct with proper units.**

## Answer Key

### Guided Practice
1. $5.75
2. 3.17 kg
3. 0.925 km
4. 20 tiles
5. 9.1 degrees Celsius

### Independent Practice — Tier 1
1. 24.60 - 18.35 = 6.25 kg more on Saturday
2. $6.00 / 8 = $0.75 per bottle
3. 2.75 x 6 = 16.50 km
4. 10 / 0.25 = 1000 / 25 = 40 pieces
5. 2 x $7.95 = $15.90. $25.00 - $15.90 = $9.10 remaining. $9.10 < $10.00, so no, she does not have enough for the poster.

### Independent Practice — Tier 2
1. Subtraction. 2.50 - 0.75 = 1.75 kg remaining.
2. Multiplication. 4 x $1.35 = $5.40. Answer: $5.40.
3. Step 1: 8.0 - 2.4 = 5.6 meters. Step 2: 5.6 / 4 = 1.4 meters per piece.
4. Estimate: 3 x $5.50 = $16.50, not $15.47. Exact: 3 x $5.49 = $16.47. The given answer of $15.47 is incorrect.

### Independent Practice — Tier 3
1. For 12 cookies: 1.5 cups. For 36 cookies: 36 is 3 times 12, so 3 x 1.5 = 4.5 cups. For 8 cookies: 8 is 2/3 of 12, so (2/3) x 1.5 = 1.0 cup. (Or: 1.5 / 12 = 0.125 cups per cookie; 8 x 0.125 = 1.0 cup.)
2. Total so far: 3.2 + 4.15 + 2.8 = 10.15 km. Remaining: 20 - 10.15 = 9.85 km needed on Thursday and Friday.
3. Pack: $4.50 for 6, which is $4.50 / 6 = $0.75 each. Individual: $0.89 each. The pack is cheaper. Savings: 6 x $0.89 = $5.34. $5.34 - $4.50 = $0.84 saved.
4. Mass of one sample: 3.75 / 5 = 0.75 grams. Mass of 12: 12 x 0.75 = 9.0 grams.
5. Gas used: 300 km requires 3 x 6.5 = 19.5 liters. Remaining: 45 - 19.5 = 25.5 liters.

### Exit Ticket
1. 5.40 - 1.85 = 3.55 meters
2. 8 x 0.35 = 2.80 kg
3. 3 x $2.45 = $7.35. $7.35 + $1.89 = $9.24 total.

## Teacher Notes

- This is the capstone lesson of the unit. It integrates all skills and requires the highest level of thinking: reading comprehension, planning, computation, and verification.
- The 4-step problem-solving process (Understand, Plan, Solve, Check) should be explicitly modeled and then expected for every problem. Have students write out their plan before computing.
- Multi-step problems are where students most often make errors -- not computational errors, but organizational ones. They lose track of intermediate results or skip a step. Encourage neat, labeled work.
- For struggling students, the scaffolded problems break multi-step problems into explicit sub-steps. This is training wheels for the planning process.
- Real-world contexts matter. Use contexts the students care about: shopping, sports, cooking, science experiments. When students see the relevance, motivation increases.
- Labeling with units is not optional. An answer of "3.55" means nothing without "meters." Build this habit now.
- If time allows, have students create their own word problems and trade with a partner. Writing problems deepens understanding of the operations.
- This lesson serves as a review before the unit assessment. If significant gaps appear during this lesson, reteach the relevant operation before assessing.
