# Facilitator Guide — Grade 5 Math, Unit 4: Decimals — All Operations

**Grade:** 5
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 4 — Decimals: All Operations
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 4-5 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Partially facilitated (facilitator introduces lessons and supports guided practice; student works more independently during warm-up and independent practice)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit brings together all four arithmetic operations with decimal numbers. Students begin by strengthening their understanding of decimal place value through thousandths, then progress through addition, subtraction, estimation, multiplication, and division of decimals. The unit culminates with mixed practice and multi-step word problems drawn from real-world contexts such as money, measurement, and science.

Students enter this unit having worked extensively with whole-number operations and fraction arithmetic in Units 1-3. The decimal work here extends those ideas: decimals are simply another way to represent fractions with denominators of 10, 100, and 1,000. The unit follows a Concrete-Representational-Abstract (CRA) progression throughout, using base-ten blocks, decimal grids, and money models before transitioning to standard algorithms.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Read, write, and compare decimals through thousandths, explaining the x10 and /10 relationship between adjacent place values
- Add and subtract decimals to hundredths using the standard algorithm, lining up decimal points
- Estimate decimal sums, differences, products, and quotients using rounding and front-end estimation
- Multiply a decimal by a whole number and by another decimal, placing the decimal point correctly in the product
- Divide a decimal by a whole number and divide a whole number by a decimal, using strategies to handle the decimal point
- Solve multi-step word problems involving decimal operations in real-world contexts
- Use estimation to check the reasonableness of computed answers

**Why this matters:** Decimal fluency is essential for everyday life -- money, measurement, science, and data all use decimals. Mastery of decimal operations in Grade 5 prepares students for ratio and rate reasoning in Grade 6 and for working with all rational numbers in Grade 7.

**Standards:** 5.NBT.7 — Add, subtract, multiply, and divide decimals to hundredths, using concrete models or drawings and strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction; relate the strategy to a written method and explain the reasoning used.

Before beginning this unit, confirm:

- [ ] Understanding of decimal notation for tenths and hundredths (Grade 4 decimal introduction)
- [ ] Ability to compare decimals using place value (Grade 4)
- [ ] Fluency with multi-digit whole-number addition, subtraction, and multiplication (Grade 5 prerequisite)
- [ ] Understanding of long division with whole numbers (Grade 5, prior units)
- [ ] Familiarity with fraction-decimal equivalence (1/10 = 0.1, 1/100 = 0.01)

## Lesson Summaries and Facilitation Notes

### Lesson 1: Decimal Place Value Review
**Goal:** Strengthen understanding of tenths, hundredths, and thousandths and the x10/divide-by-10 relationship between adjacent places.
**Key moves:** Use base-ten blocks to represent decimals. Show that moving one place to the right divides the value by 10, and moving one place to the left multiplies by 10. Read and write decimals in standard, expanded, and word form.
**Watch for:** Students who read 0.30 as "thirty" rather than "three tenths" or "thirty hundredths." Reinforce that the decimal point separates wholes from parts.

### Lesson 2: Adding Decimals
**Goal:** Add decimals to hundredths using the standard algorithm.
**Key moves:** Begin with money contexts (dollars and cents) so students see the need to line up decimal points. Model with base-ten blocks: combine tenths with tenths and hundredths with hundredths, regrouping as needed. Transition to the vertical algorithm.
**Watch for:** Students who line up the last digits instead of the decimal points (e.g., aligning 3.4 + 2.17 incorrectly). Use grid paper to enforce alignment.

### Lesson 3: Subtracting Decimals
**Goal:** Subtract decimals to hundredths, annexing zeros as needed.
**Key moves:** Model with base-ten blocks, showing "trading" (exchanging a tenth for ten hundredths). Emphasize annexing zeros so both numbers have the same number of decimal places before subtracting (e.g., 5.3 - 2.17 becomes 5.30 - 2.17).
**Watch for:** Students who drop the decimal point or who subtract the smaller digit from the larger regardless of position (e.g., computing 5.30 - 2.17 = 3.23 instead of 3.13).

### Lesson 4: Estimating with Decimals
**Goal:** Use rounding and front-end estimation to estimate sums, differences, products, and quotients involving decimals.
**Key moves:** Round each decimal to the nearest whole number, then compute mentally. Introduce front-end estimation (use the leading digits, then adjust). Apply to checking the reasonableness of exact answers.
**Watch for:** Students who estimate correctly but then use the estimate as their final answer. Reinforce: estimation checks your work; it does not replace exact computation.

### Lesson 5: Multiplying Decimals by Whole Numbers
**Goal:** Multiply a decimal by a whole number by extending whole-number multiplication and placing the decimal point.
**Key moves:** Start with money: 3 items at $2.45 each. Use repeated addition to connect to multiplication. Show that 3 x 2.45 = 3 x 245 hundredths = 735 hundredths = 7.35. Develop the rule: multiply as if both are whole numbers, then count decimal places.
**Watch for:** Students who forget to place the decimal point in the product. The "count the decimal places" rule needs to be grounded in understanding, not just memorized.

### Lesson 6: Multiplying Decimals by Decimals
**Goal:** Multiply a decimal by a decimal using area models and the decimal-place-counting method.
**Key moves:** Use a 10x10 grid to model 0.3 x 0.4. Shade 3 columns for 0.3 and 4 rows for 0.4. The overlap (12 small squares) represents 0.12. Connect to the rule: 1 decimal place + 1 decimal place = 2 decimal places in the product.
**Watch for:** Students who think 0.3 x 0.4 = 1.2 (forgetting to count both factors' decimal places). The area model powerfully shows why the product must be smaller than either factor when both factors are less than 1.

### Lesson 7: Dividing Decimals by Whole Numbers
**Goal:** Divide a decimal by a whole number using extended long division.
**Key moves:** Start with sharing money: $8.64 shared among 4 people. Use base-ten blocks to distribute. Transition to long division, showing that the decimal point in the quotient goes directly above the decimal point in the dividend.
**Watch for:** Students who ignore the decimal point and compute 864 / 4 = 216, then don't know where to place it. Anchor back to estimation: $8.64 / 4 should be about $2, so the answer must be 2.16, not 21.6 or 216.

### Lesson 8: Dividing Whole Numbers by Decimals
**Goal:** Divide a whole number by a decimal by multiplying both the divisor and dividend by a power of 10.
**Key moves:** Frame the question: "How many 0.5s are in 3?" Connect to fraction division (3 / 1/2 = 6). Show the strategy: multiply both numbers by 10 (or 100) to make the divisor a whole number, then divide. Verify: 3 / 0.5 = 30 / 5 = 6.
**Watch for:** Students who only multiply one number by the power of 10. Emphasize: both must be multiplied to keep the quotient the same. Use the "equivalent fractions" idea: 3/0.5 = 30/5.

### Lesson 9: Decimal Operations — Mixed Practice
**Goal:** Practice all four operations with decimals and build the skill of choosing the correct operation.
**Key moves:** Present problems that require students to decide which operation to use. Include problems where estimation determines reasonableness. Mix computation with short word problems.
**Watch for:** Students who default to addition or multiplication for everything. Encourage reading word problems carefully and asking: "Am I combining, taking away, finding a total of equal groups, or splitting into equal groups?"

### Lesson 10: Decimal Word Problems
**Goal:** Solve multi-step word problems using decimal operations in real-world contexts.
**Key moves:** Use contexts from money (shopping, budgets), measurement (lengths, weights), and science (temperature, rainfall). Model the problem-solving process: understand, plan, solve, check. Emphasize labeling answers with units.
**Watch for:** Students who struggle to identify the operations in multi-step problems. Break the problem into parts: "What does the first sentence tell you? What does the second sentence ask you to find?"

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Why It Happens | How to Address |
|---------------|---------------|----------------|
| Lining up the last digits instead of the decimal points when adding/subtracting | Students apply whole-number alignment habits | Use grid paper with a highlighted decimal-point column; always annex zeros first |
| Thinking a longer decimal is always larger (0.125 > 0.5) | Confusion between number of digits and value | Compare by place: 0.5 = 0.500, which is greater than 0.125. Use base-ten blocks. |
| Placing the decimal point incorrectly in a product | Mechanical rule without understanding | Ground the rule in estimation: "3 x 2.4 is about 3 x 2 = 6, so 7.2 makes sense but 72 does not." |
| Thinking multiplication always makes numbers bigger | True for whole numbers greater than 1, not for decimals less than 1 | Use the grid model: 0.3 x 0.4 = 0.12, which is smaller than both factors. Ask: "Does your answer make sense?" |
| Forgetting to multiply both divisor and dividend when dividing by a decimal | Students focus on changing the divisor only | Use equivalent fraction reasoning: 6/0.3 = 60/3. Both parts of the fraction must be multiplied. |
| Dropping trailing zeros and changing the value (writing 2.5 instead of 2.50 when needed) | Thinking trailing zeros don't matter | Explain that 2.5 = 2.50 in value, but when aligning for subtraction, writing the zero prevents errors. |

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Decimal** | A number written using a decimal point to show values less than one (tenths, hundredths, thousandths) |
| **Decimal point** | The dot between the ones place and the tenths place |
| **Tenths** | The first place to the right of the decimal point; each tenth is 1/10 |
| **Hundredths** | The second place to the right of the decimal point; each hundredth is 1/100 |
| **Thousandths** | The third place to the right of the decimal point; each thousandth is 1/1000 |
| **Annex a zero** | Write a zero at the end of a decimal without changing its value (e.g., 3.5 = 3.50) |
| **Estimate** | An approximate answer found by rounding or using mental math |
| **Round** | Replace a number with the nearest specified place value (e.g., 3.47 rounds to 3.5 or to 3) |
| **Front-end estimation** | Using the leading (leftmost) digits to estimate, then adjusting |
| **Product** | The result of multiplication |
| **Quotient** | The result of division |
| **Equivalent** | Having the same value (e.g., 0.3 = 0.30) |

## Differentiation Strategies

### Struggling Learners (Tier 2)
- Continue using base-ten blocks and decimal grids through all lessons (delay transition to purely abstract computation)
- Provide graph paper for all addition and subtraction to enforce decimal alignment
- Offer a reference card with the steps for each operation (add: line up points; multiply: count decimal places; divide by decimal: multiply both by 10)
- Reduce the number of practice problems but increase guided practice time
- Use money contexts extensively -- students are often more comfortable with dollars and cents than abstract decimals
- Allow estimation as a first step before exact computation to build number sense

### Advanced Learners (Tier 3)
- Extend to operations with thousandths (e.g., 0.125 x 0.4)
- Challenge problems involving all four operations in a single multi-step problem
- Explore decimal-fraction connections: convert between fractions and decimals, then compute both ways and compare
- Investigation: "When you multiply two decimals less than 1, is the product always less than both factors? Prove it."
- Real-world data projects: calculate batting averages, unit prices, or metric conversions
- Create their own word problems for classmates to solve

## Connection to Later Learning

- **Grade 6:** Students will divide fractions by fractions (extending the decimal division concepts here), work with ratios and rates (which rely on fluent decimal computation), and plot rational numbers on the coordinate plane.
- **Grade 7:** Students will compute with all rational numbers including negative decimals, convert fluently between fractions, decimals, and percents, and solve percent problems.
- **Middle school science and data:** Measurement, data analysis, and scientific notation all require decimal fluency.
- **Everyday life:** Budgeting, shopping, cooking measurements, and understanding statistics all use decimal operations.
