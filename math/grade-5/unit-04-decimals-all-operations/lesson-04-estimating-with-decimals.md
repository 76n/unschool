# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 4

## Topic
Estimating with Decimals

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Round decimals to the nearest whole number, nearest tenth, or nearest hundredth.
- Use rounding to estimate sums, differences, products, and quotients involving decimals.
- Apply front-end estimation as an alternative strategy for addition and subtraction.
- Use estimates to check the reasonableness of exact decimal computations.

## Prerequisites
- Adding and subtracting decimals (Lessons 2-3).
- Rounding whole numbers to any place (Grade 4).
- Decimal place value through thousandths (Lesson 1).

## Materials Needed
- Number line handouts (marked in tenths).
- Whiteboard and markers.
- Index cards for exit ticket.

**Standards:** 5.NBT.4, 5.NBT.7

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review rounding with whole numbers and simple decimals.

1. Round 347 to the nearest hundred: ___
   - **Answer: 300**
2. Round 4.7 to the nearest whole number: ___
   - **Answer: 5**
3. Is 3.82 closer to 3 or to 4?
   - **Answer: 4** (because 0.82 is more than 0.5)

Say: "In the last two lessons, you learned to add and subtract decimals exactly. Today we are going to learn estimation -- finding a quick approximate answer. Estimation is not about being lazy. It is a powerful tool for checking whether your exact answer makes sense."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Estimate:** An approximate value found through rounding or mental math; close to the exact answer but faster to compute.
- **Round:** Replace a number with the closest value at a given place (e.g., 3.47 rounds to 3.5 at the tenths place, or to 3 at the ones place).
- **Front-end estimation:** Use the leftmost digits to get a quick estimate, then adjust by looking at the remaining digits.
- **Reasonableness:** Whether an answer makes sense given the numbers in the problem.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Rounding decimals.**

Say: "Rounding decimals follows the same rules as rounding whole numbers. Look at the digit one place to the right of where you are rounding. If it is 5 or more, round up. If it is less than 5, round down."

Write examples:
- Round 6.43 to the nearest whole number. Look at the tenths digit (4). Since 4 < 5, round down. **Answer: 6**
- Round 6.43 to the nearest tenth. Look at the hundredths digit (3). Since 3 < 5, round down. **Answer: 6.4**
- Round 3.876 to the nearest hundredth. Look at the thousandths digit (6). Since 6 >= 5, round up. **Answer: 3.88**

Show on a number line: plot 6.43 between 6 and 7. It is closer to 6.

**Step 2 — Estimating sums and differences by rounding.**

Write: 4.73 + 8.19

Say: "Round each number to the nearest whole number. 4.73 rounds to 5. 8.19 rounds to 8. 5 + 8 = 13. So the sum is approximately 13."

Compute the exact answer: 4.73 + 8.19 = 12.92

Say: "Is 12.92 close to 13? Yes! So our answer is reasonable."

Write: 15.6 - 8.34

Say: "Round: 15.6 rounds to 16. 8.34 rounds to 8. 16 - 8 = 8. The difference is approximately 8."

Exact: 15.60 - 8.34 = 7.26. Close to 8? Yes, reasonably close.

**Step 3 — Front-end estimation.**

Say: "Front-end estimation is another way to estimate. Use the front digits (the largest place values), then adjust."

Write: 3.82 + 5.47 + 2.15

Say: "Front digits: 3 + 5 + 2 = 10. Now look at the decimal parts: 0.82 + 0.47 + 0.15. Those are roughly 0.8 + 0.5 + 0.2 = 1.5. Adjusted estimate: 10 + 1.5 = 11.5."

Exact: 3.82 + 5.47 + 2.15 = 11.44. Very close!

**Step 4 — Estimating products and quotients.**

Write: 3 x 4.85

Say: "Round 4.85 to 5. So 3 x 5 = 15. The exact product should be close to 15."
Exact: 3 x 4.85 = 14.55. Close!

Write: 9.3 / 3

Say: "Round 9.3 to 9. So 9 / 3 = 3. The quotient should be about 3."
Exact: 9.3 / 3 = 3.1. Very close!

Say: "Here is the big idea: after you compute an exact answer, always check it against your estimate. If your exact answer is far from your estimate, you probably made an error."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Estimate 7.89 + 4.23, then check the exact answer.
- Estimate: 8 + 4 = 12
- Exact: 7.89 + 4.23 = 12.12
- 12.12 is close to 12. Reasonable.

**Example B:** Estimate 20.5 - 6.78.
- Estimate: 21 - 7 = 14
- Exact: 20.50 - 6.78 = 13.72
- 13.72 is close to 14. Reasonable.

**Example C:** A student computed 6.2 x 4 = 2.48. Is this reasonable?
- Estimate: 6 x 4 = 24.
- 2.48 is nowhere near 24. The student likely misplaced the decimal point. The correct answer is 24.8.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

For each problem, estimate first, then decide if the given "exact answer" is reasonable.

1. Estimate 9.14 + 3.67. Is an exact answer of 12.81 reasonable?
   - Estimate: 9 + 4 = 13. Yes, 12.81 is close to 13. **Reasonable.**

2. Estimate 14.8 - 5.92. Is an exact answer of 8.88 reasonable?
   - Estimate: 15 - 6 = 9. Yes, 8.88 is close to 9. **Reasonable.**

3. Estimate 5 x 3.9. Is an exact answer of 1.95 reasonable?
   - Estimate: 5 x 4 = 20. No, 1.95 is far from 20. **Not reasonable.** (Correct answer: 19.5)

4. Use front-end estimation: 6.45 + 2.31 + 8.09
   - Front: 6 + 2 + 8 = 16. Adjust: 0.45 + 0.31 + 0.09 is about 0.85.
   - **Estimate: about 16.85.** (Exact: 16.85)

5. Estimate 12.4 / 4. Is an exact answer of 3.1 reasonable?
   - Estimate: 12 / 4 = 3. Yes, 3.1 is close to 3. **Reasonable.**

**Facilitator tip:** Emphasize that estimates do not have to be exact. The point is to get in the "neighborhood" of the correct answer so you can spot big errors.

## Teacher Notes

- This lesson develops number sense, which is arguably more important than computational skill. A student who can estimate effectively will catch their own errors throughout the rest of this unit.
- Front-end estimation is less familiar to most students than rounding but is often faster for adding several numbers. Introduce it as an alternative, not a replacement for rounding.
- The "reasonableness check" should become a habit. From this point forward, encourage the student to estimate before computing every problem in this unit.
- Some students may resist estimation because they want the "right" answer. Explain that estimation is the first step toward the right answer -- it tells you what neighborhood the answer lives in.
- This lesson can be completed in one session for most students. If time permits, fold estimation practice into the warm-ups of all subsequent lessons.
- Connection to real life: "When you are at the store, you estimate to make sure you have enough money. You do not need the exact total -- just a good approximation."
