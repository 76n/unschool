**11.** 6 x 345 = 2070. Two decimal places. **20.70 = 20.7**
(2 points)

**12.** 8 x 7 = 56. Two decimal places. **0.56**
(2 points)

**13.** 4 x 9 = 36. Two decimal places (1 + 1). **0.36**

**14.** 25 x 13 = 325. Two decimal places (1 + 1). **3.25**

**15.** On a 10x10 grid (representing 1 whole), shade 3 columns (representing 0.3) and 5 rows (representing 0.5). The overlapping region has 3 x 5 = 15 small squares. Each square is 1 hundredth (0.01). So 15 squares = 0.15. Therefore, 0.3 x 0.5 = 0.15.
(3 points)

**16.** Estimate: 5 x 3 = 15. The answer should be about 15, not 1.30. The student misplaced the decimal point. Correct answer: 5 x 2.6 = 13.0.

### Section 4: Division

**17.** 9.6 / 3 = **3.2**
(2 points)

**18.** 7.04 / 8 = **0.88**
(2 points)

**19.** 6 / 0.5. Multiply both by 10: 60 / 5 = **12**
(2 points)

**20.** 12 / 0.4. Multiply both by 10: 120 / 4 = **30**
(2 points)

**21.** To divide 8 by 0.2:
Step 1: The divisor (0.2) has 1 decimal place, so multiply both numbers by 10.
Step 2: 8 x 10 = 80 and 0.2 x 10 = 2.
Step 3: 80 / 2 = 40.
This works because multiplying both the dividend and divisor by the same number creates an equivalent division -- the quotient does not change. It is like creating an equivalent fraction: 8/0.2 = 80/2.
Answer: 8 / 0.2 = 40.
(3 points)

**22.** 4.56 / 6 = 0.76 meters.
Check: 6 x 0.76 = 4.56. Correct.
(3 points)

(2 points)

**24.** 20 / 2.5. Multiply both by 10: 200 / 25 = **8 days**.
(2 points)

**25.** Total: 3.45 + 2.80 + 4.15 = **10.40 km**. Wednesday vs. Tuesday: 4.15 - 2.80 = **1.35 km** farther on Wednesday.

## Mastery Criteria

A student demonstrates mastery of this unit when they can:

- Add and subtract decimals to hundredths with proper alignment and regrouping
- Multiply decimals (by whole numbers and by other decimals) with correct decimal-point placement
- Divide decimals by whole numbers and divide whole numbers by decimals using appropriate strategies
- Use estimation to check the reasonableness of all four operations
- Solve multi-step word problems requiring two or more decimal operations
- Explain their reasoning and strategies, not just produce correct answers

---

## Partial Credit Guidelines

- **Computation problems (Sections 2-4, Problems 5-8, 11-14, 17-20):** Award 1 point for correct process with an arithmetic error if the method is clearly shown.
- **Place value problems (Section 1):** Award 1 point for a correct expanded form with one minor error (e.g., writing 0.005 as 0.05).
- **Estimation problem (Problem 9):** Award 1 point each for the estimate, exact answer, and comparison, independently. A student can earn the estimate point even with a wrong exact answer.
- **Explanation problems (Problems 15, 16, 21):** Award 1 point for a correct answer even if the explanation is incomplete. Full credit requires both a correct answer and a clear explanation.
- **Word problems (Section 5):** Award partial credit for correct intermediate steps even if the final answer has an error. For multi-step problems, award credit for each correctly completed step.
- **Check problems (Problem 22):** Award 1 point for correct division, 1 for showing the multiplication check, and 1 for units, independently.

## Reteaching Recommendations

### If the student struggles with Section 1 (Place Value):
- Revisit Lesson 1 with base-ten blocks. Rebuild the place-value chart from ones through thousandths.
- Practice the "annex zeros" strategy for comparing decimals until it is automatic.

### If the student struggles with Section 2 (Addition/Subtraction):
- Revisit Lessons 2-3. Use graph paper for every problem to enforce decimal alignment.
- Practice subtraction from whole numbers (e.g., 10 - 3.45) specifically, as this requires chain regrouping.
- Provide additional money-context problems.

### If the student struggles with Section 3 (Multiplication):
- Revisit Lesson 5 (decimal x whole number) using repeated addition as a bridge.
- Revisit Lesson 6 (decimal x decimal) using the 10x10 area model. Do not move to the abstract counting rule until the student can explain why it works using the grid.
- Practice estimating products before computing to catch decimal-placement errors.

### If the student struggles with Section 4 (Division):
- Revisit Lesson 7 (decimal / whole number) with money sharing. Focus on placing the decimal point in the quotient before dividing.
- Revisit Lesson 8 (whole number / decimal) using number lines. Practice the multiply-both-by-10 strategy with single-step problems before advancing to word problems.
- Connect to Unit 3 (fraction division) to reinforce the concept.

### If the student struggles with Section 5 (Problem Solving):
- The issue may be reading comprehension or planning, not computation. Practice the 4-step process (Understand, Plan, Solve, Check) with simpler one-step word problems first.
- Have the student underline the question, circle the given numbers, and write the operation before computing.
- Provide problems broken into explicit sub-steps (as in Lesson 10 scaffolded practice).

---

## Common Patterns to Watch

- **Misaligned addition/subtraction:** Student adds or subtracts without lining up decimal points. Errors in Problems 5-8 suggest reteaching the alignment strategy.
- **Decimal-point error in multiplication:** Student gets the correct digits but places the decimal wrong (e.g., 13.0 instead of 1.30 or vice versa). The counting rule is not understood. Reteach with estimation as a check.
- **Dividing by a decimal without adjusting:** Student divides 6 / 0.5 and gets 0.12 (dividing 6 by 5 and then misplacing the decimal). The multiply-both-by-10 strategy was not applied. Reteach Lesson 8.
- **Correct computation, no units:** Student gives numerical answers without "km," "$," "kg," etc. Reinforce that units are part of a complete answer.
- **Word problem setup errors:** Student identifies the wrong operation or applies operations in the wrong order. Reteach the planning step from Lesson 10.
