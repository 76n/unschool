# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 9

## Topic
Decimal Operations — Mixed Practice

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify which operation (addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division) is needed to solve a given problem.
- Perform all four operations with decimals accurately and efficiently.
- Use estimation to choose among possible answers and verify computed results.
- Explain their reasoning for choosing a particular operation.

## Prerequisites
- Adding and subtracting decimals (Lessons 2-3).
- Multiplying decimals by whole numbers and by decimals (Lessons 5-6).
- Dividing decimals by whole numbers and dividing whole numbers by decimals (Lessons 7-8).
- Estimating with decimals (Lesson 4).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers.
- Graph paper.
- Index cards for exit ticket.
- Operation reference card (optional, for Tier 2 students).

**Standards:** 5.NBT.7

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Rapid review of all four operations with simple decimal problems.

Solve each problem. Name the operation you used.

1. 3.5 + 2.8 = ?
   - **Answer: 6.3** (addition)
2. 7.2 - 4.5 = ?
   - **Answer: 2.7** (subtraction)
3. 4 x 0.6 = ?
   - **Answer: 2.4** (multiplication)
4. 6 / 0.3 = ?
   - **Answer: 20** (division)

Say: "You have now learned all four operations with decimals. Today is about putting them all together. The challenge is not just computing -- it is figuring out which operation to use."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Operation:** Addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division -- the action you perform on numbers.
- **Key words:** Words in a problem that suggest which operation to use (but always think about the context, not just the words!).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Review the four operations and when to use them.**

Write on the board:

| Operation | When to Use It | Example Context |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Addition | Combining amounts, finding a total | "How much altogether?" |
| Subtraction | Finding a difference, how much more/less, what is left | "How much change?" "How much farther?" |
| Multiplication | Equal groups, area, repeated addition | "5 items at $2.30 each" |
| Division | Sharing equally, finding how many groups fit | "Split $12.60 among 4 people" "How many 0.5s in 8?" |

Say: "Before you compute, always ask yourself: what is the problem really asking me to do?"

**Step 2 — Practice identifying the operation.**

Read each problem aloud. Do not solve yet -- just identify the operation.

a) "A box weighs 3.45 kg. Another box weighs 2.8 kg. What is the total weight?"
   - **Addition** (combining two weights)

b) "You have $20.00. You spend $13.75. How much money is left?"
   - **Subtraction** (finding what remains)

c) "Ribbon costs $1.25 per meter. How much does 6 meters cost?"
   - **Multiplication** (equal groups / repeated amount)

d) "A rope is 15 meters long. It is cut into pieces that are 0.5 meters each. How many pieces?"
   - **Division** (how many groups fit)

**Step 3 — Mixed computation practice with estimation.**

Write: 4.5 + 3.28

Say: "Estimate: 5 + 3 = 8. Exact: 4.50 + 3.28 = 7.78. Close to 8? Yes."

Write: 12 - 7.65

Say: "Estimate: 12 - 8 = 4. Exact: 12.00 - 7.65 = 4.35. Close to 4? Yes."

Write: 2.5 x 0.4

Say: "Estimate: 3 x 0.4 is about 1.2. Exact: 25 x 4 = 100, two decimal places, so 1.00 = 1.0. Close to 1.2? Yes."

Write: 7.2 / 0.8

Say: "Think: how many 0.8s fit in 7.2? Multiply both by 10: 72 / 8 = 9. Estimate: 7 / 1 = 7. The answer 9 is reasonable since 0.8 < 1, so the quotient should be larger than 7.2."

**Step 4 — Two-step problems.**

Write: "Sam buys 3 notebooks at $2.49 each and pays with a $10.00 bill. How much change does he receive?"

Say: "Step 1: find the total cost. 3 x $2.49 = $7.47. Step 2: find the change. $10.00 - $7.47 = $2.53."

Say: "Always identify the steps before computing. Ask: what do I need to find first? Then what?"

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Sort and solve.
- 8.5 - 3.72 = ?
  Operation: subtraction. 8.50 - 3.72 = **4.78**

**Example B:** Sort and solve.
- 0.6 x 0.9 = ?
  Operation: multiplication. 6 x 9 = 54, two decimal places. **0.54**

**Example C:** Two-step.
- A store sells apples at $1.20 each. Jan buys 5 apples and 1 bag of oranges for $3.50. What is the total cost?
  Step 1: 5 x $1.20 = $6.00. Step 2: $6.00 + $3.50 = $9.50.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

For each problem, identify the operation, estimate, then solve.

1. 6.3 + 4.75 = ?
   - Operation: addition. Estimate: 6 + 5 = 11.
   - **Answer: 11.05**

2. 10 - 3.68 = ?
   - Operation: subtraction. Estimate: 10 - 4 = 6.
   - **Answer: 6.32**

3. 3.5 x 2.4 = ?
   - Operation: multiplication. Estimate: 4 x 2 = 8.
   - **Answer: 8.40 = 8.4**

4. 14.4 / 0.6 = ?
   - Operation: division. Multiply both by 10: 144 / 6 = 24.
   - **Answer: 24**

5. Maria walks 1.25 km to school and 1.25 km back. She does this 5 days a week. How many km does she walk in a week?
   - Step 1: 1.25 + 1.25 = 2.50 km per day. Step 2: 2.50 x 5 = 12.50 km.
   - **Answer: 12.5 km**

**Facilitator tip:** For the word problem, model the process of reading the entire problem first, then planning the steps before computing.

## Teacher Notes

- This lesson is about synthesis and decision-making. The computation should be largely automatic by now; the challenge is recognizing which operation to apply.
- If a student struggles with a particular operation, that is a signal to revisit the relevant lesson before proceeding. Mixed practice reveals gaps.
- Encourage students to estimate before computing every problem. This lesson provides a perfect opportunity to show the value of estimation as an error-detection tool.
- The two-step problems are critical preparation for Lesson 10 (word problems). Model the planning process: "Read the whole problem. What do I need to find? What information is given? What do I compute first?"
- For Tier 2 students, the operation reference card can be a helpful scaffold. It lists the four operations with their key question ("Am I combining? Separating? Finding equal groups? Sharing?").
- Common error in mixed practice: students default to the most recently learned operation (division, since it was the last topic). Mixing all four operations fights this recency bias.
- The Tier 3 "ordering" problem (comparing 3.5 + 2.1, 3.5 - 2.1, 3.5 x 2.1, 3.5 / 2.1) builds deep number sense about how operations behave differently.
