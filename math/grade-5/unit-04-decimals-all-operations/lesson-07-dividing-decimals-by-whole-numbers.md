# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 7

## Topic
Dividing Decimals by Whole Numbers

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Divide a decimal by a whole number using extended long division.
- Place the decimal point in the quotient directly above the decimal point in the dividend.
- Model decimal division with base-ten blocks and sharing contexts.
- Use estimation and multiplication to check the reasonableness of a quotient.

## Prerequisites
- Multiplying decimals by whole numbers (Lesson 5).
- Long division of whole numbers (Grade 5 prerequisite).
- Decimal place value through hundredths (Lesson 1).

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (flat = 1 whole, rod = 0.1, unit cube = 0.01).
- Play money (dollars, dimes, pennies).
- Whiteboard and markers.
- Graph paper (for long division alignment).
- Index cards for exit ticket.

**Standards:** 5.NBT.7

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review long division and connect to a sharing context.

1. Divide: 84 / 4 = ?
   - **Answer: 21**
2. Four friends share $8.40 equally. Estimate how much each person gets.
   - **Answer: about $2** (8 / 4 = 2)
3. Multiply to check: if 3 x 2.5 = 7.5, then 7.5 / 3 = ?
   - **Answer: 2.5**

Say: "You already know long division with whole numbers. Today we are going to divide decimals by whole numbers. The process is almost identical -- you just need to keep track of the decimal point."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Dividend:** The number being divided (what you start with).
- **Divisor:** The number you divide by.
- **Quotient:** The result of the division.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete model: sharing money.**

Say: "Let's divide $8.64 among 4 people. Use play money."

Lay out: 8 dollar bills, 6 dimes, 4 pennies.

- Share the dollars: 8 / 4 = 2 dollars each.
- Share the dimes: 6 / 4 = 1 dime each, with 2 dimes left over.
- Trade the 2 leftover dimes for 20 pennies. Now we have 20 + 4 = 24 pennies.
- Share the pennies: 24 / 4 = 6 pennies each.

Each person gets: $2.16.

Say: "So 8.64 / 4 = 2.16. Each person gets two dollars and sixteen cents."

**Step 2 — Long division with the decimal point.**

Write the long division:
```
       2.16
    --------
4 ) 8.64
    8
    ---
    0 6
      4
    ---
      24
      24
      --
       0
```

Say: "Here is the most important rule for this lesson: the decimal point in the quotient goes directly above the decimal point in the dividend. Once you place it, divide normally."

Walk through each step:
- 4 goes into 8 two times. Write 2 above the 8.
- 8 - 8 = 0. Bring down the 6. Place the decimal point in the quotient above the decimal in 8.64.
- 4 goes into 6 one time. Write 1 above the 6.
- 6 - 4 = 2. Bring down the 4.
- 4 goes into 24 six times. Write 6 above the 4.
- 24 - 24 = 0. Done.

**Step 3 — When the dividend has fewer decimal places than needed.**

Write: 9.5 / 2

```
       4.75
    --------
2 ) 9.50
    8
    ---
    1 5
    1 4
    ---
      10
      10
      --
       0
```

Say: "9.5 divided by 2. First, I place the decimal in the quotient above the decimal in 9.5. Then I divide: 2 into 9 is 4 (with remainder 1). Bring down the 5: 2 into 15 is 7 (with remainder 1). I need to keep going, so I annex a zero: 2 into 10 is 5. Answer: 4.75."

Estimate check: 9.5 / 2 should be about 10 / 2 = 5. 4.75 is close to 5. Reasonable.

**Step 4 — Zero in the quotient.**

Write: 6.12 / 6

```
       1.02
    --------
6 ) 6.12
    6
    ---
    0 1
    0
    ---
      12
      12
      --
       0
```

Say: "6 into 6 is 1. Bring down the 1. 6 into 1 is 0 -- write 0 in the quotient. Bring down the 2. 6 into 12 is 2. Answer: 1.02."

Say: "Do not forget the zero! Some students write 1.2 instead of 1.02. Check: 6 x 1.02 = 6.12. Correct."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 7.5 / 3
- Divide: 3 into 7 = 2 remainder 1. Bring down 5: 3 into 15 = 5.
- **Answer: 2.5**
- Check: 3 x 2.5 = 7.5.

**Example B:** 15.6 / 4
- 4 into 15 = 3 remainder 3. Bring down 6: 4 into 36 = 9.
- **Answer: 3.9**
- Check: 4 x 3.9 = 15.6.

**Example C:** 2.46 / 6
- 6 into 2 = 0 remainder 2. Bring down 4: 6 into 24 = 4. Bring down 6: 6 into 6 = 1.
- **Answer: 0.41**
- Check: 6 x 0.41 = 2.46.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Solve together using long division. Estimate first.

1. 9.6 / 4 = ?
   - Estimate: 10 / 4 = 2.5
   - **Answer: 2.4**

2. 3.15 / 5 = ?
   - Estimate: 3 / 5 = about 0.6
   - **Answer: 0.63**

3. 14.4 / 6 = ?
   - Estimate: 14 / 6 = about 2.3
   - **Answer: 2.4**

4. $12.75 / 3 = ?
   - Estimate: $12 / 3 = $4
   - **Answer: $4.25**

5. 8.04 / 4 = ?
   - Estimate: 8 / 4 = 2
   - **Answer: 2.01** (Watch for the zero in the quotient!)

**Facilitator tip:** If the student consistently misplaces the decimal, have them write the decimal point in the quotient first (directly above the dividend's decimal) before beginning the division.

## Teacher Notes

- The key procedural skill is placing the decimal point in the quotient. The rule is simple: it goes directly above the decimal point in the dividend. Drill this by having students mark the decimal point in the quotient before they begin dividing.
- Estimation is crucial. If a student computes 8.64 / 4 and gets 21.6 (they divided 864 by 4 = 216 and placed the decimal wrong), the estimate of about $2 immediately reveals the error.
- Sharing money is the most concrete model. Use it for the first several problems.
- Common error: forgetting the zero in quotients like 6.12 / 6 = 1.02. Students write 1.2 because they skip the tenths place. Stress that every place in the dividend must be accounted for in the quotient.
- For students who need more support, have them use graph paper to keep digits aligned. One digit per square, with the decimal point in its own column.
- This lesson pairs naturally with Lesson 5 (multiplying decimals by whole numbers) because multiplication and division are inverse operations. Encourage students to check every division with multiplication.
- Preview: "Next lesson, we will tackle the trickier case: dividing a whole number by a decimal."
