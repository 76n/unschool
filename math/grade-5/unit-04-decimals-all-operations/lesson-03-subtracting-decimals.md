# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 3

## Topic
Subtracting Decimals

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract decimals to hundredths using the standard algorithm with proper decimal-point alignment.
- Annex zeros to create like decimal places before subtracting.
- Regroup (borrow) across the decimal point when necessary.
- Use addition to check subtraction results.

## Prerequisites
- Adding decimals with alignment (Lesson 2).
- Decimal place value through thousandths (Lesson 1).
- Fluent subtraction of multi-digit whole numbers with regrouping.

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (flat = 1 whole, rod = 0.1, unit cube = 0.01).
- Decimal grid paper (10x10 grids).
- Graph paper (for vertical alignment).
- Whiteboard and markers.
- Index cards for exit ticket.

**Standards:** 5.NBT.7

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review decimal addition and whole-number subtraction.

1. Compute: 4.35 + 2.7 = ?
   - **Answer: 7.05**
2. Subtract: 503 - 278 = ?
   - **Answer: 225**
3. True or false: 6.5 - 2.3 = 4.2
   - **Answer: True**

Say: "Subtraction with decimals works just like addition -- the most important step is lining up the decimal points. But we also need to be ready to annex zeros and regroup. Let's learn how."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Regroup (borrow):** When a digit is too small to subtract from, exchange one unit from the next larger place.
- **Annex zeros:** Write zeros at the end of a decimal so both numbers have the same number of decimal places.
- **Difference:** The result of subtraction.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete model.**

Say: "Let's subtract 2.45 - 1.23 using base-ten blocks."

Build 2.45: 2 flats, 4 rods, 5 unit cubes.
Remove 1.23: take away 1 flat, 2 rods, 3 unit cubes.
Remaining: 1 flat, 2 rods, 2 unit cubes = 1.22.

Say: "That was straightforward because every digit in the bottom number was smaller than the corresponding digit in the top number. But what happens when we need to regroup?"

**Step 2 — Subtraction with regrouping.**

Write: 3.42 - 1.67

```
  3.42
- 1.67
------
```

Say: "Start at the hundredths place. We need to subtract 7 from 2. We cannot do that, so we regroup."

- Borrow 1 tenth from the 4 in the tenths place. The 4 becomes 3. The 2 hundredths become 12 hundredths.
- Hundredths: 12 - 7 = 5
- Tenths: 3 - 6. Still cannot subtract. Borrow 1 whole from the 3 in the ones place. The 3 becomes 2. The 3 tenths become 13 tenths.
- Tenths: 13 - 6 = 7
- Ones: 2 - 1 = 1

**Answer: 1.75**

Say: "Check with addition: 1.75 + 1.67 = 3.42. It works!"

**Step 3 — Annexing zeros.**

Write: 5.3 - 2.17

Say: "These numbers have different numbers of decimal places. Annex a zero to 5.3 so it becomes 5.30."

```
  5.30
- 2.17
------
```

- Hundredths: 0 - 7. Cannot subtract. Borrow from the 3. The 3 becomes 2. The 0 becomes 10.
- Hundredths: 10 - 7 = 3
- Tenths: 2 - 1 = 1
- Ones: 5 - 2 = 3

**Answer: 3.13**

Say: "Without the annexed zero, a student might write 5.3 - 2.17 and get confused about what to subtract from what. The zero makes it clear."

**Step 4 — Subtracting from a whole number.**

Write: 8 - 3.46

Say: "8 is a whole number. Write it as 8.00."

```
  8.00
- 3.46
------
```

- Hundredths: 0 - 6. Regroup. Borrow from tenths: tenths is also 0. Borrow from ones: 8 becomes 7, tenths becomes 10, then borrow 1 tenth to hundredths: tenths becomes 9, hundredths becomes 10.
- Hundredths: 10 - 6 = 4
- Tenths: 9 - 4 = 5
- Ones: 7 - 3 = 4

**Answer: 4.54**

Say: "Check: 4.54 + 3.46 = 8.00. Correct."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 6.8 - 4.35
- Annex zero: 6.80 - 4.35
- Hundredths: 0 - 5. Borrow. 8 becomes 7. 0 becomes 10. 10 - 5 = 5.
- Tenths: 7 - 3 = 4.
- Ones: 6 - 4 = 2.
- **Answer: 2.45**

**Example B:** 10 - 6.73
- Write as: 10.00 - 6.73
- Hundredths: 0 - 3. Borrow chain from ones. 10 becomes 9, tenths becomes 10, tenths becomes 9, hundredths becomes 10. Hundredths: 10 - 3 = 7.
- Tenths: 9 - 7 = 2.
- Ones: 9 - 6 = 3.
- **Answer: 3.27**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Solve together. Annex zeros first, then subtract vertically.

1. 7.6 - 3.24 = ?
   - 7.60 - 3.24
   - **Answer: 4.36**

2. 4.05 - 1.8 = ?
   - 4.05 - 1.80
   - **Answer: 2.25**

3. 9 - 5.37 = ?
   - 9.00 - 5.37
   - **Answer: 3.63**

4. 12.4 - 8.56 = ?
   - 12.40 - 8.56
   - **Answer: 3.84**

5. $20.00 - $13.47 = ?
   - **Answer: $6.53**

**Facilitator tip:** If the student makes errors with regrouping across the decimal point, go back to base-ten blocks. Show the "trading" process: exchange 1 rod for 10 unit cubes, or 1 flat for 10 rods.

## Teacher Notes

- Annexing zeros is the single most important strategy in this lesson. Drill it until it becomes automatic: before subtracting, make both numbers have the same number of decimal places.
- Subtracting from a whole number (like 8 - 3.46) requires chain regrouping and is the hardest skill in this lesson. Use base-ten blocks to make the borrowing visible.
- The check-with-addition habit is valuable and should be encouraged throughout this unit. It catches errors and reinforces the inverse relationship between addition and subtraction.
- Common error: students who write 5.3 - 2.17 = 3.23 (subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in each column without regard to borrowing). This indicates they need more work with regrouping in the whole-number context first.
- Connect to money: "You have $5.30 and spend $2.17. How much is left?" This grounds the abstract computation in a real scenario.
- If the student completed Lesson 2 quickly, Lessons 2 and 3 can be combined into a single long session focused on both addition and subtraction.
