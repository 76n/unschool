# Facilitator Guide — Grade 5 Math, Unit 3: Dividing Fractions

**Grade:** 5
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 3 — Dividing Fractions
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (facilitator leads instruction; student works independently for practice)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit introduces division involving unit fractions. Students explore two types of problems: dividing a whole number by a unit fraction (e.g., 4 / 1/3) and dividing a unit fraction by a whole number (e.g., 1/3 / 5). Through concrete and pictorial models -- fraction strips, number lines, and partitioning diagrams -- students build conceptual understanding before developing computational procedures. The unit emphasizes real-world contexts and reasoning about the size of quotients.

This unit builds directly on Unit 1 (Adding and Subtracting Fractions) and Unit 2 (Multiplying Fractions). The relationship between multiplication and division is a central theme: division "undoes" multiplication, and students use this connection to check their work and reason about answers.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Divide a whole number by a unit fraction using visual models and the standard procedure (e.g., 6 / 1/4 = 24)
- Divide a unit fraction by a whole number using visual models and the standard procedure (e.g., 1/4 / 6 = 1/24)
- Explain why dividing by a unit fraction produces a larger number and dividing a unit fraction by a whole number produces a smaller number
- Distinguish between the two types of problems and choose the appropriate strategy
- Solve real-world word problems involving division of unit fractions
- Use estimation and the relationship between multiplication and division to check the reasonableness of answers

**Why this matters:** Fraction division is the foundation for rates, ratios, and proportional reasoning in middle school. Understanding "how many 1/4-cup servings are in 3 cups?" prepares students for unit rate problems like "miles per hour" and for algebraic work with rational numbers.

## Lesson Summaries and Facilitation Notes

### Lesson 1: Meaning of Fraction Division
**Goal:** Connect fraction division to the two meanings of whole-number division (sharing and measurement) and build intuition for what happens when fractions are involved.
**Key moves:** Start with familiar whole-number division (12 / 3 as "how many groups of 3 in 12?" and "12 shared among 3"). Show that the same two interpretations apply when fractions appear. Use physical fraction strips to explore "How many 1/4s are in 2?" and "1/3 shared among 4 people."
**Watch for:** Students who think dividing always makes a number smaller. This is only true for division by numbers greater than 1. Dividing by a fraction less than 1 actually makes the number larger.

### Lesson 2: Whole Number / Unit Fraction — Visual Models
**Goal:** Use fraction strips and number lines to divide whole numbers by unit fractions.
**Key moves:** Model 3 / 1/4 on a number line: "How many jumps of 1/4 fit into 3?" Count the jumps (12). Use fraction strips: lay out 3 whole strips, then see how many 1/4 pieces cover them. Connect the visual to the equation. Repeat with several examples.
**Watch for:** Students who confuse 3 / 1/4 with 3 x 1/4. Emphasize the question: "How many 1/4s fit in 3?" (division) vs. "What is 1/4 of 3?" (multiplication).

### Lesson 3: Whole Number / Unit Fraction — Procedure
**Goal:** Develop the rule: whole number / 1/n = whole number x n.
**Key moves:** After multiple visual examples, guide students to see the pattern: 2 / 1/3 = 6, 2 / 1/4 = 8, 2 / 1/5 = 10. "What do you notice?" The answer is always the whole number times the denominator. State the rule: to divide by 1/n, multiply by n. Connect to the "invert and multiply" idea conceptually.
**Watch for:** Students applying the rule mechanically without understanding. Continue to ask "Does this make sense?" and verify with a quick model.

### Lesson 4: Whole Number / Unit Fraction — Word Problems
**Goal:** Solve real-world measurement division problems involving whole number / unit fraction.
**Key moves:** Teach students to identify the structure: "How many [unit fraction]-sized pieces fit into [whole number]?" Examples include recipes, lengths of ribbon, servings. Model drawing a diagram for each problem before computing. Emphasize labeling the answer with appropriate units.
**Watch for:** Students who set up the division in the wrong order (writing 1/4 / 3 instead of 3 / 1/4). Have students restate the question in their own words first.

### Lesson 5: Unit Fraction / Whole Number — Visual Models
**Goal:** Use partitioning diagrams and fraction strips to divide a unit fraction by a whole number.
**Key moves:** Model 1/2 / 3: "Start with 1/2. Share it equally among 3 people. Each person gets 1/6." Draw a rectangle, shade 1/2, then divide the shaded part into 3 equal pieces. Each piece is 1/6 of the whole. Repeat with 1/3 / 2, 1/4 / 5, etc.
**Watch for:** Students confusing this with multiplying (1/2 x 3 = 3/2). Stress that dividing means sharing, so the result must be smaller than what you started with.

### Lesson 6: Unit Fraction / Whole Number — Procedure
**Goal:** Develop the rule: 1/n / whole number = 1/(n x whole number).
**Key moves:** Build from visual examples. 1/2 / 3 = 1/6, 1/3 / 4 = 1/12, 1/5 / 2 = 1/10. "What pattern do you see?" The denominator is the product of the original denominator and the whole number. Connect to invert and multiply: 1/n / w = 1/n x 1/w = 1/(n x w).
**Watch for:** Students who multiply the numerator by the whole number instead of the denominator. Anchor back to the visual model: "We are making the pieces smaller, so the denominator gets bigger."

### Lesson 7: Unit Fraction / Whole Number — Word Problems
**Goal:** Solve real-world sharing problems involving unit fraction / whole number.
**Key moves:** Teach students to identify the structure: "[unit fraction] shared equally among [whole number] people." Examples: sharing a fraction of a pizza, dividing a fraction of a mile into equal sections. Draw diagrams first, then compute.
**Watch for:** Students who confuse "1/3 divided by 4" with "4 divided by 1/3." The two give very different answers. Ask: "Is the answer supposed to be bigger or smaller than 1/3?"

### Lesson 8: Mixed Practice
**Goal:** Distinguish between whole number / unit fraction and unit fraction / whole number, and choose the correct approach.
**Key moves:** Present problems without context clues and ask: "Which type is this?" Sort problems into two categories. Then solve. Include problems where students must write the equation from a word problem and determine the type.
**Watch for:** Students who apply the same procedure to both types. Use side-by-side comparison: 6 / 1/3 = 18 vs. 1/3 / 6 = 1/18. "These look similar but the answers are very different!"

### Lesson 9: Reasoning About Fraction Division
**Goal:** Estimate quotients, check reasonableness, and explore the relationship between multiplication and division of fractions.
**Key moves:** Present division problems and ask students to estimate before computing. Use the multiplication-division connection: if 6 / 1/3 = 18, then 18 x 1/3 = 6. Practice "checking" division with multiplication. Discuss why dividing by a number less than 1 gives a result larger than the dividend.
**Watch for:** Students who rely purely on procedures without number sense. Push for explanations: "Why does it make sense that 4 / 1/5 = 20?"

### Lesson 10: Review and Problem Solving
**Goal:** Consolidate all skills with comprehensive review and multi-step problems.
**Key moves:** Start with a quick-sort activity (classify and solve problems of both types). Then move to multi-step problems that combine fraction division with other operations. Review common errors before the assessment.
**Watch for:** Students who have mastered procedures but struggle with word problems. Provide extra diagram practice for these students.

## Differentiation Strategies

### Struggling Learners
- Continue using fraction strips and number line models alongside the procedure through Lessons 3, 6, and 8
- Provide a two-column reference card: "Whole number / unit fraction: multiply the whole number by the denominator" and "Unit fraction / whole number: multiply the denominators"
- Break word problems into explicit steps: (1) identify the numbers, (2) determine which type of division, (3) draw a model, (4) compute, (5) check with multiplication
- Allow extended time on Lessons 2 and 5 (visual models) before introducing procedures

### Advanced Learners
- Explore division with non-unit fractions as a preview of Grade 6 content (e.g., 3 / 2/5)
- Create their own word problems for each type and trade with a partner
- Investigate: "Is division of fractions commutative? Why or why not?" (Compare a / b vs. b / a)
- Multi-step challenge problems combining fraction division with multiplication and addition

## Common Errors and How to Address Them

| Error | Likely Cause | Remediation |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Confusing 6 / 1/3 with 6 x 1/3 | Mixing up division and multiplication | Side-by-side: "How many 1/3s in 6?" (18) vs. "What is 1/3 of 6?" (2). Use number line for division. |
| Writing 1/3 / 6 = 6/3 = 2 | Inverting the wrong number | Return to the sharing model: "1/3 shared among 6 must be less than 1/3." The answer should be tiny. |
| Getting the division order backwards from a word problem | Not identifying dividend vs. divisor | Teach the question: "What are we starting with? What are we dividing it by?" The starting amount is the dividend. |
| Saying 4 / 1/2 = 2 (dividing 4 by 2 instead of by 1/2) | Treating the fraction as its denominator | Ask: "How many halves are in 1? (2.) So how many halves are in 4? (8.)" Count on the number line. |
| Forgetting to simplify or giving non-unit fraction answers for Type 2 | Arithmetic carelessness | Encourage checking: "Multiply your answer by the divisor. Do you get back to the dividend?" |

## Assessment Guidance

The **Unit Assessment** covers all major skills. Administer after Lesson 10.

**Mastery criteria (80% or above = 40/50 points):**
- Uses visual models to represent both types of fraction division
- Correctly divides a whole number by a unit fraction using the standard procedure
- Correctly divides a unit fraction by a whole number using the standard procedure
- Distinguishes between the two types and selects the appropriate method
- Solves at least one real-world word problem involving fraction division
- Checks answers using the relationship between multiplication and division

**If below mastery:** Reteach Lessons 2-3 (whole number / unit fraction with visual models) and Lessons 5-6 (unit fraction / whole number with visual models) with additional concrete support. Use the mixed practice from Lesson 8 to rebuild the ability to distinguish the two types before reassessing.
