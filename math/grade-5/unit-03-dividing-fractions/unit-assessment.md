## Answer Key

### Section 1: Conceptual Understanding

**1.** Number line from 0 to 3, with tick marks at every 1/4 (0, 1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 1, 5/4, 6/4, 7/4, 2, 9/4, 10/4, 11/4, 3). Counting the jumps: 12 jumps of 1/4 fit into 3.
**Answer: 3 / 1/4 = 12**
(3 points)

**2.** Rectangle divided into 3 equal rows. Top row shaded to represent 1/3. A vertical line divides the rectangle into 2 columns, splitting the shaded row into 2 equal parts. Total parts in rectangle: 3 x 2 = 6. Each small part is 1/6 of the whole.
**Answer: 1/3 / 2 = 1/6**
(3 points)

**3.** Sample answer: "4 / 1/2 = 8 because the question is asking how many halves fit into 4. Each whole has 2 halves, so 4 wholes have 4 x 2 = 8 halves."
(2 points: 1 for answer of 8, 1 for explanation mentioning halves and how they fit into 4)

**4.** Sample answer: "When you divide 1/4 by 3, you are sharing 1/4 equally among 3 people. Since you are splitting an already small amount into more pieces, each piece must be smaller than 1/4. The answer is 1/12, which is less than 1/4."
(2 points: 1 for recognizing the answer is smaller, 1 for explaining sharing/splitting makes pieces smaller)

### Section 4: Mixed Practice and Reasoning

**17.**
a) 4 / 1/5 = 4 x 5 = **20**. Type: **Type 1** (whole number / unit fraction).
b) 1/6 / 7 = 1/(6 x 7) = **1/42**. Type: **Type 2** (unit fraction / whole number).
(4 points: 1 for each classification, 1 for each answer)

**18.**
a) **10 / 1/3** is larger. Explanation: 10 / 1/3 asks "how many thirds fit in 10?" which gives a large number (30). Meanwhile, 1/3 / 10 asks "1/3 shared among 10" which gives a tiny fraction (1/30). 30 is much larger than 1/30.
b) **1/4 / 2** is larger. (1/2 / 4 = 1/8 and 1/4 / 2 = 1/8. They are actually equal.) Accept either answer since both equal 1/8. If a student says they are equal, award full credit.
(4 points: 1 for correct choice in a, 2 for explanation in a, 1 for correct choice in b. For 18b, accept "equal" or either choice with explanation that both are 1/8.)

### Section 5: Word Problems

**19.** Equation: 6 / 1/3 = ?
6 x 3 = 18.
**Answer: 18 pieces**
(2 points)

**20.** Equation: 1/4 / 3 = ?
1/(4 x 3) = 1/12.
**Answer: 1/12 of a gallon**
(2 points)

**21.** Step 1: 5 / 1/4 = 5 x 4 = 20 pieces.
Step 2: 20 / 3 = 6 remainder 2.
**Answer: 6 complete bookmarks** (with 2 pieces left over).
(2 points: 1 for 20 pieces, 1 for 6 bookmarks)

**22.** Step 1: Each friend gets 1/2 / 3 = 1/(2 x 3) = 1/6 of the pan.
Step 2: Each sibling gets half of 1/6: 1/6 / 2 = 1/(6 x 2) = 1/12 of the pan.
**Answer: 1/12 of the pan**
(2 points: 1 for 1/6 per friend, 1 for 1/12 per sibling)

## Scoring Guide

| Score | Level | Action |
|-------|-------|--------|
| 40-50 | Mastery | Proceed to next unit |
| 30-39 | Approaching | Reteach weak areas, then reassess those sections |
| Below 30 | Below expectations | Reteach Lessons 2-3 and 5-6 with visual model support, then Lesson 8 for mixed practice |

### Partial Credit Guidelines

- **Computation problems (Sections 2-3):** Award 1 point for correct process with an arithmetic error if the method is clearly shown. Deduct 0.5 points for a correct numerical answer with no work shown.
- **Visual model problems (Section 1, Problems 1-2):** Award partial credit for a correct diagram even if the written answer has an arithmetic error. Award 1 point for a correct answer with an incomplete or unclear diagram.
- **Explanation problems (Section 1, Problems 3-4):** Award 1 point for a correct answer even if the explanation is incomplete. Full credit requires both a correct answer and a clear, mathematically accurate explanation.
- **Classification problems (Section 4):** Award credit independently for correct classification and correct computation. A student can earn the classification point even if the computation is wrong, and vice versa.
- **Word problems (Section 5):** Award 1 point for a correct equation setup even if the final answer has a computation error. For multi-step problems, award partial credit for correctly completing the first step.
- **Verification problems (Problems 10, 16):** Award 1 point for setting up the multiplication check correctly, even if there is an arithmetic error.

### Common Patterns to Watch

- **Errors in Section 2 (whole number / unit fraction):** Student may be computing whole number x unit fraction instead of dividing. For example, writing 6 / 1/3 = 6/3 = 2 instead of 6 x 3 = 18. Reteach Lessons 2-3 with number line models showing how many fractional pieces fit into the whole number.
- **Errors in Section 3 (unit fraction / whole number):** Student may be multiplying the numerator by the whole number (e.g., 1/4 / 6 = 6/4) instead of multiplying the denominator (1/24). Reteach Lessons 5-6 with partitioning diagrams showing that sharing makes pieces smaller.
- **Errors in Section 4 (mixed practice):** Student applies the same procedure to both types, indicating they cannot distinguish between them. Reteach Lesson 8 with side-by-side comparisons and sorting activities.
- **Errors in Section 5 (word problems):** Student sets up the equation with the numbers reversed (e.g., writing 1/3 / 6 instead of 6 / 1/3). Reteach the problem-solving steps from Lessons 4 and 7: identify the starting amount (dividend) and what you are dividing by (divisor).
- **Not checking answers:** If students get wrong answers that they could have caught with a multiplication check, emphasize the checking habit from Lesson 9.
