# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 3, Lesson 9

## Topic
Reasoning About Fraction Division

## Learning Objectives
- Estimate quotients involving fractions before computing.
- Use the relationship between multiplication and division to check answers.
- Explain why dividing by a unit fraction produces a result larger than the dividend.
- Explain why dividing a unit fraction by a whole number produces a result smaller than the dividend.

## Prerequisites
- Fluency with both types of fraction division (Lessons 2-8).
- Multiplying fractions by whole numbers (Unit 2).
- Understanding of inverse operations.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Paper and pencil.
- Index cards or half-sheets for exit ticket.

**Standards:** 5.NF.7 — Apply and extend previous understandings of division to divide unit fractions by whole numbers and whole numbers by unit fractions.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate estimation skills and build number sense.

1. Write on the board: "Without computing, decide if each answer is greater than 10 or less than 10."
   - 8 / 1/2 (Greater — there are many halves in 8.)
   - 1/4 / 3 (Less — sharing 1/4 among 3 gives a tiny amount.)
   - 3 / 1/4 (Greater — there are many fourths in 3.)
   - 1/6 / 2 (Less — sharing 1/6 between 2 gives a tiny amount.)
2. Have students share their reasoning for each.
3. Say: "Today is all about reasoning. We want to make sense of our answers, not just compute. A good mathematician always checks: does my answer make sense?"

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Estimate:** a reasonable guess about the size of an answer, made before computing.
- **Reasonableness:** whether an answer makes sense given the problem.
- **Inverse relationship:** multiplication and division undo each other; if a / b = c, then c x b = a.
- **Number sense:** the ability to understand the size and relationships between numbers.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Estimating before computing.**

Say: "Before you solve any fraction division problem, you should be able to predict whether the answer will be big or small, and roughly how big."

Write on the board: 7 / 1/3 = ?

Say: "Let's estimate. How many thirds are in 1? (3.) How many thirds are in 7? It should be about 7 x 3 = 21. Let's compute: 7 x 3 = 21. Our estimate matches exactly!"

Write: 1/5 / 4 = ?

Say: "Let's estimate. We start with 1/5, which is a small number (about 0.2). We share it among 4. The answer should be very small -- about 0.05. Let's compute: 1/(5 x 4) = 1/20 = 0.05. Our estimate is confirmed."

**Step 2 — The multiplication-division connection.**

Say: "Multiplication and division are inverse operations. They undo each other. We have been using this to check our work, but let's look at it more carefully."

Write on the board:

If 6 / 1/3 = 18, then:
- 18 x 1/3 = 6 (the quotient times the divisor equals the dividend)
- 6 / 18 = 1/3 (the dividend divided by the quotient equals the divisor)

Say: "These three facts form a fact family, just like with whole numbers. If you know one, you can find the others."

Practice with another set:
If 1/4 / 5 = 1/20, then:
- 1/20 x 5 = 5/20 = 1/4 (quotient x divisor = dividend)
- 1/4 / 1/20 = 5 (dividend / quotient = divisor -- this one is harder, but it works!)

**Step 3 — Why does dividing by a fraction less than 1 give a bigger answer?**

Say: "This is one of the most confusing ideas in math. Many people think division always makes things smaller. But that is only true when you divide by a number greater than 1."

Write on the board:
- 12 / 4 = 3 (dividing by 4 makes it smaller)
- 12 / 2 = 6 (dividing by 2, still smaller, but not as much)
- 12 / 1 = 12 (dividing by 1, stays the same)
- 12 / 1/2 = 24 (dividing by 1/2, gets BIGGER!)
- 12 / 1/4 = 48 (dividing by 1/4, gets even BIGGER!)

Say: "See the pattern? As the divisor gets smaller, the quotient gets bigger. Dividing by 1/2 means 'how many halves fit in 12?' -- and the answer is 24 because halves are small, so you need a lot of them."

**Step 4 — Why does dividing a fraction by a whole number give a smaller answer?**

Say: "This one is more intuitive. If you have a small amount and share it among many people, each person gets very little."

Write on the board:
- 1/2 / 1 = 1/2 (sharing among 1, stays the same)
- 1/2 / 2 = 1/4 (sharing among 2, gets smaller)
- 1/2 / 4 = 1/8 (sharing among 4, gets even smaller)
- 1/2 / 10 = 1/20 (sharing among 10, very small)

Say: "As the number of people increases, each person's share gets smaller. This makes complete sense."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** "Is 9 / 1/4 greater than or less than 30? Explain without computing exactly."
Reasoning: 9 / 1/4 means "how many fourths in 9." There are 4 fourths in each whole. So there are about 9 x 4 = 36 fourths. 36 > 30.
**Answer: Greater than 30** (exact answer: 36)

**Example B:** "Sam says 1/3 / 8 = 8/3. Is his answer reasonable?"
Reasoning: We are sharing 1/3 (a small amount, less than 1) among 8 people. Each person should get a very small amount -- much less than 1/3. Sam's answer 8/3 = 2 2/3 is bigger than 1. That is not reasonable.
**Answer: Not reasonable. The correct answer is 1/3 / 8 = 1/24.**

**Example C:** "Check this division using multiplication: 5 / 1/6 = 30."
Check: 30 x 1/6 = 30/6 = 5. Since we get back to the dividend, the answer is correct.
Also: 5 / 30 = 5/30 = 1/6. This gives back the divisor. Confirmed.
**Answer: The answer 30 is correct.**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Without computing, is 4 / 1/8 greater than 20? Explain.
   - Yes. 4 x 8 = 32, and 32 > 20. There are 8 eighths per whole, so 4 wholes have 32 eighths.
   - **Answer: Yes, it equals 32.**

2. Without computing, is 1/5 / 6 greater than 1/5? Explain.
   - No. We are sharing 1/5 among 6 people. Each person gets less than 1/5.
   - **Answer: No, it equals 1/30, which is less than 1/5.**

3. Check using multiplication: 8 / 1/5 = 40. Is this correct?
   - Check: 40 x 1/5 = 40/5 = 8. Correct.
   - **Answer: Yes, confirmed.**

4. Check using multiplication: 1/4 / 3 = 1/12. Is this correct?
   - Check: 1/12 x 3 = 3/12 = 1/4. Correct.
   - **Answer: Yes, confirmed.**

5. Jamal says 6 / 1/2 = 3. Is his answer reasonable? What mistake did he make?
   - Not reasonable. 6 / 1/2 should be bigger than 6 (dividing by a number less than 1). Jamal computed 6 x 1/2 = 3 instead of 6 / 1/2 = 12. He multiplied by 1/2 instead of dividing by 1/2.
   - **Answer: Not reasonable. Correct answer is 12. Jamal multiplied instead of dividing.**

**Facilitator tip:** Encourage students to state their reasoning before computing. The goal of this lesson is number sense, not just procedure.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Estimate First, Then Compute
For each problem, first estimate (greater or less than the given benchmark), then compute to verify.

1. Is 7 / 1/5 greater or less than 30? Compute to check.
2. Is 1/3 / 9 greater or less than 1/10? Compute to check.
3. Is 10 / 1/3 greater or less than 25? Compute to check.
4. Is 1/8 / 4 greater or less than 1/20? Compute to check.
5. Is 6 / 1/4 greater or less than 20? Compute to check.

### Part B: Check with Multiplication
Verify each answer using multiplication. Write "correct" or "incorrect" and give the right answer if incorrect.

6. 4 / 1/3 = 12
7. 1/5 / 2 = 2/5
8. 9 / 1/2 = 18
9. 1/6 / 4 = 1/24
10. 3 / 1/10 = 1/30

### Part C: Reasoning and Explanation
11. Explain in your own words why 5 / 1/4 = 20 makes sense. Use the idea of "how many fourths."
12. Marcus computed 1/2 / 6 and got 3. Without computing, explain why 3 cannot be the correct answer. What is the correct answer?
13. Write a fact family for: 8 / 1/3 = 24. (Write three related equations.)

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. Greater than 30. Compute: 7 x 5 = 35. Since 35 > 30, the estimate is correct.
2. Less than 1/10. Compute: 1/(3 x 9) = 1/27. Since 1/27 < 1/10 (27 > 10 so the fraction is smaller), the estimate is correct.
3. Greater than 25. Compute: 10 x 3 = 30. Since 30 > 25, the estimate is correct.
4. Less than 1/20. Compute: 1/(8 x 4) = 1/32. Since 1/32 < 1/20 (32 > 20), the estimate is correct.
5. Greater than 20. Compute: 6 x 4 = 24. Since 24 > 20, the estimate is correct.

**Part B**
6. Correct. Check: 12 x 1/3 = 12/3 = 4.
7. Incorrect. Check: 2/5 x 2 = 4/5, but it should equal 1/5. The correct answer is 1/5 / 2 = 1/10. Check: 1/10 x 2 = 2/10 = 1/5.
8. Correct. Check: 18 x 1/2 = 9.
9. Correct. Check: 1/24 x 4 = 4/24 = 1/6.
10. Incorrect. 3 / 1/10 should be large (Type 1). Check: 1/30 x 1/10 does not equal 3. The correct answer is 3 x 10 = 30. Check: 30 x 1/10 = 3.

**Part C**
11. 5 / 1/4 = 20 because the question is "how many fourths are in 5?" Each whole has 4 fourths. So 5 wholes have 5 x 4 = 20 fourths. Since fourths are small pieces, it takes many of them to fill 5 wholes, which is why the answer is larger than 5.
12. The answer 3 cannot be correct because we are sharing 1/2 (which is less than 1) among 6 people. Each person should get much less than 1/2, so the answer must be a small fraction less than 1. An answer of 3 is way too large. The correct answer is 1/2 / 6 = 1/12.
13. Fact family for 8 / 1/3 = 24:
    - 8 / 1/3 = 24
    - 24 x 1/3 = 8
    - 8 / 24 = 1/3

## Assessment Check

Solve 4 / 1/6 = ? WITHOUT using the procedure. Instead, explain your reasoning in at least two sentences. Then verify your answer using multiplication.

**Target response:** There are 6 sixths in every whole. So in 4 wholes, there are 4 x 6 = 24 sixths. Therefore 4 / 1/6 = 24. Verification: 24 x 1/6 = 24/6 = 4. The answer checks out. The answer 24 is bigger than 4, which makes sense because we are dividing by a small number (1/6).

## Extension

- **Divisor Investigation:** What happens to the quotient of 12 / 1/n as n gets larger? Compute 12 / 1/2, 12 / 1/3, 12 / 1/4, 12 / 1/6, and 12 / 1/12. Describe the pattern and explain why it happens. (Answers: 24, 36, 48, 72, 144. As n increases, the unit fraction gets smaller, and more of them fit in 12.)
- **True or False Challenge:** Decide if each statement is true or false, and explain.
  - "Dividing a number by 1/2 gives the same result as multiplying by 2." (True: a / 1/2 = a x 2.)
  - "Dividing a number by 1/3 gives the same result as multiplying by 1/3." (False: a / 1/3 = a x 3, not a x 1/3.)
  - "1/4 / 5 is the same as 1/4 x 1/5." (True: both equal 1/20.)
