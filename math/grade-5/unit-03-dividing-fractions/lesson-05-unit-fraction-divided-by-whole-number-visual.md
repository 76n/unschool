# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 3, Lesson 5

## Topic
Unit Fraction / Whole Number: Visual Models

## Learning Objectives
- Use partitioning diagrams to model dividing a unit fraction by a whole number.
- Use fraction strips to model dividing a unit fraction by a whole number.
- Interpret the quotient as "a unit fraction shared equally among a whole number of groups."
- Explain why the quotient is smaller than the original unit fraction.

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of division as sharing: "Divide equally among groups" (Lesson 1).
- Dividing a whole number by a unit fraction using visual models (Lesson 2).
- Understanding that unit fractions have a numerator of 1.

## Materials Needed
- Fraction strips (halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, eighths, tenths, twelfths).
- Grid paper (for drawing partitioning diagrams).
- Colored pencils or markers (at least 2 colors).
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet for Independent Practice.

**Standards:** 5.NF.7b — Interpret division of a unit fraction by a non-zero whole number, and compute such quotients.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate the sharing meaning of division and preview fraction sharing.

1. Write on the board: "12 / 4 = ?"
2. Ask: "If 12 cookies are shared equally among 4 friends, how many does each friend get?" (3 cookies.)
3. Now write: "1/2 / 4 = ?"
4. Ask: "If half a cookie is shared equally among 4 friends, how much does each friend get? Will the answer be bigger or smaller than 1/2?" (Smaller, because we are sharing a small amount among several people.)
5. Say: "Today we will use pictures and fraction strips to find exact answers when we divide a unit fraction by a whole number. This is the second type of fraction division we are studying."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Partitioning diagram:** a drawing where a shape is divided into equal parts, some are shaded, and then the shaded part is further divided.
- **Sharing division:** dividing an amount equally among a number of groups.
- **Unit fraction:** a fraction with a numerator of 1.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Partitioning diagram for 1/2 / 3.**

Draw a rectangle on the board. Divide it into 2 equal parts (halves) with a horizontal line. Shade the top half.

Say: "This shaded region represents 1/2. Now we want to divide 1/2 by 3. That means: share this half equally among 3 people."

Draw 2 vertical lines through the entire rectangle, creating 3 columns. The shaded half is now divided into 3 equal pieces.

Say: "Each person gets one of these 3 small pieces from the shaded half. But how big is each piece compared to the whole rectangle?"

Count the total number of equal pieces in the entire rectangle: 2 rows x 3 columns = 6 pieces. Each piece is 1/6 of the whole.

Say: "So 1/2 / 3 = 1/6. Each person gets 1/6 of the whole."

Write on the board: 1/2 / 3 = 1/6.

```
+-------+-------+-------+
|///////|///////|///////|  <-- 1/2 shaded, split into 3 parts
+-------+-------+-------+     Each shaded part = 1/6 of the whole
|       |       |       |
+-------+-------+-------+
```

**Step 2 — Fraction strip model for 1/2 / 3.**

Say: "Let's confirm with fraction strips."

Lay out a 1/2 strip. Now find fraction strips where 3 pieces exactly cover the 1/2 strip. The answer is 1/6 strips: three 1/6 strips equal 3/6 = 1/2.

Say: "Each 1/6 strip is one share. So 1/2 / 3 = 1/6. The fraction strip model matches our diagram."

**Step 3 — Partitioning diagram for 1/3 / 2.**

Draw a rectangle. Divide it into 3 equal rows. Shade the top row (1/3).

Say: "We want to share 1/3 equally between 2 people."

Draw 1 vertical line through the rectangle, making 2 columns. The shaded row is now split into 2 pieces.

Total pieces in the rectangle: 3 rows x 2 columns = 6. Each piece is 1/6.

Say: "So 1/3 / 2 = 1/6. Each person gets 1/6 of the whole."

```
+----------+----------+
|//////////|//////////|  <-- 1/3 shaded, split into 2 parts
+----------+----------+     Each shaded part = 1/6 of the whole
|          |          |
+----------+----------+
|          |          |
+----------+----------+
```

**Step 4 — Partitioning diagram for 1/4 / 5.**

Draw a rectangle. Divide it into 4 equal rows. Shade the top row (1/4).

Say: "We want to share 1/4 equally among 5 people."

Draw 4 vertical lines, making 5 columns. The shaded row is split into 5 pieces.

Total pieces: 4 rows x 5 columns = 20. Each piece is 1/20.

Say: "So 1/4 / 5 = 1/20."

**Step 5 — Observe the pattern.**

Write on the board:
- 1/2 / 3 = 1/6 (note: 2 x 3 = 6)
- 1/3 / 2 = 1/6 (note: 3 x 2 = 6)
- 1/4 / 5 = 1/20 (note: 4 x 5 = 20)

Say: "Do you see a pattern? When we divide 1/n by a whole number w, the answer is 1/(n x w). The denominator gets multiplied by the whole number. This makes sense because sharing makes pieces smaller, and smaller pieces mean a larger denominator. We will formalize this rule in Lesson 6."

### Worked Examples

**Example A: 1/3 / 4**
Draw a rectangle divided into 3 rows. Shade 1 row (1/3). Draw 3 vertical lines to make 4 columns.
Total pieces: 3 x 4 = 12. Each piece is 1/12.
The shaded row has 4 small pieces. Each person gets 1 piece = 1/12.
**Answer: 1/3 / 4 = 1/12**

**Example B: 1/4 / 2**
Draw a rectangle divided into 4 rows. Shade 1 row (1/4). Draw 1 vertical line to make 2 columns.
Total pieces: 4 x 2 = 8. Each piece is 1/8.
The shaded row has 2 small pieces. Each person gets 1 piece = 1/8.
**Answer: 1/4 / 2 = 1/8**

**Example C: 1/5 / 3**
Draw a rectangle divided into 5 rows. Shade 1 row (1/5). Draw 2 vertical lines to make 3 columns.
Total pieces: 5 x 3 = 15. Each piece is 1/15.
The shaded row has 3 small pieces. Each person gets 1 piece = 1/15.
**Answer: 1/5 / 3 = 1/15**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

For each problem, draw a partitioning diagram and solve.

1. 1/2 / 4 = ?
   - Rectangle divided into 2 rows, 4 columns. Total: 8 pieces. Shaded row has 4 pieces. Each person gets 1/8.
   - **Answer: 1/8**

2. 1/6 / 2 = ?
   - Rectangle divided into 6 rows, 2 columns. Total: 12 pieces. Shaded row has 2 pieces. Each person gets 1/12.
   - **Answer: 1/12**

3. 1/3 / 5 = ?
   - Rectangle divided into 3 rows, 5 columns. Total: 15 pieces. Shaded row has 5 pieces. Each person gets 1/15.
   - **Answer: 1/15**

4. 1/5 / 2 = ?
   - Rectangle divided into 5 rows, 2 columns. Total: 10 pieces. Shaded row has 2 pieces. Each person gets 1/10.
   - **Answer: 1/10**

5. 1/4 / 3 = ?
   - Rectangle divided into 4 rows, 3 columns. Total: 12 pieces. Shaded row has 3 pieces. Each person gets 1/12.
   - **Answer: 1/12**

**Facilitator tip:** Walk around and check that students are drawing the correct number of rows (based on the unit fraction's denominator) and columns (based on the whole number divisor). A common error is confusing which number determines the rows and which determines the columns.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Partitioning Diagrams
Draw a diagram for each problem and solve.

1. 1/2 / 5 = ?
2. 1/3 / 3 = ?
3. 1/6 / 4 = ?
4. 1/4 / 4 = ?
5. 1/8 / 2 = ?
6. 1/5 / 4 = ?

### Part B: Fraction Strip Models
Use fraction strips (or draw them) to solve.

7. 1/2 / 2 = ? (Find a fraction strip where 2 pieces exactly cover a 1/2 strip.)
8. 1/3 / 2 = ? (Find a fraction strip where 2 pieces exactly cover a 1/3 strip.)
9. 1/4 / 3 = ? (Find a fraction strip where 3 pieces exactly cover a 1/4 strip.)

### Part C: Word Problems
Write the division equation, draw a diagram, and solve.

10. Leo has 1/3 of a pizza left. He shares it equally with his brother. How much of the whole pizza does each person get?
11. A piece of ribbon is 1/4 of a yard long. It is cut into 5 equal pieces. How long is each piece?
12. There is 1/2 of a pan of brownies left. It is divided equally among 6 students. What fraction of the whole pan does each student get?

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. 1/2 / 5 = 1/10
2. 1/3 / 3 = 1/9
3. 1/6 / 4 = 1/24
4. 1/4 / 4 = 1/16
5. 1/8 / 2 = 1/16
6. 1/5 / 4 = 1/20

**Part B**
7. 1/2 / 2 = 1/4 (two 1/4 strips cover one 1/2 strip)
8. 1/3 / 2 = 1/6 (two 1/6 strips cover one 1/3 strip)
9. 1/4 / 3 = 1/12 (three 1/12 strips cover one 1/4 strip)

**Part C**
10. 1/3 / 2 = 1/6 of the whole pizza per person.
11. 1/4 / 5 = 1/20 of a yard per piece.
12. 1/2 / 6 = 1/12 of the whole pan per student.

## Assessment Check

Draw a partitioning diagram to solve: 1/3 / 4 = ?
Explain in a sentence why the answer is less than 1/3.

**Target response:** Drawing shows a rectangle split into 3 rows and 4 columns (12 total pieces). The shaded row (1/3) contains 4 pieces, and each person gets 1 of those 4 pieces, which is 1/12 of the whole. So 1/3 / 4 = 1/12. The answer is less than 1/3 because we are splitting an already-small amount (1/3) into 4 even smaller parts.

## Extension

- **Shrinking Fractions:** Solve 1/2 / 2, then 1/4 / 2, then 1/8 / 2, then 1/16 / 2. What happens to the fraction each time you divide by 2? (Answers: 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32. Each time, the denominator doubles.)
- **Real-World Design:** You have 1/3 of a sheet of poster board. You want to make 6 equal bookmarks from it. Draw a diagram showing how to cut the poster board, and determine what fraction of the full sheet each bookmark represents. (Answer: 1/3 / 6 = 1/18 of the full sheet.)
