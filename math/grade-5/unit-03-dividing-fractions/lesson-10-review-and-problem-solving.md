# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 3, Lesson 10

## Topic
Review and Problem Solving

## Learning Objectives
- Demonstrate fluency with both types of fraction division (whole number / unit fraction and unit fraction / whole number).
- Solve multi-step problems that combine fraction division with other operations.
- Explain solution strategies and justify answers using estimation and multiplication checks.
- Prepare for the unit assessment by reviewing key concepts and common errors.

## Prerequisites
- All previous lessons in this unit (Lessons 1-9).
- Adding and subtracting fractions (Unit 1).
- Multiplying fractions (Unit 2).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Paper and pencil.
- Fraction strips (optional, for students who want visual support).
- Student worksheet for Independent Practice.

**Standards:** 5.NF.7 — Apply and extend previous understandings of division to divide unit fractions by whole numbers and whole numbers by unit fractions.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick review of both types to assess readiness.

1. Write six problems on the board. Students solve all six as fast as they can:
   - 5 / 1/3 = ? (15)
   - 1/4 / 6 = ? (1/24)
   - 8 / 1/2 = ? (16)
   - 1/6 / 3 = ? (1/18)
   - 3 / 1/5 = ? (15)
   - 1/2 / 7 = ? (1/14)
2. Review answers. For any problem many students missed, do a quick verbal explanation.
3. Say: "Today we put everything together. We will review the key ideas, solve some challenging problems, and get ready for the assessment."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Multi-step problem:** a problem that requires more than one operation to solve.
- **Fact family:** a set of related multiplication and division equations using the same numbers.
- **Strategy:** a plan or approach for solving a problem.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Summary of the two types.**

Write the two-type summary on the board:

Say: "Let's review our two rules one last time."

**Type 1: Whole Number / Unit Fraction**
- Rule: W / 1/n = W x n
- Example: 6 / 1/4 = 6 x 4 = 24
- Meaning: "How many fourths fit in 6?"
- The answer is larger than the whole number.

**Type 2: Unit Fraction / Whole Number**
- Rule: 1/n / W = 1/(n x W)
- Example: 1/4 / 6 = 1/(4 x 6) = 1/24
- Meaning: "1/4 shared among 6 people"
- The answer is smaller than the unit fraction.

Say: "Both types can be checked with multiplication. If a / b = c, then c x b = a."

**Step 2 — Multi-step problem strategies.**

Say: "Some problems require more than one step. You might need to divide and then add, or multiply and then divide. The key is to break the problem into steps."

Write: "A baker has 4 cups of flour and 3 cups of sugar. Each mini-cake needs 1/4 cup of flour and 1/6 cup of sugar. How many mini-cakes can the baker make if both ingredients are needed?"

Say: "This requires two separate divisions, then we take the smaller result."
- Flour: 4 / 1/4 = 16 mini-cakes.
- Sugar: 3 / 1/6 = 18 mini-cakes.
- The baker can only make 16 mini-cakes because the flour runs out first.

**Step 3 — Reviewing common mistakes.**

Say: "Before the assessment, let's review the most common mistakes."

Write each error on the board and correct it:

1. **Confusing the types:** 6 / 1/3 is NOT 1/18. It is 18. (If the whole number comes first, multiply by the denominator.)
2. **Treating division as multiplication:** 4 / 1/2 is NOT 2. It is 8. (Dividing by 1/2 means multiplying by 2, not multiplying by 1/2.)
3. **Getting the wrong dividend in word problems:** Always identify what you start with and what you divide by.
4. **Forgetting to check:** Always multiply quotient x divisor to verify.

**Step 4 — Practice with a multi-step word problem.**

Write: "Rosa has 1/3 of a yard of ribbon. She cuts it into 4 equal pieces. She uses 2 of the pieces on a gift. What total length of ribbon did she use on the gift?"

Say: "Step 1: Find the length of each piece. 1/3 / 4 = 1/12 yard per piece."
"Step 2: She uses 2 pieces. 2 x 1/12 = 2/12 = 1/6 yard."
"Answer: Rosa used 1/6 of a yard of ribbon on the gift."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** "A path is 6 miles long. Mile markers are placed every 1/4 mile. How many mile markers are there? If painting each marker costs $3, what is the total cost?"
Step 1: 6 / 1/4 = 24 markers.
Step 2: 24 x $3 = $72.
**Answer: 24 markers; total cost is $72.**

**Example B:** "There is 1/2 of a pie left. It is shared equally among 8 students. Then each student eats half of their share. How much of the original pie did each student eat?"
Step 1: 1/2 / 8 = 1/16 per student.
Step 2: Each eats half their share: 1/16 / 2 = 1/32.
**Answer: Each student ate 1/32 of the original pie.**

**Example C:** "Tom has 5 feet of wire. He cuts pieces that are each 1/6 foot long. He needs 3 pieces for each ornament. How many complete ornaments can he make? How many pieces are left over?"
Step 1: 5 / 1/6 = 30 pieces.
Step 2: 30 / 3 = 10 ornaments with 0 pieces left over.
**Answer: 10 complete ornaments, no leftover pieces.**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Solve each problem. Show all steps and check your work.

1. 7 / 1/4 = ?
   - 7 x 4 = 28. Check: 28 x 1/4 = 7.
   - **Answer: 28**

2. 1/5 / 9 = ?
   - 1/(5 x 9) = 1/45. Check: 1/45 x 9 = 9/45 = 1/5.
   - **Answer: 1/45**

3. 10 / 1/8 = ?
   - 10 x 8 = 80. Check: 80 x 1/8 = 10.
   - **Answer: 80**

4. 1/3 / 7 = ?
   - 1/(3 x 7) = 1/21. Check: 1/21 x 7 = 7/21 = 1/3.
   - **Answer: 1/21**

5. Multi-step: A garden bed is 3 feet long. Seeds are planted every 1/6 foot. If seeds come in packets of 6, how many packets does the gardener need?
   - Step 1: 3 / 1/6 = 18 seeds needed.
   - Step 2: 18 / 6 = 3 packets.
   - **Answer: 3 packets**

**Facilitator tip:** For the multi-step problem, ask students to identify each step before computing. This builds the habit of planning before solving.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Quick Review (Both Types)
Solve each problem.

1. 9 / 1/3 = ?
2. 1/8 / 5 = ?
3. 4 / 1/6 = ?
4. 1/2 / 10 = ?
5. 6 / 1/5 = ?
6. 1/4 / 8 = ?
7. 11 / 1/2 = ?
8. 1/10 / 4 = ?

### Part B: Multi-Step Word Problems

9. A roll of tape is 8 meters long. Each piece of tape is 1/4 meter. Marco uses 3 pieces for each box he wraps. How many boxes can he wrap?
10. There is 1/3 of a cake left after a party. The leftover cake is shared among 6 people. Then each person gives half of their piece to a friend. What fraction of the whole cake does each friend receive?
11. A carpenter has 5 boards, each 1 foot long. He cuts each board into pieces that are 1/8 foot long. He then bundles the pieces in groups of 10. How many complete bundles can he make?
12. A farmer has 4 acres of land. He plants corn on 1/2 of the land. The corn section is divided into 6 equal rows. What fraction of the total land is each row?

### Part C: Reasoning

13. Without computing, put these in order from least to greatest: 1/3 / 5, 5 / 1/3, 1/5 / 3.
14. Write a word problem that can be solved using the equation 4 / 1/3 = 12.
15. Write a word problem that can be solved using the equation 1/4 / 5 = 1/20.

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. 9 / 1/3 = 27
2. 1/8 / 5 = 1/40
3. 4 / 1/6 = 24
4. 1/2 / 10 = 1/20
5. 6 / 1/5 = 30
6. 1/4 / 8 = 1/32
7. 11 / 1/2 = 22
8. 1/10 / 4 = 1/40

**Part B**
9. Step 1: 8 / 1/4 = 32 pieces. Step 2: 32 / 3 = 10 boxes with 2 pieces left over. **10 complete boxes.**
10. Step 1: 1/3 / 6 = 1/18 per person. Step 2: 1/18 / 2 = 1/36 per friend. **Each friend gets 1/36 of the whole cake.**
11. Step 1: Each board yields 1 / 1/8 = 8 pieces. 5 boards yield 5 x 8 = 40 pieces. Step 2: 40 / 10 = 4 complete bundles. **4 bundles.**
12. Step 1: Corn section = 1/2 x 4 = 2 acres. Step 2: Each row = 2 / 6 = 2/6 = 1/3 acre. As a fraction of total land: 1/3 out of 4 acres -- but the question asks "what fraction of total land," so each row is (1/3) / 4 = 1/3 acre, and 1/3 of 1 acre / 4 total acres. Alternative: 1/2 of the land / 6 rows = 1/12 of the total land per row. **Each row is 1/12 of the total land.**

**Part C**
13. 1/3 / 5 = 1/15. 1/5 / 3 = 1/15. 5 / 1/3 = 15.
    Order from least to greatest: 1/3 / 5 = 1/5 / 3 (they are equal at 1/15), then 5 / 1/3 = 15.
    **1/3 / 5 = 1/5 / 3 < 5 / 1/3** (or equivalently: 1/15, 1/15, 15).

14. Sample answer: "A rope is 4 meters long. It is cut into pieces that are each 1/3 of a meter. How many pieces are there?" (Accept any reasonable word problem that matches 4 / 1/3 = 12.)

15. Sample answer: "A strip of paper is 1/4 of a foot long. It is cut into 5 equal pieces. How long is each piece?" (Accept any reasonable word problem that matches 1/4 / 5 = 1/20.)

## Assessment Check

Solve this two-step problem:
A restaurant has 3 gallons of soup. Each bowl holds 1/4 gallon. The restaurant serves soup for lunch and dinner. At lunch, they fill 8 bowls. How many bowls of soup are left for dinner?

**Target response:**
Step 1: Total bowls from 3 gallons: 3 / 1/4 = 12 bowls.
Step 2: Bowls left for dinner: 12 - 8 = 4 bowls.
Check: 12 x 1/4 = 3 gallons (total is correct). 4 bowls for dinner makes sense because 8 + 4 = 12.

If the school makes 15 art projects, how many science projects can they make with the remaining paint? (Art: 15 x 1/5 = 3 gallons used. Remaining: 6 - 3 = 3 gallons. Science: 3 / 1/3 = 9 projects.)
