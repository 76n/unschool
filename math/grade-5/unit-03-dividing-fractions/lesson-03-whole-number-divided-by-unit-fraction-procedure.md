# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 3, Lesson 3

## Topic
Whole Number / Unit Fraction: Developing the Procedure

## Learning Objectives
- Identify and describe the pattern: whole number / 1/n = whole number x n.
- Apply the procedure to compute whole number / unit fraction without drawing a model.
- Explain why the procedure works by connecting it to the visual models from Lesson 2.
- Verify answers using the inverse relationship between multiplication and division.

## Prerequisites
- Using fraction strips and number lines to divide a whole number by a unit fraction (Lesson 2).
- Understanding that dividing by a unit fraction asks "how many unit fractions fit in?" (Lessons 1-2).
- Whole-number multiplication fluency.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Fraction strips (available for students who need concrete support).
- Paper and pencil.
- Index cards or half-sheets for exit ticket.

**Standards:** 5.NF.7a — Interpret division of a whole number by a unit fraction, and compute such quotients.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Surface the pattern from Lesson 2 data.

1. Write these completed problems from Lesson 2 on the board:
   - 3 / 1/4 = 12
   - 2 / 1/3 = 6
   - 4 / 1/2 = 8
   - 2 / 1/5 = 10
   - 5 / 1/3 = 15
2. Ask: "Look at these problems. What pattern do you notice between the dividend, the fraction, and the answer?"
3. Give students 1-2 minutes to discuss with a partner.
4. Gather responses. Guide students toward: "The answer is the whole number times the denominator of the unit fraction."
5. Say: "Today we are going to confirm this pattern, understand why it works, and turn it into a rule we can use every time."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Procedure:** a step-by-step method for solving a type of problem.
- **Inverse operations:** operations that undo each other (multiplication and division are inverses).
- **Reciprocal:** flipping a fraction upside down (the reciprocal of 1/4 is 4/1 = 4).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Confirm the pattern with a new example.**
Write on the board: 6 / 1/5 = ?

Say: "Let's think about this with our model first. How many fifths are in 1 whole? (5.) How many fifths are in 6 wholes? (6 x 5 = 30.) So 6 / 1/5 = 30."

Write: 6 / 1/5 = 30 = 6 x 5.

Say: "The pattern holds again. The answer is the whole number times the denominator."

**Step 2 — Explain WHY the pattern works.**

Say: "Why does this work? Think about it this way. Each whole has n equal pieces when we divide it into n-ths. So if we have W wholes, the total number of pieces is W x n."

Write on the board:
- W / 1/n = W x n
- "Dividing by 1/n is the same as multiplying by n."

Say: "This connects to something called the reciprocal. The reciprocal of 1/n is n/1, which is just n. So dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal. You will see this idea again in middle school with all fractions, but for now we focus on unit fractions."

**Step 3 — The checking step: multiply to verify.**

Say: "Here is how we check our work. If 6 / 1/5 = 30, then 30 x 1/5 should give us back 6. Let's verify: 30 x 1/5 = 30/5 = 6. It works!"

Write the checking rule:
- If a / b = c, then c x b = a.
- Example: 6 / 1/5 = 30, so 30 x 1/5 = 6.

Say: "Always check your division by multiplying the quotient by the divisor. You should get back to the dividend."

**Step 4 — Practice the procedure step by step.**

Write the steps on the board:
1. Identify the whole number (dividend) and the unit fraction (divisor).
2. Multiply the whole number by the denominator of the unit fraction.
3. Write the answer.
4. Check: multiply the answer by the unit fraction. You should get the original whole number.

### Worked Examples

**Example A: 8 / 1/3**
Step 1: Whole number = 8, unit fraction = 1/3, denominator = 3.
Step 2: 8 x 3 = 24.
Step 3: 8 / 1/3 = 24.
Step 4: Check: 24 x 1/3 = 24/3 = 8. Correct.
**Answer: 24**

**Example B: 5 / 1/4**
Step 1: Whole number = 5, unit fraction = 1/4, denominator = 4.
Step 2: 5 x 4 = 20.
Step 3: 5 / 1/4 = 20.
Step 4: Check: 20 x 1/4 = 20/4 = 5. Correct.
**Answer: 20**

**Example C: 10 / 1/6**
Step 1: Whole number = 10, unit fraction = 1/6, denominator = 6.
Step 2: 10 x 6 = 60.
Step 3: 10 / 1/6 = 60.
Step 4: Check: 60 x 1/6 = 60/6 = 10. Correct.
**Answer: 60**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Solve each problem using the procedure. Show the check step.

1. 4 / 1/5 = ?
   - 4 x 5 = 20. Check: 20 x 1/5 = 4.
   - **Answer: 20**

2. 7 / 1/2 = ?
   - 7 x 2 = 14. Check: 14 x 1/2 = 7.
   - **Answer: 14**

3. 3 / 1/8 = ?
   - 3 x 8 = 24. Check: 24 x 1/8 = 3.
   - **Answer: 24**

4. 9 / 1/3 = ?
   - 9 x 3 = 27. Check: 27 x 1/3 = 9.
   - **Answer: 27**

5. 6 / 1/4 = ?
   - 6 x 4 = 24. Check: 24 x 1/4 = 6.
   - **Answer: 24**

6. 12 / 1/5 = ?
   - 12 x 5 = 60. Check: 60 x 1/5 = 12.
   - **Answer: 60**

**Facilitator tip:** For problems 1-3, ask students to also verify with a quick mental number line: "Can you picture how many [fraction] jumps fit in [whole number]?" This keeps the conceptual connection alive as they learn the procedure.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Computation
Solve using the procedure. Show your check step for each.

1. 5 / 1/6 = ?
2. 8 / 1/4 = ?
3. 3 / 1/10 = ?
4. 7 / 1/3 = ?
5. 4 / 1/8 = ?
6. 11 / 1/2 = ?
7. 9 / 1/5 = ?
8. 6 / 1/6 = ?

### Part B: Explain the Connection
9. Marcus says "4 / 1/3 = 12 because there are 12 thirds in 4 wholes." Is he correct? Explain using a visual model or the procedure.
10. Priya computed 7 / 1/4 = 7/4. What mistake did she make? What is the correct answer?
11. Fill in the blank: ___ / 1/5 = 40. Explain how you found your answer.

### Part C: Word Problems
12. A shelf is 3 feet long. Books are each 1/6 of a foot wide. How many books fit on the shelf?
13. A carpenter has a board that is 8 feet long. He needs pieces that are 1/4 of a foot each. How many pieces can he cut?

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. 5 / 1/6 = 5 x 6 = 30. Check: 30 x 1/6 = 5.
2. 8 / 1/4 = 8 x 4 = 32. Check: 32 x 1/4 = 8.
3. 3 / 1/10 = 3 x 10 = 30. Check: 30 x 1/10 = 3.
4. 7 / 1/3 = 7 x 3 = 21. Check: 21 x 1/3 = 7.
5. 4 / 1/8 = 4 x 8 = 32. Check: 32 x 1/8 = 4.
6. 11 / 1/2 = 11 x 2 = 22. Check: 22 x 1/2 = 11.
7. 9 / 1/5 = 9 x 5 = 45. Check: 45 x 1/5 = 9.
8. 6 / 1/6 = 6 x 6 = 36. Check: 36 x 1/6 = 6.

**Part B**
9. Yes, Marcus is correct. Using the procedure: 4 / 1/3 = 4 x 3 = 12. Visually, each whole has 3 thirds, and 4 wholes have 4 x 3 = 12 thirds.
10. Priya multiplied 7 x 1/4 instead of dividing 7 by 1/4. She confused division with multiplication. The correct answer is 7 / 1/4 = 7 x 4 = 28.
11. _40_ / 1/5 would mean ? x 5 = 40, so ? = 40 / 5 = 8. Answer: 8 / 1/5 = 40. Check: 8 x 5 = 40.

**Part C**
12. 3 / 1/6 = 3 x 6 = 18 books.
13. 8 / 1/4 = 8 x 4 = 32 pieces.

## Assessment Check

Solve: 9 / 1/4 = ?
Show the procedure AND explain in words why the answer makes sense.

**Target response:** 9 / 1/4 = 9 x 4 = 36. This makes sense because each whole has 4 fourths, so 9 wholes have 9 x 4 = 36 fourths. The answer is larger than 9 because we are dividing by a number less than 1. Check: 36 x 1/4 = 36/4 = 9.

## Extension

- **Big Numbers:** Solve 100 / 1/8. Can you solve it without a calculator? Explain your reasoning. (Answer: 100 x 8 = 800. There are 800 eighths in 100 wholes.)
- **Backwards Challenge:** The answer to a division problem is 42. The divisor is a unit fraction. Find three different possible dividends and divisors that give 42. (Example solutions: 7 / 1/6 = 42, 6 / 1/7 = 42, 42 / 1/1 = 42, 21 / 1/2 = 42, 14 / 1/3 = 42.)
