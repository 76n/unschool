# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 3, Lesson 1

## Topic
Meaning of Fraction Division

## Learning Objectives
- Explain the two meanings of division: sharing (partitive) and measurement (how many groups).
- Apply both meanings of division to problems involving fractions.
- Predict whether a quotient involving a fraction will be greater than or less than the dividend.
- Use concrete materials to model simple fraction division situations.

## Prerequisites
- Fluency with whole-number division facts (e.g., 12 / 3 = 4).
- Understanding of fractions as parts of a whole (Unit 1).
- Multiplying fractions by whole numbers (Unit 2, Lessons 1-2).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Fraction strips (1 set per student).
- Counters or small objects (12 per student).
- Index cards or half-sheets for exit ticket.

**Standards:** 5.NF.7 — Apply and extend previous understandings of division to divide unit fractions by whole numbers and whole numbers by unit fractions.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of the two meanings of whole-number division.

1. Write on the board: "12 / 3 = 4."
2. Ask: "Can someone explain what 12 / 3 means?" Accept both interpretations:
   - **Sharing:** "12 objects shared equally among 3 groups. Each group gets 4."
   - **Measurement:** "How many groups of 3 fit into 12? The answer is 4 groups."
3. Have students use 12 counters to model both interpretations at their desks.
4. Write on the board: "Today we are going to see what happens when division involves fractions. The same two ideas -- sharing and measurement -- still work."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Dividend:** the number being divided (the starting amount).
- **Divisor:** the number you divide by.
- **Quotient:** the result of division.
- **Measurement division (repeated subtraction):** asking "How many groups of [divisor] fit into [dividend]?"
- **Sharing division (partitive):** asking "If [dividend] is shared equally among [divisor] groups, how much is in each group?"

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Measurement division with a fraction.**
Write on the board: 2 / 1/4 = ?

Say: "Let's think about this as a measurement problem. The question is: how many pieces of size 1/4 fit into 2 wholes?"

Take out fraction strips. Lay out 2 whole strips. Then count how many 1/4 pieces it takes to cover them.

Say: "One whole has 4 fourths. Two wholes have 8 fourths. So 2 / 1/4 = 8."

Say: "Notice something interesting -- we divided, but the answer (8) is bigger than what we started with (2). That is because we divided by a number less than 1. When you divide by a small number, you get a big result."

**Step 2 — Sharing division with a fraction.**
Write on the board: 1/2 / 3 = ?

Say: "Now let's think about sharing. The question is: if we have 1/2 of a pizza and we share it equally among 3 people, how much does each person get?"

Draw a rectangle on the board. Shade half of it. Then divide the shaded half into 3 equal pieces.

Say: "Each person gets one of these small pieces. How big is each piece compared to the whole pizza? The whole pizza has 6 pieces of this size (3 in each half). So each piece is 1/6 of the whole pizza. That means 1/2 / 3 = 1/6."

Say: "This time, the answer (1/6) is smaller than what we started with (1/2). That makes sense -- we shared a small amount among several people, so each person gets even less."

**Step 3 — Two types of problems in this unit.**
Write on the board:
- **Type 1:** Whole number / unit fraction (like 2 / 1/4 = 8) -- measurement division
- **Type 2:** Unit fraction / whole number (like 1/2 / 3 = 1/6) -- sharing division

Say: "In this unit, we will study both types. The order matters! 2 / 1/4 is very different from 1/4 / 2. Let's always ask ourselves: what is the question really asking?"

**Step 4 — Predict the size of the answer.**

Say: "Here is a helpful thinking tool. Before you calculate, ask yourself: should the answer be bigger or smaller than the dividend?"

Write the rules:
- Dividing by a number less than 1 makes the answer BIGGER than the dividend.
- Dividing by a number greater than 1 makes the answer SMALLER than the dividend.

Say: "This is the opposite of what happens with multiplication. In Unit 2, we learned that multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes the answer smaller. Division does the reverse."

### Worked Examples

**Example A: 3 / 1/2**
Question: How many halves fit into 3?
Visual: Lay out 3 whole fraction strips. Each whole contains 2 halves.
Count: 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 halves.
**Answer: 3 / 1/2 = 6**
Check: Is 6 bigger than 3? Yes. We divided by a number less than 1, so that makes sense.

**Example B: 1/3 / 2**
Question: If 1/3 of a pie is shared equally between 2 people, how much does each person get?
Visual: Draw a whole pie divided into thirds. Shade one third. Split that shaded third into 2 equal pieces. Each piece is 1/6 of the whole pie.
**Answer: 1/3 / 2 = 1/6**
Check: Is 1/6 smaller than 1/3? Yes. We divided by a number greater than 1, so that makes sense.

**Example C: 4 / 1/3**
Question: How many thirds fit into 4?
Visual: Each whole has 3 thirds. Four wholes have 4 x 3 = 12 thirds.
**Answer: 4 / 1/3 = 12**
Check: Is 12 bigger than 4? Yes. Dividing by 1/3 (less than 1) gives a bigger number.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. For each, identify the type (measurement or sharing) and use fraction strips or a drawing.

1. 2 / 1/3 = ?
   - Type: Measurement (how many 1/3s fit into 2?)
   - **Answer: 6**

2. 1/4 / 2 = ?
   - Type: Sharing (1/4 shared between 2 people)
   - **Answer: 1/8**

3. 5 / 1/2 = ?
   - Type: Measurement (how many 1/2s fit into 5?)
   - **Answer: 10**

4. 1/5 / 3 = ?
   - Type: Sharing (1/5 shared among 3 people)
   - **Answer: 1/15**

5. 1 / 1/6 = ?
   - Type: Measurement (how many 1/6s fit into 1?)
   - **Answer: 6**

**Facilitator tip:** After each problem, ask: "Is the answer bigger or smaller than the dividend? Does that match what we predicted?"

## Assessment Check

Solve this problem and explain your reasoning:

A board is 3 feet long. It is cut into pieces that are each 1/6 of a foot long. How many pieces are there? Is the answer greater than or less than 3? Why?

**Target response:** 3 / 1/6 = 18 pieces. The answer is greater than 3 because we are dividing by a number less than 1, which gives a larger result. Each piece is very small (1/6 of a foot), so many pieces fit into 3 feet.

## Extension

- **Create Your Own:** Write one word problem for each type (measurement and sharing). Trade with a partner and solve each other's problems.
- **Explore:** What happens when you divide a whole number by 1/10? Try 1 / 1/10, 2 / 1/10, and 5 / 1/10. What pattern do you notice? Why does this pattern make sense?
