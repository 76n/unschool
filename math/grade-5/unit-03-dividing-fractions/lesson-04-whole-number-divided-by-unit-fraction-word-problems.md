# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 3, Lesson 4

## Topic
Whole Number / Unit Fraction: Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
- Identify real-world situations that require dividing a whole number by a unit fraction.
- Translate word problems into division equations of the form whole number / unit fraction.
- Solve word problems and express answers with appropriate units.
- Draw diagrams to support problem solving and verify answers.

## Prerequisites
- Computing whole number / unit fraction using the procedure (Lesson 3).
- Understanding the measurement interpretation: "How many [unit fractions] fit in [whole number]?" (Lessons 1-2).
- Reading comprehension of grade-level word problems.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Paper and pencil.
- Colored pencils (for diagrams).
- Student worksheet for Independent Practice.

**Standards:** 5.NF.7c — Solve real-world problems involving division of unit fractions by non-zero whole numbers and division of whole numbers by unit fractions.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review the procedure and connect to a real-world situation.

1. Write on the board: "A recipe uses 1/4 cup of oil. You have 3 cups of oil. How many recipes can you make?"
2. Ask: "What operation do we need?" (Division: 3 / 1/4.)
3. Ask: "What is 3 / 1/4?" (3 x 4 = 12 recipes.)
4. Ask: "Does 12 make sense? Is it more than 3?" (Yes, because each recipe uses a small amount, so we can make many recipes.)
5. Say: "Today we will practice solving many word problems like this one. The key is figuring out what the problem is really asking."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Measurement division context:** a problem that asks "How many [unit fraction]-sized pieces fit into [whole number]?"
- **Diagram:** a simple drawing that represents the quantities in a problem.
- **Label:** writing the units (feet, cups, servings, etc.) with your numerical answer.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Identify the structure of a word problem.**

Say: "In word problems that involve whole number / unit fraction, you will usually see a question like: 'How many [small pieces] are in [a larger amount]?' The small pieces are unit fractions. The larger amount is a whole number."

Write the problem-solving steps on the board:
1. **Read** the problem carefully. Identify the total amount (dividend) and the size of each piece (divisor).
2. **Write** the division equation.
3. **Draw** a simple diagram.
4. **Solve** using the procedure: whole number x denominator.
5. **Check** by multiplying the answer by the unit fraction.
6. **Label** your answer with the correct units.

**Step 2 — Work through a problem together.**

Write on the board: "A roll of ribbon is 6 meters long. Mia cuts pieces that are each 1/3 of a meter long. How many pieces can she cut?"

Say: "Let's follow our steps."
- Step 1: The total is 6 meters. Each piece is 1/3 meter.
- Step 2: Equation: 6 / 1/3 = ?
- Step 3: Draw 6 rectangles (representing 6 meters). Divide each into 3 equal parts.
- Step 4: 6 x 3 = 18.
- Step 5: Check: 18 x 1/3 = 18/3 = 6. Correct.
- Step 6: Mia can cut 18 pieces.

**Step 3 — Work through a second problem.**

Write: "A race is 4 miles long. There is a water station every 1/2 mile. How many water stations are there along the course?"

Say: "Follow the steps with me."
- Total distance: 4 miles. Spacing: 1/2 mile.
- Equation: 4 / 1/2 = ?
- Draw a number line from 0 to 4. Mark every 1/2 mile.
- 4 x 2 = 8.
- Check: 8 x 1/2 = 4. Correct.

Say: "Be careful with problems like this. The question asks about stations 'along the course.' If there is a station at the start (mile 0), there would be 9 stations total, but if the question is about the number of half-mile segments, the answer is 8. Always read carefully to see exactly what is being counted."

For this problem, we count the segments: **8 water stations** (placed at 1/2, 1, 1 1/2, 2, 2 1/2, 3, 3 1/2, and 4 miles).

**Step 4 — Discuss common traps.**

Say: "Watch out for problems that look like division but are really multiplication, or vice versa. Ask yourself: am I finding how many pieces fit in a whole, or am I finding a fraction of a whole?"

Write two problems side by side:
- "How many 1/4-cup servings are in 3 cups?" --> 3 / 1/4 = 12 (division)
- "What is 1/4 of 3 cups?" --> 1/4 x 3 = 3/4 (multiplication)

Say: "See the difference? The first asks 'how many,' which is division. The second asks 'what is a part of,' which is multiplication."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** "A fence is 5 meters long. Posts are placed every 1/4 of a meter. How many spaces between posts are there?"
Equation: 5 / 1/4 = 5 x 4 = 20.
Check: 20 x 1/4 = 5. Correct.
**Answer: 20 spaces**

**Example B:** "Dad has 2 gallons of lemonade. He pours glasses that each hold 1/8 of a gallon. How many glasses can he pour?"
Equation: 2 / 1/8 = 2 x 8 = 16.
Check: 16 x 1/8 = 16/8 = 2. Correct.
**Answer: 16 glasses**

**Example C:** "A garden bed is 3 feet wide. Carrot rows are planted every 1/6 of a foot apart. How many rows fit across the bed?"
Equation: 3 / 1/6 = 3 x 6 = 18.
Check: 18 x 1/6 = 18/6 = 3. Correct.
**Answer: 18 rows**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Solve each problem. Write the equation, draw a quick diagram, solve, and check.

1. A board is 4 feet long. It is cut into pieces that are each 1/6 of a foot. How many pieces are there?
   - 4 / 1/6 = 4 x 6 = 24 pieces. Check: 24 x 1/6 = 4.
   - **Answer: 24 pieces**

2. A bag has 7 pounds of dog food. Each serving is 1/2 pound. How many servings are in the bag?
   - 7 / 1/2 = 7 x 2 = 14 servings. Check: 14 x 1/2 = 7.
   - **Answer: 14 servings**

3. A path is 2 miles long. Benches are placed every 1/5 of a mile. How many sections are there between benches?
   - 2 / 1/5 = 2 x 5 = 10 sections. Check: 10 x 1/5 = 2.
   - **Answer: 10 sections**

4. Sara has 6 yards of fabric. She needs 1/3 of a yard for each headband. How many headbands can she make?
   - 6 / 1/3 = 6 x 3 = 18 headbands. Check: 18 x 1/3 = 6.
   - **Answer: 18 headbands**

5. A school hallway is 8 meters long. Tiles are each 1/4 of a meter wide. How many tiles fit along the hallway?
   - 8 / 1/4 = 8 x 4 = 32 tiles. Check: 32 x 1/4 = 8.
   - **Answer: 32 tiles**

**Facilitator tip:** After solving, ask one student to explain their diagram for each problem. Hearing different students describe their visual models reinforces the concept for the whole group.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Set Up and Solve
Write the division equation and solve.

1. A 3-mile trail has markers every 1/4 mile. How many markers are along the trail (not counting the start)?
2. A baker has 5 cups of flour. Each mini-cake requires 1/6 of a cup. How many mini-cakes can she make?
3. A rope is 4 meters long. It is cut into segments of 1/8 meter each. How many segments are there?
4. There are 6 liters of water. Water bottles each hold 1/5 of a liter. How many bottles can be filled?
5. A wall is 10 feet long. Decorative tiles that are each 1/3 of a foot wide are placed along the wall. How many tiles fit?

### Part B: Choose the Operation
For each problem, decide if it is multiplication or division, write the equation, and solve.

6. How many 1/4-pound burgers can be made from 3 pounds of meat?
7. A recipe uses 1/3 cup of butter. Tanya makes 1/2 of the recipe. How much butter does she need?
8. A relay race is 2 miles long. Each runner runs 1/6 of a mile. How many runners are needed?
9. A farmer plants seeds in rows spaced 1/5 of a meter apart across a 4-meter field. How many rows does he plant?

### Part C: Multi-Step Problems

10. Luis has 3 feet of wire. He cuts pieces that are each 1/4 foot long. He uses 2 pieces for each craft project. How many complete craft projects can he make?
11. A juice bar has 8 gallons of smoothie mix. Small cups hold 1/3 gallon and large cups hold 1/2 gallon. How many more small cups can be filled than large cups?

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. 3 / 1/4 = 12 markers
2. 5 / 1/6 = 30 mini-cakes
3. 4 / 1/8 = 32 segments
4. 6 / 1/5 = 30 bottles
5. 10 / 1/3 = 30 tiles

**Part B**
6. Division: 3 / 1/4 = 12 burgers
7. Multiplication: 1/2 x 1/3 = 1/6 cup of butter
8. Division: 2 / 1/6 = 12 runners
9. Division: 4 / 1/5 = 20 rows

**Part C**
10. Step 1: 3 / 1/4 = 12 pieces. Step 2: 12 / 2 = 6 complete craft projects.
11. Small cups: 8 / 1/3 = 24 cups. Large cups: 8 / 1/2 = 16 cups. Difference: 24 - 16 = 8 more small cups.

## Assessment Check

A chef has 4 cups of cream. He uses 1/3 of a cup for each dessert. How many desserts can he make? Write the equation, solve, and explain why your answer is reasonable.

**Target response:** 4 / 1/3 = 4 x 3 = 12 desserts. This is reasonable because each dessert uses only a small amount (1/3 cup), so 4 cups can make many desserts. Check: 12 x 1/3 = 12/3 = 4 cups. The answer checks out.

## Extension

- **Create a Menu:** Invent a restaurant menu where each dish uses a unit-fraction amount of an ingredient (e.g., 1/4 cup of cheese, 1/6 pound of meat). Then determine how many servings of each dish can be made from whole-number amounts of each ingredient.
- **Two-Step Challenge:** A gardener has 9 feet of fencing. She uses pieces of 1/3 foot each. She builds pens that require 6 pieces each. How many complete pens can she build? (Answer: 9 / 1/3 = 27 pieces. 27 / 6 = 4 pens with 3 pieces left over.)
