# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 3, Lesson 7

## Topic
Unit Fraction / Whole Number: Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
- Identify real-world situations that require dividing a unit fraction by a whole number.
- Translate word problems into division equations of the form unit fraction / whole number.
- Solve word problems and express answers with appropriate units.
- Draw diagrams to support problem solving and verify answers.

## Prerequisites
- Computing unit fraction / whole number using the procedure (Lesson 6).
- Understanding the sharing interpretation: "A unit fraction shared equally among groups" (Lessons 1, 5).
- Distinguishing this type from whole number / unit fraction (Lessons 2-4).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Paper and pencil.
- Colored pencils (for diagrams).
- Student worksheet for Independent Practice.

**Standards:** 5.NF.7c — Solve real-world problems involving division of unit fractions by non-zero whole numbers and division of whole numbers by unit fractions.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review the procedure and connect to a real-world sharing scenario.

1. Write on the board: "Three friends equally share 1/2 of a pan of lasagna. How much does each friend get?"
2. Ask: "What equation do we write?" (1/2 / 3 = ?)
3. Ask: "What is 1/2 / 3?" (1/6.)
4. Ask: "Does 1/6 make sense? Is it less than 1/2?" (Yes. We split a half among 3 people, so each gets a small piece.)
5. Say: "Today we will solve many word problems where a unit fraction is shared or divided among a whole number of groups. The key is recognizing that the fraction is the starting amount and the whole number is what we divide it among."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Sharing context:** a situation where an amount is divided equally among people or groups.
- **Equation:** a number sentence that uses an equals sign to show two values are the same.
- **Reasonable answer:** an answer that makes sense given the context (e.g., sharing makes things smaller).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Identify the structure of word problems.**

Say: "In these problems, you will see a situation where someone starts with a unit fraction of something and shares it equally among a whole number of groups. The question asks: 'How much does each group get?'"

Write the problem-solving steps on the board:
1. **Read** the problem. Identify the starting amount (a unit fraction) and the number of groups (a whole number).
2. **Write** the division equation: unit fraction / whole number.
3. **Draw** a quick diagram.
4. **Solve** using the procedure: multiply the denominator by the whole number.
5. **Check** by multiplying the answer by the whole number.
6. **Label** the answer with units.

**Step 2 — Work through a problem together.**

Write: "A pitcher has 1/4 of a gallon of juice. It is poured equally into 3 glasses. How much juice is in each glass?"

Say: "Let's follow our steps."
- Step 1: Starting amount = 1/4 gallon. Number of groups = 3 glasses.
- Step 2: Equation: 1/4 / 3 = ?
- Step 3: Draw a rectangle. Shade 1/4. Divide that quarter into 3 equal parts.
- Step 4: 1/4 / 3 = 1/(4 x 3) = 1/12.
- Step 5: Check: 1/12 x 3 = 3/12 = 1/4. Correct.
- Step 6: Each glass has 1/12 of a gallon.

**Step 3 — Work through a second problem.**

Write: "A dog walk is 1/3 of a mile long. The walk is divided into 4 equal sections by rest stops. How long is each section?"

Say: "Follow the steps with me."
- Starting amount: 1/3 mile. Number of sections: 4.
- Equation: 1/3 / 4 = ?
- Draw a number line from 0 to 1. Mark 1/3. Then divide the segment from 0 to 1/3 into 4 equal parts.
- 1/3 / 4 = 1/(3 x 4) = 1/12.
- Check: 1/12 x 4 = 4/12 = 1/3. Correct.
- Each section is 1/12 of a mile.

**Step 4 — Contrast with the other type.**

Say: "Be careful! Some problems involve the other type of division. Let's compare."

Write two problems side by side:
- "1/3 of a gallon is shared among 4 people." --> 1/3 / 4 = 1/12 (This lesson's type)
- "3 gallons are split into 1/4-gallon servings." --> 3 / 1/4 = 12 (Lesson 4's type)

Say: "In the first problem, we start with a small fraction and share it. In the second, we start with a whole number and count how many fractional pieces fit. Ask yourself: am I sharing a fraction, or am I counting how many fractions fit in a whole number?"

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** "A garden has 1/5 of an acre of sunflowers. The sunflower area is divided equally among 3 rows. How much of an acre is each row?"
Equation: 1/5 / 3 = 1/(5 x 3) = 1/15 of an acre.
Check: 1/15 x 3 = 3/15 = 1/5. Correct.
**Answer: 1/15 of an acre**

**Example B:** "Maria has 1/2 of a pound of clay. She splits it into 8 equal balls. How heavy is each ball?"
Equation: 1/2 / 8 = 1/(2 x 8) = 1/16 of a pound.
Check: 1/16 x 8 = 8/16 = 1/2. Correct.
**Answer: 1/16 of a pound**

**Example C:** "A piece of rope is 1/6 of a yard long. It is cut into 2 equal pieces. How long is each piece?"
Equation: 1/6 / 2 = 1/(6 x 2) = 1/12 of a yard.
Check: 1/12 x 2 = 2/12 = 1/6. Correct.
**Answer: 1/12 of a yard**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Solve each problem. Write the equation, draw a quick diagram, solve, and check.

1. Four children equally share 1/3 of a pizza. How much pizza does each child get?
   - 1/3 / 4 = 1/12 of the pizza. Check: 1/12 x 4 = 4/12 = 1/3.
   - **Answer: 1/12 of the pizza**

2. A board is 1/4 of a foot long. It is cut into 5 equal pieces. How long is each piece?
   - 1/4 / 5 = 1/20 of a foot. Check: 1/20 x 5 = 5/20 = 1/4.
   - **Answer: 1/20 of a foot**

3. A bottle contains 1/8 of a liter of paint. The paint is divided equally into 2 cups. How much paint is in each cup?
   - 1/8 / 2 = 1/16 of a liter. Check: 1/16 x 2 = 2/16 = 1/8.
   - **Answer: 1/16 of a liter**

4. A farm has 1/2 of an acre of carrots. The carrot patch is divided into 6 equal rows. How much of an acre is each row?
   - 1/2 / 6 = 1/12 of an acre. Check: 1/12 x 6 = 6/12 = 1/2.
   - **Answer: 1/12 of an acre**

5. A strip of paper is 1/5 of a meter long. It is folded into 3 equal sections. How long is each section?
   - 1/5 / 3 = 1/15 of a meter. Check: 1/15 x 3 = 3/15 = 1/5.
   - **Answer: 1/15 of a meter**

**Facilitator tip:** After each problem, ask students: "How did you know this was a sharing problem and not a measurement problem?" Reinforce the key difference: we started with a fraction and split it into groups.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Set Up and Solve
Write the division equation and solve.

1. Six friends share 1/4 of a cake equally. How much cake does each friend get?
2. A bag contains 1/3 of a kilogram of sand. The sand is split equally into 5 containers. How much sand is in each container?
3. A trail segment is 1/6 of a mile long. It is divided into 3 equal sections for a relay. How long is each section?
4. A container holds 1/2 of a cup of yogurt. It is divided equally among 4 bowls. How much yogurt is in each bowl?
5. A piece of wire is 1/8 of a meter long. It is cut into 2 equal pieces. How long is each piece?

### Part B: Choose the Operation
For each problem, decide if it is unit fraction / whole number OR whole number / unit fraction. Write the equation and solve.

6. A plank is 1/5 of a yard long. It is cut into 4 equal pieces. How long is each piece?
7. A farmer has 6 gallons of milk. Each jug holds 1/3 of a gallon. How many jugs can be filled?
8. There is 1/4 of a pie left. It is shared equally among 3 friends. How much pie does each friend get?
9. A garden row is 5 feet long. Seed holes are placed every 1/6 of a foot. How many seed holes are there?

### Part C: Multi-Step Problems

10. Sam has 1/2 of a bar of chocolate. He gives half of his share to his sister. Then his sister splits her piece equally with 2 friends. How much of the original chocolate bar does each of his sister's friends get?
11. Three families share 1/4 of an acre of community garden equally. Each family then plants vegetables in half of their section. How much of the acre does each family use for vegetables?

### Answer Key

**Part A**
1. 1/4 / 6 = 1/24 of the cake per friend.
2. 1/3 / 5 = 1/15 of a kilogram per container.
3. 1/6 / 3 = 1/18 of a mile per section.
4. 1/2 / 4 = 1/8 of a cup per bowl.
5. 1/8 / 2 = 1/16 of a meter per piece.

**Part B**
6. Unit fraction / whole number: 1/5 / 4 = 1/20 of a yard per piece.
7. Whole number / unit fraction: 6 / 1/3 = 18 jugs.
8. Unit fraction / whole number: 1/4 / 3 = 1/12 of the pie per friend.
9. Whole number / unit fraction: 5 / 1/6 = 30 seed holes.

**Part C**
10. Step 1: Sam gives his sister half of 1/2: 1/2 / 2 = 1/4.
    Step 2: His sister splits 1/4 equally with 2 friends (3 people total): 1/4 / 3 = 1/12.
    Each of his sister's friends gets 1/12 of the original bar.

11. Step 1: Each family gets 1/4 / 3 = 1/12 of the acre.
    Step 2: Each family uses half their section for vegetables: 1/12 / 2 = 1/24 of the acre.
    Each family uses 1/24 of the acre for vegetables.

## Assessment Check

A recipe makes 1/3 of a batch of cookies. If 4 people share the cookies equally, what fraction of a full batch does each person get? Write the equation, solve, and explain why your answer is reasonable.

**Target response:** 1/3 / 4 = 1/12 of a batch per person. This is reasonable because we started with only 1/3 of a batch (already less than a full batch) and split it 4 ways, so each person gets a small fraction. Check: 1/12 x 4 = 4/12 = 1/3. The answer is correct.

## Extension

- **Real-Life Sharing:** Think of 3 real-life situations where you would divide a unit fraction by a whole number. Write each as a word problem, draw a diagram, and solve.
- **Comparison Challenge:** Which gives a larger answer: 1/3 / 5 or 1/5 / 3? Predict first, then compute both. Explain why one is larger. (Answer: 1/3 / 5 = 1/15 and 1/5 / 3 = 1/15. They are equal! This is because 3 x 5 = 5 x 3 = 15. This works only because the multiplication of the denominators is commutative.)
