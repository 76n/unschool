# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 5, Lesson 1

## Topic
Place Value to Millions (Reviewing and Extending the Place Value System)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Name and identify all place values from the ones place through the hundred-millions place.
- Identify the three place-value periods (ones, thousands, millions) and explain how each period repeats the hundreds-tens-ones pattern.
- Read and write whole numbers through the hundred millions in standard form, expanded form, and word form.
- State the value of any digit in a number through the hundred millions.
- Compare and order multi-digit numbers through the hundred millions.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to read and write whole numbers to the millions (Grade 4).
- Understanding that each place value is 10 times the place to its right (Grade 4).
- Familiarity with reading commas as period boundaries in large numbers.

## Materials Needed
- Place-value chart drawn on whiteboard (or printed): billions | hundred-millions | ten-millions | millions | hundred-thousands | ten-thousands | thousands | hundreds | tens | ones
- Colored markers (use one color per period: millions = blue, thousands = orange, ones = red)
- Whiteboard and markers
- Paper and pencil
- Index cards for exit ticket

**Standards:** 5.NBT.1, 5.NBT.3

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of place value and diagnose any gaps before extending to higher places.

Write on the board:
1. "What is the value of the digit 4 in 4,250,000?"
2. "Write 3,000,000 + 700,000 + 60,000 + 9,000 as a single number."
3. "Write 5,812,403 in word form."
4. "Which is greater: 6,340,000 or 6,304,000? How do you know?"

Give the student 2-3 minutes to answer, then discuss briefly.

Say: "You already know about millions. Today we are going to make sure your understanding of the whole place-value system from ones through millions is solid — and then we will start stretching toward billions in our next lesson."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Solidify the structure of the base-ten place-value system through the millions and emphasize the repeating period pattern.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Place value:** The value of a digit based on its position in a number.
- **Period:** A group of three consecutive places in a large number, separated by a comma. The three periods covered today are the ones period (ones, tens, hundreds), thousands period (thousands, ten-thousands, hundred-thousands), and millions period (millions, ten-millions, hundred-millions).
- **Standard form:** The ordinary way of writing a number using digits and commas (e.g., 47,320,005).
- **Expanded form:** Writing a number as the sum of the values of each digit (e.g., 40,000,000 + 7,000,000 + 300,000 + 20,000 + 5).
- **Word form:** Writing the number using words (e.g., forty-seven million, three hundred twenty thousand, five).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Draw and label the place-value chart.**

Draw this on the board:

```
| Hundred-Millions | Ten-Millions | Millions | Hundred-Thousands | Ten-Thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
```

Write the value of each place below:

```
| 100,000,000 | 10,000,000 | 1,000,000 | 100,000 | 10,000 | 1,000 | 100 | 10 | 1 |
```

Color-code: circle the millions period in blue, thousands in orange, ones in red.

Say: "Notice the pattern within each period. Every period has the same three names: hundreds, tens, and ones. We just add the period name at the end: hundred-thousands, ten-thousands, thousands. Or: hundred-millions, ten-millions, millions."

**Step 2 — The x10 pattern between places.**

Point to the ones place (1) and move left:
- Ones: 1
- Tens: 10 (ten times greater)
- Hundreds: 100 (ten times greater)
- Thousands: 1,000 (ten times greater)
- Ten-thousands: 10,000 (ten times greater)
- Continue up through hundred-millions.

Say: "Every time we move one place to the left, the value is multiplied by 10. This is the foundation of our whole number system. Moving right divides by 10."

**Step 3 — Reading numbers using the period method.**

Write: 47,320,005

Say: "Here is how to read this number systematically. Look at each period from left to right.
- Millions period: 47 → 'forty-seven million'
- Thousands period: 320 → 'three hundred twenty thousand'
- Ones period: 005 → 'five'
Read together: forty-seven million, three hundred twenty thousand, five."

Practice: write 5,004,800 on the board and ask the student to read it aloud. Answer: five million, four thousand, eight hundred.

**Step 4 — Expanded form.**

Write: 29,045,103

Expanded form: 20,000,000 + 9,000,000 + 40,000 + 5,000 + 100 + 3

Note: we skip any place with a zero digit — there is no hundreds-thousands place digit, no tens digit.

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Write 308,070,050 in word form.
- Millions period: 308 → three hundred eight million
- Thousands period: 070 → seventy thousand
- Ones period: 050 → fifty
- Word form: three hundred eight million, seventy thousand, fifty

**Example B:** Write the expanded form of 5,604,022.
- 5,000,000 + 600,000 + 4,000 + 20 + 2

**Example C:** Compare 48,350,000 and 48,305,000.
- Both have 48 in the millions period: equal so far.
- Thousands period: 350 vs. 305. Compare hundred-thousands: both have 3. Ten-thousands: 5 vs. 0. Since 5 > 0, the first number is greater.
- 48,350,000 > 48,305,000

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Work through these together, using the place-value chart.

1. Write 72,506,014 in word form.
   - **Answer:** seventy-two million, five hundred six thousand, fourteen

2. Write in standard form: "four hundred million, three thousand, sixty."
   - **Answer:** 400,003,060

3. What is the value of the digit 9 in 195,340,000?
   - **Answer:** 90,000,000 (90 million; the 9 is in the ten-millions place)

4. Write the expanded form of 83,007,500.
   - **Answer:** 80,000,000 + 3,000,000 + 7,000 + 500

5. Order from greatest to least: 24,100,000 and 24,010,000 and 24,001,000.
   - Compare millions period: all 24 million — equal.
   - Thousands period: 100 vs. 010 vs. 001. The first is greatest.
   - **Answer:** 24,100,000 > 24,010,000 > 24,001,000

**Facilitator tip:** If the student makes errors on word form, slow down and have them write the digits in the chart one period at a time. Reading the chart period-by-period (millions first, then thousands, then ones) and saying only the three-digit number within each period before adding the period name is the most reliable strategy.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Write 6,050,400 in word form.
2. Write in standard form: "thirty million, five hundred twelve thousand, three hundred."
3. What is the value of the digit 8 in 208,741,000?
4. Write the expanded form of 45,030,700.
5. Compare using <, >, or =: 7,203,000 ___ 7,230,000

**Stretch:**
1. The population of a country is about 134 million, 800 thousand, 5 hundred. Write this in standard form.
2. A number has a 6 in the ten-millions place, a 0 in the millions place, a 4 in the hundred-thousands place, and zeros elsewhere. Write this number in standard form, expanded form, and word form.
3. List all the ways you could write the value of the 5 in 5,400,000: as a digit, as a word, and in expanded form.

**Challenge:**
1. Which digit in 370,208,000 has a value of 200,000? What is the value of the digit to its left? How many times larger is it?
2. I am a nine-digit number. My millions digit is twice my ones digit. My thousands digit is the same as my ones digit. All other digits are 0. My ones digit is 3. What number am I?
3. True or false: In any number, the digit in the ten-millions place always has a value exactly 10 times the value of the digit in the millions place. Explain your reasoning.

### Interleaved Review
*(From Units 1-4)*

**Review 1 (Unit 4 — Decimals):** Calculate 12.5 × 10. Then calculate 12.5 × 100. Describe what is happening to the digits each time.
- **Answer:** 12.5 × 10 = 125; 12.5 × 100 = 1,250. Each multiplication by 10 shifts all digits one place to the left, making the number 10 times larger.

**Review 2 (Unit 1 — Fractions):** Calculate 3/4 + 5/6.
- **Answer:** LCD = 12; 9/12 + 10/12 = 19/12 = 1 7/12

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. What is the value of the digit 7 in 270,500,000?
2. Write 50,006,030 in word form.
3. Is 803,400,000 greater than or less than 830,400,000? How do you know?

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the place-value chart and rebuild it from ones upward, having the student name each place and its value.
- Focus particularly on the period structure: show that each period reads like a three-digit number before the period name is added.
- Practice just reading numbers aloud from the chart before attempting written forms.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Proceed to Lesson 2 (Place Value to Billions).
- Challenge: Ask the student to find a real-world number greater than 100,000,000 (a country's population, a sports salary total, a national statistic) and write it in standard, expanded, and word form.

## Extension (Optional)
**Large-Number Investigation:** Look up the following and write each in standard form, then write the word form and expanded form.
- The approximate area of the Sahara Desert in square miles (about 3,552,000).
- The approximate distance from Earth to the Moon in miles (about 238,855 miles — this one is in the hundred-thousands!).
- The approximate number of books in the U.S. Library of Congress (about 17,000,000).

After writing each, put them in order from least to greatest. Which measurement surprised you?

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- This lesson is primarily review for students who completed Grade 4 successfully. Treat it as a confidence-building session rather than new instruction.
- If the student makes errors on the warm-up, slow down and spend more time in guided practice. Do not rush to independent practice if foundational gaps remain.
- The color-coding strategy (one color per period) is very effective at helping students see the repeating structure. Use it consistently in this lesson and Lesson 2.
- When students write expanded form, remind them to skip zero-valued digits (e.g., the expanded form of 5,003,000 is 5,000,000 + 3,000, not 5,000,000 + 0 + 0 + 3,000 + 0 + 0 + 0).
- The interleaved review problem connects to what is coming in Lessons 5-7 of this unit. Note the student's response — if they can already describe the digit-shift pattern fluently, the upcoming lessons will build on that understanding more quickly.

### Independent Practice — Challenge
1. The digit 2 has a value of 200,000 (it is in the hundred-thousands place). The digit to its left is 0 in the millions place, with a value of 0. (Or, the digit two places to its left is the tens place — but that is 0 also. The question intends the next place left: millions place = 0.) Note: accept any correct identification. If the student identifies the digit 0 to the immediate left (millions), its value is 0, and 0 is not 10 times more — this shows that zero digits do not maintain the ×10 relationship in the usual sense.
2. My ones digit is 3. My millions digit = 2 × 3 = 6. My thousands digit = 3. All others are 0. The number is 306,003,003.
3. False. The statement would be true only if both digits are non-zero. Consider 370,208,000: the ten-millions digit is 7 (value 70,000,000) and the millions digit is 0 (value 0). The value 70,000,000 is not 10 times 0. The place values themselves satisfy the 10× relationship, but the value of a specific digit depends on what digit is there — a 0 digit has value 0 regardless of its place.
