## Answer Key

### Section 1: Place Value to Billions

**1.** 7,040,200,000
- Billions: 007 → seven billion
- Millions: 040 → forty million
- Thousands: 200 → two hundred thousand
- Ones: 000 → (none)
- **Word form: seven billion, forty million, two hundred thousand**
(2 points)

**2.** "twenty-five billion, three million, sixty"
- Billions: 025 → 025,___,___,___
- Millions: 003 → 025,003,___,___
- Thousands: 000 → 025,003,000,___
- Ones: 060 → 025,003,000,060
- **Standard form: 25,003,000,060**
(2 points)

**3.** 381,000,000,000
- The 8 is in the ten-billions place.
- **Value: 80,000,000,000 (eighty billion)**
(2 points)

**4.** 4,005,000,700
- **Expanded form: 4,000,000,000 + 5,000,000 + 700**
(Accept also: 4 × 10⁹ + 5 × 10⁶ + 7 × 10²)
(2 points)

**5.** Billions period: all 6 billion. Hundred-millions: 0 vs. 4 vs. 0. The number with 4 in hundred-millions (6,400,000,000) is greatest. Remaining two: ten-millions: 4 vs. 0 → 6,040,000,000 > 6,004,000,000.
- **Greatest to least: 6,400,000,000 > 6,040,000,000 > 6,004,000,000**
(2 points)

### Section 2: Exponents and Powers of 10

**6.** 4³ = 4 × 4 × 4 = **64**
(2 points)

**7.** 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = **32**
(2 points)

**8.** 1,000,000 = **10⁶**
(1 point)

**9.** 10⁸ = **100,000,000**
(1 point)

**10.** The student multiplied 3 × 4 = 12 instead of computing 3 × 3 × 3 × 3. The exponent 4 tells how many times 3 is used as a factor, not how many times to multiply 3 by 4. **3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81.**
(2 points: 1 for error identification, 1 for correct value 81)

**11.** 10⁵ = 100,000. **The hundred-thousands place.**
(2 points)

### Section 3: Multiplying by Powers of 10

**12.** 57 × 1,000 = **57,000**
(1 point)

**13.** 0.48 × 100 = **48**
(1 point)

**14.** 0.006 × 10⁴ = 0.006 × 10,000. Shift 4 left: **60**
(2 points)

**15.** 3.5 × ___ = 350,000. Shift 3.5 left until it reaches 350,000: 3.5 → 35 → 350 → 3,500 → 35,000 → 350,000 (5 places). **Power of 10: 10⁵. Standard form: 100,000.**
(2 points: 1 for 10⁵, 1 for 100,000)

**16.** When you multiply by 10³, every digit in the number shifts three places to the left on the place-value chart. Each digit takes on a place value 1,000 times greater than its original place, and three placeholder zeros are added to fill the three vacated place positions.
(2 points: 1 for "every digit shifts three places left," 1 for mention of placeholder zeros or value increase)

**17.** 275 × 10,000 = 275 × 10⁴ = **2,750,000 bolts**.
(2 points: 1 for setup 275 × 10,000 or 275 × 10⁴, 1 for correct answer)

---

### Section 4: Dividing by Powers of 10

**18.** 9,000 ÷ 1,000 = **9**
(1 point)

**19.** 64,000 ÷ 10⁴ = 64,000 ÷ 10,000 = **6.4**
(1 point)

**20.** 0.5 ÷ 100 = **0.005**
(Shift 2 right: 0.5 → 0.05 → 0.005)
(2 points)

**21.** 8,400 ÷ ___ = 0.84. Shift 8,400 right by how many places to reach 0.84? The 8 goes from thousands to tenths = 4 places right; the 4 goes from hundreds to hundredths = 4 places right. So divide by 10⁴ = **10,000**.
Check: 0.84 × 10,000 = 8,400 ✓
(2 points)

**22.** 7.2 ÷ 1,000 = **0.0072 grams**. Verification: 0.0072 × 1,000 = 7.2 ✓
(2 points: 1 for correct division result, 1 for correct multiplication verification)

**23.** 350 ÷ 10 = **35**; 350 ÷ 100 = **3.5**; 350 ÷ 1,000 = **0.35**.
Pattern: Each time we divide by another factor of 10, every digit shifts one more place to the right (and the number becomes 10 times smaller).
(2 points: 1 for all three correct answers, 1 for clear pattern description)

### Section 5: Scientific Notation

**24.** 84,000,000. Coefficient: 8.4. Places: decimal moves 7 left from end of number. **8.4 × 10⁷**
(2 points)

**25.** 1.06 × 10⁵. Move decimal 5 right: 1.06 → 106,000. **106,000**
(2 points)

**26.** 5,000,000,000 = 5 × 10⁹ (coefficient is 5.0, which equals 5). **5 × 10⁹**
(2 points)

**27.** 45 × 10⁶ is not in correct scientific notation because the coefficient 45 is not less than 10 (the coefficient must be at least 1 and less than 10). Correct form: 45 × 10⁶ = 4.5 × 10 × 10⁶ = **4.5 × 10⁷**.
(2 points: 1 for explanation of why 45 violates the rule, 1 for correct rewrite 4.5 × 10⁷)

**28.** 1.496 × 10⁸ = 1.496 × 100,000,000 = 149,600,000 ≈ 150,000,000. 150,000,000 = 150 million. **Yes, the scientist is correct** (rounding 149.6 million to 150 million is a reasonable approximation).
(2 points: 1 for correct conversion showing ≈ 149,600,000, 1 for confirming the scientist's statement is correct)

### Section 6: Spaced Review — Fractions and Decimals

**29.** 2/3 + 3/4. LCD = 12. 8/12 + 9/12 = **17/12 = 1 5/12**
(2 points)

**30.** 5/6 × 3/5 = 15/30 = **1/2**
(2 points)

**31.** 4 ÷ 1/5 = 4 × 5/1 = **20**
(Ask: "How many fifths are in 4 wholes?" 5 fifths per whole × 4 wholes = 20.)

**32.** 3.6 × 0.04. Ignore decimals: 36 × 4 = 144. Count decimal places: 1 + 2 = 3. **0.144**

## Mastery Criteria

A student demonstrates mastery of this unit when they can:

- Read and write numbers through the billions in standard, expanded, and word form.
- Evaluate exponential expressions with whole-number bases.
- Write powers of 10 from 10⁰ to 10⁹ and match each to a place on the place-value chart.
- Multiply and divide whole numbers and decimals by powers of 10, explaining the digit shift.
- Convert numbers between standard form and scientific notation.
- Apply these skills to multi-step real-world problems.
- Explain their reasoning, not just produce correct answers.

## Partial Credit Guidelines

- **Computation problems (Sections 1, 3, 4):** Award 1 point for correct process with a minor arithmetic error if the method is clearly shown.
- **Exponent problems (Section 2):** 4³ and 2⁵ — award 1 point if the student correctly writes out the repeated multiplication but makes an arithmetic error. Full credit requires the correct final value.
- **Scientific notation (Section 5):** Award 1 point per conversion for correct process (correct coefficient OR correct exponent) even if one element has a small error.
- **Explanation problems (Problems 10, 16, 23, 27):** Award 1 point for the mathematical content even if the written explanation is incomplete; full credit requires both correctness and clarity.
- **Word problems (Problems 17, 22, 28):** Award credit for each correct intermediate step independently. A student who sets up correctly but makes an arithmetic error should receive setup credit.
- **Section 6 (Spaced Review):** Apply the same standards as above. These problems count toward the total — mastery requires fluency in prior units as well as the current unit.

## Reteaching Recommendations

### If the student struggles with Section 1 (Place Value to Billions):
- Revisit Lessons 1-2. Use the color-coded period chart to rebuild the billions period as a natural extension of millions.
- Practice reading numbers aloud using the period-by-period strategy.
- Focus on correctly identifying place names (confusing ten-millions with hundred-millions is common).

### If the student struggles with Section 2 (Exponents):
- Revisit Lesson 3. Return to the bacteria-doubling story. Have the student write out every factor before condensing to exponent notation.
- Do daily practice of 5 evaluations (mix of bases and exponents) until automatic.
- The 3⁴ = 12 error (confusing exponent with multiplication) must be fully addressed before moving on. Return to the definition at the start of every session.

### If the student struggles with Section 3 (Multiplying by Powers of 10):
- Revisit Lessons 5-6. Use the place-value chart with physical tracing for every problem until the pattern is automatic.
- If errors are with whole numbers: focus on Lesson 5.
- If errors are with decimals: focus on Lesson 6. Use the decimal-column extension of the chart.

### If the student struggles with Section 4 (Dividing by Powers of 10):
- Revisit Lesson 7. Emphasize the inverse relationship: division undoes multiplication. Have the student verify every division with inverse multiplication.
- Common error: shifting in the wrong direction. Establish the rule: dividing makes numbers smaller, so digits move right.

### If the student struggles with Section 5 (Scientific Notation):
- Revisit Lesson 9. Focus on the coefficient rule (1 ≤ a < 10) as a non-negotiable constraint.
- Practice the two-step conversion: (1) write coefficient, (2) count the shift.
- Do not proceed until the student can fluently convert in both directions.

### If the student struggles with Section 6 (Spaced Review):
- Fraction errors: revisit relevant lessons from Units 1-3 as needed.
- Decimal errors: revisit relevant lessons from Unit 4 as needed.
- These are not new skills — a struggle here indicates retention gaps from earlier units, not a failure to understand the current unit. Schedule targeted review sessions.

## Common Error Patterns to Watch

- **Confusing period names** (saying "forty million" when the number is in the billions period): Use the chart consistently; have the student name each period aloud before reading.
- **Exponent = multiplication** (3⁴ = 12 error): Address at every exponent problem. Require writing out all factors.
- **Wrong direction when multiplying/dividing** (shifting right for multiplication instead of left): The inverse check (multiply ÷ verify = check) is the fastest fix.
- **Coefficient violation in scientific notation** (50 × 10³): The student found significant digits but did not adjust the exponent. Remind: every shift of the coefficient must be compensated in the exponent.
- **Placeholder zeros forgotten** (writing 4.7 × 10⁴ = 47,000 as 4700 or 470): Count the decimal shift explicitly — 4 places from 4.7 requires two zeros added.
