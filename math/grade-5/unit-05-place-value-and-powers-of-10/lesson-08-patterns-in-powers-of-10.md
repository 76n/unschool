# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 5, Lesson 8

## Topic
Patterns in Powers of 10 (×10 Shifts Left, ÷10 Shifts Right — A Unified Framework)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Describe the unified pattern for multiplication and division by powers of 10: ×10 shifts each digit one place left (higher value), ÷10 shifts each digit one place right (lower value).
- Apply this pattern fluently to problems combining multiplication and division by powers of 10.
- Use the extended place-value chart (billions through thousandths) as a single tool for all powers-of-10 operations.
- Solve multi-step problems that require choosing whether to multiply or divide by a power of 10.
- Verify results using the inverse relationship and estimation.
- Make connections between the place-value pattern and real-world scales (from billionths to billions).

## Prerequisites
- Fluency multiplying whole numbers by powers of 10 (Lesson 5).
- Fluency multiplying decimals by powers of 10 (Lesson 6).
- Fluency dividing whole numbers and decimals by powers of 10 (Lesson 7).
- Understanding of the extended place-value chart and the period structure (Lessons 1-4).

## Materials Needed
- Extended place-value chart on whiteboard (billions through thousandths), spanning the full available board space
- Two different colors of chalk or marker for the direction arrows (e.g., red for ×10 arrows pointing left, blue for ÷10 arrows pointing right)
- Whiteboard and markers
- Paper and pencil
- Index cards for exit ticket

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review and diagnose fluency with multiplication and division by powers of 10 before synthesizing.

Write on the board — give the student 3 minutes:
1. 47 × 1,000 = ___
2. 0.006 × 10,000 = ___
3. 3,500 ÷ 100 = ___
4. 0.4 ÷ 1,000 = ___
5. Fill in the blank: ___ × 100 = 3,600

Discuss any errors. Use the results as diagnostic information.

Say: "Today we bring everything together. Instead of treating multiplication and division by powers of 10 as two separate skills, we see them as one unified pattern — the place-value sliding system."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Present the unified model: the place-value chart as a two-way system, where ×10 = shift left and ÷10 = shift right, and these operations are perfectly symmetric.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Symmetric pattern:** A pattern that works the same way in both directions; the ×10 and ÷10 shifts are symmetric — each undoes the other.
- **Chain of operations:** A sequence of multiplications and divisions by powers of 10 that can be simplified by combining the total shift.
- **Net shift:** The result of combining multiple ×10 and ÷10 operations (e.g., ×100 then ÷10 = ×10 net).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Draw the complete unified reference chart.**

Draw the full chart with arrows in both directions:

```
← × 10 (shift left, value increases) ←←←←←←←←←←
←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←

Billions | H-Mill | T-Mill | Mills | H-Thou | T-Thou | Thou | Hund | Tens | Ones | . | Tenths | Hundredths | Thousandths
  10⁹   |  10⁸   |  10⁷  |  10⁶  |  10⁵   |  10⁴  |  10³ |  10² |  10¹ |  10⁰ | . |  10⁻¹  |    10⁻²    |    10⁻³

→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→→
→ ÷ 10 (shift right, value decreases) →
```

(Note to facilitator: the 10⁻¹, 10⁻², 10⁻³ notation is presented informally here — we will use it only to show the visual pattern. Students will formalize negative exponents in Grade 6.)

Say: "The arrows show two directions. Every × 10 moves you one step left. Every ÷ 10 moves you one step right. Moving left × 10 times is × 10ⁿ. Moving right n times is ÷ 10ⁿ."

**Step 2 — Use the chart to solve a chain problem.**

Write: Start with 0.003. Multiply by 10 five times.

Count 5 steps left: 0.003 → 0.03 → 0.3 → 3 → 30 → 300

Result: 0.003 × 10⁵ = 300

Write: Start with 70,000. Divide by 10 seven times.

Count 7 steps right: 70,000 → 7,000 → 700 → 70 → 7 → 0.7 → 0.07 → 0.007

Result: 70,000 ÷ 10⁷ = 0.007

**Step 3 — Net shift: combining multiplication and division.**

Write: 3.5 × 10,000 ÷ 100

Instead of computing step by step, combine: × 10,000 = +4 places left; ÷ 100 = 2 places right. Net: 4 − 2 = 2 places left.

3.5 shifted 2 left = 350

Verify: 3.5 × 10,000 = 35,000; 35,000 ÷ 100 = 350 ✓

**Step 4 — The symmetry principle.**

Say: "The ×10 and ÷10 operations are perfectly symmetric. If you multiply by 10³ and then divide by 10³, you get back exactly where you started. The operations cancel."

Write: 4.7 × 1,000 ÷ 1,000 = 4.7 (unchanged)

Write: 0.056 ÷ 10 × 10 = 0.056 (unchanged)

Say: "This symmetry is why we can always verify any multiplication by doing the inverse division."

**Step 5 — Reading patterns from the chart.**

Write the following and ask the student to complete each row:

| Starting number | Operation | Result |
|----------------|-----------|--------|
| 5 | × 10² | 500 |
| 500 | ÷ 10⁴ | 0.05 |
| 0.05 | × 10³ | 50 |
| 50 | ÷ 10 | 5 |

Say: "Look at the starting and ending values. After all four operations, we ended up at 5 — right where we started. The operations balanced out. (Net: +2 −4 +3 −1 = 0 shifts.)"

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Calculate 6.2 × 10³ ÷ 10.
- Net shift: 3 left − 1 right = 2 left.
- 6.2 shifted 2 left = 620.
- Answer: 620

**Example B:** A conversion: 4,500 mm = ___ m. (1 m = 1,000 mm, so divide by 1,000.)
- 4,500 ÷ 1,000 = 4.5 m

**Example C:** Error analysis: A student says "0.08 × 10² ÷ 10 = 8 ÷ 10 = 0.8." Is this correct?
- Step 1: 0.08 × 100 = 8. ✓
- Step 2: 8 ÷ 10 = 0.8. ✓
- The student's answer is correct! The process is valid.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Work together.

1. Use the chart to find: 0.004 × 10⁴.
   - **Answer:** 40 (shift 4 left)

2. Use the chart to find: 900,000 ÷ 10⁵.
   - **Answer:** 9

3. Calculate the net shift and answer: 7 × 10³ ÷ 10².
   - Net: 3 left − 2 right = 1 left. 7 × 10 = 70.
   - **Answer:** 70

4. Fill in the blank: 0.05 × ___ = 5,000.
   - We need to shift 0.05 left by how many places to reach 5,000?
   - 0.05 has a 5 in the hundredths place. 5,000 has a 5 in the thousands place. Count: hundredths → tenths → ones → tens → hundreds → thousands = 5 places left.
   - **Answer:** 0.05 × 10⁵ = 5,000

5. Verify: 0.05 × 100,000 = 5,000 ✓

**Facilitator tip:** For problem 4, count the shifts on the chart together, pointing to each column. This builds the habit of using the chart as a reference tool even for verification.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Use the place-value chart to complete this table:

   | Number | Operation | Result |
   |--------|-----------|--------|
   | 0.006 | × 10⁴ | ___ |
   | 7,000 | ÷ 10³ | ___ |
   | 0.45 | × 10² | ___ |
   | 300 | ÷ 10⁴ | ___ |

2. Calculate the net shift: 8 × 100 ÷ 1,000 = ___
3. Fill in the blank: 0.003 × ___ = 3,000.

**Stretch:**
1. Start with 1. Multiply by 10 eight times. What is the result? Write it as a power of 10 and in standard form.
2. Start with 1,000,000. Divide by 10 nine times. What is the result?
3. Complete the chain: 0.007 × 10⁴ ÷ 10² × 10 ÷ 10³. Show net shift, then compute.
4. A recipe uses 0.0025 kg of a rare spice. The chef makes 100 servings per day and operates the restaurant for 1,000 days. How many kg of the spice are needed in total? Write as powers-of-10 multiplication.

**Challenge:**
1. Starting from 1, what single operation could you perform to land on 1,000,000,000? Express as a multiplication by a power of 10.
2. A quantity starts at 5 × 10⁴. After applying a series of three operations (all multiplications or divisions by powers of 10), it ends up as 5 × 10⁰ = 5. Write a set of three operations that produces this result. Can you find more than one set?
3. The table below shows incomplete data. Fill in all missing values:

   | Step | Operation | Value Before | Value After |
   |------|-----------|-------------|-------------|
   | 1 | × 10² | ___ | 4,500 |
   | 2 | ÷ 10³ | 4,500 | ___ |
   | 3 | × 10 | ___ | 0.45 |
   | 4 | ÷ ___ | 0.45 | 0.0045 |

### Interleaved Review
*(From Units 1-4)*

**Review 1 (Unit 2 — Multiplying Fractions):** Calculate 2 1/2 × 1 3/4. Show as improper fractions.
- **Answer:** 5/2 × 7/4 = 35/8 = 4 3/8

**Review 2 (Unit 4 — Decimals):** Calculate 0.8 × 0.9. Estimate first.
- **Answer:** Estimate: 1 × 1 = 1. Exact: 8 × 9 = 72; two decimal places → 0.72

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. Complete: 0.009 × 10³ = ___
2. Complete: 8,000,000 ÷ 10⁵ = ___
3. Describe the unified pattern for multiplying and dividing by powers of 10 in one or two sentences.

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct (including a coherent description in question 3).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- The student needs more isolated practice before synthesis. Return to Lesson 5 (multiply whole numbers) or Lesson 7 (divide) as needed based on which direction causes errors.
- Practice each direction separately with 5-6 problems before attempting net-shift problems.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Proceed to Lesson 9 (Introduction to Scientific Notation).
- Preview: "Scientists who work with very large numbers — like the distance from Earth to distant stars, or the number of atoms in a gram of carbon — need a notation that uses powers of 10 in a standardized way. That notation is called scientific notation, and you are now ready to learn it."

## Extension (Optional)
**Powers of 10 as Scale Factors:**

In photography and microscopy, "scale" describes how much bigger or smaller a measurement is compared to the real size. Scales are often powers of 10.

1. A microscope magnifies 400×. If a cell appears 2 cm wide under the microscope, how wide is the actual cell? (Divide by 400 — not a power of 10, but estimate: 400 ≈ 10²·⁶. What is 2 ÷ 400 exactly?)
   - 2 ÷ 400 = 0.005 cm actual width

2. A map has a scale of 1 cm = 100 km = 100,000 m = 10⁵ m. On the map, two cities are 3.4 cm apart. What is their actual distance in meters?
   - 3.4 × 10⁵ = 340,000 m

3. A photograph is enlarged to 1,000 times actual size. A detail in the photo measures 8 cm. What is the actual size?
   - 8 ÷ 1,000 = 0.008 cm

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- This lesson is a synthesis lesson. Its purpose is consolidation, not new content. Students who mastered Lessons 5-7 will move through this quickly and confidently.
- The "net shift" concept (combining ×10ⁿ and ÷10ᵐ into a single net operation) is an optional extension for students who are ready — it previews the exponent laws (10ⁿ ÷ 10ᵐ = 10^(n-m)) that will be formalized in Grade 6.
- The two-directional chart is the key visual for this lesson. Leave it on the board and reference it throughout independent practice.
- If exit ticket results show that errors cluster in one direction (students are fine multiplying but make errors dividing, or vice versa), address that specific direction before proceeding to scientific notation. Scientific notation requires correct application of both directions.
- The extension (scale factors) connects beautifully to photography, maps, and microscopy — real-world contexts where students see the relevance of powers-of-10 scaling.
