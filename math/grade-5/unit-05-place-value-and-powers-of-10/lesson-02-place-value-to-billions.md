# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 5, Lesson 2

## Topic
Place Value to Billions (Reading, Writing, and Comparing Very Large Numbers)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Name all place values from the ones place through the hundred-billions place.
- Identify the billions period as the next repeating period after millions, following the same hundreds-tens-ones pattern.
- Read and write whole numbers through the hundred billions in standard form, expanded form, and word form.
- State the value of any digit in a number through the hundred billions.
- Compare and order multi-digit numbers through the hundred billions.
- Connect billion-scale numbers to real-world contexts (world population, distances, national debt).

## Prerequisites
- Mastery of place values through the hundred millions (Lesson 1).
- Understanding of the period structure (ones, thousands, millions) and the repeating hundreds-tens-ones pattern.
- Ability to read and write numbers in standard, expanded, and word form through 999,999,999.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

Write on the board:

2. What is the value of the 3 in 438,000,000?

Give the student 2 minutes, then discuss.

Say: "You are solid on millions. Today we go one step further — into the billions. Billions appear constantly in real life: population counts, distances in space, money amounts. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to read and write any number up to a hundred billion."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Extend the place-value chart into the billions period, connect to real-world numbers, and practice all three forms.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Billion:** 1,000,000,000 — one thousand millions; written as a 1 followed by 9 zeros.
- **Billions period:** The fourth period in a large number (billions, ten-billions, hundred-billions), occupying digits 10–12 from the right.
- **Hundred-billions:** The place value 100,000,000,000 — the largest place covered in this unit.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Extend the place-value chart.**

Add the billions period to the chart drawn in Lesson 1:

```
Billions Period           | Millions Period          | Thousands Period    | Ones Period
Hundred-B | Ten-B | Billions | Hundred-M | Ten-M | Millions | Hund-T | Ten-T | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones
100B       | 10B   | 1B       | 100M      | 10M   | 1M       | 100K   | 10K   | 1K        | 100      | 10   | 1
```

Color-code the billions period in a new color (green).

Say: "Notice the billions period is the same structure as every other period: hundreds, tens, ones — then the period name. There is no mystery here. It is just the next group of three."

Ask: "If I keep going past hundred-billions, what comes next?" (Answer: the trillions period — trillions, ten-trillions, hundred-trillions. We will not focus on trillions today, but the pattern continues.)

**Step 2 — Real-world billion-scale numbers.**

Write on the board:
- World population: approximately 8,000,000,000

Say: "This is 8 billion. The 8 is in the billions place. There are nine zeros after it. Using our period system: 8 in the billions period, then three periods of zeros."

Write: 8,000,000,000
- Billions: 008 → eight billion
- Millions: 000 → (no millions)
- Thousands: 000 → (no thousands)
- Ones: 000 → (no ones)

Read: eight billion.

Write: 35,000,000,000 (approximate U.S. national debt in dollars at time of writing this lesson)
- Billions: 035 → thirty-five billion

Write: 384,000 (Earth-Moon distance in km) vs. 150,000,000 (Earth-Sun distance in km) vs. 40,000,000,000,000 (Proxima Centauri distance in km).

Say: "That last one — 40 trillion kilometers — is beyond the billions! Astronomers need even bigger numbers. But let us focus on billions today."

**Step 3 — Reading billion-scale numbers.**

Write: 6,034,200,000

Period-by-period:
- Billions: 006 → six billion
- Millions: 034 → thirty-four million
- Thousands: 200 → two hundred thousand
- Ones: 000 → (nothing)

Read: six billion, thirty-four million, two hundred thousand.

**Step 4 — Expanded form with billions.**

Write: 6,034,200,000

Expanded form: 6,000,000,000 + 30,000,000 + 4,000,000 + 200,000

**Step 5 — Comparing numbers with billions.**

Write: 7,500,000,000 ___ 7,050,000,000

- Billions period: both have 7 billion — equal.
- Millions period: 500 vs. 050. Hundreds of millions: 5 vs. 0. Since 5 > 0, the first number is greater.
- 7,500,000,000 > 7,050,000,000

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Write 24,500,000,000 in word form.
- Billions: 024 → twenty-four billion
- Millions: 500 → five hundred million
- Thousands: 000 → (nothing)
- Ones: 000 → (nothing)
- Word form: twenty-four billion, five hundred million

**Example B:** Write in standard form: "one hundred three billion, forty million, twelve thousand, seven."
- Billions: 103 → 103,___,___,___
- Millions: 040 → 103,040,___,___
- Thousands: 012 → 103,040,012,___
- Ones: 007 → 103,040,012,007
- Standard form: 103,040,012,007

**Example C:** What is the value of the 5 in 51,000,000,000?
- The 5 is in the ten-billions place.
- Its value is 50,000,000,000 (50 billion).

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Work together using the place-value chart.

1. Write 9,300,000,000 in word form.
   - **Answer:** nine billion, three hundred million

2. Write in standard form: "twelve billion, six hundred thousand."
   - **Answer:** 12,000,600,000

3. What is the value of the 8 in 87,000,000,000?
   - **Answer:** 80,000,000,000 (eighty billion; the 8 is in the ten-billions place)

4. Write the expanded form of 5,040,200,000.
   - **Answer:** 5,000,000,000 + 40,000,000 + 200,000

5. Compare: 3,400,000,000 ___ 3,040,000,000
   - **Answer:** 3,400,000,000 > 3,040,000,000 (hundred-millions: 4 > 0)

**Facilitator tip:** Students often confuse "billion" and "million" when reading numbers with both. Have the student use a finger to cover each period in turn and read only that period's value, then lift the finger and add the period name.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Write 15,000,300,000 in word form.
2. Write in standard form: "two billion, four million, fifty thousand."
3. What is the value of the digit 6 in 6,900,000,000?
4. Write the expanded form of 30,005,000,100.
5. Order from least to greatest: 4,500,000,000 and 4,050,000,000 and 4,005,000,000.

**Stretch:**
1. The distance from Earth to Pluto is approximately 5,900,000,000 kilometers. Write this in word form and expanded form.
2. Country A has a population of 1,418,000,000. Country B has a population of 1,344,000,000. Which country has a larger population? By how much?
3. A number has a 2 in the hundred-billions place, a 5 in the billions place, a 7 in the ten-millions place, and zeros everywhere else. Write the number in standard form and word form.

**Challenge:**
1. The speed of light is approximately 300,000,000 meters per second. Light travels for 8 minutes to reach Earth from the Sun. Without calculating, is the Earth-Sun distance closer to 100 million km, 100 billion km, or 1 trillion km? Explain how you can tell using place value reasoning. (Note: you are reasoning about order of magnitude, not computing exactly.)
2. A country's GDP is $18,620,000,000,000. Write this in word form. This number has 14 digits — what is the largest period it reaches? What is the name of that place value?
3. Arrange these three numbers in order from greatest to least and explain how you compared them: 99,999,000,000; 100,000,000,000; 100,000,000,001.

### Interleaved Review
*(From Units 1-4)*

**Review 1 (Unit 3 — Dividing Fractions):** Calculate 6 ÷ 1/3. Draw a model if it helps.
- **Answer:** 6 ÷ 1/3 = 6 × 3 = 18 (there are 18 one-thirds in 6 wholes)

**Review 2 (Unit 4 — Decimals):** A book costs $12.75. A student buys 4 copies. What is the total cost?

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. Write 7,004,000,000 in word form.
2. What is the value of the 3 in 300,000,000,000?
3. Compare: 45,000,000,000 ___ 45,000,000,001. Write <, >, or =.

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the place-value chart. Have the student place each digit of a number in the chart before reading it.
- Focus on the period-reading strategy: read each period separately (e.g., "24 billion, 500 million") before combining.
- Practice with just two-period numbers (billions and ones; billions and millions) before combining all four periods.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Proceed to Lesson 3 (Understanding Exponents).
- Ask the student: "We need 9 zeros to write 1 billion (1,000,000,000). Is there a shorter way to write '10 multiplied by itself 9 times'? We will find out next lesson."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- The most important conceptual move in this lesson is reinforcing that the billions period is not unique or mysterious — it follows the same pattern as every other period. Students who see this connection understand the infinite extendability of the number system.
- Many 10-11 year olds have heard the word "billion" but have little sense of its magnitude relative to million. A useful illustration: a million seconds is about 11.5 days; a billion seconds is about 31.7 years. This fact is memorable and reinforces why the distinction matters.
- The expanded form of billion-scale numbers with many zero-value periods can be confusing. Remind students that they only write terms for non-zero digits. Skip zeros in expanded form.
- The challenge comparison problem (99,999,000,000 vs. 100,000,000,000 vs. 100,000,000,001) is excellent for understanding that 9s in a lower period do not make a number larger than a 1 in a higher period.

### Independent Practice — Challenge
1. The speed of light is 300,000,000 m/s = 3 × 10⁸ m/s. In 8 minutes (480 seconds), light travels approximately 480 × 300,000,000 = 144,000,000,000 m ≈ 144 billion meters = 144 million km ≈ 150 million km. So the Earth-Sun distance is closest to 100 million km (it is about 150 million km, which is in the hundreds-of-millions range). The key place-value reasoning: 150 million km is a 9-digit number in km, which is in the millions period — not the billions.
2. $18,620,000,000,000. The number has digits in the trillions period (specifically, 18 trillion 620 billion). Word form: eighteen trillion, six hundred twenty billion. The largest place value is the ten-trillions place (the 1 in 18 trillion is in the ten-trillions place).
3. Greatest to least: 100,000,000,001 > 100,000,000,000 > 99,999,000,000. Explanation: The first two numbers both have 100 in the billions period; comparing the ones period, 000,000,001 > 000,000,000. The third number has only 99 in the billions period, so it is smaller than any number with 100 in the billions period.
