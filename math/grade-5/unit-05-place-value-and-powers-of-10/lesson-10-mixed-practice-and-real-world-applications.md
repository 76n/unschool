# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 5, Lesson 10

## Topic
Mixed Practice and Real-World Applications (Astronomy, Population, Microscopic Measurements)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Apply all unit skills (place value, exponents, powers of 10, multiplying/dividing by powers of 10, and scientific notation) in a single integrated lesson.
- Choose the appropriate skill or tool for each problem type (e.g., recognizing when scientific notation is the natural form for an answer, or when to multiply vs. divide by a power of 10).
- Solve multi-step real-world problems drawn from astronomy, population data, and biology.
- Identify and correct errors in others' work (error analysis).
- Use estimation to check the reasonableness of answers involving very large or very small numbers.

## Prerequisites
- Mastery of all prior lessons in this unit (Lessons 1-9).
- Ability to read and write numbers through billions (Lessons 1-2).
- Ability to evaluate exponents (Lesson 3).
- Ability to convert between powers of 10 and standard form (Lesson 4).
- Fluency multiplying and dividing by powers of 10 (Lessons 5-7).
- Unified place-value model (Lesson 8).
- Ability to convert to/from scientific notation (Lesson 9).

**Purpose:** Quick fluency check across all unit skills.

2. Write 0.00008 × 10⁵.
3. What is the value of 6² + 10³?

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model the problem-solving process for multi-step real-world problems involving place value and powers of 10.

### Key Vocabulary
*(Review vocabulary from earlier lessons; no new vocabulary in this lesson)*
- **Light-year:** The distance light travels in one year — approximately 9.461 × 10¹² km. (Useful context; students do not need to memorize this.)
- **Astronomical Unit (AU):** The average distance from Earth to the Sun — approximately 1.5 × 10⁸ km. (Useful context.)

### Instruction Notes

**Model Problem 1 — Astronomy: Comparing Distances**

Say: "Astronomers measure distances in the solar system. Let us work with some real values."

Present: "The Sun has a diameter of about 1,392,700 km. Earth has a diameter of about 12,742 km."

Questions:
a. Write each diameter in scientific notation.
b. About how many times larger is the Sun's diameter than Earth's?

Work through step by step:
a. Sun: 1,392,700 ≈ 1.4 × 10⁶ km (rounding 1.3927 to 1.4 for simplicity)
   Earth: 12,742 ≈ 1.27 × 10⁴ km (or exactly 1.2742 × 10⁴)

b. Compare the scales: Sun is 10⁶, Earth is 10⁴. The ratio of powers: 10⁶ ÷ 10⁴ = 10² = 100. So the Sun's diameter is approximately 100 times Earth's diameter.

(More precisely: 1,392,700 ÷ 12,742 ≈ 109. So about 109 times larger. The powers-of-10 estimate gave 100 — a good order-of-magnitude estimate.)

Say: "We used powers of 10 to estimate without a calculator. The Sun is about 100 times wider than Earth."

**Model Problem 2 — Population: Comparing Counts**

Present: "In 2024, the world population was approximately 8.1 × 10⁹ people. The population of one city is approximately 3.8 × 10⁶ people."

Questions:
a. Write each in standard form.
b. How many times larger is the world population than the city population?
c. If each person in the world used 100 liters of water per day, how many liters total per day?

Work through:
a. World: 8.1 × 10⁹ = 8,100,000,000. City: 3.8 × 10⁶ = 3,800,000.
b. 8.1 × 10⁹ ÷ 3.8 × 10⁶. Focus on the powers of 10: 10⁹ ÷ 10⁶ = 10³ = 1,000. And 8.1 ÷ 3.8 ≈ 2. So about 2,000 times larger.
c. 8.1 × 10⁹ × 100 = 8.1 × 10⁹ × 10² = 8.1 × 10¹¹ liters per day.

Say: "Scientific notation makes step c simple. Instead of multiplying billions by hundreds, we just add exponents: 10⁹ × 10² = 10¹¹."

**Model Problem 3 — Error Analysis**

Write: "A student converts 6.3 × 10⁵ to standard form and writes 6,300. Is this correct?"

Work through:
- 6.3 × 10⁵ = 6.3 × 100,000. The decimal moves 5 places right: 6.3 → 630,000.
- The correct standard form is 630,000, not 6,300.
- The student only moved the decimal 2 places (getting 630), then stopped — or confused 10⁵ with 10² = 100.

Say: "Always count the exponent: 5 places right from 6.3 gives 6.30000, which is 630,000."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Work together on these real-world problems.

1. **Astronomy:** Saturn is approximately 1,432,000,000 km from the Sun. Write this in scientific notation, then state which place on the place-value chart the leading digit (1) occupies.
   - **Answer:** 1.432 × 10⁹ km. The leading digit 1 is in the billions place (10⁹).

2. **Population:** A country has a population of 4.2 × 10⁷ people. Its area is approximately 3.5 × 10⁵ km². About how many people per km² does this country have? (Divide population by area, focusing on powers of 10.)
   - Power-of-10 estimate: 10⁷ ÷ 10⁵ = 10² = 100. Coefficient: 4.2 ÷ 3.5 ≈ 1.2. Approximate answer: about 120 people per km².
   - **Answer:** Approximately 120 people per km².

3. **Biology:** A single bacterium has a length of about 0.002 mm. A scientist lines up 10,000 bacteria end to end. What is the total length in mm? In cm? (1 cm = 10 mm)
   - Total mm: 0.002 × 10,000 = 0.002 × 10⁴ = 20 mm.
   - Convert to cm: 20 mm ÷ 10 = 2 cm.
   - **Answer:** 20 mm = 2 cm.

4. **Error Analysis:** A student says "the number 0.7 × 10⁴ is in scientific notation and equals 700." Find both errors.
   - Error 1: 0.7 × 10⁴ is NOT in scientific notation because the coefficient 0.7 is less than 1 (must be ≥ 1).
   - Error 2: 0.7 × 10⁴ = 0.7 × 10,000 = 7,000 — not 700.
   - Correct scientific notation: 7 × 10³ = 7,000.
   - **Answer:** Two errors — coefficient violation AND incorrect calculation (7,000, not 700).

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. The Sun is approximately 1.5 × 10⁸ km from Earth. Write this distance in standard form.
2. The Amazon River carries approximately 209,000 cubic meters of water per second. Write this in scientific notation.
3. A grain of pollen is about 0.00005 m in diameter. If you lined up 100,000 pollen grains end to end, what would the total length be in meters?
4. Evaluate: 2³ + 10² = ___
5. Order from least to greatest: 4 × 10⁸; 4 × 10⁶; 4.1 × 10⁷.

**Stretch:**
1. The Andromeda Galaxy is about 2.537 × 10¹⁹ km from Earth. The nearest star (Proxima Centauri) is about 4.0 × 10¹³ km away. Without calculating exactly, about how many times farther is Andromeda than Proxima Centauri? (Compare the powers of 10.)
2. A stadium holds 50,000 fans. The Super Bowl is watched by about 120,000,000 viewers worldwide. Express both in scientific notation and determine how many "stadium-sized" audiences the TV viewership represents (using powers of 10 to estimate).
3. In 2024, the U.S. national debt was approximately $35,000,000,000,000. If this debt were divided equally among 335,000,000 Americans, approximately how much would each person owe? Express both numbers in scientific notation and estimate the quotient.

**Challenge:**
1. Light travels at approximately 3 × 10⁸ meters per second. The Sun is 1.5 × 10¹¹ meters from Earth. How many seconds does light take to travel from the Sun to Earth? (Divide distance by speed. Hint: focus on the powers of 10 for the calculation.)
2. The human body contains approximately 37 trillion cells (3.7 × 10¹³). Each cell contains approximately 3 billion base pairs of DNA (3 × 10⁹). About how many DNA base pairs are there in the entire human body? Express in scientific notation.
3. A scientist claims: "A sample has so many bacteria that if you lined them up end to end, they would circle Earth's equator (about 40,000 km) three times. Each bacterium is 0.002 mm long." How many bacteria are in the sample? Express in scientific notation. (Convert all measurements to the same unit first.)

### Interleaved Review
*(From Units 1-4)*

**Review 1 (Unit 1 — Fractions):** A piece of string is 4 1/2 meters long. It is cut into pieces each 3/8 meter long. How many pieces are there?
- **Answer:** 4 1/2 ÷ 3/8 = 9/2 × 8/3 = 72/6 = 12 pieces

**Review 2 (Unit 4 — Decimals):** A bottle of juice holds 1.5 liters. A school buys 24 bottles for a party. How many liters of juice is that?
- **Answer:** 1.5 × 24 = 36 liters

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. The speed of light is 300,000,000 meters per second. Write this in scientific notation.
2. A researcher reports that a sample contains 6.8 × 10⁶ cells. How many cells is this in standard form?
3. Circle all expressions that are NOT in correct scientific notation: a) 4.5 × 10³  b) 0.9 × 10⁴  c) 23 × 10⁵  d) 7.01 × 10⁸

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct (question 3 requires correctly identifying both b and c).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Work through the specific problem types missed. The most common issue at this stage is scientific notation conversion or identification of coefficient violations.
- Do not delay the unit assessment indefinitely — if the student has met mastery on 80% of earlier lessons, proceed to the assessment and use the section results to identify which specific lessons need targeted reteaching.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Proceed to Unit Assessment.
- Encourage the student: "You have learned a new language that scientists use every day. These skills will appear in every science class you take from now on."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- This lesson serves two purposes: (1) consolidation before the unit assessment, and (2) diagnostic identification of any remaining gaps.
- The error-analysis problems are particularly valuable — students who can identify and correct errors in others' work demonstrate understanding rather than just procedural knowledge.
- The stretch and challenge problems intentionally push beyond Grade 5 expectations. Use them for students who are ready. Do not feel pressure to complete all tiers for every student.
- Real-world contexts: if the student is studying astronomy or biology in school, connect directly to what they are learning. These connections make the math memorable and meaningful.
- If the student struggled with the warm-up, use this session to reteach rather than simply practicing. Identify the weakest skill area from the warm-up errors and spend the first 10 minutes re-instructing that skill before moving to practice.
- The unit assessment should be administered in the session following this lesson. Do not wait more than two days.
