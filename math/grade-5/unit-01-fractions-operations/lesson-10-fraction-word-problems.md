# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 1, Lesson 10

## Topic

Fraction Word Problems

## Learning Objectives

- Students will solve real-world word problems requiring addition of fractions and mixed numbers.
- Students will solve real-world word problems requiring subtraction of fractions and mixed numbers (with and without regrouping).
- Students will apply the Read-Plan-Solve-Check strategy to organize their problem-solving process.
- Students will express all answers in simplest form and include appropriate labels (units).

## Prerequisites

- Adding fractions and mixed numbers with unlike denominators (Lessons 1-5, 7)
- Subtracting mixed numbers with and without regrouping (Lessons 8-9)
- Simplifying fractions (Lesson 6)
- Reading comprehension at grade level

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers
- Student math journals
- Pencils
- Printed practice worksheet (Independent Practice problems)
- Exit ticket slips
- "Read-Plan-Solve-Check" anchor chart (or poster)

**Standards:** 5.NF.1, 5.NF.2

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Practice identifying the operation needed for a word problem without solving it yet, building the skill of reading carefully before computing.

Write the following on the board. Students should write "ADD" or "SUBTRACT" in their journals for each — they do NOT solve yet.

1. "Maria had 3 1/2 cups of flour. She used 1 3/4 cups for a recipe. How much flour does she have left?" (SUBTRACT)
2. "One shelf is 2 1/3 feet long. Another shelf is 4 1/6 feet long. What is the total length?" (ADD)
3. "David walked 5/6 of a mile to school and 2/3 of a mile home. How far did he walk in total?" (ADD)
4. "A ribbon is 6 1/4 yards long. You cut off 3 5/8 yards. How much ribbon remains?" (SUBTRACT)

Discuss: "How did you decide? What clue words helped you?" (Left = subtract, total = add, remains = subtract, in total = add.)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Read-Plan-Solve-Check:** A four-step strategy for solving word problems.
  - **Read:** Read the problem carefully. What is the question asking?
  - **Plan:** Decide the operation (addition or subtraction). Identify the numbers.
  - **Solve:** Set up the equation and compute. Find a common denominator, regroup if needed, simplify.
  - **Check:** Reread the question. Does your answer make sense? Did you include units?
- **Label (units):** The measurement or item type in the answer (cups, feet, miles, etc.).
- **Estimate:** A rough answer to check if your exact answer is reasonable.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the Read-Plan-Solve-Check strategy.**

Display the anchor chart. Explain each step. Say: "Word problems can feel tricky, but they all use the same fraction skills we have learned. The hardest part is understanding what the problem is asking. Our strategy helps us slow down and think before we compute."

**Step 2: Model a word problem with addition.**

Write on the board: "A recipe calls for 2 2/3 cups of white flour and 1 3/4 cups of wheat flour. How many cups of flour are needed in all?"

- **Read:** The question asks for the total cups of flour.
- **Plan:** "In all" means we add. Numbers: 2 2/3 and 1 3/4.
- **Solve:** 2 2/3 + 1 3/4. LCD = 12. Convert: 2 8/12 + 1 9/12. Wholes: 3. Fractions: 8/12 + 9/12 = 17/12 = 1 5/12. Regroup: 3 + 1 5/12 = 4 5/12.
- **Check:** Does 4 5/12 cups make sense? 2 2/3 is about 2.7 and 1 3/4 is about 1.75. Sum is about 4.45. And 4 5/12 is approximately 4.42. Yes, that is reasonable. Include the label: **4 5/12 cups.**

**Step 3: Model a word problem with subtraction (no regrouping).**

Write: "Sam has a board that is 8 5/6 feet long. He cuts off 3 1/3 feet. How much of the board is left?"

- **Read:** The question asks how much is left.
- **Plan:** "Left" means subtract. Numbers: 8 5/6 and 3 1/3.
- **Solve:** 8 5/6 - 3 1/3. LCD = 6. Convert: 8 5/6 - 3 2/6. Wholes: 5. Fractions: 5/6 - 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2.
- **Check:** About 8.8 - 3.3 = 5.5. And 5 1/2 = 5.5. Correct. **5 1/2 feet.**

**Step 4: Model a word problem with subtraction (regrouping needed).**

Write: "Jenna practiced piano for 5 1/4 hours this week. Last week she practiced 3 3/4 hours. How many more hours did she practice this week?"

- **Read:** "How many more" = find the difference.
- **Plan:** Subtract. Numbers: 5 1/4 and 3 3/4.
- **Solve:** 5 1/4 - 3 3/4. Need to regroup: 5 1/4 = 4 5/4. Subtract: 4 5/4 - 3 3/4. Wholes: 1. Fractions: 5/4 - 3/4 = 2/4 = 1/2.
- **Check:** About 5.25 - 3.75 = 1.5. And 1 1/2 = 1.5. Correct. **1 1/2 hours.**

**Step 5: Model a multi-step word problem.**

Write: "A water bottle holds 4 1/2 cups. In the morning you drink 1 3/4 cups. At lunch you drink 1 1/3 cups. How much water is left?"

- **Read:** Find how much water is left after two drinks.
- **Plan:** First add the two amounts drunk, then subtract from the total.
- **Solve:**
  - Total drunk: 1 3/4 + 1 1/3. LCD = 12. 1 9/12 + 1 4/12 = 2 13/12 = 3 1/12.
  - Remaining: 4 1/2 - 3 1/12. LCD = 12. 4 6/12 - 3 1/12. Wholes: 1. Fractions: 6/12 - 1/12 = 5/12.
- **Check:** About 4.5 - (1.75 + 1.33) = 4.5 - 3.08 = 1.42. And 1 5/12 is approximately 1.42. Correct. **1 5/12 cups.**

**Step 6: Emphasize reasonableness.**

"Always re-read the question and ask: does my answer make sense? If the question asks how much is left, your answer should be less than what you started with. If the question asks for a total, your answer should be more than either part."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** "A garden bed is 3 1/4 feet wide. Another garden bed is 2 5/8 feet wide. What is the total width?"
- Add: 3 1/4 + 2 5/8. LCD = 8. 3 2/8 + 2 5/8. Wholes: 5. Fractions: 2/8 + 5/8 = 7/8.
- Answer: **5 7/8 feet**

**Example B:** "Tyler had 6 1/3 pounds of clay. He used 2 5/6 pounds for a sculpture. How much clay does he have left?"
- Subtract: 6 1/3 - 2 5/6. LCD = 6. 6 2/6 - 2 5/6. Regroup: 5 8/6 - 2 5/6. Wholes: 3. Fractions: 8/6 - 5/6 = 3/6 = 1/2.
- Answer: **3 1/2 pounds**

**Example C:** "A trail is 7/8 of a mile long. Maya has walked 1/3 of a mile. How much farther does she need to walk?"
- Subtract: 7/8 - 1/3. LCD = 24. 21/24 - 8/24 = 13/24.
- Answer: **13/24 of a mile**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems as a class using Read-Plan-Solve-Check. Students attempt each problem first.

1. A bag contains 5 1/2 pounds of apples. You eat apples weighing 1 3/4 pounds. How many pounds of apples are left?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What operation do we use? How do you know? Does the answer require regrouping?"

2. On Saturday, Emma ran 2 1/3 miles. On Sunday, she ran 3 1/2 miles. How far did she run over the weekend?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What clue word tells us to add? After adding, check: does the fraction need regrouping?"

3. A recipe calls for 3/4 cup of brown sugar and 2/3 cup of white sugar. How much total sugar is needed?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find the common denominator. Is the answer proper or improper? Convert if needed."

4. A bookshelf is 4 1/6 feet tall. A table is 2 5/6 feet tall. How much taller is the bookshelf?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "How much taller means we subtract. Do you need to regroup?"

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

Solve each problem. Show all work using Read-Plan-Solve-Check. Write answers in simplest form with labels.

1. A board is 8 1/4 feet long. A carpenter cuts off 3 5/8 feet. How long is the remaining board?
2. Mia drank 1 2/3 cups of juice in the morning and 2 1/4 cups in the afternoon. How many total cups did she drink?

**Scoring:**
- 2/2 correct with strategy steps shown and labels included = Mastery
- 1/2 correct or missing labels/work = Approaching mastery
- 0/2 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met

- Have the student use a graphic organizer with four boxes labeled "Read," "Plan," "Solve," and "Check." Walk through one problem together, filling in each box.
- Provide problems with the operation already identified (e.g., "ADD: 2 1/3 + 1 1/2 = ?") so the student can focus on the computation. Gradually remove the operation hint.
- Pair with a peer tutor to work through 2-3 additional word problems verbally, explaining their reasoning aloud.

### If Mastery Is Met

- Challenge the student to write a two-step word problem that requires both addition and subtraction of mixed numbers. Solve it and trade with a partner.
- Give the student a "Reasonableness Check" task: provide 3 word problems with answers already filled in (some correct, some wrong). The student must determine which answers are reasonable and which are not, without solving — just estimating.

## Extension (Optional)

**Recipe Doubling Project:** Provide students with a simple recipe card with fractional ingredient amounts (e.g., 1 1/2 cups flour, 3/4 cup sugar, 2 1/3 cups milk). Students must:
1. Double the recipe (add each amount to itself).
2. Figure out how much of each ingredient is left if they started with specific amounts (e.g., started with 5 cups of flour, used the doubled amount — how much flour remains?).

This combines addition and subtraction of mixed numbers with a real-world context that 10-11 year olds find engaging.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors

- **Choosing the wrong operation.** Students may add when they should subtract, or vice versa. Teach students to underline key words ("left," "remaining," "total," "in all," "how much more") and connect them to operations. However, caution students that key words are not always reliable — understanding the situation matters more.
- **Forgetting to include labels/units.** After solving, students write a bare number like "4 5/12" instead of "4 5/12 cups." Remind them that real-world answers need units.
- **Not simplifying.** Students may correctly compute but leave the answer unsimplified. Build the habit: "Simplify is always the last step."
- **Ignoring multi-step structure.** In problems with two or more operations, students may only do one step and think they are done. Encourage rereading the question after each step: "Have I answered what was asked?"
- **Computation errors carry over.** A mistake in finding the LCD or regrouping leads to a wrong final answer even though the setup was correct. Encourage the "Check" step — estimating with whole numbers to see if the answer is reasonable.

### Pacing Advice

- The Warm-Up is designed to be quick (identifying operations only). Do not let it extend into full solutions.
- Spend the most time on Direct Instruction Steps 2-4. The multi-step problem (Step 5) is important but can be shortened if time is tight.
- During Guided Practice, if students are confidently choosing operations and solving, move quickly to Independent Practice.
- The ★★★ Challenge problems (especially #8 and #9) are multi-step and may take extra time. Assign as homework or for early finishers.
- This lesson works well as a review and consolidation lesson. If students are struggling, it is fine to spend two days on word problems.
