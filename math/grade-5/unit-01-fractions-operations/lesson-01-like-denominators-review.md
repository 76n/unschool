# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 1, Lesson 1

## Topic
Review: Adding and Subtracting with Like Denominators

## Learning Objectives
- Students will add fractions with like denominators and express the sum in simplest form.
- Students will subtract fractions with like denominators and express the difference in simplest form.
- Students will explain why only the numerators change when denominators are the same.

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of what a fraction represents (parts of a whole).
- Ability to identify the numerator and denominator of a fraction.
- Experience with basic addition and subtraction facts through 20.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Fraction circle manipulatives or printed fraction strips (1 set per student).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 5.NF.1 — Add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators (including mixed numbers) by replacing given fractions with equivalent fractions in such a way as to produce an equivalent sum or difference of fractions with like denominators.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of fraction vocabulary and the meaning of numerator/denominator.

1. Display the fraction 5/8 on the board.
2. Ask: "What does the 8 tell us?" (the total number of equal parts) "What does the 5 tell us?" (how many of those parts we are talking about).
3. Have students sketch a rectangle divided into 8 equal parts and shade 5 of them.
4. Quick turn-and-talk: "If I shade 2 more eighths, how many eighths are shaded now?" (7/8).

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Numerator** — the top number of a fraction; tells how many parts are counted.
- **Denominator** — the bottom number of a fraction; tells how many equal parts make up the whole.
- **Like denominators** — fractions that share the same denominator (e.g., 3/8 and 2/8 both have denominator 8).
- **Simplest form** — a fraction whose numerator and denominator share no common factor other than 1.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a concrete model.**
Hold up a fraction circle divided into 8 equal pieces. Place 3 pieces in one group and 2 pieces in another. Ask: "How many eighths in this group? And this group? If I combine them, how many eighths altogether?"

**Step 2 — State the rule.**
"When fractions have the same denominator, we add (or subtract) only the numerators. The denominator stays the same because the size of each piece has not changed."

Write the general form on the board:
- a/d + b/d = (a + b)/d
- a/d - b/d = (a - b)/d

**Step 3 — Walk through addition.**
Show: 3/8 + 2/8.
"The denominators are both 8 — the pieces are the same size. Add the numerators: 3 + 2 = 5. The answer is 5/8."

**Step 4 — Walk through subtraction.**
Show: 7/10 - 4/10.
"The denominators are both 10. Subtract the numerators: 7 - 4 = 3. The answer is 3/10."

**Step 5 — Simplify when possible.**
Show: 2/6 + 2/6 = 4/6.
"Can we simplify 4/6? Yes — divide numerator and denominator by 2 to get 2/3."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Addition**
3/8 + 2/8
= (3 + 2)/8
= 5/8 (already in simplest form)

**Example B — Subtraction**
7/10 - 4/10
= (7 - 4)/10
= 3/10 (already in simplest form)

**Example C — Simplifying the result**
2/6 + 2/6
= (2 + 2)/6
= 4/6
= 2/3 (simplified by dividing numerator and denominator by 2)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. Students attempt each problem first, then discuss.

1. 1/5 + 3/5 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Do the denominators match? What do we do with the numerators?"

2. 5/9 - 2/9 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What operation are we performing on the numerators?"

3. 3/10 + 5/10 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "After you add, check — can the answer be simplified?"

4. 7/8 - 3/8 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What is 7 minus 3? Does the denominator change?"

5. 1/6 + 3/6 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What is 4/6 in simplest form? How do you know?"

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### ★ On-Level
1. 2/7 + 4/7 = ?
2. 8/9 - 5/9 = ?
3. 3/12 + 5/12 = ?
4. 6/10 - 2/10 = ?

### ★★ Stretch
5. 4/8 + 3/8 = ? (simplify your answer)
6. 11/12 - 5/12 = ? (simplify your answer)
7. A pizza is cut into 10 equal slices. Maria eats 3/10 of the pizza and James eats 4/10. What fraction of the pizza did they eat together?

### ★★★ Challenge
8. Sam has 7/8 of a cup of flour. He uses 3/8 of a cup for one recipe and 2/8 of a cup for another. How much flour does he have left? Write your answer in simplest form.
9. Find two different fractions with a denominator of 12 that add up to 1/2.

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: basic fraction concepts from Grade 4)*
10. Write a fraction that represents the shaded part of a shape divided into 6 equal parts with 5 parts shaded.
11. Is 3/4 greater than, less than, or equal to 1/2? Explain how you know.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
Solve the following two problems. Show your work.

**Problem 1:** 5/12 + 4/12 = ?
**Problem 2:** 9/10 - 6/10 = ?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Pull students into a small group for reteaching using fraction strip manipulatives.
- Provide three additional practice problems with concrete models before moving to abstract notation.
- Pair the student with a peer tutor for guided practice during the next session.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Students may begin the Extension activity below.
- Students may serve as peer tutors for classmates who need additional support.

## Extension (Optional)
- **Fraction Puzzle:** Create a set of addition and subtraction equations with like denominators where all answers simplify to a unit fraction (e.g., 1/2, 1/3, 1/4). How many different equations can you create for each unit fraction?
- **Real-World Connection:** Write a short word problem involving adding or subtracting fractions with like denominators. Trade with a partner and solve each other's problem.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Adding or subtracting the denominators along with the numerators.** For example, writing 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/16. Redirect by asking: "Did the size of each piece change?" Use fraction strips to show that the pieces remain eighths.
- **Forgetting to simplify.** Remind students to check whether the numerator and denominator share a common factor after computing.
- **Writing an improper fraction as an error.** For example, thinking 5/8 + 4/8 = 9/8 is wrong. Clarify that improper fractions are valid and can also be written as mixed numbers (1 1/8).

### Pacing Advice
- If students grasp the Warm-Up quickly (within 2-3 minutes), move directly into Direct Instruction and use the saved time for additional Guided Practice problems.
- If many students struggle during Guided Practice, pause Independent Practice and reteach using manipulatives. The On-Level Independent Practice problems can be assigned as homework instead.
- This lesson is a review of Grade 4 skills. If the class demonstrates strong mastery, consider condensing this lesson to 30 minutes and beginning Lesson 2 in the same session.
