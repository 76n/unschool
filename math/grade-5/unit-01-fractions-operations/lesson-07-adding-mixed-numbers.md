# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 1, Lesson 7

## Topic

Adding Mixed Numbers

## Learning Objectives

- Students will add two mixed numbers with like denominators.
- Students will add two mixed numbers with unlike denominators by first finding a common denominator.
- Students will regroup when the fractional parts sum to an improper fraction (e.g., 2 5/6 becomes 3 and something).
- Students will express all answers in simplest form.

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of mixed numbers and improper fractions (converting between them)
- Adding fractions with like and unlike denominators (Lessons 1-5)
- Finding least common denominators
- Simplifying fractions (Lesson 6)

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers
- Fraction strips or number line printouts
- Student math journals
- Pencils
- Printed practice worksheet (Independent Practice problems)
- Exit ticket slips

**Standards:** 5.NF.1

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review adding fractions with unlike denominators and converting improper fractions to mixed numbers, preparing students for the main lesson.

1. Write on the board: "Solve: 3/4 + 5/6."
2. Give students 2 minutes to solve in their journals.
   - Common denominator: 12. Convert: 9/12 + 10/12 = 19/12.
   - Convert to mixed number: 19/12 = 1 7/12.
3. Ask: "What did you do when the answer was more than one whole?" (Converted the improper fraction to a mixed number.)
4. Say: "Today we will add mixed numbers — numbers that already have a whole number part and a fraction part."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Mixed number:** A number made up of a whole number and a fraction (e.g., 3 1/4).
- **Improper fraction:** A fraction where the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator (e.g., 7/4).
- **Regroup (carry over):** When the fractional parts add up to more than one whole, convert the extra whole to add to the whole-number part.
- **Common denominator:** A shared denominator needed to add fractions with unlike denominators.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Present the strategy for adding mixed numbers.**

Write on the board:
"To add mixed numbers:
1. Add the whole numbers.
2. Add the fractions (find a common denominator if needed).
3. If the fraction part is improper, regroup.
4. Simplify if needed."

**Step 2: Model an example with like denominators (no regrouping).**

Write: 3 2/5 + 1 1/5

- Add whole numbers: 3 + 1 = 4.
- Add fractions: 2/5 + 1/5 = 3/5.
- Combine: 4 3/5.
- Check: 3/5 is proper and already simplified.
- Answer: **4 3/5**

**Step 3: Model the key example with unlike denominators (no regrouping).**

Write: 2 1/3 + 3 1/4

- Add whole numbers: 2 + 3 = 5.
- Add fractions: 1/3 + 1/4. LCD = 12. Convert: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.
- Combine: 5 7/12.
- Check: 7/12 is proper. GCF of 7 and 12 is 1 — already simplified.
- Answer: **5 7/12**

**Step 4: Model an example that requires regrouping.**

Write: 4 3/4 + 2 3/4

- Add whole numbers: 4 + 2 = 6.
- Add fractions: 3/4 + 3/4 = 6/4.
- 6/4 is improper! Convert: 6/4 = 1 2/4 = 1 1/2.
- Regroup: 6 + 1 1/2 = 7 1/2.
- Answer: **7 1/2**

**Step 5: Model another regrouping example with unlike denominators.**

Write: 3 5/6 + 2 2/3

- Add whole numbers: 3 + 2 = 5.
- Add fractions: 5/6 + 2/3. LCD = 6. Convert 2/3 = 4/6. So 5/6 + 4/6 = 9/6.
- 9/6 is improper! Convert: 9/6 = 1 3/6 = 1 1/2.
- Regroup: 5 + 1 1/2 = 6 1/2.
- Answer: **6 1/2**

**Step 6: Reinforce the regrouping idea.**

"Whenever your fraction part adds up to more than one whole — meaning the numerator is bigger than the denominator — you need to regroup. Pull out the extra whole number and add it to your whole-number sum."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 1 2/5 + 4 1/5
- Whole numbers: 1 + 4 = 5.
- Fractions: 2/5 + 1/5 = 3/5.
- Answer: **5 3/5**

**Example B:** 2 1/3 + 1 1/2
- Whole numbers: 2 + 1 = 3.
- Fractions: 1/3 + 1/2. LCD = 6. 2/6 + 3/6 = 5/6.
- Answer: **3 5/6**

**Example C:** 5 3/4 + 1 3/8
- Whole numbers: 5 + 1 = 6.
- Fractions: 3/4 + 3/8. LCD = 8. 6/8 + 3/8 = 9/8.
- 9/8 = 1 1/8. Regroup: 6 + 1 1/8 = 7 1/8.
- Answer: **7 1/8**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems as a class. Students attempt each problem first, then share.

1. 2 1/4 + 3 2/4
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Start with the whole numbers. Then add the fractions. Do you need to regroup?"

2. 1 2/3 + 2 1/6
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "The denominators are different. What is the LCD of 3 and 6?"

3. 4 5/8 + 2 7/8
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Add the fractions. What happens when you get 12/8? Is that proper or improper?"

4. 3 1/2 + 2 3/4
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find the common denominator first, then add the fractions. Do you need to regroup?"

5. 6 2/3 + 1 5/6
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "After adding, check: is the fraction part improper? Don't forget to simplify at the end."

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

Solve the following problems. Show all work. Write answers in simplest form.

1. 3 1/4 + 2 1/3
2. 4 5/6 + 2 5/6

**Scoring:**
- 2/2 correct with work shown = Mastery
- 1/2 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0/2 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met

- Use fraction strips to physically model adding mixed numbers. Have the student combine the whole-number strips first, then combine the fraction strips, showing how they "trade" excess fraction strips for a whole.
- Provide a step-by-step checklist card that the student keeps on their desk: (1) Add wholes, (2) Find LCD, (3) Add fractions, (4) Regroup if improper, (5) Simplify.
- Reteach with like-denominator problems first before introducing unlike denominators.

### If Mastery Is Met

- Have the student write and solve their own "regrouping required" mixed-number addition problem, then trade with a partner to check each other's work.
- Challenge: "I added two mixed numbers and got 10 1/3. One of the numbers was 4 5/6. What was the other number?" (Answer: 5 1/2, since 10 1/3 - 4 5/6 = 9 8/6 - 4 5/6 = 5 3/6 = 5 1/2.)

## Extension (Optional)

**Building Lengths Project:** Give students a set of "lumber measurements" written as mixed numbers (e.g., 2 1/4 ft, 3 3/8 ft, 1 5/6 ft). Students must plan how to combine boards to build shelves of specified lengths. They add mixed numbers to see which combinations of boards meet the required lengths, practicing regrouping and simplification in a real-world context.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors

- **Forgetting to regroup.** Students write answers like 5 9/6 instead of recognizing that 9/6 needs to be converted and regrouped. Train students to always check: "Is my numerator bigger than my denominator?"
- **Adding whole numbers to whole numbers and fractions to fractions incorrectly by cross-mixing.** For example, adding the whole number to the numerator. Reinforce the structure: wholes with wholes, fractions with fractions.
- **Forgetting to find a common denominator.** Some students add fractions with unlike denominators directly (e.g., 1/3 + 1/4 = 2/7). Remind students that we can only add fractions when the denominators are the same.
- **Not simplifying the final answer.** Remind students: "Our last step is always to simplify."

### Pacing Advice

- Steps 2-3 in Direct Instruction (no regrouping) should move quickly if students are solid from previous lessons. Spend more time on Steps 4-5 (regrouping), as this is the new concept.
- If students breeze through Guided Practice 1-2, jump to problem 3 (regrouping) quickly.
- If the class needs more time on regrouping, assign On-Level problems for classwork and Stretch/Challenge for homework.
- The Interleaved Review problems should be quick; if students are struggling, it signals a need to revisit Lesson 6.
