**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** Written; facilitator reads word problems aloud if needed

**Materials:** Paper and pencil. Fraction strips available on request (note if used).

## Section A: Like Denominators and Equivalent Fractions (3 items)

**A1.** 3/8 + 4/8 = ___  (7/8)

**A2.** Write two fractions equivalent to 2/3. (___ and ___)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Any two correct equivalents (e.g., 4/6 and 6/9, or 8/12 and 10/15) | 1 point |
| Only one correct equivalent | 0.5 points |

**A3.** 9/10 − 3/10 = ___  (6/10 = 3/5)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| 3/5 (simplified) | 1 point |
| 6/10 (correct but not simplified) | 0.5 points |

## Section B: Adding Fractions with Unlike Denominators (3 items)

**B1.** 1/4 + 1/3 = ___

Show your work.

(LCD = 12. 3/12 + 4/12 = 7/12)

**B2.** 2/5 + 1/4 = ___

(LCD = 20. 8/20 + 5/20 = 13/20)

**B3.** 3/8 + 1/6 = ___

Show your work.

(LCD = 24. 9/24 + 4/24 = 13/24)

---

## Section C: Subtracting Fractions with Unlike Denominators (3 items)

**C1.** 3/4 − 1/3 = ___

Show your work.

(LCD = 12. 9/12 − 4/12 = 5/12)

**C2.** 5/6 − 1/4 = ___

(LCD = 12. 10/12 − 3/12 = 7/12)

**C3.** 7/8 − 2/3 = ___

Show your work.

(LCD = 24. 21/24 − 16/24 = 5/24)

---

## Section D: Mixed Numbers (4 items)

**D1.** 2 1/3 + 1 1/4 = ___

Show your work.

(Whole numbers: 2 + 1 = 3. Fractions: 1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12. Answer: 3 7/12)

**D2.** 3 2/5 + 2 4/5 = ___

(Whole: 3 + 2 = 5. Fractions: 2/5 + 4/5 = 6/5 = 1 1/5. Regroup: 5 + 1 1/5 = 6 1/5)

**D3.** 5 3/4 − 2 1/4 = ___

(Whole: 5 − 2 = 3. Fractions: 3/4 − 1/4 = 2/4 = 1/2. Answer: 3 1/2)

**D4.** 4 1/6 − 1 5/6 = ___

Show your work.

(Can't subtract 5/6 from 1/6. Regroup: 4 1/6 = 3 7/6. Now: 3 7/6 − 1 5/6 = 2 2/6 = 2 1/3)

---

## Section E: Word Problems (3 items)

**E1.** "A recipe calls for 2/3 cup of flour and 1/4 cup of sugar. How many cups of dry ingredients are needed in total?"

Show your work.
Answer: ___ cups

(2/3 + 1/4 = 8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12 cup)

**E2.** "Mia ran 3 1/2 miles on Monday and 2 3/4 miles on Tuesday. How far did she run altogether?"

Show your work.
Answer: ___ miles

(3 1/2 + 2 3/4 = 3 2/4 + 2 3/4 = 5 5/4 = 6 1/4 miles)

**E3.** "A board is 8 1/3 feet long. A carpenter cuts off 2 5/6 feet. How long is the remaining piece?"

Show your work.
Answer: ___ feet

(8 1/3 − 2 5/6. LCD = 6: 8 2/6 − 2 5/6. Can't subtract 5/6 from 2/6. Regroup: 7 8/6 − 2 5/6 = 5 3/6 = 5 1/2 feet)

## Rubric and Mastery Determination

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Next Steps |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Mastered** | 13-16 (80-100%) with ≥50% per section | Solid fraction addition and subtraction | Advance to Unit 2 (Multiplying Fractions). |
| **Approaching** | 10-12 (63-79%) | Partial understanding; specific gaps | Identify weak areas. If LCD is the issue → reteach Lesson 3. If mixed number regrouping → reteach Lesson 9. If simplifying → reteach Lesson 6. |
| **Developing** | Below 10 (<63%) | Significant gaps | Check prerequisites: Can the student find equivalent fractions? Does the student understand what a fraction represents? Address conceptual gaps before procedural practice. |

### Section-Specific Reteaching Guide

| If struggling in... | Focus on... |
|---|---|
| Section A | Review Grade 4 fraction concepts. Use fraction strips to visualize equivalent fractions. |
| Section B | Return to Lessons 3-4. The LCD process must be solid. Use visual models (fraction strips on number lines) to show why common denominators are needed. |
| Section C | If B is fine but C isn't: the student may be making subtraction errors, not fraction errors. Practice subtraction of fractions with like denominators first. |
| Section D | If adding mixed numbers is fine but subtracting with regrouping isn't: focus on Lesson 9 specifically. The regrouping concept (borrowing a whole) is the hardest skill. |
| Section E | If computation is correct but setup is wrong: practice identifying the operation (adding vs. subtracting) from word problem context. If computation is wrong: fix the computation first. |

---

## Error Analysis Guide

| Error Pattern | Example | Likely Cause | Intervention |
|--------------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Adds numerators and denominators | 1/3 + 1/4 = 2/7 | Doesn't understand common denominators | Return to fraction strips. Show that 1/3 + 1/4 is NOT 2/7 by measuring. |
| Finds LCD but only converts one fraction | 1/3 + 1/4 → 4/12 + 1/4 | Procedural omission | Emphasize: BOTH fractions must be converted. Check: "Do both fractions have the same denominator now?" |
| Doesn't simplify | Writes 6/8 instead of 3/4 | Doesn't check for common factors | Teach the habit: "Can both top and bottom be divided by the same number?" |
| Mixed number regrouping errors | 4 1/6 − 1 5/6 = 3 4/6 (subtracted wrong way) | Didn't regroup the whole number | Show with models: "You can't take 5 sixths from 1 sixth. Borrow a whole." |

## Facilitator Observations

- Does the student find LCDs quickly or need to list many multiples?
- Does the student simplify automatically or need reminding?
- Does the student use visual models (fraction strips) or work purely with numbers?
- Does the student check answers for reasonableness (e.g., "1/3 + 1/4 should be more than 1/2 but less than 1")?
