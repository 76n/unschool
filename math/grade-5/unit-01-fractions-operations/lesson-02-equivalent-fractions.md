# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 1, Lesson 2

## Topic
Equivalent Fractions Review

## Learning Objectives
- Students will generate equivalent fractions by multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number.
- Students will verify that two fractions are equivalent using visual models (fraction strips or area models).
- Students will explain why multiplying numerator and denominator by the same number produces an equivalent fraction.

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of what a fraction represents (parts of a whole).
- Ability to add and subtract fractions with like denominators (Lesson 1).
- Multiplication facts through 12 x 12.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Printed fraction strips (1 set per student) — strips for halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, eighths, and twelfths.
- Colored pencils or crayons for shading area models.
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 5.NF.1 — Add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators (including mixed numbers) by replacing given fractions with equivalent fractions in such a way as to produce an equivalent sum or difference of fractions with like denominators.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Connect the idea of "same amount, different name" to everyday experience and activate prior knowledge of equivalent fractions from Grade 4.

1. Display two rectangles of the same size on the board. Divide the first into 2 equal parts and shade 1. Divide the second into 4 equal parts and shade 2.
2. Ask: "Which rectangle has more shaded — the first or the second?" (They are the same.)
3. Write: 1/2 = 2/4. Ask: "How can the fractions look different but represent the same amount?"
4. Have students turn and talk: "Can you name another fraction equal to 1/2?"

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Equivalent fractions** — fractions that represent the same part of a whole, even though they have different numerators and denominators (e.g., 1/2 and 3/6).
- **Identity property of multiplication** — multiplying any number by 1 does not change its value. Since 2/2 = 1, 3/3 = 1, etc., multiplying a fraction by n/n produces an equivalent fraction.
- **Fraction strip** — a paper strip divided into equal parts, used to compare fractions visually.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Visual discovery with fraction strips.**
Have students lay the 1/2 strip next to the 1/4 strips. Ask: "How many fourths does it take to cover 1/2?" (2 fourths.) Now try sixths: "How many sixths cover 1/2?" (3 sixths.) Record: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6.

**Step 2 — Identify the pattern.**
Write the chain on the board: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8.
Ask: "What is happening to the numerator and denominator each time?" Guide students to see that both are being multiplied by the same number (x2, x3, x4).

**Step 3 — State the rule.**
"To create an equivalent fraction, multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the same nonzero number. This is the same as multiplying the fraction by 1 (for example, 2/2 or 3/3), so the value does not change."

Write the general form: a/b = (a x n)/(b x n), where n is any nonzero whole number.

**Step 4 — Demonstrate with a targeted example.**
"Find a fraction equivalent to 2/3 with a denominator of 12."
Ask: "What do I multiply 3 by to get 12?" (4.)
"So I also multiply the numerator by 4: 2 x 4 = 8. Therefore 2/3 = 8/12."

**Step 5 — Verify with a visual model.**
Draw two rectangles. Divide one into 3 equal columns and shade 2. Divide the other into 12 equal parts (3 columns x 4 rows) and shade 8. Confirm visually that the shaded areas are equal.

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Building a chain of equivalents**
1/2 = ?/4 = ?/6 = ?/8
- 1/2 = (1 x 2)/(2 x 2) = 2/4
- 1/2 = (1 x 3)/(2 x 3) = 3/6
- 1/2 = (1 x 4)/(2 x 4) = 4/8

**Example B — Finding a specific equivalent**
Write 3/5 with a denominator of 20.
- 5 x 4 = 20, so multiply numerator and denominator by 4.
- 3/5 = (3 x 4)/(5 x 4) = 12/20

**Example C — Checking equivalence**
Are 4/6 and 6/9 equivalent?
- Simplify 4/6: divide both by 2 → 2/3.
- Simplify 6/9: divide both by 3 → 2/3.
- Both simplify to 2/3, so yes, they are equivalent.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. Students attempt each problem first, then discuss.

1. Find the missing number: 1/3 = ?/9
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What do you multiply 3 by to get 9? Do the same to the numerator."

2. Find the missing number: 2/5 = ?/15
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What times 5 equals 15?"

3. Write two fractions equivalent to 3/4.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Try multiplying by 2/2 first, then by 3/3."

4. Are 2/8 and 1/4 equivalent? Prove it.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Can you simplify 2/8? What do you get?"

5. Find the missing number: ?/10 = 2/5
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What did we multiply the denominator by? Multiply the numerator by the same number."

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
Solve the following two problems. Show your work.

**Problem 1:** Write a fraction equivalent to 3/5 with a denominator of 20.
**Problem 2:** Are 4/10 and 2/5 equivalent? Explain how you know.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct with valid explanation = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Reteach using fraction strips: have the student physically lay strips side by side to see that equivalent fractions cover the same length.
- Provide a "multiply by 1" anchor chart showing 2/2 = 1, 3/3 = 1, 4/4 = 1, etc.
- Assign three additional practice problems using small denominators (halves, thirds, fourths) before progressing.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Students may begin the Extension activity below.
- Students may create an "Equivalent Fractions Poster" showing chains of equivalents for 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, and 1/5.

## Extension (Optional)
- **Fraction Wall:** Draw a fraction wall showing halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, eighths, and twelfths. Color-code all fractions equivalent to 1/2 in one color, all fractions equivalent to 1/3 in another, and so on.
- **Equivalence Detective:** Give students a list of 10 fractions and ask them to sort the fractions into groups of equivalents. Include distractors (fractions that do not match any group).

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Multiplying only the numerator or only the denominator.** For example, writing 1/3 = 1/9. Remind students: "Whatever you do to the bottom, you must also do to the top." Use the identity property (multiplying by n/n = 1) to reinforce this.
- **Confusing "equivalent" with "equal numerators."** Some students think 1/3 and 1/6 are equivalent because they share the numerator 1. Use fraction strips to show that 1/3 and 1/6 are different amounts.
- **Not recognizing simplification as the reverse process.** Students who can generate equivalents by multiplying sometimes struggle with simplifying (dividing). Emphasize that simplifying means "un-multiplying" — finding a common factor to divide out.

### Pacing Advice
- Spend extra time on Step 1 (visual discovery) if students have weak fraction sense. The concrete-to-abstract progression is critical.
- If the class is strong from Lesson 1, the Warm-Up and Step 1 can be condensed to 5 minutes total, leaving more time for Independent Practice.
- This lesson is foundational for Lessons 3-5. Ensure at least 80% of students demonstrate mastery before moving on. If not, consider a reteach day before Lesson 3.
