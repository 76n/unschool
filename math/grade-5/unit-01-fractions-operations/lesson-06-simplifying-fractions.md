# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 1, Lesson 6

## Topic

Simplifying Fractions

## Learning Objectives

- Students will identify the greatest common factor (GCF) of the numerator and denominator of a fraction.
- Students will simplify fractions by dividing the numerator and denominator by their GCF.
- Students will determine whether a fraction is already in simplest form.
- Students will understand that a simplified fraction and its original form are equivalent (represent the same amount).

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of what a fraction represents (parts of a whole)
- Ability to identify factors of a number
- Multiplication and division facts through 12
- Equivalent fractions (Lessons 1-5 concepts)

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers
- Fraction strips or fraction circle manipulatives
- Student math journals
- Pencils
- Printed practice worksheet (Independent Practice problems)
- Exit ticket slips

**Standards:** 5.NF.1

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of factors and equivalent fractions to bridge into simplifying.

1. Write the following on the board: "List all the factors of 12. List all the factors of 8."
2. Give students 1 minute to write factors in their journals.
   - Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
   - Factors of 8: 1, 2, 4, 8
3. Ask: "Which factors do 12 and 8 have in common?" (1, 2, 4)
4. Ask: "Which is the greatest common factor?" (4)
5. Quick review: "We know 2/4 = 1/2. How did we get from 2/4 to 1/2?" Take 2-3 student responses. Connect the idea that we divided both the numerator and denominator by the same number.

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Simplify (reduce):** To rewrite a fraction in its simplest form by dividing the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor.
- **Greatest Common Factor (GCF):** The largest number that divides evenly into two or more numbers.
- **Simplest form:** A fraction where the numerator and denominator share no common factor other than 1.
- **Equivalent fractions:** Fractions that represent the same value, even though they use different numbers.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the concept of simplifying.**

Write 6/8 on the board. Draw a rectangle divided into 8 equal parts, shade 6 of them. Say: "This shows 6/8. Can we write this fraction using smaller numbers but still show the same amount?"

**Step 2: Connect to equivalent fractions using a visual model.**

Draw another rectangle of the same size, divided into 4 equal parts, shade 3 of them. Say: "Look — 3/4 covers the exact same area as 6/8. They are equivalent. But 3/4 uses smaller numbers. We say 3/4 is the simplified form of 6/8."

**Step 3: Teach the GCF method.**

Write on the board:
- "To simplify a fraction, find the GCF of the numerator and denominator, then divide both by the GCF."
- Show: 6/8 → Factors of 6: 1, 2, 3, 6. Factors of 8: 1, 2, 4, 8. GCF = 2.
- Divide: 6 / 2 = 3 and 8 / 2 = 4. So 6/8 = 3/4.

**Step 4: Model a second example.**

Write 9/12 on the board.
- Factors of 9: 1, 3, 9. Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. GCF = 3.
- Divide: 9 / 3 = 3 and 12 / 3 = 4. So 9/12 = 3/4.

**Step 5: Show a fraction already in simplest form.**

Write 5/8 on the board.
- Factors of 5: 1, 5. Factors of 8: 1, 2, 4, 8. GCF = 1.
- "When the GCF is 1, the fraction is already in simplest form. We can't simplify it further."

**Step 6: Emphasize the rule.**

"From now on, we will always write our fraction answers in simplest form. After every addition or subtraction problem, check: can I simplify?"

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Simplify 4/10.
- Factors of 4: 1, 2, 4. Factors of 10: 1, 2, 5, 10. GCF = 2.
- 4 / 2 = 2 and 10 / 2 = 5.
- 4/10 = **2/5**

**Example B:** Simplify 8/12.
- Factors of 8: 1, 2, 4, 8. Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. GCF = 4.
- 8 / 4 = 2 and 12 / 4 = 3.
- 8/12 = **2/3**

**Example C:** Simplify 15/20.
- Factors of 15: 1, 3, 5, 15. Factors of 20: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20. GCF = 5.
- 15 / 5 = 3 and 20 / 5 = 4.
- 15/20 = **3/4**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems as a class. Students attempt each problem in their journals first, then share answers.

1. Simplify 6/9.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What are the factors of 6? What are the factors of 9? Which is the greatest factor they share?"

2. Simplify 10/15.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Can anyone find the GCF of 10 and 15? What do we do with it?"

3. Simplify 4/12.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Think about what number goes into both 4 and 12 evenly. What is the biggest one?"

4. Is 7/10 in simplest form? How do you know?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "List the factors of 7. List the factors of 10. Do they share any factor other than 1?"

5. Simplify 12/16.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Be careful — there are several common factors. Make sure you find the greatest one so you simplify in one step."

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

Solve the following problems. Show your work.

1. Simplify 8/20.
2. Simplify 9/15.
3. Is 7/12 in simplest form? Explain how you know.

**Scoring:**
- 3/3 correct = Mastery
- 2/3 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-1/3 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met

- Pair the student with a peer tutor for a reteach session using fraction strips. Have them physically show that 8/20 and 2/5 cover the same area.
- Provide a "factor rainbow" graphic organizer to help students systematically list factors.
- Reteach in small group using numbers under 12 first (e.g., simplify 2/4, 3/6, 4/8) before advancing to larger numbers.

### If Mastery Is Met

- Have the student create a "Simplify This!" mini-quiz with 5 problems for a classmate to solve.
- Challenge them to find all fractions with a denominator of 24 that simplify to a fraction with a denominator of 6.

## Extension (Optional)

**Fraction Art Activity:** Students create a piece of art on grid paper by shading fractional parts of different shapes. Each shaded portion must be labeled with an unsimplified fraction and its simplified form. For example, a rectangle with 6 out of 10 squares shaded is labeled "6/10 = 3/5." Display completed artwork on the class bulletin board.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors

- **Not finding the greatest common factor.** Students may divide by a common factor that is not the greatest (e.g., simplifying 8/12 by dividing by 2 to get 4/6 instead of dividing by 4 to get 2/3). Remind students that if they can still simplify, the fraction is not yet in simplest form. Teach the "double-check": after simplifying, ask "do the numerator and denominator still share a common factor?"
- **Dividing only the numerator or only the denominator.** Stress that you must divide both parts of the fraction by the same number to keep it equivalent.
- **Thinking that simplifying changes the value.** Use visual models to reinforce that 6/8 and 3/4 represent the same amount.
- **Confusing factors with multiples.** If a student lists multiples instead of factors, review the difference: factors go into a number, multiples are products of a number.

### Pacing Advice

- If the Warm-Up takes longer because students struggle with listing factors, spend an extra 2-3 minutes on it. Factor fluency is essential for this lesson.
- During Guided Practice, if most students handle problems 1-3 quickly, move on. If not, do an additional example before Independent Practice.
- The Interleaved Review problems may take longer for students who need to find common denominators. Allow them to finish these for homework if time runs short.

## Answer Key

### Guided Practice

1. 6/9: GCF of 6 and 9 is 3. 6/3 = 2, 9/3 = 3. **Answer: 2/3**
2. 10/15: GCF of 10 and 15 is 5. 10/5 = 2, 15/5 = 3. **Answer: 2/3**
3. 4/12: GCF of 4 and 12 is 4. 4/4 = 1, 12/4 = 3. **Answer: 1/3**
