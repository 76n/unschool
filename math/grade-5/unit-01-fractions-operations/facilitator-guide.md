# Facilitator Guide — Grade 5 Math, Unit 1: Adding and Subtracting Fractions

**Grade:** 5
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 1 — Adding and Subtracting Fractions
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (facilitator leads instruction; student works independently for practice)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit builds on Grade 4's introduction to fractions to develop fluency with fraction addition and subtraction. Students learn to add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators by finding common denominators. They also work with mixed numbers and apply these skills to word problems.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Add and subtract fractions with like denominators (review from Grade 4)
- Find equivalent fractions using multiplication
- Find the least common denominator (LCD) of two fractions
- Add fractions with unlike denominators
- Subtract fractions with unlike denominators
- Add and subtract mixed numbers (with and without regrouping)
- Simplify fractions to lowest terms
- Solve word problems involving fraction addition and subtraction

**Why this matters:** Fraction operations are a critical gateway to algebra, ratios, and proportional reasoning. Students who understand fractions conceptually — not just procedurally — are far better prepared for middle school mathematics.

---

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm:

- [ ] Understands what a fraction represents (parts of a whole)
- [ ] Can identify numerator and denominator
- [ ] Can add and subtract fractions with the SAME denominator (e.g., 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8)
- [ ] Can convert between improper fractions and mixed numbers
- [ ] Can find equivalent fractions (e.g., 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6)
- [ ] Knows basic multiplication facts (needed for finding common denominators)

## Materials Needed for This Unit

- **Fraction strips or fraction circles** (can be homemade from paper)
- **Number line** (marked in halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, eighths, twelfths)
- **Paper and pencil**
- **Colored pencils** (for fraction models)

## Pacing Guidance

| Lesson | Topic | Sessions |
|--------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Review: Adding and Subtracting Like Denominators | 1 |
| 2 | Equivalent Fractions Review | 1 |
| 3 | Finding the Least Common Denominator (LCD) | 1 |
| 4 | Adding Fractions with Unlike Denominators | 1-2 |
| 5 | Subtracting Fractions with Unlike Denominators | 1 |
| 6 | Simplifying Fractions | 1 |
| 7 | Adding Mixed Numbers | 1-2 |
| 8 | Subtracting Mixed Numbers (No Regrouping) | 1 |
| 9 | Subtracting Mixed Numbers (With Regrouping) | 1-2 |
| 10 | Fraction Word Problems | 1 |
| — | Unit Assessment | Separate session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lessons 3-4 (LCD and unlike denominators) are the conceptual core. Take extra time if needed.
- Lesson 9 (subtracting mixed numbers with regrouping) is the hardest skill. Budget extra time.
- If equivalent fractions (Lesson 2) are not solid, do NOT proceed — everything else depends on them.

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1/3 + 1/4 = 2/7 (adds numerators AND denominators) | Doesn't understand that you need common denominators | Use fraction strips. Show that 1/3 and 1/4 are different-sized pieces — you can't just count them together. |
| Student finds a common denominator but doesn't convert both fractions | Forgets to multiply the numerator too | Emphasize: "Whatever you do to the bottom, you must do to the top." Use equivalent fractions visually. |
| 3/4 − 1/2 = 2/2 = 1 (subtracts numerators and denominators separately) | Same misconception as adding: doesn't find common denominators first | Return to fraction strips and visual models. |
| Struggles with mixed number regrouping (e.g., 3 1/4 − 1 3/4) | Doesn't know how to "borrow" a whole and convert to fraction form | Show with models: 3 1/4 = 2 + 1 + 1/4 = 2 + 4/4 + 1/4 = 2 5/4. Now subtract. |
| Doesn't simplify answers | Either doesn't know how or forgets | Teach the "can both the top and bottom be divided by the same number?" check. |

## Conversation Starters

- "If I eat 1/3 of a pizza and you eat 1/4, did we eat more or less than the whole pizza? How much did we eat together?"
- "Why can't we just add 1/3 + 1/4 = 2/7? What's wrong with that?"
- "How is subtracting mixed numbers like subtracting two-digit numbers with regrouping?"
- "Can a fraction be greater than 1? What does that look like?"

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if:

- The student doesn't understand what a fraction represents (Grade 3 concept)
- Equivalent fractions are not understood after instruction (foundational for everything in this unit)
- Multiplication facts are not fluent (needed for finding common denominators)
- The student shows persistent confusion between addition procedures and fraction procedures
