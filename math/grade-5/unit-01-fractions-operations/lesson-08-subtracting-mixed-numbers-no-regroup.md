# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 1, Lesson 8

## Topic

Subtracting Mixed Numbers (No Regrouping)

## Learning Objectives

- Students will subtract mixed numbers with like denominators when no regrouping is needed.
- Students will subtract mixed numbers with unlike denominators by first finding a common denominator, when no regrouping is needed.
- Students will simplify all answers to simplest form.
- Students will verify subtraction answers using addition.

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of mixed numbers
- Subtracting fractions with like and unlike denominators (Lessons 1-5)
- Finding least common denominators
- Simplifying fractions (Lesson 6)
- Adding mixed numbers (Lesson 7)

## Materials Needed

- Whiteboard and markers
- Fraction strips or fraction bar manipulatives
- Student math journals
- Pencils
- Printed practice worksheet (Independent Practice problems)
- Exit ticket slips

**Standards:** 5.NF.1

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review subtraction of fractions with unlike denominators and recall adding mixed numbers, setting the stage for subtracting mixed numbers.

1. Write on the board: "Solve: 7/8 - 1/4."
2. Give students 1 minute to solve. (LCD = 8. 7/8 - 2/8 = 5/8.)
3. Write: "Solve: 3 1/5 + 2 2/5."
4. Give students 1 minute to solve. (5 3/5.)
5. Say: "We have been adding mixed numbers. Today we will learn to subtract mixed numbers. The process is very similar — we work with the whole numbers and fractions separately."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary

- **Subtract:** To find the difference between two numbers.
- **No regrouping:** The fraction being subtracted (the second fraction) is smaller than or equal to the fraction it is being subtracted from, so we do not need to borrow from the whole number.
- **Simplest form:** The fraction with the numerator and denominator divided by their GCF.
- **Check with addition:** After subtracting, add the answer to the number you subtracted; you should get the original number back.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Present the strategy for subtracting mixed numbers.**

Write on the board:
"To subtract mixed numbers (no regrouping):
1. Subtract the whole numbers.
2. Subtract the fractions (find a common denominator if needed).
3. Simplify if needed.
4. Check: add the answer to the number you subtracted."

**Step 2: Model an example with like denominators.**

Write: 5 3/4 - 2 1/4

- Subtract whole numbers: 5 - 2 = 3.
- Subtract fractions: 3/4 - 1/4 = 2/4.
- Simplify: 2/4 = 1/2.
- Answer: **3 1/2**
- Check: 3 1/2 + 2 1/4 = 3 2/4 + 2 1/4 = 5 3/4. Correct!

**Step 3: Model an example with unlike denominators.**

Write: 7 5/6 - 3 1/3

- Subtract whole numbers: 7 - 3 = 4.
- Subtract fractions: 5/6 - 1/3. LCD = 6. Convert: 5/6 - 2/6 = 3/6.
- Simplify: 3/6 = 1/2.
- Answer: **4 1/2**
- Check: 4 1/2 + 3 1/3 = 4 3/6 + 3 2/6 = 7 5/6. Correct!

**Step 4: Model another example with unlike denominators.**

Write: 9 3/4 - 4 1/8

- Subtract whole numbers: 9 - 4 = 5.
- Subtract fractions: 3/4 - 1/8. LCD = 8. Convert: 6/8 - 1/8 = 5/8.
- Check: GCF of 5 and 8 is 1 — already simplified.
- Answer: **5 5/8**
- Check: 5 5/8 + 4 1/8 = 9 6/8 = 9 3/4. Correct!

**Step 5: Stress when this method works.**

"This method works when the fraction you are subtracting is smaller than or equal to the fraction you are subtracting from. Look at 5 3/4 - 2 1/4: we can subtract 1/4 from 3/4 with no problem. In our next lesson, we will learn what to do when the fraction we are subtracting is larger — that requires regrouping."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 6 4/5 - 2 1/5
- Wholes: 6 - 2 = 4. Fractions: 4/5 - 1/5 = 3/5.
- Answer: **4 3/5**

**Example B:** 8 2/3 - 5 1/6
- Wholes: 8 - 5 = 3. Fractions: 2/3 - 1/6. LCD = 6. 4/6 - 1/6 = 3/6 = 1/2.
- Answer: **3 1/2**

**Example C:** 10 7/10 - 3 1/5
- Wholes: 10 - 3 = 7. Fractions: 7/10 - 1/5. LCD = 10. 7/10 - 2/10 = 5/10 = 1/2.
- Answer: **7 1/2**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems as a class. Students try each problem in their journals first, then share answers.

1. 4 3/5 - 1 1/5
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Subtract the wholes, then the fractions. Can you subtract 1/5 from 3/5?"

2. 6 5/8 - 2 1/8
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "These have like denominators. Subtract the whole numbers, then the fractions. Do you need to simplify?"

3. 8 3/4 - 3 1/2
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "The denominators are different. What is the LCD of 4 and 2? Convert before subtracting."

4. 5 7/10 - 2 1/5
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Convert 1/5 to tenths first. Then subtract."

5. 9 5/6 - 4 1/3
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "After subtracting, check your answer: can you simplify? Then verify with addition."

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

Solve the following problems. Show all work. Write answers in simplest form.

1. 6 5/8 - 3 1/8
2. 7 3/4 - 2 1/3

**Scoring:**
- 2/2 correct with work shown = Mastery
- 1/2 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0/2 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met

- Use fraction strip manipulatives to model subtraction. Have the student build the first mixed number with strips, then physically remove the strips representing the second mixed number.
- Provide a step-by-step checklist card: (1) Subtract wholes, (2) Find LCD if needed, (3) Subtract fractions, (4) Simplify, (5) Check with addition.
- Practice with 3-4 additional like-denominator problems before attempting unlike denominators.

### If Mastery Is Met

- Give the student a "work backward" challenge: "The answer to a subtraction problem is 4 1/6. The number that was subtracted is 3 2/3. What was the original number?"
- Have the student create a subtraction problem that requires finding a common denominator and results in a fraction that needs simplifying.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors

- **Subtracting the smaller whole number from the larger regardless of position.** For example, in 7 - 3, a student might write 3 - 7 instead. Reinforce that we always subtract the bottom number from the top number in vertical format, or the second from the first in horizontal format.
- **Forgetting to convert to a common denominator.** Students may subtract numerators directly even when denominators differ. Always ask: "Are the denominators the same? If not, what do you need to do first?"
- **Not simplifying.** Students may leave answers like 3 2/4 instead of 3 1/2. Remind them to check at the end.
- **Trying to regroup when they don't need to.** Some students may be confused about when regrouping is needed. Emphasize: "Look at the fractions — can you subtract the second fraction from the first? If yes, no regrouping needed."

### Pacing Advice

- Like-denominator subtraction (Step 2) should be quick since it mirrors addition from Lesson 7. Spend no more than 2-3 minutes on it.
- Unlike-denominator subtraction (Steps 3-4) is the core of this lesson. Spend 6-8 minutes here.
- If students are very comfortable, you may preview what happens when the fraction you need to subtract is larger (setting up Lesson 9), but do not teach regrouping in this lesson.
- Interleaved Review problems should take 2-3 minutes. If time is short, assign them as homework.
