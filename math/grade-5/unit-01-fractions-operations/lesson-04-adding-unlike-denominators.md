# Math — Grade 5 — Unit 1, Lesson 4

## Topic
Adding Fractions with Unlike Denominators

## Learning Objectives
- Students will add two fractions with unlike denominators by finding the LCD, converting to equivalent fractions, and adding the numerators.
- Students will express sums in simplest form.
- Students will verify their answers using visual models or estimation.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to add fractions with like denominators (Lesson 1).
- Ability to generate equivalent fractions (Lesson 2).
- Ability to find the least common denominator of two fractions (Lesson 3).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Fraction strips (1 set per student).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- "Adding Unlike Fractions" process chart (Steps 1-4) posted on wall or projected.
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 5.NF.1 — Add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators (including mixed numbers) by replacing given fractions with equivalent fractions in such a way as to produce an equivalent sum or difference of fractions with like denominators.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review LCD and equivalent fraction conversion to prepare for today's full addition process.

1. Write on the board: "Find the LCD of 1/3 and 1/4, then rewrite both fractions with that denominator."
2. Give students 2 minutes to work independently. (LCD = 12; 1/3 = 4/12; 1/4 = 3/12.)
3. Ask: "Now that both fractions have the same denominator, what would you do if you wanted to add them?" (Add the numerators: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.)
4. Tell students: "That is exactly what we are learning today — the full process for adding fractions with unlike denominators."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Unlike denominators** — fractions with different denominators (e.g., 1/3 and 1/4).
- **LCD (Least Common Denominator)** — the smallest number that both denominators divide into evenly.
- **Equivalent fractions** — fractions that name the same amount, used here to rewrite fractions with a common denominator.
- **Simplest form** — a fraction whose numerator and denominator share no common factor other than 1.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Present the 4-step process.**
Post or write the following process chart on the board:

1. Find the LCD of the two denominators.
2. Rewrite each fraction as an equivalent fraction with the LCD.
3. Add the numerators. Keep the denominator the same.
4. Simplify the sum if possible.

"These four steps work every time you add fractions with unlike denominators."

**Step 2 — Model with the anchor problem: 1/3 + 1/4.**
- Step 1: LCD of 3 and 4. Multiples of 3: 3, 6, 9, 12. Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12. LCD = 12.
- Step 2: 1/3 = 4/12 and 1/4 = 3/12.
- Step 3: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.
- Step 4: Is 7/12 in simplest form? 7 and 12 share no common factor other than 1. Yes, 7/12 is the final answer.

**Step 3 — Verify with fraction strips.**
Lay the 1/3 strip and the 1/4 strip end to end. Compare the total length to twelfths strips. Confirm that 1/3 + 1/4 lines up with 7 twelfths.

**Step 4 — Model a second problem: 2/5 + 1/4.**
- Step 1: LCD of 5 and 4. Multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, 20. Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20. LCD = 20.
- Step 2: 2/5 = 8/20 and 1/4 = 5/20.
- Step 3: 8/20 + 5/20 = 13/20.
- Step 4: 13 and 20 share no common factor other than 1. Final answer: 13/20.

**Step 5 — Model a problem that requires simplifying: 1/4 + 1/6.**
- Step 1: LCD of 4 and 6. Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12. Multiples of 6: 6, 12. LCD = 12.
- Step 2: 1/4 = 3/12 and 1/6 = 2/12.
- Step 3: 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12.
- Step 4: 5 and 12 share no common factor other than 1. Final answer: 5/12.

### Worked Examples

**Example A — 1/3 + 1/4**
1. LCD of 3 and 4 = 12
2. 1/3 = 4/12; 1/4 = 3/12
3. 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12
4. 7/12 is in simplest form.
**Answer: 7/12**

**Example B — 2/5 + 1/4**
1. LCD of 5 and 4 = 20
2. 2/5 = 8/20; 1/4 = 5/20
3. 8/20 + 5/20 = 13/20
4. 13/20 is in simplest form.
**Answer: 13/20**

**Example C — 1/6 + 3/8**
1. LCD of 6 and 8 = 24
2. 1/6 = 4/24; 3/8 = 9/24
3. 4/24 + 9/24 = 13/24
4. 13/24 is in simplest form.
**Answer: 13/24**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. Students attempt each problem first, then discuss.

1. 1/2 + 1/3 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What is the LCD of 2 and 3? Rewrite both fractions, then add."

2. 1/4 + 2/5 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What is the LCD of 4 and 5? Remember to convert both fractions."

3. 2/3 + 1/6 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Is 6 already a multiple of 3? If so, what is the LCD?"

4. 3/8 + 1/4 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Do you need to convert both fractions, or just one?"

5. 1/3 + 3/5 = ?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "After adding, check — is your answer in simplest form?"

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
Solve the following two problems. Show all four steps of your work.

**Problem 1:** 1/4 + 1/3 = ?
**Problem 2:** 2/5 + 1/6 = ?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct with all steps shown = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct or correct answer but steps missing = Partial understanding; review the process.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which step is causing difficulty (finding LCD, converting fractions, or adding numerators).
- If LCD is the issue, review Lesson 3 with concrete number line or multiple-listing activities.
- If conversion is the issue, review Lesson 2 with fraction strips.
- Provide a laminated "4-Step Process" card for the student to reference during future practice.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Students may begin the Extension activity below.
- Students may create their own addition problems for a partner to solve, including an answer key.

## Extension (Optional)
- **Fraction Sum Puzzles:** Give students a target sum (e.g., 11/12). Challenge them to find two fractions with unlike denominators that add up to the target. How many different pairs can they find?
- **Real-World Application:** Find a simple recipe online. Identify two fractional ingredient amounts with unlike denominators and calculate their combined total.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Adding the denominators.** For example, writing 1/3 + 1/4 = 2/7. This is the most common error. Redirect immediately: "Did you find a common denominator first? You cannot add fractions until the pieces are the same size."
- **Finding the LCD correctly but converting only one fraction.** Remind students that *both* fractions must be rewritten with the LCD before adding.
- **Converting fractions incorrectly.** For example, converting 1/3 to 3/12 instead of 4/12 (multiplying only the denominator). Reinforce: "Whatever you multiply the denominator by, you must multiply the numerator by the same number."
- **Forgetting to simplify.** Encourage students to always check their answer by asking: "Do the numerator and denominator share any common factor?"

### Pacing Advice
- The 4-step process chart is a critical scaffold. Keep it visible throughout the lesson and for subsequent lessons.
- If students breeze through the Warm-Up, transition quickly into the first worked example.
- If many students struggle during Guided Practice, slow down and do Problems 1-3 as a whole class before having students attempt Problems 4-5 with a partner.
- Some students may benefit from writing the 4 steps on their paper as headings before solving each problem.
- This is the culminating skill of the unit's first half. Take the time to build fluency here before proceeding to subtraction in Lesson 5.
