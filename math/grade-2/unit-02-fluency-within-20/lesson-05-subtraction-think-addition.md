# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 2, Lesson 5

## Topic
Subtraction: Think-Addition Strategy

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve subtraction facts within 20 by thinking of the related addition fact (e.g., 14 - 6 = ? becomes "6 + ? = 14")
- Explain why the think-addition strategy works using fact families
- Solve at least 10 subtraction facts within 20 using think-addition

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 4: Fact families (understanding that addition and subtraction are inverse operations)
- Fluency with addition strategies (doubles, near-doubles, make 10) from Lessons 1-3

## Materials Needed
- 20 counters
- Paper and pencil
- Flashcards — subtraction facts within 20
- Number line 0-20

**Standards:** 2.OA.2

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review fact families and activate the connection between addition and subtraction.

1. Write the fact family for 6, 9, 15: ___, ___, ___, ___
2. If 7 + 8 = 15, then 15 - 7 = ___
3. 8 + ___ = 12
4. 5 + ___ = 11

**Facilitator note:** Problems 3 and 4 are "missing addend" problems — they are subtraction in disguise. If the student solves them by thinking "What do I add to 8 to get 12?" they are already using think-addition. Point this out: "You just did subtraction by using addition! That is exactly what we are learning today."

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Think-addition:** A subtraction strategy where you ask "What do I add to the smaller number to get the bigger number?"
- **Missing addend:** The unknown number in an addition equation (in 6 + ? = 14, the missing addend is 8)
- **Minuend:** The number you start with in subtraction (in 14 - 6, the minuend is 14)
- **Subtrahend:** The number you subtract (in 14 - 6, the subtrahend is 6)

### Instruction Notes

**The Big Idea (3 minutes)**

Say: "Yesterday you learned that addition and subtraction are in the same family. Today we are going to use that to make subtraction EASY. Here is the secret: every subtraction problem is really an addition problem in disguise."

Write on paper:
- **14 - 6 = ?** is the SAME as asking **6 + ? = 14**

Say: "Instead of thinking 'What is 14 minus 6?' you think 'What do I ADD to 6 to GET 14?' If you know your addition facts, you can answer subtraction questions quickly."

**Step 1: Demonstrate with Counters (4 minutes)**

Place 14 counters on the table.
Say: "I want to subtract 6." Cover 6 counters with your hand. "How many are showing?"
The student counts: 8.
Say: "So 14 - 6 = 8. But I could also have asked: I have 6 counters hidden and 14 total. How many are showing? That is 6 + ? = 14. Same question!"

Do another:
Place 11 counters. Cover 4.
"11 - 4 = ? Think: 4 + ? = 11. I know 4 + 7 = 11, so 11 - 4 = 7."

**Step 2: Practice the Think-Addition Process (5 minutes)**

Model the thinking out loud for each problem:

- **13 - 8 = ?**
  Think: "8 + ? = 13. I know 8 + 5 = 13 (make-10: 8 + 2 + 3 = 13). So 13 - 8 = 5."

- **15 - 9 = ?**
  Think: "9 + ? = 15. I know 9 + 6 = 15 (make-10: 9 + 1 + 5 = 15). So 15 - 9 = 6."

- **12 - 7 = ?**
  Think: "7 + ? = 12. I know 7 + 5 = 12 (near-doubles: 6 + 6 = 12, or make-10: 7 + 3 + 2 = 12). So 12 - 7 = 5."

Say: "Notice that I am using all the addition strategies you already know — doubles, near-doubles, and make-10 — to solve subtraction! You do not need new strategies for subtraction."

**Step 3: When Think-Addition Is Especially Helpful (3 minutes)**

Say: "Think-addition is most helpful when the number being subtracted is close to the answer."

- **11 - 5 = ?** Think: "5 + ? = 11. 5 + 5 = 10, plus 1 more = 11. So 5 + 6 = 11. Answer: 6." (Used doubles!)
- **16 - 8 = ?** Think: "8 + ? = 16. 8 + 8 = 16. Answer: 8." (Used doubles!)

Say: "If you know your doubles, you can solve lots of subtraction facts instantly!"

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 14 - 8 = ?
- Think-addition: 8 + ? = 14
- I know 8 + 6 = 14 (make-10: 8 + 2 + 4 = 14)
- So 14 - 8 = 6
- Check: 8 + 6 = 14. Correct!

**Example B:** 17 - 9 = ?
- Think-addition: 9 + ? = 17
- I know 9 + 8 = 17 (make-10: 9 + 1 + 7 = 17)
- So 17 - 9 = 8
- Check: 9 + 8 = 17. Correct!

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

For each problem, have the student say the think-addition sentence out loud before solving.

1. 13 - 5 = ___
   *Prompt: "Say: 5 + what equals 13?"*
   (5 + 8 = 13, so 13 - 5 = 8)

2. 11 - 7 = ___
   *Prompt: "Think: 7 + what equals 11?"*
   (7 + 4 = 11, so 11 - 7 = 4)

3. 16 - 9 = ___
   *If the student hesitates: "What do you add to 9 to make 16? Use make-10 if it helps."*
   (9 + 7 = 16, so 16 - 9 = 7)

4. 15 - 8 = ___
   *Ask: "What addition strategy helps you find 8 + ? = 15?"*
   (8 + 7 = 15, so 15 - 8 = 7)

5. 12 - 6 = ___
   *Ask: "Do you recognize a doubles fact hiding here?"*
   (6 + 6 = 12, so 12 - 6 = 6)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
Solve:
1. 13 - 9 = ___
2. 15 - 7 = ___
3. 11 - 6 = ___
4. 16 - 8 = ___

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-2/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student cannot connect subtraction to addition: Return to fact families (Lesson 4). Use counters to show the part-part-whole relationship for every missed problem.
- If the student understands the idea but is slow: This is expected at this stage. Speed will come with practice in Lessons 6-8. Focus on accuracy now.
- Practice 5 subtraction facts with counters and the think-addition prompt before moving on.

### If Mastery Is Met
Say: "You now have a powerful tool: any addition fact you know instantly gives you a subtraction fact. In the next three lessons, we will practice all the facts until they are super fast. We will start with the easiest group — sums to 10."

## Extension (Optional)

**Subtraction Detective:** Give the student these subtraction facts and ask them to name the addition fact that helps:
- 13 - 4 = ? (4 + 9 = 13, so answer is 9)
- 18 - 9 = ? (9 + 9 = 18, so answer is 9)
- 11 - 3 = ? (3 + 8 = 11, so answer is 8)

**Number Bond Challenge:** Draw a number bond (circle at top, two circles at bottom connected by lines). Put 16 at the top and 7 in one bottom circle. "What goes in the other circle? How do you know?" (9, because 7 + 9 = 16.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Student subtracts instead of thinking addition:** For 14 - 6, student tries to count back from 14, losing track. Fix: Explicitly say "Instead of going backward, go forward. What do I add to 6 to reach 14?"
2. **Confusing which number to start from:** For 14 - 6, student thinks "14 + ? = 6" instead of "6 + ? = 14." Fix: Say "We start from the SMALLER number and add UP to the bigger number."
3. **Think-addition works but is slow:** Student understands the concept but still takes 5-10 seconds per fact. Fix: This is normal for Lesson 5. Speed building happens in Lessons 6-8. Praise the correct strategy.
4. **Not connecting to previously learned addition strategies:** Student uses think-addition but then counts on from the subtrahend. Fix: Prompt the specific strategy: "You need 8 + ? = 15. Can you use make-10? What does 8 need to get to 10?"

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson is conceptually important — make sure the student truly understands WHY think-addition works before drilling for speed.
- If the student already naturally uses think-addition, move quickly through direct instruction and spend more time on independent practice.
- Target total time: 35-40 minutes.
