# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 2, Lesson 10

## Topic
Timed Challenge: Self-Assessment, Progress Tracking, and Celebrating Growth

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Complete a timed assessment of mixed addition and subtraction facts within 20
- Track their own fluency progress using a progress chart
- Identify facts they have mastered and facts they are still working on
- Celebrate their growth from the beginning of the unit to now

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-9: All strategies, fluency practice, and games
- Student should be accurate with most facts within 20

## Materials Needed
- Timer or stopwatch
- Paper and pencil
- Colored pencils or crayons (for progress chart)
- Timed Challenge worksheets (provided below — or write the problems on paper)
- Graph paper or a printed progress chart template
- Flashcards (for targeted follow-up)

**Standards:** 2.OA.2

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Light practice to calm nerves and activate recall before the timed challenge.

Say: "Today is your Timed Challenge! But first, let's warm up. This is not a test — it is a chance to see how much you have grown."

Solve orally — no pressure, just warm up:
1. 5 + 5 = ___
2. 8 + 7 = ___
3. 12 - 4 = ___
4. 16 - 9 = ___

Say: "You solved those well. Remember — today, try your best, but do not worry about being perfect. The goal is to see YOUR progress, not to compare yourself to anyone else."

---

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Timed challenge:** A practice activity where you solve as many facts as possible in a set time
- **Progress tracking:** Recording your scores over time to see improvement
- **Personal best:** Your own best score — the only one that matters

### Instruction Notes

**Setting the Right Tone (3 minutes)**

Say: "Here is what is going to happen today:"

1. "You will solve as many addition and subtraction facts as you can in 2 minutes."
2. "I will time you. When I say 'Start,' begin solving. When I say 'Stop,' put your pencil down."
3. "Skip any problem that feels too hard — come back to it if you have time."
4. "We will score it together — correct answers only."
5. "Then you will chart your score on a progress graph."

Say: "This is NOT a test. This is a challenge against yourself. The question is not 'Did I get a perfect score?' The question is 'How much have I improved?'"

**Setting Up the Progress Chart (2 minutes)**

On graph paper, help the student create a simple bar chart:
- X-axis: "Challenge Number" (1, 2, 3, 4, 5...)
- Y-axis: "Facts Correct in 2 Minutes" (0 to 40, in increments of 5)

Say: "After today's challenge, you will color in your first bar. Each time we do this, you will add another bar. You will be able to SEE yourself getting faster!"

### Worked Examples

Model the timed challenge process with 3 sample facts:
- Show: 4 + 3 = 7 (write the answer quickly)
- Show: 9 + 6 = 15 (write the answer)
- Show: 11 - 5 = 6 (write the answer)

Say: "See? Just write the answer. Do not show your work. If you know it, write it. If you are stuck, skip it."

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
This lesson's check for understanding is the timed challenge itself.

**Scoring Benchmarks:**

| Correct in 2 min | Accuracy | Level |
|-------------------|----------|-------|
| 30+ correct | 90%+ | Mastery — ready for Unit Assessment |
| 20-29 correct | 85%+ | Approaching mastery — continue daily practice |
| Under 20 correct | Below 85% | Needs more practice — repeat Lessons 6-8 as needed |

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify the facts the student got wrong or skipped. These are the focus facts.
- Create a "Personal Practice Pack" of 10-15 flashcards with just these facts.
- Practice the Personal Pack for 5 minutes daily for 1-2 weeks before taking the Unit Assessment.
- Repeat the Timed Challenge every 3-4 days to track progress.

### If Mastery Is Met
Say: "Look at how many facts you can solve in just 2 minutes! You should be really proud. Your brain has learned to do something amazing — it turned hard thinking into fast, automatic knowledge. You are ready for the Unit Assessment!"

## Extension (Optional)

**Goal Setting:** Have the student set a personal goal for their next timed challenge. Write it on the progress chart: "Next time, I want to get ___ correct in 2 minutes."

**Teach a Friend:** Ask the student: "If you were teaching a younger student who did not know their facts yet, what would you teach them first? What strategies would you share?" Have them write or say their advice.

**Fact Trophy Card:** Create an index card "trophy" for the student listing their achievements:
- "I know all my doubles!"
- "I can use make-10!"
- "I solved ___ facts in 2 minutes!"

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Anxiety interferes with performance:** Student freezes or works much slower than in practice. Fix: Reassure them. "This is not a grade. Just do your best. Skip the hard ones." Consider reducing the time to 1 minute to make it feel less overwhelming.
2. **Rushing causes careless errors:** Student writes wrong answers to go fast. Fix: "Let's focus on accuracy first. A wrong answer does not count, so it is better to slow down and be correct."
3. **Student is discouraged by their score:** Fix: "You solved [X] problems correctly in 2 minutes. That means you know [X] facts really well! Let's look at which ones you missed and practice those."
4. **Student wants to do many rounds:** If the student is energized and wants to keep doing challenges, let them! But stop after 3 rounds to prevent fatigue-induced errors.

### Pacing Advice
- Spend the most time on the actual timed challenge and the debrief/charting. These are the core of the lesson.
- The warm-up and mini practice round are important for reducing anxiety. Do not skip them.
- If the student is very anxious, do the timed challenge as a "game" rather than a "challenge": "Let's see how many we can do together!"
- This lesson can be repeated weekly throughout the rest of the school year as a maintenance activity.
- Target total time: 35-40 minutes.
