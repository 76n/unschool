# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 2, Lesson 2

## Topic
Doubles and Near-Doubles Fluency

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Recall all doubles facts from 1 + 1 through 9 + 9 within 3 seconds each
- Use a known doubles fact to solve near-doubles (e.g., 6 + 7 = 6 + 6 + 1 = 13)
- Explain why near-doubles work by connecting to the doubles fact

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: Review of addition strategies (counting on, doubles, make 10)
- Ability to add 1 to any number within 20

## Materials Needed
- Doubles flashcards (9 cards: 1+1 through 9+9)
- 20 counters
- Two ten-frames
- Paper and pencil
- Number line 0-20

**Standards:** 2.OA.2

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick oral drill on doubles to assess starting fluency.

Say: "I am going to say a doubles fact. Tell me the answer as fast as you can. Ready?"

Call out these facts one at a time. Note which ones are automatic (< 2 seconds) and which require thinking:
1. 3 + 3 = ___
2. 7 + 7 = ___
3. 5 + 5 = ___
4. 9 + 9 = ___

**Facilitator note:** Most students know the smaller doubles (1+1 through 5+5) quickly. The larger doubles (6+6 through 9+9) often need more practice. Pay attention to which facts need the most work.

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Doubles fact:** An addition fact where both addends are the same number (e.g., 4 + 4 = 8)
- **Near-doubles fact:** An addition fact where the addends differ by exactly 1 (e.g., 4 + 5 = 9)
- **Addend:** A number being added

### Instruction Notes

**Part A — Mastering Doubles (5 minutes)**

Say: "Doubles are the anchors of your addition facts. Once you know them by heart, they help you solve lots of other facts."

Write the complete doubles chart together. For each fact, use a real-world connection to make it memorable:

| Fact | Answer | Memory Hint |
|------|--------|-------------|
| 1 + 1 | 2 | One hand has 1 thumb, the other has 1 thumb — 2 thumbs! |
| 2 + 2 | 4 | A bicycle has 2 wheels. Two bicycles have 4 wheels. |
| 3 + 3 | 6 | An insect has 3 legs on each side — 6 legs! |
| 4 + 4 | 8 | A spider has 4 legs on each side — 8 legs! |
| 5 + 5 | 10 | 5 fingers on each hand — 10 fingers! |
| 6 + 6 | 12 | A dozen eggs is 6 in each row — 12! |
| 7 + 7 | 14 | Two weeks — 7 days each — is 14 days! |
| 8 + 8 | 16 | Two octopuses have 16 arms! |
| 9 + 9 | 18 | A 9-pin half of bowling has 18 pins total! |

Practice: Point to facts randomly and have the student answer. Repeat until each fact comes in under 3 seconds.

**Part B — Near-Doubles (5 minutes)**

Say: "Now let's use our doubles to solve near-doubles. A near-double is when the two numbers are just 1 apart."

**Step 1:** Write 6 + 7 on paper.
Say: "I see that 6 and 7 are neighbors — they are only 1 apart. I know 6 + 6 = 12. Since 7 is one MORE than 6, the answer is 12 + 1 = 13."

**Step 2:** Demonstrate with counters on ten-frames:
- Place 6 counters in one group, 7 in another.
- "Look — the 7 group is the same as the 6 group, plus one extra."

**Step 3:** Write 8 + 9 on paper.
Say: "8 and 9 are neighbors. I know 8 + 8 = 16. Since 9 is one more than 8, the answer is 16 + 1 = 17."

**Step 4:** Show an alternative approach: "You can also think of it as: 9 + 9 = 18, and 8 is one LESS than 9, so 18 - 1 = 17. Either way works!"

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 5 + 6
- I know the doubles fact: 5 + 5 = 10.
- 6 is one more than 5, so: 10 + 1 = 11.
- Answer: 5 + 6 = 11.

**Example B:** 7 + 8
- I know the doubles fact: 7 + 7 = 14.
- 8 is one more than 7, so: 14 + 1 = 15.
- Answer: 7 + 8 = 15.
- Check: I also know 8 + 8 = 16, and 7 is one less than 8, so 16 - 1 = 15. Same answer!

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Part A — Doubles Speed Round (3 minutes):**
Call out doubles facts in random order. Student answers orally. Go through all 9 doubles at least twice.

**Part B — Near-Doubles (7 minutes):**

1. 4 + 5 = ___
   *Ask: "What doubles fact helps you here?"* (4 + 4 = 8, so 4 + 5 = 9)

2. 7 + 6 = ___
   *Ask: "Does it matter which doubles fact you use — 6 + 6 or 7 + 7?"* (No: 6 + 6 + 1 = 13 or 7 + 7 - 1 = 13)

3. 8 + 7 = ___
   *If student hesitates: "What is 7 + 7? Now add one more."*

4. 9 + 8 = ___
   *Ask: "Which doubles fact did you use?"*

5. 3 + 4 = ___
   *Ask: "Could you also use counting on for this one? Which way is faster for you?"*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
Solve:
1. 8 + 8 = ___
2. 6 + 7 = ___
3. 9 + 8 = ___
4. 5 + 4 = ___

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-2/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If doubles are shaky: Drill the doubles chart daily for 3-5 minutes using flashcards. Focus on the facts the student misses most.
- If near-doubles are the issue: Practice more examples linking the near-double to its related double. Use counters to show the "one more" relationship.
- Re-assess after 2-3 days of targeted practice.

### If Mastery Is Met
Say: "You are becoming a doubles expert! Tomorrow we will work on the make-10 strategy and get really fast with facts like 8 + 5 and 9 + 6."

## Extension (Optional)

**Doubles Bingo:** Create a 3x3 bingo grid. Write doubles answers (2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18) in random order. The facilitator calls out a doubles fact (e.g., "7 + 7"). The student finds the answer and covers it. First to get three in a row wins!

**Pattern Hunting:** Look at the doubles answers: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18. Ask: "What do you notice about all the doubles answers?" (They are all even numbers.) "Why do you think that is?" (Because you are adding two of the same number — an even group.)

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Doubles confusion for 6+6 and above:** Students sometimes mix up 6+6=12 with 7+7=12. Reinforce with memory hints (dozen eggs = 12, two weeks = 14).
2. **Adding 2 instead of 1 for near-doubles:** Student does 7 + 8 as 7 + 7 + 2 = 16. Fix: Use counters. Line up 7 and 8. "Count the extra ones. Is it 1 extra or 2 extra?"
3. **Not recognizing a near-doubles opportunity:** Student does 6 + 7 by counting on from 6 (slow). Prompt: "Are these numbers neighbors? What doubles fact is hiding in there?"
4. **Reversing the strategy for near-doubles below:** Student does 6 + 5 as 6 + 6 + 1 = 13 (wrong — should subtract 1). Fix: Clarify: "5 is one LESS than 6, so start with 6 + 6 = 12 and subtract 1. Or use 5 + 5 = 10 and add 1."

### Pacing Advice
- Spend more time on Part A (doubles) if the student does not have them memorized. Doubles mastery is essential before near-doubles make sense.
- If the student already knows all doubles instantly, spend only 2 minutes on Part A and focus on near-doubles.
- Target total time: 35-40 minutes.
