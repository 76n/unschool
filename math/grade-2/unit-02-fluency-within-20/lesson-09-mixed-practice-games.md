# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 2, Lesson 9

## Topic
Mixed Practice Games for Fact Fluency

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Practice addition and subtraction facts within 20 through varied game formats
- Maintain accuracy while building speed in a low-pressure game context
- Identify which facts they still find challenging and which are automatic

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-8: All strategies and fluency practice for facts within 20
- Student should be accurate (not necessarily fast) with all facts within 20

## Materials Needed
- Two standard dice (6-sided)
- Flashcards — complete set of addition and subtraction facts within 20
- Paper and pencil
- Timer or stopwatch
- A deck of playing cards with face cards removed (Aces = 1, number cards = face value) — OR number cards labeled 1-10
- Counters or tokens (for game pieces)
- Colored pencils (optional, for coloring game)

**Standards:** 2.OA.2

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick mixed drill to activate all facts.

Solve orally — facilitator calls out facts, student answers as fast as possible:
1. 6 + 8 = ___
2. 15 - 9 = ___
3. 9 + 7 = ___
4. 12 - 5 = ___

Say: "Today is GAME DAY! We are going to play some fun math games to practice our facts. Games are one of the best ways to get faster because you are having fun while you practice."

---

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Mental math:** Solving problems in your head without writing anything down
- **Fluency game:** A game designed to practice math facts in a fun, engaging way

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Today is different from our other lessons. Instead of learning something new, we are going to play games that practice everything you have learned. I will teach you four different games. We will play each one for about 7-8 minutes."

**Rules for Game Day:**
1. **Speed matters, but accuracy matters more.** A wrong answer does not count.
2. **Say the whole fact out loud** when you solve it. This helps your brain remember it.
3. **No counting on fingers during games.** Use your strategies — doubles, near-doubles, make-10, think-addition. If you are stuck, say the strategy out loud.
4. **Have fun!** These are games, not tests.

### Worked Examples

Before starting games, demonstrate one round of each game so the student understands the rules. (See each game below.)

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

### Game 1: Addition War (10 minutes)

**Setup:** Divide the number cards (or playing cards, Aces through 10) into two equal piles — one for the student, one for the facilitator.

**How to play:**
1. Both players flip over the top card from their pile at the same time.
2. The first player to correctly call out the SUM of the two cards wins both cards.
3. If both players call out the answer at the same time, each player flips another card, and the first to call the sum of ALL four cards wins all four cards.
4. Play until one pile runs out. The player with more cards wins.

**Facilitator strategy:** Let the student win some rounds by pausing slightly. On harder facts, say the fact together if the student is stuck: "7 + 8... that is 15! Let's both remember that."

*After 5 minutes, switch to Subtraction War: flip two cards and subtract the smaller from the larger.*

---

## Independent Practice (20 minutes)

Play the following three games. Spend about 7 minutes on each. The student can play these with a partner (facilitator, sibling, or friend) or adapt them for solo play as described.

### Game 2: Roll and Add / Roll and Subtract (7 minutes)

**Setup:** Two standard dice. Paper and pencil for recording.

**How to play:**
1. Roll both dice. Add the two numbers. Write the addition fact and answer.
2. Roll again. This time, SUBTRACT the smaller number from the larger. Write the subtraction fact.
3. Alternate between addition and subtraction rolls.
4. After 3 minutes, count how many correct facts you have written.
5. Rest for 30 seconds, then try to beat your score in the next 3 minutes.

**Variation for harder facts:** Use one standard die and one special die (or spinner) showing 5-10. Or roll one die and add it to a fixed number (like 8 or 9): "8 + [die roll] = ?"

**Solo scoring:**
- Under 10 facts in 3 minutes: Keep practicing!
- 10-15 facts: Good pace!
- 16+ facts: Speed champion!

### Game 3: Flashcard Relay (7 minutes)

**Setup:** Full set of flashcards spread face-down on a table. Timer.

**How to play:**
1. Start the timer.
2. The student picks up a card, reads the fact, and says the answer.
3. If correct, the card goes in the "Done" pile. If incorrect or too slow (more than 5 seconds), the card goes in a "Try Again" pile.
4. Continue until all cards are in the "Done" pile.
5. Record the total time.
6. Shuffle the "Try Again" cards and go through them once more.

**Challenge mode:** Set a target time (e.g., 3 minutes for 20 cards). Can the student beat the clock?

### Game 4: Make the Target (7 minutes)

**Setup:** Number cards 1-10 (or playing cards). Paper and pencil.

**How to play:**
1. The facilitator draws a number card — this is the TARGET number (e.g., 14).
2. The student must find TWO number cards from their hand (deal 5 cards) that add up to the target.
3. If they can, they lay down the pair and score 1 point. Draw 2 replacement cards.
4. If they cannot make the target, the facilitator draws a new target number.
5. Play 10 rounds. How many points can the student score?

**Variation — Subtraction:** The target number is the DIFFERENCE. "Find two cards where the bigger number minus the smaller number equals the target."

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
After all the games, do a final quick-fire round. The facilitator says each fact; the student answers orally.

1. 7 + 5 = ___
2. 16 - 9 = ___
3. 8 + 6 = ___
4. 13 - 8 = ___

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-2/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which facts are still causing trouble. These are the student's "personal challenge facts."
- Write each challenge fact on an index card. Practice these specific cards for 3 minutes daily.
- Play Game 3 (Flashcard Relay) again, using only the challenge facts.

### If Mastery Is Met
Say: "Game day was great practice! Tomorrow is our final lesson — the Timed Challenge. You will see how many facts you can solve in a set time and track your own progress. It is not a test — it is a chance to see how far you have come!"

## Extension (Optional)

### Bonus Game: Four in a Row (Fact Fluency Edition)

**Setup:** Draw a 5x5 grid on paper. Write different numbers from 0-18 in the squares (some can repeat). Each player uses a different colored pencil.

**How to play:**
1. Roll two dice (or draw two number cards).
2. Add or subtract the numbers (player's choice).
3. If the result appears on the grid, color in that square.
4. First player to get 4 in a row (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal) wins.

### Bonus Game: Number Bond Match

**Setup:** Write numbers 0-10 on individual cards. Make two sets.

**How to play:**
1. Lay all cards face-down (memory-style).
2. Flip two cards. If they add up to 10, keep the pair and score a point.
3. If they do not add up to 10, flip them back face-down.
4. Continue until all pairs are found.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Rushing and making mistakes during games:** The competitive element causes the student to guess rather than think. Fix: Pause the game. "Remember: a wrong answer does not count. Take one extra second to make sure."
2. **Avoiding hard facts:** Student may deliberately avoid picking up certain flashcards or hesitate on certain combinations. Fix: Note which facts are avoided — these are the ones that need the most practice. Shuffle them to the top.
3. **Frustration when losing:** If the student is playing against the facilitator and losing, they may shut down. Fix: Adjust the difficulty. Let the student play the games cooperatively instead of competitively. "Let's see if WE can beat our record together!"
4. **Fatigue:** Four games in one session is a lot. Fix: If the student is getting tired, skip Game 4 and spend extra time on whichever game they enjoyed most.

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson is intentionally lighter on structure and heavier on practice. The "instruction" is the game rules.
- Adjust game timing based on engagement. If the student loves Addition War, play it longer. If they are bored with Roll and Add, switch to something else.
- This lesson can be repeated on multiple days as a fun supplement. Consider using one game at the start of each subsequent math session as a warm-up.
- Target total time: 40 minutes.
