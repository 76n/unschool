# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 2, Lesson 7

## Topic
Speed and Accuracy: Fluency with Facts Summing to 11-15

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Recall addition facts with sums of 11-15 within 3 seconds each
- Recall the related subtraction facts within 3 seconds each
- Identify which strategy (doubles, near-doubles, make 10) is most efficient for each fact in this range

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-5: All addition and subtraction strategies
- Lesson 6: Fluency with sums to 10
- Doubles facts memorized (especially 6+6=12, 7+7=14)
- Make-10 with 8 and 9

## Materials Needed
- Flashcards for addition/subtraction facts with sums 11-15
- Timer or stopwatch
- Paper and pencil
- "I Know It," "Almost," and "Still Learning" envelopes from Lesson 6
- Number line 0-20

**Standards:** 2.OA.2

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review sums-to-10 fluency and transition to larger sums.

**Speed Round — Sums to 10 (2 minutes):**
Call out 8 facts rapidly; student answers orally:
1. 4 + 6 = ___
2. 10 - 3 = ___
3. 5 + 4 = ___
4. 8 - 5 = ___

**Bridge to New Facts (3 minutes):**
Now try these:
5. 6 + 6 = ___
6. 9 + 4 = ___

Ask: "Were the last two harder? They should be — their sums are bigger than 10. Today we will work on facts like these."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Sum:** The result of adding two numbers
- **Efficient:** Getting the answer quickly using the best strategy

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: What Facts Are We Learning? (3 minutes)**

Say: "Last time we practiced all the facts with sums of 10 or less. Today we add the next group: facts that sum to 11, 12, 13, 14, and 15."

Write the key facts on paper:

| Sum = 11 | Sum = 12 | Sum = 13 | Sum = 14 | Sum = 15 |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| 2 + 9 | 3 + 9 | 4 + 9 | 5 + 9 | 6 + 9 |
| 3 + 8 | 4 + 8 | 5 + 8 | 6 + 8 | 7 + 8 |
| 4 + 7 | 5 + 7 | 6 + 7 | 7 + 7 | 8 + 7 |
| 5 + 6 | 6 + 6 | 7 + 6 | 8 + 6 | 9 + 6 |
| | | | 9 + 5 | |

Say: "This looks like a lot, but remember: each fact has a twin (6 + 7 is the same as 7 + 6), and you already know strategies for all of them!"

**Step 2: Best Strategies for Each Cluster (5 minutes)**

Go through each sum and identify the fastest strategy:

**Sum = 11:**
- 2 + 9 = 11 (make-10: 9 + 1 + 1 = 11; or counting on)
- 3 + 8 = 11 (make-10: 8 + 2 + 1 = 11)
- 4 + 7 = 11 (make-10: 7 + 3 + 1 = 11)
- 5 + 6 = 11 (near-doubles: 5 + 5 + 1 = 11)

**Sum = 12:**
- 3 + 9 = 12 (make-10: 9 + 1 + 2 = 12)
- 4 + 8 = 12 (make-10: 8 + 2 + 2 = 12)
- 5 + 7 = 12 (make-10: 7 + 3 + 2 = 12)
- 6 + 6 = 12 (doubles!)

**Sum = 13:**
- 4 + 9 = 13 (make-10: 9 + 1 + 3 = 13)
- 5 + 8 = 13 (make-10: 8 + 2 + 3 = 13)
- 6 + 7 = 13 (near-doubles: 6 + 6 + 1 = 13)

**Sum = 14:**
- 5 + 9 = 14 (make-10: 9 + 1 + 4 = 14)
- 6 + 8 = 14 (make-10: 8 + 2 + 4 = 14)
- 7 + 7 = 14 (doubles!)

**Sum = 15:**
- 6 + 9 = 15 (make-10: 9 + 1 + 5 = 15)
- 7 + 8 = 15 (near-doubles: 7 + 7 + 1 = 15)

Say: "Doubles and make-10 do most of the work. If one addend is 8 or 9, use make-10. If both addends are the same or one apart, use doubles or near-doubles."

**Step 3: Sort These Facts (2 minutes)**

Using flashcards, quickly sort the sums-to-15 facts into "I Know It," "Almost," and "Still Learning" — just like in Lesson 6. This identifies the student's focus facts.

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 5 + 8 = ?
- Strategy: Make-10. "8 needs 2 to make 10. Take 2 from the 5, leaving 3. 10 + 3 = 13."
- Answer: 13.

**Example B:** 13 - 6 = ?
- Strategy: Think-addition. "6 + ? = 13. I know 6 + 7 = 13 (near-doubles: 6 + 6 + 1). So 13 - 6 = 7."
- Answer: 7.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Flashcard Power Drill:**

Take the "Almost" and "Still Learning" cards. Work through them using this process:

1. 6 + 7 = ___
   *If slow: "What doubles fact is nearby?" (6 + 6 = 12, so 6 + 7 = 13)*
   Say the fact together 3 times.

2. 8 + 4 = ___
   *If slow: "Use make-10. How many more does 8 need?" (2, so 8 + 2 + 2 = 12)*
   Say the fact together 3 times.

3. 14 - 7 = ___
   *Ask: "Do you see a doubles fact hiding here?" (7 + 7 = 14, so 14 - 7 = 7)*

4. 13 - 8 = ___
   *Ask: "Think addition: 8 + ? = 13." (8 + 5 = 13, so 13 - 8 = 5)*

5. 9 + 5 = ___
   *Ask: "Make-10 or near-doubles — which is faster for you here?"*
   (Make-10: 9 + 1 + 4 = 14. Near-doubles: not a clean fit. Make-10 wins.)

After working through the hard facts, shuffle ALL sums-to-15 flashcards and go through the entire set. Time the student. Record the time.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
Solve — aim for under 3 seconds each:
1. 8 + 6 = ___
2. 13 - 5 = ___
3. 7 + 8 = ___
4. 12 - 9 = ___

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct AND all within 3 seconds = Mastery
- 4/4 correct but some slow = Approaching mastery
- 0-3/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify the specific facts causing trouble. Add them to the "Still Learning" pile for daily practice.
- Focus on the strategy that works best for each difficult fact. Write the strategy on the back of the flashcard as a reminder.
- Consider repeating this lesson before moving to Lesson 8 (sums to 20).

### If Mastery Is Met
Say: "Excellent — you are now fluent with facts up to 15! Tomorrow we tackle the final group: sums from 16 to 20. These are the hardest facts, but you already have all the strategies you need."

## Extension (Optional)

**Around the World (solo version):** Lay out 10 flashcards face-down in a circle. The student flips one, solves it as fast as possible, then moves to the next. Time one lap around the circle. Challenge them to go faster on the second lap.

**Pattern Hunt:** "Look at all the facts that sum to 12: 3+9, 4+8, 5+7, 6+6. What happens to one addend as the other goes up by 1?" (One goes up, the other goes down — they are always adjusting to keep the sum the same.)

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Confusing sums that are close together:** Student says 6 + 7 = 12 (should be 13) because they confuse it with 6 + 6 = 12. Fix: Reinforce near-doubles: "6 + 7 is one MORE than 6 + 6. So 12 + 1 = 13."
2. **Subtraction errors from think-addition mistakes:** Student says 13 - 5 = 7 (should be 8). The underlying addition fact is wrong: they think 5 + 7 = 13 when it is 5 + 8 = 13. Fix: Check the addition fact first.
3. **Mixing up make-10 decomposition:** For 4 + 8, student does 8 + 4 + ... instead of 8 + 2 + 2. Fix: Always decompose the SMALLER number to fill up the LARGER to 10.
4. **Giving up on hard facts:** Student says "I do not know" instead of trying a strategy. Fix: Prompt the strategy: "What is 8 + 2? Good, that is 10. Now how much is left from the other number?"

### Pacing Advice
- This lesson has more facts than Lesson 6. Expect it to take the full 40 minutes.
- If the student's "Still Learning" pile has more than 8 cards, consider splitting this lesson into two sessions.
- The most commonly difficult facts in this range are: 6+7, 8+5, 9+4, 7+8, and their related subtraction facts. Focus energy on these.
- Target total time: 35-40 minutes.
