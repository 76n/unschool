# Facilitator Guide — Grade 2 Math, Unit 2: Fluency Within 20

**Grade:** 2
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 2 — Fluency Within 20
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Fully facilitated (adult is present and actively guiding throughout the session)

---

## Unit Overview

In Unit 1, students learned to add and subtract two-digit numbers within 100 using strategies based on place value. They already understand *how* to add and subtract — now they need to become *fast and accurate* with the basic facts within 20.

This unit focuses on **automaticity**: knowing facts so well that the answer comes quickly, without counting on fingers or drawing pictures every time. Think of it like learning to read sight words — once you know them by heart, your brain is free to focus on bigger ideas.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Recall all addition facts within 20 with speed and accuracy
- Recall all subtraction facts within 20 with speed and accuracy
- Use the relationship between addition and subtraction (fact families) to derive unknown facts
- Apply strategies (doubles, near-doubles, make 10, think-addition) flexibly and efficiently
- Self-monitor progress on fact fluency and set personal goals

**Why this matters:** Fluent recall of basic facts is the foundation for all later mathematics. Students who must stop and count for 8 + 7 will struggle to keep up with multi-digit computation, fractions, and word problems. Fluency is not about memorization without understanding — it is about understanding *so well* that recall becomes automatic.

---

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm:

- [ ] Can add and subtract within 20 using at least one strategy (counting on, doubles, make 10) — Grade 1 Unit 1
- [ ] Understands that addition and subtraction are related (e.g., if 5 + 3 = 8, then 8 - 3 = 5) — introduced in Grade 1
- [ ] Can add and subtract within 100 using place value strategies — Unit 1
- [ ] Understands the equal sign as "the same as"

**If the student cannot reliably solve facts within 20 using any strategy**, do not begin this unit. Return to Grade 1 materials to build strategic understanding first. This unit builds speed on a foundation of understanding — it cannot replace that foundation.

## Materials Needed for This Unit

- **Flashcards** — Addition and subtraction facts within 20. Can be store-bought or homemade on index cards.
  - **Homemade alternative:** Cut index cards in half. Write one fact on the front (e.g., "8 + 5") and the answer on the back ("13").
- **Ten-frames** (two per student) — printed or drawn on paper (2 rows of 5 squares each)
  - **Homemade alternative:** Draw two 2x5 grids on a sheet of paper
- **Counters** — 20 small objects (buttons, coins, beans, or cereal pieces)
- **Number line 0-20** (can be drawn on a strip of paper)
- **Dice** — Two standard dice (or one 10-sided die if available)
- **Timer or stopwatch** — phone timer works fine
- **Paper and pencil**
- **Colored pencils or crayons** (for tracking progress)
- **Progress tracking chart** (template provided in Lesson 10; can also be made on graph paper)

## Pacing Guidance

| Lesson | Topic | Sessions |
|--------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Review Addition Strategies (counting on, doubles, make 10) | 1 |
| 2 | Doubles and Near-Doubles Fluency | 1 |
| 3 | Make-10 Strategy Fluency | 1 |
| 4 | Addition Fact Families | 1 |
| 5 | Subtraction: Think-Addition | 1 |
| 6 | Fluency — Sums to 10 | 1 |
| 7 | Fluency — Sums to 15 | 1 |
| 8 | Fluency — Sums to 20 | 1 |
| 9 | Mixed Practice Games | 1 |
| 10 | Timed Challenge and Self-Assessment | 1 |
| — | Unit Assessment | Separate session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lessons 6-8 are the core fluency-building lessons. If the student needs extra time, repeat any of these before moving on. You can spend 2-3 sessions on Lesson 8 if the student is close but not yet fluent.
- Lesson 9 (games) is designed to be repeatable. Come back to it whenever the student needs a change of pace or additional practice.
- If the student is already fast and accurate with sums to 10 (Lesson 6), you may move quickly through it and spend more time on Lessons 7-8.
- **Daily fact practice (5 minutes):** Starting from Lesson 2, begin each day with a quick flashcard or oral drill of previously learned facts, even on days when you are not doing a math lesson. Consistency is more important than duration.

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Student counts on fingers for every fact, even simple ones like 3 + 2 | Student has not internalized basic facts; still relying on counting | Do not ban finger-counting — it shows understanding. Instead, build toward automaticity gradually. Start with the easiest facts (doubles, +0, +1) and help the student "just know" those before moving to harder facts. |
| Student confuses addition and subtraction strategies (e.g., tries to "make 10" when subtracting) | Strategies are jumbled; student needs clearer mental models | Slow down. Focus on one strategy at a time. Use the think-addition approach for subtraction: "For 13 - 8, think: 8 + ? = 13." |
| Student is accurate but very slow (takes 5+ seconds per fact) | Understanding is present but automaticity is not | This is normal early in the unit. Speed comes with practice. Use timed activities to build urgency, but focus on personal improvement, not comparison to others. |
| Student memorizes some facts but not their related facts (knows 7 + 8 = 15 but not 15 - 7 = 8) | Fact families are not connected in the student's mind | Explicitly teach fact families (Lesson 4). Always present addition and subtraction facts together: "If you know 7 + 8 = 15, you automatically know 15 - 7 and 15 - 8." |
| Student performs well in practice but poorly under time pressure | Test anxiety or insufficient automaticity | Reduce pressure. Use shorter timed intervals (30 seconds instead of 1 minute). Focus on beating their own previous score, not meeting a fixed target. Celebrate growth. |
| Student reverses operation in subtraction (e.g., 14 - 6: does 6 - 4 = 2, says the answer is 2) | Confusing subtraction with finding the difference between digits | Return to concrete models. Use counters: "Start with 14 counters. Take away 6. How many are left?" Rebuild the meaning of subtraction before working on speed. |

## Conversation Starters

Use these throughout the unit to deepen understanding and check that speed is built on comprehension:

- "What is 8 + 7? How did you get that so fast? What is happening in your brain?"
- "If you know that 6 + 6 = 12, how does that help you figure out 6 + 7?"
- "What is 9 + 5? Now what is 14 - 9? How are those two facts connected?"
- "Which facts are easiest for you? Which ones are you still working on?"
- "Why is it useful to know your math facts quickly? When might that help you?"
- "If someone forgot what 8 + 5 equals, what strategy would you teach them?"
- "Can you think of three different addition facts that equal 12?"

## Teaching Notes: The Balance Between Speed and Understanding

This unit walks a careful line. The goal is **automaticity** — but not at the expense of understanding.

**What to do:**
- Always allow the student to explain their thinking, especially early in the unit
- Celebrate strategies, not just correct answers
- Use timed practice to build excitement and track growth, not to create anxiety
- Focus on personal improvement: "Last time you got 12 in one minute. Let's see if you can beat that!"

**What to avoid:**
- Do not tell the student to "just memorize" without ensuring they have a strategy
- Do not use timed activities as punishment or high-stakes evaluation
- Do not compare the student to other children
- Do not rush past understanding to get to speed — a student who understands *why* 8 + 5 = 13 will remember it longer than one who memorized it without understanding

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if:

- The student cannot use any strategy to solve facts within 20 (not a speed issue — a comprehension issue)
- The student shows signs of math anxiety that interfere with learning (avoidance, tears, shutting down during math time)
- After 3-4 weeks of consistent daily practice, the student still cannot recall most facts within 10
- The student has significant difficulty with the concept that addition and subtraction are related

---

## What Comes Next

After this unit, students move to **Unit 3: Introduction to Multiplication as Equal Groups**, where they will use their fluent addition facts to understand repeated addition and the concept of multiplication. Strong fact fluency makes this transition much smoother — a student who can quickly add 4 + 4 + 4 is ready to discover that this is 3 groups of 4.
