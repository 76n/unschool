# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 2, Lesson 3

## Topic
Make-10 Strategy for Addition Fluency

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Use the make-10 strategy to add 8 or 9 to any single-digit number
- Decompose one addend to create a group of 10 and add the remainder
- Explain the make-10 process using ten-frames or drawings

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: Review of addition strategies
- Lesson 2: Doubles and near-doubles fluency
- Knows number pairs that make 10 (1+9, 2+8, 3+7, 4+6, 5+5)

## Materials Needed
- Two ten-frames (printed or drawn)
- 20 counters
- Paper and pencil
- Number line 0-20

**Standards:** 2.OA.2

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review doubles/near-doubles from Lesson 2 and assess make-10 readiness.

**Part A — Doubles Speed Drill (2 minutes):**
Say each fact; student answers orally:
1. 7 + 7 = ___
2. 8 + 9 = ___

**Part B — Pairs That Make 10 (3 minutes):**
Say: "Tell me the number that goes with each of these to make 10."
3. 8 + ___ = 10
4. 6 + ___ = 10

**Facilitator note:** If the student does not know the pairs that make 10 automatically, spend extra time drilling them before proceeding. Make-10 depends on this knowledge. Use ten-frames: place 8 counters and ask "How many empty spaces? That is how many you need to make 10."

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Make 10:** A strategy where you break apart one addend to create a group of 10, then add the leftover
- **Decompose:** Break a number into smaller parts (e.g., 5 = 2 + 3)
- **Compose:** Put numbers together to make a new number

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Today we are going to become experts at the make-10 strategy. This is the single most useful strategy for facts where one number is 8 or 9."

**Step 1: Review Pairs That Make 10 (3 minutes)**

Write the pairs on paper. Have the student fill in the blanks:
- 9 + ___ = 10 (1)
- 8 + ___ = 10 (2)
- 7 + ___ = 10 (3)
- 6 + ___ = 10 (4)
- 5 + ___ = 10 (5)

Say: "These pairs are your secret weapon. If you know these by heart, make-10 becomes super fast."

**Step 2: Make-10 with 9 (5 minutes)**

Say: "When you add 9 to something, you only need 1 more to make 10. That makes it easy!"

Demonstrate with ten-frames:
- **9 + 4:** Place 9 counters in the ten-frame (one space empty). Place 4 counters outside. "I need 1 to fill the ten-frame." Move 1 counter in. "Now I have 10, and 3 left. 10 + 3 = 13."
- Write the thinking: 9 + 4 = 9 + 1 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13.

Do two more with the student watching:
- **9 + 6:** 9 + 1 + 5 = 10 + 5 = 15
- **9 + 7:** 9 + 1 + 6 = 10 + 6 = 16

Say: "Do you see the pattern? When you add 9 to a number, the answer is always 1 less than adding 10. 9 + 4 = 13 (which is 14 - 1). 9 + 6 = 15 (which is 16 - 1)."

**Step 3: Make-10 with 8 (5 minutes)**

Say: "When you add 8, you need 2 more to make 10."

Demonstrate:
- **8 + 5:** Place 8 counters in the ten-frame (two spaces empty). Place 5 counters outside. "I need 2 to fill the ten-frame." Move 2 counters in. "Now I have 10 and 3 left. 10 + 3 = 13."
- Write: 8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13.

Do two more:
- **8 + 7:** 8 + 2 + 5 = 10 + 5 = 15
- **8 + 4:** 8 + 2 + 2 = 10 + 2 = 12

**Step 4: Make-10 with 7 (2 minutes)**

Say: "You can also use make-10 when adding 7. You need 3 more to make 10."

- **7 + 5:** 7 + 3 + 2 = 10 + 2 = 12
- **7 + 6:** 7 + 3 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13

Say: "Make-10 works best when one number is big — like 7, 8, or 9. For smaller numbers, doubles or counting on might be faster."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 9 + 5
- Step 1: "9 needs 1 more to make 10."
- Step 2: "Break apart 5 into 1 and 4."
- Step 3: "9 + 1 = 10. Then 10 + 4 = 14."
- Answer: 9 + 5 = 14.

**Example B:** 8 + 6
- Step 1: "8 needs 2 more to make 10."
- Step 2: "Break apart 6 into 2 and 4."
- Step 3: "8 + 2 = 10. Then 10 + 4 = 14."
- Answer: 8 + 6 = 14.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Use the ten-frame for the first two problems, then try without it.

1. 9 + 3 = ___
   *Ask: "How many more does 9 need to make 10? Break apart the 3."*
   (9 + 1 + 2 = 10 + 2 = 12)

2. 8 + 3 = ___
   *Ask: "How many more does 8 need to make 10? Break apart the 3."*
   (8 + 2 + 1 = 10 + 1 = 11)

3. 9 + 8 = ___
   *Ask: "Which number will you make into 10 — the 9 or the 8?"*
   (Either works: 9 + 1 + 7 = 17 or 8 + 2 + 7 = 17)

4. 8 + 9 = ___
   *Ask: "Is this the same as 9 + 8? Why?"*
   (Yes — addition is commutative; order does not change the sum. Answer: 17)

5. 7 + 8 = ___
   *Ask: "You could use make-10 OR near-doubles here. Try both. Which is faster for you?"*
   (Make-10: 8 + 2 + 5 = 15. Near-doubles: 7 + 7 + 1 = 15.)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
Solve as quickly as you can:
1. 9 + 5 = ___
2. 8 + 6 = ___
3. 9 + 8 = ___
4. 7 + 5 = ___

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-2/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student does not know pairs that make 10: Drill these pairs until they are automatic. Use ten-frames daily.
- If the student can make 10 but decomposes incorrectly: Slow down and use counters for every step. "You said 8 needs 3 to make 10 — let's check with the ten-frame."
- Repeat guided practice problems before moving on.

### If Mastery Is Met
Say: "Make-10 is working well for you! Tomorrow we will discover something amazing — that every addition fact has a subtraction fact hiding inside it. This will help us learn subtraction facts twice as fast."

## Extension (Optional)

**9 Plus Pattern:** Write all the 9+ facts in a column:
- 9 + 1 = 10
- 9 + 2 = 11
- 9 + 3 = 12
- 9 + 4 = 13
- 9 + 5 = 14
- 9 + 6 = 15
- 9 + 7 = 16
- 9 + 8 = 17
- 9 + 9 = 18

Ask: "What patterns do you see in the answers?" (The ones digit is always 1 less than the number being added to 9. The tens digit is always 1. The digits of the answer always sum to 9.)

**Real-World Connection:** "You have 8 crayons in a box that holds 10. Your friend gives you 5 more crayons. How many crayons do you have? Did you fill the box? How many extra?"

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Wrong pair for 10:** Student says 8 needs 3 to make 10 (should be 2). Fix: Use the ten-frame to count empty spaces. Drill pairs that make 10 separately.
2. **Decomposing the wrong number:** Student tries to make 10 from the smaller number (e.g., for 9 + 4, tries to make 4 into 10). Fix: Say "We always want to make the BIGGER number into 10, because it is closer."
3. **Losing track of the remainder:** Student does 8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + ... then forgets the 3. Fix: Write it out in steps. Use counters physically.
4. **Not seeing the connection between 9 + 4 and 8 + 5:** Both equal 13 but by different paths. Point this out: "Make-10 can reach the same answer from different starting points."

### Pacing Advice
- The +9 facts are easiest (only need 1 to make 10). Start there and build confidence before moving to +8 and +7.
- If the student is not fluent with pairs that make 10, pause and drill those first. Everything in this lesson depends on that knowledge.
- Target total time: 35-40 minutes.
