# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 2, Lesson 1

## Topic
Review Addition Strategies: Counting On, Doubles, Make 10

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Name and demonstrate three strategies for adding within 20: counting on, doubles/near-doubles, and make 10
- Choose an appropriate strategy for a given addition fact
- Solve addition facts within 20 using at least two different strategies

## Prerequisites
- Grade 1 Unit 1: Addition and subtraction within 20 (strategies introduced)
- Grade 2 Unit 1: Addition and subtraction within 100 (strategies applied to two-digit numbers)

## Materials Needed
- Two ten-frames (printed or drawn)
- 20 counters (buttons, beans, or coins)
- Number line 0-20
- Paper and pencil
- Flashcards for facts within 20 (optional — will be used more in later lessons)

**Standards:** 2.OA.2

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge and assess current fluency level.

Say: "Let's see how many of these you can solve. Try to answer as quickly as you can — but it is more important to be correct than fast."

Solve these orally (facilitator reads, student answers aloud):
1. 3 + 4 = ___
2. 5 + 5 = ___
3. 2 + 9 = ___
4. 6 + 6 = ___

After each answer, ask: "How did you figure that out?"

**Facilitator note:** Listen carefully to the strategies the student uses. This tells you their starting point. If the student says "I just know it," ask: "But *how* do you know? What helps your brain remember?" Some students will count on fingers, some will recall instantly, some will use a strategy. All of these are valid starting points. Record which facts are automatic and which require effort.

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Strategy:** A plan or method for solving a problem
- **Counting on:** Starting from the bigger number and counting up by the smaller number
- **Doubles:** Adding a number to itself (e.g., 4 + 4)
- **Near-doubles:** Adding two numbers that are one apart (e.g., 4 + 5 = 4 + 4 + 1)
- **Make 10:** Breaking apart a number to create a group of 10, then adding what is left
- **Fluency:** Being able to solve problems quickly and correctly

### Instruction Notes

Say: "This unit is all about becoming fast and confident with your addition and subtraction facts. You already know strategies for solving them — now we are going to practice until they become automatic, like how you can read the word 'the' without sounding it out."

**Strategy 1: Counting On (3 minutes)**

Say: "When one addend is small — like 1, 2, or 3 — you can just count on from the bigger number."

Demonstrate with the number line:
- Show: 9 + 2. Point to 9 on the number line. Count on 2 hops: "10, 11." So 9 + 2 = 11.
- Show: 7 + 3. Point to 7. Count: "8, 9, 10." So 7 + 3 = 10.

Say: "Counting on is great when one number is small. But what if both numbers are bigger? Then we need a faster strategy."

**Strategy 2: Doubles and Near-Doubles (5 minutes)**

Say: "Doubles are facts where both numbers are the same. These are some of the easiest facts to remember."

Write the doubles on paper together:
- 1 + 1 = 2
- 2 + 2 = 4
- 3 + 3 = 6
- 4 + 4 = 8
- 5 + 5 = 10
- 6 + 6 = 12
- 7 + 7 = 14
- 8 + 8 = 16
- 9 + 9 = 18

Say: "Once you know the doubles, you can use them for near-doubles. Near-doubles are facts where the numbers are just one apart."

Demonstrate:
- 6 + 7: "I know 6 + 6 = 12. Since 7 is one more than 6, the answer is 12 + 1 = 13."
- 8 + 9: "I know 8 + 8 = 16. Since 9 is one more than 8, the answer is 16 + 1 = 17."

Use counters on the ten-frames to show: place 6 counters, then 7 counters. "See? 6 + 7 is just like 6 + 6 with one extra."

**Strategy 3: Make 10 (5 minutes)**

Say: "This is the most powerful strategy. If one number is close to 10, we can make it INTO 10 and then add the rest."

Demonstrate with ten-frames:
- Show: 8 + 5. Place 8 counters in the first ten-frame. Place 5 counters beside it. "I need 2 more to fill the ten-frame. I take 2 from the 5." Move 2 counters to fill the ten-frame. "Now I have 10 and 3 left over. 10 + 3 = 13."
- Show: 9 + 4. Place 9 counters. "I only need 1 more to make 10." Take 1 from the 4. "10 + 3 = 13."

Say: "Notice how useful this is when one number is 8 or 9 — they are so close to 10!"

**Key point:** Say: "You do not have to use the same strategy every time. Good mathematicians pick the strategy that works best for each problem."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 7 + 5
- **Using counting on:** Start at 7, count on 5: "8, 9, 10, 11, 12." Answer: 12.
- **Using near-doubles:** 6 + 6 = 12, and 7 + 5 is the same sum (move 1 from the 6 to the 5). Answer: 12.
- **Using make 10:** 7 + 3 = 10, and 5 - 3 = 2, so 10 + 2 = 12. Answer: 12.

Say: "All three strategies give us 12! You can pick whichever one feels fastest to you."

**Example B:** 9 + 6
- **Using make 10:** 9 + 1 = 10, take 1 from the 6 leaving 5, so 10 + 5 = 15. Answer: 15.
- **Using near-doubles:** 8 + 7 = 15... but that requires knowing another fact. Or: 9 + 9 = 18, but 6 is 3 less than 9, so 18 - 3 = 15. (This is trickier.)

Say: "For 9 + 6, make-10 is probably the fastest strategy. The best strategy depends on the problem!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Solve each problem together. After the student answers, ask which strategy they used.

1. 8 + 4 = ___
   *Ask: "Which strategy did you use? Could you use a different one?"*

2. 7 + 7 = ___
   *Ask: "What kind of fact is this? What makes doubles easy to remember?"*

3. 9 + 5 = ___
   *If the student counts on fingers: "That works! Now let me show you a faster way. What is 9 + 1? And how much is left from the 5?"*

4. 6 + 5 = ___
   *Ask: "Is this close to a doubles fact? Which one?"*

5. 8 + 7 = ___
   *Ask: "What strategy would you choose for this one? Why?"*

**Facilitator note:** The goal is not to force one strategy — it is to make sure the student has at least two strategies available. If a student always counts on, gently encourage them to try make-10 or doubles: "Counting on works great for small numbers, but let's try a shortcut for this one."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### ★ On-Level
Solve each fact. Write the strategy you used (CO = counting on, D = doubles, ND = near-doubles, M10 = make 10).

1. 6 + 3 = ___   Strategy: ___
2. 8 + 8 = ___   Strategy: ___
3. 9 + 7 = ___   Strategy: ___
4. 5 + 6 = ___   Strategy: ___
5. 7 + 4 = ___   Strategy: ___

### ★★ Stretch
Solve using TWO different strategies for each. Show both.

6. 8 + 6 = ___   Strategy 1: ___   Strategy 2: ___
7. 9 + 8 = ___   Strategy 1: ___   Strategy 2: ___

### ★★★ Challenge
8. Write three different addition facts that equal 14. _______________
9. Which strategy would you teach a friend for solving 9 + 7? Explain why.

### Interleaved Review

10. 45 + 30 = ___
11. 62 - 20 = ___

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket
Solve as quickly as you can:
1. 9 + 3 = ___
2. 7 + 8 = ___
3. 6 + 6 = ___
4. 8 + 5 = ___

**Scoring:**
- 4/4 correct = Mastery
- 3/4 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-2/4 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which strategy the student is missing. If they cannot make 10, spend extra time with ten-frames before Lesson 3. If they do not know doubles, drill the doubles list together.
- Have the student practice the 5 missed facts with counters before moving on.

### If Mastery Is Met
Preview: "You remembered your strategies well! Tomorrow we are going to zoom in on doubles and near-doubles and practice them until they are super fast."

---

## Extension (Optional)

**Strategy Sort:** Write 10 addition facts on slips of paper. Have the student sort them into three piles: "Best solved by counting on," "Best solved by doubles/near-doubles," and "Best solved by make 10." Discuss: some facts could go in more than one pile — that is fine!

**Real-World Connection:** "You have 8 apples and your friend gives you 6 more. How many do you have? What strategy did you use? Now make up your own story for 9 + 4."

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Counting on incorrectly:** Student starts counting from the number instead of the next number. For 9 + 3, they say "9, 10, 11" (getting 11) instead of "10, 11, 12" (getting 12). Fix: Use the number line and tap each hop.
2. **Near-doubles confusion:** Student adds 2 instead of 1 for near-doubles. For 7 + 8, they say 7 + 7 = 14, then 14 + 2 = 16. Fix: Show with counters that 8 is only ONE more than 7, not two.
3. **Make-10 decomposition error:** Student breaks apart the wrong addend or breaks it incorrectly. For 8 + 5, they say 8 + 5 = 8 + 3 + 2 (breaking 5 into 3 + 2 instead of 2 + 3). Fix: This actually gives the same result, but if the student loses track, always break apart the smaller addend to fill up to 10 from the larger one.
4. **Overreliance on one strategy:** Student uses counting on for everything, even 8 + 7. This works but is slow. Gently encourage trying other strategies for larger addends.

### Pacing Advice
- This is a review lesson, not a new-concept lesson. If the student is solid on all three strategies, move through Direct Instruction quickly and spend more time on practice.
- If the student is shaky on strategies, spend the full time on instruction and reduce independent practice. It is better to understand the strategies well than to rush through problems.
- Target total time: 35-40 minutes.
