# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 2, Lesson 4

## Topic
Addition Fact Families: Connecting Addition and Subtraction

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify all four facts in a fact family (e.g., 8 + 5 = 13, 5 + 8 = 13, 13 - 8 = 5, 13 - 5 = 8)
- Explain why knowing one addition fact gives you three other facts "for free"
- Write a complete fact family given any one of its members

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: Review of addition strategies
- Lesson 2: Doubles and near-doubles
- Lesson 3: Make-10 strategy
- Understanding that addition and subtraction are related (introduced in Grade 1)

## Materials Needed
- 20 counters
- Paper and pencil
- Three small cups or containers (for part-part-whole demonstrations)
- Index cards (for creating fact family cards)

**Standards:** 2.OA.2

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick addition drill and introduce the idea of related facts.

Solve orally:
1. 6 + 8 = ___
2. 8 + 6 = ___
3. 9 + 4 = ___
4. 4 + 9 = ___

Ask: "What do you notice about problems 1 and 2? What about 3 and 4?"

**Facilitator note:** The student should notice that switching the addends gives the same sum. This is the commutative property (you do not need to use this term). If the student does not notice, point it out: "6 + 8 and 8 + 6 both equal 14. The order does not matter!"

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Fact family:** A group of related addition and subtraction facts that use the same three numbers
- **Commutative property:** The order of addends does not change the sum (you can say "addition order does not matter")
- **Inverse operations:** Addition and subtraction are opposites — one undoes the other
- **Part-part-whole:** A way of seeing numbers: two parts combine to make a whole

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Part-Part-Whole (4 minutes)**

Place 13 counters on the table. Separate them into a group of 8 and a group of 5. Use two cups for the parts and one cup for the whole.

Say: "We have three numbers: 8, 5, and 13. The 8 and 5 are the PARTS. The 13 is the WHOLE. Every fact family is about parts and a whole."

Show the relationship:
- Combine the parts: 8 + 5 = 13 (parts make the whole)
- Combine in the other order: 5 + 8 = 13 (still parts make the whole)
- Take away one part from the whole: 13 - 8 = 5 (whole minus one part = other part)
- Take away the other part: 13 - 5 = 8 (whole minus one part = other part)

Write all four on paper:
```
8 + 5 = 13
5 + 8 = 13
13 - 8 = 5
13 - 5 = 8
```

Say: "This is a FACT FAMILY. All four facts use the same three numbers: 5, 8, and 13."

**Step 2: Building Fact Families (5 minutes)**

Do two more examples together:

**Fact family for 7, 9, 16:**
- 7 + 9 = 16
- 9 + 7 = 16
- 16 - 7 = 9
- 16 - 9 = 7

**Fact family for 6, 6, 12:**
Say: "What happens when both parts are the same?"
- 6 + 6 = 12
- 6 + 6 = 12 (same fact!)
- 12 - 6 = 6
- 12 - 6 = 6 (same fact!)

Say: "Doubles families only have TWO different facts instead of four. That is because switching the addends gives the same fact."

**Step 3: Why Fact Families Matter (3 minutes)**

Say: "Here is the amazing thing. If you memorize ONE addition fact, you get THREE more facts for free! If you know 8 + 5 = 13, you automatically know:"
- 5 + 8 = 13 (switch the order)
- 13 - 8 = 5 (subtract one part from the whole)
- 13 - 5 = 8 (subtract the other part from the whole)

Say: "This means you do not have to memorize addition facts AND subtraction facts separately. They are connected!"

**Step 4: Hands-On Practice (3 minutes)**

Give the student 15 counters. Say: "Split these into two groups — any way you want."

Suppose they make groups of 9 and 6. Ask: "What is the fact family for 9, 6, and 15?"
Have the student write all four facts.

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Write the fact family for 6, 7, and 13.
- 6 + 7 = 13
- 7 + 6 = 13
- 13 - 6 = 7
- 13 - 7 = 6

**Example B:** I know that 9 + 8 = 17. What other facts do I know?
- 8 + 9 = 17 (switch addends)
- 17 - 9 = 8 (whole minus one part)
- 17 - 8 = 9 (whole minus other part)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Write the fact family for 4, 9, and 13.
   *Ask: "What are the two parts? What is the whole?"*
   - 4 + 9 = 13, 9 + 4 = 13, 13 - 4 = 9, 13 - 9 = 4

2. I know that 7 + 5 = 12. Write the other three facts in this family.
   *Ask: "What happens if we switch the addends? Now, what subtraction facts can we write?"*
   - 5 + 7 = 12, 12 - 7 = 5, 12 - 5 = 7

3. Write the fact family for 8, 8, and 16.
   *Ask: "How is a doubles fact family different?"*
   - 8 + 8 = 16, 16 - 8 = 8 (only two distinct facts)

4. Fill in the blank: If 6 + 9 = 15, then 15 - 9 = ___.
   *If the student hesitates: "The whole is 15. One part is 9. What is the other part?"*
   - 6

5. Fill in the blank: If 14 - 8 = 6, then 8 + 6 = ___.
   *Ask: "How did you figure that out?"*
   - 14

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. Write the fact family for 5, 8, 13:
   ___ + ___ = ___
   ___ + ___ = ___
   ___ - ___ = ___
   ___ - ___ = ___

2. If 9 + 7 = 16, then 16 - 7 = ___.

3. If 11 - 4 = 7, then 4 + 7 = ___.

**Scoring:**
- 3/3 correct (fact family has all 4 facts correct counts as 1) = Mastery
- 2/3 correct = Approaching mastery
- 0-1/3 correct = Needs reteaching

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to part-part-whole with counters. Build several examples physically: separate 14 counters into two groups, then write all four facts. Repeat with different totals.
- Focus on the connection: "If you PUT TOGETHER 8 and 5 to get 13, then you can TAKE AWAY 8 from 13 to get back to 5."
- Reteach before moving to Lesson 5, which depends heavily on this concept.

### If Mastery Is Met
Say: "Now you know that addition and subtraction are partners. Tomorrow we will use this to become fast at subtraction — without having to learn a whole new set of facts!"

## Extension (Optional)

**Fact Family Houses:** Draw a simple house shape for each fact family. Write the three numbers on the roof and the four facts inside the house. Have the student create "houses" for 5 different fact families.

**Detective Challenge:** "I am thinking of a fact family. Here are my clues: (1) The whole is between 10 and 15. (2) One part is exactly 3 more than the other part. What is my fact family?" (Answer: 5, 8, 13 — since 8 - 5 = 3, and 5 + 8 = 13.)

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
1. **Writing the subtraction facts backward:** Student writes 5 - 13 = 8 instead of 13 - 5 = 8. Fix: Remind the student that the WHOLE (biggest number) always comes first in subtraction. "You cannot take 13 away from 5 — you do not have enough!"
2. **Forgetting the commutative addition fact:** Student writes only 8 + 5 = 13, not 5 + 8 = 13. Fix: Ask "Can I switch the addends? Does 5 + 8 also equal 13?"
3. **Thinking doubles families have 4 facts:** Student writes 6 + 6 = 12 twice and 12 - 6 = 6 twice as if they are different. Fix: Explain that these are the same facts, so a doubles family only has 2 unique facts.
4. **Confusing parts and whole:** Student writes 8 + 13 = 5. Fix: Return to counters. "The whole is the biggest number. The parts are smaller and they add up to the whole."

### Pacing Advice
- This is a concept-heavy lesson. Do not rush the direct instruction. If the student does not understand why fact families work, the rest of this unit will be harder.
- If running short on time, prioritize guided practice over independent practice. The "aha" moment of understanding fact families is more important than drilling problems.
- Target total time: 35-40 minutes.
