# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 5, Lesson 3

## Topic
Adding Tens to Three-Digit Numbers

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Add multiples of 10 to any three-digit number mentally
- Recognize when adding tens requires regrouping tens into a new hundred
- Explain what happens in the hundreds place when tens regroup

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1–2: Adding and subtracting hundreds
- Unit 1: Adding tens to two-digit numbers (with and without regrouping)
- Unit 4: Place value, understanding that 10 tens = 1 hundred

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks: hundreds flats, tens rods, unit cubes
- Place value mat per student
- Whiteboard and dry-erase marker
- Number line (0–1,000 marked by hundreds, with tens marked lightly if possible)
- Math journal

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review adding tens to two-digit numbers (bridging prior knowledge to new content).

**Activity — "Quick Tens" (choral response):**

Rapid-fire questions displayed on the board. Students respond chorally or on whiteboards.

- 45 + 20 = ___ (65)
- 63 + 30 = ___ (93)
- 78 + 20 = ___ (98 — just within 2-digit range)
- 56 + 40 = ___ (96)
- 85 + 30 = ___ (115 — this one crosses into 3-digit territory!)

Pause on the last one: "This answer has three digits now! Today we're going to figure out how that works."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Extend adding-tens mental math to three-digit numbers, including across a hundreds boundary.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Regroup (tens to hundreds):** When you have 10 or more tens, you trade 10 tens for 1 hundred
- **Hundreds boundary:** The point where the tens place "fills up" and the hundreds digit increases by 1 (e.g., going from _90s to the next hundred)

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1 — No Regrouping (concrete):**

Build 324 on the place value mat. "I want to add 50. That means I add 5 tens — 5 rods."

Add 5 rods to the tens column. "Now I have 3 + 5 = 8 tens. The hundreds and ones didn't change. 324 + 50 = 374."

**Part 2 — With Regrouping (concrete):**

Build 362 on the place value mat (3 flats, 6 rods, 2 cubes). "Now I want to add 70. Let me add 7 rods."

Place 7 rods in the tens column. "I now have 6 + 7 = 13 rods. But wait — can I have 13 tens? No! When I have 10 or more tens, I need to make a trade."

"I take 10 rods out of the tens column and replace them with 1 flat in the hundreds column." (Physically trade 10 rods for 1 flat.)

"Now I have: 4 flats (hundreds) + 3 rods (tens) + 2 cubes (ones) = 432. So 362 + 70 = 432."

Ask: "What happened to the hundreds place?" (It went up by 1.) "Why?" (Because we traded 10 tens for 1 hundred.)

**Pictorial:** Draw 3 squares, 6 lines, 2 dots. Add 7 more lines (show as dashes). Circle groups of 10 lines, trade for a new square. Result: 4 squares, 3 lines, 2 dots.

**Abstract:**
```
  362
+  70
-----
  432
```
"6 tens + 7 tens = 13 tens. Write 3 in the tens place, carry 1 to the hundreds place. 3 + 1 = 4 hundreds."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (no regrouping):** 451 + 30 = ?
- 5 tens + 3 tens = 8 tens. Hundreds and ones unchanged.
- **Answer: 481**

**Example 2 (with regrouping):** 580 + 60 = ?
- 8 tens + 6 tens = 14 tens. Write 4, carry 1 hundred.
- 5 + 1 = 6 hundreds. **Answer: 640**

**Example 3 (with regrouping, mental strategy):** 270 + 80 = ?
- "270 is 2 hundreds and 7 tens. 7 tens + 8 tens = 15 tens = 1 hundred and 5 tens. So 2 + 1 = 3 hundreds, 5 tens. Answer: 350."
- **Answer: 350**

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. 363 + 80 = ___
2. Did you need to regroup? Circle: YES / NO. Explain: ___________________________

**Mastery = 443 with explanation that 6 + 8 = 14 tens, requiring a trade of 10 tens for 1 hundred.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Use blocks to rebuild. Have student place 6 rods and 8 rods and physically count all rods. Ask: "Is 14 more than 10? So what do we do?" Guide the trade step by step.

### If Mastery Is Met
Challenge: "Find two multiples of 10 (both less than 100) that you could add to 560 to get exactly 700. Is there more than one answer?" (560 + 140 — but 140 isn't under 100 alone. Actually: 560 + 40 = 600, then 600 + 100 = 700; or as single addition 560 + 140 but it exceeds the < 100 constraint. Good discussion: they'd need 560 + 40 + 100 or recognize it takes more than one step.)

## Extension (Optional)
**"Crossing the Hundred" Number Line Activity:**
Give student a large number line from 0 to 500 (with tens marked). Start at a given number (e.g., 360) and make hops of the same size (e.g., +40 each hop). Record: 360, 400, 440, 480, 520… Notice: "When did we cross the hundreds boundary?"

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The key conceptual leap in this lesson is the regrouping of tens into hundreds — this is the heart of 2.NBT.7. Do not skip the physical trading step.
- Some students will try to add tens mentally by just looking at the tens digit and will forget to update the hundreds when regrouping. The "carry 1" notation in the algorithm is easy to skip — emphasize it in writing every time.
- Students who are strong at adding tens to two-digit numbers may still struggle here because they need to hold the hundreds in mind while working with the tens. The place value mat is essential scaffolding.
- Compare Problems 8 and 9 in Stretch explicitly: adding tens vs. adding hundreds results in different changes to the number. This builds 2.NBT.9 (explaining why strategies work).
