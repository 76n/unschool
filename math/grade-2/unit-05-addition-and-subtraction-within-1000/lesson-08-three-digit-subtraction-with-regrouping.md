# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 5, Lesson 8

## Topic
Three-Digit Subtraction with Regrouping

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract a two-digit or three-digit number from a three-digit number with regrouping
- Trade 1 ten for 10 ones and/or 1 hundred for 10 tens as needed
- Check subtraction answers using addition

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1–7: All prior unit lessons, especially Lessons 4 (subtracting tens with regrouping) and 5 (three-digit + two-digit with regrouping)
- Unit 1: Two-digit subtraction with regrouping

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks: hundreds flats, tens rods, unit cubes
- Place value mat per student
- Whiteboard and dry-erase marker
- Math journal
- Anchor chart: "When to Trade" (updated to include both ones and tens trades)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review the concept of trading to subtract; connect to prior lessons.

**Activity — "Trade or No Trade?" Quick Sort:**

Show each problem. Students write T (trade needed) or NT (no trade needed) on whiteboard.

- 763 − 40 (T: 6 < 4? No, 6 ≥ 4, NT)
- 532 − 80 (T: 3 < 8, trade needed)
- 845 − 213 (look column by column: ones 5−3, NT; tens 4−1, NT; → NT)
- 627 − 359 (ones 7−9: T; tens will also need to be checked)
- 900 − 45 (ones 0−5: T; tens 0−4 after trade: still need trade from hundreds)

"Today we'll learn how to handle these trades in full three-digit subtraction."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build the three-digit subtraction algorithm with regrouping step by step, using concrete and pictorial supports.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Trade (ones from tens):** Take 1 ten from the tens column, break it into 10 ones, and add them to the ones column
- **Trade (tens from hundreds):** Take 1 hundred from the hundreds column, break it into 10 tens, and add them to the tens column
- **Inverse:** The opposite operation; subtraction and addition are inverses

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1 — Regrouping Ones (concrete):**

Build 543 on the place value mat. "I need to subtract 217. Let me start with the ones."

"3 ones − 7 ones? I don't have enough ones! I need to trade." Remove 1 rod from the tens column and replace with 10 cubes. "Now I have 13 ones and 3 tens (instead of 4 tens)."

"Ones: 13 − 7 = 6."
"Tens: 3 − 1 = 2."
"Hundreds: 5 − 2 = 3."
"Answer: 543 − 217 = 326."

**Abstract notation:**
```
   4 13
   543
-  217
------
   326
```
Cross out 5→4 in hundreds... wait — actually only the tens change here. Correct:
```
   4 13
   5 43
-  2 17
-------
   3 26
```
"Cross out the 4 in tens, write 3. Cross out the 3 in ones, write 13. Subtract: 13−7=6, 3−1=2, 5−2=3."

**Part 2 — Regrouping Tens (concrete):**

Build 724 on the place value mat. Subtract 381.

"Ones: 4 − 1 = 3. Fine!"
"Tens: 2 − 8? Only 2 tens, need to take away 8. Trade 1 hundred for 10 tens." Remove 1 flat, add 10 rods. "Now: 6 hundreds, 12 tens."
"Tens: 12 − 8 = 4."
"Hundreds: 6 − 3 = 3."
"Answer: 343."

**Abstract:**
```
   6 12
   724
-  381
------
   343
```

**Part 3 — Regrouping Both (brief example):**

854 − 376:
"Ones: 4 − 6? Trade 1 ten → 14 ones, 4 tens."
"Tens: 4 − 7? Trade 1 hundred → 14 tens, 7 hundreds."
"Compute: 14−6=8, 14−7=7... wait: 14 tens − 7 tens = 7 tens."
"Wait, let me recount: we had 4 tens, traded 1 for 10 ones → 3 tens. Then need to subtract 7 tens from 3 tens: not enough! Trade from hundreds."

Redo more carefully:
- Ones: 4 − 6: trade. Now 14 ones, 4−1=3 tens, 8 hundreds.
- Tens: 3 − 7: trade. Now 13 tens, 8−1=7 hundreds.
- Compute: ones 14−6=8; tens 13−7=6 (wait: 13−7=6, but we're subtracting 7, the tens digit of 376): 13−7=6... no: tens digit of 376 is 7, so 13−7=6. Hmm but check: 854−376 = 478.

Let me recompute carefully:
- 854 − 376
- Ones: 4 < 6 → trade 1 ten: ones become 14, tens become 4 (was 5) → wait: 854 has 5 tens, 4 ones.
- After trade: 4 tens, 14 ones.
- Tens: 4 < 7 → trade 1 hundred: tens become 14, hundreds become 7 (was 8).
- Compute: 14−6=8 (ones); 14−7=7 (tens); 7−3=4 (hundreds).
- Answer: **478** ✓

**Abstract:**
```
   7 14
   8 54 → after ones trade: ones=14, tens=4(from 5)
   ↓
   7 14 4
   854
−  376
------
   478
```

Show this clearly on the board with all crossing-out steps.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (ones regroup only):** 762 − 438 = ?
- Ones: 2 < 8 → trade. 12 ones, 5 tens.
- Tens: 5 − 3 = 2. Hundreds: 7 − 4 = 3.
- **Answer: 324**

**Example 2 (tens regroup only):** 631 − 254 = ?
- Ones: 1 < 4 → trade. 11 ones, 2 tens.
- Tens: 2 < 5 → trade. 12 tens, 5 hundreds.
- Hmm — actually ones regroup is happening too. Let me pick a cleaner example.

**Example 2 (revised — tens regroup only):** 853 − 472 = ?
- Ones: 3 < 2? No! 3 ≥ 2. 3 − 2 = 1. No trade.
- Tens: 5 − 7? 5 < 7 → trade. 15 tens, 7 hundreds.
- Tens: 15 − 7 = 8. Hundreds: 7 − 4 = 3.
- **Answer: 381**

**Example 3 (both regroup):** 654 − 287 = ?
- Ones: 4 < 7 → trade. 14 ones, 4 tens (was 5).
- Tens: 4 < 8 → trade. 14 tens, 5 hundreds (was 6).
- Ones: 14 − 7 = 7. Tens: 14 − 8 = 6. Hundreds: 5 − 2 = 3.
- **Answer: 367**

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. Solve: 725 − 348 = ___
2. Show your work. Mark where you traded.

**Mastery = 377 with correct algorithm showing trade from tens column (ones: 5<8, trade; then tens: 1 ten left, 1<4, trade from hundreds).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Return to blocks. Do 725 − 348 physically: build 725, try to take 8 ones (only 5 available — trade 1 ten), then try to take 4 tens (only 1 available after trade — trade 1 hundred). Narrate each step.

### If Mastery Is Met
Enrichment: "Can you invent a three-digit subtraction problem where you need to trade from the hundreds to tens but NOT from tens to ones? What does that look like?"

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The 600 − 245 type problem (zeros in tens and ones) is a major stumbling block. Address it explicitly: "You can't trade 0 tens for ones — you must first trade from hundreds to get tens, then trade from tens to get ones." Two trades from left to right.
- Emphasize checking with addition every time: 573 − 248 = 325 → verify: 325 + 248 = 573. This builds 2.NBT.9 and catches errors.
- Students who are solid on this lesson are ready for the estimation lesson (Lesson 9) and mixed practice (Lesson 10). Students who are still shaky should get additional concrete practice with blocks.
- Connect to 2.NBT.9: "Why do we start with the ones? What would happen if we started with the hundreds?"
