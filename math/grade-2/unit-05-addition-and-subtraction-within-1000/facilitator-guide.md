# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 5: Addition and Subtraction Within 1,000
## Facilitator Guide

---

## Unit Overview

In this unit, students extend their understanding of addition and subtraction to three-digit numbers within 1,000. Building on their solid foundation with place value (Unit 4) and addition/subtraction within 100 (Unit 1), students learn to work with hundreds, apply regrouping strategies to larger numbers, and develop estimation skills. The unit culminates in solving multi-step word problems using all learned strategies.

**Duration:** 10 lessons (~45–50 minutes each)
**Grade:** 2
**Subject:** Math

## Prerequisites

Students should enter this unit with mastery of:
- **Unit 1:** Addition and subtraction within 100, including regrouping with two-digit numbers; understanding of the relationship between addition and subtraction
- **Unit 2:** Fluency with addition and subtraction within 20; mental math strategies
- **Unit 3:** Equal groups and arrays (multiplication foundations)
- **Unit 4:** Place value to 1,000 — reading/writing three-digit numbers, understanding hundreds/tens/ones, expanded form, comparing three-digit numbers, skip-counting by 5s, 10s, and 100s

## Facilitation Approach

### CRA Progression

Every new concept follows the **Concrete → Pictorial → Abstract (CRA)** progression:

1. **Concrete:** Students manipulate base-ten blocks on a place value mat. They physically trade 10 ones for 1 ten, or 10 tens for 1 hundred.
2. **Pictorial:** Students draw quick sketches — a flat square for a hundred, a vertical line for a ten, a dot for a one. They cross out and redraw to show trades.
3. **Abstract:** Students write number sentences and use the standard algorithm.

Do not rush through the concrete stage. Many students need several lessons of hands-on work before they are ready to move to abstract notation.

### Place Value Language

Use consistent, precise language throughout the unit. Avoid shortcut phrases that obscure place value.

| Instead of… | Say… |
|-------------|------|
| "Carry the 1" | "Trade 10 ones for 1 ten" / "Regroup 10 ones as 1 ten" |
| "Borrow" | "Trade 1 ten for 10 ones" / "Regroup 1 ten as 10 ones" |
| "Put a zero" | "There are zero tens (or ones) in this place" |

### Discussion Prompts
- "How do you know which place to start adding/subtracting?"
- "When do we need to make a trade? What are we trading?"
- "Does your answer make sense? Is it close to your estimate?"
- "Can you explain why this strategy works?"

## Common Misconceptions and Remediation

| Misconception | What You Might See | Remediation |
|---------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Adds/subtracts digit by digit without attending to place value | 342 + 215 = 557 but writes answer as "5, 5, 7" without understanding | Return to base-ten blocks; have student build both numbers and combine by place |
| Forgets to regroup (addition) | 164 + 58 = 112 (adds 4+8=12, writes 12 in ones column) | Use base-ten blocks to show 12 ones cannot fit; must trade 10 for 1 ten |
| Subtracts smaller from larger regardless of position | 432 − 217 → subtracts 2 from 7 in ones column | Use blocks; build 432, try to take away 7 ones — need to trade |
| Loses track of regrouped amounts | Forgets to add carried ten, or subtracts extra after regrouping | Use written notation (small "1" above tens column); practice with place value mat |
| Estimation: rounds whole number, not to nearest 10/100 | Rounds 347 to 300 instead of 350 when rounding to tens | Use number line; plot number between benchmarks |
| Applying 2-digit strategies blindly to 3-digit | Tries to hold all three digits in working memory without structure | Structured recording sheet with clearly labeled columns |

## Differentiation

### Support (Students Below Grade Level)
- Begin every lesson at the concrete stage longer; do not move to pictorial until student demonstrates understanding with blocks
- Reduce problem count; prioritize conceptual understanding over volume
- Use color-coded place value mats (green = hundreds, yellow = tens, red = ones)
- Allow students to use a completed addition/subtraction within 100 reference chart
- Partner students with a peer who can model think-alouds

### On Grade Level
- Follow standard lesson sequence; move through CRA as paced
- Emphasize explaining reasoning (2.NBT.9)
- Include all interleaved review problems

### Extension (Students Above Grade Level)
- Introduce problems requiring multiple regroupings (e.g., regrouping in both ones and tens place)
- Pose open-ended challenges: "Find all the ways to add two three-digit numbers to get 750"
- Word problems with multiple steps or extra information
- Begin exploring four-digit numbers informally

## Assessment Guidance

### Formative (Daily)
- Exit tickets at the end of each lesson — see individual lesson plans
- Observe students during concrete manipulative work; note whether they can articulate the trade
- Check journals for correct recording of regrouping notation

### Summative
- Unit Assessment at the end of Unit 5 (see `unit-assessment.md`)
- Covers all 10 lesson topics
- Includes computation, explanation/reasoning, and word problems
- Mastery threshold: 80% overall, with full credit on at least one explanation item

### Observation Checklist
During lessons, track whether each student can:
- [ ] Add/subtract hundreds mentally (2.NBT.8)
- [ ] Add/subtract tens mentally, including across hundreds (2.NBT.8)
- [ ] Add three-digit numbers with regrouping using blocks (2.NBT.7)
- [ ] Subtract three-digit numbers with regrouping using blocks (2.NBT.7)
- [ ] Record regrouping correctly in written form (2.NBT.7)
- [ ] Explain why a strategy works (2.NBT.9)
- [ ] Estimate sums and differences by rounding (2.NBT.7)
- [ ] Solve word problems involving three-digit addition/subtraction (2.NBT.7)

## Unit Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Regroup | To trade a unit from one place for 10 of the next smaller place (or vice versa) |
| Trade | To exchange a group of 10 for 1 of the next larger place (e.g., 10 ones → 1 ten) |
| Hundreds place | The position in a three-digit number that tells how many groups of 100 |
| Tens place | The position that tells how many groups of 10 |
| Ones place | The position that tells how many single units |
| Sum | The result of addition |
| Difference | The result of subtraction |
| Estimate | A close-but-not-exact answer, found by rounding |
| Round | To find the nearest 10 or 100 |

---

## Family Communication Notes

At the start of the unit, send home a note explaining:
- Students are extending addition/subtraction to three-digit numbers
- The vocabulary "trade" or "regroup" (not "carry" or "borrow")
- Ways to practice at home: count collections of objects, play "make 1,000" card games, look for three-digit numbers in everyday life (prices, distances, page numbers)
