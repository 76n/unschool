# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 5, Lesson 9

## Topic
Estimation with Three-Digit Numbers (Rounding to Nearest 10 and 100)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Round a three-digit number to the nearest 10
- Round a three-digit number to the nearest 100
- Use rounding to estimate sums and differences of three-digit numbers
- Explain whether an estimate is an overestimate or underestimate

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1–8: All prior unit lessons (addition and subtraction with regrouping)
- Unit 4: Place value, comparing three-digit numbers, number lines
- Unit 1: Basic number sense for two-digit rounding (informal)

## Materials Needed
- Number line (0–1,000, labeled at every 100, with tens marked between hundreds)
- Whiteboard and dry-erase marker
- Math journal
- Pencils
- Optional: Ruler or number line strips (0–10 for the "rounding mountain" visual)

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach rounding to the nearest 10 and nearest 100 using number line and place value strategy.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Round:** To find the nearest 10 or 100 (or other place value unit) to a given number
- **Estimate:** An approximate answer found using rounded numbers
- **Overestimate:** When your estimate is higher than the exact answer
- **Underestimate:** When your estimate is lower than the exact answer
- **Benchmark numbers:** Round numbers we round to, like 100, 200, 300, 10, 20, etc.

### Instruction Notes

**Rounding to the Nearest 10:**

Display a number line segment from 340 to 350. Place the number 347 on it.

"Is 347 closer to 340 or 350? Look at the number line — it's between them. We check the ones digit: 7. When the ones digit is 5, 6, 7, 8, or 9 → we round UP to the next ten. When the ones digit is 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4 → we round DOWN (keep the same ten)."

"347 → ones digit is 7 → round up → 350."

The "Rounding Rule":
- Ones digit 0–4: round down (keep the tens digit, change ones to 0)
- Ones digit 5–9: round up (increase the tens digit by 1, change ones to 0)

Practice together:
- 362 → ones = 2 → round down → 360
- 785 → ones = 5 → round up → 790
- 431 → ones = 1 → round down → 430
- 498 → ones = 8 → round up → 500 (note: the ten changes to the next hundred!)

**Rounding to the Nearest 100:**

Display a number line from 300 to 400. Place 347 on it.

"Is 347 closer to 300 or 400? We check the tens digit: 4. When the tens digit is 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4 → round down to the lower hundred. When the tens digit is 5, 6, 7, 8, or 9 → round up."

"347 → tens digit is 4 → round down → 300."

The "Rounding to Hundreds Rule":
- Tens digit 0–4: round down (keep hundreds digit, change tens and ones to 0)
- Tens digit 5–9: round up (increase hundreds digit by 1, change tens and ones to 0)

Practice together:
- 362 → tens = 6 → round up → 400
- 785 → tens = 8 → round up → 800
- 431 → tens = 3 → round down → 400
- 250 → tens = 5 → round up → 300

**Using Estimates:**

"Why estimate? Sometimes we just need a close answer quickly. Like if you have 347 stickers and your friend has 285, about how many do you have together?"

"Round both: 347 ≈ 350, 285 ≈ 290. 350 + 290 = 640. The actual answer (632) is close!"

"Or round to hundreds: 347 ≈ 300, 285 ≈ 300. 300 + 300 = 600. That's a rougher estimate but still useful."

Introduce notation: ≈ means "approximately equal to."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Round 463 to the nearest 10.
- Ones digit = 3 → round down.
- **460**

**Example 2:** Round 463 to the nearest 100.
- Tens digit = 6 → round up.
- **500**

**Example 3:** Estimate 374 + 218 by rounding to the nearest 100.
- 374 ≈ 400. 218 ≈ 200. 400 + 200 = 600.
- Is this an overestimate or underestimate? 374 rounded up (+26), 218 rounded down (−18). Net: slightly over.
- Actual: 374 + 218 = 592. Estimate was 600 — slightly over (overestimate).

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. Round 547 to the nearest 10: ___
2. Round 547 to the nearest 100: ___
3. Use your answer to #2 to estimate: 547 + 213 ≈ ___

**Mastery = 550; 500; 700 (using 500 + 200 = 700).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Return to the number line. Plot 547 between 540 and 550; show it is closer to 550. Then plot 547 between 500 and 600; show it is closer to 500. Walk through each rounding decision with the physical number line before asking students to use the digit rule.

### If Mastery Is Met
Challenge: "Round 547 to the nearest 10 and then use THAT rounded number to estimate 547 + 213. Do you get a different estimate? Which estimate (rounding to 10 vs. 100) is closer to the exact answer?" (550 + 210 = 760; 500 + 200 = 700; exact = 760. So rounding to nearest 10 gives a much more precise estimate.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Rounding to the nearest 100 can feel counterintuitive when the tens digit is 5 (exactly in the middle). The convention "round up when exactly at the midpoint" should be stated as a rule without excessive justification at Grade 2.
- The ≈ symbol may be new. Introduce it gently: "This wiggly equals sign means 'approximately' or 'about.'"
- Some students will round perfectly but then compute the estimate incorrectly (e.g., round to 400 and 300 but then compute 400 + 300 wrong). Celebrate the rounding skill and support the computation separately.
- Connect estimation back to lessons 1–8: "When is an exact answer necessary? When is an estimate good enough?" (e.g., checking at the grocery store vs. a math test).
- Overestimate/underestimate language builds mathematical reasoning and satisfies 2.NBT.9 indirectly.
