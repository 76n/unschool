## Part B: Algorithm Answer Key (2 points each)

*Award 1 point for correct process (evidence of correct regrouping notation) + 1 point for correct answer. If the answer is correct but work is not shown, award 1 point only.*

11. 324 + 253 = **577** (no regrouping)

12. 547 + 238 = **785**
    - Ones: 7+8=15, write 5 carry 1
    - Tens: 4+3+1=8
    - Hundreds: 5+2=7
    - Process points: "1" should appear above tens column

13. 465 + 376 = **841**
    - Ones: 5+6=11, write 1 carry 1
    - Tens: 6+7+1=14, write 4 carry 1
    - Hundreds: 4+3+1=8
    - Process points: two carried "1"s should appear

14. 738 + 184 = **922**
    - Ones: 8+4=12, write 2 carry 1
    - Tens: 3+8+1=12, write 2 carry 1
    - Hundreds: 7+1+1=9
    - Process points: two carried "1"s

15. 896 − 342 = **554** (no regrouping)
    - Ones: 6−2=4; Tens: 9−4=5; Hundreds: 8−3=5

16. 765 − 438 = **327**
    - Ones: 5<8, trade: 15−8=7; tens become 5 (was 6)
    - Tens: 5−3=2; Hundreds: 7−4=3
    - Process points: crossed-out digits and regrouped values visible

17. 824 − 367 = **457**
    - Ones: 4<7, trade: 14−7=7; tens become 1 (was 2)
    - Tens: 1<6, trade: 11−6=5; hundreds become 7 (was 8)
    - Hundreds: 7−3=4
    - Process points: two trade steps visible

18. 600 − 248 = **352**
    - Ones: 0<8, need to trade tens, but tens=0; must first trade hundreds:
      600 → trade 1 hundred: 5 hundreds, 10 tens, 0 ones
      then trade 1 ten: 5 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones
    - Ones: 10−8=2; Tens: 9−4=5; Hundreds: 5−2=3
    - Process points: chain of two trades visible

## Part D: Explanation Scoring Rubric (2 points each)

**Question 22:**

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|---------|
| 2 | Clearly explains that the small "1" represents 1 ten that was regrouped/traded from the ones place because 4 + 8 = 12 ones (which is 1 ten and 2 ones), and the 1 ten must be added to the tens column. Uses place value vocabulary. |
| 1 | Partially correct — identifies that the "1" comes from regrouping or is a "carried" value, but explanation is incomplete or lacks place value reasoning. |
| 0 | No response, or explanation is incorrect (e.g., "It means we borrowed from somewhere else"). |

**Sample full-credit response:** "The small 1 above the tens column means that we had too many ones — 4 ones + 8 ones = 12 ones. Since we can only have one digit in the ones place, we traded 10 ones for 1 ten. That 1 ten goes into the tens column, so we write it as a small '1' to remind us to add it when we add the tens."

**Question 23:**

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|---------|
| 2 | Correctly identifies that Mia is wrong. Explains that the issue is in the ones column (2 ones minus 6 ones is not possible without trading), not the tens column. Uses the words ones and/or tens correctly. |
| 1 | Identifies Mia is wrong but explanation is incomplete, unclear, or uses incorrect vocabulary. |
| 0 | Says Mia is correct, or no response. |

**Sample full-credit response:** "Mia is not correct. She is looking at the tens digits (4 and 7... wait, 7 and 3), but the problem starts in the ones place. We have 2 ones and need to take away 6 ones. Since 2 is less than 6, we need to make a trade. We trade 1 ten for 10 ones, so we have 12 ones. Then we can subtract."

*(Note: 742 − 376: ones digit of 742 is 2, ones digit of 376 is 6. Tens digit of 742 is 4, tens digit of 376 is 7. Mia was comparing the wrong columns — she said "4 is bigger than 3" (hundreds comparison), or she may have confused which digits to compare. The key issue is the ones column needs a trade.)*

## Next Steps by Performance Level

**Exceeds Mastery:** Student is ready for Unit 6. Offer enrichment with four-digit numbers, multi-step word problems, or introduction to mental math strategies (compensation, friendly numbers).

**Mastery:** Student moves to Unit 6 on grade level. Monitor understanding of regrouping in the early lessons of Unit 6.

**Approaching Mastery:** Before moving to Unit 6, provide targeted review on missed areas. Use the score breakdown by part to identify gaps:
- Part A weak → Review Lessons 1–4 (mental math)
- Part B addition weak → Review Lessons 5–7
- Part B subtraction weak → Review Lesson 8
- Part C weak → Review Lesson 9
- Parts D/E weak → Review Lesson 10 (word problems and explanation)

**Not Yet Mastery:** Student needs significant review of this unit before proceeding. Return to concrete manipulative work. Consider re-teaching Lessons 5–8 with additional small-group support.
