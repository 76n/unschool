# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 5, Lesson 4

## Topic
Subtracting Tens from Three-Digit Numbers

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract multiples of 10 from any three-digit number mentally
- Trade 1 hundred for 10 tens when there are not enough tens to subtract from
- Explain the regrouping process using place value language

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 3: Adding tens to three-digit numbers (including regrouping)
- Unit 1: Subtracting tens from two-digit numbers with regrouping
- Unit 4: Understanding 1 hundred = 10 tens

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks: hundreds flats, tens rods, unit cubes
- Place value mat per student
- Whiteboard and dry-erase marker
- Anchor chart: "When to Trade a Hundred" (created during instruction)
- Math journal

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect inverse operations and review adding tens (Lesson 3).

**Activity — "Undo It!":**

Write three equations on the board with blanks:
- 430 + 60 = 490 → 490 − 60 = ___
- 520 + 80 = 600 → 600 − 80 = ___
- 380 + 40 = 420 → 420 − 40 = ___

Students complete the subtraction by recognizing it undoes the addition. Ask: "How did knowing the addition help you?"

Then pose: "What if we need to subtract 60 from 324? Can we just take away from the tens column?" Allow predictions — this sets up the need for today's lesson.

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Develop understanding of trading 1 hundred for 10 tens in order to subtract.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Trade (hundreds to tens):** Exchange 1 hundred for 10 tens so there are enough tens to subtract from
- **Regroup:** Rearranging the same total amount into different sized groups

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1 — No Regrouping Needed (concrete):**

Build 587 (5 flats, 8 rods, 7 cubes). "I need to subtract 50 — take away 5 tens."

Remove 5 rods. "8 − 5 = 3 rods. Hundreds and ones didn't change. 587 − 50 = 537." Clean and easy.

**Part 2 — Regrouping Needed (concrete):**

Build 432 (4 flats, 3 rods, 2 cubes). "Now I need to subtract 70 — take away 7 tens. I look at my tens column. I only have 3 rods. Can I take away 7 rods? No!"

"So I need to get more tens. I can trade one of my hundreds for 10 tens." (Remove 1 flat from hundreds column, replace with 10 rods in the tens column.)

"Now I have 3 flats + (3 + 10) = 13 rods + 2 cubes. But wait — let me check: I started with 4 hundreds, now I have 3 — and I added 10 to my 3 tens to get 13 tens. I still have the same total: 300 + 130 + 2 = 432. Good — I haven't changed the number, just reorganized it!"

"Now I can take away 7 rods: 13 − 7 = 6 rods. My answer: 3 flats, 6 rods, 2 cubes = 362."

"So 432 − 70 = 362."

Make an anchor chart: **"When to Trade a Hundred for 10 Tens: When the tens digit of the number I have is LESS THAN the tens digit I'm trying to subtract."**

**Pictorial:**
Draw 4 squares, 3 lines, 2 dots. Cross out 1 square; draw 10 new lines. Now have 13 lines. Cross out 7. Left: 3 squares, 6 lines, 2 dots.

**Abstract:**
```
    3 13
  4  3 2
−    7 0
---------
  3  6 2
```
"Cross out the 4 in hundreds, write 3. Cross out the 3 in tens, write 13. Then subtract: 13 − 7 = 6. Hundreds: 3 − 0 = 3."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (no regrouping):** 764 − 40 = ?
- 6 tens − 4 tens = 2 tens. Hundreds and ones unchanged.
- **Answer: 724**

**Example 2 (regrouping):** 521 − 80 = ?
- 2 tens < 8 tens — need to trade! Trade 1 hundred for 10 tens: now 4 hundreds and 12 tens.
- 12 − 8 = 4 tens. **Answer: 441**

**Example 3 (regrouping):** 803 − 50 = ?
- 0 tens < 5 tens — need to trade! Trade 1 hundred for 10 tens: now 7 hundreds and 10 tens.
- 10 − 5 = 5 tens. **Answer: 753**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students identify whether regrouping is needed before solving.

Before solving each problem, student must first answer: "Do I need to trade? YES or NO?"

1. 675 − 30 = ___ (trade needed? ___)
2. 482 − 90 = ___ (trade needed? ___)
3. 340 − 60 = ___ (trade needed? ___)
4. 910 − 50 = ___ (trade needed? ___)
5. 726 − 80 = ___ (trade needed? ___)

**Facilitator guidance:** If student says "no" but regrouping is needed, ask: "How many tens do you have? How many are you trying to take away? Is that enough?" This metacognitive pre-check reduces errors.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. 321 − 60 = ___
2. Did you need to trade a hundred for tens? Circle: YES / NO
3. In one sentence, explain what you did.

**Mastery = 261, YES, and explanation mentions trading 1 hundred for 10 tens because there were only 2 tens.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Return to blocks. Build 321, ask how many tens, ask how many they need to subtract, identify that 2 < 6. Walk through the trade physically. Practice: 542 − 70 using the same approach.

### If Mastery Is Met
Extension challenge: "What's the greatest multiple of 10 you could subtract from 400 and still get a three-digit answer?" (390 — giving 10. Or if we require a three-digit result, 390 gives 10 which is two digits. So 380 gives 20, still two digits... 310 gives 90, still two digits. Greatest multiple of 10 subtracted from 400 to give a three-digit result: 400 − 10 = 390 — wait, all results from 400 − 10 to 400 − 390 are three-digit except 400 − 390 = 10. So the greatest multiple of 10 giving a three-digit result is 300, giving 100.)

## Extension (Optional)
**"Trade or No Trade?" Sorting Game:**
Write 10 subtraction problems (mix of with/without regrouping) on cards. Students sort them into two piles: "Trade Needed" and "No Trade Needed" WITHOUT computing the answer. Then verify by computing. Discuss: "How did you decide without solving?"

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The check-first strategy ("Do I need to trade?") is a metacognitive habit that reduces procedural errors. Emphasize it every lesson.
- Problem 4 in Guided Practice (910 − 50) is a potential stumbling block: students see a 1 in the tens place but may forget it's 1 ten, not 1 one. Reinforce place value reading.
- Example 3 (803 − 50) models an important case: when the tens digit is 0. This is the most challenging version and previews multi-step regrouping in Lesson 8.
- The anchor chart from today ("When to Trade a Hundred") should remain posted through Lesson 8.
