# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 5, Lesson 10

## Topic
Mixed Practice and Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Choose the correct operation (addition or subtraction) to solve a word problem
- Apply three-digit addition and subtraction strategies including regrouping
- Use estimation to check reasonableness of answers
- Solve multi-step word problems involving three-digit numbers

- All Lessons 1–9 of this unit

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach a problem-solving framework for word problems; model two-step problems.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Operation:** What you do to numbers (add, subtract, multiply, divide)
- **Key words:** Words in a problem that hint at the operation needed
- **Multi-step problem:** A problem that requires more than one computation to solve
- **Reasonable:** An answer that makes sense given the context

### Instruction Notes

**Word Problem Framework (create reference card with students):**

Post and discuss:
1. **Read** the problem carefully.
2. **Understand:** What do you know? What are you trying to find?
3. **Plan:** What operation do I need? (Add? Subtract?)
4. **Solve:** Show your work.
5. **Check:** Does my answer make sense? Use estimation to check.

**Key word discussion (important caveat!):**

Common keywords for addition: *in all, total, altogether, combined, both, more than*
Common keywords for subtraction: *left over, remaining, how many more, fewer, difference, took away*

"But be careful — sometimes the problem doesn't use these words. We always need to think about what's really happening in the story."

**Model a one-step problem:**

"The school library has 463 fiction books and 258 nonfiction books. How many books are in the library altogether?"

Walk through each step of the framework:
1. Read. 2. Know: 463 fiction, 258 nonfiction. Find: total. 3. Plan: addition (combining two groups). 4. Solve: 463 + 258 = 721. 5. Check: 460 + 260 ≈ 720 ✓ reasonable.

**Model a two-step problem:**

"A bakery baked 542 muffins on Saturday. They sold 278 muffins on Saturday. Then they baked 165 more on Sunday. How many muffins do they have now?"

Walk through:
1. Read carefully — two things happen.
2. Step 1: How many after Saturday's sales? 542 − 278 = 264.
3. Step 2: Then baked 165 more. 264 + 165 = 429.
4. Check: After selling: about 540 − 280 = 260. Then +165: 260 + 165 = 425. Close to 429 ✓.
5. **Answer: 429 muffins.**

Emphasize: "For a two-step problem, you need to find an intermediate answer first, then use it in the second step."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (one-step, subtraction):** A farm has 825 animals. 347 are chickens. The rest are other animals. How many are not chickens?
- 825 − 347 = 478. **478 animals are not chickens.**

**Example 2 (one-step, addition):** Diego collected 374 stamps. His sister collected 289 stamps. How many stamps do they have together?
- 374 + 289 = 663. **663 stamps.**

**Example 3 (two-step):** A school store had 300 pencils. They sold 128 pencils on Monday and 95 pencils on Tuesday. How many pencils are left?
- Step 1: 128 + 95 = 223 pencils sold.
- Step 2: 300 − 223 = 77 pencils left.
- **77 pencils remain.**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students work through word problems using the framework; facilitator supports operation choice.

For each problem, students must write: (1) the equation, (2) the answer with a label.

1. Mia has 456 marbles. She gives 138 to her friend. How many marbles does she have left?

2. A toy store sold 324 toys in January and 259 toys in February. How many toys were sold in those two months?

3. A garden has 516 red flowers and 275 yellow flowers. After a storm, 148 flowers are damaged. How many flowers are not damaged? *(two-step)*

**Facilitator guidance:** For problem 3, encourage students to re-read and identify the two steps. "What happens first? What question do we answer next?"

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. A bag has 342 red beads and 219 blue beads. How many beads are there in all?
2. A farmer picked 683 apples. She used 247 apples to make pies. How many apples are left?
3. The school raised $475 on Monday and $316 on Tuesday. How much money was raised in total?
4. There were 800 seats at the concert. 564 seats were filled. How many seats were empty?

**Stretch:**
5. A factory made 378 red cars and 456 blue cars. They shipped out 275 cars. How many cars remain?
6. Sam read 247 pages in Week 1 and 189 pages in Week 2. His goal was to read 500 pages. How many more pages does he need to read?
7. A jar has 325 yellow marbles, 248 green marbles, and 127 blue marbles. How many marbles are in the jar?

**Challenge:**
8. A library had 912 books. They gave away some books and now have 657. Then they received a donation of 85 books. How many books do they have now?
9. Two classes each counted their book collections. Class A has 347 books and Class B has 285 books. The school wants to have a combined total of 800 books. How many more books do they need?
10. Write your own two-step word problem using three-digit numbers. Solve it. Trade with a partner and solve their problem.

### Interleaved Review
*(Skills from Units 1–4)*

R1. 57 + 68 = ___ *(Unit 1: two-digit addition with regrouping)*
R2. Round 475 to the nearest 100. *(Lesson 9: estimation)*
R3. There are 4 equal groups of 6. Write a multiplication equation and find the total. *(Unit 3: equal groups)*

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Solve this two-step word problem:
"A pet store has 435 fish. They sell 178 fish on Saturday and 97 fish on Sunday. How many fish are left?"

Write two equations. Show your work.

**Mastery = Equation 1: 178 + 97 = 275 (or 435 − 178 = 257 then 257 − 97 = 160); Equation 2: 435 − 275 = 160. Answer: 160 fish.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Simplify to one step: "The pet store has 435 fish and sells 178. How many are left?" Once that is correct, add the second step. Scaffold by asking: "After Saturday, how many fish are left? Now use THAT number for Sunday."

### If Mastery Is Met
Extension: "Write a word problem where the answer is exactly 500. It must involve both addition and subtraction."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson is a review and synthesis lesson. The goal is not new content but integration and fluency.
- If students are struggling with word problem comprehension, focus on the "understand" step: have them draw a simple diagram (e.g., a box showing total, two parts labeled) before choosing an operation.
- The two-step problems are a stretch for many second graders. Celebrate partial success — getting Step 1 correct is meaningful progress.
- Use this lesson to inform your assessment of readiness for the unit test. Students who struggle here need review of specific lessons, not this lesson repeated.
- 2.NBT.9 connection: Ask students to explain their strategy choice. "Why did you add? Why subtract? How do you know your answer is reasonable?"
- Preview: "You've mastered addition and subtraction within 1,000! In our next unit, we'll start exploring measurement and data using these same skills."
