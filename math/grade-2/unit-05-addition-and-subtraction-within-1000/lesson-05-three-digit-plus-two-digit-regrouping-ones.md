# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 5, Lesson 5

## Topic
Three-Digit + Two-Digit Addition with Regrouping in the Ones Place

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Add a two-digit number to a three-digit number using the standard algorithm
- Regroup 10 ones as 1 ten when the ones sum is 10 or greater
- Record regrouping correctly in written form

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1–4: Adding and subtracting hundreds and tens with/without regrouping
- Unit 1: Adding two-digit numbers with regrouping in the ones place
- Unit 4: Understanding 10 ones = 1 ten

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks: hundreds flats, tens rods, unit cubes
- Place value mat per student
- Whiteboard and dry-erase marker
- Math journal with vertical algorithm workspace (three columns labeled)
- Anchor chart: "Steps for Adding with Regrouping" (created during lesson)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review ones-place regrouping from Unit 1 (two-digit context).

**Activity — "Ones Overflow" Review:**

Write on whiteboards:
- 47 + 25 = ___ (ones: 7+5=12, regroup)
- 63 + 18 = ___ (ones: 3+8=11, regroup)
- 52 + 34 = ___ (no regroup)
- 76 + 17 = ___ (ones: 6+7=13, regroup)

Quick class discussion: "When do we need to regroup in the ones place?" (When the ones add up to 10 or more.)

"Today we're going to do the same thing — but the first number has THREE digits."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Extend ones-place regrouping to three-digit numbers; establish algorithm notation.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Algorithm:** A step-by-step method for solving a problem
- **Regroup ones:** Trade 10 ones for 1 ten when the ones total is 10 or more
- **Carry:** (informal term) — we prefer "regroup" or "trade" to emphasize meaning

### Instruction Notes

**Concrete:**

Build 347 on the place value mat (3 flats, 4 rods, 7 cubes). "I want to add 26 — so I put 2 rods and 6 cubes on the mat."

"Start with the ones column. I have 7 cubes + 6 cubes = 13 cubes. That's more than 10! I trade 10 cubes for 1 rod." (Physically swap 10 cubes for 1 rod, placing it in the tens column.) "Now I have 3 cubes left in the ones column."

"Tens column: I started with 4 rods, added 2 rods, and I just got 1 more from the trade. That's 4 + 2 + 1 = 7 rods."

"Hundreds column: still 3 flats."

"My answer: 3 hundreds, 7 tens, 3 ones = 373. So 347 + 26 = 373."

**Pictorial:**
Draw 3 squares, 4 lines, 7 dots (for 347). Add 2 lines and 6 dots (for 26). Circle 10 dots, trade for a new line. Count totals.

**Abstract (with recording):**
```
    1
  347
+  26
-----
  373
```
Build the anchor chart together:
**Steps for Adding with Regrouping:**
1. Add the ones. Is the total 10 or more? → Write the ones digit, carry the 1 ten.
2. Add the tens (don't forget the carried 1!). Is the total 10 or more? → Write the tens digit, carry if needed.
3. Add (or bring down) the hundreds.
4. Check: Is the answer reasonable?

Emphasize: "The little '1' above the tens column represents the 1 ten we traded — we must write it down so we don't forget to add it!"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 263 + 48 = ?
- Ones: 3 + 8 = 11. Write 1, carry 1.
- Tens: 6 + 4 + 1 (carried) = 11. Write 1, carry 1.
- Hundreds: 2 + 1 (carried) = 3.
- **Answer: 311**

*(Note: this example spills into tens regrouping too — use it to preview Lesson 7, or simplify to an example with no tens regrouping for cleaner instruction.)*

**Example 1 (revised — ones regroup only):** 435 + 47 = ?
- Ones: 5 + 7 = 12. Write 2, carry 1.
- Tens: 3 + 4 + 1 = 8. No regroup.
- Hundreds: 4.
- **Answer: 482**

**Example 2:** 572 + 19 = ?
- Ones: 2 + 9 = 11. Write 1, carry 1.
- Tens: 7 + 1 + 1 = 9. No regroup.
- Hundreds: 5.
- **Answer: 591**

**Example 3:** 648 + 35 = ?
- Ones: 8 + 5 = 13. Write 3, carry 1.
- Tens: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8. No regroup.
- Hundreds: 6.
- **Answer: 683**

2.   516 + 37 = ___
3.   783 + 9 = ___
4.   247 + 45 = ___

**Facilitator guidance:** Before each problem, ask "Will we need to regroup in the ones place?" Students predict, then verify. Check that carried tens are recorded in writing, not just held mentally.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. Solve: 437 + 46 = ___
2. Show your work using the vertical algorithm. Circle where you regrouped.

**Mastery = 483 with correct vertical algorithm showing the carried 1 above the tens column.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Have student first solve with base-ten blocks, physically trading 10 cubes for 1 rod. Then have them record each step as they perform it with the blocks. The recording should mirror what the blocks show.

### If Mastery Is Met
Challenge: "Solve 437 + 46 a different way — maybe using mental math or a number line. Do you get the same answer? Which way do you prefer and why?"

## Extension (Optional)
**"Make It Regroup" Puzzle:**
Give the student a three-digit number (e.g., 453). Ask: "Find as many two-digit numbers as you can that, when added to 453, require regrouping in the ones place." Students list them (any number with ones digit 7, 8, or 9, or any combination where ones sum ≥ 10). Discuss the pattern.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson is a crucial bridge: students are now applying the regrouping algorithm to three-digit numbers. The only new element compared to Unit 1 is the presence of the hundreds column — but that column does not participate in this lesson (no regrouping spills into hundreds).
- If a student regroups correctly in the ones but forgets to add the carried 1 to the tens, that is the most common error. Address it explicitly: "The little 1 is a whole ten — we must add it!"
- Choose worked examples carefully: avoid examples where both ones AND tens regroup, as that is Lesson 7. Keep this lesson focused on ones-only regrouping.
- If time permits: Connect back to 2.NBT.9 by asking a student to explain WHY we can only put one digit in each column.
