# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 5, Lesson 2

## Topic
Subtracting Hundreds from Three-Digit Numbers

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract multiples of 100 from any three-digit number mentally
- Explain why only the hundreds digit changes when subtracting hundreds
- Connect addition and subtraction of hundreds using the inverse relationship

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: Adding hundreds to three-digit numbers
- Understanding of subtraction as "taking away" or "finding the difference"
- Place value knowledge of hundreds, tens, ones

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks: hundreds flats, tens rods, unit cubes
- Place value mat per student
- Number line (0–1,000 marked by hundreds)
- Dry-erase whiteboard and marker per student
- Math journal

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review addition of hundreds and prime inverse thinking.

**Activity — "What's the Connection?"**

Write on the board:
- 300 + 400 = 700
- 700 − 400 = ___
- 700 − 300 = ___

Ask students to complete and explain: "If you know the addition fact, how does it help with subtraction?"

Then do a quick-fire round: Teacher says "500 + 300" → students hold up whiteboard with answer (800). Then teacher immediately says "800 − 300" → students answer (500).

Five pairs of problems to build automaticity.

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Develop understanding that subtracting hundreds removes flats from the hundreds column, leaving tens and ones unchanged.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Difference:** The result when you subtract one number from another
- **Inverse operations:** Addition and subtraction are opposites; each undoes the other

### Instruction Notes

**Start with the concrete (base-ten blocks):**

Build 752 on the place value mat using 7 flats, 5 rods, 2 cubes. Say: "This is 752."

"I need to subtract 400. That means I take away 4 hundreds." Remove 4 flats from the mat.

"What's left? Count the flats: 1, 2, 3. Three hundreds. The rods and cubes didn't move — still 5 tens and 2 ones. So 752 − 400 = 352."

Ask: "What was the only part of the number that changed?" (The hundreds digit.)

"Does this remind you of yesterday? Adding hundreds only changed the hundreds digit — subtracting hundreds does the same thing!"

**Move to pictorial:**

Draw: 7 squares, 5 lines, 2 dots. Cross out 4 squares. Count remaining: 3 squares, 5 lines, 2 dots → 352.

**Move to abstract:**

```
  752
− 400
-----
  352
```
"Ones: 2 − 0 = 2. Tens: 5 − 0 = 5. Hundreds: 7 − 4 = 3. Answer: 352."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 680 − 200 = ?
- Hundreds: 6 − 2 = 4. Tens: 8. Ones: 0.
- **Answer: 480**

**Example 2:** 930 − 500 = ?
- Hundreds: 9 − 5 = 4. Tens: 3. Ones: 0.
- **Answer: 430**

**Example 3:** 517 − 300 = ?
- Hundreds: 5 − 3 = 2. Tens: 1. Ones: 7.
- **Answer: 217**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm understanding with facilitator support; catch errors at the concrete stage.

Have students use blocks for the first two, then move to abstract for the remaining.

1. 648 − 300 = ___
2. 850 − 400 = ___
3. 973 − 600 = ___
4. 500 − 200 = ___
5. 786 − 500 = ___

**Facilitator guidance:** Watch for students who try to subtract from the ones or tens. Ask: "We're taking away hundreds — which column do we work with?" For problem 4 (500 − 200), some students may find it tricky because there are no tens or ones. Use blocks to show 5 flats, remove 2, get 3 flats = 300.

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. 674 − 200 = ___
2. 891 − 300 = ___
3. 760 − 400 = ___
4. 900 − 600 = ___
5. 543 − 100 = ___
6. ___ − 300 = 400 (missing minuend)
7. 816 − ___ = 416 (missing subtrahend)

**Stretch:**
8. 752 − 300 − 200 = ___ (subtract two chunks of hundreds)
9. A library has 847 books. 500 books are checked out. How many remain on the shelves?
10. Start at 960. Subtract 100 three times. Where do you end up? Write the equation.

**Challenge:**
11. I subtract a multiple of 100 from 835 and get 235. What did I subtract?
12. Farmer Joe planted 900 seeds. Rabbits ate some. Now there are 300 left. How many hundreds did the rabbits eat? Write an equation.
13. A store has 678 apples. On Monday they sell 200. On Tuesday they sell 300 more. How many apples are left?

### Interleaved Review
*(Skills from Units 1–4)*

R1. 82 − 37 = ___ *(Unit 1: two-digit subtraction with regrouping)*
R2. Compare: 487 ___ 478. Write <, >, or =. *(Unit 4: comparing three-digit numbers)*
R3. 7 + 8 = ___ *(Unit 2: addition within 20)*

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**
1. 863 − 400 = ___
2. In 863 − 400 = 463, which digit changed and why?

**Mastery = correct answer (463) AND explanation names the hundreds digit changing because we subtracted hundreds.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Go back to concrete. Have student build 863, then physically remove 4 flats, then count what remains. Practice "say-it-first" strategy: before writing, student says aloud "8 hundreds take away 4 hundreds is 4 hundreds; tens and ones stay the same."

### If Mastery Is Met
Pose: "Is there a three-digit number where you CANNOT subtract 500? Why not?" (Any number with fewer than 5 hundreds — e.g., you can't subtract 500 from 342 without going below zero using this strategy. Introduce as a preview of why we'll need different strategies later.)

---

## Extension (Optional)
**"Hundreds Target" Game:**
Write the target number 500 on the board. Students are given a starting number (e.g., 873) and must find how many hundreds to subtract to get as close to 500 as possible without going below. Discuss: "How did you decide?"

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson mirrors Lesson 1 in structure. The symmetry is intentional — students should be making the connection that addition and subtraction of hundreds both affect only the hundreds digit.
- Some students may be unsure whether to look at the hundreds of the number being subtracted or the original number. Keep reinforcing: "We're taking away hundreds, so we go to the hundreds column of our starting number."
- For students who are ready, introduce the number line notation: start at 863, jump left by 400, land on 463. This reinforces the concept spatially.
- Missing addend/subtrahend problems (problems 6 and 7) preview algebraic thinking — if students struggle, connect to known addition/subtraction facts: "What number do you add to 300 to get 700?"
