# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 1, Lesson 8

## Topic
Subtracting Within 100 (No Regrouping)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract a two-digit number from a two-digit number when no regrouping is needed (e.g., 67 − 23 = 44)
- Use base-ten blocks and a place value mat to model two-digit subtraction
- Apply the break-apart strategy: subtract tens, subtract ones, combine
- Record subtraction in written (vertical) form

## Prerequisites
- Subtracting tens (Lesson 7)
- Understanding place value: tens and ones (Lesson 1)
- Addition of two two-digit numbers (Lessons 5-6)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (at least 9 tens rods and 10 ones cubes)
- Place value mat (paper with "Tens" and "Ones" columns)
- Paper and pencil
- Hundred chart (optional, for checking)

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach subtracting a two-digit number from a two-digit number (ones digit large enough, no regrouping).

### Key Vocabulary
- **Subtract (ones, then tens):** When subtracting two-digit numbers, we subtract the ones first, then the tens — just like adding
- **Break apart:** Split a number into tens and ones to subtract each part separately
- **No regrouping:** The top digit in each place is big enough to subtract from — no trading needed

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Set up the problem with blocks.**
Write: 67 − 23 = ?

Place 67 on the place value mat: 6 tens rods in the Tens column, 7 ones cubes in the Ones column.

"I have 67. I need to take away 23. Let's start with the ones."

**Step 2: Subtract the ones.**
"I need to take away 3 ones from 7 ones." Remove 3 ones cubes.

"How many ones are left?" (4.)

"Good. 7 − 3 = 4 ones."

**Step 3: Subtract the tens.**
"Now I need to take away 2 tens from 6 tens." Remove 2 tens rods.

"How many tens are left?" (4.)

"Good. 6 − 2 = 4 tens."

**Step 4: Read the answer.**
"4 tens and 4 ones = 44. So 67 − 23 = 44."

**Step 5: Record in writing.**
Write on paper:
```
  67
− 23
----
  44
```

"Ones first: 7 − 3 = 4. Write 4 in the ones place."
"Tens next: 6 − 2 = 4. Write 4 in the tens place."
"The answer is 44."

**Step 6: Break-apart strategy.**
"Let's try another way to think about 67 − 23."
- 67 = 60 + 7
- 23 = 20 + 3
- Subtract the tens: 60 − 20 = 40
- Subtract the ones: 7 − 3 = 4
- Combine: 40 + 4 = 44

"Same answer: 44. We broke both numbers apart, subtracted each place, and put the answer back together."

**Step 7: Check with addition.**
"Let's check: 44 + 23 = ? 4 + 3 = 7. 4 + 2 = 6. That gives us 67." ✓

"You can always check subtraction by adding the answer to the number you subtracted."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 58 − 34

Blocks: Start with 5 tens and 8 ones. Remove 4 ones (8 − 4 = 4 remain). Remove 3 tens (5 − 3 = 2 remain). Answer: 24.

Written:
```
  58
− 34
----
  24
```

Check: 24 + 34 = 58. ✓

**Example 2:** 89 − 52

Break apart: 80 − 50 = 30. 9 − 2 = 7. 30 + 7 = 37.

Written:
```
  89
− 52
----
  37
```

Check: 37 + 52 = 89. ✓

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices two-digit subtraction without regrouping.

**Problem 1 (with blocks): 76 − 43**
Student places 7 tens and 6 ones on the mat. Removes 3 ones (6 − 3 = 3). Removes 4 tens (7 − 4 = 3). Answer: 33. ✓
Ask: "Check with addition: 33 + 43 = ?" (76.) ✓

**Problem 2 (with blocks and writing): 95 − 41**
Student builds 95, removes 1 one and 4 tens. Answer: 54. ✓
Student writes it vertically:
```
  95
− 41
----
  54
```
Ask: "Did you need to regroup?" (No, 5 is bigger than 1 in the ones, and 9 is bigger than 4 in the tens.)

**Problem 3 (break-apart): 86 − 35**
"Break both numbers apart."
80 − 30 = 50. 6 − 5 = 1. 50 + 1 = 51. ✓

*Facilitator prompt: If the student tries to subtract the larger digit from the smaller (e.g., in 86 − 35, subtracting 6 − 5 as 5 − 6), gently redirect: "In subtraction, we always take away from the top number. 86 is our starting amount. Which number has more ones — 86 or 35?"*

**Problem 4 (writing, blocks available): 79 − 56**
Student writes vertically and solves: 9 − 6 = 3, 7 − 5 = 2. Answer: 23. ✓
Ask: "How do you know you don't need to regroup?" (Because 9 is bigger than 6, so I have enough ones to subtract.)

**Purpose:** Build fluency with two-digit subtraction (no regrouping).

**★ On-level:**

2. 67 − 34 = ___ (33)
3. 89 − 56 = ___ (33)
4. 75 − 42 = ___ (33)
5. 96 − 63 = ___ (33)

**★★ Stretch:**
6. 84 − 51 = ___ (33) "Check your answer with addition."
7. "I had 78 stickers. I gave 45 to my friend. How many do I have left?" ___ (33) "Write the number sentence." (78 − 45 = 33)
8. "What's different about subtracting 67 − 23 and adding 23 + 44?" (They're related — 67 − 23 = 44, and 23 + 44 = 67. Subtraction and addition are opposites.)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "Find a subtraction problem where the answer is 25 and you do NOT need to regroup." (Many answers, e.g., 69 − 44, 58 − 33, 47 − 22, 36 − 11)
10. "Can you always subtract without regrouping? Think of a problem where you CAN'T subtract without regrouping." (e.g., 52 − 18: you can't take 8 from 2 without regrouping.) "We'll learn how to handle that soon!"

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess two-digit subtraction without regrouping.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Solve 87 − 45. Show your work. (42)
2. Solve 69 − 36. (33)
3. "How can you check if your answer to 87 − 45 is correct?" (Add: 42 + 45 = 87.)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct, with written work or explanation.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student subtracts in the wrong direction (e.g., does 5 − 7 instead of 7 − 5 in 87 − 45): use blocks. "You're starting with 87. You're taking 45 away from 87." The physical model makes the direction clear.
- If the student confuses the tens and ones columns: draw the place value columns clearly on the paper with a thick line. Label "Tens" and "Ones" above.
- If computation errors (e.g., 7 − 5 = 3): review basic subtraction facts within 10 before continuing.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 9 (Subtracting Within 100 — With Regrouping). Preview: "What happens when you need to subtract more ones than you have? Get ready to make another trade!"

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson mirrors addition from Lesson 5.** In Lesson 5, students added two two-digit numbers without regrouping. Now they subtract. The structure is parallel: work with ones first, then tens. Students who are comfortable with the addition process should transition smoothly.

**The "no regrouping" condition.** This lesson intentionally avoids problems where the ones digit being subtracted is larger than the ones digit of the starting number (e.g., we don't do 63 − 25 because 5 > 3). This lets the student focus on the subtraction process without the added complexity of regrouping. Lesson 9 introduces regrouping.

**How to tell if regrouping is needed.** Teach the student to look at the ones digits: "Is the top ones digit bigger than the bottom ones digit? If yes, no regrouping needed. If no, you'll need to regroup." This becomes critical in Lesson 9.

**Checking with addition.** Encourage the student to check every subtraction answer by adding. This is not just a checking strategy — it deepens the connection between addition and subtraction. Ask: "How can you be sure 67 − 23 = 44?" The answer "because 44 + 23 = 67" shows real understanding.

**Common error: subtracting the smaller from the larger in each column.** Some students do 62 − 48 as "8 − 2 = 6, 6 − 4 = 2, answer 26" — they subtract the smaller digit from the larger in each place regardless of which number is on top. This produces wrong answers and must be caught early. The blocks prevent this error because the student is physically taking away from the top number.
