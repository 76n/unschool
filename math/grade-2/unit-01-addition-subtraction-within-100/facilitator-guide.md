# Facilitator Guide — Grade 2 Math, Unit 1: Addition and Subtraction Within 100

**Grade:** 2
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 1 — Addition and Subtraction Within 100
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Partially facilitated (adult introduces lessons, supports guided practice, monitors independent work)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit is a significant leap: students move from single-digit and teen-number arithmetic to working with two-digit numbers up to 100. They learn to add and subtract by thinking in tens and ones, building on place value understanding.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Add within 20 fluently (building on Grade 1)
- Add a two-digit number and a one-digit number (e.g., 34 + 7)
- Add a two-digit number and a multiple of 10 (e.g., 34 + 20)
- Add two two-digit numbers (e.g., 36 + 47) using strategies based on place value
- Subtract multiples of 10 from two-digit numbers (e.g., 73 − 40)
- Subtract within 100 using strategies (e.g., 52 − 18)
- Solve one- and two-step word problems within 100
- Understand and apply mental math strategies: breaking apart numbers, compensation, making friendly numbers

**Why this matters:** Two-digit addition and subtraction are the bridge between basic arithmetic and multi-digit computation. Students who develop flexible place-value strategies here are well-prepared for three- and four-digit operations in Grade 3. Students who rely on rote procedures without understanding struggle when numbers get larger.

---

## Prerequisite Check

Before beginning this unit, confirm:

- [ ] Fluent addition and subtraction within 20 (Grade 1 Unit 1)
- [ ] Understands place value: a two-digit number is made of tens and ones (e.g., 47 = 4 tens + 7 ones)
- [ ] Can count by 10s to 100 (10, 20, 30... 100)
- [ ] Can read and write numbers to 100
- [ ] Understands the equal sign as "the same as"

---

## Materials Needed for This Unit

- **Base-ten blocks** (or bundled sticks/craft sticks): tens rods and ones cubes. At least 10 tens and 20 ones.
  - **Homemade alternative:** Bundle 10 craft sticks with a rubber band to make a "ten." Use individual sticks as ones.
- **Hundred chart** (10×10 grid showing 1-100)
- **Number line 0-100** (can be homemade on a long strip of paper)
- **Paper and pencil**
- **Place value mat** (a piece of paper divided into "Tens" and "Ones" columns)

## Pacing Guidance

| Lesson | Topic | Sessions |
|--------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Review: Fluency Within 20 and Place Value | 1 |
| 2 | Adding Tens (30 + 40, 50 + 20) | 1 |
| 3 | Adding a Two-Digit and One-Digit Number (No Regrouping) | 1 |
| 4 | Adding a Two-Digit and One-Digit Number (With Regrouping) | 1-2 |
| 5 | Adding Two Two-Digit Numbers (No Regrouping) | 1 |
| 6 | Adding Two Two-Digit Numbers (With Regrouping) | 1-2 |
| 7 | Subtracting Tens | 1 |
| 8 | Subtracting Within 100 (No Regrouping) | 1 |
| 9 | Subtracting Within 100 (With Regrouping) | 1-2 |
| 10 | Word Problems Within 100 | 1 |
| — | Unit Assessment | Separate session |

**Adjusting the pace:**
- Lessons 4, 6, and 9 involve regrouping (the hardest concept). Budget 2 sessions each if needed.
- If the student is still not fluent within 20 (Lesson 1), pause and revisit Grade 1 materials.

## Common Misconceptions

| What You Might See | What It Means | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| 34 + 28 = 512 (adds 3+2=5, 4+8=12, writes 512) | Student adds each column separately without understanding place value | Use base-ten blocks. Show that 34 is 3 tens and 4 ones, not "a 3 and a 4." Rebuild the connection between the digits and their values. |
| Student can't regroup (e.g., when ones add to more than 9) | Regrouping is a new concept; 10 ones = 1 ten hasn't clicked | Trade physically: exchange 10 ones-cubes for 1 tens-rod. Make the trade visible and tangible. Do many trades before connecting to written computation. |
| Student subtracts the smaller digit from the larger in each column (e.g., 42−18: does 8−2=6, 4−1=3, gets 36) | Doesn't understand subtraction with regrouping | Use base-ten blocks to show: "You can't take 8 ones from 2 ones. You need to break apart a ten into 10 ones first." |
| Student uses a procedure correctly but can't explain why it works | Procedural fluency without conceptual understanding | Go back to base-ten blocks for every problem. Ask "Why did you do that?" at each step. The student should be able to explain with reference to tens and ones. |
| Student struggles with the hundred chart for addition/subtraction | Doesn't see the pattern: moving right = +1, moving down = +10 | Explicitly teach the hundred chart patterns. Color in the path for 34 + 23: move down 2 rows (+20) and right 3 spaces (+3) to land on 57. |

## Conversation Starters

- "What's 40 + 30? How do you know without counting?"
- "If I add 7 to 45, what happens to the ones digit? What about the tens?"
- "How is adding 36 + 20 different from adding 36 + 27?"
- "I need to subtract 53 − 28. Do I have enough ones to subtract 8? What should I do?"
- "Can you solve 49 + 32 in your head? What strategy did you use?"

---

## When to Seek Additional Help

Consider seeking additional support if:

- The student cannot fluently add and subtract within 20 after extended practice (Grade 1-level gap)
- The student consistently demonstrates no understanding of place value (treats 47 as "a 4 and a 7" rather than 40 + 7)
- Regrouping remains incomprehensible after multiple lessons with concrete manipulatives
- Math anxiety increases to the point of avoidance or distress

---

## What Comes Next

After this unit, students move to **Unit 2: Fluent Addition and Subtraction Within 20**, which focuses on memorizing basic facts (building toward automaticity). The strategies from this unit provide the understanding; Unit 2 builds speed.

Then **Unit 3: Introduction to Multiplication as Equal Groups** extends arithmetic into the multiplicative realm.
