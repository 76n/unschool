# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 1, Lesson 7

## Topic
Subtracting Tens

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Subtract multiples of 10 from multiples of 10 (e.g., 70 − 30 = 40)
- Subtract a multiple of 10 from any two-digit number (e.g., 85 − 40 = 45)
- Use base-ten blocks and the hundred chart to model subtraction of tens
- Connect subtraction to addition (if 30 + 40 = 70, then 70 − 30 = 40)

## Prerequisites
- Adding tens (Lesson 2)
- Adding two two-digit numbers with regrouping (Lesson 6)
- Subtraction within 20 (Grade 1 fluency)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (at least 9 tens rods and 10 ones cubes)
- Hundred chart (10x10 grid showing 1-100)
- Place value mat
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review adding tens and activate subtraction thinking.

1. "What is 30 + 50?" (80) *(Lesson 2)*
2. "What is 46 + 30?" (76) *(Lesson 2)*
3. "What is 14 − 6?" (8) *(Grade 1 review)*
4. "If 20 + 40 = 60, what is 60 − 20?" (40) "How do you know?" (Because adding and subtracting are opposites.)

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach subtraction of multiples of 10 using blocks, the hundred chart, and the addition connection.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Subtract:** Take away; find how many are left or find the difference
- **Multiple of 10:** A number you say when counting by 10s (10, 20, 30, 40, 50...)
- **Difference:** The answer when you subtract

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Subtract multiples of 10 from multiples of 10 (blocks).**
Write: 70 − 30 = ?

Place 7 tens rods on the place value mat. "I have 70. I need to take away 30. How many tens rods do I remove?" (3.)

Remove 3 tens rods. "How many are left?" (4 tens rods = 40.)

"70 − 30 = 40."

**Step 2: Connect to addition.**
"Think about it this way: 30 + ___ = 70. What fills the blank?" (40.)
"Subtraction and addition are related. If you know 30 + 40 = 70, then you also know 70 − 30 = 40 AND 70 − 40 = 30."

Write the fact family:
- 30 + 40 = 70
- 40 + 30 = 70
- 70 − 30 = 40
- 70 − 40 = 30

**Step 3: Subtract a multiple of 10 from a two-digit number (blocks).**
Write: 85 − 40 = ?

Place 8 tens rods and 5 ones cubes on the mat. "I have 85 — that's 8 tens and 5 ones. I need to take away 40. Are we taking away any ones?" (No, just tens.)

Remove 4 tens rods. "How many tens are left?" (4.) "How many ones?" (5, still the same.)

"85 − 40 = 45. The ones didn't change at all."

**Step 4: Use the hundred chart.**
Point to 85 on the hundred chart. "To subtract 10, I move UP one row. Watch."

85 → 75 (subtract 10) → 65 (subtract 10) → 55 (subtract 10) → 45 (subtract 10).

"I moved up 4 rows because I subtracted 4 tens — that's 40. 85 − 40 = 45."

"On the hundred chart, adding tens means moving DOWN and subtracting tens means moving UP."

**Step 5: Notice the pattern.**
Write several examples:
- 85 − 40 = 45
- 63 − 20 = 43
- 97 − 50 = 47

"What do you notice about the ones digit?" (It stays the same!) "When you subtract tens, only the tens digit changes. The ones digit doesn't move."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 60 − 20

Blocks: 6 tens, remove 2, leaves 4 tens = 40.
Addition check: 20 + 40 = 60. ✓
Hundred chart: start at 60, move up 2 rows → 50 → 40. ✓

**Example 2:** 74 − 30

Blocks: 7 tens and 4 ones. Remove 3 tens. Left with 4 tens and 4 ones = 44.
Addition check: 30 + 44 = 74. ✓
"The ones digit stayed at 4."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices subtracting tens with scaffolded support.

**Problem 1 (blocks): 90 − 50**
Student places 9 tens rods, removes 5. Counts: 4 tens = 40. ✓
Ask: "What addition fact goes with this?" (50 + 40 = 90)

**Problem 2 (blocks): 68 − 30**
Student places 6 tens and 8 ones, removes 3 tens. Left with 3 tens and 8 ones = 38. ✓
Ask: "Did the ones change?" (No, still 8.)

**Problem 3 (hundred chart): 76 − 40**
Student finds 76 on the chart, moves up 4 rows: 76 → 66 → 56 → 46 → 36. ✓
Ask: "How many rows did you move up?" (4.) "Why 4?" (Because 40 is 4 tens.)

**Problem 4 (mental math): 53 − 20**
"Try this one in your head. What's 53 − 20?" (33.)
"How did you figure it out?" (5 tens minus 2 tens is 3 tens, ones stay the same — 33.)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student is unsure about mental subtraction, return to blocks. The goal is for the student to see the pattern: only the tens digit changes. Once they see it, mental math follows naturally.*

**Problem 5 (mental math): 81 − 50**
Student: 81 − 50 = 31. ✓
"Check with addition: 50 + 31 = ?" (81.) ✓

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency subtracting tens.

**★ On-level:**
1. 80 − 30 = ___ (50)
2. 60 − 40 = ___ (20)
3. 75 − 20 = ___ (55)
4. 93 − 60 = ___ (33)
5. 58 − 30 = ___ (28)

**★★ Stretch:**
6. 87 − 50 = ___ (37) "Check with addition: 50 + ___ = 87."
7. "I start at 94 on the hundred chart and move up 3 rows. Where do I land?" (64)
8. "What is 100 − 40?" (60) "How many tens rods would be left if you started with 10?" (6)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "___ − 30 = 45. What number did I start with?" (75) "How do you know?"
10. "I'm thinking of a number. When I subtract 50, I get 26. When I add 20, I get 96. What's my number?" (76. Check: 76 − 50 = 26 ✓. 76 + 20 = 96 ✓.)

### Interleaved Review
- "36 + 47 = ___?" (83) *(Lesson 6)*
- "28 + 5 = ___?" (33) *(Lesson 4)*
- "15 − 9 = ___?" (6) *(Grade 1 review)*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess subtraction of tens.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 70 − 40 = ___ (30)
2. 86 − 50 = ___ (36)
3. "If 30 + 40 = 70, what is 70 − 40?" (30) "How do you know?" (Because subtraction undoes addition.)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student struggles with multiples of 10 only (Problem 1): return to base-ten blocks and practice 5 more problems removing tens rods. Connect each one to an addition fact.
- If the student struggles when ones are present (Problem 2): emphasize that the ones do not change. Do several problems where the student first predicts the ones digit, then checks with blocks.
- Use the hundred chart as a visual anchor: moving up = subtracting 10.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 8 (Subtracting Within 100 — No Regrouping). Preview: "Now we'll subtract ones AND tens at the same time!"

## Extension (Optional)
**Hundred Chart Hop:** "Start at 97. Subtract 30. Now subtract 20 more. Where are you?" (97 → 67 → 47.) "What's the total amount you subtracted?" (50.)

**Tens subtraction chain:** "Start at 100. Subtract 10. Now subtract 20 from your answer. Now subtract 30 from THAT answer. What do you get?" (100 → 90 → 70 → 40.) "What's the total you subtracted?" (60.) "Is 10 + 20 + 30 = 60?" (Yes!)

**Real-world connection:** "A jar has 80 marbles. You scoop out a handful and count 30. How many are still in the jar?" (50.) "Your friend scoops out 20 more. How many now?" (30.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Subtracting tens is the mirror of adding tens (Lesson 2).** Students who were comfortable adding tens should find this lesson manageable. The key insight is the same: only the tens digit changes when you add or subtract a multiple of 10.

**The addition connection is powerful.** When a student is stuck on 70 − 30, asking "What plus 30 makes 70?" often unlocks the answer. This "think addition" strategy for subtraction will become increasingly important in Lessons 8 and 9.

**The hundred chart pattern.** Moving down on the hundred chart adds 10; moving up subtracts 10. If the student learned this pattern when adding tens, subtraction of tens should feel like the natural reverse. Spend time with the chart if the student is visual.

**Ones don't change.** This is the big pattern to notice. When you subtract 40 from 85, the 5 ones are untouched — only the tens change. Point this out explicitly and have the student predict: "If I do 73 − 20, what will the ones digit be?" (3.) This builds toward Lesson 8 where both tens and ones are subtracted.

**This lesson typically takes one session.** Because it mirrors the structure of adding tens, most students pick it up quickly. If extra time is available, use it for the extension activities or additional mental math practice.
