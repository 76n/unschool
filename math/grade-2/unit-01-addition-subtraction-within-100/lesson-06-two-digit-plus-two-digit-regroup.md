# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 1, Lesson 6

## Topic
Adding Two Two-Digit Numbers (With Regrouping)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Add two two-digit numbers when the ones digits sum to more than 9 (e.g., 36 + 47 = 83)
- Physically regroup 10 ones as 1 ten using base-ten blocks
- Record regrouping in writing alongside concrete models
- Use the break-apart strategy to add two-digit numbers with regrouping

## Prerequisites
- Adding two two-digit numbers without regrouping (Lesson 5)
- Adding a two-digit number and a one-digit number with regrouping (Lesson 4)
- Understanding place value: tens and ones (Lesson 1)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (at least 15 tens rods and 20 ones cubes)
- Place value mat (paper with "Tens" and "Ones" columns)
- Paper and pencil
- Hundred chart (optional, for checking answers)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate regrouping from Lesson 4 and review no-regrouping addition from Lesson 5.

1. "What is 27 + 5?" (32) "Did you need to regroup?" (Yes, 7 + 5 = 12, so trade 10 ones for 1 ten.) *(Lesson 4)*
2. "What is 43 + 25?" (68) "Did you need to regroup?" (No, 3 + 5 = 8, no trade needed.) *(Lesson 5)*
3. "What is 60 + 30?" (90) *(Lesson 2)*
4. "15 − 8 = ___?" (7) *(Grade 1 fluency review)*

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach adding two two-digit numbers when the ones make more than 9, requiring a trade.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Regroup:** Trade 10 ones for 1 ten (also called "carrying" or "trading")
- **Break apart:** Split a number into its tens and ones to add each part separately

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Set up the problem with blocks.**
Write: 36 + 47 = ?

Place 36 on the place value mat: 3 tens rods in the Tens column, 6 ones cubes in the Ones column.
Place 47 below it: 4 tens rods in the Tens column, 7 ones cubes in the Ones column.

"We need to add 36 and 47. Let's start with the ones."

**Step 2: Add the ones — discover the problem.**
Push all the ones cubes together: 6 + 7 = 13 ones cubes.

"I have 13 ones. Can I fit 13 in the ones column?" (No — the ones column holds single ones, and we have more than 9.)

"What do we do when we have 10 or more ones?" (Trade 10 ones for 1 ten!)

**Step 3: Regroup — physically trade.**
Count out 10 ones cubes and exchange them for 1 tens rod. Place the new tens rod in the Tens column. 3 ones cubes remain in the Ones column.

"We traded 10 ones for 1 ten. Now I have 3 ones left."

**Step 4: Add the tens.**
"Now count all the tens: 3 tens + 4 tens + 1 new ten = 8 tens."

Push all tens rods together: 8 tens rods in the Tens column.

**Step 5: Read the answer.**
"8 tens and 3 ones = 83. So 36 + 47 = 83."

**Step 6: Record in writing.**
Write on paper:
```
  36
+ 47
----
```

"Ones first: 6 + 7 = 13. Write 3 in the ones place. Write a small 1 above the tens column — that's the ten we traded."

```
   1
  36
+ 47
----
  83
```

"Tens: 3 + 4 + 1 = 8. Write 8 in the tens place. The answer is 83."

**Step 7: Break-apart strategy (alternative recording).**
"Another way to think about 36 + 47:"
- 36 = 30 + 6
- 47 = 40 + 7
- Add the ones: 6 + 7 = 13
- Add the tens: 30 + 40 = 70
- Combine: 70 + 13 = 83

"Both methods give 83. The blocks, the written method, and the break-apart strategy all work."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 28 + 35

With blocks: 8 + 5 = 13 ones. Trade 10 ones for 1 ten. 3 ones remain. Tens: 2 + 3 + 1 = 6 tens. Answer: 63.

Written:
```
   1
  28
+ 35
----
  63
```

Break apart: 20 + 30 = 50. 8 + 5 = 13. 50 + 13 = 63.

**Example 2:** 54 + 29

Ones: 4 + 9 = 13. Trade 10 ones for 1 ten. 3 ones remain. Tens: 5 + 2 + 1 = 8 tens. Answer: 83.

Written:
```
   1
  54
+ 29
----
  83
```

Break apart: 50 + 20 = 70. 4 + 9 = 13. 70 + 13 = 83.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices regrouping with blocks and then transitions toward written recording.

**Problem 1 (with blocks): 46 + 37**
Ask: "Start with the ones. What is 6 + 7?" (13)
"Do you need to regroup?" (Yes) "Make the trade."
Student trades 10 ones for 1 ten, 3 ones remain.
"Now add the tens: 4 + 3 + 1 = ?" (8)
"What's the answer?" (83) ✓

**Problem 2 (with blocks and writing): 57 + 26**
Student builds 57 and 26 on the mat. Adds ones: 7 + 6 = 13. Trades. 3 ones remain.
Adds tens: 5 + 2 + 1 = 8. Answer: 83.
Student also records in written form with the small 1 above the tens.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student forgets to add the regrouped ten, point to the extra tens rod on the mat. "Did you count this one? Where did it come from?"*

**Problem 3 (writing with blocks available): 38 + 24**
Student writes the problem vertically, solves with writing, and can check with blocks.
8 + 4 = 12. Write 2, carry 1. 3 + 2 + 1 = 6. Answer: 62. ✓

**Problem 4 (break-apart strategy): 45 + 38**
"Break both numbers apart. What are the tens? What are the ones?"
40 + 30 = 70. 5 + 8 = 13. 70 + 13 = 83. ✓

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles with the break-apart strategy, go back to blocks. The concrete model is the priority. The break-apart strategy is an alternative, not a requirement.*

**Purpose:** Build fluency with two-digit addition requiring regrouping.

**★ On-level:**
1. 29 + 43 = ___ (72)
2. 36 + 18 = ___ (54)
3. 55 + 27 = ___ (82)
4. 47 + 34 = ___ (81)
5. 68 + 15 = ___ (83)

**★★ Stretch:**
6. 49 + 36 = ___ (85) Show your work using break-apart or written method.
7. 58 + 34 = ___ (92) "Did you need to regroup? How do you know?"
8. "Sam says 37 + 45 = 72. Is he right? How do you know?" (No. 7 + 5 = 12, regroup. 3 + 4 + 1 = 8. Answer is 82, not 72. Sam forgot to add the regrouped ten.)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "Find two two-digit numbers that add up to 71, where you MUST regroup." (Many answers, e.g., 36 + 35, 47 + 24, 58 + 13)
10. "Is it possible to add two two-digit numbers and get an answer greater than 100? Try to find two numbers that do." (Yes, e.g., 55 + 55 = 110 or 67 + 48 = 115)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess whether the student can add two two-digit numbers with regrouping.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Solve 27 + 46. Show your work. (73)
2. Solve 55 + 38. (93)
3. "When you add 36 + 47, you get 13 ones. What do you do next?" (Trade 10 ones for 1 ten. Write 3 in the ones place and carry the 1 to the tens column.)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct, with evidence of regrouping (written work or explanation).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student is not trading/regrouping: return to base-ten blocks and do 5 more problems physically. The student must see the trade happen with their hands.
- If the student regroups with blocks but can't record it in writing: slow down. Do 3 problems side-by-side — blocks on the mat, writing on paper — connecting each step.
- If the student forgets to add the regrouped ten: use a brightly colored marker for the carried 1. Ask "What does this little 1 mean?" every time.
- Budget a second session if needed. Regrouping in addition is a critical skill.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (Subtracting Tens). Preview: "We've been adding — now it's time to take away!"

## Extension (Optional)
**Regrouping Riddle:** "I'm thinking of two numbers. Both are between 20 and 50. When I add them, I get exactly 75, and I need to regroup. What could my numbers be?" (Many answers: 28 + 47, 39 + 36, 46 + 29, etc.) Have the student find at least three different pairs and verify each one with blocks or writing.

**Real-world connection:** "You have 36 crayons and your friend has 47 crayons. If you put all the crayons together, how many do you have? Can you fit them in a box that holds 80 crayons?" (83 total, no — you'd need a bigger box.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is a big lesson.** Adding two two-digit numbers with regrouping combines everything from Lessons 2-5. The student must manage place value, ones-column addition, recognizing when to regroup, making the trade, and then adding the tens including the new ten. That's a lot of steps. Be patient.

**The physical trade is the key moment.** When the student counts out 10 ones cubes and replaces them with 1 tens rod, they are experiencing regrouping concretely. This must happen many times before the written "carry the 1" makes sense. Do not skip the blocks.

**Common error: forgetting the regrouped ten.** The most frequent mistake is adding the tens without the extra ten from regrouping (e.g., 36 + 47: student gets 73 instead of 83). The blocks prevent this — the extra rod is sitting right there. When transitioning to writing, the small "1" above the tens column serves the same purpose.

**Break-apart strategy is optional.** Some students find it easier to think of 36 + 47 as 30 + 40 + 6 + 7 = 70 + 13 = 83. Others prefer the vertical written method. Both are valid. Let the student use whichever feels more natural, but make sure they can explain why it works.

**Two sessions may be needed.** If the student can regroup with blocks but is shaky with written recording, a second session that focuses on the written method (with blocks available for checking) is a good investment.
