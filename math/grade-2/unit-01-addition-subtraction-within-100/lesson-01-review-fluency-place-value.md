# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 1, Lesson 1

## Topic
Review — Fluency Within 20 and Place Value

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Add and subtract within 20 fluently using known strategies (counting on, making 10, doubles, near-doubles)
- Represent any two-digit number as a combination of tens and ones (e.g., 74 = 7 tens + 4 ones)
- Use base-ten blocks to build and read two-digit numbers

## Prerequisites
- Grade 1 Unit 1 mastery: addition and subtraction within 20
- Ability to count to 100
- Basic understanding that two-digit numbers have a tens place and a ones place

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (tens rods and ones cubes) — at least 9 tens and 9 ones
- Place value mat (paper divided into "Tens" and "Ones" columns)
- Hundred chart (10x10 grid showing 1-100)
- Paper and pencil
- 20 small objects for counting (buttons, coins, or cereal pieces)

**Standards:** 2.NBT.5, 2.NBT.1

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of basic facts and get the student thinking about numbers.

Say to the student: "Before we start our new unit, let's warm up our math brains with some quick facts."

Solve these as fast as you can:
1. 8 + 5 = ___
2. 14 − 6 = ___
3. 7 + 7 = ___
4. 16 − 9 = ___

Then ask: "Which one was easiest for you? What strategy did you use?"

**Facilitator note:** Listen for strategies like "I counted on," "I used doubles," or "I made a 10." If the student counts on fingers for every problem, note this — it may indicate that Grade 1 fluency is not yet solid.

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review the two foundational skills this unit builds upon: fluency within 20 and place value understanding.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Addend:** A number being added (in 3 + 5, both 3 and 5 are addends)
- **Sum:** The answer when you add (3 + 5 = 8; the sum is 8)
- **Difference:** The answer when you subtract (8 − 3 = 5; the difference is 5)
- **Tens:** Groups of 10 (the tens rod in base-ten blocks represents 10)
- **Ones:** Single units (the small cube in base-ten blocks represents 1)
- **Place value:** The value of a digit based on its position in a number (in 47, the 4 is in the tens place and means 40)

### Instruction Notes

**Part A — Fluency Within 20 (7 minutes)**

Say: "In first grade, you learned lots of ways to add and subtract. Let's review three important strategies."

**Strategy 1: Counting On**
Say: "When one number is small, we can count on from the bigger number."
- Show: 9 + 3. Start at 9, count on 3: "10, 11, 12." So 9 + 3 = 12.

**Strategy 2: Doubles and Near-Doubles**
Say: "When the two numbers are the same or almost the same, we can use doubles."
- Show: 6 + 6 = 12 (doubles).
- Show: 6 + 7 — "This is 6 + 6 plus one more. 12 + 1 = 13."

**Strategy 3: Make a 10**
Say: "This is the most powerful strategy. We break apart one number to make a 10."
- Show: 8 + 5. "I need 2 more to make 8 into 10. I take 2 from the 5, leaving 3. Now I have 10 + 3 = 13."
- Use objects or fingers to show the 2 moving from the 5 to the 8.

Say: "These same strategies will help us with bigger numbers in this unit!"

**Part B — Place Value Review (8 minutes)**

Bring out the base-ten blocks and the place value mat.

Say: "Every two-digit number is made of tens and ones. Let's build some numbers."

**Step 1:** Place 3 tens rods and 5 ones cubes on the place value mat.
Ask: "How many tens? How many ones? What number is this?"
Answer: 3 tens and 5 ones = 35.

**Step 2:** Say the number 62. Ask the student to build it.
Guide: "How many tens rods do you need? How many ones cubes?"
The student should place 6 tens rods and 2 ones cubes.

**Step 3:** Write the number 48 on paper. Ask: "What does the 4 mean? What does the 8 mean?"
Answer: The 4 means 4 tens (or 40). The 8 means 8 ones.

Say: "This is important: the 4 in 48 does not just mean 4 — it means 40! That is called place value."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Addition within 20 using Make a 10:**
Problem: 7 + 8
- Step 1: "I want to make a 10. 7 needs 3 more to become 10."
- Step 2: "I break 8 into 3 and 5."
- Step 3: "Now I have 10 + 5 = 15."
- Answer: 7 + 8 = 15

**Example 2 — Building a number with base-ten blocks:**
Problem: Show the number 57 with base-ten blocks.
- Step 1: "57 has a 5 in the tens place. I need 5 tens rods." (Place 5 rods on mat.)
- Step 2: "57 has a 7 in the ones place. I need 7 ones cubes." (Place 7 cubes on mat.)
- Step 3: "5 tens is 50. 7 ones is 7. 50 + 7 = 57."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice both skills together with facilitator support.

**Part A — Fluency (problems 1-4).** Have the student solve these and name the strategy they use.

1. 9 + 6 = ___
   - Ask: "What strategy are you using?"
2. 15 − 8 = ___
   - If the student struggles: "Can you think of the addition fact? 8 + ___ = 15?"
3. 7 + 5 = ___
4. 13 − 7 = ___

**Part B — Place Value (problems 5-8).** Use base-ten blocks and the place value mat.

5. Build the number 83 with base-ten blocks. "How many tens? How many ones?"
6. The facilitator builds 4 tens and 9 ones. "What number did I build?"
7. "In the number 76, what does the 7 mean? What does the 6 mean?"
8. "I have 5 tens and 0 ones. What number is that?" (Answer: 50 — reinforce that a number can have zero ones.)

**Facilitator note:** If the student confuses the digit with its value (says "the 7 means 7" instead of "70"), gently redirect: "The 7 is in the tens place. How much is 7 tens?" Count tens rods together if needed: "10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70."

**Purpose:** Student works alone to build fluency and confidence.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment to gauge mastery.

### Exit Ticket

1. Solve: 8 + 7 = ___
2. Solve: 14 − 6 = ___
3. Write the number shown: 4 tens and 2 ones = ___
4. In the number 59, what does the 5 mean? ___

**Mastery Criteria:** 3 out of 4 correct, including at least one from each skill (fluency and place value).

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- **Fluency gap:** Return to Grade 1 addition/subtraction practice. Use flashcards or an online fact-practice tool for 5-10 minutes daily. Focus on the "Make a 10" strategy.
- **Place value gap:** Spend extra time building numbers with base-ten blocks. Have the student build 5-10 different numbers and say "[digit] tens and [digit] ones equals [number]" for each one.
- Repeat this lesson before moving to Lesson 2.

### If Mastery Is Met
Preview: "Tomorrow we are going to start adding bigger numbers! We will begin by adding tens — like 30 + 40. You already know how to count by 10s, so you are ready."

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Purpose:** Enrichment for students who finish early or demonstrate advanced understanding.

**Number Riddles:** Write (or say) these riddles for the student to solve:
- "I am a two-digit number. My tens digit is 3 more than my ones digit. My ones digit is 2. What number am I?" (Answer: 52)
- "I am between 60 and 70. My ones digit plus my tens digit equals 13. What number am I?" (Answer: 67)

**Real-World Connection:** Ask the student to find two-digit numbers around the house (page numbers in a book, numbers on food packages, a clock showing minutes). For each one, have them tell you how many tens and how many ones it has.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Misconceptions
- **Digit vs. value confusion:** Students often say the 4 in 43 "means 4" instead of "means 40." Consistently ask "What does this digit *mean*?" and connect back to base-ten blocks.
- **Counting instead of using strategies:** If the student finger-counts for every addition fact within 20, they may not be ready for this unit. Fluency within 20 is a prerequisite. Consider spending 2-3 sessions on fact-strategy practice before continuing.
- **Zero in the ones place:** Numbers like 30, 50, 80 sometimes confuse students. Show with blocks: "3 tens and 0 ones — there are no ones cubes, and that is okay."

### Pacing Adjustments
- If the student is very fluent within 20 and solid on place value, this lesson can be shortened to 30 minutes and you can move quickly to Lesson 2.
- If the student is shaky on either skill, consider splitting this into two sessions: one for fluency review, one for place value review.

### Discussion Questions
- "Why do you think we call it 'place value'? What does the 'place' of a digit tell us?"
- "Can you think of a time you used adding or subtracting in real life this week?"
