# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 4, Lesson 5

## Topic
Different Ways to Show a Number

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Represent the same number in standard form, expanded form, word form, and with base-ten blocks
- Move flexibly between all four representations
- Understand that regrouping changes how a number looks but not its value (e.g., 3 hundreds + 2 tens = 2 hundreds + 12 tens)
- Prepare for addition and subtraction with regrouping in Unit 5

## Prerequisites
- Identifying hundreds, tens, and ones (Lesson 2)
- Reading and writing three-digit numbers in standard and word form (Lesson 3)
- Writing numbers in expanded form (Lesson 4)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (flats, rods, unit cubes)
- Place value chart (H | T | O)
- "Four Ways" worksheet (four boxes labeled: Standard Form, Expanded Form, Word Form, Blocks/Drawing)
- Paper and pencil
- 10 rods rubber-banded together to show trading for 1 flat

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick review of all three written representations.

1. Write 463 on the board. "What form is this?" (Standard form.) "Write it in expanded form." (400 + 60 + 3) "Now write it in word form." (four hundred sixty-three)
2. "You just showed one number three different ways. Which way is easiest for you? Why?"
3. "Today we'll add a fourth way — drawing blocks — and we'll practice switching between ALL four. We'll also discover something surprising about trading blocks."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Unify all four representations and introduce flexible regrouping.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Representation:** A way of showing a number (standard form, expanded form, word form, base-ten blocks)
- **Regroup:** Trade 10 of one unit for 1 of the next larger unit, or 1 of a larger unit for 10 of the next smaller unit
- **Equivalent:** Having the same value

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Four ways to show 258.**
Draw four boxes on the board (or use the worksheet). Build 258 with blocks and fill in each box together:
- **Standard form:** 258
- **Expanded form:** 200 + 50 + 8
- **Word form:** two hundred fifty-eight
- **Blocks/Drawing:** Draw 2 large squares (flats), 5 tall rectangles (rods), 8 small squares (cubes)

"All four boxes show the SAME number. The number didn't change — only how we wrote it."

**Step 2: Starting from any representation.**
Show expanded form: 600 + 30 + 5. "What is the standard form?" (635) "Word form?" (six hundred thirty-five) "Build it with blocks." Student builds 6 flats, 3 rods, 5 cubes. "See? We can start from any box and fill in the others."

**Step 3: Regrouping — same value, different look.**
Build 320 with 3 flats and 2 rods. "This is 320." Now take away 1 flat and replace it with 10 rods. "How many flats now?" (2) "How many rods?" (12) "Is it still 320? Let's count: 200 + 120 = 320. Yes! I traded 1 hundred for 10 tens. The number looks different, but the VALUE is the same."

Do it again: take 1 rod from the 12 rods and replace it with 10 unit cubes. "Now I have 2 flats, 11 rods, and 10 cubes. Still 320? 200 + 110 + 10 = 320. Yes!"

"This is called regrouping. You'll use it a LOT when you learn to add and subtract big numbers in Unit 5."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Start with word form: "four hundred seven."
- Standard form: 407
- Expanded form: 400 + 7 (or 400 + 0 + 7)
- Blocks: 4 flats, 0 rods, 7 cubes
- "Can I regroup? Sure — trade 1 flat for 10 rods: 3 flats, 10 rods, 7 cubes. Still 407."

**Example B:** Start with blocks: 5 flats, 1 rod, 3 cubes.
- Standard form: 513
- Expanded form: 500 + 10 + 3
- Word form: five hundred thirteen
- "What if I regroup 1 rod into 10 cubes? I get 5 flats, 0 rods, 13 cubes. Still 513."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student fills in all four representations and explores regrouping.

**Activity 1: Four-ways practice**
Give the student one representation. They fill in the other three.
- Start: Standard form 746. Student writes: expanded (700 + 40 + 6), word (seven hundred forty-six), draws blocks (7 flats, 4 rods, 6 cubes).
- Start: Word form "three hundred ninety." Student writes: standard (390), expanded (300 + 90), draws blocks (3 flats, 9 rods, 0 cubes).
- Start: Expanded form 100 + 60 + 2. Student writes: standard (162), word (one hundred sixty-two), draws blocks.

**Activity 2: Regrouping exploration**
- "Build 240 with blocks. Now trade 1 flat for 10 rods. What do you have?" (1 flat, 14 rods, 0 cubes) "Is it still 240?" (Yes — 100 + 140 = 240)
- "Build 315. Trade 1 rod for 10 cubes. What do you have?" (3 flats, 0 rods, 15 cubes) "Is it still 315?" (Yes — 300 + 0 + 15 = 315)
- "Why might we want to regroup?" (To have enough tens or ones to subtract — preview of Unit 5.)

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
1. Write 534 in expanded form and word form. (500 + 30 + 4; five hundred thirty-four)
2. Write the standard form: "two hundred fifteen." (215)
3. Draw base-ten blocks for 321. (3 flats, 2 rods, 1 cube)
4. 700 + 60 + 8 = ___ in standard form. (768)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
1. Fill in all four representations for 482. (482; 400 + 80 + 2; four hundred eighty-two; 4 flats, 8 rods, 2 cubes)
2. Fill in all four representations for 309. (309; 300 + 9; three hundred nine; 3 flats, 0 rods, 9 cubes)
3. "I have 2 flats and 15 rods. What number is this?" (2 hundreds + 15 tens = 200 + 150 = 350)
4. "Is 4 flats and 12 rods the same as 520?" (Yes — 400 + 120 = 520)
5. True or false: 600 + 40 + 3 = six hundred forty-three. (True)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
1. "Show 450 with blocks three different ways using regrouping." (e.g., 4 flats + 5 rods; 3 flats + 15 rods; 4 flats + 4 rods + 10 cubes)
2. "I have 1 flat, 23 rods, and 5 cubes. What is my number in standard form?" (1 hundred + 23 tens + 5 = 100 + 230 + 5 = 335)
3. "Write 700 in expanded form. How many different ways can you regroup 7 flats?" (e.g., 6 flats + 10 rods, 5 flats + 20 rods, etc.)
4. "A number in expanded form is ___ + 50 + 9. The number is 859. What is the missing part?" (800)
5. "Explain in your own words why 2 hundreds + 14 tens + 3 ones = 343." (Because 14 tens = 140, and 200 + 140 + 3 = 343.)

---

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. Write 627 in expanded form AND word form. (600 + 20 + 7; six hundred twenty-seven)
2. "I have 3 flats and 12 rods. What number is this?" (3 hundreds + 12 tens = 300 + 120 = 420)

**Mastery = Both correct. Student converts fluently between representations and understands that regrouping preserves value.**

## Teacher Notes

**This lesson is a synthesis.** Lessons 1-4 each introduced a building block: hundreds, place value chart, word form, expanded form. This lesson ties them together. The student should feel confident that they can show any three-digit number in multiple ways. If a student struggles, identify WHICH representation is the weak link and review that specific lesson.

**Regrouping is a preview, not a mastery goal.** The regrouping activities in this lesson are designed to plant a seed. Students do NOT need to master regrouping today. The goal is for them to see that trading 1 flat for 10 rods does not change the total — this "aha" moment will pay off enormously in Unit 5 when they add and subtract with regrouping.

**Use the "Four Ways" worksheet.** Having the four boxes side by side is powerful. The student can see at a glance that all four represent the same number. Over time, they begin to move fluidly between representations, which is a hallmark of number sense.

**Common errors:**
- Student confuses standard form and expanded form (writes 482 in the expanded form box). Remind them: "Standard form is the SHORT way. Expanded form is the LONG way with plus signs."
- Student draws the wrong number of blocks. Have them count their drawing: "How many flats did you draw? Each flat is 100. So that's ___. Does it match the number?"
- During regrouping, student changes the total. Always verify: "Let's count everything up. Is the total still the same?" If not, find where the trade went wrong.

**Facilitator language tip:** Use the phrase "same number, different look" repeatedly. This reinforces the core idea that representations are flexible, but the underlying value never changes.

**Looking ahead:** In Unit 5, students will add and subtract three-digit numbers with regrouping. The comfort with "trading" blocks built in this lesson — 1 flat for 10 rods, 1 rod for 10 cubes — directly supports the regrouping process in addition and subtraction algorithms.
