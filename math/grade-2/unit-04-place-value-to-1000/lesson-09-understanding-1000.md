# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 4, Lesson 9

## Topic
Understanding 1,000

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Know that 10 hundreds = 1,000
- Count by 100s from 100 to 1,000
- Understand 1,000 as a benchmark number
- Visualize the size of 1,000 (ten hundred-flats stacked)

## Prerequisites
- Understanding hundreds, tens, and ones places (Lessons 1-5)
- Skip counting by 100 from any number (Lesson 8)
- Comparing and ordering three-digit numbers (Lessons 6-7)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (at least 10 hundred-flats, plus tens rods and ones cubes)
- Paper and pencil
- A jar of small objects (beans, beads, or paperclips — about 1,000 if possible)
- Drawings or photographs showing groups of 1,000

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build anticipation and connect to what the student already knows about groups of 10.

1. "How many ones make a ten?" (10) Hold up a tens rod. "How many tens make a hundred?" (10) Hold up a hundreds flat. "Do you see the pattern? Every time we get 10 of something, we make the next bigger thing."
2. "So... how many hundreds do you think make the NEXT bigger thing?" (10!) "And what do we call 10 hundreds?" Let the student guess.
3. "Today we're going to discover the number ONE THOUSAND. It's a really big number — let's see just how big!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build understanding of 1,000 as 10 hundreds through concrete experience and visual models.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Thousand:** The number 1,000, equal to 10 hundreds
- **Benchmark number:** A "landmark" number that helps us estimate and compare (100, 500, 1,000)
- **Ones, tens, hundreds, thousands:** The places in our number system, each 10 times the one before

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Concrete — Stack the hundreds.**
"Let's count hundreds together. Every time I put down a hundred-flat, you tell me the total."

Place flats one at a time: "100... 200... 300... 400... 500... 600... 700... 800... 900..."

Place the 10th flat: "What comes next?" (1,000!)

"You just counted to one thousand! How many hundred-flats did we use?" (10) "So 10 hundreds = 1,000."

Stack all 10 flats on top of each other. "Look at this tower. This is what 1,000 looks like in base-ten blocks. It's called a thousands cube."

**Step 2: Concrete — Feel the size of 1,000.**
If you have a jar of about 1,000 small objects: "Let's see what 1,000 beans looks like." Pour them out. "That's a LOT of beans!"

If you don't have 1,000 objects, use estimation: "A page of a book has about 250 words. How many pages would it take to get to 1,000 words?" (About 4 pages.) "A chapter book might have 5,000 or 10,000 words!"

**Step 3: Representational — Chart the pattern.**
Write:

| Ones | Tens    | Hundreds | Thousands |
|------|---------|----------|-----------|
| 1    | 10      | 100      | 1,000     |
| 1 cube | 1 rod (10 cubes) | 1 flat (10 rods) | 1 big cube (10 flats) |

"See the pattern? Each place is 10 times the place before it. 10 ones = 1 ten. 10 tens = 1 hundred. 10 hundreds = 1 thousand."

**Step 4: Abstract — Writing 1,000.**
"1,000 has a 1 in the thousands place and zeros everywhere else. It means 1 thousand, 0 hundreds, 0 tens, 0 ones."

"We use a comma to help read big numbers: 1,000. The comma goes between the thousands and the hundreds."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** How many hundreds are in 1,000?
"We stacked 10 hundred-flats. 10 hundreds = 1,000."

**Example B:** Count by 100s to 1,000.
"100, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, 900, 1,000. That's 10 counts of 100."

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student explores 1,000 through counting, building, and estimating.

**Activity 1: Build toward 1,000**
Give the student hundred-flats one at a time.

"You have 7 hundred-flats. That's 700. How many more hundreds do you need to reach 1,000?" (3 more — 800, 900, 1,000.)

"You have 400. How many more hundreds to reach 1,000?" (6 more.)

"You have 900. How many more?" (Just 1 more!)

**Activity 2: Is it closer to 0 or 1,000?**
The facilitator names a number. The student decides if it's closer to 0 or closer to 1,000.

- 250: "Closer to 0 or 1,000?" (Closer to 0 — it's only 250 away from 0, but 750 away from 1,000.)
- 800: (Closer to 1,000 — only 200 away.)
- 500: "Right in the middle! It's 500 away from both."
- 650: (Closer to 1,000 — 350 away from 1,000, 650 away from 0.)

"500 is the halfway point to 1,000. Numbers less than 500 are closer to 0. Numbers greater than 500 are closer to 1,000."

---

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
Use base-ten blocks to help.
1. How many hundreds make 1,000? (10)
2. Count by 100s: 100, 200, ___, ___, 500, ___, ___, ___, ___, 1,000. (300, 400, 600, 700, 800, 900)
3. You have 600. How many more hundreds to get to 1,000? (4 more — 400 more)
4. Is 300 closer to 0 or closer to 1,000? (Closer to 0)
5. Is 900 closer to 0 or closer to 1,000? (Closer to 1,000)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
1. 10 hundreds = ___. (1,000)
2. ___ hundreds = 1,000. (10)
3. You have 850. How much more to reach 1,000? (150)
4. Count by 100s starting at 350: 350, ___, ___, ___, ___, ___, ___. (450, 550, 650, 750, 850, 950)
5. Is 480 closer to 0 or 1,000? How do you know? (Closer to 0; 480 < 500, and 500 is the halfway point.)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
1. How many tens are in 1,000? (100 tens — because 10 tens = 100, and 10 hundreds = 1,000, so 10 x 10 = 100 tens.)
2. How many ones are in 1,000? (1,000 ones.)
3. "A book has 200 pages. How many books would you need to have 1,000 pages?" (5 books.)
4. "What number is exactly halfway between 0 and 1,000?" (500.) "What number is exactly halfway between 500 and 1,000?" (750.)
5. "Start at 1,000. Count backwards by 100: 1,000, 900, ___... all the way to 0." (800, 700, 600, 500, 400, 300, 200, 100, 0)

### Interleaved Review
- "Skip count by 10 starting at 472: ___, ___, ___." (482, 492, 502) *(Lesson 8 review)*
- "Order from least to greatest: 908, 890, 809." (809, 890, 908) *(Lesson 7 review)*

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student understands that 10 hundreds = 1,000 and can reason about 1,000 as a benchmark.

1. How many hundreds are in 1,000? (10)
2. You have 700. How many more hundreds until you reach 1,000? (3 more hundreds, or 300 more)
3. Is 620 closer to 0 or closer to 1,000? (Closer to 1,000 — it's more than 500.)

**Mastery = All three correct. Student knows 10 hundreds = 1,000 and can reason about distance from 1,000.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to stacking hundred-flats. Count them one at a time to 1,000, emphasizing "10 hundreds = 1,000." Repeat this several times.
- For the benchmark question, draw a number line from 0 to 1,000 with a mark at 500. Place the number on the line to see which end it's closer to.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 10 (Place Value Problem Solving).

## Teacher Notes

**This lesson is about wonder as much as math.** 1,000 is a big number for a 7-year-old. Take time to let the student feel how big it is — stack the blocks, look at the jar of beans, imagine 1,000 steps. Building intuition for magnitude is just as important as knowing the fact "10 hundreds = 1,000."

**The powers-of-ten pattern is foundational.** 10 ones = 1 ten. 10 tens = 1 hundred. 10 hundreds = 1 thousand. This "ten of these makes one of those" pattern is the backbone of our entire number system. Plant this seed now — it will grow throughout the student's math life.

**The comma in 1,000.** This is many students' first encounter with commas in numbers. Explain simply: "When numbers get big, we use a comma to help us read them. The comma separates the thousands from the hundreds."

**Common errors:**
- Thinking 1,000 comes right after 100. The student may not realize there are 900 numbers between 100 and 1,000. The hundred-flat stacking activity helps build this sense of distance.
- Confusing "how many hundreds to 1,000" with "what number plus mine equals 1,000." They're the same question stated differently — but "how many hundreds" asks for a count (3), while "how much more" asks for an amount (300). Practice both phrasings.
- Writing 1,000 as 1000 or 100. Show the comma and the four digits clearly.

**Real-world connections:** "About 1,000 steps from our house to the park." "This bag of rice has about 1,000 grains in one cup." "There are about 1,000 students in a big school." These comparisons help the number feel real.
