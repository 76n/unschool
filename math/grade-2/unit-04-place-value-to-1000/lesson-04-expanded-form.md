# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 4, Lesson 4

## Topic
Expanded Form

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Write a three-digit number in expanded form (e.g., 347 = 300 + 40 + 7)
- Convert from expanded form back to standard form
- Connect expanded form to the base-ten block representation
- Explain that expanded form shows the VALUE of each digit

## Prerequisites
- Identifying hundreds, tens, and ones and their values (Lesson 2)
- Reading and writing three-digit numbers (Lesson 3)
- Understanding of addition with multi-digit sums (conceptual, not algorithmic)

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (flats, rods, unit cubes)
- Place value chart (H | T | O)
- Paper and pencil
- Index cards or sticky notes for writing values

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review digit values to prepare for expanded form.

1. Write 462 on the board. "What is the value of the 4?" (400) "The 6?" (60) "The 2?" (2)
2. "If I add those values together — 400 + 60 + 2 — what do I get?" (462) "Interesting! The values ADD UP to the number."
3. "Today we'll learn a special way to write numbers that shows every digit's value. It's called expanded form."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach expanded form as a way to break a number into the values of its digits.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Standard form:** The usual way we write a number (462)
- **Expanded form:** A number written as the sum of the values of its digits (400 + 60 + 2)
- **Value:** How much a digit is worth based on its place

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: From blocks to expanded form.**
Build 385 with blocks: 3 flats, 8 rods, 5 cubes. Separate the blocks into three groups. Place a sticky note under each group. Write "300" under the flats, "80" under the rods, "5" under the cubes. "Each group has a value. 300 + 80 + 5. This is expanded form — we EXPAND the number to show each part."

**Step 2: From standard form to expanded form.**
Write 729 on the board. "Let's expand it. The 7 is in the hundreds place — its value is 700. The 2 is in the tens place — its value is 20. The 9 is in the ones place — its value is 9. So 729 = 700 + 20 + 9." Write it out.

**Step 3: Numbers with zero.**
Write 506 on the board. "The 5 is worth 500. The 0 is worth ... 0! The 6 is worth 6. So 506 = 500 + 0 + 6. We can also just write 500 + 6, since adding zero doesn't change anything. But it's fine to include the 0 to remember the tens place."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 253 = ?
"2 hundreds = 200. 5 tens = 50. 3 ones = 3."
Expanded form: 200 + 50 + 3.
Check: 200 + 50 + 3 = 253. Correct!

**Example B:** 400 + 10 + 8 = ?
"400 is 4 hundreds. 10 is 1 ten. 8 is 8 ones. Put the digits together: 4, 1, 8."
Standard form: 418.
Build 418 with blocks to verify: 4 flats, 1 rod, 8 cubes.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student converts between standard form and expanded form using blocks for support.

**Activity 1: Standard to expanded**
- "Write 647 in expanded form." Student writes: 600 + 40 + 7. "Build it with blocks to check."
- "Write 390 in expanded form." (300 + 90 + 0, or 300 + 90) "How many ones blocks?" (0)
- "Write 208 in expanded form." (200 + 0 + 8, or 200 + 8) "Why is there no tens part?" (There are 0 tens.)

**Activity 2: Expanded to standard**
- "300 + 50 + 2 = ?" Student writes 352. "What digit goes in the hundreds place?" (3)
- "100 + 70 + 6 = ?" (176) "Build it to check."
- "800 + 4 = ?" (804) "Careful — where are the tens?" (There are no tens, so the tens digit is 0.)

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
1. Write 135 in expanded form. (100 + 30 + 5)
2. Write 260 in expanded form. (200 + 60 + 0, or 200 + 60)
3. 400 + 20 + 9 = ___ (429)
4. 500 + 30 + 1 = ___ (531)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
1. Write 847 in expanded form. (800 + 40 + 7)
2. Write 502 in expanded form. (500 + 2)
3. 600 + 70 + 3 = ___ (673)
4. 900 + 5 = ___ (905)
5. Match: Which number equals 300 + 10 + 6? Choices: 316, 361, 136. (316)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
1. Write 999 in expanded form. (900 + 90 + 9)
2. "Is 400 + 50 + 12 a correct expanded form? Why or why not?" (Not standard expanded form — 12 ones should be regrouped as 1 ten and 2 ones, making the number 462, which is 400 + 60 + 2.)
3. "Write two different addition expressions that equal 350." (e.g., 300 + 50 and 200 + 150 — accept any valid decomposition)
4. "A number in expanded form is 700 + ___ + 3. The number is 743. What is the missing part?" (40)
5. "The expanded form of a number is 200 + 80 + 6. Write it in standard form AND word form." (286; two hundred eighty-six)

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)

1. Write 573 in expanded form. (500 + 70 + 3)
2. 800 + 20 + 1 = ___ in standard form. (821)
3. Write 409 in expanded form. (400 + 9, or 400 + 0 + 9)

**Mastery = All three correct, including the zero-tens case. Student shows that expanded form reveals the value of each digit.**

## Teacher Notes

**Expanded form makes the invisible visible.** When students write 347, the values 300, 40, and 7 are hidden inside the compact notation. Expanded form pulls them apart. This is why it matters: it builds the foundation for addition and subtraction with regrouping, because students who understand 347 = 300 + 40 + 7 can later reason about "borrowing" from the hundreds.

**Blocks first, always.** For each new number, have the student build it with blocks, physically separate the flats, rods, and cubes, and THEN write the expanded form. The physical separation is the concrete anchor for the abstract notation.

**Common errors:**
- Student writes 347 as 3 + 4 + 7 instead of 300 + 40 + 7. Redirect: "The 3 is not just 3. It's in the hundreds place. What is it worth?" Build with blocks to reinforce.
- Student writes 506 as 500 + 60 instead of 500 + 6 (confuses which place the zero is in). Point to the place value chart: "The 0 is in the tens column. The 6 is in the ones column."
- Student forgets the plus signs: writes "300 40 7" instead of "300 + 40 + 7." Remind them expanded form is an addition sentence.

**Connection to prior lessons:** Explicitly link expanded form to the place value chart from Lesson 2: "The chart shows 3 in the H column, 4 in the T column, 7 in the O column. Expanded form shows the VALUES: 300 + 40 + 7. Both tell us the same thing in different ways."

**Facilitator language tip:** Say "expand" with a hand gesture — spread your hands apart as if stretching something. This kinesthetic cue helps 7-year-olds remember that expanded form "stretches out" a number to show its parts.
