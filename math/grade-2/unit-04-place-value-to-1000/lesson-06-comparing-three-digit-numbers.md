# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 4, Lesson 6

## Topic
Comparing Three-Digit Numbers

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Compare two three-digit numbers using >, <, and =
- Use the place-by-place comparison strategy (hundreds first, then tens, then ones)
- Use base-ten blocks to verify comparisons
- Understand the "open end faces the bigger number" mnemonic for > and <

## Prerequisites
- Understanding hundreds, tens, and ones places (Lessons 1-5)
- Building and reading three-digit numbers with base-ten blocks
- Writing three-digit numbers in standard, expanded, and word form

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (hundreds flats, tens rods, ones cubes)
- Paper and pencil
- Comparison symbol cards (>, <, =)
- Number cards or sticky notes

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review place value and activate prior knowledge of comparison.

1. "Quick review: What number has 4 hundreds, 7 tens, and 2 ones?" (472) "What about 4 hundreds, 2 tens, and 7 ones?" (427) "Are those the same number?" (No!)
2. "Which is bigger: 50 or 30? How do you know?" (50, because 5 tens is more than 3 tens.) "You already know how to compare — today we're going to compare BIG numbers."
3. "Remember the symbols > and <? Show me which way the mouth opens for 'greater than.'" Review: the open end always faces the bigger number.

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the place-by-place comparison strategy using concrete models first.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Compare:** To decide which number is greater, which is less, or if they are equal
- **Greater than (>):** The first number is bigger (open end faces the bigger number)
- **Less than (<):** The first number is smaller (pointed end faces the smaller number)
- **Equal to (=):** Both numbers have the same value

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Concrete — Build and compare with blocks.**
Build 352 with base-ten blocks: 3 flats, 5 rods, 2 cubes. Build 317 next to it: 3 flats, 1 rod, 7 cubes.

"Let's compare these two numbers. Where do we start? The BIGGEST place — hundreds."

"How many hundreds in 352?" (3) "How many hundreds in 317?" (3) "They're the same! So we move to the next place — tens."

"How many tens in 352?" (5) "How many tens in 317?" (1) "5 tens is more than 1 ten. We can stop right here! 352 is greater than 317."

Write: 352 > 317.

**Step 2: Representational — Draw quick pictures.**
"Let's try another pair: 248 and 261."

Draw quick pictures (squares for hundreds, lines for tens, dots for ones). Compare hundreds: both have 2 — same. Compare tens: 4 tens vs. 6 tens. 4 < 6, so 248 < 261.

"Notice: we didn't even need to look at the ones! Once we find a place where the digits are different, we know the answer."

**Step 3: Abstract — Digits only.**
"Now just use the digits: 589 and 524."

"Hundreds: both 5. Same. Tens: 8 vs. 2. 8 > 2. Done. 589 > 524."

"What if the hundreds AND tens are the same? Like 436 and 431?"

"Hundreds: both 4. Same. Tens: both 3. Same. Ones: 6 vs. 1. 6 > 1. So 436 > 431."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Compare 673 and 698.
- Hundreds: 6 = 6. Move on.
- Tens: 7 vs. 9. 7 < 9.
- 673 < 698. "The open end of < faces 698 because 698 is the bigger number."

**Example B:** Compare 450 and 450.
- Hundreds: 4 = 4. Tens: 5 = 5. Ones: 0 = 0.
- Every place is the same! 450 = 450.
- "When all the digits match, the numbers are equal."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices the comparison strategy with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Build-and-Compare**
The facilitator names two numbers. The student builds both with base-ten blocks, then compares place by place, speaking the strategy aloud.
- Compare 215 and 251. (215 < 251 — tens place: 1 < 5)
- Compare 389 and 382. (389 > 382 — ones place: 9 > 2)
- Compare 500 and 500. (500 = 500 — all places match)

"Each time, say it like this: 'First I check hundreds. Then I check tens. Then I check ones.'"

**Activity 2: Fill in the symbol**
The facilitator writes pairs on paper. The student writes >, <, or = between them.
- 741 ___ 714 (>)
- 305 ___ 350 (< )
- 628 ___ 628 (=)
- 199 ___ 200 (< )

"For that last one — 199 and 200 — which hundreds digit is bigger?" (2 > 1, so 200 is greater.)

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
Use base-ten blocks to help. Write >, <, or =.
1. 342 ___ 324 (>)
2. 156 ___ 165 (<)
3. 400 ___ 400 (=)
4. 210 ___ 201 (>)
5. 99 ___ 100 (<)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
Compare without blocks. Write >, <, or =.
1. 573 ___ 537 (>)
2. 489 ___ 498 (<)
3. 650 ___ 605 (>)
4. 811 ___ 811 (=)
5. 399 ___ 400 (<)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
1. 762 ___ 726. Explain how you know using place value language. (762 > 726; hundreds are the same, but 6 tens > 2 tens)
2. Fill in a digit to make this true: 4_5 > 435. What digits could go in the blank? (4, 5, 6, 7, 8, or 9 — any digit that makes the tens place at least 4, but greater: so 4 gives 445 > 435 yes, so 4 through 9)
3. True or false: "The number with the biggest ones digit is always the greatest number." (False — 109 has a bigger ones digit than 920, but 920 is greater.)
4. Write three different true statements using >, <, or = with the numbers 583, 538, and 583. (583 > 538, 538 < 583, 583 = 583)

### Interleaved Review
- "What is 347 in expanded form?" (300 + 40 + 7) *(Lesson 3 review)*
- "15 + 8 = ___" (23) *(Unit 1 fact fluency)*

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can compare three-digit numbers using place value.

1. Write >, <, or =: 682 ___ 628. (>)
2. Write >, <, or =: 345 ___ 345. (=)
3. Which place value helped you decide in question 1? (The tens place: 8 > 2.)

**Mastery = All three correct. Student can articulate which place decided the comparison.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to base-ten blocks. Build both numbers side by side and compare place by place physically. Say aloud: "I check hundreds first. If they match, I check tens. If they match, I check ones."
- Practice with numbers that differ only in the hundreds place first (e.g., 300 vs. 500) before moving to numbers that differ in tens or ones.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (Ordering Numbers to 1,000).

## Teacher Notes

**The place-by-place strategy is the core skill.** Make sure the student always starts at the hundreds place and works right. This left-to-right habit mirrors how we read numbers and will carry into multi-digit comparison in later grades.

**"Open end faces the bigger number" is the simplest mnemonic.** Some students prefer "the alligator eats the bigger number." Either works — pick one and use it consistently.

**Common errors:**
- Looking at ones first: A student might say 352 < 317 because 2 < 7. Redirect to hundreds, then tens, then ones.
- Confusing > and <: If the student mixes up the symbols, practice with physical cards — the wide-open side always faces the bigger number.
- Numbers with different digit counts (e.g., 99 vs. 100): Remind the student that a three-digit number is always greater than a two-digit number.

**Connection to real life:** "If one toy costs 215 cents and another costs 251 cents, which costs more?" Using money or measurement contexts makes comparison feel purposeful.
