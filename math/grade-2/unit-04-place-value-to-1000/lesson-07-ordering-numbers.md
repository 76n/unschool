# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 4, Lesson 7

## Topic
Ordering Numbers to 1,000

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Order 3-4 three-digit numbers from least to greatest
- Order 3-4 three-digit numbers from greatest to least
- Place three-digit numbers on a number line
- Use the comparison strategy systematically to sort a set of numbers

## Prerequisites
- Comparing two three-digit numbers using >, <, and = (Lesson 6)
- Understanding hundreds, tens, and ones places (Lessons 1-5)
- Reading and writing three-digit numbers

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (hundreds flats, tens rods, ones cubes)
- Paper and pencil
- Number cards (index cards or sticky notes)
- Drawn number line (on paper or whiteboard)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review comparing two numbers to prepare for ordering multiple numbers.

1. "Quick comparisons — tell me >, <, or =." 463 ___ 436 (>). 285 ___ 290 (<). 500 ___ 500 (=).
2. "How did you decide for 463 and 436?" (Hundreds are the same; tens: 6 > 3, so 463 is greater.)
3. "Today we're going to put THREE or FOUR numbers in order — from smallest to biggest, and from biggest to smallest."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach a systematic strategy for ordering multiple three-digit numbers.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Order:** To arrange numbers from smallest to largest, or largest to smallest
- **Least to greatest:** Starting with the smallest number and ending with the largest
- **Greatest to least:** Starting with the largest number and ending with the smallest
- **Number line:** A line with numbers placed in order, spaced by their value

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Concrete — Build and line up.**
"Let's put these numbers in order from least to greatest: 384, 217, 492."

Build each number with base-ten blocks. "First, let's compare the hundreds. 217 has 2 hundreds, 384 has 3 hundreds, 492 has 4 hundreds. The hundreds are all different! So we can order them just by looking at hundreds."

Arrange the block models left to right: 217, 384, 492.

"Least to greatest: 217, 384, 492."

**Step 2: Representational — Use a number line.**
Draw a number line from 200 to 500 with marks at each hundred.

"Let's place those same numbers. 217 is just past 200. 384 is between 300 and 400, closer to 400. 492 is very close to 500."

Plot the numbers. "See how they go left to right, smallest to largest? A number line always shows order."

**Step 3: Abstract — Digit strategy with same hundreds.**
"Now try: 537, 581, 519. These ALL start with 5 hundreds! So we look at tens."

"519 has 1 ten, 537 has 3 tens, 581 has 8 tens. So: 519, 537, 581."

"What if two numbers have the same hundreds AND tens? Like 346, 341, 372?"

"346 and 341 both have 3 hundreds and 4 tens. Check ones: 1 < 6, so 341 < 346. 372 has 7 tens, which is more than 4 tens, so 372 is the greatest."

"Order: 341, 346, 372."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** Order from least to greatest: 650, 298, 650, 415.
- Compare hundreds: 2, 6, 6, 4.
- Smallest hundreds: 298 (2 hundreds) comes first.
- Next: 415 (4 hundreds).
- 650 and 650: same hundreds, same tens, same ones — they're equal, so they go next to each other.
- Answer: 298, 415, 650, 650.

**Example B:** Order from greatest to least: 730, 703, 738.
- Hundreds: all 7. Same! Check tens: 3, 0, 3.
- 703 has 0 tens — that's the smallest. But we want greatest first.
- 730 and 738 both have 3 tens. Check ones: 0 vs. 8. 738 > 730.
- Greatest to least: 738, 730, 703.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices ordering numbers with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Card Sort**
Write each number on a separate card. The student physically arranges the cards in order.

- Order from least to greatest: 155, 510, 351. (155, 351, 510)
  "How did you decide?" (Hundreds: 1, 5, 3 — so 155 first, then 351, then 510.)

- Order from greatest to least: 482, 428, 489. (489, 482, 428)
  "All have 4 hundreds. So you checked tens: 8, 2, 8. Then 482 and 489 — check ones: 9 > 2."

**Activity 2: Number Line Placement**
Draw a number line from 300 to 400 with marks at 310, 320, 330... 390.

"Place these numbers on the number line: 375, 318, 342."

Student marks approximate positions. "Now read them left to right — that's least to greatest." (318, 342, 375)

"Read right to left — that's greatest to least." (375, 342, 318)

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 (Approaching)
Use base-ten blocks or draw quick pictures to help.
1. Order from least to greatest: 125, 312, 210. (125, 210, 312)
2. Order from greatest to least: 500, 300, 700. (700, 500, 300)
3. Order from least to greatest: 441, 414, 444. (414, 441, 444)
4. Order from greatest to least: 268, 862, 628. (862, 628, 268)

### Tier 2 (On Level)
Order without blocks.
1. Least to greatest: 593, 395, 539. (395, 539, 593)
2. Greatest to least: 847, 874, 871. (874, 871, 847)
3. Least to greatest: 606, 660, 600. (600, 606, 660)
4. Greatest to least: 215, 251, 225, 205. (251, 225, 215, 205)
5. Place on a number line from 100 to 200: 145, 120, 178. (120, 145, 178 from left to right)

### Tier 3 (Advanced)
1. Order from least to greatest: 499, 500, 501, 489. (489, 499, 500, 501)
2. "I am thinking of a number between 340 and 360 with a 7 in the ones place." What is the number? (347 or 357) Order those two from least to greatest. (347, 357)
3. Write four three-digit numbers that all have 6 hundreds, then order them from greatest to least. (Answers vary — e.g., 691, 672, 638, 604 becomes 691, 672, 638, 604)
4. True or false: "When ordering numbers, you only ever need to look at the hundreds place." Explain. (False — if the hundreds are the same, you must check tens, and sometimes ones.)

### Interleaved Review
- "Write >, <, or =: 730 ___ 703." (>) *(Lesson 6 review)*
- "What is the value of the 5 in 253?" (50) *(Lesson 2 review)*

## Exit Ticket (2-3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can order three-digit numbers.

1. Order from least to greatest: 672, 627, 726. (627, 672, 726)
2. Order from greatest to least: 555, 550, 505. (555, 550, 505)
3. "How did you decide the order in question 2?" (All have 5 hundreds. 555 and 550 have 5 tens but 555 has more ones. 505 has 0 tens, so it's the smallest.)

**Mastery = Both orderings correct. Student can explain the strategy.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Reduce to two numbers at a time. Have the student compare just two, decide which is greater, then introduce a third number and figure out where it fits. Build up to four numbers gradually.
- Return to base-ten blocks to make the size of each number visible and physical.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 8 (Skip Counting by 10s and 100s).

## Teacher Notes

**Ordering is comparing, repeated.** The student already knows how to compare two numbers. Ordering just means doing that comparison multiple times. If the student struggles, break it down: "Which of these is the smallest? Pull it out. Now which of the remaining is smallest? Pull it out." This "selection sort" approach is intuitive.

**Number lines build number sense.** Placing numbers on a number line forces the student to think about relative magnitude, not just digit comparison. Even a rough sketch helps — precision isn't the goal; relative position is.

**Greatest to least is harder.** Most students find "least to greatest" more natural. For greatest to least, one strategy is to find the order least to greatest first, then reverse it.

**Common errors:**
- Reversing the order (writing greatest to least when asked for least to greatest). Circle the direction words in the problem.
- Forgetting to check tens and ones when hundreds are the same. Remind: "Same hundreds? Zoom into tens. Same tens? Zoom into ones."
- Spacing on a number line: Students may space numbers evenly regardless of value. Model how 301 and 399 are far apart, while 301 and 305 are close together.
