# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 4, Lesson 3

## Topic
Reading and Writing Three-Digit Numbers

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read three-digit numbers aloud correctly (e.g., "three hundred forty-seven")
- Write three-digit numbers in standard form from spoken names
- Write numbers in word form (e.g., 347 = "three hundred forty-seven")
- Handle numbers with zero in the tens or ones place (e.g., 305, 420)

## Prerequisites
- Identifying hundreds, tens, and ones digits (Lesson 2)
- Using a place value chart (H | T | O)
- Reading and writing two-digit numbers in word form

## Materials Needed
- Base-ten blocks (flats, rods, unit cubes)
- Place value chart
- Word form reference card (list of number words: one through nine, ten through nineteen, twenty through ninety)
- Paper and pencil
- Dry-erase board or whiteboard

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review reading two-digit numbers and bridge to three-digit reading.

1. Write 54 on the board. "Read this number." (fifty-four) "Good. Fifty means 5 tens. Four means 4 ones."
2. Write 80 on the board. "Read this." (eighty) "Notice we don't say 'eighty-zero.' When there are zero ones, we just say 'eighty.'"
3. "Today we'll learn to read and write numbers that have THREE digits — numbers in the hundreds."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the pattern for reading and writing three-digit numbers in words.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Standard form:** A number written with digits (347)
- **Word form:** A number written with words (three hundred forty-seven)
- **Hyphen:** The dash between compound number words (forty-seven, not forty seven)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: The reading pattern.**
Write 347 on the board. Build it with blocks: 3 flats, 4 rods, 7 cubes. "Here is how we read this number. First, we say the hundreds: 'three hundred.' Then we say the tens and ones together, just like a two-digit number: 'forty-seven.' Put it all together: 'three hundred forty-seven.'"

Write the pattern on the board:
- Say the hundreds digit + "hundred"
- Then say the two-digit number formed by the tens and ones

**Step 2: Numbers with a zero in the tens place.**
Write 305 on the board. Build it: 3 flats, 0 rods, 5 cubes. "There are zero tens. We do NOT say 'three hundred zero five' or 'three hundred and five.' We say: 'three hundred five.' We skip the tens word entirely because there are no tens."

**Step 3: Numbers with a zero in the ones place.**
Write 420 on the board. Build it: 4 flats, 2 rods, 0 cubes. "There are zero ones. We say: 'four hundred twenty.' We don't say 'twenty-zero.' Just stop at twenty."

### Worked Examples

**Example A:** 586. "Say the hundreds: 'five hundred.' The last two digits are 86, which is 'eighty-six.' So we read: 'five hundred eighty-six.'" Write: five hundred eighty-six.

**Example B:** 710. "Say the hundreds: 'seven hundred.' The last two digits are 10, which is 'ten.' We read: 'seven hundred ten.'" Write: seven hundred ten. "Notice — no ones to say."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices reading numbers aloud and writing them in word form.

**Activity 1: Read aloud**
Show these numbers one at a time. Student reads each aloud.
- 263 ("two hundred sixty-three")
- 408 ("four hundred eight")
- 550 ("five hundred fifty")
- 911 ("nine hundred eleven")
- 100 ("one hundred")
After each, ask: "How did you know what to say for the tens and ones part?"

**Activity 2: Write in standard form from spoken name**
Say each number aloud. Student writes it in digits.
- "Six hundred thirty-two" (632)
- "Two hundred nine" (209)
- "Eight hundred seventy" (870)
- "One hundred fourteen" (114)
For any errors, build the number with blocks and fill in the place value chart together.

## Teacher Notes

**Read before write.** Let the student read numbers aloud many times before asking them to write word form. Hearing the pattern ("___ hundred ___") builds the mental model that makes writing easier.

**The "teens" are tricky.** Numbers like 113, 215, and 718 require the student to know the teen words (thirteen, fifteen, eighteen). If the student stumbles, briefly review: 11 = eleven, 12 = twelve, 13 = thirteen, 14 = fourteen, 15 = fifteen, 16 = sixteen, 17 = seventeen, 18 = eighteen, 19 = nineteen.

**Hyphen rule.** Numbers from twenty-one through ninety-nine use a hyphen when both the tens and ones digits are nonzero: forty-seven, sixty-three. Numbers like "twenty" or "fifty" do NOT get a hyphen. This is a spelling convention worth pointing out but should not become a major focus — reading comprehension matters more than perfect spelling at this stage.

**Common errors:**
- Student says "three hundred AND forty-seven." In everyday English this is common, but in math class we drop the "and." Gently model: "In math we say 'three hundred forty-seven' — no 'and.'"
- Student writes "305" when they hear "three hundred fifty." This means they heard the 5 but put it in the ones place. Build 305 and 350 side by side with blocks and compare.
- Student freezes on numbers with zero in the tens place (e.g., 208). Practice these specifically: "two hundred eight." Build with blocks to show 2 flats, 0 rods, 8 cubes.

**Facilitator language tip:** When a student reads a number incorrectly, do not just say "no." Instead, build the number with blocks and walk through it: "How many hundreds? How many tens? How many ones? Now let's say it together."
