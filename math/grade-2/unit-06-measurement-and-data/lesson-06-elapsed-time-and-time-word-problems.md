# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 6, Lesson 6

## Topic
Elapsed Time and Time Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Determine the end time when given a start time and elapsed time
- Determine elapsed time when given start and end times (in 5-minute intervals)
- Solve simple time word problems by counting forward on a clock or number line
- Use A.M. and P.M. appropriately in context

## Prerequisites
- Telling time to 5 minutes on an analog clock (Lesson 5)
- Skip counting by 5s (Unit 3)
- Addition within 60 (Unit 1)

## Materials Needed
- Student analog practice clocks (one per student)
- Large demonstration clock
- "Time Number Line" strip (marked 0 to 60 in increments of 5)
- Clock-face recording sheets
- Mini whiteboards
- A daily schedule posted on the board (e.g., Math starts at 9:00, ends at 9:45)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review time-reading and connect to the idea of time passing.

**Before and After:**
Display the demonstration clock at 4:00. Ask: "What time is this?" (4:00)
Move the minute hand to show 4:20. "What time is it now?" (4:20) "How many minutes passed?" (20 minutes — count by 5s: 5, 10, 15, 20)

Move to 4:45. "How many minutes have passed since 4:00?" (45 minutes — count by 5s around the clock)

Ask: "How can we figure out how many minutes have passed?" Allow responses. Transition: "Today we'll learn how to find how much time passes — and figure out what time something ends."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce elapsed time using the clock and a time number line as pictorial supports.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Elapsed time:** How much time passes from a start time to an end time
- **Start time:** The time something begins
- **End time:** The time something finishes
- **Count forward:** Moving ahead in time to find a later time

### Instruction Notes

**Concrete phase:**
Students set their clocks to 2:00. "Art class starts at 2:00 and lasts 30 minutes. What time does it end?" Count together: move the minute hand in 5-minute jumps — 2:05, 2:10, 2:15, 2:20, 2:25, 2:30. "Art ends at 2:30."

**Pictorial phase:**
Introduce the Time Number Line:
Draw: 0 — 5 — 10 — 15 — 20 — 25 — 30 — 35 — 40 — 45 — 50 — 55 — 60

Model: "We start at 10:00. Jump forward 25 minutes." Draw hops of 5 on the number line, counting: +5, +10, +15, +20, +25 → end at :25. "The new time is 10:25."

Finding elapsed time:
"Library starts at 1:15 and ends at 1:45. How long is library?" On the time number line, mark :15 and :45. Count the hops between them: 30 minutes.

**Abstract phase:**
Write: Start time: 3:10. Elapsed: 20 minutes. End time: 3:10 + 20 = 3:30.

Model thinking: "I add the minutes. 10 + 20 = 30. So the end time is 3:30."

What if minutes go over 60? Use the clock as the reference — students are not expected to handle hour rollovers in this lesson (save for stretch problems).

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:**
Recess starts at 10:30 and lasts 15 minutes. What time does recess end?
10:30 → count by 5s → 10:35 → 10:40 → 10:45.
Answer: Recess ends at 10:45.

**Example 2:**
Math starts at 9:00 and ends at 9:40. How long is math class?
Count from :00 to :40: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40 → 40 minutes.
Answer: Math class is 40 minutes long.

**Example 3:**
Lunch starts at 11:15 and is 30 minutes long. When does lunch end?
11:15 + 30 minutes: count forward → 11:45.
Answer: Lunch ends at 11:45.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Use student clocks and mini whiteboards together.

**Problem 1:** PE starts at 2:00 and lasts 25 minutes. Set your clock to 2:00, then count forward 25 minutes. Write the end time: ___

**Problem 2:** A movie starts at 6:10 and ends at 6:50. How long is the movie?
Count the minutes from 6:10 to 6:50: ___ minutes.

**Problem 3:** A nap begins at 1:30 and lasts 20 minutes. What time does the child wake up?
Start time + elapsed time = end time: ___

**Problem 4:** School ends at 3:15. Reading club meets for 35 minutes after school. What time does reading club end?
Show on your clock: ___

---

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Story time starts at 4:00 and lasts 20 minutes. What time does it end? ___
2. Cooking class starts at 3:30 and ends at 4:00. How long is cooking class? ___ minutes
3. A bath starts at 7:15 and lasts 15 minutes. What time does it end? ___
4. Draw the start time and end time on clock faces:
   - A game starts at 5:00 and lasts 40 minutes. Start: ___ End: ___

**Stretch:**
5. Sofia arrives at the park at 2:10 P.M. She leaves at 2:55 P.M. How long did she stay at the park? Show on a time number line.
6. A bus ride starts at 7:30 A.M. and lasts 25 minutes. What time does the bus arrive? Is that A.M. or P.M.?

**Challenge:**
7. Dad starts cooking at 5:15 P.M. The food takes 45 minutes to cook. What time is dinner ready? (Hint: count past :60 to the next hour.) Draw a clock to show the dinner time.
8. Sam has 3 activities today: swimming (30 min), homework (25 min), reading (15 min). If he starts at 3:00 P.M., what time does he finish all three activities?

### Interleaved Review
R1. 35 + 45 = ___ (Unit 1 — addition within 100)
R2. What is 5 × 3 using skip counting? (Unit 3 — multiplication introduction)
R3. 500 − 200 = ___ (Unit 4 — place value subtraction)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Soccer practice starts at 4:15 and lasts 30 minutes. What time does it end? ___
2. [Two clock faces: first shows 7:20, second shows 7:50] How much time passed from the first clock to the second? ___ minutes

**Mastery = both correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to concrete clock practice: set start time, physically count each 5-minute jump.
- Use the time number line as a persistent scaffold.
- Limit problems to round starting times (on the hour or half hour) initially.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Try Challenge problem 7 or 8.
- Ask: "What if something starts at 11:40 and lasts 30 minutes? What tricky thing happens?" (rolls past 12:00) Discuss without requiring mastery.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**My Day Timeline:** Create a timeline of your typical school day. Show at least 6 events with start and end times. For each event, calculate how long it lasts. Draw analog clocks for at least 3 events.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Keep the time number line (0–60 in 5s) visible throughout. It is a key bridge between the clock and abstract addition.
- The challenge problems introduce "crossing the hour" (e.g., 5:15 + 45 min = 6:00). This is appropriate for stretch learners in Grade 2 but should not be required for all students. Acknowledge the complexity and return to it in Grade 3.
- A.M. and P.M. should be used in context throughout the lesson. Ask questions like "Is 7:30 in the morning or evening? How do you know? What label does that get?"
- Daily Schedule anchor: keep the classroom daily schedule posted and reference it. "Look at our schedule — we started math at 9:00. What time did we finish? How long was math today?"
