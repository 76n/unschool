# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 6, Lesson 4

## Topic
Solving Word Problems Involving Length

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve addition and subtraction word problems using lengths
- Represent length problems on a number line
- Write and solve equations to find unknown lengths
- Use measurement context to choose appropriate operations

## Prerequisites
- Measuring in inches and centimeters (Lessons 1–2)
- Comparing lengths and finding differences (Lesson 3)
- Addition and subtraction within 100 (Unit 1)
- Number lines (Unit 1 context)

## Materials Needed
- Rulers (inches and centimeters)
- Number line strips (0–100, one per student, or drawn in journals)
- Mini whiteboards
- Word problem cards (prepared in advance — 6 problems of increasing complexity)
- Math journals

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review comparison subtraction and warm up story problem thinking.

**Story Start:**
Read aloud: "Mia has a ribbon that is 12 inches long. She cuts off 5 inches to wrap a gift. How much ribbon does she have left?"

Ask students to write the equation on their whiteboard before solving. Take responses. Confirm: 12 − 5 = 7 inches.

Then ask: "What did I do to solve it? Did I add or subtract? How did you know?"

Transition: "Today we'll solve more measurement word problems — some where we add, some where we subtract. We'll use number lines to help."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model using number lines and equations to solve length word problems; address the three basic problem types (add to, take from, compare).

### Key Vocabulary
- **Word problem:** A story that uses math; you decide the operation and solve
- **Number line:** A line with numbers in order; useful for showing addition and subtraction with lengths
- **Unknown:** The number we are trying to find (sometimes shown as ___ or a box)
- **Equation:** A math sentence with an equals sign showing two amounts are the same

### Instruction Notes

**Concrete phase:**
Use actual ribbon or yarn strips. Act out a word problem physically: "Here is a 10-inch piece. We cut 3 inches off. What is left?" Cut the yarn, then measure.

**Pictorial phase:**
Draw a number line on the board (0–20). Model jumping:
- Addition: start at 7, jump 5 forward → land on 12
- Subtraction: start at 15, jump 6 backward → land on 9

**Abstract phase:**
Show three problem structures and matching equations:
1. **Add To (Result Unknown):** "The path is 14 cm. The trail adds another 8 cm. How long is the whole path?" → 14 + 8 = ___
2. **Take From (Result Unknown):** "A string is 20 cm. 7 cm is cut off. How long is it now?" → 20 − 7 = ___
3. **Compare (Difference Unknown):** "Wall A is 35 cm wide. Wall B is 22 cm wide. How much wider is Wall A?" → 35 − 22 = ___

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:**
"A caterpillar crawled 9 inches Monday and 6 inches Tuesday. How far did it crawl in total?"
Number line: Start at 9, jump 6 → 15.
Equation: 9 + 6 = 15 inches.

**Example 2:**
"A rope is 25 cm long. Sam cuts off 8 cm. How long is the rope now?"
Number line: Start at 25, jump back 8 → 17.
Equation: 25 − 8 = 17 cm.

**Example 3 (Two steps):**
"A shelf is 30 inches wide. A book is 8 inches wide, and a plant pot is 6 inches wide. How much space is left on the shelf?"
Step 1: 8 + 6 = 14 inches used
Step 2: 30 − 14 = 16 inches remaining.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through the following problems together. Students use number line strips on their desks. Facilitator circulates.

**Problem 1:** A garden path is 18 cm. Workers add another 7 cm. How long is the path now?
(Number line: 18 + 7 = ___)

**Problem 2:** A piece of tape is 15 inches long. Mia uses 6 inches. How much tape is left?
(Number line: 15 − 6 = ___)

**Problem 3:** Jake's pencil is 8 inches. Kim's pencil is 5 inches. How much longer is Jake's pencil?
(Equation: ___ − ___ = ___)

For each problem, ask: "What is the story telling us? What do we need to find? Should we add or subtract?"

---

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. A blue ribbon is 13 inches. A red ribbon is 7 inches. How much longer is the blue ribbon?
   Equation: ___ − ___ = ___ inches

2. A snake moves 22 cm forward, then 9 cm more. How far does it move in all?
   Number line jump: Start at ___, jump ___. Answer: ___ cm

3. A piece of string is 40 cm. Lena cuts off 15 cm. How much is left?
   Equation: ___ − ___ = ___ cm

4. Ana has a yarn piece that is 6 inches long. She needs a piece that is 10 inches. How many more inches does she need?
   Equation: ___ + ___ = 10, so ___ more inches

**Stretch:**
5. A classroom wall is 90 inches wide. Three posters hang in a row: 12 inches, 15 inches, and 18 inches wide. What is the total width of the three posters? How much wall space is not covered by posters?

6. A worm moves 14 cm in the morning and some more in the afternoon. By the end of the day it has moved 32 cm. How far did it move in the afternoon?
   ___ + ___ = 32

**Challenge:**
7. A piece of rope is 50 cm long. Maria cuts it into 3 pieces. One piece is 12 cm, another is 19 cm. How long is the third piece? Show all your work.
8. A number line shows a jump from 27 to 65. Write a word problem about length that could be described by this jump.

### Interleaved Review
R1. 300 + 400 = ___ (Unit 4 — place value addition)
R2. 75 − 37 = ___ (Unit 5 — subtraction within 100)
R3. Count by 2s: 14, 16, ___, ___, ___, ___ (Unit 3 — skip counting)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)

**Exit Ticket:**
1. A path is 45 cm long. A shortcut makes it 18 cm shorter. How long is the shortcut path? Write an equation. ___ − ___ = ___
2. Ella's desk is 24 inches wide. Max's desk is 30 inches wide. How much wider is Max's desk? ___ in.

**Mastery = both correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Use physical yarn to act out problems before writing equations.
- Focus on identifying the question: "What are we trying to find — the whole, or a part?"
- Provide a graphic organizer with blanks: "We know: ___ and ___. We need to find: ___. We should add / subtract."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Invite student to write their own measurement word problem for a classmate to solve.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Story Lab:** Create a "measurement mini-book" with three original word problems involving lengths (one add-to, one take-from, one compare). Include drawings and equations. Swap books with a partner to solve.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Number line jumps are essential for this lesson. Do not rush students to abstract equations; students who need the number line as a scaffold should use it freely throughout.
- When students write equations, require them to include the unit label in their answer (inches or cm). "7" is not a complete answer; "7 inches" is.
- Multi-step problems (like Example 3 and Stretch problem 5) are a stretch for many second graders. Scaffold by drawing a visual diagram: a total bar divided into parts.
- Watch for students who always add regardless of the problem structure. Ask "What is the problem asking — how much altogether, or how much more/less/remains?"
