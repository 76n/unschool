# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 6, Lesson 1

## Topic
Measuring Length in Inches

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Select a ruler as the appropriate tool for measuring length
- Align an object correctly with the 0 mark on a ruler
- Measure the length of an object to the nearest inch
- Record measurements using the label "inches" or "in."

## Prerequisites
- Familiarity with numbers 0–12
- Understanding of "longer" and "shorter" in everyday language
- Basic counting skills

## Materials Needed
- Rulers with inch markings (one per student)
- Inch tiles or linking cubes (for concrete stage)
- 5–6 classroom objects of varying lengths (pencil, crayon, eraser, small book, glue stick)
- Student math journals or recording sheets
- Mini whiteboards and markers
- Measurement recording chart (table with Object / Estimate / Actual Measurement columns)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of comparison language and build number sense around length.

**Longer or Shorter?**
Display two objects (e.g., a pencil and a crayon). Ask:
- "Which one is longer? How do you know?"
- "About how many of your fingers wide is the crayon?"

Then ask students to each find one object in front of them and share whether it is longer or shorter than their hand. Take 3–4 quick responses.

Transition: "Today we are going to stop guessing and start measuring — using a tool called a ruler."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the inch as a standard unit and teach correct ruler technique using the CRA progression.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Inch (in.):** A unit used to measure length in the United States; about the width of a grown-up's thumb
- **Ruler:** A flat tool marked with inches (and sometimes centimeters) used to measure length
- **Length:** How long something is from one end to the other
- **Estimate:** A careful guess before measuring

### Instruction Notes

**Concrete phase:**
Give each student 6–8 inch tiles or linking cubes. Demonstrate:
1. Place tiles end-to-end along a pencil with no gaps and no overlaps.
2. Count the tiles: "This pencil is about 5 inches long."
Have students measure their crayon using tiles. Share results.

Discuss: "This works, but what if we measured something really long? We would need hundreds of tiles! Rulers mark the inches for us so we can measure quickly."

**Pictorial phase:**
Draw a ruler on the board (0 through 6) and a pencil above it. Show:
- The left end of the pencil lines up with the 0 mark (NOT the end of the ruler).
- Read where the right end of the pencil points.

Ask students to sketch a ruler and a short crayon in their journals and write the measurement.

**Abstract phase:**
Show written measurements: "5 in." and "5 inches." Explain both ways to write it.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:**
A crayon is placed on a ruler. Its left end is at 0 and its right end is at 4. Ask: "How long is the crayon?" → 4 inches.

**Example 2:**
A glue stick's left end is at 0 and its right end falls between 3 and 4, closer to 3. Ask: "To the nearest inch, how long is the glue stick?" → 3 inches. Explain: "We look at which inch mark it is closest to."

**Example 3 (Common error):**
Show a pencil placed with its left end at the 1 mark (not 0). Ask: "What did I do wrong?" Discuss. Then correct: "Always start at zero."

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Students measure 3 objects from their measurement kit with their ruler. Facilitator circulates asking guiding questions:
- "Where is your ruler starting?"
- "Which inch mark is closest to the end of the object?"

Objects to measure (displayed or distributed):
1. A red crayon
2. A new pencil
3. An eraser

Students record on their chart:
| Object | My Estimate | Actual Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Red crayon | ___ in. | ___ in. |
| Pencil | ___ in. | ___ in. |
| Eraser | ___ in. | ___ in. |

After recording, compare with a partner: "Did you get the same measurement?"

---

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
Use your ruler to measure each object below (drawn on the recording sheet, each above a ruler from 0 to 6):
1. [Drawn crayon above a ruler, right end at 3] — How long is the crayon? ___
2. [Drawn pencil above a ruler, right end at 5] — How long is the pencil? ___
3. Measure your own pencil. It is ___ inches long.
4. Measure your math journal (the short side). It is about ___ inches long.

**Stretch:**
5. A marker is 6 inches long. A crayon is 4 inches long. Which is longer? How much longer? ___ inches longer.
6. Draw a line that is 3 inches long. Draw a second line that is 5 inches long. Which is longer?

**Challenge:**
7. Measure three objects in the room. Order them from shortest to longest. Record in a table.
8. "A piece of yarn is 8 inches long. I cut off 3 inches. How long is the yarn now? Draw a ruler to show your thinking."

### Interleaved Review
R1. What is 47 + 36? Show your work. (Unit 1 — addition within 100)
R2. I had 15 stickers. I gave away 8. How many do I have left? (Unit 2 — subtraction within 20)
R3. Count by 5s: 5, 10, ___, ___, ___, ___. (Unit 3 — skip counting)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)

**Exit Ticket:**
1. [Picture of a worm above a ruler 0–6, right end at 4] How long is the worm? ___ inches
2. A student started measuring at the "2" on the ruler instead of "0." Is that correct? Circle: YES / NO. Explain: _______________

**Mastery = both items correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Pull the student to work one-on-one with inch tiles to rebuild the connection between counting units and the ruler.
- Practice aligning to 0 with 5–6 more objects before attempting ruler-only measurement.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Challenge the student to find an object that is "between 4 and 5 inches" and write a sentence explaining how they know.
- Introduce the concept of half inches if the student is interested.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Scavenger Hunt:** Can you find something in the room that is exactly 1 inch long? Exactly 6 inches? More than 10 inches? Record your findings.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The single most important technique in this lesson is aligning the object with the 0 mark. Emphasize this multiple times using Think-Alouds during demonstrations.
- If student rulers only show inches on one side and centimeters on the other, have students use the inches side only today and keep the centimeter side face-down.
- For students who struggle with the concept of "nearest inch," use a number line on the board showing 0–6 and physically move a dot to show "closer to 3 than to 4."
- Collect recording charts as an informal formative check.
