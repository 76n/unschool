# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 6, Lesson 10

## Topic
Data Interpretation and Mixed Review

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Answer multi-step questions about data from bar graphs and picture graphs
- Generate measurement data and display it on a line plot
- Apply all Unit 6 skills (measurement, time, money, data) in mixed problem contexts
- Demonstrate readiness for the Unit 6 Assessment

## Prerequisites
- All Unit 6 lessons (Lessons 1–9)
- Reading and creating graphs (Lesson 9)
- Line plot introduction (Lesson 3)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick review of all four Unit 6 strands to activate memory before mixed practice.

**"Four Corner" Quick Review:**
Ask one question from each strand; students answer on whiteboards:
1. Measurement: "A crayon is 7 cm. A pencil is 12 cm. How much longer is the pencil?" (5 cm)
2. Time: "What time is shown when the short hand is on 4 and the long hand is on 6?" (4:30)
3. Money: "Count: 1 quarter, 2 dimes, 1 penny. Total?" (46¢)
4. Data: "A bar graph shows: cats = 5, dogs = 8. How many more dogs than cats?" (3)

Celebrate answers. "Today we put ALL of these skills together!"

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce line plots as a way to display measurement data; review data interpretation with multi-step questions.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Line plot:** A number line with Xs placed above values to show how often each value occurs
- **Most common (mode):** The value that appears most often in the data
- **Range:** The difference between the largest and smallest values in a data set
- **Multi-step problem:** A problem that requires more than one operation to solve

### Instruction Notes

**Concrete → Pictorial (Line Plot):**
"Yesterday we measured pencils. Let's say 8 students measured their pencils and got these lengths (in inches): 5, 6, 5, 7, 6, 5, 8, 6."

First, sort physical pencils (or cards labeled with lengths) into groups: all 5-inch pencils together, all 6-inch together, etc.

Then transfer to a line plot:
Draw a number line from 4 to 9. Place an X above the number for each measurement.

```
        X X X
  X   X X   X
4   5   6   7   8   9
```

Ask:
- "Which length is most common?" (5 and 6, tied with 3 each — or adjust data to avoid tie)
- "What is the range?" (Longest − shortest = 8 − 5 = 3)
- "How many pencils were measured total?" (Count all Xs = 8)

**Multi-step data problems:**
"The bar graph shows: Monday = 12 books, Tuesday = 8 books, Wednesday = 15 books."
Q: "How many more books were read on Wednesday than on Monday?" 15 − 12 = 3.
Q: "How many books were read in all three days?" 12 + 8 + 15 = 35.
"That second question is two steps: add three numbers."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (Line plot creation):**
Data: lengths in inches: 3, 3, 4, 5, 3, 4, 6, 5. Create a line plot. Most common = 3. Range = 6 − 3 = 3.

**Example 2 (Multi-step data):**
Picture graph: Red = 4, Blue = 7, Yellow = 5, Green = 3.
Q: "How many more students picked blue than red and yellow combined?"
Step 1: Red + Yellow = 4 + 5 = 9.
Step 2: Blue − 9 = 7 − 9. "Wait — blue (7) is less than 9. So actually more students picked red and yellow combined than blue." Correct interpretation: red and yellow combined (9) is 2 more than blue (7).
Reinforce careful reading: always re-read the question!

**Example 3 (Mixed strand):**
"Maria measured 3 ribbons: 8 in., 5 in., and 11 in. She wants to buy the shortest one. It costs 20¢ per inch. How much will she pay?"
Step 1: Shortest ribbon = 5 inches.
Step 2: 5 × 20¢ = 100¢ = $1.00. (Use repeated addition: 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 = 100¢.)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Line Plot Activity:**
Each student measures 5 crayons (or crayon-length strips) to the nearest inch and records the data. Combine all student data into a class line plot on the board.

Ask the class:
1. What is the most common crayon length?
2. What is the range (longest − shortest)?
3. How many crayons are shorter than 5 inches?
4. If one more crayon is measured and it is 7 inches, how does the line plot change?

**Mixed Review Set (whiteboards):**
5. Show 8:35 on your analog clock.
6. Count: 2 quarters + 1 dime + 3 nickels. Total?
7. A worm crawls 14 cm. A snail crawls 9 cm. How much farther does the worm crawl?

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
Use the bar graph provided (Book Club Data — books read each month):
- January: 10
- February: 6
- March: 14
- April: 8

1. Which month had the most books read? ___
2. Which month had the fewest? ___
3. How many more books were read in March than in February? ___
4. How many books were read in January and April combined? ___
5. [Line plot given: X marks at 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 5, 5]
   a. How many items were measured? ___
   b. What is the most common measurement? ___
   c. What is the range? ___

**Stretch:**
6. The bar graph above shows books read from January through April. How many books were read in all four months combined?
7. If the goal was to read 40 books total by the end of April, did the Book Club meet its goal? How many books over or under was it?
8. Measure 6 objects in the room to the nearest inch. Record the data and create a line plot. Write one question about your line plot.

**Challenge:**
9. A bar graph shows 5 students' savings: Ali = 45¢, Bea = 30¢, Cal = 55¢, Dana = 20¢, Eli = 50¢.
   a. Who has the most savings?
   b. How much more does Cal have than Dana?
   c. If Ali and Bea combine their money, do they have enough to buy an item that costs 80¢?
   d. How much money do all 5 students have together?

### Interleaved Review
R1. 425 + 368 = ___ (Unit 5 — addition within 1,000)
R2. A class counts: 17 boys and 14 girls. How many students in total? (Unit 2 — addition within 20 / within 100)
R3. Skip count by 25s: 25, 50, ___, ___, ___ (Unit 3 / Lesson 7 money connection)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)

**Exit Ticket:**
1. [Line plot with Xs at: 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4] What is the most common measurement? ___. What is the range? ___
2. The bar graph shows: soccer = 9, tennis = 4, swimming = 7. How many students play soccer AND swimming combined? ___

**Mastery = both correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review line plots one-on-one using linking cube towers (convert cube height to X on line plot).
- For multi-step data problems, use a two-step graphic organizer: "Step 1: ___. Step 2: ___."

### If Mastery Is Met
- "Can you find a data question that requires three steps to answer? Write it and solve it."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson serves as both a new lesson (line plots and multi-step data) AND a review session for the unit assessment. Balance the two purposes: spend the first 20 minutes on new/reviewed content and the last 20 minutes on mixed practice.
- The mixed-strand independent practice is deliberately designed to give students a preview of the assessment structure. After the lesson, review any skills that caused widespread difficulty.
- Collect exit tickets and review them before the assessment. Students who miss the exit ticket should receive targeted reteaching.
- Announce the Unit 6 Assessment for the following session. Encourage students to review vocabulary and practice at home using flash cards or review games.
