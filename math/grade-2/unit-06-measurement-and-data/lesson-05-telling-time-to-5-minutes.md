# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 6, Lesson 5

## Topic
Telling Time to 5 Minutes (Analog Clock)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify the hour hand and minute hand on an analog clock
- Read the hour hand to identify the hour
- Skip count by 5s around the clock face to find the minutes
- Read and write time to the nearest 5 minutes (e.g., 3:25, 7:50)

## Prerequisites
- Skip counting by 5s to 60 (Unit 3)
- Numbers 1–12 on the clock face
- Basic understanding of "hour" and "minute" from prior experience

## Materials Needed
- Student analog practice clocks with moveable hands (one per student; paper-plate clocks with brass fasteners if needed)
- Large demonstration clock
- Clock face worksheet (12 blank clock outlines)
- Anchor chart: "Clocks" showing hand colors, skip count by 5s around the face
- Mini whiteboards
- Index cards with times written on them (for matching game)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate skip counting by 5s and connect to the clock face.

**Skip Count Around the Clock:**
Display the large clock. Point to each number (1 through 12) and have students skip count by 5s aloud together:
"1 → 5, 2 → 10, 3 → 15, 4 → 20, 5 → 25, 6 → 30, 7 → 35, 8 → 40, 9 → 45, 10 → 50, 11 → 55, 12 → 60 (which is back to :00)"

Write the 5s sequence on the board as students count:
5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 55, (60 = 0)

Ask: "How many minutes are in one hour?" → 60 minutes.

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach hand identification and systematic time-reading procedure.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Hour hand:** The short hand; points to the hour; moves slowly
- **Minute hand:** The long hand; points to the minutes; moves around the whole clock once per hour
- **A.M.:** Times from midnight (12:00 A.M.) to just before noon
- **P.M.:** Times from noon (12:00 P.M.) to just before midnight
- **Colon:** The ":" symbol separating hours and minutes in written time (e.g., 4:35)

### Instruction Notes

**Concrete phase:**
Give each student a student clock. Say: "Right now, show 3 o'clock." Students move both hands to 3. Confirm: the short hand points at 3, the long hand points at 12. "3:00."

"Now I'll move the minute hand slowly while you watch the clock." Slowly move it to 6. "What do you notice about the hour hand?" It has moved to between 3 and 4 — still past 3. "So the hour is still 3, but the minute hand is pointing to 6, which means 30 minutes. The time is 3:30."

**Pictorial phase:**
Draw two clock faces on the board:
- Clock A: Short hand on 7, long hand on 12. → 7:00
- Clock B: Short hand just past 2, long hand on 9. Ask: "The short hand is just past 2, so the hour is 2. The long hand is on 9 — count by 5s: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45. The time is 2:45."

**Abstract phase:**
Write "6:20" on the board. "What does the 6 mean? What does the 20 mean?" Have students show 6:20 on their student clocks.

**Reading Procedure Poster (build with students):**
Step 1: Look at the SHORT hand → that is the HOUR.
Step 2: Look at the LONG hand → count by 5s from 12 → that is the MINUTES.
Step 3: Write: Hour : Minutes

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:**
Clock: Short hand on 4, long hand on 12. → 4:00. Say: "Four o'clock."

**Example 2:**
Clock: Short hand just past 9, long hand on 3. → Count by 5s to 3: 5, 10, 15. → 9:15. Say: "Nine fifteen."

**Example 3:**
Clock: Short hand between 1 and 2, long hand on 7. → Count by 5s to 7: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35. → 1:35. Say: "One thirty-five."

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Facilitator sets the demonstration clock to 5 different times. For each time, students:
1. Set their own clock to match.
2. Write the time on their whiteboard (H:MM format).

Times to use:
- 2:00
- 5:30
- 10:15
- 8:45
- 1:20

After each, check student clocks and boards. Address any errors immediately.

Then reverse: facilitator calls out a time verbally, students show it on their clock and write it.
- "Show seven forty-five." → 7:45
- "Show three oh five." → 3:05 (introduce leading zero for minutes less than 10)

---

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
Write the time shown on each clock face (8 clock pictures with hands drawn):
1. Short hand on 2, long hand on 12 → ___:___
2. Short hand just past 6, long hand on 6 → ___:___
3. Short hand just past 11, long hand on 9 → ___:___
4. Short hand between 4 and 5, long hand on 3 → ___:___

Draw hands on the blank clock faces:
5. Show 7:00
6. Show 10:30
7. Show 3:15
8. Show 5:45

**Stretch:**
9. Write three times you might see on a school morning (getting up, eating breakfast, arriving at school). Draw each on a clock face.
10. If school starts at 8:00 and you wake up at 6:30, is 6:30 before or after 8:00? How do you know?

**Challenge:**
11. The minute hand has made one full trip around the clock. How many minutes have passed? ___
12. "It is 3:00. In 25 minutes, what time will it be? Draw the clock to show the new time." → 3:25

### Interleaved Review
R1. 17 − 8 = ___ (Unit 2)
R2. What number has 5 hundreds, 0 tens, 3 ones? (Unit 4)
R3. 246 + 130 = ___ (Unit 5)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)

**Exit Ticket:**
1. [Clock face with short hand on 8, long hand on 4] What time does this clock show? ___:___
2. Draw the hands on a blank clock to show 11:25.

**Mastery = both correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Drill skip counting by 5s around the clock face separately.
- Practice hand identification: show a clock and ask only "Which hand is the minute hand? Point to it." before asking for the time.
- Provide a clock with the minute values (5, 10, 15...) already written next to each number.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Ask: "Can you tell me two different times where the minute hand points to the same number? What is different about those two times?"

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Time Diary:** Write your schedule for tomorrow morning. Write the time for: wake up, breakfast, getting dressed, leaving for school. Draw an analog clock for each event.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Color coding is highly effective for this lesson. If student clocks have color-coded hands, use those colors consistently in all drawings and discussions. If not, use red and blue markers when drawing clock examples.
- Introduce 3:05 carefully — students may read it as 3:5. Explain: "We always write two digits for the minutes. If there are fewer than 10 minutes, we put a zero in front: 3:05."
- The "just past" language for the hour hand can confuse students. A helpful rule: "If the short hand has passed a number, that number is the hour — even if it's moved a little past it."
- Some students confuse the concept of the minute hand being "on 9" meaning "9 minutes" vs. "45 minutes." Reteach skip counting by 5s if this confusion arises.
