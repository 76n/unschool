# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 6: Measurement and Data
## Facilitator Guide

---

## Unit Overview

Unit 6 brings measurement and data into focus for Grade 2 students. Over 10 lessons, students develop hands-on skills measuring length in both customary (inches) and metric (centimeters) units, telling time to the nearest 5 minutes on analog clocks, counting and comparing collections of coins, and reading and creating bar graphs and picture graphs. The unit emphasizes real-world application throughout: students measure actual objects in the classroom, read real analog clocks, handle play money in store scenarios, and collect and organize authentic data.

This unit connects strongly to prior learning. Addition and subtraction skills from Units 1–2 support money calculations and data comparisons. Skip counting from Unit 3 underpins coin counting (nickels by 5s, dimes by 10s). Place value understanding from Unit 4 helps students interpret measurement numbers. Fluency with larger numbers from Unit 5 supports elapsed time and money word problems.

## Facilitation Approach

### Concrete–Pictorial–Abstract (CRA) Progression

Every topic in this unit follows the CRA progression:

**Concrete:** Students use physical objects first. They measure real items with real rulers, handle actual play coins, move clock hands on student clocks, and build bar graphs with stacks of cubes.

**Pictorial:** Students represent their concrete work in drawings and diagrams. They draw rulers with objects above them, draw clock faces, draw coin sets, and draw or complete printed bar and picture graphs.

**Abstract:** Only after concrete and pictorial experience do students work with numbers and symbols alone. This includes number sentences for length comparisons, written times (3:25), monetary values ($0.47), and data interpretation questions using only a printed graph.

### Pacing Guidance

Each lesson is designed for approximately 45–50 minutes. If students need more time on concrete work, extend the Guided Practice phase and assign fewer Independent Practice items. Return to the abstract work in the next session's warm-up.

### Partner and Group Work

Many activities in this unit work best in pairs or small groups (measuring objects, running coin store scenarios, conducting surveys). Assign partners thoughtfully, pairing students who can support each other's concrete work without one student dominating.

## Common Misconceptions

### Measurement
- **Not starting at zero:** Students often start measuring from the "1" mark on a ruler rather than the "0" end. Emphasize aligning the object's left edge with the 0 mark.
- **Gaps and overlaps when using unit tiles:** When using inch tiles or cubes to measure, students sometimes leave gaps or overlap tiles. Model careful tile placement repeatedly.
- **Confusing inches and centimeters:** Help students build a reference: one inch is about the width of a thumb; one centimeter is about the width of a pinky fingernail. Keep anchor posters visible throughout the unit.
- **Measuring the wrong attribute:** Young students sometimes measure the diagonal of an object rather than its length. Always clarify which dimension you are measuring.

### Time
- **Mixing up the hour and minute hands:** Students frequently confuse which hand is which. Use color coding (red = short = hour; blue = long = minute) on practice clocks.
- **Counting minute marks instead of 5-minute intervals:** When reading past the 12, some students count each tick instead of skip-counting by 5s. Practice skip counting by 5s around the clock face as a regular warm-up.
- **Writing time in incorrect order:** Students may write minutes before hours (e.g., 25:3 instead of 3:25). Emphasize: hours first, then colon, then minutes.

### Money
- **Treating all coins as equal value:** Students may count 3 dimes and 3 pennies as "6 cents." Return to the concrete: sort by coin type, then count by value.
- **Forgetting that nickels = 5 and dimes = 10:** Post a coin reference chart. Connect to skip counting: "Count nickels like you skip count by 5s."
- **Starting over when adding a new coin type:** Teach students to count on from their running total rather than restarting.

### Data
- **Not reading the scale on bar graphs:** Students may look only at bar height without reading the axis. Practice asking "What does each unit on the axis stand for?" before reading any graph.
- **Confusing "more" and "fewer" in data questions:** Use sentence frames: "_____ has ___ more/fewer than _____."

## Differentiation

### For Students Who Need More Support
- Allow extended use of concrete manipulatives (rulers with large markings, oversized play coins).
- Provide partially completed recording sheets (measurement tables with some entries filled in).
- For time: use pre-drawn clock faces with the hands already drawn; student identifies the time rather than drawing both hands.
- For money: limit to pennies and nickels before introducing dimes and quarters.
- Use number lines prominently for length and money word problems.
- Pair with a peer buddy during partner activities.

### For Students Who Are Ready for More
- Introduce feet and meters as larger units of measurement.
- Challenge students to measure in both inches and centimeters and explain the relationship.
- For time: introduce A.M. and P.M.; discuss what you might be doing at various times of day.
- For money: include dollar bills; challenge students to make the same amount multiple ways.
- For data: have students design their own survey questions, collect data from classmates, and present their graphs with written conclusions.

## Assessment Guidance

### Formative Assessment (During Each Lesson)
- **Exit Tickets:** Each lesson ends with a 2–3 item exit ticket. Review these before the next lesson to identify students needing reteaching.
- **Observation:** During concrete activities, circulate and note measurement technique, clock-reading accuracy, and coin-counting strategy.
- **Whiteboard responses:** Use whole-group whiteboard responses during warm-ups and check-for-understanding moments.

### Summative Assessment
The Unit 6 Assessment (12 items plus 2 performance tasks) covers all 10 standards (2.MD.1–10). Allow approximately 45–60 minutes. The assessment includes:
- 4 items on measurement (2.MD.1–5)
- 2 items on time (2.MD.7)
- 3 items on money (2.MD.8)
- 3 items on data/graphs (2.MD.9–10)

**Mastery threshold:** 80% or higher (approximately 10/12 items correct plus satisfactory performance task completion).

### Reteach Priorities
If students score below 80%, prioritize reteaching based on the following error patterns:
1. Measurement errors → Return to Lessons 1–2 with concrete manipulatives
2. Time errors → Return to Lesson 5 with student clocks; drill skip counting by 5s
3. Money errors → Return to Lesson 7; rebuild coin values concretely
4. Data errors → Return to Lesson 9; rebuild graphs with physical objects before paper graphs

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- **Preparation:** Before Lesson 1, assemble measurement kits (ruler + 5–8 classroom objects of varying lengths) for each student or pair.
- **Before Lesson 5:** Prepare or borrow enough student analog clocks. If clocks are unavailable, create paper-plate clocks with brass fasteners as a low-cost alternative.
- **Before Lesson 7:** Count and bag play money sets. Include approximately 10 pennies, 5 nickels, 5 dimes, and 4 quarters per student or pair.
- **Before Lesson 9:** Design a simple class survey question (e.g., favorite fruit from 4 choices) and prepare the tally chart in advance.
- **Real-world connections:** Throughout the unit, point out measurement in daily life — reading a recipe (cups), checking the weather (degrees), looking at a clock in the classroom, reading prices at a school store.
- **Vocabulary wall:** Add key terms to the math word wall as they are introduced: *inch, centimeter, estimate, length, analog clock, hour hand, minute hand, penny, nickel, dime, quarter, bar graph, picture graph, data, scale.*
