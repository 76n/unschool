# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 6, Lesson 7

## Topic
Counting Coins (Pennies, Nickels, Dimes, Quarters)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify the four coins (penny, nickel, dime, quarter) and their values
- Count a mixed collection of coins by sorting and counting on
- Write coin amounts using the cent sign (¢) and dollar sign ($0.__)
- Determine if a set of coins has more or less than a given amount

## Prerequisites
- Skip counting by 5s and 10s (Unit 3)
- Addition within 100 (Unit 1)
- Numbers and number sense to 100

## Materials Needed
- Play money sets: pennies, nickels, dimes, quarters (10 pennies, 5 nickels, 5 dimes, 4 quarters per student)
- Coin reference chart (picture, name, value, distinguishing features for each coin)
- Sorting mats (one section per coin type)
- Mini whiteboards
- Recording sheets for coin counting practice
- Optional: real coins for show-and-tell

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review skip counting by 5s and 10s; activate prior knowledge of coins.

**Skip Count Connect:**
Call out each count:
- "Count by 10s from 0 to 100 — go!" (Practice for dimes)
- "Count by 5s from 0 to 50 — go!" (Practice for nickels)
- "Count by 25s: 25, 50, 75, 100." (Practice for quarters)

Then hold up a play quarter: "Who has seen one of these before? What is it? What is it worth?" Take responses.

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce coin identities and values; teach a systematic counting strategy.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Penny:** A copper-colored coin worth 1 cent (1¢)
- **Nickel:** A silver coin worth 5 cents (5¢)
- **Dime:** The smallest silver coin; worth 10 cents (10¢)
- **Quarter:** A silver coin; worth 25 cents (25¢)
- **Cent (¢):** The symbol for cents; 100 cents = 1 dollar
- **Dollar ($):** 100 cents; written as $1.00

### Instruction Notes

**Concrete phase — Coin Identification:**
Distribute play money. Students sort coins on their sorting mat into 4 groups: pennies, nickels, dimes, quarters.

Build the coin reference chart together:
| Coin | Color | Size | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Penny | Copper/brown | Medium | 1¢ |
| Nickel | Silver | Larger | 5¢ |
| Dime | Silver | Smallest | 10¢ |
| Quarter | Silver | Largest | 25¢ |

Reinforce: "The dime is the smallest coin but worth more than the penny AND the nickel!"

**Concrete phase — Counting Strategy:**
Teach the "count by greatest value first" strategy:
1. Sort coins by type.
2. Count the largest-value coins first.
3. Continue counting on with smaller coins.

Model: Place 2 quarters, 1 dime, 2 nickels, 3 pennies on the mat.
- Quarters: 25, 50
- Dime: 60
- Nickels: 65, 70
- Pennies: 71, 72, 73
Total: 73¢

**Pictorial phase:**
Draw a coin collection on the board. Students count from the picture, recording their running total.

**Abstract phase:**
Write totals using both notations: "73¢" and "$0.73." Explain: "When we write it with a dollar sign, we put a zero and a dot before the cents. 73¢ = $0.73."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:**
3 dimes, 2 nickels, 4 pennies.
Dimes: 10, 20, 30. Nickels: 35, 40. Pennies: 41, 42, 43, 44. Total: 44¢.

**Example 2:**
1 quarter, 3 dimes.
Quarter: 25. Dimes: 35, 45, 55. Total: 55¢.

**Example 3:**
2 quarters, 2 dimes, 1 nickel.
Quarters: 25, 50. Dimes: 60, 70. Nickel: 75. Total: 75¢.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Set out each coin collection below with actual play coins. Students count and record.

1. 1 quarter, 2 dimes, 1 penny → ___¢
2. 3 nickels, 4 pennies → ___¢
3. 2 dimes, 3 nickels, 2 pennies → ___¢
4. 1 quarter, 1 dime, 3 pennies → ___¢

After each, ask a student to share their counting sequence. Correct any errors in the counting-on strategy.

Then ask: "Which collection has the most? Which has the least?"

---

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
Count each group of coins and write the total in cents:
1. [Image description: 2 nickels, 3 pennies] → ___¢
2. [Image description: 1 dime, 2 nickels, 1 penny] → ___¢
3. [Image description: 1 quarter, 2 pennies] → ___¢
4. [Image description: 2 dimes, 3 nickels] → ___¢
5. Do you have enough money to buy an eraser for 30¢ if you have 1 dime, 3 nickels, and 2 pennies? Show your counting.

**Stretch:**
6. Show two different ways to make 20¢ using coins. Draw and label each coin.
7. I have 1 quarter, 1 dime, and some nickels. I have exactly 50¢. How many nickels do I have? Explain.
8. Order these amounts from least to greatest: 43¢ / 1 quarter + 1 dime / 2 dimes + 2 nickels

**Challenge:**
9. Make exactly 99¢ using the fewest possible coins. What coins do you use? How many total coins?
10. A class collected coins in a jar. There are 5 dimes, 4 nickels, 7 pennies, and 2 quarters. How much money is in the jar? Write the amount in both ¢ and $ notation.

### Interleaved Review
R1. 27 + 48 = ___ (Unit 1)
R2. Count by 2s: 18, 20, ___, ___, ___, ___ (Unit 3)
R3. 400 + 350 = ___ (Unit 5)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Count the coins: 1 quarter, 1 dime, 2 nickels. Total: ___¢
2. Which coin is worth more — a nickel or a dime? ___. How much more? ___¢

**Mastery = both correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to counting a single coin type first (all pennies, all nickels, all dimes) before mixing.
- Practice connecting skip counting sequences to coins: "Count these dimes like you skip count by 10s."
- Use a running total recording strip where students write their total after each coin is counted.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Challenge: "Can you make 50¢ three different ways? Record each way."

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Coin Combinations:** How many different ways can you make 25¢? (Use only pennies, nickels, and dimes.) List each combination. Who found the most ways?

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The single most common error is not sorting before counting. Reinforce: "Sort first, then count greatest to least."
- Watch for students who assign wrong values (e.g., think a dime is worth 1 cent because it is small). The coin reference chart should remain visible the entire lesson.
- Counting on from the running total (not restarting at each new coin type) takes practice. Model Think-Alouds: "I have 30 cents so far from the dimes... now I add a nickel... 35. I don't go back to zero, I count on."
- The ¢ vs. $0.__ notation is formally introduced here. Students are not yet expected to add dollars and cents; that is a Grade 3 skill.
