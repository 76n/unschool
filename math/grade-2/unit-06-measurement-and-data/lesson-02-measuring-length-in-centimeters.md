# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 6, Lesson 2

## Topic
Measuring Length in Centimeters

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify the centimeter side of a ruler
- Measure the length of an object to the nearest centimeter
- Record measurements using the label "centimeters" or "cm"
- Measure the same object in both inches and centimeters and recognize that the numbers differ

## Prerequisites
- Measuring length in inches (Lesson 1)
- Counting to 30
- Understanding that different units can measure the same attribute

## Materials Needed
- Rulers with both inch and centimeter markings (one per student)
- Centimeter cubes or square tiles (about 10 per student)
- Same or similar classroom objects used in Lesson 1 (pencil, crayon, eraser, small book)
- Dual-unit recording chart (Object / Length in Inches / Length in Centimeters)
- Mini whiteboards
- Anchor poster: "1 inch ≈ 2½ centimeters" (prepare in advance)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review inch measurement from Lesson 1 and build curiosity about a new unit.

**Quick Measurement Review:**
Give students a ruler. Ask them to quickly measure their pencil in inches and show the answer on their whiteboard. Confirm answers.

Then ask: "Look at the other side of your ruler. What do you notice?" Take responses.

Say: "The other side shows a different unit — centimeters. Today we'll learn to measure with centimeters and find out how they compare to inches."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the centimeter as a metric unit and build fluency measuring with it.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Centimeter (cm):** A metric unit of length; about the width of a pinky fingernail
- **Metric system:** A measurement system based on 10s, used in most countries
- **Standard/Customary system:** The measurement system used in the U.S. that includes inches and feet

### Instruction Notes

**Concrete phase:**
Distribute centimeter cubes. Ask students to line up 5 cubes edge-to-edge. Say: "Each cube is 1 centimeter wide. Let's count: 1 cm, 2 cm, 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm." Now lay the cubes along a short pencil. Count how many cubes fit along it — that is its length in centimeters.

**Pictorial phase:**
Turn the ruler to the centimeter side. Model on the board:
1. Align the left end of an object with the 0 mark.
2. Read the number at the right end of the object.
Emphasize: "The zero rule is the same — always start at 0, whether you're measuring in inches or centimeters."

Draw an object on the board above a cm ruler. Ask students to read the measurement.

**Abstract phase:**
Show: "This pencil is 7 inches long and 18 centimeters long. Same pencil — two different numbers! Why?" Guide students to understand: centimeters are smaller units, so you need more of them.

Display the anchor poster: **1 inch ≈ 2½ centimeters.**

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:**
A crayon measures from 0 to 9 on the centimeter side. Length = 9 cm.

**Example 2:**
A book spine measures from 0 to 20 cm. Length = 20 cm. "That is 20 centimeters, or we can say about 8 inches."

**Example 3 (Both units):**
Measure the same eraser. In inches: 2 in. In centimeters: 5 cm. "The centimeter number is bigger because centimeters are smaller units."

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Students measure three objects using the centimeter side of their ruler and record in the dual-unit chart.

| Object | Length in Inches | Length in Centimeters |
|---|---|---|
| Crayon | ___ in. | ___ cm |
| Pencil | ___ in. | ___ cm |
| Small book (short side) | ___ in. | ___ cm |

Partners compare their measurements. Discuss:
- "Which number is bigger — the inches or the centimeters?"
- "Why do you think that is?"

---

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. [Drawing of a fish above a centimeter ruler, right end at 8] How long is the fish? ___ cm
2. [Drawing of a worm above a centimeter ruler, right end at 12] How long is the worm? ___ cm
3. Measure your crayon in centimeters: ___ cm
4. Measure your thumb from the base to the tip: about ___ cm

**Stretch:**
5. A rope is 15 cm long. A second rope is 9 cm long. Which is longer? How much longer?
6. Measure the same object in inches AND centimeters. Which number is bigger? Write a sentence to explain why.

**Challenge:**
7. A snake is 24 cm long. A lizard is 17 cm long. How much longer is the snake? Show your work with a number line or equation.
8. "If 1 inch is about 2½ centimeters, and a pencil is 6 inches long, about how many centimeters long is the pencil? Explain your reasoning." (Approximate answer accepted.)

### Interleaved Review
R1. 63 − 28 = ___ (Unit 1 — subtraction within 100)
R2. 8 + 7 = ___ (Unit 2 — addition within 20)
R3. What is the value of 3 hundreds, 4 tens, and 6 ones? (Unit 4 — place value)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)

**Exit Ticket:**
1. [Picture of a caterpillar above a cm ruler, right end at 6] How long is the caterpillar? ___ cm
2. A student measures a pencil and gets 7 inches. Her friend measures the same pencil and gets 18 centimeters. Are they both right? Explain. _______________

**Mastery = both items correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to centimeter cubes; rebuild the connection that each cube = 1 cm, then transfer to reading the ruler.
- Practice reading specific positions on a drawn ruler before measuring physical objects.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Ask the student: "If you measured something very long — like the hallway — would you use centimeters or something bigger? Why?" (Preview of meters.)

---

## Extension (Optional)
**Two-Unit Table:** Find 5 objects in the room. Measure each in both inches and centimeters. Do you notice a pattern between the two numbers? Can you write a rule?

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Help students flip their rulers correctly: the inch side typically says "inches" and the centimeter side says "cm." Practice flipping before the lesson begins.
- Reinforce the anchor benchmark throughout: "A centimeter is about as wide as your pinky fingernail. An inch is about as wide as your thumb."
- Students are not expected to convert precisely between inches and centimeters — the goal is to recognize that the same object will have a larger number in centimeters than in inches.
- The comparison activity (measuring the same object in both units) is key for meeting 2.MD.2.

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice:**
Measurements will vary slightly based on actual objects. Typical values:
- Crayon: ~3–4 in. / ~8–10 cm
- Pencil: ~7 in. / ~18 cm
- Small book (short side): ~5–6 in. / ~13–15 cm
In all cases, the centimeter number should be larger.

**Independent Practice (On-level):**

2. 12 cm
3. Answers vary
4. Answers vary (typically 4–6 cm)

**Stretch:**
5. The 15 cm rope is longer; it is 6 cm longer (15 − 9 = 6)
6. Centimeter number is bigger; centimeters are smaller units so more are needed

**Challenge:**

8. 6 × 2½ = 15 cm (accept 14–16 cm as reasonable)
