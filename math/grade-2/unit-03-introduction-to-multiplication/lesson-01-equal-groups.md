# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 3, Lesson 1

## Topic
Equal Groups

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify whether groups are equal (same number in each) or unequal
- Create equal groups of a given size from a collection of objects
- Count the total by counting all objects or by skip counting

## Prerequisites
- Counting within 50
- Skip counting by 2s, 5s, and 10s (basic familiarity)
- Addition within 20 (Unit 2 fluency)

## Materials Needed
- 40+ counting objects (beans, cubes, buttons)
- Small cups, plates, or bags (for grouping)
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate grouping concepts and counting.

1. Put 12 objects on the table in a messy pile. "How many are there?" (Student counts — 12)
2. "That was a lot of counting. Is there a way to organize them so they're easier to count?"
3. If the student suggests groups, great! If not, say: "What if I put them in groups?"

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Define "equal groups" and show why they matter.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Group:** A set of objects together
- **Equal groups:** Groups that all have the same number of objects
- **Unequal groups:** Groups that have different numbers of objects

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Show equal vs. unequal groups.**
Make 3 cups with 4 beans each. "Each cup has 4 beans. These are equal groups — every group is the same."

Now make 3 cups with 4, 2, and 6 beans. "These are NOT equal groups. The cups have different amounts."

**Step 2: Count equal groups.**
Return to 3 cups of 4. "How many beans altogether? I can count by 4s: 4, 8, 12. Or I can add: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. Equal groups are easy to count because every group is the same."

**Step 3: Why equal groups matter.**
"Equal groups are special. When every group has the same number, we can use shortcuts like skip counting. This is the beginning of something called multiplication — you'll learn more about that this unit!"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 4 groups of 3. Make 4 cups, each with 3 objects. Count: 3, 6, 9, 12. Total: 12.

**Example 2:** 2 groups of 5. Make 2 cups, each with 5 objects. Count: 5, 10. Total: 10.

**Example 3:** Show 3 cups with 5, 3, and 5 objects. "Are these equal groups?" (No — one cup has 3 instead of 5.) "Fix them so they are equal." (Move 1 from a cup of 5 to the cup of 3, or add 2 to the cup of 3.)

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student creates and identifies equal groups.

**Activity 1: Make equal groups**
- "Take 15 cubes. Make 3 equal groups." Student should put 5 in each group. "How many in each group?" (5) "How many groups?" (3) "How many total?" (15)
- "Take 12 cubes. Make 4 equal groups." (3 in each) "How many in each?" (3)
- "Take 10 cubes. Make 2 equal groups." (5 in each)

**Activity 2: Identify equal groups**
Show pictures or arrangements:
- 3 rows of 4 dots: "Equal groups?" (Yes — 4 in each row)
- Groups of 6, 4, 6: "Equal groups?" (No — the middle group has 4)
- 5 groups of 2: "Equal groups?" (Yes — 2 in each group)

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice creating and identifying equal groups.

**★ On-level:**
1. Draw 3 equal groups of 4 circles. How many circles total? (12)
2. Draw 4 equal groups of 2 circles. How many circles total? (8)
3. Are these equal groups? Group A: 5, Group B: 5, Group C: 5. (Yes)
4. Are these equal groups? Group A: 3, Group B: 4, Group C: 3. (No)
5. 20 objects split into 4 equal groups = ___ in each group (5)
6. 18 objects split into 3 equal groups = ___ in each group (6)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. "A teacher puts 24 pencils into 4 equal cups. How many pencils in each cup?" (6)
8. "Can you split 13 objects into 2 equal groups? Why or why not?" (No — 13 is odd; you'd have 6 and 7, which aren't equal.)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "How many different ways can you make equal groups from 12 objects? List them." (1 group of 12, 2 groups of 6, 3 groups of 4, 4 groups of 3, 6 groups of 2, 12 groups of 1)

### Interleaved Review
- "47 + 30 = ___" (77) *(Grade 1 Unit 3 or Grade 2 Unit 1)*
- "85 − 20 = ___" (65)

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can create and count equal groups.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Make 3 equal groups of 5 objects. How many total? (15)
2. Are these equal groups: 4, 4, 4, 4? (Yes)

**Mastery = Both correct. Student creates groups of exactly 5 and identifies equal groups.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice with physical objects: "Put 3 in this cup, 3 in this cup, 3 in this cup. Are they equal?" Start with very small groups (2s) and build up.
- If the student can't split objects evenly, work on "fair sharing" — dealing objects one at a time like cards until they run out.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 2 (Repeated Addition).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Keep this concrete.** The entire lesson should involve physical objects. The student must handle beans, cubes, or buttons and physically make groups. Pictures come later.

**"Groups of" language is critical.** Always say "3 groups of 4" — never just "3 and 4." This language becomes the bridge to multiplication in Lesson 6.

**Common errors:**
- Student makes groups but they're unequal: "1 more in that cup, 2 fewer in this one." Practice "dealing" — distribute objects one at a time to each cup, like dealing cards.
- Student confuses "3 groups of 4" with "3 and 4" (totals 7 instead of 12). Always build it physically to show the difference.
