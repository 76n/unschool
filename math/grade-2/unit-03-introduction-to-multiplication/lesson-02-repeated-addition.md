# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 3, Lesson 2

## Topic
Repeated Addition

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Write a repeated addition sentence for equal groups (e.g., 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12)
- Solve repeated addition problems by adding
- Connect equal groups from Lesson 1 to addition sentences

## Prerequisites
- Equal groups (Lesson 1)
- Addition within 20 (fluency)
- Addition within 100 (basic)

## Materials Needed
- Counting objects and cups
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review equal groups and activate addition.

1. "Make 4 groups of 3 cubes. How many total?" (12)
2. "5 + 5 = ?" (10) "5 + 5 + 5 = ?" (15) "How did you figure that out?" (Added 5 three times)
3. "2 + 2 + 2 = ?" (6)

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show that counting equal groups is the same as adding the same number repeatedly.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Repeated addition:** Adding the same number over and over
- **Addend:** Each number being added

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: From groups to addition.**
Make 3 cups with 4 objects each. "Cup 1 has 4. Cup 2 has 4. Cup 3 has 4. To find the total, I can add: 4 + 4 + 4." Write it on paper. "4 + 4 + 4 = 12."

"Repeated addition is when you add the same number again and again."

**Step 2: Practice reading repeated addition.**
Write: 5 + 5 + 5 + 5. "How many 5s?" (4) "So this is 4 groups of 5." Count: 5, 10, 15, 20. "5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20."

Write: 2 + 2 + 2. "How many 2s?" (3) "3 groups of 2." Count: 2, 4, 6. "2 + 2 + 2 = 6."

**Step 3: From addition to groups.**
Write: 6 + 6 = 12. "How many groups?" (2) "How many in each group?" (6) "Can you make that with cubes?" (2 cups of 6)

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 3 groups of 5. Repeated addition: 5 + 5 + 5 = 15.

**Example 2:** 5 groups of 2. Repeated addition: 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10.

**Example 3:** 4 groups of 10. Repeated addition: 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 40.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student writes and solves repeated addition sentences.

**Activity 1: Groups → addition sentence**
- 2 groups of 4: Write ___ + ___ = ___ (4 + 4 = 8)
- 5 groups of 3: Write ___ + ___ + ___ + ___ + ___ = ___ (3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 15)
- 3 groups of 10: Write ___ + ___ + ___ = ___ (10 + 10 + 10 = 30)

**Activity 2: Addition sentence → groups**
- 7 + 7 + 7 = ? "How many groups?" (3) "How many in each?" (7) "Total?" (21)
- 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = ? (5 groups of 4 = 20)

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build fluency with repeated addition.

**★ On-level:**
1. 3 groups of 2: ___ + ___ + ___ = ___ (2 + 2 + 2 = 6)
2. 4 groups of 5: ___ + ___ + ___ + ___ = ___ (5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20)
3. 2 groups of 8: ___ + ___ = ___ (8 + 8 = 16)
4. 5 + 5 + 5 = ___ (15)
5. 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = ___ (12)
6. 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 = ___ (50)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. "There are 4 bags. Each bag has 6 oranges. Write a repeated addition sentence and solve." (6 + 6 + 6 + 6 = 24)
8. "Write a repeated addition sentence that equals 12 using only 4s." (4 + 4 + 4 = 12)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "Write two different repeated addition sentences that equal 20." (Examples: 10+10, 5+5+5+5, 4+4+4+4+4, 2+2+2+2+2+2+2+2+2+2)
10. "6 + 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 = ___." (30) This one is harder! Can you use what you know to figure it out?

### Interleaved Review
- "53 − 8 = ___" (45) *(Grade 2 Unit 1 or Grade 1 Unit 3)*
- "10 more than 78 = ___" (88)

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student connects groups to repeated addition.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 3 groups of 4. Write the repeated addition sentence and solve. (4 + 4 + 4 = 12)
2. 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = ___ (25)

**Mastery = Both correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Go back to physical groups. Line up cups, count each, write the addition sentence, then solve step by step.
- If the student can make equal groups but can't write the addition sentence, scaffold: "There are 3 cups. Each has 4. So we write 4 ... plus 4 ... plus 4."

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (Skip Counting by 2s, 5s, and 10s).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Repeated addition IS multiplication.** This lesson doesn't name it yet, but every repeated addition sentence is a multiplication fact. 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 is 3 × 4 = 12. Let the student discover efficiency: "Isn't there a faster way than writing all these plus signs?"

**Don't rush to the × sign.** The × sign comes in Lesson 6. Let the student sit with repeated addition and feel the need for a shortcut.

**Common errors:**
- Student adds the number of groups to the group size: "4 groups of 3 = 4 + 3 = 7." Always build physically to show the difference.
- Student loses track in long repeated additions (e.g., 2+2+2+2+2+2). Encourage skip counting or tally marks to track.
