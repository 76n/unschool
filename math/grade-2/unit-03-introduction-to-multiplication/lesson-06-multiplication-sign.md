# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 3, Lesson 6

## Topic
The Multiplication Sign (×)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read and write the × sign
- Understand that × means "groups of" (e.g., 3 × 4 means "3 groups of 4")
- Convert between repeated addition and multiplication notation
- Solve simple multiplication problems using repeated addition or skip counting

## Prerequisites
- Equal groups, repeated addition, arrays (Lessons 1-5)
- Skip counting by 2s, 5s, and 10s (Lesson 3)

## Materials Needed
- Counting objects
- Paper and pencil
- Previously built arrays (or grid paper)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Set up the need for a new symbol.

1. Write on paper: 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = ___. "How many 5s?" (6) "What's the total?" (30)
2. "That was a LOT of plus signs! Imagine if you had 10 groups of 5 — you'd write 5 ten times! There has to be a shorter way..."
3. "Today you're going to learn a brand new math symbol."

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the × sign as a shortcut for repeated addition.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Multiplication:** A way to find the total when you have equal groups
- **Times (×):** The multiplication sign — means "groups of"
- **Factors:** The numbers being multiplied
- **Product:** The answer to a multiplication problem

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the symbol.**
Write: 3 × 4.
"This is called a multiplication sentence. The × is the multiplication sign. We read this as '3 times 4.'"

**Step 2: Define what it means.**
"3 × 4 means 3 groups of 4. Let's build it: 3 cups, 4 objects in each." Build it. Count: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12.

"3 × 4 = 12."

**Step 3: Connect to repeated addition.**
"Instead of writing 4 + 4 + 4, we write 3 × 4. Same thing, shorter."

| Repeated Addition | Multiplication |
|---|---|
| 4 + 4 + 4 | 3 × 4 = 12 |
| 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 | 4 × 5 = 20 |
| 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 | 5 × 2 = 10 |

**Step 4: Reading multiplication.**
"3 × 4: the first number (3) tells how many groups. The second number (4) tells how many in each group. 3 groups of 4."

**Step 5: Important — multiplication is NOT addition.**
"3 × 4 does NOT mean 3 + 4. 3 + 4 = 7. But 3 × 4 = 12. Multiplication gives a bigger answer because you're adding groups, not just two numbers."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** 2 × 5. "2 groups of 5." 5 + 5 = 10. So 2 × 5 = 10.

**Example 2:** 4 × 3. "4 groups of 3." 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12. So 4 × 3 = 12.

**Example 3:** 6 × 10. "6 groups of 10." Skip count: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60. So 6 × 10 = 60.

**Example 4:** 1 × 7. "1 group of 7." That's just 7! 1 × 7 = 7. "Multiplying by 1 gives you the same number."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads, writes, and solves multiplication sentences.

**Activity 1: Repeated addition → multiplication**
- 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = ___ × ___ = ___ (5 × 3 = 15)
- 10 + 10 + 10 = ___ × ___ = ___ (3 × 10 = 30)
- 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = ___ × ___ = ___ (4 × 2 = 8)

**Activity 2: Multiplication → solve**
- 3 × 5 = ? "3 groups of 5." Skip count: 5, 10, 15. (15)
- 4 × 10 = ? (40)
- 2 × 7 = ? 7 + 7 = 14. (14)

**Activity 3: Word problem**
- "There are 5 boxes with 2 pencils in each. Write a multiplication sentence and solve." (5 × 2 = 10)

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice reading, writing, and solving multiplication.

**★ On-level:**
1. 2 × 4 = ___ (8)
2. 3 × 5 = ___ (15)
3. 5 × 10 = ___ (50)
4. 4 × 2 = ___ (8)
5. Write as multiplication: 6 + 6 + 6 = ___ × ___ = ___ (3 × 6 = 18)
6. Write as multiplication: 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = ___ × ___ = ___ (4 × 5 = 20)

**★★ Stretch:**
7. "There are 3 tables with 4 chairs at each. Write a multiplication sentence. How many chairs?" (3 × 4 = 12)
8. "Is 2 × 5 the same as 5 × 2? Build both and check." (Yes — both equal 10.)

**★★★ Challenge:**
9. "What is 7 × 5? Use skip counting by 5s." (35)
10. "0 × 4 = ___. What does '0 groups of 4' mean?" (No groups at all = 0)

### Interleaved Review
- "8 + 8 = ___" (16) *(Unit 2 fact fluency)*
- "Count by 2s to 20." *(Lesson 3)*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student understands the × sign.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 4 × 5 = ___ (20)
2. Write as multiplication: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = ___ × ___ = ___ (6 × 3 = 18)

**Mastery = Both correct. Student correctly interprets × as "groups of."**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to physical groups. Build the multiplication problem with cups/objects every time. Say: "3 × 4 means 3 cups with 4 in each." Then write the sentence next to the physical model.
- If the student confuses × with +, practice pairs: "3 + 4 = ? and 3 × 4 = ?" Build both to show the difference.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (Multiplication as Skip Counting).

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is a landmark lesson.** The × sign is one of the most important symbols in all of math. Introduce it with ceremony — the student is learning a powerful new tool.

**"Groups of" language is the bridge.** Always translate: "3 × 4 means 3 groups of 4." This prevents the × = + confusion.

**The commutative property:** Students will notice that 3 × 4 and 4 × 3 both equal 12. Acknowledge this as a real discovery: "You found something important! The order doesn't change the answer in multiplication." Don't formalize the name yet.

**Common errors:**
- Treating × as +: 3 × 4 = 7. Always rebuild with objects.
- Confusing factor order: "Does 3 × 4 mean 3 groups of 4, or 4 groups of 3?" Both give 12, so either interpretation works. Convention says "3 groups of 4," but don't penalize the reverse.
