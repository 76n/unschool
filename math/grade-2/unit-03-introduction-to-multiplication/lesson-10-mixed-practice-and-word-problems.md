# Math — Grade 2 — Unit 3, Lesson 10

## Topic
Mixed Practice and Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Solve multiplication problems using any strategy (equal groups, arrays, skip counting, repeated addition)
- Solve word problems involving equal groups and arrays
- Identify when a situation calls for multiplication

## Prerequisites
- All prior lessons in this unit (Lessons 1-9)

## Materials Needed
- Counting objects (available, not required)
- Paper and pencil

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick review of all strategies.

1. "3 × 5 = ?" (15) — skip count
2. "2 × 9 = ?" (18) — doubles
3. "4 × 10 = ?" (40) — add a zero
4. "Show 3 × 4 as repeated addition." (4 + 4 + 4 = 12)

"Today you put it all together: equal groups, arrays, skip counting, and the × sign."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model solving word problems with multiplication.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Recognizing multiplication in word problems.**
"Multiplication problems usually involve EQUAL GROUPS. Look for clues like: 'each,' 'every,' 'per,' 'groups of,' 'rows of.'"

**Step 2: Model problem 1.**
"There are 4 bags. Each bag has 5 oranges. How many oranges altogether?"

Think aloud:
- "4 bags — that's the number of groups."
- "5 oranges in each — that's the size of each group."
- "Multiplication: 4 × 5."
- "Skip count by 5s: 5, 10, 15, 20. Answer: 20 oranges."

**Step 3: Model problem 2.**
"A garden has 3 rows of tomato plants. Each row has 6 plants. How many tomato plants?"

Think aloud:
- "3 rows — number of groups."
- "6 in each row — group size."
- "3 × 6 = ? Use repeated addition: 6 + 6 + 6 = 18. Answer: 18 plants."

**Step 4: Model problem 3 (array).**
"Chairs are arranged in 5 rows with 10 chairs in each row. How many chairs?"

- "5 × 10 = 50 chairs."

### Decision Checklist for Students
1. **Are there equal groups?** (If yes → multiplication)
2. **How many groups?** (First factor)
3. **How many in each group?** (Second factor)
4. **Solve using skip counting, repeated addition, or the pattern.**
5. **Check: Does my answer make sense?**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Solve mixed problems together.

**Problem 1:** 6 × 2 = ? (12) "Strategy?" (Doubles: 6 + 6 or skip count by 2s)

**Problem 2:** "There are 7 tables with 5 cups on each. How many cups?" (7 × 5 = 35)

**Problem 3:** Draw an array for 4 × 3 and find the total. (4 rows of 3 = 12)

**Problem 4:** "A pack has 10 markers. You buy 3 packs. How many markers?" (3 × 10 = 30)

**Problem 5:** "Is this a multiplication problem? 'Maya has 12 apples and eats 3.' " (No — that's subtraction: 12 − 3 = 9. The groups aren't equal groups being combined.)

## Independent Practice (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Mixed problem set covering all unit skills.

**★ On-level — Computation:**
1. 3 × 2 = ___ (6)
2. 5 × 5 = ___ (25)
3. 7 × 10 = ___ (70)
4. 4 × 5 = ___ (20)
5. 9 × 2 = ___ (18)
6. 6 × 10 = ___ (60)

**★★ Stretch — Word Problems:**
7. "There are 5 fish tanks. Each tank has 2 fish. How many fish?" (5 × 2 = 10)
8. "A parking lot has 3 rows of cars. Each row has 10 cars. How many cars?" (3 × 10 = 30)
9. "Each student gets 5 crayons. There are 8 students. How many crayons are needed?" (8 × 5 = 40)
10. "Eggs come in cartons of 10. You buy 4 cartons. How many eggs?" (4 × 10 = 40)
11. "Draw an array for 2 × 7. Write the repeated addition sentence." (7 + 7 = 14)

**★★★ Challenge:**
12. "Write your own multiplication word problem using 3 × 5. Then solve it." (Student-generated; answer: 15)
13. "A store sells stickers in packs of 5. Maria buys 6 packs. Her brother buys 4 packs. How many stickers do they have altogether?" (6 × 5 = 30, 4 × 5 = 20, total = 50)

### Interleaved Review
- "100 − 40 = ___" (60)
- "How many tens and ones in 85?" (8 tens, 5 ones)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Final unit readiness check.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. 4 × 5 = ___ (20)
2. 8 × 2 = ___ (16)
3. "There are 6 boxes with 10 pencils in each. How many pencils?" (60)

**Mastery = All three correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify whether the gap is conceptual (doesn't understand "groups of") or computational (can't skip count efficiently).
- Conceptual: return to Lesson 1 (equal groups) and Lesson 6 (× sign).
- Computational: practice skip counting daily (Lesson 3) and specific fact families (Lessons 8-9).

### If Mastery Is Met
Proceed to the Unit Assessment.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson is about putting it all together.** Watch for:
- Can the student identify multiplication situations in word problems?
- Can the student choose an efficient strategy?
- Can the student distinguish multiplication from addition and subtraction?

**Not every problem is multiplication.** Include a few non-multiplication problems in discussion to keep the student thinking critically rather than assuming every problem in a "multiplication unit" requires multiplication.

**Celebrate progress.** The student has gone from counting objects one by one to using multiplication — a huge conceptual leap. Acknowledge this growth.
