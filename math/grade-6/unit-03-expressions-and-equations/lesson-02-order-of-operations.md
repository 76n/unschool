# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 3, Lesson 2

## Topic
Order of Operations

## Learning Objectives
- Apply the order of operations (PEMDAS/GEMDAS) to evaluate multi-step numerical expressions.
- Correctly handle grouping symbols (parentheses, brackets) before other operations.
- Evaluate expressions containing exponents within the order of operations.
- Explain why a standard order of operations is necessary for consistency.

## Prerequisites
- Writing numerical expressions from word phrases (Lesson 1).
- Understanding of exponent notation and evaluating simple powers (Lesson 1).
- Fluency with the four basic operations on whole numbers.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- PEMDAS reference card (optional, for struggling learners).
- Paper and pencil.

**Standards:** 6.EE.1 — Write and evaluate numerical expressions involving whole-number exponents.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Spark debate about the need for a standard order of operations.

1. Write on the board: 3 + 4 x 2.
2. Say: "Solve this in your head. What answer did you get?"
3. Some students may say 14 (adding first: 3 + 4 = 7, then 7 x 2 = 14). Others may say 11 (multiplying first: 4 x 2 = 8, then 3 + 8 = 11).
4. Say: "We got two different answers from the same expression. That would be a problem if we were building a bridge or calculating medicine dosages. Math needs one right answer, which is why mathematicians agreed on a standard set of rules for which operations to perform first. These rules are called the order of operations."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Order of operations** — The agreed-upon rules for the sequence in which operations are performed when evaluating an expression.
- **PEMDAS** — A mnemonic for the order of operations: Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication/Division, Addition/Subtraction.
- **GEMDAS** — An alternative mnemonic: Grouping symbols, Exponents, Multiplication/Division, Addition/Subtraction. (Preferred because it includes brackets and braces, not just parentheses.)
- **Grouping symbols** — Symbols that indicate operations to be performed first: parentheses ( ), brackets [ ], braces { }.
- **Evaluate** — To find the numerical value of an expression.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce the hierarchy.**

Write the order of operations on the board:

1. **G** — Grouping symbols (parentheses, brackets, braces): Do what is inside first.
2. **E** — Exponents: Evaluate any powers.
3. **M/D** — Multiplication and Division: Work left to right.
4. **A/S** — Addition and Subtraction: Work left to right.

Say: "Here is the critical thing most people get wrong: multiplication and division are at the SAME level. You do not always multiply before you divide. You work left to right, doing whichever comes first. The same is true for addition and subtraction — they are at the same level, and you work left to right."

**Step 2 — Demonstrate with the warm-up problem.**

Write: 3 + 4 x 2.

Say: "No grouping symbols. No exponents. We have multiplication and addition. Multiplication comes before addition in the order of operations. So: 4 x 2 = 8, then 3 + 8 = 11. The correct answer is 11."

Write: (3 + 4) x 2.

Say: "Now the parentheses change everything. We do what is inside the parentheses first: 3 + 4 = 7. Then 7 x 2 = 14. Parentheses override the default order."

**Step 3 — Demonstrate with exponents.**

Write: 2 + 3^2 x 4.

Say: "Let us work through this step by step. Are there grouping symbols? No. Are there exponents? Yes: 3^2 = 9. Now we have 2 + 9 x 4. Multiplication before addition: 9 x 4 = 36. Then 2 + 36 = 38."

**Step 4 — Demonstrate left-to-right for same-level operations.**

Write: 24 ÷ 6 x 2.

Say: "Multiplication and division are the same level. We go left to right. 24 ÷ 6 = 4, then 4 x 2 = 8. The answer is 8, NOT 24 ÷ 12 = 2."

Write: 15 - 3 + 7.

Say: "Addition and subtraction are the same level. Left to right: 15 - 3 = 12, then 12 + 7 = 19."

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Expression with parentheses and multiplication.**

Evaluate: 5 x (8 - 3) + 7.

- Step 1 (Grouping): 8 - 3 = 5. Now we have: 5 x 5 + 7.
- Step 2 (No exponents.)
- Step 3 (Multiplication): 5 x 5 = 25. Now we have: 25 + 7.
- Step 4 (Addition): 25 + 7 = 32.
- Answer: **32**.

**Example B: Expression with exponents, multiplication, and addition.**

Evaluate: 4^2 - 2 x 3 + 1.

- Step 1 (No grouping symbols.)
- Step 2 (Exponents): 4^2 = 16. Now we have: 16 - 2 x 3 + 1.
- Step 3 (Multiplication): 2 x 3 = 6. Now we have: 16 - 6 + 1.
- Step 4 (Addition/Subtraction, left to right): 16 - 6 = 10, then 10 + 1 = 11.
- Answer: **11**.

**Example C: Nested grouping symbols.**

Evaluate: 2 x [3 + (10 - 6)^2].

- Step 1 (Innermost grouping first): 10 - 6 = 4. Now we have: 2 x [3 + 4^2].
- Step 2 (Exponents inside the brackets): 4^2 = 16. Now we have: 2 x [3 + 16].
- Step 3 (Brackets): 3 + 16 = 19. Now we have: 2 x 19.
- Step 4 (Multiplication): 2 x 19 = 38.
- Answer: **38**.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Evaluate each expression. Show each step.

1. 8 + 2 x 5
   - Answer: 8 + 10 = **18**

2. (8 + 2) x 5
   - Answer: 10 x 5 = **50**

3. 12 ÷ 4 + 3^2
   - Answer: 12 ÷ 4 = 3, then 3^2 = 9, so 3 + 9 = **12**

4. 20 - 3 x (4 + 1)
   - Answer: 4 + 1 = 5, then 3 x 5 = 15, then 20 - 15 = **5**

5. 6^2 ÷ (2 + 1) - 7
   - Answer: 6^2 = 36, then 2 + 1 = 3, then 36 ÷ 3 = 12, then 12 - 7 = **5**

6. 48 ÷ 8 x 3
   - Answer: 48 ÷ 8 = 6, then 6 x 3 = **18**

**Facilitator tip:** For problem 6, ask: "Why don't we multiply 8 x 3 first?" to reinforce the left-to-right rule for same-level operations.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Basic Order of Operations

Evaluate each expression. Show all steps.

1. 7 + 3 x 6
2. (7 + 3) x 6
3. 15 - 8 ÷ 2
4. 36 ÷ 6 + 2 x 5
5. 4 x 9 - 3 x 7
6. 100 - 5^2 x 3
7. 18 ÷ 3 x 2 + 4
8. 30 - (6 + 4) x 2

### Part B: Expressions with Exponents and Grouping

9. 2^3 + 4 x 5
10. (2 + 3)^2 - 10
11. 3 x [2 + (8 - 5)^2]
12. 50 - 2 x (3^2 + 1)

### Part C: Word Problems

13. Mia says 12 ÷ 2 x 3 = 2 because she divides 12 by 6. What did she do wrong, and what is the correct answer?

14. Write an expression that uses at least three different operations and evaluates to exactly 20. Show your work to prove it equals 20.

### Answer Key

**Part A**

1. 7 + 3 x 6 = 7 + 18 = **25**
2. (7 + 3) x 6 = 10 x 6 = **60**
3. 15 - 8 ÷ 2 = 15 - 4 = **11**
4. 36 ÷ 6 + 2 x 5 = 6 + 10 = **16**
5. 4 x 9 - 3 x 7 = 36 - 21 = **15**
6. 100 - 5^2 x 3 = 100 - 25 x 3 = 100 - 75 = **25**
7. 18 ÷ 3 x 2 + 4 = 6 x 2 + 4 = 12 + 4 = **16**
8. 30 - (6 + 4) x 2 = 30 - 10 x 2 = 30 - 20 = **10**

**Part B**

9. 2^3 + 4 x 5 = 8 + 20 = **28**
10. (2 + 3)^2 - 10 = 5^2 - 10 = 25 - 10 = **15**
11. 3 x [2 + (8 - 5)^2] = 3 x [2 + 3^2] = 3 x [2 + 9] = 3 x 11 = **33**
12. 50 - 2 x (3^2 + 1) = 50 - 2 x (9 + 1) = 50 - 2 x 10 = 50 - 20 = **30**

**Part C**

13. Mia multiplied 2 x 3 = 6 first, then divided 12 ÷ 6 = 2. But multiplication and division are at the same level, so she should work left to right. The correct solution: 12 ÷ 2 = 6, then 6 x 3 = **18**.

14. Answers will vary. One example: 2 x (3 + 7) = 2 x 10 = 20. Another: 5^2 - 10 ÷ 2 = 25 - 5 = 20. Accept any expression with three or more operations that correctly evaluates to 20 with proper order of operations shown.

## Assessment Check

Evaluate: 3 + 2^2 x (7 - 4).

**Target response:** 7 - 4 = 3, then 2^2 = 4, then 4 x 3 = 12, then 3 + 12 = **15**. If the student gets a different answer, identify which step was done out of order and reteach that part of the hierarchy.

## Extension

- **Insert Parentheses Challenge:** Place parentheses in the expression 2 + 3 x 4 - 1 to make it equal to each of the following: (a) 19 (already correct without parentheses: 2 + 12 - 1 = 13... actually 2 + 3 x 4 - 1 = 13), (b) try to make it equal exactly 15 by placing parentheses. Explore: (2 + 3) x (4 - 1) = 5 x 3 = 15. Find other target values by placing parentheses differently.

- **Order of Operations Story:** Write a real-world story problem where the order of operations matters. For example: "A class has 3 rows of 8 desks, plus the teacher's desk. How many desks total?" (3 x 8 + 1 = 25, not 3 x 9 = 27.) Create two more story problems where parentheses change the meaning.
