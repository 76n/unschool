# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 3, Lesson 7

## Topic
Solving One-Step Equations: Addition and Subtraction

## Learning Objectives
- Solve equations of the form x + a = b by subtracting from both sides.
- Solve equations of the form x - a = b by adding to both sides.
- Understand inverse operations as "undoing" what was done to the variable.
- Check solutions by substituting back into the original equation.

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of equations and solutions (Lesson 6).
- The balance scale metaphor for equations (Lesson 6).
- Fluency with addition and subtraction of whole numbers, fractions, and decimals.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Balance scale diagram (drawn large on the board for repeated reference).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Paper and pencil.

**Standards:** 6.EE.7 — Solve real-world and mathematical problems by writing and solving equations of the form x + p = q and x - p = q for cases in which p, q, and x are all nonnegative rational numbers.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate the balance scale metaphor and build intuition for "undoing" an operation.

1. Draw a balance scale on the board. On the left pan, draw a box (representing the unknown) and 4 small circles (representing 4). On the right pan, draw 10 small circles (representing 10). Write below: ? + 4 = 10.
2. Ask: "What number is in the box to keep the scale balanced?" (6.) "How did you figure that out?" (Students might say "I thought 'what plus 4 is 10'" or "I subtracted 4 from 10.")
3. Say: "You just solved an equation! You figured out that the unknown value is 6. Today we are going to learn a systematic method for solving equations like this — a method that works even when the numbers are harder."
4. Say: "The key idea: if something was added to the variable, we undo it by subtracting. If something was subtracted from the variable, we undo it by adding. These are called inverse operations."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Inverse operations** — Operations that undo each other. Addition and subtraction are inverse operations. Multiplication and division are inverse operations.
- **Isolate the variable** — To get the variable alone on one side of the equation.
- **Solution** — The value of the variable that makes the equation true.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Solving x + a = b (addition equation).**

Write on the board: x + 5 = 12.

Draw the balance scale: left side has "x + 5," right side has "12."

Say: "Our goal is to get x by itself — to isolate the variable. Right now, x has 5 added to it. To undo adding 5, we subtract 5. But here is the critical rule: whatever we do to one side of the equation, we MUST do to the other side to keep the scale balanced."

Write the solution steps:

```
x + 5 = 12
x + 5 - 5 = 12 - 5    (subtract 5 from both sides)
x + 0 = 7
x = 7
```

Say: "On the left side, +5 and -5 cancel out, leaving just x. On the right side, 12 - 5 = 7. So x = 7."

Say: "Always check your answer. Substitute x = 7 back into the original equation: 7 + 5 = 12. 12 = 12. It checks out."

Redraw the balance scale showing the subtraction from both sides to reinforce the visual.

**Step 2 — Solving x - a = b (subtraction equation).**

Write on the board: x - 3 = 8.

Say: "Now 3 is being subtracted from x. To undo subtraction, we add. Add 3 to both sides."

```
x - 3 = 8
x - 3 + 3 = 8 + 3    (add 3 to both sides)
x = 11
```

Say: "Check: 11 - 3 = 8. 8 = 8. Correct."

**Step 3 — Equations with the variable on the right side.**

Write: 15 = n + 6.

Say: "This is the same type of equation, just written with the variable on the right. It still means n + 6 = 15. Subtract 6 from both sides."

```
15 = n + 6
15 - 6 = n + 6 - 6
9 = n
```

Say: "So n = 9. Check: 15 = 9 + 6 = 15. Correct."

**Step 4 — Equations with fractions and decimals.**

Write: x + 1.5 = 4.

Say: "The same process works with decimals. Subtract 1.5 from both sides."

```
x + 1.5 = 4
x + 1.5 - 1.5 = 4 - 1.5
x = 2.5
```

Say: "Check: 2.5 + 1.5 = 4. Correct."

Write: y - 1/3 = 2/3.

Say: "And with fractions. Add 1/3 to both sides."

```
y - 1/3 = 2/3
y - 1/3 + 1/3 = 2/3 + 1/3
y = 3/3 = 1
```

Say: "Check: 1 - 1/3 = 2/3. Correct."

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Addition equation.**

Solve: n + 14 = 25.

- Subtract 14 from both sides: n + 14 - 14 = 25 - 14.
- n = 11.
- Check: 11 + 14 = 25. 25 = 25. Correct.
- Answer: **n = 11**.

**Example B: Subtraction equation.**

Solve: y - 9 = 17.

- Add 9 to both sides: y - 9 + 9 = 17 + 9.
- y = 26.
- Check: 26 - 9 = 17. 17 = 17. Correct.
- Answer: **y = 26**.

**Example C: Equation with a decimal.**

Solve: m + 3.7 = 10.

- Subtract 3.7 from both sides: m = 10 - 3.7.
- m = 6.3.
- Check: 6.3 + 3.7 = 10. 10 = 10. Correct.
- Answer: **m = 6.3**.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Solve each equation. Show your steps and check your answer.

1. x + 8 = 15
   - Answer: x = 15 - 8 = **7**. Check: 7 + 8 = 15.

2. n - 6 = 10
   - Answer: n = 10 + 6 = **16**. Check: 16 - 6 = 10.

3. y + 23 = 40
   - Answer: y = 40 - 23 = **17**. Check: 17 + 23 = 40.

4. m - 2.5 = 7.5
   - Answer: m = 7.5 + 2.5 = **10**. Check: 10 - 2.5 = 7.5.

5. 20 = a + 13
   - Answer: a = 20 - 13 = **7**. Check: 20 = 7 + 13 = 20.

6. x + 1/4 = 3/4
   - Answer: x = 3/4 - 1/4 = **1/2**. Check: 1/2 + 1/4 = 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4.

**Facilitator tip:** If the student tries to guess-and-check, redirect: "Great, you found the answer! Now let me show you the algebraic method — subtract from both sides — because it will work even when the numbers are too big or complicated to guess."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Addition Equations

Solve each equation. Show your work and check your answer.

1. x + 9 = 21
2. n + 15 = 32
3. a + 4.2 = 9.8
4. y + 2/5 = 4/5
5. 30 = p + 18

### Part B: Subtraction Equations

Solve each equation. Show your work and check your answer.

6. x - 7 = 14
7. m - 11 = 25
8. y - 1.6 = 3.4
9. k - 3/8 = 5/8
10. 42 = n - 8

### Part C: Word Problems

11. Sara has some money. She earns $15 more from chores. Now she has $47. Write and solve an equation to find how much money Sara started with.

12. A bookshelf had some books. After donating 12 books, there are 35 books left. Write and solve an equation to find how many books were on the shelf originally.

13. The temperature was t degrees in the morning. It rose 8 degrees by noon, reaching 72 degrees. Write and solve an equation to find the morning temperature.

### Answer Key

**Part A**

1. x + 9 = 21. Subtract 9: x = 21 - 9 = **12**. Check: 12 + 9 = 21.
2. n + 15 = 32. Subtract 15: n = 32 - 15 = **17**. Check: 17 + 15 = 32.
3. a + 4.2 = 9.8. Subtract 4.2: a = 9.8 - 4.2 = **5.6**. Check: 5.6 + 4.2 = 9.8.
4. y + 2/5 = 4/5. Subtract 2/5: y = 4/5 - 2/5 = **2/5**. Check: 2/5 + 2/5 = 4/5.
5. 30 = p + 18. Subtract 18: p = 30 - 18 = **12**. Check: 30 = 12 + 18 = 30.

**Part B**

6. x - 7 = 14. Add 7: x = 14 + 7 = **21**. Check: 21 - 7 = 14.
7. m - 11 = 25. Add 11: m = 25 + 11 = **36**. Check: 36 - 11 = 25.
8. y - 1.6 = 3.4. Add 1.6: y = 3.4 + 1.6 = **5**. Check: 5 - 1.6 = 3.4.
9. k - 3/8 = 5/8. Add 3/8: k = 5/8 + 3/8 = 8/8 = **1**. Check: 1 - 3/8 = 5/8.
10. 42 = n - 8. Add 8: n = 42 + 8 = **50**. Check: 42 = 50 - 8 = 42.

**Part C**

11. Let m = the money Sara started with. Equation: m + 15 = 47. Subtract 15: m = 47 - 15 = **$32**. Sara started with $32.
12. Let b = the original number of books. Equation: b - 12 = 35. Add 12: b = 35 + 12 = **47 books**. There were 47 books originally.
13. Equation: t + 8 = 72. Subtract 8: t = 72 - 8 = **64 degrees**. The morning temperature was 64 degrees.

## Assessment Check

Solve: x - 4.5 = 11.5. Show your steps and check.

**Target response:** x - 4.5 + 4.5 = 11.5 + 4.5. x = **16**. Check: 16 - 4.5 = 11.5. 11.5 = 11.5. Correct. If the student subtracts instead of adding, revisit the inverse operation concept: subtraction is undone by addition.

## Extension

- **Balance Scale Problems:** Draw a balance scale for each equation, showing what is on each side. Then show the scale after you perform the inverse operation on both sides. Do this for: (a) x + 7 = 15, (b) y - 4 = 9, (c) 20 = n + 6.

- **Create Your Own:** Write three equations (one addition, one subtraction, one with a decimal or fraction) that all have the solution x = 10. For example: x + 5 = 15, x - 3 = 7, x + 2.5 = 12.5. Verify each by substituting x = 10.
