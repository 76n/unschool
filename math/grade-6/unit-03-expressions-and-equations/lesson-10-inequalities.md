# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 3, Lesson 10

## Topic
Inequalities

## Learning Objectives
- Understand and use inequality symbols (<, >, <=, >=) to compare quantities.
- Translate real-world constraints into mathematical inequalities.
- Graph inequalities on a number line using open and closed circles.
- Solve simple one-step inequalities using inverse operations and recognize that inequalities have many solutions.

## Prerequisites
- Comparing and ordering integers and rational numbers (Unit 2).
- Solving one-step equations using inverse operations (Lessons 7-8).
- Understanding of the number line, including negative numbers (Unit 2).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Number line handouts (from -10 to +10).
- Colored pencils or markers (for graphing on number lines).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Paper and pencil.

**Standards:** 6.EE.5 — Understand solving an equation or inequality as a process of answering a question: which values from a specified set, if any, make the equation or inequality true? 6.EE.8 — Write an inequality of the form x > c or x < c to represent a constraint or condition in a real-world or mathematical problem. Recognize that inequalities of the form x > c or x < c have infinitely many solutions; represent solutions of such inequalities on number line diagrams.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Connect inequality concepts to real-world experience and prior knowledge of comparing numbers.

1. Ask: "To ride the roller coaster, you must be at least 48 inches tall. Can a person who is 50 inches tall ride?" (Yes.) "Can a person who is 48 inches tall ride?" (Yes.) "Can a person who is 46 inches tall ride?" (No.)
2. Say: "There is not one single height that works — there are MANY heights that work. Any height of 48 inches or more is allowed. In math, we write this as h >= 48. The symbol >= means 'greater than or equal to.'"
3. Say: "An equation like x = 5 has just one solution. But an inequality like x >= 48 has infinitely many solutions — 48, 49, 50, 50.5, 100, and so on. Today we learn how to write, solve, and graph inequalities."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Inequality** — A mathematical sentence that compares two expressions using <, >, <=, or >=.
- **< (less than)** — The value on the left is smaller than the value on the right.
- **> (greater than)** — The value on the left is larger than the value on the right.
- **<= (less than or equal to)** — The value on the left is smaller than or the same as the value on the right.
- **>= (greater than or equal to)** — The value on the left is larger than or the same as the value on the right.
- **Solution set** — The set of all values that make the inequality true.
- **Open circle** — Used on a number line graph to show that a value is NOT included (used with < and >).
- **Closed circle** — Used on a number line graph to show that a value IS included (used with <= and >=).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Inequality symbols and their meanings.**

Write on the board:

| Symbol | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| < | less than | x < 5 means x is less than 5 |
| > | greater than | x > 3 means x is greater than 3 |
| <= | less than or equal to | x <= 7 means x is 7 or less |
| >= | greater than or equal to | x >= 2 means x is 2 or more |

Say: "The pointed end of the symbol always points to the smaller value. Think of it as an arrow pointing to the smaller number."

Say: "The line under the symbol (in <= and >=) means 'or equal to.' It includes the boundary value."

**Step 2 — Translating real-world situations to inequalities.**

Write examples:

- "You must be at least 13 years old to create an account." Let a = age. a >= 13.
- "The speed limit is 55 mph." Let s = speed. s <= 55.
- "The temperature will be below freezing (below 32 degrees F)." Let t = temperature. t < 32.
- "You need more than $20 to buy the game." Let m = money. m > 20.

Say: "Key words: 'at least' means >= (that number or more). 'At most' means <= (that number or less). 'More than' means > (strictly greater). 'Less than' or 'fewer than' means < (strictly less)."

**Step 3 — Graphing inequalities on a number line.**

Draw a number line from 0 to 10.

Say: "Let us graph x > 3."

Draw an open circle at 3 and shade/draw an arrow to the right.

Say: "The open circle at 3 means 3 is NOT included — x must be strictly greater than 3. The shading to the right shows all the values that ARE included: 3.1, 4, 5, 100, and everything else to the right of 3."

Now graph x <= 7.

Draw a closed (filled-in) circle at 7 and shade to the left.

Say: "The closed circle at 7 means 7 IS included — x can be 7 or anything less. The shading to the left shows all values: 7, 6, 5, 0, -3, and everything else to the left of 7."

**Step 4 — Solving simple one-step inequalities.**

Say: "Solving an inequality works just like solving an equation — use inverse operations. The difference is that the answer is not a single number but a range of values."

Write: x + 4 > 10.

```
x + 4 > 10
x + 4 - 4 > 10 - 4    (subtract 4 from both sides)
x > 6
```

Say: "The solution is x > 6. That means any value greater than 6 works. Let us verify: try x = 7. Is 7 + 4 > 10? Is 11 > 10? Yes! Try x = 5. Is 5 + 4 > 10? Is 9 > 10? No — so 5 is correctly excluded."

Write: 3x <= 18.

```
3x <= 18
3x ÷ 3 <= 18 ÷ 3    (divide both sides by 3)
x <= 6
```

Say: "The solution is x <= 6. Any value of 6 or less works."

Say: "Important note for later grades: if you ever multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, the inequality symbol flips direction. We will not encounter that situation much in this course, but it is good to know it exists."

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Translate and graph.**

A movie theater charges reduced prices for children age 12 and under. Write an inequality for the ages that qualify. Graph it.

- Let a = age. Children 12 and under: a <= 12.
- Graph: closed circle at 12, shade to the left.
- Answer: **a <= 12**.

**Example B: Solve and graph.**

Solve: x - 5 >= 3. Graph the solution.

- Add 5 to both sides: x >= 3 + 5 = 8.
- Graph: closed circle at 8, shade to the right.
- Answer: **x >= 8**.

**Example C: Solve a multiplication inequality.**

Solve: 2x < 14. Graph the solution.

- Divide both sides by 2: x < 7.
- Graph: open circle at 7, shade to the left.
- Verify: try x = 6: 2(6) = 12 < 14. Yes. Try x = 7: 2(7) = 14. Is 14 < 14? No — 7 is correctly excluded.
- Answer: **x < 7**.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Write an inequality: "You must be at least 16 years old to drive." Let a = age.
   - Answer: **a >= 16**

2. Write an inequality: "The box can hold at most 50 pounds." Let w = weight.
   - Answer: **w <= 50**

3. Graph x > 4 on a number line.
   - Answer: Open circle at 4, shade to the right.

4. Graph x <= 2 on a number line.
   - Answer: Closed circle at 2, shade to the left.

5. Solve: x + 7 > 15. Graph the solution.
   - Answer: x > 15 - 7 = **x > 8**. Open circle at 8, shade right.

6. Solve: n/4 >= 3. Graph the solution.
   - Answer: n >= 3 x 4 = **n >= 12**. Closed circle at 12, shade right.

**Facilitator tip:** For the graphing problems, physically draw the number line and have the student place the circle and shade the correct direction. Ask: "Is this number included?" to determine open vs. closed circle.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Writing Inequalities

Write an inequality for each situation. Define your variable.

1. A roller coaster requires riders to be taller than 42 inches.
2. You can spend at most $25 on a gift.
3. A swimming pool must have a temperature of at least 75 degrees Fahrenheit.
4. To qualify for free shipping, your order must be more than $35.
5. A parking garage has a height limit of 6 feet.

### Part B: Graphing Inequalities

Graph each inequality on a number line.

6. x > 5
7. x <= 3
8. x >= -2
9. x < 0

### Part C: Solving Inequalities

Solve each inequality and graph the solution.

10. x + 3 < 10
11. x - 6 >= 4
12. 5x > 25
13. x/2 <= 8
14. n + 1.5 > 6

### Part D: Word Problems

15. Tyler has saved $38 for a new video game that costs at least $50. Write and solve an inequality to find how much more money Tyler needs.

16. A bag of candy can hold no more than 20 pieces. There are already 13 pieces in the bag. Write and solve an inequality to find how many more pieces can be added.

### Answer Key

**Part A**

1. Let h = height in inches. **h > 42**.
2. Let c = cost in dollars. **c <= 25**.
3. Let t = temperature in degrees Fahrenheit. **t >= 75**.
4. Let o = order total in dollars. **o > 35**.
5. Let h = vehicle height in feet. **h <= 6**.

**Part B**

6. x > 5: Open circle at 5, shade right.
7. x <= 3: Closed circle at 3, shade left.
8. x >= -2: Closed circle at -2, shade right.
9. x < 0: Open circle at 0, shade left.

**Part C**

10. x + 3 < 10. Subtract 3: **x < 7**. Open circle at 7, shade left.
11. x - 6 >= 4. Add 6: **x >= 10**. Closed circle at 10, shade right.
12. 5x > 25. Divide by 5: **x > 5**. Open circle at 5, shade right.
13. x/2 <= 8. Multiply by 2: **x <= 16**. Closed circle at 16, shade left.
14. n + 1.5 > 6. Subtract 1.5: **n > 4.5**. Open circle at 4.5, shade right.

**Part D**

15. Let m = additional money needed. 38 + m >= 50. Subtract 38: m >= 12. Tyler needs **at least $12 more**. Graph: closed circle at 12, shade right.

16. Let p = pieces that can be added. 13 + p <= 20. Subtract 13: p <= 7. **At most 7 more pieces** can be added. Graph: closed circle at 7, shade left (and the domain is also p >= 0 since you cannot add negative pieces).

## Assessment Check

Solve and graph: x - 3 > 5.

**Target response:** Add 3 to both sides: x > 8. Graph: open circle at 8, shade to the right. If the student uses a closed circle, ask: "Is x = 8 a solution? Let us check: 8 - 3 = 5. Is 5 > 5? No, 5 is not greater than 5 — it is equal. So 8 is not included, and we use an open circle."

## Extension

- **Compound Inequalities (Introduction):** Some situations have two constraints at once. For example: "A healthy room temperature is between 65 and 75 degrees." This means t >= 65 AND t <= 75. We can write this as 65 <= t <= 75. Try graphing this on a number line (closed circles at both 65 and 75, shade between them). Write compound inequalities for: (a) Ages that can ride a ride (must be at least 6 and at most 14). (b) Prices you can afford (more than $5 but at most $20).

- **Inequality vs. Equation Comparison:** For the equation x + 3 = 10 and the inequality x + 3 < 10, solve both. Then answer: How are their solutions different? (The equation has one solution: x = 7. The inequality has infinitely many: any x less than 7.) Graph both on the same number line using different colors.
