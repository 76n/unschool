# Unit Assessment — Grade 6 Math, Unit 3: Expressions and Equations

**Grade:** 6
**Unit:** 3 — Expressions and Equations
**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** Written; facilitator reads word problems aloud if needed
**Estimated Duration:** 35-45 minutes
**Materials:** Pencil, scratch paper, number line handout. No calculators.

---

## Assessment Overview

| Section | Skill Assessed | Lesson Source | Points |
|---------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| 1 — Writing and Evaluating Expressions | Write numerical/algebraic expressions from word phrases, evaluate with order of operations and substitution | Lessons 1-4 | 12 |
| 2 — Equivalent Expressions | Apply distributive property, combine like terms, identify equivalent expressions | Lesson 5 | 10 |
| 3 — Solving One-Step Equations | Solve addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division equations using inverse operations | Lessons 6-8 | 12 |
| 4 — Inequalities | Write, solve, and graph inequalities on a number line | Lesson 10 | 8 |

| — | **Total** | — | **50** |

**Mastery threshold:** 80% (40 of 50 points) AND at least 50% in each section.

## Instructions

Read each problem carefully. Show all of your work in the space provided. Write answers in simplest form unless otherwise stated. You may use the number line handout for Section 4. No calculators are allowed.

## Section 1: Writing and Evaluating Expressions (12 points)

**1.** Write a numerical or algebraic expression for each word phrase. **(1 point each)**

(a) The product of 9 and the sum of 3 and 7

(b) 6 less than a number n

(c) Twice a number y, increased by 5

(d) The quotient of 48 and the difference of 10 and 4

**2.** Write each expression using an exponent, or evaluate the given exponent. **(1 point each)**

(a) 7 x 7 x 7 (write using an exponent)

(b) Evaluate: 2^5

**3.** Evaluate each expression using the order of operations. Show all steps. **(2 points each)**

(a) 4 + 3 x (8 - 2)

(b) 5^2 - 6 x 3 + 1

**4.** Evaluate the expression 3x + 2y when x = 4 and y = 5. Show the substitution step. **(2 points)**

## Answer Key

### Section 1: Writing and Evaluating Expressions (12 points)

**1(a):** 9 x (3 + 7) or 9(3 + 7) **(1 point)**

**1(b):** n - 6 **(1 point)**

**1(c):** 2y + 5 **(1 point)**

**1(d):** 48 ÷ (10 - 4) or 48/(10 - 4) **(1 point)**

**2(a):** 7^3 **(1 point)**

**2(b):** 2^5 = 2 x 2 x 2 x 2 x 2 = **32** **(1 point)**

**3(a):** 4 + 3 x (8 - 2)
- Parentheses: 8 - 2 = 6
- Multiplication: 3 x 6 = 18
- Addition: 4 + 18 = **22**
**(2 points: 1 point for correct order of operations steps, 1 point for correct answer)**

**3(b):** 5^2 - 6 x 3 + 1
- Exponent: 5^2 = 25
- Multiplication: 6 x 3 = 18
- Left to right: 25 - 18 + 1 = 7 + 1 = **8**
**(2 points: 1 point for correct order of operations steps, 1 point for correct answer)**

**4:** 3x + 2y when x = 4, y = 5.
- Substitution: 3(4) + 2(5)
- Compute: 12 + 10 = **22**
**(2 points: 1 point for correct substitution, 1 point for correct answer)**

### Section 2: Equivalent Expressions (10 points)

**5(a):** 7x + 3 + 2x - 1 = (7x + 2x) + (3 - 1) = **9x + 2** **(2 points)**

**5(b):** 4a + 5b + 6a - 2b = (4a + 6a) + (5b - 2b) = **10a + 3b** **(2 points)**

**6(a):** 3(x + 8) = 3x + 24 **(2 points)**

**6(b):** 5(2n - 4) = 10n - 20 **(2 points)**

**7:** 4(x + 3) + 2x = 4x + 12 + 2x = (4x + 2x) + 12 = **6x + 12** **(2 points: 1 point for correct distribution, 1 point for correctly combining like terms)**

### Section 3: Solving One-Step Equations (12 points)

**8(a):** x + 13 = 25. Subtract 13 from both sides: x = 25 - 13 = **12**. Check: 12 + 13 = 25. Correct. **(2 points)**

**8(b):** n - 8 = 14. Add 8 to both sides: n = 14 + 8 = **22**. Check: 22 - 8 = 14. Correct. **(2 points)**

**8(c):** 6y = 42. Divide both sides by 6: y = 42 ÷ 6 = **7**. Check: 6(7) = 42. Correct. **(2 points)**

**8(d):** m/5 = 9. Multiply both sides by 5: m = 9 x 5 = **45**. Check: 45/5 = 9. Correct. **(2 points)**

**9:** Substitute x = 7 into 3x - 5 = 16.
- Left side: 3(7) - 5 = 21 - 5 = 16.
- Right side: 16.
- 16 = 16. **Yes, x = 7 is a solution.** **(2 points: 1 point for correct substitution and computation, 1 point for correct conclusion)**

**10(a):** **Expression** (no equal sign) **(1 point)**

**10(b):** **Equation** (has an equal sign) **(1 point)**

### Section 4: Inequalities (8 points)

**11(a):** Let h = height in inches. **h >= 48** **(1 point)**

**11(b):** Let s = speed in mph. **s <= 35** **(1 point)**

**12(a):** x > 4: **Open circle at 4, arrow/shading to the right.** **(2 points: 1 point for correct circle type, 1 point for correct direction)**

**12(b):** x <= -1: **Closed circle at -1, arrow/shading to the left.** **(2 points: 1 point for correct circle type, 1 point for correct direction)**

**13:** x + 6 > 10. Subtract 6 from both sides: **x > 4**. Graph: open circle at 4, shade to the right. **(2 points: 1 point for correct solution, 1 point for correct graph)**

### Section 5: Word Problems (8 points)

**14:** Let n = number of notebooks. Equation: 4n = 28. Divide by 4: n = 28 ÷ 4 = **7 notebooks**. Check: 4(7) = 28. Maria bought 7 notebooks. **(4 points: 1 point for defining variable, 1 point for correct equation, 1 point for correct solution, 1 point for check/context)**

**15:** Let b = books before the donation. Equation: b + 15 = 62. Subtract 15: b = 62 - 15 = **47 books**. Check: 47 + 15 = 62. The library had 47 books before the donation. **(4 points: 1 point for defining variable, 1 point for correct equation, 1 point for correct solution, 1 point for check/context)**

## Error Analysis Guide

| Error Pattern | Example | Likely Cause | Intervention |
|--------------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Exponent treated as multiplication | Writes 2^5 = 10 instead of 32 | Confusing repeated multiplication with simple multiplication | Write out expanded form every time: 2^5 = 2 x 2 x 2 x 2 x 2. Count the factors to confirm the exponent count. Practice 5-6 exponent problems with expanded form. |
| Order of operations ignored | Evaluates 4 + 3 x 6 = 42 instead of 22 | Computes left to right without considering hierarchy | Review the PEMDAS/GEMDAS hierarchy. Circle the operation to perform first before computing. Use color coding: highlight multiplication/division in one color, addition/subtraction in another. |
| Distributes to only one term | Writes 3(x + 8) = 3x + 8 instead of 3x + 24 | Incomplete understanding of distributive property | Use the area model (rectangle with width 3, two sections x and 8). Both sections get multiplied. Practice with arrows: draw an arrow from the outside number to EACH inside term. |
| Combines unlike terms | Simplifies 3x + 5 as 8x | Does not distinguish variable terms from constants | Color code: variable terms in blue, constants in red. Only same-color terms combine. Ask: "Is 3 apples + 5 oranges equal to 8 apple-oranges?" |
| Uses wrong inverse operation | For x + 5 = 12, adds 5 to get x = 17 | Confusion about which operation "undoes" addition | Return to balance scale: "If I add more to the left side, does it get closer to balancing or farther?" Show that adding makes the left side bigger (17) which does not equal 12. Subtracting brings it back to balance. |
| Confuses open and closed circles | Uses closed circle for x > 4 | Unsure when to include boundary value | Mnemonic: "Equal sign under the inequality symbol = closed (filled-in) circle. No equal sign = open (hollow) circle." Test the boundary value each time: does x = 4 satisfy x > 4? No (4 is not > 4), so use open circle. |
| Writes inequality backwards | Writes 48 >= h instead of h >= 48 for "at least 48 inches" | Reads inequality from wrong direction | Always read the inequality as a sentence: "h >= 48" reads "h is greater than or equal to 48." Practice reading 5 inequalities aloud and checking they match the situation. |
| Equation set up incorrectly for word problem | For "15 more than a number is 62," writes 15 + 62 = n | Difficulty translating words to mathematical relationships | Use the four-step scaffold. Underline the unknown. Restate the problem: "Something + 15 = 62." Assign the variable to "something." Write the equation: n + 15 = 62. Check: does the equation say the same thing as the words? |

## Facilitator Observations

During the assessment, note the following to guide future instruction:

- Does the student follow the order of operations automatically, or does she/he need to pause and recall the hierarchy? (Automaticity indicates readiness for more complex expressions in future units.)

- When solving equations, does the student write out the inverse operation step, or does she/he jump to the answer mentally? (Writing steps is essential for preventing errors on harder equations in Grade 7.)

- Does the student use the balance scale mental model when solving equations, or has she/he transitioned to a purely procedural approach? (Both are acceptable, but conceptual understanding should underpin the procedure.)

- Does the student correctly distinguish between open and closed circles on number line graphs? (This distinction carries forward to graphing systems of inequalities in later courses.)

- On word problems, does the student define the variable before writing the equation, or does she/he jump straight to computation? (Defining the variable first builds a habit that is critical for multi-step problems in Grade 7.)

- Which sections does the student complete most quickly and most slowly? (Speed patterns can reveal both strengths and areas of uncertainty, informing differentiation for Unit 4.)
