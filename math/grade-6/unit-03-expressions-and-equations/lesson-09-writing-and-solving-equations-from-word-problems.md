# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 3, Lesson 9

## Topic
Writing and Solving Equations from Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the unknown quantity in a word problem and assign a variable to it.
- Translate a real-world situation into a one-step equation.
- Solve the equation using inverse operations.
- Interpret the solution in the context of the original problem and verify that it makes sense.

## Prerequisites
- Solving one-step equations with all four operations (Lessons 7-8).
- Translating word phrases into algebraic expressions (Lesson 3).
- Understanding of the relationship between operations and real-world actions.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards (for a sorting/matching activity, optional).
- Paper and pencil.

**Standards:** 6.EE.6 — Use variables to represent numbers and write expressions when solving a real-world or mathematical problem; understand that a variable can represent an unknown number. 6.EE.7 — Solve real-world and mathematical problems by writing and solving equations of the form x + p = q and px = q for cases in which p, q, and x are all nonnegative rational numbers.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Practice identifying the operation embedded in a real-world situation.

1. Read aloud: "A shirt costs $12 more than a pair of socks." Ask: "What operation connects the shirt price and the socks price?" (Addition — the shirt price is the socks price plus $12.)
2. Read aloud: "There are 5 equal teams with a total of 30 players." Ask: "What operation connects the number of teams, players per team, and total?" (Multiplication — 5 times the players per team equals 30.)
3. Read aloud: "After spending $8, you have $15 left." Ask: "What operation is happening?" (Subtraction — your starting amount minus $8 equals $15.)
4. Say: "Today we put it all together: we read a word problem, figure out the unknown, write an equation, solve it, and then make sure our answer makes sense in the real world."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Unknown** — The quantity we do not know and want to find.
- **Variable** — The letter we use to represent the unknown.
- **Equation** — A mathematical sentence that states two expressions are equal.
- **Context check** — Verifying that the solution makes sense in the real-world situation (e.g., no negative ages, no fractional people).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — The four-step process.**

Write on the board:

1. **Read and Identify:** What is unknown? Assign a variable.
2. **Translate:** Write an equation that represents the relationship.
3. **Solve:** Use inverse operations.
4. **Check:** Substitute back AND ask: "Does this answer make sense in the real world?"

Say: "We are going to follow these four steps every time. The hardest part is usually Step 2 — figuring out which operation connects the quantities. Let us practice."

**Step 2 — Demonstrate with an addition/subtraction problem.**

Read aloud: "Elena has some stickers. She gives away 9 stickers and has 24 left. How many stickers did she start with?"

Say: "Step 1: The unknown is the number of stickers Elena started with. Let s = stickers she started with."

Say: "Step 2: She started with s stickers, gave away 9, and has 24 left. That means: s - 9 = 24."

Say: "Step 3: Solve. Add 9 to both sides: s = 24 + 9 = 33."

Say: "Step 4: Check. 33 - 9 = 24. Yes! And 33 stickers is a reasonable number for someone to have. The answer is 33 stickers."

**Step 3 — Demonstrate with a multiplication/division problem.**

Read aloud: "A box of granola bars contains 8 bars. You buy enough boxes to have 56 granola bars. How many boxes did you buy?"

Say: "Step 1: The unknown is the number of boxes. Let b = number of boxes."

Say: "Step 2: Each box has 8 bars, and you have b boxes, giving 56 bars total. Equation: 8b = 56."

Say: "Step 3: Solve. Divide both sides by 8: b = 56 ÷ 8 = 7."

Say: "Step 4: Check. 8(7) = 56. Yes! And 7 boxes is a reasonable number. The answer is 7 boxes."

**Step 4 — A common pitfall: choosing the wrong operation.**

Read aloud: "Marcus is 3 years older than his sister. Marcus is 14 years old. How old is his sister?"

Say: "Some students might write 14 + 3 = 17. But wait — Marcus is OLDER, so his sister is YOUNGER. Let s = sister's age. Marcus is 3 years older than his sister: s + 3 = 14. Solve: s = 14 - 3 = 11. His sister is 11 years old."

Say: "Always re-read the problem after writing the equation to make sure it matches the situation."

### Worked Examples

**Example A: Subtraction situation.**

Jordan scored some points in a basketball game. After the scorekeeper corrected a 4-point error (removing 4 points), his score was 18. How many points did Jordan originally score?

- Step 1: Let p = Jordan's original score.
- Step 2: p - 4 = 18.
- Step 3: Add 4: p = 18 + 4 = 22.
- Step 4: Check: 22 - 4 = 18. Correct. 22 points is reasonable.
- Answer: **Jordan originally scored 22 points.**

**Example B: Division situation.**

A pizza is cut into equal slices. Each person at the table gets 3 slices, and there are 8 people. How many slices does the pizza have?

- Step 1: Let s = total number of slices.
- Step 2: s ÷ 8 = 3 (total slices divided equally among 8 people gives 3 each).
- Step 3: Multiply by 8: s = 3 x 8 = 24.
- Step 4: Check: 24 ÷ 8 = 3. Correct. 24 slices is a lot but possible for a large pizza!
- Answer: **The pizza has 24 slices.**

**Example C: Multiplication with decimals.**

Movie tickets cost $8.50 each. A group spent $42.50 on tickets. How many tickets did they buy?

- Step 1: Let t = number of tickets.
- Step 2: 8.50t = 42.50.
- Step 3: Divide by 8.50: t = 42.50 ÷ 8.50 = 5.
- Step 4: Check: 8.50(5) = 42.50. Correct. 5 tickets makes sense (whole number of tickets).
- Answer: **They bought 5 tickets.**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

For each problem, write an equation and solve. Show all four steps.

1. A book has 200 pages. You have read some pages and have 75 pages left. How many pages have you read?
   - Let p = pages read. p + 75 = 200. Solve: p = 200 - 75 = **125 pages**.

2. You split a bag of 42 marbles equally among 6 friends. How many marbles does each friend get?
   - Let m = marbles per friend. 6m = 42. Solve: m = 42 ÷ 6 = **7 marbles**.

3. After adding 2.5 liters of water to a tank, the tank holds 10 liters. How much water was in the tank before?
   - Let w = water before. w + 2.5 = 10. Solve: w = 10 - 2.5 = **7.5 liters**.

4. A rope is cut into 4 equal pieces. Each piece is 3.5 feet long. How long was the rope originally?
   - Let r = original length. r/4 = 3.5. Solve: r = 3.5 x 4 = **14 feet**.

5. Keisha is twice as old as her brother. Keisha is 16 years old. How old is her brother?
   - Let b = brother's age. 2b = 16. Solve: b = 16 ÷ 2 = **8 years old**.

**Facilitator tip:** For problem 5, some students might write b = 16 x 2 = 32. Ask: "If Keisha is twice as old as her brother, should her brother be older or younger than her?" This context check reveals the error immediately.

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Write and Solve

Write an equation for each situation and solve it. Show all four steps.

1. A bag of apples weighs 5 pounds. After eating some apples, the bag weighs 3.2 pounds. How many pounds of apples were eaten?

2. There are 28 students in a class. They form equal groups of 4 for a project. How many groups are there?

3. A savings account has $135. After depositing more money, the account has $200. How much was deposited?

4. Pencils cost $0.75 each. You spend $6.00 on pencils. How many did you buy?

5. A board is 9 feet long. After cutting off a piece, the remaining board is 5.5 feet long. How long was the piece that was cut off?

### Part B: Choose the Correct Equation

For each problem, choose the correct equation from the options given. Then solve.

6. "A number divided by 3 is 12."
   (a) 3n = 12    (b) n/3 = 12    (c) n - 3 = 12

7. "Twice a number is 30."
   (a) n + 2 = 30    (b) n/2 = 30    (c) 2n = 30

8. "A number decreased by 7 is 15."
   (a) n - 7 = 15    (b) 7 - n = 15    (c) n + 7 = 15

### Part C: Multi-Context Word Problems

9. The perimeter of a square is 36 inches. Write and solve an equation to find the side length. (Reminder: perimeter of a square = 4 x side length.)

10. A recipe calls for 3 cups of flour. You only have 1 3/4 cups. How many more cups of flour do you need? Write and solve an equation.

11. Three friends equally share the cost of a gift that costs $27.75. How much does each person pay? Write and solve an equation.

### Answer Key

**Part A**

1. Let a = pounds of apples eaten. 5 - a = 3.2. Subtract from both sides (or rearrange): a = 5 - 3.2 = **1.8 pounds**. Check: 5 - 1.8 = 3.2.
2. Let g = number of groups. 4g = 28. Divide by 4: g = 28 ÷ 4 = **7 groups**. Check: 4(7) = 28.
3. Let d = amount deposited. 135 + d = 200. Subtract 135: d = 200 - 135 = **$65**. Check: 135 + 65 = 200.
4. Let p = number of pencils. 0.75p = 6.00. Divide by 0.75: p = 6.00 ÷ 0.75 = **8 pencils**. Check: 0.75(8) = 6.00.
5. Let c = length cut off. 9 - c = 5.5. Solve: c = 9 - 5.5 = **3.5 feet**. Check: 9 - 3.5 = 5.5.

**Part B**

6. Correct equation: **(b) n/3 = 12**. Solve: n = 12 x 3 = **36**. Check: 36/3 = 12.
7. Correct equation: **(c) 2n = 30**. Solve: n = 30 ÷ 2 = **15**. Check: 2(15) = 30.
8. Correct equation: **(a) n - 7 = 15**. Solve: n = 15 + 7 = **22**. Check: 22 - 7 = 15.

**Part C**

9. Let s = side length. 4s = 36. Divide by 4: s = 36 ÷ 4 = **9 inches**. Check: 4(9) = 36.
10. Let c = cups needed. 1 3/4 + c = 3. Subtract 1 3/4: c = 3 - 1 3/4 = 3 - 7/4 = 12/4 - 7/4 = **5/4 = 1 1/4 cups**. Check: 1 3/4 + 1 1/4 = 3.
11. Let p = each person's share. 3p = 27.75. Divide by 3: p = 27.75 ÷ 3 = **$9.25**. Check: 3(9.25) = 27.75.

## Assessment Check

A school needs 150 chairs for an assembly. They already have 85 chairs set up. How many more chairs do they need? Write and solve an equation.

**Target response:** Let c = additional chairs needed. 85 + c = 150. Subtract 85: c = 150 - 85 = **65 chairs**. Check: 85 + 65 = 150. If the student writes the equation incorrectly (e.g., c - 85 = 150), re-read the problem together and identify which quantity is the total and which are the parts.

## Extension

- **Two-Question Problems:** Create a word problem that requires writing and solving an equation, AND the answer to that problem becomes the input for a second equation. For example: "Mia earned $d per hour for 6 hours and made $72 total. (a) How much did she earn per hour? (b) She spent $4 of her hourly wage on lunch each day. Write an expression for how much of each hour's wage she kept." Solve part (a) to get $12/hour, then part (b) is 12 - 4 = $8 per hour.

- **Write the Problem:** Given the equation 7x = 42, write three different word problems that this equation could represent. For example: (1) "Seven identical bags weigh 42 pounds. How much does each bag weigh?" (2) "A weekly allowance of x dollars over 7 weeks totals $42." (3) "A rectangle with length 7 and width x has an area of 42." Solve to confirm x = 6 for each.
