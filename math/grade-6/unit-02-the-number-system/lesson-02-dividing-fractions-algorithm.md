# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 2, Lesson 2

## Topic
Dividing Fractions: Invert and Multiply

## Learning Objectives
- State and apply the algorithm for dividing fractions: a/b ÷ c/d = a/b x d/c.
- Explain WHY inverting and multiplying produces the correct quotient, connecting the algorithm to the measurement model from Lesson 1.
- Use the "Keep, Change, Flip" mnemonic to correctly set up fraction division problems.
- Divide a fraction by a fraction (including unit fractions, non-unit fractions, and whole numbers written as fractions).
- Check answers using estimation and multiplication.

## Prerequisites
- Conceptual understanding of fraction division as "how many of this fit into that?" (Lesson 1).
- The two-step reasoning model: divide by the unit fraction, then group (Lesson 1).
- Multiplying fractions (numerator x numerator, denominator x denominator).
- Simplifying fractions to lowest terms.
- Converting whole numbers to fraction form (e.g., 5 = 5/1).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Fraction strips from Lesson 1 (for reference and verification).
- Number line handouts (0 to 4, marked in various fractional intervals).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 6.NS.1 — Interpret and compute quotients of fractions, and solve word problems involving division of fractions by fractions, e.g., by using visual fraction models and equations to represent the problem.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review the two-step reasoning from Lesson 1 and create readiness for seeing the pattern that leads to the algorithm.

1. Ask: "Yesterday we found that 4 ÷ 2/3 = 6 using a two-step method. Remind me — what were the two steps?"
   - Step 1: 4 ÷ 1/3 = 12 (how many thirds in 4).
   - Step 2: 12 ÷ 2 = 6 (group the thirds into pairs).
2. Write the two-step process symbolically: 4 ÷ 2/3 → (4 x 3) ÷ 2 = 12 ÷ 2 = 6.
3. Now rewrite: (4 x 3) ÷ 2 = 4 x 3/2 = 4 x 3/2 = 12/2 = 6.
4. Say: "Look at that! Dividing by 2/3 gave the same result as multiplying by 3/2. The fraction 3/2 is what you get when you flip 2/3 upside down. Today we will see why this always works and turn it into a reliable method."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Reciprocal** — The reciprocal of a fraction a/b is b/a. When you multiply a fraction by its reciprocal, the product is 1 (e.g., 2/3 x 3/2 = 6/6 = 1).
- **Invert** — To flip a fraction; swap the numerator and denominator.
- **"Keep, Change, Flip"** — A mnemonic for the fraction division algorithm: Keep the first fraction, Change the operation from ÷ to x, Flip the second fraction.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Establish the pattern with examples from Lesson 1.**

Write three problems solved in Lesson 1 and their results:

| Division Problem | Two-Step Result | Equivalent Multiplication |
|-----------------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| 2 ÷ 1/3 = 6 | 2 x 3 = 6 | 2 x 3/1 = 6 |
| 4 ÷ 2/3 = 6 | (4 x 3) ÷ 2 = 6 | 4 x 3/2 = 6 |
| 6 ÷ 2/5 = 15 | (6 x 5) ÷ 2 = 15 | 6 x 5/2 = 15 |

Ask: "What pattern do you see?" Guide toward: "Every time we divide by a fraction, we get the same answer as multiplying by its reciprocal (flipped version)."

**Step 2 — Explain WHY inverting works.**

Say: "Let's think about why. When we divide by 2/3, we are asking 'how many 2/3s fit into this amount?' Our two-step approach was:

1. First find how many 1/3s fit in (multiply by 3, the denominator).
2. Then group them into sets of 2 (divide by 2, the numerator).

So dividing by 2/3 means: multiply by 3, then divide by 2. That is the same as multiplying by 3/2. And 3/2 is the reciprocal of 2/3."

Write the general rule:

**a/b ÷ c/d = a/b x d/c**

"Dividing by c/d is the same as multiplying by d/c. The two-step reasoning (multiply by denominator, divide by numerator) collapses into one step: multiply by the reciprocal."

**Step 3 — Introduce the "Keep, Change, Flip" mnemonic.**

Write on the board:

**Keep** the first fraction exactly as it is.
**Change** the division sign (÷) to a multiplication sign (x).
**Flip** the second fraction (write its reciprocal).

Walk through an example:

3/4 ÷ 2/5

- **Keep:** 3/4
- **Change:** ÷ becomes x
- **Flip:** 2/5 becomes 5/2

So: 3/4 x 5/2 = 15/8 = 1 7/8.

Check: Does 1 7/8 groups of 2/5 equal 3/4? Verify: 15/8 x 2/5 = 30/40 = 3/4. Yes.

**Step 4 — Practice the algorithm with estimation checks.**

Emphasize: "Before computing, estimate. Ask yourself: should the answer be bigger or smaller than 1?"

Example: 2/3 ÷ 1/4.

- Estimate: 2/3 is larger than 1/4, so more than one 1/4 fits into 2/3. The answer should be greater than 1. Also, 2/3 is about two-and-a-half fourths, so the answer should be around 2 to 3.
- Compute: 2/3 x 4/1 = 8/3 = 2 2/3.
- Check estimate: 2 2/3 is between 2 and 3. Reasonable.
- Verify: 8/3 x 1/4 = 8/12 = 2/3. Correct.

**Step 5 — Handle special cases.**

*Dividing by a whole number:* Write the whole number as a fraction over 1, then flip.

3/5 ÷ 2 = 3/5 ÷ 2/1 = 3/5 x 1/2 = 3/10.

*Dividing a whole number by a fraction:* Write the whole number as a fraction over 1.

5 ÷ 1/3 = 5/1 ÷ 1/3 = 5/1 x 3/1 = 15.

*Dividing a fraction by itself:* The answer is always 1.

3/7 ÷ 3/7 = 3/7 x 7/3 = 21/21 = 1. (Makes sense: exactly one group of 3/7 fits into 3/7.)

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Fraction ÷ fraction.**

1/2 ÷ 3/8

- Estimate: 1/2 = 4/8. How many 3/8s fit into 4/8? About one and a little more.
- Keep, Change, Flip: 1/2 x 8/3 = 8/6 = 4/3 = 1 1/3.
- Verify: 4/3 x 3/8 = 12/24 = 1/2. Correct.
- Estimate check: 1 1/3 is just above 1. Reasonable.

**Example B — Fraction ÷ fraction, requiring simplification.**

4/9 ÷ 2/3

- Estimate: 4/9 is less than 2/3 (since 4/9 < 6/9 = 2/3), so fewer than one 2/3 fits. Answer should be less than 1.
- Keep, Change, Flip: 4/9 x 3/2 = 12/18 = 2/3.
- Verify: 2/3 x 2/3 = 4/9. Correct.
- Estimate check: 2/3 < 1. Reasonable.

**Example C — Whole number ÷ fraction (connecting to Lesson 1).**

3 ÷ 3/4

- Estimate: 3/4 is close to 1, so the answer should be close to 3, but a bit more (since groups are slightly smaller than 1).
- Rewrite: 3/1 ÷ 3/4. Keep, Change, Flip: 3/1 x 4/3 = 12/3 = 4.
- Verify: 4 x 3/4 = 12/4 = 3. Correct.
- Confirm with Lesson 1 model: on a number line 0 to 3 (marked in fourths), count jumps of 3/4: 0→3/4, 3/4→6/4, 6/4→9/4, 9/4→12/4. Four jumps.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. For each, have the student (1) estimate first, (2) apply Keep, Change, Flip, (3) simplify, and (4) check.

1. 3/5 ÷ 1/2
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Is 3/5 larger or smaller than 1/2? So should the answer be more or less than 1?"

2. 5/6 ÷ 5/12
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Before computing, notice that 5/12 is about half of 5/6. What does that tell you about the answer?"

3. 7/8 ÷ 3/4
   - *Facilitator tip:* "After flipping, can you simplify before multiplying? Look for common factors across numerator and denominator."

4. 4 ÷ 2/7
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Rewrite 4 as 4/1 first. Then apply Keep, Change, Flip."

5. 2/3 ÷ 4
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Rewrite 4 as 4/1. What is the reciprocal of 4/1?"

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A — Applying the Algorithm

1. Solve: 3/4 ÷ 1/8.

2. Solve: 2/5 ÷ 3/10.

3. Solve: 5/6 ÷ 2/3.

4. Solve: 7 ÷ 1/4.

### Part B — Estimation and Verification

5. Solve 4/5 ÷ 2/3. Before computing, write an estimate. After computing, verify by multiplying your answer by the divisor.

6. Solve 1/3 ÷ 5/6. Before computing, predict: will the answer be greater than or less than 1? Explain. Then compute and check.

7. A piece of fabric is 3/4 of a yard long. You need strips that are each 1/8 of a yard. How many strips can you cut? Write the division equation and solve.

### Part C — Reasoning and Error Analysis

8. Marco solved 2/3 ÷ 4/5 and got 8/15. He applied Keep, Change, Flip as: 2/3 x 4/5. What mistake did Marco make? What is the correct answer?

9. Two students solve 5/8 ÷ 1/4.
   - Student A says: "5/8 x 4/1 = 20/8 = 5/2 = 2 1/2."
   - Student B says: "5/8 x 1/4 = 5/32."
   - Which student is correct? Explain the error the other student made.

10. Explain in your own words why dividing by 2/3 gives the same result as multiplying by 3/2. You may use words, numbers, or a diagram.

### Answer Key

**Part A**

1. 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 x 8/1 = 24/4 = 6.
2. 2/5 ÷ 3/10 = 2/5 x 10/3 = 20/15 = 4/3 = 1 1/3.
3. 5/6 ÷ 2/3 = 5/6 x 3/2 = 15/12 = 5/4 = 1 1/4.
4. 7 ÷ 1/4 = 7/1 x 4/1 = 28.

**Part B**

5. Estimate: 4/5 is a bit more than 2/3, so the answer is a bit more than 1. Compute: 4/5 x 3/2 = 12/10 = 6/5 = 1 1/5. Verify: 6/5 x 2/3 = 12/15 = 4/5. Correct.
6. Prediction: 1/3 < 5/6, so fewer than one group of 5/6 fits into 1/3. Answer is less than 1. Compute: 1/3 x 6/5 = 6/15 = 2/5. Verify: 2/5 x 5/6 = 10/30 = 1/3. Correct.
7. 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 x 8/1 = 24/4 = 6 strips.

**Part C**

8. Marco flipped the wrong fraction. He should have flipped 4/5 to get 5/4, not kept it as 4/5. Correct solution: 2/3 x 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6.
9. Student A is correct. Student B multiplied by 1/4 instead of flipping it to 4/1. Student B forgot the "Flip" step — she kept the second fraction unchanged instead of taking its reciprocal.
10. Sample explanation: "Dividing by 2/3 asks 'how many 2/3s fit in?' First you find how many thirds fit in (multiply by 3), then you group them in sets of 2 (divide by 2). Multiplying by 3 then dividing by 2 is the same as multiplying by 3/2, which is the reciprocal of 2/3."

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket

1. Solve: 3/5 ÷ 2/7. Show all steps using Keep, Change, Flip.

2. A container holds 5/6 of a gallon of juice. Each glass holds 1/3 of a gallon. How many glasses can be filled? Write a division equation and solve.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct with clear steps = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; identify whether the error is procedural (flipping the wrong fraction) or conceptual (not understanding what to do).
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed; return to the two-step model from Lesson 1 and rebuild the connection to invert-and-multiply.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Go back to the two-step model: "To divide by 2/5, first multiply by 5 (how many fifths?), then divide by 2 (group them)." Write out 3-4 problems using both the two-step method and Keep, Change, Flip side by side until the student sees they always give the same answer. Then have the student practice Keep, Change, Flip with simple problems (unit fraction divisors) before progressing to non-unit fractions.

### If Mastery Is Met
Challenge: "Is it possible for a fraction ÷ a fraction to give an answer less than both fractions? Find an example or explain why not." (Answer: No — the quotient a/b ÷ c/d = (a x d)/(b x c). If a/b > 0 and c/d > 0 and c/d > a/b, then the quotient is less than 1 but not necessarily less than both fractions. For example, 1/6 ÷ 1/2 = 1/3, which is less than 1/2 but greater than 1/6.)
