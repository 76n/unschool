# Unit Assessment — Grade 6 Math, Unit 2: The Number System

**Grade:** 6
**Unit:** 2 — The Number System
**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** Written; facilitator reads word problems aloud if needed
**Estimated Duration:** 35-45 minutes
**Materials:** Pencil, scratch paper, number line and coordinate plane handouts. No calculators.

## Instructions

Read each problem carefully. Show all of your work in the space provided. Write answers in simplest form unless otherwise stated. You may use the number line and coordinate plane handouts to help you. No calculators are allowed.

## Section 1: Dividing Fractions (14 points)

**1.** Compute each quotient. Show your work and simplify your answer. **(2 points each)**

(a) 3/4 ÷ 2/5

(b) 5/6 ÷ 1/3

(c) 2/9 ÷ 4/7

**2.** Compute each quotient. Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first. Show your work and write your answer as a mixed number in simplest form. **(2 points each)**

(a) 2 1/3 ÷ 1/2

(b) 4 1/2 ÷ 1 1/4

**3.** A ribbon is 3/4 of a yard long. You need pieces that are each 3/8 of a yard. How many pieces can you cut from the ribbon? Show your work and write your answer as a complete sentence. **(4 points)**

## Section 2: Integers and the Number Line (10 points)

**4.** Plot each integer on the number line below. Label each point with its letter. **(3 points: 1 point per correct placement)**

```
A = −6      B = 3      C = −1

<----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+---->
   −8   −6   −4   −2    0    2    4    6    8
```

(a) −7 _____ −3

## Answer Key

### Section 1: Dividing Fractions (14 points)

**1(a):** 3/4 ÷ 2/5 = 3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8 = **1 7/8** (2 points)

**1(b):** 5/6 ÷ 1/3 = 5/6 × 3/1 = 15/6 = **5/2 = 2 1/2** (2 points)

**1(c):** 2/9 ÷ 4/7 = 2/9 × 7/4 = 14/36 = **7/18** (2 points)

**2(a):** 2 1/3 ÷ 1/2 = 7/3 ÷ 1/2 = 7/3 × 2/1 = 14/3 = **4 2/3** (2 points)

**2(b):** 4 1/2 ÷ 1 1/4 = 9/2 ÷ 5/4 = 9/2 × 4/5 = 36/10 = 18/5 = **3 3/5** (2 points)

**3:** 3/4 ÷ 3/8 = 3/4 × 8/3 = 24/12 = **2 pieces.** "You can cut 2 pieces from the ribbon." (4 points)

### Section 2: Integers and the Number Line (10 points)

**4:** A = −6 (on the −6 tick mark), B = 3 (halfway between 2 and 4), C = −1 (halfway between −2 and 0). (3 points: 1 point each)

**5(a):** −7 **<** −3 (5(b):) 4 **>** −9 (5(c):) 0 **>** −5 (5(d):) −12 **=** −12 (4 points: 1 point each)

**6(a):** **−8** (6(b):) **15** (6(c):) The number is **0**. Zero is its own opposite because 0 + 0 = 0, and 0 is the only number that is neither positive nor negative. (3 points: 1 point each)

### Section 3: Absolute Value (10 points)

**7(a):** |−9| = **9** (7(b):) |14| = **14** (7(c):) |0| = **0** (3 points: 1 point each)

**8(a):** |−6| = 6, |4| = 4, so 6 **>** 4. Answer: **>**

**8(b):** −5 and |−5| = 5, so −5 **<** 5. Answer: **<**

**8(c):** |−3| = 3 and |3| = 3, so 3 **=** 3. Answer: **=**

**8(d):** |−8| = 8 and −8, so 8 **>** −8. Answer: **>**

(4 points: 1 point each)

**9(a):** The submarine's distance from sea level is |−240| = 240 feet. The helicopter's distance from sea level is |180| = 180 feet. Since 240 > 180, the **submarine is farther from sea level**. (2 points)

**9(b):** Absolute value represents the **distance from sea level** regardless of whether the object is above or below sea level. (1 point)

### Section 4: Rational Numbers (12 points)

**10:** A = −1.5 (halfway between −1 and −2), B = 3/4 = 0.75 (between 0 and 1, closer to 1), C = −2 3/4 = −2.75 (between −3 and −2, closer to −3), D = 1.25 (between 1 and 2, closer to 1). (4 points: 1 point each)

**11:** Convert all to decimals: −2/3 ≈ −0.667, 0.5, −1.75, 3/4 = 0.75, −0.5, 1. Ordered from least to greatest: **−1.75, −2/3, −0.5, 0.5, 3/4, 1** (4 points: full credit for correct order with conversion work shown; deduct 1 point per number out of place, minimum 0)

**12(a):** −3/4 = −0.75, −2/3 ≈ −0.667. Since −0.75 < −0.667 (farther from zero), **−3/4 < −2/3**. Answer: **<** (2 points)

**12(b):** −1.25 = −5/4. Since both equal the same value, **−1.25 = −5/4**. Answer: **=** (2 points)

### Section 5: Coordinate Plane (14 points)

**13:** A(3, −4) in Quadrant IV, B(−5, 2) in Quadrant II, C(−3, −6) in Quadrant III, D(4, 5) in Quadrant I. (4 points: 1 point each)

**14(a):** (−7, 4) — x is negative, y is positive — **Quadrant II**

**14(b):** (2, −3) — x is positive, y is negative — **Quadrant IV**

**14(c):** (0, −5) — x is 0 — **on the y-axis** (not in any quadrant)

(3 points: 1 point each)

**15(a):** Reflect (5, 3) across the x-axis → **(5, −3)** (y changes sign)

**15(b):** Reflect (5, 3) across the y-axis → **(−5, 3)** (x changes sign)

**15(c):** Reflect (−2, −7) across the x-axis → **(−2, 7)** (y changes sign)

**15(d):** Reflect (−2, −7) across the y-axis → **(2, −7)** (x changes sign)

(4 points: 1 point each)

**16(a):** Points (4, 3) and (4, −5) share x = 4. Distance = |3 − (−5)| = |3 + 5| = |8| = **8 units**. (1.5 points)

**16(b):** Points (−6, 2) and (3, 2) share y = 2. Distance = |−6 − 3| = |−9| = **9 units**. (1.5 points)

## Error Analysis Guide

| Error Pattern | Example | Likely Cause | Intervention |
|--------------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Inverts the wrong fraction | Computes 3/4 ÷ 2/5 as 4/3 × 2/5 instead of 3/4 × 5/2 | Confusion about which fraction to flip | Drill "Keep-Change-Flip": keep the FIRST fraction, change ÷ to ×, flip the SECOND fraction. Have student circle the second fraction before flipping. |
| Forgets to convert mixed numbers before dividing | Tries to divide 2 1/3 ÷ 1/2 by dividing whole and fraction parts separately | Does not recognize that the invert-and-multiply algorithm requires improper fractions | Always require the first step to be written: "Convert to improper fractions." Practice 5 conversions before attempting division. |
| Believes −10 > −3 because 10 > 3 | Writes −10 > −3 | Applies whole-number comparison rules to negative numbers | Return to the number line. Plot both numbers and ask: "Which is to the right?" Emphasize: for negatives, closer to zero = greater. Practice with 8-10 pairs. |
| Writes \|−9\| = −9 | Claims absolute value of a negative is negative | Confuses the sign of a number with its absolute value | Return to the definition: absolute value = distance from zero. Distance is never negative. Have student draw the number on a number line and count units to zero for 5-6 examples. |
| Confuses a number with its absolute value when comparing | Writes −5 > \|−5\| (thinks they are the same) | Does not distinguish between a negative number and its absolute value | Create a two-column table: "Number" and "Its Absolute Value." Fill in 8 rows. Then compare each pair. Emphasize: a negative number is always less than its own absolute value. |
| Plots (x, y) as (y, x) on the coordinate plane | Plots (3, −4) by going up 3 and right −4 | Reverses the order of coordinates | Reinforce "x is the hallway (horizontal), y is the elevator (vertical)." Have student write "x = ___, y = ___" before plotting. Practice 10 points with explicit labeling. |
| Reflects across the wrong axis | Reflects (5, 3) across the x-axis as (−5, 3) instead of (5, −3) | Confuses which coordinate changes sign during reflection | Mnemonic: "Reflecting across the x-axis changes the y-coordinate, and reflecting across the y-axis changes the x-coordinate — the axis you cross is the one that stays the same." Practice with folded graph paper. |
| Subtracts coordinates incorrectly for distance | Computes distance between (4, 3) and (4, −5) as \|3 − 5\| = 2 instead of \|3 − (−5)\| = 8 | Drops the negative sign when subtracting | Write the subtraction explicitly: 3 − (−5) = 3 + 5 = 8. Reinforce that subtracting a negative means adding. Have student count the distance on the coordinate plane to verify. |
| Does not simplify quotients | Writes 14/36 as the final answer instead of 7/18 | Forgets or does not know how to find GCF for simplification | After every division, add a "Simplify" step. Practice finding GCF for 5-6 fraction pairs. Require all answers in simplest form. |

## Facilitator Observations

During the assessment, note the following to guide future instruction:

- Does the student apply "Keep-Change-Flip" consistently for fraction division, or does she/he hesitate about which fraction to invert? (Hesitation may indicate the procedure is not yet automatic.)

- When comparing negative numbers, does the student sketch a number line or rely on mental reasoning? (Students who skip the number line are more prone to sign errors.)

- Does the student compute absolute values automatically, or does she/he confuse absolute value with the original signed number? (This distinction is critical for Grade 7 operations with integers.)

- When plotting on the coordinate plane, does the student check the signs of coordinates to determine the quadrant before plotting, or does she/he plot by trial and error? (A systematic approach indicates stronger conceptual understanding.)

- Does the student show conversion work when comparing or ordering rational numbers, or does she/he guess at the order? (Guessing will not scale to harder problems in later units.)

- Which sections does the student complete most quickly and most slowly? (Speed patterns can reveal both strengths and areas of uncertainty, informing differentiation for Unit 3.)
