# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 2, Lesson 3

## Topic
Dividing Mixed Numbers

## Learning Objectives
- Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions as a necessary first step for division.
- Divide mixed numbers by fractions, fractions by mixed numbers, and mixed numbers by mixed numbers using the invert-and-multiply algorithm.
- Estimate quotients involving mixed numbers before computing (using whole-number benchmarks).
- Convert improper-fraction results back to mixed numbers in simplest form.
- Solve real-world word problems that require division of mixed numbers.

## Prerequisites
- Converting between mixed numbers and improper fractions fluently (Grade 5).
- Dividing fractions using invert and multiply / Keep, Change, Flip (Lesson 2).
- Multiplying fractions and simplifying products (Grade 5).
- Estimating with fractions by rounding to the nearest whole number or benchmark fraction.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Number line handouts (0 to 6, marked in halves and fourths) for estimation reference.
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.
- Measuring cups or a diagram of measuring cups (optional, for the word problem context).

**Standards:** 6.NS.1 — Interpret and compute quotients of fractions, and solve word problems involving division of fractions by fractions, e.g., by using visual fraction models and equations to represent the problem.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review mixed-number-to-improper-fraction conversion and set the stage for applying the division algorithm to mixed numbers.

1. Quick conversion drill — convert each mixed number to an improper fraction:
   - 2 1/3 = ? (7/3)
   - 1 3/4 = ? (7/4)
   - 3 1/2 = ? (7/2)
   - 4 2/5 = ? (22/5)

2. Ask: "Why do we need to convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before dividing?" Accept responses. Guide toward: "Our Keep, Change, Flip algorithm works on fractions in a/b form. A mixed number is not in that form, so we convert first."

3. Say: "Today we combine two skills you already have: converting mixed numbers and the invert-and-multiply algorithm. By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to divide any combination of fractions, mixed numbers, and whole numbers."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Mixed number** — A number consisting of a whole-number part and a fraction part (e.g., 3 1/2).
- **Improper fraction** — A fraction where the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator (e.g., 7/2).
- **Estimate** — An approximation of the answer, used to check whether a computed result is reasonable.
- **Benchmark** — A familiar, easy-to-use number (like 1, 1/2, 2, 3) used as a reference point for estimation.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Establish the procedure with a clear example.**

Write on the board: **3 1/2 ÷ 1 1/4 = ?**

Say: "We cannot apply Keep, Change, Flip directly to mixed numbers. We need fractions. Let's convert both."

- 3 1/2 = (3 x 2 + 1)/2 = 7/2
- 1 1/4 = (1 x 4 + 3)/4 = 7/4

Now rewrite: 7/2 ÷ 7/4.

Apply Keep, Change, Flip: 7/2 x 4/7 = 28/14 = 2.

Say: "So 3 1/2 ÷ 1 1/4 = 2. Let's check: does this make sense? We are asking 'how many 1 1/4s fit into 3 1/2?' Since 1 1/4 + 1 1/4 = 2 1/2, that is not enough. But 1 1/4 + 1 1/4 + ... wait, let's think more carefully. Two groups of 1 1/4: 2 x 1 1/4 = 2 1/2. That is less than 3 1/2. Hmm, let me recheck."

Recheck: 2 x 1 1/4 = 2 x 5/4 = 10/4 = 2 1/2. But we said the answer is 2. Let me recalculate.

Actually, re-do the conversion: 1 1/4 = (1 x 4 + 1)/4 = 5/4.

Wait — restate for the student: "Let me be careful. 1 1/4 means 1 and 1/4, so (1 x 4 + 1)/4 = 5/4."

Correction on the board: The problem is 3 1/2 ÷ 1 1/4.

- 3 1/2 = 7/2
- 1 1/4 = 5/4

Rewrite: 7/2 ÷ 5/4 = 7/2 x 4/5 = 28/10 = 14/5 = 2 4/5.

Estimate check: 3 1/2 ÷ 1 1/4. Round: roughly 3.5 ÷ 1.25. Since 1.25 x 2 = 2.5 and 1.25 x 3 = 3.75, the answer is between 2 and 3. Our answer of 2 4/5 is between 2 and 3. Reasonable.

Say: "This example shows the complete procedure: (1) estimate, (2) convert to improper fractions, (3) Keep, Change, Flip, (4) multiply, (5) simplify and convert back to a mixed number, (6) check against your estimate."

**Step 2 — Walk through the full procedure as a numbered process.**

Write the **Division of Mixed Numbers Procedure:**

1. **Estimate** — Round each number to the nearest whole number and divide mentally.
2. **Convert** — Rewrite every mixed number (and whole number) as an improper fraction.
3. **Keep, Change, Flip** — Keep the first fraction, change ÷ to x, flip the second fraction.
4. **Multiply** — Multiply numerators together and denominators together.
5. **Simplify** — Reduce the fraction to lowest terms.
6. **Convert back** — If the result is an improper fraction, rewrite it as a mixed number.
7. **Check** — Compare to your estimate. Does the answer make sense?

**Step 3 — Second worked example with a whole-number result.**

Write: **4 ÷ 2/3 = ?**

- Estimate: About 4 ÷ 1 = 4, but 2/3 is less than 1, so the answer should be greater than 4. About 6.
- Convert: 4 = 4/1. The fraction 2/3 stays as is.
- Keep, Change, Flip: 4/1 x 3/2 = 12/2 = 6.
- Simplify: Already simplified.
- Check: 6 x 2/3 = 12/3 = 4. Correct. Matches estimate.

Say: "This is a problem we saw in Lesson 1. Notice we get the same answer using the algorithm. The method works for any combination."

**Step 4 — Third worked example: fraction ÷ mixed number.**

Write: **2/3 ÷ 1 1/2 = ?**

- Estimate: 2/3 is less than 1, and 1 1/2 is greater than 1. So fewer than one 1 1/2 fits into 2/3. The answer is less than 1.
- Convert: 2/3 stays. 1 1/2 = 3/2.
- Keep, Change, Flip: 2/3 x 2/3 = 4/9.
- Simplify: 4/9 is already in lowest terms.
- Convert back: 4/9 is a proper fraction, so no conversion needed.
- Check: 4/9 x 3/2 = 12/18 = 2/3. Correct. 4/9 < 1, which matches the estimate.

**Step 5 — Emphasize the estimation habit.**

Say: "Estimation is your safety net. Many errors in fraction division come from flipping the wrong fraction or making a conversion mistake. If your computed answer doesn't match your estimate, go back and check your work."

Give a quick estimation example: "If someone says 2 1/4 ÷ 3/4 = 1/3, does that make sense?" (No — 2 1/4 is much larger than 3/4, so many 3/4-portions fit. The answer should be around 3, not 1/3.)

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Mixed number ÷ fraction.**

2 2/3 ÷ 1/3 = ?

- Estimate: About 3 ÷ 1/3 = 9. The answer should be close to 8 or 9.
- Convert: 2 2/3 = 8/3. The divisor 1/3 stays.
- Keep, Change, Flip: 8/3 x 3/1 = 24/3 = 8.
- Check: 8 x 1/3 = 8/3 = 2 2/3. Correct.
- Estimate check: 8 is close to 9. Reasonable.

**Example B — Mixed number ÷ mixed number.**

4 1/2 ÷ 1 1/3 = ?

- Estimate: About 5 ÷ 1 = 5, or more precisely, about 4.5 ÷ 1.33 ≈ 3.4. So between 3 and 4.
- Convert: 4 1/2 = 9/2. 1 1/3 = 4/3.
- Keep, Change, Flip: 9/2 x 3/4 = 27/8.
- Simplify: 27/8 is already in lowest terms.
- Convert: 27/8 = 3 3/8.
- Check: 27/8 x 4/3 = 108/24 = 9/2 = 4 1/2. Correct.
- Estimate check: 3 3/8 is between 3 and 4. Reasonable.

**Example C — Word problem.**

A recipe calls for 1 1/4 cups of flour per batch. You have 3 1/2 cups of flour. How many full batches can you make?

- Estimate: About 3.5 ÷ 1.25 ≈ 3 batches.
- Division: 3 1/2 ÷ 1 1/4 = 7/2 ÷ 5/4 = 7/2 x 4/5 = 28/10 = 14/5 = 2 4/5.
- Interpret: 2 4/5 batches means you can make 2 full batches with some flour left over (4/5 of a third batch).
- Check: 2 x 1 1/4 = 2 x 5/4 = 10/4 = 2 1/2 cups used. Remaining: 3 1/2 − 2 1/2 = 1 cup. Is 1 cup enough for another batch? No, you need 1 1/4 cups. So 2 full batches is correct.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

Work through these problems together. For each, require the student to estimate before computing.

1. 1 1/2 ÷ 3/4
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Estimate first: about how many 3/4s fit into 1 1/2? Think: 3/4 + 3/4 = 1 1/2, so the answer should be exactly 2."

2. 3 1/3 ÷ 5/6
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Convert 3 1/3 to an improper fraction. What is 3 x 3 + 1? Then apply Keep, Change, Flip."

3. 2 1/4 ÷ 1 1/2
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Both are mixed numbers, so convert both. Estimate: about 2 ÷ 1.5 ≈ 1.3 or so."

4. 5 ÷ 1 2/3
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Write 5 as 5/1 and convert 1 2/3 to an improper fraction."

5. How many 1 1/4-cup servings are in 3 1/2 cups of yogurt?
   - *Facilitator tip:* "Write the division equation first: 3 1/2 ÷ 1 1/4. Then estimate and solve."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A — Computation

1. Solve: 2 1/2 ÷ 1/4.

2. Solve: 4 2/3 ÷ 1 1/6.

3. Solve: 3/5 ÷ 2 1/2.

4. Solve: 5 1/4 ÷ 1 3/4.

### Part B — Word Problems

5. A board is 6 1/2 feet long. You need to cut it into pieces that are each 1 3/4 feet long. How many full pieces can you cut? How much wood is left over?

6. A container holds 4 1/2 gallons of water. Each bucket holds 3/4 of a gallon. How many buckets can be filled completely?

7. Maria has 2 1/3 pounds of trail mix. She puts 1/3 of a pound into each snack bag. How many snack bags can she fill?

### Part C — Estimation and Reasoning

8. Without computing the exact answer, determine which is greater: 6 1/2 ÷ 2 1/4 or 6 1/2 ÷ 3 1/4. Explain your reasoning.

9. Jake solved 3 1/2 ÷ 1 1/4 and got 35/8. He then said the answer is 4 3/8. Find Jake's error. What is the correct answer?

10. Create your own word problem that can be solved by computing 2 1/2 ÷ 1/3. Then solve it and explain what the quotient means in context.

### Answer Key

**Part A**

1. 2 1/2 ÷ 1/4 = 5/2 x 4/1 = 20/2 = 10.
2. 4 2/3 ÷ 1 1/6 = 14/3 ÷ 7/6 = 14/3 x 6/7 = 84/21 = 4.
3. 3/5 ÷ 2 1/2 = 3/5 ÷ 5/2 = 3/5 x 2/5 = 6/25.
4. 5 1/4 ÷ 1 3/4 = 21/4 ÷ 7/4 = 21/4 x 4/7 = 84/28 = 3.

**Part B**

5. 6 1/2 ÷ 1 3/4 = 13/2 ÷ 7/4 = 13/2 x 4/7 = 52/14 = 26/7 = 3 5/7. You can cut 3 full pieces. Leftover: 3 5/7 − 3 = 5/7 of a piece, which is 5/7 x 1 3/4 = 5/7 x 7/4 = 35/28 = 5/4 = 1 1/4 feet of wood left over.
6. 4 1/2 ÷ 3/4 = 9/2 x 4/3 = 36/6 = 6 buckets exactly.
7. 2 1/3 ÷ 1/3 = 7/3 x 3/1 = 21/3 = 7 snack bags.

**Part C**

8. 6 1/2 ÷ 2 1/4 is greater. When dividing the same number by a smaller divisor, you get a larger quotient. Since 2 1/4 < 3 1/4, dividing by 2 1/4 produces the larger result. (6 1/2 ÷ 2 1/4 ≈ 2.9; 6 1/2 ÷ 3 1/4 ≈ 2.0.)
9. Jake's computation: 7/2 ÷ 5/4 = 7/2 x 4/5 = 28/10 = 14/5 = 2 4/5. Jake likely made an error in the Keep, Change, Flip step or in simplifying, arriving at 35/8 instead of 28/10. The correct answer is 14/5 = 2 4/5, not 4 3/8.
10. Answers will vary. Sample: "You have 2 1/2 yards of ribbon. Each bow requires 1/3 of a yard. How many bows can you make?" Solution: 2 1/2 ÷ 1/3 = 5/2 x 3/1 = 15/2 = 7 1/2. You can make 7 complete bows with 1/2 of a third-yard piece left over.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket

1. Solve: 2 3/4 ÷ 1/2. Show all steps (estimate, convert, Keep-Change-Flip, simplify, check).

2. A pitcher holds 2 1/2 cups of lemonade. Each glass holds 5/8 of a cup. How many glasses can be filled? Write the equation and solve.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct with all steps shown = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; identify whether the error is in conversion, in applying the algorithm, or in simplifying.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed; isolate which sub-skill is breaking down (conversion? flipping? multiplication? simplification?).

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Diagnose the specific breakdown:
- If conversion is the issue: practice converting mixed numbers to improper fractions and back with 5-6 additional examples until fluent.
- If the algorithm application is the issue: return to Lesson 2 and practice Keep, Change, Flip with proper fractions only. Then gradually reintroduce mixed numbers.
- If simplification is the issue: review GCF and reducing fractions before continuing.

Work through the Exit Ticket problems together, having the student narrate each step aloud.

### If Mastery Is Met
Challenge: "A hiking trail is 8 3/4 miles long. Aisha hikes 2 1/8 miles per hour. How long will it take her to complete the trail? Express your answer as a mixed number and interpret the fractional part in minutes." (8 3/4 ÷ 2 1/8 = 35/4 ÷ 17/8 = 35/4 x 8/17 = 280/68 = 70/17 = 4 2/17 hours. The fractional part 2/17 of an hour ≈ 7.06 minutes.)

## Extension

1. **Recipe Scaling:** A recipe for a single batch requires 1 1/3 cups of milk, 2 1/4 cups of flour, and 3/4 cup of sugar. You have 5 cups of flour. How many full batches can you make based on the flour alone? Do you have enough of the other ingredients for that many batches if you have 4 cups of milk and 3 cups of sugar? Show all work.

2. **Division Detective:** For each equation, determine whether the answer is correct without fully computing. Use estimation and reasoning.
   - 3 3/4 ÷ 1 1/4 = 3 (Correct or Incorrect?)
   - 5 1/3 ÷ 2/3 = 8 (Correct or Incorrect?)
   - 7 ÷ 1 1/2 = 14/3 (Correct or Incorrect?)

3. **Connecting Operations:** Solve each group and describe what you notice:
   - Group 1: 2 1/2 x 1 1/3 = ? and 2 1/2 ÷ 1 1/3 = ?
   - Group 2: 3 1/4 x 2/5 = ? and 3 1/4 ÷ 2/5 = ?

   For each pair, how is the quotient related to the product? Why is the quotient always larger than the product when the divisor/multiplier is a fraction less than 1?
