# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 2, Lesson 9

## Topic
The Coordinate Plane: All Four Quadrants

## Learning Objectives
- Review ordered pairs (x, y) and plotting in Quadrant I.
- Identify and name all four quadrants of the coordinate plane and the sign patterns of coordinates in each.
- Plot and read ordered pairs with positive and negative coordinates in all four quadrants.
- Determine which quadrant a point lies in based on the signs of its x- and y-coordinates.

## Prerequisites
- Plotting ordered pairs in the first quadrant (Grade 5).
- Understanding of positive and negative numbers on a number line (Lessons 4-5).
- Rational numbers on the number line (Lesson 7).
- Understanding of the x-axis (horizontal) and y-axis (vertical).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Coordinate plane grids (all four quadrants, -10 to +10 on each axis) — at least 3-4 copies per student.
- Colored pencils (one color per quadrant is helpful).
- Ruler (for drawing axes if using blank graph paper).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 6.NS.6b — Understand signs of numbers in ordered pairs as indicating locations in quadrants of the coordinate plane; recognize that when two ordered pairs differ only by signs, the locations of the points are related by reflections across one or both axes. 6.NS.6c — Find and position integers and other rational numbers on a horizontal or vertical number line diagram; find and position pairs of integers and other rational numbers on a coordinate plane.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review plotting in Quadrant I and motivate the need for additional quadrants.

1. "Plot these points on a coordinate plane: A(2, 5), B(4, 1), C(0, 3)." (All in Quadrant I or on an axis.)
2. Now ask: "What if I want to plot the point (-3, 2)? The x-value is negative. Where would that go?"
3. Say: "In Grade 5, we only used the part of the coordinate plane where both x and y are positive — that is one quadrant. But our axes can extend in all directions, just like a number line extends in both directions. Today we are going to unlock the full coordinate plane — all four quadrants."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Coordinate plane** — A two-dimensional surface formed by two perpendicular number lines: the x-axis (horizontal) and the y-axis (vertical).
- **Quadrant** — One of the four sections of the coordinate plane, created by the intersection of the x-axis and y-axis. They are numbered I, II, III, and IV using Roman numerals, starting in the upper right and going counterclockwise.
- **Origin** — The point (0, 0) where the x-axis and y-axis intersect.
- **Ordered pair** — A pair of numbers (x, y) that gives the location of a point on the coordinate plane. The x-coordinate comes first (horizontal position), and the y-coordinate comes second (vertical position).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Extend the axes.**

Draw a coordinate plane on the board with both axes extending from -6 to +6.

"In Grade 5, we used only the upper-right portion where both axes are positive. Now we are extending both axes to include negative numbers — just like extending a number line."

Label:
- Positive x-direction: right.
- Negative x-direction: left.
- Positive y-direction: up.
- Negative y-direction: down.
- The origin at (0, 0).

**Step 2 — Introduce the four quadrants.**

"The two axes divide the plane into four sections called quadrants."

Draw and label:

| Quadrant | Location | x-sign | y-sign | Example |
|----------|----------|--------|--------|---------|
| I   | Upper right | + | + | (3, 4)   |
| II  | Upper left  | - | + | (-3, 4)  |
| III | Lower left  | - | - | (-3, -4) |
| IV  | Lower right | + | - | (3, -4)  |

"The quadrants are numbered counterclockwise, starting from the upper right. A helpful memory aid: you start where everything is positive (Quadrant I), then go counterclockwise."

"Points on an axis (where x = 0 or y = 0) are not in any quadrant. For example, (0, 5) is on the y-axis, and (3, 0) is on the x-axis."

**Step 3 — Demonstrate plotting in each quadrant.**

"To plot (-3, 4): Start at the origin. Move 3 units LEFT (because x is negative). Then move 4 units UP (because y is positive). Mark the point. This is in Quadrant II."

"To plot (-2, -5): Start at the origin. Move 2 units LEFT. Then move 5 units DOWN. Mark the point. Quadrant III."

"To plot (4, -1): Start at the origin. Move 4 units RIGHT. Then move 1 unit DOWN. Mark the point. Quadrant IV."

"Always remember: x first (horizontal), y second (vertical). A helpful phrase: 'Run before you rise' — run left or right along the x-axis, then rise or fall along the y-axis."

**Step 4 — Reading coordinates from a plotted point.**

Plot a point at (-4, 3) without labeling it.

"To read this point's coordinates: How far left or right of the origin is it? 4 units left, so x = -4. How far up or down from the x-axis is it? 3 units up, so y = 3. The ordered pair is (-4, 3)."

**Step 5 — Determining the quadrant from signs alone.**

"You can identify the quadrant without even plotting — just look at the signs."

"(-7, 2): x is negative, y is positive. That is the (-, +) pattern. Quadrant II."

"(5, -8): x is positive, y is negative. That is the (+, -) pattern. Quadrant IV."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Plotting and identifying the quadrant.**

"Plot (3, -5) on the coordinate plane. What quadrant is it in?"

- Start at the origin.
- Move 3 units right (x = 3 is positive).
- Move 5 units down (y = -5 is negative).
- Mark the point.
- x is positive, y is negative: Quadrant IV.

**Example B — Reading coordinates from a graph.**

"A point is plotted 2 units to the left of the origin and 6 units below the x-axis. What are its coordinates? What quadrant is it in?"

- Left of origin: x = -2.
- Below x-axis: y = -6.
- Coordinates: (-2, -6).
- Both are negative: Quadrant III.

**Example C — Identifying quadrant by signs.**

"Without plotting, determine the quadrant for each point:
- (4, 7): both positive → Quadrant I.
- (-1, 9): (-, +) → Quadrant II.
- (-3, -3): (-, -) → Quadrant III.
- (6, -2): (+, -) → Quadrant IV.
- (0, -5): x = 0 → on the y-axis, not in any quadrant."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Plot these four points on the same coordinate plane: A(4, 3), B(-4, 3), C(-4, -3), D(4, -3). Label each point and identify its quadrant.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Start with point A. How many units right? How many units up? Now for point B, the x-value changed to negative. What does that mean for your movement?"

2. A point is located at (-5, 2). In which quadrant is it? Explain how you know without looking at a graph.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What are the signs? Negative x means left of the y-axis, positive y means above the x-axis."

3. Read the coordinates of these plotted points (facilitator plots them on the board):
   - Point P: 3 units left, 4 units up.
   - Point Q: 1 unit right, 6 units down.
   - Point R: 5 units left, 2 units down.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "For each point, identify the horizontal direction first (left = negative x, right = positive x), then the vertical direction (up = positive y, down = negative y)."

4. Without plotting, determine which quadrant each point is in:
   - (a) (8, -3)
   - (b) (-2, -7)
   - (c) (-1, 4)
   - (d) (5, 5)
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Just look at the signs. Which column of our quadrant chart does each pair match?"

5. Maya plots the point (3, -2) but places it at (-2, 3). What mistake did she make?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "She reversed the coordinates. Which value is x and which is y? Remember: x comes first (horizontal), y comes second (vertical)."
   - *If the student struggles:* "Write down 'x = 3, y = -2' before plotting. Move 3 right, then 2 down."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Plotting Points (On-Level)

1. Plot and label each point on a coordinate plane. Identify the quadrant (or axis) for each.
   - A(5, 2)
   - B(-3, 4)
   - C(-6, -1)
   - D(2, -4)
   - E(0, -3)
   - F(-4, 0)

2. Write the coordinates of a point in each quadrant:
   - Quadrant I: ( ___, ___ )
   - Quadrant II: ( ___, ___ )
   - Quadrant III: ( ___, ___ )
   - Quadrant IV: ( ___, ___ )

3. True or false: The point (-2, 5) is in Quadrant IV. Explain.

### Part B: Reading and Reasoning (Stretch)

4. A treasure map shows the following landmarks on a coordinate plane:
   - Castle: (3, 7)
   - Dragon's cave: (-5, 4)
   - Hidden lake: (-2, -6)
   - Tower: (6, -3)

   (a) Which landmark is in Quadrant III?
   (b) Which quadrant is the castle in?
   (c) Name a point that would be on the x-axis between the dragon's cave and the tower.

5. Plot the points A(-3, 1), B(2, 1), C(2, -4), D(-3, -4). Connect them in order. What shape did you draw? Which quadrants does this shape cover?

6. Without plotting, determine if each statement is true or false:
   - (a) If x > 0 and y < 0, the point is in Quadrant IV.
   - (b) If both coordinates are negative, the point is in Quadrant II.
   - (c) A point on the y-axis has an x-coordinate of 0.

### Part C: Application (Challenge)

7. Create a simple picture (house, arrow, or letter) by plotting at least 8 points that use all four quadrants. List your points in order and have a partner (or facilitator) try to recreate the picture by plotting them.

8. A video game character starts at the origin (0, 0). It moves according to these instructions:
   - Move 4 units right and 3 units up.
   - Then move 7 units left.
   - Then move 5 units down.

   What are the final coordinates? What quadrant is the character in?

9. Explain why a point with coordinates (a, 0), where a is any number, is never inside a quadrant. What about the point (0, 0)?

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: comparing rational numbers — Lesson 8)*

10. Order from least to greatest: -3/4, 0.5, -1.2, 2/3.

11. Insert < or >: -5/6 ___ -0.9.

---

### Answer Key

**Part A**

1. A(5, 2) — Quadrant I. B(-3, 4) — Quadrant II. C(-6, -1) — Quadrant III. D(2, -4) — Quadrant IV. E(0, -3) — on the y-axis (not in a quadrant). F(-4, 0) — on the x-axis (not in a quadrant).

2. Answers vary. Examples: I: (1, 1). II: (-1, 1). III: (-1, -1). IV: (1, -1). Accept any ordered pair with the correct sign pattern.

3. False. (-2, 5): x is negative and y is positive, which is the (-, +) pattern. This is Quadrant II, not Quadrant IV.

**Part B**

4. (a) Hidden lake (-2, -6) — both coordinates negative: Quadrant III. (b) Castle (3, 7) — both positive: Quadrant I. (c) Answers vary. Any point of the form (a, 0) where -5 < a < 6. Example: (0, 0) or (1, 0).

5. The shape is a rectangle. Vertices: A(-3, 1) is in Quadrant II, B(2, 1) is in Quadrant I, C(2, -4) is in Quadrant IV, D(-3, -4) is in Quadrant III. The rectangle covers all four quadrants.

6. (a) True. (+, -) is the Quadrant IV pattern. (b) False. Both negative (-, -) is Quadrant III, not II. (c) True. Points on the y-axis have x = 0.

**Part C**

7. Answers vary. Check that the student uses at least 8 points, plots them accurately, and uses all four quadrants.

8. Start at (0, 0). Move 4 right and 3 up: (4, 3). Move 7 left: (4 - 7, 3) = (-3, 3). Move 5 down: (-3, 3 - 5) = (-3, -2). Final coordinates: (-3, -2). This is in Quadrant III.

9. A point (a, 0) lies on the x-axis. Since quadrants are defined as the regions between the axes (not including the axes themselves), any point on an axis is on the boundary, not inside a quadrant. The origin (0, 0) is the intersection of both axes, so it is also not inside any quadrant.

**Interleaved Review**

10. Convert to decimals: -0.75, 0.5, -1.2, 0.667. Order: -1.2, -3/4, 0.5, 2/3.

11. -5/6 ≈ -0.833. Compare -0.833 and -0.9. -0.833 is closer to zero, so -5/6 > -0.9.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket

1. Plot the point (-4, -2) on a coordinate plane. What quadrant is it in?

2. A point is in Quadrant II. Which of the following could be its coordinates?
   - (a) (3, -5)
   - (b) (-3, 5)
   - (c) (-3, -5)
   - (d) (3, 5)

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

**Exit Ticket Answers:**
1. Start at origin, move 4 left, 2 down. Point is at (-4, -2). Both coordinates are negative: Quadrant III.
2. (b) (-3, 5). Quadrant II has the pattern (-, +): x is negative and y is positive.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Return to the sign chart. Write the four quadrant sign patterns on a card and keep it visible. Have the student practice with 6-8 points by first writing the sign pattern, then identifying the quadrant, and finally plotting. Start with integer coordinates only before introducing fractions or decimals. Use the mnemonic "All Students Take Calculus" (Quadrants I, II, III, IV: All positive, Sine positive, Tangent positive, Cosine positive) — though for Grade 6, a simpler version is just the sign chart taped to the workspace.

### If Mastery Is Met
Challenge the student: "If I tell you a point is exactly 5 units from the origin and in Quadrant II, can you find its coordinates? Can you find more than one possibility?" (This previews distance on the coordinate plane and connects to the Pythagorean theorem informally. Example: (-3, 4) or (-4, 3).)

## Extension

1. **Quadrant Art:** On a coordinate plane from -10 to +10, design a picture using at least 20 points connected by line segments. Each quadrant must contain at least part of the picture. Write out the ordered list of points so someone else could recreate it.

2. **City Grid:** Design a city map on a coordinate plane. Place buildings at specific coordinates in all four quadrants. Write directions from one building to another using coordinate language (e.g., "Start at the library (-4, 2). Move 7 units right and 5 units down to reach the park (3, -3).").
