# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 2, Lesson 7

## Topic
Rational Numbers on the Number Line

## Learning Objectives
- Extend the number line to include negative fractions and negative decimals.
- Plot rational numbers (negative fractions, negative decimals, and mixed numbers) at precise locations on a number line.
- Use benchmark fractions as reference points for placing rational numbers.
- Apply the same ordering and absolute value principles from integers to all rational numbers.

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of negative numbers and the number line (Lesson 4).
- Comparing and ordering integers on the number line (Lesson 5).
- Absolute value as distance from zero (Lesson 6).
- Familiarity with fractions, decimals, and mixed numbers (Grade 5).
- Converting between fractions, decimals, and mixed numbers.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Number line handouts (horizontal, extending from -5 to +5, with tick marks at every 0.25 or 1/4).
- Colored pencils (one color for positive values, another for negative values).
- Fraction strips or a fraction-decimal conversion chart.
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 6.NS.6c — Find and position integers and other rational numbers on a horizontal or vertical number line diagram; find and position pairs of integers and other rational numbers on a coordinate plane.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of integers on the number line and connect to the idea that non-integer numbers also have positions.

1. "Plot these integers on a number line: -3, 1, -1, 4, -5." (Quick review from Lesson 5.)
2. Ask: "Is there a number between -1 and 0?" Let the student think. "What about -0.5? Or -1/2? Where would that go?"
3. Say: "So far we have worked with negative whole numbers — integers. But fractions and decimals can be negative too. Today we are going to place numbers like -1.5, -3/4, and -2 1/2 on the number line."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Rational number** — Any number that can be written as a fraction a/b where a and b are integers and b is not zero. This includes positive and negative fractions, decimals, and integers.
- **Benchmark fraction** — A commonly used fraction (such as 1/4, 1/2, 3/4) that serves as a reference point for estimating and placing other fractions.
- **Mixed number** — A number made up of a whole number and a fraction (e.g., 2 1/2 or -2 1/2).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to what students already know.**

"You already know how to place integers on a number line. You also know that fractions and decimals live between integers. For example, 1.5 is halfway between 1 and 2. Now we are going to extend that same idea to the negative side."

Draw a number line from -4 to +4 with tick marks at every half unit.

"Just as 1.5 is halfway between 1 and 2, the number -1.5 is halfway between -2 and -1."

Plot -1.5 on the number line. Emphasize: "Negative 1.5 means 1.5 units to the LEFT of zero."

**Step 2 — Use benchmark fractions as reference points.**

"When placing fractions on the number line, it helps to use benchmarks. The most common benchmarks are 0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, and 1 — along with their negative versions."

Draw a number line from -2 to +2 with tick marks at every 1/4.

Label the benchmarks: -2, -1 3/4, -1 1/2, -1 1/4, -1, -3/4, -1/2, -1/4, 0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1, 1 1/4, 1 1/2, 1 3/4, 2.

"To place -3/4, I think: 3/4 is three-quarters of the way from 0 to 1, so -3/4 is three-quarters of the way from 0 to -1." Plot -3/4.

**Step 3 — Plot a negative decimal.**

"Let's place 2.25 on the number line. Where does it go?"

"2.25 = 2 + 0.25 = 2 + 1/4. So it is one-quarter of the way from 2 to 3." Plot 2.25.

"Now let's place -2 1/2. This is the same as -2.5. It is halfway between -3 and -2." Plot -2 1/2.

**Step 4 — Reinforce the same rules.**

"All the same rules from integer number lines still apply:
- Numbers to the RIGHT are greater.
- Numbers to the LEFT are lesser.
- The absolute value is the distance from zero. So |-1.5| = 1.5 and |2.25| = 2.25."

"Look at -3/4 and -1.5 on our number line. Which is greater? -3/4, because it is to the right of -1.5. It is closer to zero."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Plotting a negative fraction.**

"Plot -3/4 on a number line from -2 to +2."

- Step 1: Identify the two integers it falls between. -3/4 is between -1 and 0 (because 3/4 is less than 1).
- Step 2: Determine the position. 3/4 of the way from 0 toward -1.
- Step 3: Mark the point three-quarters of the way from 0 in the negative direction.
- Step 4: Check: |-3/4| = 3/4, so the point is 3/4 of a unit from zero. Correct.

**Example B — Plotting a negative mixed number.**

"Plot -2 1/2 on a number line from -4 to +4."

- Step 1: Convert to a decimal if helpful: -2 1/2 = -2.5.
- Step 2: Identify the two integers it falls between: -2.5 is between -3 and -2.
- Step 3: Determine the position. It is exactly halfway between -3 and -2.
- Step 4: Mark the point. Check: |-2 1/2| = 2 1/2 = 2.5, so the point is 2.5 units from zero. Correct.

**Example C — Plotting a positive decimal and comparing.**

"Plot 2.25 and -1.5 on the same number line. Which is greater?"

- 2.25 is between 2 and 3, one-quarter of the way from 2 to 3.
- -1.5 is between -2 and -1, halfway between them.
- 2.25 is to the right of -1.5, so 2.25 > -1.5.
- In fact, any positive number is greater than any negative number.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Plot -1/4 on a number line from -2 to +2. State its absolute value.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Where is 1/4 on the positive side? Now mirror it to the negative side."

2. Plot 1.75 and -1.75 on the same number line. What do you notice about their positions?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "These are opposites. How far is each from zero?"

3. Place these four numbers on a number line from -3 to +3: -2.5, 3/4, -1/4, 1.5.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Start by identifying which two integers each number falls between. Use benchmarks to place them precisely."

4. Order the numbers from Problem 3 from least to greatest.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Read the number line from left to right. Which number is farthest left?"

5. Maya says -1/2 is to the left of -3/4 on the number line. Is she correct? Explain.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Plot both on the number line. Which is closer to zero? Remember, closer to zero means greater for negatives, which means farther to the right."
   - *If the student struggles:* "Convert both to decimals: -1/2 = -0.5, -3/4 = -0.75. Which is closer to zero?"

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Plotting Rational Numbers (On-Level)

1. Plot each number on a number line from -3 to +3:
   - (a) -1.5
   - (b) 2/3
   - (c) -2 3/4
   - (d) 0.5

2. What is the absolute value of each number in Problem 1?

3. Plot -1/2 and -1.5 on a number line. Which is greater? Explain how you know.

### Part B: Using Benchmarks (Stretch)

4. Place these numbers on the same number line (-4 to +4): -3.25, 1 1/2, -1/3, 2.75, -2 2/3.

5. A submarine is at a depth of -2 3/4 miles. A diver is at -1/2 mile. Plot both on a vertical number line (where up is positive). Who is closer to the surface (zero)?

6. Between which two integers does each number fall?
   - (a) -4/5
   - (b) 3.7
   - (c) -2 1/8
   - (d) -0.1

### Part C: Reasoning (Challenge)

7. Name a rational number that is between -1 and -1/2. Plot it on a number line and explain how you know it belongs there.

8. Leo says that -1.25 and -5/4 are different points on the number line. Is he correct? Justify your answer.

9. List three different rational numbers that are between -2 and -1, written as: one fraction, one decimal, and one mixed number. Plot all three on a number line.

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: dividing fractions — Lessons 1-3)*

10. Compute: 3/4 ÷ 1/2.

11. Compute: 2 1/3 ÷ 1/6.

### Answer Key

**Part A**

1. Plotting:
   - (a) -1.5 is halfway between -2 and -1.
   - (b) 2/3 is two-thirds of the way from 0 to 1 (approximately 0.67).
   - (c) -2 3/4 is three-quarters of the way from -2 toward -3 (i.e., -2.75).
   - (d) 0.5 is halfway between 0 and 1.

2. Absolute values: (a) |-1.5| = 1.5. (b) |2/3| = 2/3. (c) |-2 3/4| = 2 3/4 = 2.75. (d) |0.5| = 0.5.

3. -1/2 = -0.5 and -1.5. On the number line, -1/2 is to the right of -1.5, so -1/2 > -1.5. It is closer to zero.

**Part B**

4. From left to right: -3.25, -2 2/3 (approximately -2.67), -1/3 (approximately -0.33), 1 1/2 (1.5), 2.75.

5. The submarine is at -2 3/4 = -2.75 and the diver is at -1/2 = -0.5. The diver is closer to the surface because |-0.5| = 0.5 < |-2.75| = 2.75.

6. (a) -4/5 = -0.8, between -1 and 0. (b) 3.7, between 3 and 4. (c) -2 1/8 = -2.125, between -3 and -2. (d) -0.1, between -1 and 0.

**Part C**

7. Any rational number between -1 and -0.5. Examples: -3/4 (-0.75), -2/3 (approximately -0.67), -0.6, -7/8 (-0.875). The student should plot it between -1 and -1/2 and explain it is less than -1/2 but greater than -1.

8. No. -1.25 = -5/4 because 5/4 = 1.25. They are the same point on the number line. Different representations of the same rational number occupy the same position.

9. Answers vary. Examples: -3/2 (fraction), -1.75 (decimal), -1 1/4 (mixed number). All three should be plotted between -2 and -1, and the student should verify each is greater than -2 and less than -1.

**Interleaved Review**

10. 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 x 2/1 = 6/4 = 3/2 = 1 1/2.

11. 2 1/3 ÷ 1/6 = 7/3 ÷ 1/6 = 7/3 x 6/1 = 42/3 = 14.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket

1. Plot -1 3/4 on a number line. State which two integers it falls between and give its absolute value.

2. Which is greater: -0.6 or -3/4? Explain your reasoning.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Return to benchmark fractions. Draw a number line from -2 to 0 with tick marks at every 1/4. Have the student label every tick mark as both a fraction and a decimal. Then practice placing 3-4 numbers by first converting them to the benchmark form. The visual scaffold of labeled tick marks reduces the cognitive load of estimation.

### If Mastery Is Met
Ask: "Can you name a rational number between -1/4 and -1/8? How many rational numbers are there between any two rational numbers?" (Introduce the idea that rational numbers are dense — there are infinitely many between any two.)

## Extension

1. **Temperature Timeline:** Research the daily low temperatures for a city during one winter week. Plot each temperature (including any below-zero readings) on a number line. Identify the coldest and warmest days and calculate the difference between them.

2. **Fraction-Decimal Number Line:** Create a detailed number line from -2 to +2 where every 1/8 is marked. Convert each eighth to a decimal and label both forms. This builds fluency switching between fraction and decimal representations of rational numbers.
