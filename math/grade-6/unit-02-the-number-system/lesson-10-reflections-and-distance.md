# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 2, Lesson 10

## Topic
Reflections and Distance on the Coordinate Plane

## Learning Objectives
- Reflect a point across the x-axis and y-axis by changing the sign of the appropriate coordinate.
- Identify which coordinate changes sign during a reflection across each axis.
- Calculate the distance between two points that share the same x-coordinate or the same y-coordinate using absolute value.
- Apply reflections and distance to real-world and geometric contexts.

## Prerequisites
- Plotting ordered pairs in all four quadrants (Lesson 9).
- Understanding of absolute value as distance from zero (Lesson 6).
- Knowledge of the four quadrants and their sign patterns (Lesson 9).
- Concept of opposites on the number line (Lesson 4).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Coordinate plane grids (all four quadrants, -10 to +10 on each axis) — at least 3-4 copies per student.
- Colored pencils (different colors for original points and reflected points).
- Small mirror or transparent ruler (optional, for demonstrating reflections physically).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 6.NS.6b — Understand signs of numbers in ordered pairs as indicating locations in quadrants of the coordinate plane; recognize that when two ordered pairs differ only by signs, the locations of the points are related by reflections across one or both axes. 6.NS.8 — Solve real-world and mathematical problems by graphing points in all four quadrants of the coordinate plane. Include use of coordinates and absolute value to find distances between points with the same first coordinate or the same second coordinate.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of opposites and absolute value to set up both reflections and distance.

1. "What is the opposite of 5? What is the opposite of -3?" (Answer: -5 and 3.)
2. "On a number line, how far apart are 5 and -5? How far apart are -3 and 3?" (Answer: 10 units and 6 units.)
3. "How did you find those distances?" (Add the absolute values: |5| + |-5| = 5 + 5 = 10.)
4. Say: "Today we will use opposites and absolute value on the coordinate plane. We will learn how to flip points across the axes — that is called reflection — and how to find distances between points."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Reflection** — A transformation that flips a point across a line (in this lesson, across the x-axis or y-axis), creating a mirror image.
- **Reflect across the y-axis** — The x-coordinate changes sign; the y-coordinate stays the same. Example: (3, 4) becomes (-3, 4).
- **Reflect across the x-axis** — The y-coordinate changes sign; the x-coordinate stays the same. Example: (3, 4) becomes (3, -4).
- **Distance between points** — When two points share the same x-coordinate or the same y-coordinate, the distance between them is the absolute value of the difference of the coordinates that are not the same.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce reflection across the y-axis.**

Plot the point (3, 4) on a coordinate plane.

"Imagine the y-axis is a mirror. If I look at the mirror image of (3, 4), where does it appear?"

"The point is 3 units to the RIGHT of the y-axis. Its mirror image is 3 units to the LEFT. The vertical position does not change."

Plot (-3, 4).

"When we reflect across the y-axis, the x-coordinate changes sign: 3 becomes -3. The y-coordinate stays the same: 4 stays 4."

Rule: **(x, y) reflected across the y-axis = (-x, y).**

"A helpful way to remember: the axis you reflect ACROSS stays the same. Reflecting across the y-axis? The y-coordinate stays. The other one (x) flips."

**Step 2 — Introduce reflection across the x-axis.**

Return to (3, 4).

"Now imagine the x-axis is a mirror. The point (3, 4) is 4 units ABOVE the x-axis. Its mirror image is 4 units BELOW."

Plot (3, -4).

"When we reflect across the x-axis, the y-coordinate changes sign: 4 becomes -4. The x-coordinate stays the same: 3 stays 3."

Rule: **(x, y) reflected across the x-axis = (x, -y).**

"Same memory trick: reflecting across the x-axis? The x-coordinate stays. The y flips."

**Step 3 — Reflection across both axes.**

"What if we reflect (3, 4) across BOTH axes?"

"Across y-axis: (3, 4) → (-3, 4). Then across x-axis: (-3, 4) → (-3, -4)."

"Or across x-axis first: (3, 4) → (3, -4). Then across y-axis: (3, -4) → (-3, -4)."

"Either way, we get (-3, -4). Both coordinates change sign."

Plot all four points: (3, 4), (-3, 4), (3, -4), (-3, -4). "Notice they form a rectangle, and each point is in a different quadrant."

**Step 4 — Distance between points with a shared coordinate.**

"How far apart are (3, 4) and (3, -4)? They have the same x-coordinate, so they are directly above and below each other."

"The distance is the absolute value of the difference of the y-coordinates: |4 - (-4)| = |4 + 4| = |8| = 8 units."

"Alternatively, since one is 4 units above the x-axis and the other is 4 units below, the total distance is |4| + |-4| = 4 + 4 = 8."

"How far apart are (3, 4) and (-3, 4)? Same y-coordinate, so they are directly left and right of each other."

"Distance: |3 - (-3)| = |3 + 3| = |6| = 6 units. Or: |3| + |-3| = 3 + 3 = 6."

**General rule:** For two points with the same x-coordinate, distance = |y₁ - y₂|. For two points with the same y-coordinate, distance = |x₁ - x₂|.

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Reflecting across the y-axis.**

"Reflect the point (-5, 2) across the y-axis."

- The y-coordinate stays the same: 2.
- The x-coordinate changes sign: -5 becomes 5.
- Reflected point: (5, 2).
- Check: (-5, 2) is in Quadrant II. (5, 2) is in Quadrant I. They are mirror images across the y-axis.

**Example B — Reflecting across the x-axis.**

"Reflect the point (1, -7) across the x-axis."

- The x-coordinate stays the same: 1.
- The y-coordinate changes sign: -7 becomes 7.
- Reflected point: (1, 7).
- Check: (1, -7) is in Quadrant IV. (1, 7) is in Quadrant I. They are mirror images across the x-axis.

**Example C — Finding distance between two points.**

"Find the distance between (-2, 5) and (-2, -3)."

- Same x-coordinate (-2), so the points are vertically aligned.
- Distance = |5 - (-3)| = |5 + 3| = |8| = 8 units.
- Alternatively: |5| + |-3| = 5 + 3 = 8 units. (This shortcut works because the points are on opposite sides of the x-axis.)

**Example D — Distance between points on the same side.**

"Find the distance between (4, 1) and (4, 6)."

- Same x-coordinate (4), so vertically aligned.
- Distance = |6 - 1| = |5| = 5 units.
- Both points are above the x-axis, so we subtract their y-values (no need to add absolute values).

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Reflect (-4, 3) across the y-axis. What are the new coordinates? Plot both points.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Which coordinate changes sign when reflecting across the y-axis? The x-coordinate."

2. Reflect (2, -6) across the x-axis. What are the new coordinates? Plot both points.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Which coordinate changes sign when reflecting across the x-axis? The y-coordinate."

3. Reflect (-1, -5) across the y-axis, then reflect the result across the x-axis. What are the final coordinates?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Do it in two steps. First flip x, then flip y. What do you notice?"

4. Find the distance between (3, -2) and (3, 7).
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Same x-coordinate. The distance is |7 - (-2)|. What is 7 - (-2)?"

5. A park is mapped on a coordinate plane. The fountain is at (-3, 4) and the bench is at (5, 4). How far apart are they?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Same y-coordinate. Find |(-3) - 5| or |5 - (-3)|."
   - *If the student struggles:* "Count the units on the grid from -3 to 5 along the horizontal line y = 4. You should count 8 units."

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)

### Part A: Reflections (On-Level)

1. Reflect each point across the y-axis:
   - (a) (6, 1)
   - (b) (-3, -4)
   - (c) (0, 5)

2. Reflect each point across the x-axis:
   - (a) (-2, 7)
   - (b) (4, -3)
   - (c) (8, 0)

3. Plot the point (5, -3) and its reflections across the y-axis and the x-axis. Label all three points with their coordinates.

### Part B: Distance (Stretch)

4. Find the distance between each pair of points:
   - (a) (2, 8) and (2, -4)
   - (b) (-5, 3) and (7, 3)
   - (c) (-1, -6) and (-1, 2)

5. A rectangle has vertices at A(1, 3), B(1, -2), C(6, -2), and D(6, 3). Find the length and width of the rectangle.

6. Two scuba divers are mapped on a coordinate grid. Diver A is at position (-4, -7) and Diver B is at (-4, 2). How far apart are they?

### Part C: Combined Reasoning (Challenge)

7. Point P is at (-3, 5). It is reflected across the x-axis to get point Q, and then Q is reflected across the y-axis to get point R. What are the coordinates of Q and R? What single transformation would take P directly to R?

8. A square has one vertex at (2, 2) and the opposite vertex at (-2, -2). Find the other two vertices. (Hint: Think about reflections. Where would you reflect (2, 2) to find points that complete a square?)

9. Two friends are on a coordinate grid. Alex is at (-6, 3) and Jordan is at (4, 3). They both walk toward each other along the line y = 3. If they meet at the midpoint, what are the coordinates of their meeting point? How far does each person walk?

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: ordering rational numbers — Lesson 8)*

10. Order from least to greatest: -2.5, 3/4, -1/3, 0, 1.8.

11. Insert <, >, or =: -7/8 ___ -0.875.

---

### Answer Key

**Part A**

1. (a) (6, 1) → (-6, 1). (b) (-3, -4) → (3, -4). (c) (0, 5) → (0, 5). (Note: (0, 5) is on the y-axis, so reflecting across the y-axis gives the same point.)

2. (a) (-2, 7) → (-2, -7). (b) (4, -3) → (4, 3). (c) (8, 0) → (8, 0). (Note: (8, 0) is on the x-axis, so reflecting across the x-axis gives the same point.)

3. (5, -3): reflected across y-axis = (-5, -3); reflected across x-axis = (5, 3). All three points should be plotted and labeled.

**Part B**

4. (a) |8 - (-4)| = |12| = 12 units. (b) |(-5) - 7| = |-12| = 12 units. (c) |(-6) - 2| = |-8| = 8 units.

5. Length AB (or CD): same x-coordinate. |3 - (-2)| = |5| = 5 units (this is the width). Length BC (or AD): same y-coordinate. |6 - 1| = |5| = 5 units (this is the length). The rectangle is 5 units by 5 units — in fact, it is a square.

6. Same x-coordinate (-4). Distance = |(-7) - 2| = |-9| = 9 units.

**Part C**

7. P(-3, 5) reflected across x-axis: Q = (-3, -5). Q(-3, -5) reflected across y-axis: R = (3, -5). The single transformation from P(-3, 5) to R(3, -5) is a reflection across both axes (both coordinates change sign). This is equivalent to a 180-degree rotation around the origin.

8. (2, 2) and (-2, -2) are opposite vertices. Reflecting (2, 2) across the y-axis gives (-2, 2). Reflecting (2, 2) across the x-axis gives (2, -2). The four vertices are (2, 2), (-2, 2), (-2, -2), (2, -2). Check: all sides have length 4 (e.g., from (2, 2) to (-2, 2) is |2 - (-2)| = 4).

9. Alex is at (-6, 3) and Jordan is at (4, 3). Same y-coordinate. Distance between them: |(-6) - 4| = 10 units. Midpoint x-coordinate: (-6 + 4) / 2 = -2 / 2 = -1. Meeting point: (-1, 3). Alex walks from x = -6 to x = -1: distance = |(-6) - (-1)| = |-5| = 5 units. Jordan walks from x = 4 to x = -1: distance = |4 - (-1)| = |5| = 5 units. Each walks 5 units.

**Interleaved Review**

10. Convert to decimals: -2.5, 0.75, -0.333, 0, 1.8. Order: -2.5, -1/3, 0, 3/4, 1.8.

11. -7/8 = -0.875. Answer: =.

## Assessment Check

### Exit Ticket

1. Reflect the point (-2, 6) across the y-axis. What are the new coordinates?

2. Find the distance between (-3, -1) and (-3, 5).

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

**Exit Ticket Answers:**
1. (-2, 6) reflected across y-axis: x changes sign, y stays. Answer: (2, 6).
2. Same x-coordinate (-3). Distance = |(-1) - 5| = |-6| = 6 units.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
For reflections: Have the student fold a coordinate grid along the axis of reflection. Plot the original point, fold the paper, and mark where the point appears on the other side. This concrete model makes the abstract rule tangible. Then connect: "Which coordinate changed? Which stayed the same?"

For distance: Have the student count grid squares between the two points. Then show the absolute value formula as a shortcut for what they just counted. Start with points on the same side of the axis (both positive y-values) before working with points on opposite sides.

### If Mastery Is Met
Present: "Triangle ABC has vertices at A(1, 2), B(7, 2), and C(4, 6). Reflect the entire triangle across the x-axis. What are the new vertices? Calculate the perimeter of triangle ABC using the distance formula for the horizontal side and counting for the other sides." (This previews transformations of shapes, which leads into Grade 8 geometry.)

## Extension

1. **Reflection Art:** Draw a simple shape (triangle, arrow, or letter) in Quadrant I using at least 5 vertices. Reflect it across the y-axis into Quadrant II, then across the x-axis into Quadrant III. List the coordinates of all vertices for all three shapes. What pattern do you see?

2. **Real-World Distance Problem:** A city uses a coordinate grid where each unit represents one city block. The school is at (-3, 4) and the library is at (-3, -2). The park is at (5, -2). If you walk from the school to the library and then to the park (along the grid — no diagonals), how many blocks do you walk in total? What if you could walk in a straight line from the school to the park — would it be shorter? (Explain why or why not without calculating the diagonal.)
