# Facilitator Guide — Grade 6 Math, Unit 2: The Number System

**Grade:** 6
**Subject:** Mathematics
**Unit:** 2 — The Number System
**Recommended Duration:** 10 lessons (approximately 3-4 weeks at 3 sessions/week)
**Facilitation Mode:** Guided (facilitator leads instruction; student works independently for practice)

---

## Unit Overview

This unit extends the number system beyond positive whole numbers and fractions. Students master division of fractions by fractions, then explore the world of positive and negative numbers (integers and rational numbers). They locate and compare numbers on a number line, develop understanding of absolute value, and expand the coordinate plane to all four quadrants. This unit lays the foundation for operations with signed numbers (Grade 7) and algebraic thinking.

By the end of this unit, the student will be able to:

- Divide fractions by fractions using the "invert and multiply" algorithm, with conceptual understanding
- Interpret division of fractions in real-world contexts
- Identify, locate, and compare positive and negative integers on a number line
- Understand opposites and the concept of zero as a reference point
- Determine the absolute value of a number and interpret it as distance from zero
- Compare and order rational numbers (positive and negative fractions, decimals, and integers)
- Plot ordered pairs in all four quadrants of the coordinate plane
- Reflect points across axes using coordinates

**Why this matters:** The number system expansion to negatives is one of the most important conceptual leaps in middle school math. Negative numbers model real-world situations (temperature, debt, elevation) and are essential for algebra. Understanding the coordinate plane across all quadrants is critical for graphing functions and analyzing geometric transformations.

## Lesson Summaries and Facilitation Notes

### Lesson 1: Dividing Fractions: Conceptual Foundation
**Goal:** Understand what it means to divide by a fraction using visual models.
**Key moves:** "How many 1/3s fit into 2?" → 2 ÷ 1/3 = 6. Use fraction strips and number lines. Extend to "How many 2/3s fit into 4?" → 4 ÷ 2/3 = 6. Help students see division as "how many groups of this size fit into that amount?"
**Watch for:** Students confusing fraction division with fraction multiplication. Reinforce the distinction: multiplication asks "what is a part of this?" while division asks "how many of this fit into that?"

### Lesson 2: Dividing Fractions: Invert and Multiply
**Goal:** Formalize the algorithm: a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c.
**Key moves:** Connect the algorithm to the visual model. Show why inverting works: dividing by 2/3 is the same as multiplying by 3/2 because you need 3/2 as many groups. Practice with a variety of fraction pairs, always checking with estimation or visual models.
**Watch for:** Students who invert the wrong fraction. Mnemonics: "Keep, Change, Flip" — keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, flip the second fraction.

### Lesson 3: Dividing Mixed Numbers
**Goal:** Divide mixed numbers by converting to improper fractions first.
**Key moves:** Convert both numbers to improper fractions, then apply invert and multiply. Estimate first (e.g., 3 1/2 ÷ 1 1/4 is about 3 ÷ 1 = 3). Convert answer back to mixed number.
**Watch for:** Errors in converting to improper fractions. Review the conversion process if needed.

### Lesson 4: Introducing Negative Numbers
**Goal:** Understand negative numbers as values less than zero.
**Key moves:** Start with real-world contexts: temperature below zero, floors below ground, debt. Introduce the number line extending left past zero. Define integers (…, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, …). Show that every positive number has an opposite negative number.
**Watch for:** Students thinking negative numbers are "not real" or "less important." Validate them with many real-world examples.

### Lesson 5: Comparing and Ordering Integers
**Goal:** Compare and order integers using the number line.
**Key moves:** On a number line, numbers to the right are greater. So −2 > −5 because −2 is to the right of −5. Use inequality symbols (<, >, =). Order sets of integers from least to greatest. Common confusion: "−10 is bigger than −3 because 10 > 3" — address this explicitly.
**Watch for:** The misconception that larger absolute value means larger number for negatives. Always return to the number line.

### Lesson 6: Absolute Value
**Goal:** Define absolute value as distance from zero on the number line.
**Key moves:** |−7| = 7 and |7| = 7 because both are 7 units from zero. Absolute value is always non-negative. Distinguish between the value of a number (which can be negative) and its absolute value (which cannot). Use real-world contexts: "distance from home" doesn't depend on direction.
**Watch for:** Students thinking |−7| = −7. Emphasize: absolute value = distance, and distance is never negative.

### Lesson 7: Rational Numbers on the Number Line
**Goal:** Extend the number line to include negative fractions and decimals.
**Key moves:** Plot numbers like −1.5, −3/4, 2.25, −2 1/2 on a number line. All the same rules apply: to the right is greater, distance from zero is absolute value. Connect to real-world measurement: −0.5°C, −$3.75.
**Watch for:** Difficulty placing negative fractions precisely on the number line. Use benchmark fractions (−1/2, −1, −1 1/2) as reference points.

### Lesson 8: Comparing and Ordering Rational Numbers
**Goal:** Compare and order any mix of positive and negative fractions, decimals, and integers.
**Key moves:** Convert to common form (all decimals or all fractions with common denominator) to compare. Any positive number > any negative number. Between two negatives, the one closer to zero is greater. Practice ordering sets of 5-6 mixed rational numbers.
**Watch for:** Students forgetting that −1/2 > −3/4 (because −1/2 is closer to zero). Number line is the ultimate tool for resolving confusion.

### Lesson 9: The Coordinate Plane: All Four Quadrants
**Goal:** Extend the coordinate plane to all four quadrants using signed coordinates.
**Key moves:** Review ordered pairs (x, y) in Quadrant I. Introduce the four quadrants: I (+, +), II (−, +), III (−, −), IV (+, −). Plot and read coordinates in all quadrants. Identify which quadrant a point is in by the signs of its coordinates.
**Watch for:** Reversing x and y coordinates. Reinforce: x first (horizontal), y second (vertical).

### Lesson 10: Reflections and Distance on the Coordinate Plane
**Goal:** Reflect points across axes and find distances between points with shared coordinates.
**Key moves:** Reflecting (3, 4) across the y-axis gives (−3, 4): the x-coordinate changes sign. Reflecting across the x-axis: the y-coordinate changes sign. Finding distance: if two points share the same x- or y-coordinate, the distance is the absolute value of the difference of the other coordinate.
**Watch for:** Students confusing which coordinate changes during reflection. Use the mnemonic: the axis you reflect across stays the same.

## Differentiation Strategies

### Struggling Learners
- Provide pre-made number lines with tick marks for fraction/decimal placement
- For fraction division, continue using visual models alongside the algorithm
- Use color coding: positive numbers in blue, negative numbers in red
- For the coordinate plane, label all four quadrants with their sign patterns

### Advanced Learners
- Explore operations with integers informally (adding/subtracting negatives)
- Challenge problems: plot shapes in all four quadrants and calculate perimeter
- Introduce the idea of distance formula (using absolute value) for points not on the same line
- Explore negative fractions ÷ negative fractions

---

## Common Errors and How to Address Them

| Error | Likely Cause | Remediation |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Inverting the wrong fraction in division | Procedural confusion | "Keep-Change-Flip": keep first, change ÷ to ×, flip second |
| −3 > −1 ("because 3 > 1") | Ignoring the negative sign | Return to number line: plot both, check which is to the right |
| \|−5\| = −5 | Confusing value with absolute value | Emphasize: absolute value = distance from zero = always positive |
| Plotting (3, −2) as (−2, 3) | Reversing coordinates | "x before y, run before rise" — horizontal first, vertical second |
| Reflecting across wrong axis | Confusion about which coordinate changes | Reflecting across y-axis: x changes sign. Reflecting across x-axis: y changes sign. |

## Assessment Guidance

The **Unit Assessment** covers all major skills. Administer after Lesson 10.

**Mastery criteria (80% or above):**
- Correctly divides fractions and mixed numbers
- Places positive and negative numbers on a number line
- Finds absolute values and compares rational numbers
- Plots and reads coordinates in all four quadrants
- Reflects points across axes

**If below mastery:** Reteach based on error patterns — fraction division (Lessons 1-3), integer concepts (Lessons 4-6), rational number ordering (Lessons 7-8), or coordinate plane (Lessons 9-10).
