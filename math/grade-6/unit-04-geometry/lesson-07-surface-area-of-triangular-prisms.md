# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 4, Lesson 7

## Surface Area of Triangular Prisms

### Learning Objectives
1. Identify the five faces of a triangular prism (two triangular bases and three rectangular faces).
2. Draw and label the net of a triangular prism.
3. Calculate the area of each face and sum them to find the total surface area.
4. Solve real-world problems involving the surface area of triangular prisms.

### Prerequisites
- Identify faces, edges, and vertices of triangular prisms (Lesson 5)
- Calculate the area of rectangles and triangles (Lessons 1-2)
- Calculate the surface area of rectangular prisms using nets (Lesson 6)

### Materials
- Triangular prism nets on cardstock (one per student)
- Scissors and tape
- Rulers
- Grid paper
- Colored pencils
- Geometric solid: triangular prism

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Display the following:**

1. Find the area of a rectangle that is 10 cm x 4 cm. → 40 cm²
2. Find the area of a triangle with b = 6 cm and h = 4 cm. → 12 cm²
3. A rectangular prism is 8 in x 5 in x 3 in. What is its surface area? → 158 in²

"You will use both rectangle and triangle area skills today. We are moving from rectangular prisms to triangular prisms — shapes with triangular bases."

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Triangular prism:** A 3D shape with two congruent triangular bases connected by three rectangular faces.
- **Lateral faces:** The rectangular faces on the sides of a prism (not the bases).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete: Examine the Shape:**

Hold up a triangular prism (or a Toblerone box, a tent model, or a triangular prism from the geometric solids set).

"This is a triangular prism. Touch each face and tell your partner what shape it is."

Students identify: two triangles (the bases) and three rectangles (the lateral faces).

"How many faces total?" — Five.

"To find the surface area, we need the area of all five faces. But the faces are not all the same shape — we have triangles and rectangles. So we cannot use the same formula as for rectangular prisms. Instead, we will use the net."

**Step 2 — Representational: The Net:**

Draw a triangular prism net on the board. The net shows:
- Three rectangles in a row (the lateral faces)
- One triangle attached to the top of the middle rectangle
- One triangle attached to the bottom of the middle rectangle

Label the triangular prism dimensions: triangular base has sides 5 cm, 5 cm, and 6 cm, with a height of 4 cm. The prism is 10 cm long.

"Let me label every face on the net:"

| Face | Shape | Dimensions | Area |
|------|-------|-----------|------|
| Base 1 (triangle) | Triangle | b = 6 cm, h = 4 cm | 1/2 x 6 x 4 = 12 cm² |
| Base 2 (triangle) | Triangle | b = 6 cm, h = 4 cm | 1/2 x 6 x 4 = 12 cm² |
| Rectangular face 1 | Rectangle | 10 cm x 5 cm | 50 cm² |
| Rectangular face 2 | Rectangle | 10 cm x 5 cm | 50 cm² |
| Rectangular face 3 | Rectangle | 10 cm x 6 cm | 60 cm² |

SA = 12 + 12 + 50 + 50 + 60 = **184 cm²**

**Step 3 — The Process (No Single Formula):**

"Notice there is no single shortcut formula like we had for rectangular prisms. Instead, the process is:
1. Find the area of each triangular base.
2. Find the area of each rectangular face.
3. Add all five areas together."

"The key is knowing the dimensions. Each rectangular face has one dimension that is the length (or depth) of the prism and one dimension that is a side of the triangular base."

**Important note:** "The height of the triangle is not the same as a side of the triangle unless it is a right triangle. Be careful to use the perpendicular height for the triangle area and the side lengths for the rectangles."

### Worked Example A

**Problem:** A triangular prism has triangular bases with b = 8 cm, h = 3 cm, and sides of 8 cm, 5 cm, and 5 cm. The prism is 12 cm long. Find the surface area.

| Face | Dimensions | Area |
|------|-----------|------|
| Base 1 | b = 8, h = 3 | 1/2 x 8 x 3 = 12 cm² |
| Base 2 | b = 8, h = 3 | 1/2 x 8 x 3 = 12 cm² |
| Rectangle 1 | 12 x 8 | 96 cm² |
| Rectangle 2 | 12 x 5 | 60 cm² |
| Rectangle 3 | 12 x 5 | 60 cm² |

SA = 12 + 12 + 96 + 60 + 60 = **240 cm²**

### Worked Example B

**Problem:** A tent is shaped like a triangular prism. The triangular opening has a base of 6 ft, a height of 4 ft, and slant sides of 5 ft each. The tent is 8 ft long. How much fabric covers the tent (not including the floor)?

"This is a real-world problem. The tent has no floor — so we skip the bottom rectangular face."

| Face | Dimensions | Area |
|------|-----------|------|
| Front triangle | b = 6, h = 4 | 1/2 x 6 x 4 = 12 ft² |
| Back triangle | b = 6, h = 4 | 1/2 x 6 x 4 = 12 ft² |
| Left slant | 8 x 5 | 40 ft² |
| Right slant | 8 x 5 | 40 ft² |
| ~~Floor~~ | ~~Not included~~ | ~~0~~ |

Fabric needed = 12 + 12 + 40 + 40 = **104 ft²**

"In real-world problems, think about which faces actually need to be covered."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

### Activity 1 — Build and Calculate

"Cut out the triangular prism net from your cardstock. Before folding, color each face a different color and calculate its area. Then add up all five areas to find the surface area."

Net dimensions: triangular base with b = 4 cm, h = 3 cm, and sides 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm (right triangle). Prism length = 8 cm.

| Face | Color | Dimensions | Area |
|------|-------|-----------|------|
| Base 1 | Blue | b=4, h=3 | 6 cm² |
| Base 2 | Blue | b=4, h=3 | 6 cm² |
| Rectangle (bottom) | Red | 8 x 4 | 32 cm² |
| Rectangle (back) | Green | 8 x 3 | 24 cm² |
| Rectangle (hypotenuse) | Yellow | 8 x 5 | 40 cm² |

SA = 6 + 6 + 32 + 24 + 40 = **108 cm²**

"Now fold and tape your net. Every colored piece should be visible on the outside."

### Activity 2 — Partner Practice

Find the surface area of each triangular prism.

1. Triangle: b = 10 cm, h = 6 cm, sides 10, 8, 8 cm. Prism length = 15 cm.
   - Bases: 2 x (1/2 x 10 x 6) = 60 cm²
   - Rectangles: (15 x 10) + (15 x 8) + (15 x 8) = 150 + 120 + 120 = 390 cm²
   - SA = 60 + 390 = **450 cm²**

2. Triangle: b = 7 in, h = 4 in, sides 7, 5, 5 in. Prism length = 9 in.
   - Bases: 2 x (1/2 x 7 x 4) = 28 in²
   - Rectangles: (9 x 7) + (9 x 5) + (9 x 5) = 63 + 45 + 45 = 153 in²
   - SA = 28 + 153 = **181 in²**

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 — Developing

Find the surface area of each triangular prism.

1. Triangle: b = 6 cm, h = 4 cm, sides 5, 5, 6 cm. Prism length = 10 cm.
   → Bases: 2 x 12 = 24. Rectangles: 50 + 50 + 60 = 160. SA = **184 cm²**

2. Triangle: b = 8 in, h = 3 in, sides 5, 5, 8 in. Prism length = 12 in.
   → Bases: 2 x 12 = 24. Rectangles: 60 + 60 + 96 = 216. SA = **240 in²**

3. Right triangle base: legs 3 cm and 4 cm, hypotenuse 5 cm. Prism length = 6 cm.
   → Bases: 2 x 6 = 12. Rectangles: 18 + 24 + 30 = 72. SA = **84 cm²**

4. List the five faces of any triangular prism and their shapes.
   → **Two triangles (the bases) and three rectangles (the lateral faces).**

### Tier 2 — Proficient

1. Triangle: b = 9 cm, h = 5 cm, sides 9, 7, 7 cm. Prism length = 14 cm.
   → Bases: 2 x 22.5 = 45. Rectangles: 126 + 98 + 98 = 322. SA = **367 cm²**

2. A triangular prism has a right triangle base with legs 6 in and 8 in and hypotenuse 10 in. The prism is 20 in long. Find the surface area.
   → Bases: 2 x 24 = 48. Rectangles: 120 + 160 + 200 = 480. SA = **528 in²**

3. A wooden ramp is shaped like a triangular prism. The triangular face has a base of 3 ft, height of 1.5 ft, and slant side of 3.4 ft. The ramp is 4 ft long. How much wood is used for all five faces?
   → Bases: 2 x 2.25 = 4.5. Rectangles: 12 + 6 + 13.6 = 31.6. SA = **36.1 ft²**

4. Compare: A rectangular prism is 6 x 6 x 6. A triangular prism has an equilateral triangle base with sides of 6 cm, height of 5.2 cm, and length of 6 cm. Which has the greater surface area?
   → Rect: 2(36+36+36) = 216. Tri: 2(1/2 x 6 x 5.2) + 3(6 x 6) = 31.2 + 108 = 139.2. **Rectangular prism has greater SA.**

### Tier 3 — Advanced

1. Triangle: b = 11.5 cm, h = 7.2 cm, sides 11.5, 8.6, 8.6 cm. Prism length = 16 cm.
   → Bases: 2 x 41.4 = 82.8. Rectangles: 184 + 137.6 + 137.6 = 459.2. SA = **542 cm²**

2. A gift box shaped like a triangular prism has a right triangle base with legs 5 cm and 12 cm (hypotenuse 13 cm). The box is 18 cm long. If wrapping paper costs $0.02 per cm², what is the cost to wrap the entire box?
   → Bases: 2 x 30 = 60. Rectangles: 90 + 216 + 234 = 540. SA = 600 cm². Cost = 600 x $0.02 = **$12.00**

3. An attic space is shaped like a triangular prism. The triangular face has a base of 20 ft and a height of 8 ft. The slant sides are each 10.8 ft. The attic is 40 ft long. If you want to insulate the two slant walls and the two triangular end walls (not the floor), how many square feet of insulation do you need?
   → Triangles: 2 x (1/2 x 20 x 8) = 160. Slant walls: 2 x (40 x 10.8) = 864. Total = **1,024 ft²**

4. Can a triangular prism ever have a greater surface area than a rectangular prism with the same length? Explain with an example. → **Yes — it depends on the dimensions. A very wide triangular base with a long prism length can exceed a small rectangular prism. Students should give numerical examples.**

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A triangular prism has a base triangle with b = 10 cm, h = 6 cm, and sides 10, 8, 8 cm. The prism is 12 cm long. Find the surface area.

**Problem 2:** How is finding the surface area of a triangular prism different from finding the surface area of a rectangular prism?

---

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Bases: 2 x (1/2 x 10 x 6) = 60 cm². Rectangles: (12 x 10) + (12 x 8) + (12 x 8) = 120 + 96 + 96 = 312 cm². SA = 60 + 312 = **372 cm²**
2. **A rectangular prism has 6 rectangular faces (3 pairs), so we can use the formula SA = 2(lw + lh + wh). A triangular prism has 2 triangular faces and 3 rectangular faces, so we calculate the area of each face separately and add them. There is no single shortcut formula for triangular prisms.**

---

## Teacher Notes

- **No shortcut formula:** Unlike rectangular prisms, there is no tidy formula for triangular prism surface area. This is actually a valuable teaching moment — it reinforces the concept that surface area is the sum of face areas, which is the fundamental idea.
- **Height vs. side length confusion:** The most common error is using the triangle's height as a side length for the rectangles (or vice versa). Stress: "The triangle height is the perpendicular distance inside the triangle. The triangle sides are the edges of the prism that connect to the rectangles."
- **Label everything:** Require students to make a table listing all five faces with dimensions and areas. This organized approach prevents errors.
- **Real-world context matters:** The tent problem (Worked Example B) shows that real-world surface area problems often involve fewer than all faces. Ask students: "Would a fish tank need all six faces covered?" (No — the top is open.)
- **Right triangle bases:** Right triangles are the easiest triangular bases because one leg is clearly the height. Use these for initial practice with struggling learners.
- **Connection to nets:** Keep reinforcing the net connection. If a student is stuck, say: "Imagine unfolding the prism. Draw the net. Now find the area of each piece."
- **Preparation for Lesson 8:** Tomorrow students transition from surface area to volume. Remind them of the distinction: "Surface area is the outside. Volume is the inside."
