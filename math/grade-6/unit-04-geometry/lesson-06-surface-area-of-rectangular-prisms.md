# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 4, Lesson 6

## Surface Area of Rectangular Prisms

### Learning Objectives
1. Define surface area as the total area of all faces of a 3D shape.
2. Use a net to identify and calculate the area of each face of a rectangular prism.
3. Apply the formula SA = 2(lw + lh + wh) to find the surface area of rectangular prisms.
4. Solve real-world problems involving surface area.

### Prerequisites
- Identify faces, edges, and vertices of rectangular prisms (Lesson 5)
- Unfold 3D shapes into nets (Lesson 5)
- Calculate the area of rectangles

### Materials
- Rectangular prism nets on cardstock (from Lesson 5 or new copies)
- Small rectangular boxes (cereal boxes, tissue boxes, etc.)
- Rulers
- Grid paper
- Colored pencils
- Wrapping paper scraps (optional, for real-world activity)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Display the following:**

A rectangular prism is 6 cm long, 4 cm wide, and 3 cm tall.

1. How many faces does it have?
2. Name the shape of each face.
3. Find the area of the top face.
4. Find the area of the front face.

(Answers: 6 faces; all rectangles; top = 6 x 4 = 24 cm²; front = 6 x 3 = 18 cm²)

"Today we are going to find the total area of all six faces. This total is called the surface area."

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Surface area (SA):** The total area of all the faces of a three-dimensional shape, measured in square units.
- **Opposite faces:** Faces on opposite sides of a prism; they are always congruent (same shape and size).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete: Wrapping a Box:**

Hold up a small rectangular box. Hold up a piece of wrapping paper.

"If I wanted to wrap this box with paper — no overlap, no waste — how much paper would I need? I would need enough paper to cover every face. That amount of paper is the surface area."

"Let me unfold the net and figure this out."

**Step 2 — From Net to Surface Area:**

Draw or display the net of a rectangular prism: l = 8 cm, w = 5 cm, h = 3 cm.

Label each face on the net:

"A rectangular prism has three pairs of opposite faces:"

| Face Pair | Dimensions | Area of One | Area of Pair |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Top and Bottom | 8 cm x 5 cm | 40 cm² | 80 cm² |
| Front and Back | 8 cm x 3 cm | 24 cm² | 48 cm² |
| Left and Right | 5 cm x 3 cm | 15 cm² | 30 cm² |

"Now I add all three pairs:"

SA = 80 + 48 + 30 = **158 cm²**

**Step 3 — The Formula:**

"Let me show you a shortcut. If l = length, w = width, and h = height:"

- Top/Bottom pair: 2 x (l x w) = 2lw
- Front/Back pair: 2 x (l x h) = 2lh
- Left/Right pair: 2 x (w x h) = 2wh

**SA = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh**

Or equivalently: **SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)**

"Let me verify: SA = 2(8 x 5 + 8 x 3 + 5 x 3) = 2(40 + 24 + 15) = 2(79) = 158 cm². Same answer."

**Step 4 — Units Matter:**

"Surface area is measured in square units — cm², in², ft² — because we are covering flat surfaces. Do not write cubic units. Cubic units are for volume, which we will learn later."

### Worked Example A

**Problem:** Find the surface area of a rectangular prism with l = 10 in, w = 6 in, h = 4 in.

SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)
SA = 2(10 x 6 + 10 x 4 + 6 x 4)
SA = 2(60 + 40 + 24)
SA = 2(124)
SA = 248 in²

"Step by step: multiply each pair, add, then double. Always show your work clearly."

### Worked Example B

**Problem:** A gift box is 12 cm long, 8 cm wide, and 5 cm tall. How much wrapping paper is needed to cover it completely?

SA = 2(12 x 8 + 12 x 5 + 8 x 5)
SA = 2(96 + 60 + 40)
SA = 2(196)
SA = 392 cm²

"You would need at least 392 cm² of wrapping paper."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

### Activity 1 — Net Method

"Here is a net of a rectangular prism with l = 7 cm, w = 4 cm, h = 3 cm. Color each pair of opposite faces the same color, then find the area of each face and the total surface area."

| Face | Color | Dimensions | Area |
|------|-------|-----------|------|
| Top | Blue | 7 x 4 | 28 cm² |
| Bottom | Blue | 7 x 4 | 28 cm² |
| Front | Red | 7 x 3 | 21 cm² |
| Back | Red | 7 x 3 | 21 cm² |
| Left | Green | 4 x 3 | 12 cm² |
| Right | Green | 4 x 3 | 12 cm² |

SA = 28 + 28 + 21 + 21 + 12 + 12 = **122 cm²**

### Activity 2 — Formula Method

Use the formula to find the surface area. Work with a partner.

1. l = 9 cm, w = 5 cm, h = 2 cm
   → SA = 2(45 + 18 + 10) = 2(73) = **146 cm²**

2. l = 11 in, w = 7 in, h = 4 in
   → SA = 2(77 + 44 + 28) = 2(149) = **298 in²**

"Check: did you and your partner get the same answers? If not, compare your steps to find the error."

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 — Developing

Find the surface area of each rectangular prism.

1. l = 5 cm, w = 3 cm, h = 2 cm → SA = 2(15 + 10 + 6) = 2(31) = **62 cm²**
2. l = 8 in, w = 4 in, h = 3 in → SA = 2(32 + 24 + 12) = 2(68) = **136 in²**
3. l = 6 ft, w = 6 ft, h = 6 ft → SA = 2(36 + 36 + 36) = 2(108) = **216 ft²**
4. l = 10 m, w = 5 m, h = 4 m → SA = 2(50 + 40 + 20) = 2(110) = **220 m²**
5. What is special about problem 3? What shape is it? → **It is a cube. All faces are identical squares.**

### Tier 2 — Proficient

1. l = 12 cm, w = 7 cm, h = 5 cm → SA = 2(84 + 60 + 35) = 2(179) = **358 cm²**
2. l = 9.5 in, w = 6 in, h = 4 in → SA = 2(57 + 38 + 24) = 2(119) = **238 in²**
3. A shoebox is 30 cm long, 18 cm wide, and 12 cm tall. How much cardboard is used to make the box (not counting the lid separately)? → SA = 2(540 + 360 + 216) = 2(1,116) = **2,232 cm²**
4. A cube has a surface area of 150 cm². What is the area of one face? What is the edge length? → **One face = 150 ÷ 6 = 25 cm². Edge length = 5 cm.**
5. Two rectangular prisms: Prism A is 10 x 5 x 3, Prism B is 8 x 6 x 4. Which has the greater surface area? → **Prism A: 2(50+30+15) = 190 cm². Prism B: 2(48+32+24) = 208 cm². Prism B is greater.**

### Tier 3 — Advanced

1. l = 15.5 cm, w = 8.2 cm, h = 4.6 cm → SA = 2(127.1 + 71.3 + 37.72) = 2(236.12) = **472.24 cm²**
2. A rectangular prism has a surface area of 340 in². Its length is 10 in and width is 8 in. What is its height? → 340 = 2(80 + 10h + 8h) → 170 = 80 + 18h → 90 = 18h → **h = 5 in**
3. You need to paint all six faces of a storage container that is 2 m x 1.5 m x 1 m. One can of paint covers 5 m². How many cans do you need? → SA = 2(3 + 2 + 1.5) = 13 m². 13 ÷ 5 = 2.6, so **3 cans.**
4. A company ships products in boxes that are 20 cm x 15 cm x 10 cm. They want to reduce packaging by 20%. If they keep the length and width the same, what should the new height be? → Original SA = 2(300+200+150) = 1300. Target: 1300 x 0.8 = 1040. 1040 = 2(300 + 20h + 15h) → 520 = 300 + 35h → h = 220/35 = **approximately 6.3 cm.**
5. Explain why a cube has the smallest surface area for a given volume among all rectangular prisms. (Hint: Try different prisms with a volume of 64 cm³.) → **Student explores: 4x4x4 cube SA=96; 8x4x2 prism SA=112; 16x2x2 prism SA=136. The more "stretched" the shape, the greater the surface area.**

**Problem 3:** Is surface area measured in square units or cubic units? Explain why.

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. SA = 2(45 + 27 + 15) = 2(87) = **174 cm²**
2. SA = 6 x (7 x 7) = 6 x 49 = **294 in²**
3. **Surface area is measured in square units because we are finding the area of flat surfaces (faces). Area is always in square units. Cubic units are for volume — the space inside a shape.**

## Teacher Notes

- **Net-first approach:** Always start with the net method before introducing the formula. Students who understand the net can reconstruct the formula if they forget it. Students who only memorize the formula have no fallback.
- **Color-coding pairs:** Having students color opposite faces the same color reinforces the concept that a rectangular prism has three pairs of congruent faces. This directly connects to the "2" in the formula.
- **Common error — forgetting to double:** Students often calculate lw + lh + wh but forget to multiply by 2. Remind them: "You found three face areas, but each one has a matching face on the opposite side."
- **Common error — confusing SA and volume:** Students may write cubic units or try to use l x w x h. Reinforce: "Surface area is about the outside — the wrapping paper. Volume is about the inside — filling it up."
- **Real-world extension:** Bring in actual boxes and rulers. Have students measure dimensions and calculate how much wrapping paper they would need. This is engaging and reinforces the practical meaning of surface area.
- **Cube shortcut:** For a cube with edge s: SA = 6s². This is worth mentioning because cubes appear frequently. All six faces are identical squares.
- **Preparation for Lesson 7:** Preview that tomorrow students will find the surface area of a different kind of prism — the triangular prism — where some faces are triangles instead of all rectangles.
