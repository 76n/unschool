# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Geometry Problem Solving

### Learning Objectives
1. Identify which formula (area, surface area, or volume) is appropriate for a given real-world problem.
2. Solve multi-step geometry problems that combine area, surface area, and volume.
3. Justify the choice of formula and strategy for each problem.
4. Communicate mathematical reasoning clearly using geometric vocabulary.

### Prerequisites
- Area of parallelograms, triangles, trapezoids, and composite figures (Lessons 1-4)
- Surface area of rectangular and triangular prisms (Lessons 6-7)
- Volume of rectangular prisms with whole and fractional edges (Lessons 8-9)
- All key vocabulary from Lessons 1-9

### Materials
- Formula reference sheet (all unit formulas)
- Grid paper
- Rulers
- Colored pencils
- Real-world problem handout (prepared by facilitator)

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- Review all vocabulary from Lessons 1-9 (see Facilitator Guide for the complete list).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — A Problem-Solving Framework:**

Write on the board:

**GEOMETRY PROBLEM-SOLVING STEPS:**
1. **Read** — What is the problem asking for?
2. **Identify** — Is this area, surface area, or volume? How do you know?
3. **Choose** — Which formula or strategy applies?
4. **Solve** — Show all steps clearly.
5. **Check** — Are the units correct? Does the answer make sense?

"Let me walk you through how to use this framework."

**Step 2 — Distinguishing Problem Types:**

"Here are three clues to determine what a problem is asking:"

| Clue | Measurement | Think... |
|------|------------|----------|
| Covering a flat surface (floor, wall, garden) | Area | "How much paint, carpet, or grass?" |
| Covering the outside of a 3D shape | Surface Area | "How much wrapping paper, cardboard, or fabric?" |
| Filling the inside of a 3D shape | Volume | "How much water, sand, soil, or air?" |

"When you read a problem, look for action words: cover, fill, wrap, paint, hold, pack."

### Worked Example A — Area Problem

**Problem:** A park has the shape of a trapezoid with bases of 120 m and 80 m and a height of 60 m. A rectangular playground (30 m x 20 m) sits inside the park. What is the area of the grassy part of the park?

**Step 1:** The problem asks for the grassy area — a flat surface measurement.
**Step 2:** This is an area problem (composite figure — subtraction).
**Step 3:** Area of trapezoid minus area of rectangle.

Park area = 1/2 x (120 + 80) x 60 = 1/2 x 200 x 60 = 6,000 m²
Playground area = 30 x 20 = 600 m²
Grassy area = 6,000 - 600 = **5,400 m²**

### Worked Example B — Multi-Step SA + Volume

**Problem:** Marcus is building a wooden chest that is 3 ft long, 2 ft wide, and 2 ft tall. He needs to paint the outside and fill it with packing peanuts. How much paint does he need (in square feet)? How many cubic feet of packing peanuts will it hold?

**Paint (surface area):**
SA = 2(3x2 + 3x2 + 2x2) = 2(6 + 6 + 4) = 2(16) = 32 ft²

**Packing peanuts (volume):**
V = 3 x 2 x 2 = 12 ft³

"Same box, two different questions. The outside is surface area; the inside is volume."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

### Activity 1 — Identify the Measurement

"For each problem, tell your partner: Is this area, surface area, or volume? Do not solve yet — just identify."

1. How many liters of water fill an aquarium? → **Volume**
2. How much tile covers a bathroom floor shaped like a rectangle with a triangular alcove? → **Area**
3. How much aluminum foil wraps a triangular prism-shaped sandwich? → **Surface Area**
4. A farmer wants to fence the perimeter of a triangular field. → **Neither — this is perimeter!**
5. How much soil fills a rectangular planter with dimensions 4 ft x 2 1/2 ft x 1 ft? → **Volume**

### Activity 2 — Solve Together

**Problem:** A storage shed has a rectangular floor that is 12 ft x 8 ft. The walls are 7 ft tall. The roof is shaped like a triangular prism with a triangle base of 12 ft and height of 3 ft.

**(a)** What is the floor area?
Floor area = 12 x 8 = **96 ft²**

**(b)** What is the volume of the rectangular part of the shed?
V = 12 x 8 x 7 = **672 ft³**

**(c)** What is the area of one triangular end of the roof?
A = 1/2 x 12 x 3 = **18 ft²**

"Notice how one real-world object can require multiple geometry skills."

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 — Developing

1. A triangular garden has a base of 8 ft and a height of 5 ft. How many square feet of mulch are needed to cover it?
   → A = 1/2 x 8 x 5 = **20 ft²**

2. A toy box is 2 ft long, 1.5 ft wide, and 1 ft tall. How much space is inside for toys?
   → V = 2 x 1.5 x 1 = **3 ft³**

3. A gift box is 10 in x 6 in x 4 in. How much wrapping paper is needed?
   → SA = 2(60 + 40 + 24) = 2(124) = **248 in²**

4. A parallelogram-shaped tabletop has a base of 4 ft and a height of 2.5 ft. What is its area?
   → A = 4 x 2.5 = **10 ft²**

5. A rectangular fish tank is 24 in x 12 in x 12 in. How many cubic inches of water can it hold?
   → V = 24 x 12 x 12 = **3,456 in³**

### Tier 2 — Proficient

1. A room has the shape of a rectangle (14 ft x 10 ft) with a triangular bay window area (base = 6 ft, height = 4 ft). What is the total floor area?
   → A = (14 x 10) + (1/2 x 6 x 4) = 140 + 12 = **152 ft²**

2. A cereal box is 30 cm tall, 20 cm wide, and 8 cm deep. What is its surface area? What is its volume?
   → SA = 2(600 + 240 + 160) = 2(1,000) = **2,000 cm².** V = 30 x 20 x 8 = **4,800 cm³.**

3. A triangular prism-shaped tent has a triangular face with base 8 ft, height 5 ft, and slant sides of 6.4 ft each. The tent is 10 ft long. How much fabric covers the two triangular ends and two slant sides (no floor)?
   → Triangles: 2 x (1/2 x 8 x 5) = 40. Slant sides: 2 x (10 x 6.4) = 128. Total = **168 ft²**

4. A sandbox is 6 ft long, 4 ft wide, and 1 1/2 ft deep. How many cubic feet of sand are needed to fill it?
   → V = 6 x 4 x 3/2 = **36 ft³**

5. A trapezoidal driveway has bases of 20 ft and 30 ft and a height of 15 ft. Paving costs $4.50 per square foot. What is the total cost?
   → A = 1/2 x (20 + 30) x 15 = 375 ft². Cost = 375 x $4.50 = **$1,687.50**

### Tier 3 — Advanced

1. A company ships products in boxes that are 12 in x 8 in x 6 in. They need the volume to be at least 500 in³ and the surface area to be less than 500 in². Does this box meet both requirements?
   → V = 576 in³ (yes, > 500). SA = 2(96+72+48) = 432 in² (yes, < 500). **Both requirements are met.**

2. An L-shaped room has dimensions: the overall shape is 18 ft x 12 ft, with a 6 ft x 5 ft rectangular section removed from one corner. The ceiling is 9 ft tall. What is the floor area? What is the volume of the room?
   → Floor: (18 x 12) - (6 x 5) = 216 - 30 = **186 ft².** Volume: 186 x 9 = **1,674 ft³.**

3. A triangular prism-shaped planter has a right triangle base with legs 1 1/2 ft and 2 ft (hypotenuse 2 1/2 ft) and is 4 ft long. How much soil fills it? How much wood is needed for all five faces?
   → Volume: base area x length = (1/2 x 3/2 x 2) x 4 = 3/2 x 4 = **6 ft³.**
   → SA: Triangles: 2 x 1.5 = 3. Rectangles: (4 x 1.5) + (4 x 2) + (4 x 2.5) = 6 + 8 + 10 = 24. SA = **27 ft².**

4. A builder is designing a shed. The base is a rectangle 10 ft x 8 ft. The walls are 7 ft tall. The roof is triangular with a height of 3 ft. What is the total surface area of the shed, not including the floor? (Include the four walls, two triangular roof ends, and two rectangular roof panels. The roof panels are each 10 ft long and 5 ft along the slant.)
   → Front wall + back wall: 2 x (10 x 7) = 140. Side walls: 2 x (8 x 7) = 112. Roof triangles: 2 x (1/2 x 8 x 3) = 24. Roof panels: 2 x (10 x 5) = 100. Total: 140 + 112 + 24 + 100 = **376 ft²**

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A rectangular prism box is 15 cm x 10 cm x 8 cm.
(a) What is its volume?
(b) What is its surface area?

**Problem 2:** A composite figure is made of a rectangle (12 ft x 5 ft) with a triangle (base = 12 ft, height = 4 ft) on top. What is its total area?

**Problem 3:** How do you decide whether a problem is asking for area, surface area, or volume?

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. (a) V = 15 x 10 x 8 = **1,200 cm³**
   (b) SA = 2(150 + 120 + 80) = 2(350) = **700 cm²**
2. A = (12 x 5) + (1/2 x 12 x 4) = 60 + 24 = **84 ft²**
3. **Area: covering a flat surface (like a floor or wall). Surface area: covering the outside of a 3D shape (like wrapping a box). Volume: filling the inside of a 3D shape (like water in a tank). Look for key words: cover/paint = area, wrap/coat = surface area, fill/hold = volume.**

## Teacher Notes

- **This is a review and synthesis lesson:** The goal is not to teach new content but to help students integrate and apply all the skills from Lessons 1-9. Focus on problem selection and strategy, not formula memorization.
- **Encourage the 5-step framework:** Read, Identify, Choose, Solve, Check. The biggest leap for students is going from "here is the formula, plug in numbers" to "figure out which formula to use." This is the heart of problem solving.
- **Formula reference sheets:** Allow students to use reference sheets during this lesson. The assessment (next day) should specify whether reference sheets are permitted. The goal today is strategic thinking, not memorization.
- **Common error — area vs. surface area:** Students may calculate the area of one face when asked for surface area, or calculate SA when asked for the floor area. Reinforce: "Area = one flat surface. Surface area = all faces of a 3D shape."
- **Real-world richness:** The best problems have real-world context because context helps students determine what measurement is needed. "How much paint for the walls?" is clearer than "Find the surface area of this rectangular prism."
- **Differentiation:** Tier 1 problems each require a single formula. Tier 2 problems require two steps or a composite approach. Tier 3 problems combine multiple concepts in one scenario.
- **Assessment preparation:** Remind students that the unit assessment is next. Encourage them to review their exit tickets from all lessons and the formula reference sheet. Identify any concepts that need last-minute review.
- **Celebrate growth:** This is the last lesson before the assessment. Acknowledge how much students have learned: "You started this unit knowing how to find the area of a rectangle. Now you can find the area of parallelograms, triangles, trapezoids, and composite figures, the surface area of prisms, and the volume of rectangular prisms with fractions. That is a lot of growth."
