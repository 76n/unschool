# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 4, Lesson 9

## Volume with Fractional Edge Lengths

### Learning Objectives
1. Calculate the volume of rectangular prisms with fractional edge lengths using V = l x w x h.
2. Explain how a prism can be packed with unit cubes that have fractional edge lengths.
3. Connect the formula V = l x w x h to the cube-packing model for fractional dimensions.
4. Solve real-world volume problems involving fractions and mixed numbers.

### Prerequisites
- Calculate the volume of rectangular prisms with whole-number edges (Lesson 8)
- Multiply fractions and mixed numbers (Grade 5-6, Unit 2)
- Understand that V = l x w x h counts the number of unit cubes that fill a prism

### Materials
- Unit cubes (1 cm) — for demonstration
- Grid paper
- Rulers
- Colored pencils
- Fraction reference cards (optional, for struggling learners)

**Fraction multiplication review:**

1. 1/2 x 1/2 = ?  → 1/4
2. 3/2 x 4 = ?  → 6
3. 2 1/2 x 3 = ?  → 7 1/2
4. 1/2 x 1/2 x 1/2 = ?  → 1/8

"Today we use the same volume formula as yesterday — V = l x w x h — but the edge lengths will be fractions. You will need your fraction multiplication skills."

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Fractional edge length:** A dimension of a rectangular prism that is a fraction or mixed number (e.g., 3 1/2 cm).
- **Fractional unit cube:** A cube whose edges are a unit fraction (e.g., 1/2 cm), used to pack prisms with fractional dimensions.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — The Problem with Whole Unit Cubes:**

Draw a rectangular prism: l = 3 1/2 cm, w = 2 cm, h = 1 cm.

"Yesterday, we filled prisms with 1 cm cubes. Let me try that here."

"Along the length, I can fit 3 whole cubes, but there is a 1/2 cm gap. Whole cubes do not fit perfectly."

"So I need smaller cubes — cubes with a fractional edge length."

**Step 2 — Packing with Fractional Cubes:**

"Let me use cubes that are 1/2 cm on each edge."

"Along the 3 1/2 cm length: 3.5 ÷ 0.5 = 7 cubes."
"Along the 2 cm width: 2 ÷ 0.5 = 4 cubes."
"Along the 1 cm height: 1 ÷ 0.5 = 2 cubes."

"Total small cubes: 7 x 4 x 2 = 56 cubes."

"Each small cube has a volume of 1/2 x 1/2 x 1/2 = 1/8 cm³."

"Total volume: 56 x 1/8 = 56/8 = 7 cm³."

**Step 3 — Verify with the Formula:**

"Let me check using V = l x w x h."

V = 3 1/2 x 2 x 1
V = 7/2 x 2 x 1
V = 7 cm³

"Same answer. The formula works perfectly for fractional edge lengths. The cube-packing method explains why the formula works, and the formula gives us the answer quickly."

**Step 4 — Why This Matters:**

"In the real world, measurements are almost never perfect whole numbers. A box might be 8 1/4 inches long. A planter might be 2 1/2 feet wide. The formula V = l x w x h works for any dimensions — whole numbers, fractions, or mixed numbers."

### Worked Example A

**Problem:** Find the volume of a rectangular prism with l = 4 1/2 cm, w = 3 cm, h = 2 cm.

V = 4 1/2 x 3 x 2
V = 9/2 x 3 x 2
V = 27/2 x 2
V = 27 cm³

"Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first, then multiply."

### Worked Example B

**Problem:** A rectangular prism has dimensions 2 1/2 in x 1 1/2 in x 3 in. Find its volume. Then determine how many 1/2-inch unit cubes would pack the prism.

**Part 1 — Formula:**
V = 5/2 x 3/2 x 3
V = 15/4 x 3
V = 45/4
V = 11 1/4 in³

**Part 2 — Cube packing:**
Along length: 2.5 ÷ 0.5 = 5 cubes
Along width: 1.5 ÷ 0.5 = 3 cubes
Along height: 3 ÷ 0.5 = 6 cubes
Total cubes: 5 x 3 x 6 = 90 cubes
Each cube: (1/2)³ = 1/8 in³
Total volume: 90 x 1/8 = 90/8 = 11 1/4 in³ ✓

"Both methods confirm the answer."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

### Activity 1 — Formula Practice

Find the volume of each rectangular prism. Show your work with fractions.

1. l = 5 1/2 cm, w = 4 cm, h = 2 cm
   → V = 11/2 x 4 x 2 = 44 cm³. **Wait — let me recalculate:** 11/2 x 4 = 22, 22 x 2 = **44 cm³**

2. l = 3 cm, w = 2 1/2 cm, h = 1 1/2 cm
   → V = 3 x 5/2 x 3/2 = 45/4 = **11 1/4 cm³**

"Compare answers with your partner. Did you convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying?"

### Activity 2 — Cube Packing Connection

"A prism is 2 cm x 1 1/2 cm x 1 cm. Use 1/2 cm cubes to pack it."

- Along 2 cm: 4 cubes
- Along 1 1/2 cm: 3 cubes
- Along 1 cm: 2 cubes
- Total: 4 x 3 x 2 = 24 cubes
- Volume of each: 1/8 cm³
- Total volume: 24/8 = **3 cm³**

"Verify: V = 2 x 3/2 x 1 = 3 cm³. ✓"

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 — Developing

Find the volume of each rectangular prism.

1. l = 3 1/2 cm, w = 2 cm, h = 4 cm → V = 7/2 x 2 x 4 = **28 cm³**
2. l = 5 cm, w = 2 1/2 cm, h = 2 cm → V = 5 x 5/2 x 2 = **25 cm³**
3. l = 6 cm, w = 1 1/2 cm, h = 3 cm → V = 6 x 3/2 x 3 = **27 cm³**
4. l = 4 cm, w = 4 cm, h = 2 1/2 cm → V = 4 x 4 x 5/2 = **40 cm³**
5. A box is 10 in long, 5 in wide, and 3 1/2 in tall. What is its volume? → V = 10 x 5 x 7/2 = **175 in³**

### Tier 2 — Proficient

1. l = 4 1/2 cm, w = 3 1/2 cm, h = 2 cm → V = 9/2 x 7/2 x 2 = 63/2 = **31 1/2 cm³**
2. l = 6 1/4 in, w = 4 in, h = 2 in → V = 25/4 x 4 x 2 = **50 in³**
3. l = 2 1/2 ft, w = 1 1/2 ft, h = 3 1/2 ft → V = 5/2 x 3/2 x 7/2 = 105/8 = **13 1/8 ft³**
4. A rectangular planter is 3 1/2 ft long, 1 1/2 ft wide, and 1 ft deep. How many cubic feet of soil does it hold? → V = 7/2 x 3/2 x 1 = 21/4 = **5 1/4 ft³**
5. A prism has a volume of 15 cm³. Its length is 2 1/2 cm and width is 3 cm. What is its height? → 15 = 5/2 x 3 x h → 15 = 15h/2 → h = **2 cm**
6. How many 1/2-inch cubes fit in a box that is 3 in x 2 in x 1 1/2 in? → 6 x 4 x 3 = **72 cubes**

### Tier 3 — Advanced

1. l = 5 3/4 cm, w = 2 2/3 cm, h = 3 cm → V = 23/4 x 8/3 x 3 = 46 cm³. **Let me recalculate:** 23/4 x 8/3 = 184/12 = 46/3; 46/3 x 3 = **46 cm³**
2. A rectangular prism has a volume of 22 1/2 in³. Its length is 3 in and its width is 2 1/2 in. Find its height. → 22.5 = 3 x 2.5 x h → 22.5 = 7.5h → h = **3 in**
3. A sandbox is 6 1/2 ft long, 4 ft wide, and 1 1/4 ft deep. Sand costs $3.50 per cubic foot. How much does it cost to fill the sandbox? → V = 13/2 x 4 x 5/4 = 65/2 = 32.5 ft³. Cost = 32.5 x $3.50 = **$113.75**
4. A box is 2 1/2 cm x 1 1/2 cm x 1 cm. How many 1/4-cm cubes would pack this box? What is the volume of each small cube? Verify that the total volume matches V = l x w x h. → Along each edge: 10, 6, 4 cubes. Total: 240 cubes. Each cube: 1/64 cm³. Volume: 240/64 = 3.75 = **3 3/4 cm³**. Formula: 5/2 x 3/2 x 1 = 15/4 = 3 3/4 cm³. ✓
5. Explain in your own words why we can use unit cubes with fractional edge lengths to find the volume of a prism. How does this connect to V = l x w x h? → **When edges are fractional, whole unit cubes leave gaps. Smaller cubes with fractional edges fill the space exactly. The number of small cubes times the volume of each small cube equals l x w x h. This shows the formula works for all dimensions, not just whole numbers.**

## Teacher Notes

- **Fraction skills are critical:** This lesson combines geometry and fraction operations. If students struggle, the bottleneck is usually fraction multiplication, not the volume concept. Identify whether errors are geometric or arithmetic.
- **Cube-packing is for understanding, not speed:** The cube-packing method is conceptually powerful (it proves the formula works for fractions) but slow for calculation. Students should understand it, then use V = l x w x h for efficiency.
- **Mixed number conversion:** The most common procedural error is failing to convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying. Reinforce: "Always convert first, multiply second."
- **Units with fractions:** Students may write fractional cubic units correctly (e.g., 11 1/4 cm³) but question whether a volume can be a fraction. Affirm: "Absolutely — most real-world volumes are not whole numbers."
- **Connection to 6.G.2:** This lesson directly addresses standard 6.G.2, which specifically requires students to understand volume through packing with fractional unit cubes. The cube-packing method in Guided Practice Activity 2 is not optional — it is a required component of the standard.
- **Decimal alternative:** Some students may prefer converting fractions to decimals (e.g., 3 1/2 = 3.5) and multiplying. This is valid and sometimes easier. Accept both approaches.
- **Preparation for Lesson 10:** The next lesson is a problem-solving review combining all geometry concepts. Encourage students to review all formulas: area of parallelograms, triangles, trapezoids, composite figures, surface area, and volume.
