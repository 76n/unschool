# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 4, Lesson 4

## Area of Composite Figures

### Learning Objectives
1. Identify the simple shapes that make up a composite figure.
2. Decompose composite figures into rectangles, triangles, parallelograms, and trapezoids.
3. Calculate the area of composite figures by adding or subtracting areas of simpler shapes.
4. Choose the most efficient decomposition strategy for a given figure.

### Prerequisites
- Calculate the area of rectangles, parallelograms, triangles, and trapezoids (Lessons 1-3)
- Add and subtract decimals
- Identify shapes within larger figures

### Materials
- Grid paper (1 cm)
- Rulers
- Colored pencils (at least 3 colors per student)
- Composite figure handouts (prepared by facilitator)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Formula Review — Fill in the blanks:**

1. Area of a rectangle: A = ______ x ______ (l x w)
2. Area of a parallelogram: A = ______ x ______ (b x h)
3. Area of a triangle: A = ______ x ______ x ______ (1/2 x b x h)
4. Area of a trapezoid: A = ______ x (______ + ______) x ______ (1/2 x (b1 + b2) x h)

"You now have four area formulas in your toolbox. Today you are going to use them together to find the area of shapes that are not simple rectangles, triangles, or trapezoids — they are combinations."

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Composite figure:** A shape made up of two or more simple geometric shapes combined together.
- **Decompose:** To break a complex shape into simpler shapes whose areas you can calculate.
- **Additive strategy:** Finding the total area by adding the areas of all the pieces.
- **Subtractive strategy:** Finding the area by starting with a larger simple shape and subtracting the areas of the parts that are missing.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Additive Strategy:**

Draw an L-shaped figure on the board (grid paper style):
- The L is formed by a 10 x 8 rectangle with a 4 x 5 rectangle cut from the top-right corner.

"This shape is called a composite figure because it is made of parts. How can we find its area?"

"One way is to break it into two rectangles. Let me use colored pencils to show the two pieces."

Color Rectangle A (the bottom section): 10 cm x 3 cm = 30 cm²
Color Rectangle B (the left tall section): 6 cm x 8 cm = 48 cm²

Wait — demonstrate that the decomposition depends on where you draw the line.

"Actually, there are different ways to split this. Let me show you another way:"

Rectangle A: 10 cm x 3 cm = 30 cm²
Rectangle B: 6 cm x 5 cm = 30 cm²
Total: 30 + 30 = 60 cm²

Or:

Rectangle A: 10 cm x 8 cm = 80 cm² (the full outer rectangle)
Minus the missing piece: 4 cm x 5 cm = 20 cm²
Total: 80 - 20 = 60 cm²

"Both methods give 60 cm². You can add pieces or subtract the missing piece — whichever is easier."

**Step 2 — Subtractive Strategy:**

Draw a rectangle with a triangular notch cut from one side.
- Outer rectangle: 12 cm x 8 cm
- Triangle notch: base = 4 cm, height = 3 cm

"For this figure, the subtractive method is easier. Start with the full rectangle, then subtract the triangle."

A = (12 x 8) - (1/2 x 4 x 3)
A = 96 - 6
A = 90 cm²

**Step 3 — Mixed Shapes:**

Draw a figure that is a rectangle with a triangle on top (like a simple house shape).
- Rectangle: 8 cm x 6 cm
- Triangle on top: base = 8 cm, height = 3 cm

"This composite figure has a rectangle and a triangle. I add their areas."

A = (8 x 6) + (1/2 x 8 x 3)
A = 48 + 12
A = 60 cm²

"The key steps for every composite figure problem:
1. Look at the shape carefully.
2. Decide how to break it apart — or what to subtract.
3. Find the area of each piece.
4. Add or subtract."

### Worked Example A

**Problem:** An L-shaped room has the following measurements: the full length is 14 ft, the full width is 10 ft, and there is a 6 ft x 4 ft rectangular section missing from one corner.

A = (14 x 10) - (6 x 4)
A = 140 - 24
A = 116 ft²

"We used the subtractive method. The full rectangle minus the missing corner."

### Worked Example B

**Problem:** A figure is made of a rectangle (12 cm x 5 cm) with a triangle (base = 12 cm, height = 4 cm) attached to one side.

A = (12 x 5) + (1/2 x 12 x 4)
A = 60 + 24
A = 84 cm²

"We used the additive method because the figure is two shapes combined."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

### Activity 1 — Identify and Decompose

"For each composite figure I show you, tell your partner: What simple shapes do you see? Then calculate the total area."

**Figure 1:** A T-shape on grid paper.
- Top rectangle: 10 cm x 3 cm = 30 cm²
- Bottom rectangle: 4 cm x 6 cm = 24 cm²
- Total: **54 cm²**

**Figure 2:** A rectangle (9 cm x 7 cm) with a right triangle removed (base = 3 cm, height = 4 cm).
- Rectangle: 9 x 7 = 63 cm²
- Minus triangle: 1/2 x 3 x 4 = 6 cm²
- Total: **57 cm²**

### Activity 2 — Choose Your Strategy

"For this figure, try both the additive and subtractive methods with your partner. Do you get the same answer?"

A cross shape (+) inscribed in a 10 x 10 grid. The cross has arms that are 4 cm wide.

**Additive:** Three rectangles: (10 x 4) + (3 x 4) + (3 x 4) = 40 + 12 + 12 = 64 cm²
**Subtractive:** 10 x 10 = 100 minus four corner squares (3 x 3 each) = 100 - 36 = 64 cm²

"Both give 64 cm². The subtractive method was quicker here — fewer calculations."

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 — Developing

1. An L-shaped figure: full rectangle is 8 cm x 6 cm, with a 3 cm x 2 cm piece removed from one corner.
   → A = (8 x 6) - (3 x 2) = 48 - 6 = **42 cm²**

2. A rectangle (10 in x 4 in) with a triangle on top (base = 10 in, height = 3 in).
   → A = (10 x 4) + (1/2 x 10 x 3) = 40 + 15 = **55 in²**

3. Two rectangles joined: one is 5 cm x 4 cm, the other is 7 cm x 4 cm, and they share a 4 cm side.
   → A = (5 x 4) + (7 x 4) = 20 + 28 = **48 cm²**

4. A U-shaped figure: outer rectangle 12 ft x 8 ft, inner rectangular cutout 6 ft x 4 ft.
   → A = (12 x 8) - (6 x 4) = 96 - 24 = **72 ft²**

### Tier 2 — Proficient

1. A pentagon-shaped house: rectangle base 14 cm x 8 cm with a triangle roof (base = 14 cm, height = 5 cm).
   → A = (14 x 8) + (1/2 x 14 x 5) = 112 + 35 = **147 cm²**

2. An arrow shape: rectangle 12 in x 4 in with a triangle point (base = 8 in, height = 5 in).
   → A = (12 x 4) + (1/2 x 8 x 5) = 48 + 20 = **68 in²**

3. A rectangular yard (20 m x 15 m) with a rectangular swimming pool (8 m x 5 m) inside. What is the area of the yard not covered by the pool?
   → A = (20 x 15) - (8 x 5) = 300 - 40 = **260 m²**

4. An L-shaped room: long section 16 ft x 10 ft, short section 8 ft x 6 ft, joined along the 10 ft side. The total length is 16 ft and total width is 16 ft.
   → A = (16 x 10) + (8 x 6) = 160 + 48 = **208 ft²**

5. Decompose the L-shaped room from problem 4 using the subtractive method. Do you get the same answer?
   → A = (16 x 16) - (8 x 10) = 256 - 80... (student must identify correct dimensions for subtraction based on diagram)

### Tier 3 — Advanced

1. A figure made of a trapezoid (b1 = 10 cm, b2 = 6 cm, h = 4 cm) with a rectangle (6 cm x 3 cm) on top.
   → A = [1/2 x (10 + 6) x 4] + (6 x 3) = 32 + 18 = **50 cm²**

2. A 20 ft x 20 ft square room has two triangular alcoves cut from opposite corners. Each triangle has a base of 5 ft and height of 5 ft. What is the remaining floor area?
   → A = (20 x 20) - 2 x (1/2 x 5 x 5) = 400 - 25 = **375 ft²**

3. Design a composite figure with an area of exactly 100 cm² using at least three different shapes. Draw it on grid paper, label all dimensions, and show your area calculation.
   → **Open-ended. Student must show at least three shapes, correct dimensions, and calculations totaling 100 cm².**

4. A park has the shape of a rectangle (50 m x 30 m) with a triangular section added to one short side (base = 30 m, height = 12 m) and a square fountain (4 m x 4 m) in the center. What is the total grassy area?
   → A = (50 x 30) + (1/2 x 30 x 12) - (4 x 4) = 1500 + 180 - 16 = **1,664 m²**

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** A composite figure is made of a rectangle (9 cm x 5 cm) and a triangle (base = 9 cm, height = 4 cm) attached to one side. Find the total area.

**Problem 2:** A 15 in x 10 in rectangle has a 5 in x 3 in rectangle removed from one corner. Find the remaining area.

**Problem 3:** Which strategy did you use for each problem — additive or subtractive? Why?

---

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. A = (9 x 5) + (1/2 x 9 x 4) = 45 + 18 = **63 cm²**
2. A = (15 x 10) - (5 x 3) = 150 - 15 = **135 in²**
3. **Problem 1: Additive, because two shapes are combined. Problem 2: Subtractive, because a piece is removed from a larger shape.**

## Teacher Notes

- **Decomposition is a skill, not just a step:** Students often struggle not with the formulas but with deciding how to break a figure apart. Spend time on the "seeing shapes within shapes" skill. Use colored pencils to highlight different regions.
- **Multiple correct decompositions:** Emphasize that there is usually more than one correct way to decompose a figure. Celebrate when students find different approaches that yield the same answer.
- **Common error — double-counting or missing pieces:** Students sometimes count an overlapping region twice or forget a piece. Encourage them to color each region a different color and check that the entire figure is covered with no overlaps.
- **Subtractive method misconceptions:** Some students struggle with the idea of subtracting a piece that "isn't there." Use a physical analogy: "Imagine you had the full rectangle and then cut out a piece. The leftover is the composite figure."
- **Grid paper is essential:** For this lesson, grid paper allows students to verify their calculations by counting squares. This builds confidence in the decomposition approach.
- **Transition to 3D:** This lesson completes the 2D portion of the unit. Remind students that in the next lesson, they will apply these 2D area skills to 3D shapes. "You will need to find the area of rectangles and triangles when we work with 3D shapes — so keep these formulas sharp."
