**Time:** 45-60 minutes
**Standards Assessed:** 6.G.1, 6.G.2, 6.G.3, 6.G.4

## Section 1: Area of Polygons (16 points)

**Standards: 6.G.1**

**1.** Find the area of a parallelogram with a base of 13 cm and a height of 7 cm. **(2 points)**

**2.** Find the area of a triangle with a base of 18 inches and a height of 10 inches. **(2 points)**

**3.** Find the area of a trapezoid with bases of 11 cm and 7 cm and a height of 6 cm. **(2 points)**

**4.** A parallelogram has an area of 96 ft² and a height of 8 ft. What is the length of its base? **(2 points)**

**5.** A triangle has an area of 42 m² and a base of 12 m. What is its height? **(2 points)**

**6.** A triangular sign has a base of 3 feet and a height of 2.5 feet. How many square feet of paint are needed to cover one side of the sign? **(2 points)**

**7.** A trapezoid has bases of 15 in and 9 in, a height of 8 in, and slant sides of 10 in and 10 in. What is its area? Show which measurements you use and which you do not use, and explain why. **(4 points)**

## Section 2: Composite Figures (10 points)

**Standards: 6.G.1, 6.G.3**

**8.** A figure is made of a rectangle (10 cm x 6 cm) with a right triangle attached to one of the shorter sides (base = 6 cm, height = 4 cm). Find the total area. **(3 points)**

**9.** An L-shaped room has the following dimensions: the overall outer rectangle is 16 ft x 12 ft, with a 6 ft x 4 ft rectangular section removed from one corner. What is the floor area of the room? **(3 points)**

**10.** A homeowner wants to tile a kitchen floor shaped like a rectangle (14 ft x 10 ft) with a triangular breakfast nook (base = 8 ft, height = 5 ft) extending from one side. Each tile covers 2 square feet. How many tiles are needed? **(4 points)**

## Section 3: Nets and Surface Area (16 points)

**Standards: 6.G.4**

**11.** How many faces, edges, and vertices does a triangular prism have? **(3 points — 1 point each)**

**12.** A rectangular prism is 9 cm long, 5 cm wide, and 4 cm tall. List the dimensions and area of each pair of opposite faces, then find the total surface area. **(4 points)**

**13.** A cube has edges of 6 inches. What is its surface area? **(2 points)**

**14.** A triangular prism has a right-triangle base with legs 3 cm and 4 cm (hypotenuse 5 cm). The prism is 10 cm long. Find the surface area by calculating the area of each face. **(4 points)**

**15.** Maria wants to wrap a gift box that is 25 cm long, 15 cm wide, and 10 cm tall. She has a sheet of wrapping paper that is 70 cm x 50 cm. Does she have enough paper? Show your work. **(3 points)**

## Section 4: Volume (10 points)

**Standards: 6.G.2**

**16.** Find the volume of a rectangular prism with l = 11 cm, w = 8 cm, and h = 5 cm. **(2 points)**

**17.** A rectangular storage bin is 3 ft long, 2 ft wide, and 1 1/2 ft tall. What is its volume? **(2 points)**

**18.** Find the volume of a rectangular prism with l = 4 1/2 cm, w = 2 1/2 cm, and h = 2 cm. **(3 points)**

**19.** A rectangular prism has a volume of 360 in³. Its length is 12 in and its width is 6 in. What is its height? **(3 points)**

## Section 5: Problem Solving (8 points)

**Standards: 6.G.1, 6.G.2, 6.G.4**

**20.** Jenna is building a planter box for her garden. The planter is shaped like a rectangular prism and measures 4 ft long, 2 ft wide, and 1 1/2 ft deep.

**(a)** How many cubic feet of soil does Jenna need to fill the planter? **(2 points)**

**(b)** Jenna wants to paint the outside of the planter (all four sides and the bottom, but not the top). How many square feet does she need to paint? **(3 points)**

**(c)** If soil costs $5 per cubic foot and paint covers 40 square feet per can, how much will the soil cost and how many cans of paint does Jenna need? **(3 points)**

## Answer Key

### Section 1: Area of Polygons

**1.** A = b x h = 13 x 7 = **91 cm²**

**2.** A = 1/2 x b x h = 1/2 x 18 x 10 = **90 in²**

**3.** A = 1/2 x (b1 + b2) x h = 1/2 x (11 + 7) x 6 = 1/2 x 18 x 6 = **54 cm²**

**4.** A = b x h → 96 = b x 8 → b = 96 ÷ 8 = **12 ft**

**5.** A = 1/2 x b x h → 42 = 1/2 x 12 x h → 42 = 6h → h = **7 m**

**6.** A = 1/2 x 3 x 2.5 = **3.75 ft²**

**7.** Use bases (15 in and 9 in) and height (8 in). Do not use the slant sides (10 in) because they are not perpendicular to the bases. A = 1/2 x (15 + 9) x 8 = 1/2 x 24 x 8 = **96 in²**. The slant sides are the non-parallel sides; the height is the perpendicular distance between the bases, which is what determines the area.

### Section 2: Composite Figures

**8.** Rectangle: 10 x 6 = 60 cm². Triangle: 1/2 x 6 x 4 = 12 cm². Total: 60 + 12 = **72 cm²**

**9.** Full rectangle: 16 x 12 = 192 ft². Removed section: 6 x 4 = 24 ft². Floor area: 192 - 24 = **168 ft²**

**10.** Rectangle: 14 x 10 = 140 ft². Triangle: 1/2 x 8 x 5 = 20 ft². Total: 140 + 20 = 160 ft². Tiles: 160 ÷ 2 = **80 tiles**

### Section 3: Nets and Surface Area

**11.** Faces: **5**. Edges: **9**. Vertices: **6**.

**12.**
- Top/Bottom: 9 x 5 = 45 cm² each → 90 cm²
- Front/Back: 9 x 4 = 36 cm² each → 72 cm²
- Left/Right: 5 x 4 = 20 cm² each → 40 cm²
- SA = 90 + 72 + 40 = **202 cm²**

**13.** SA = 6 x (6 x 6) = 6 x 36 = **216 in²**

**14.**
- Two triangular bases: 2 x (1/2 x 3 x 4) = 2 x 6 = 12 cm²
- Rectangle 1 (bottom): 10 x 3 = 30 cm²
- Rectangle 2 (back): 10 x 4 = 40 cm²
- Rectangle 3 (hypotenuse side): 10 x 5 = 50 cm²
- SA = 12 + 30 + 40 + 50 = **132 cm²**

**15.** SA = 2(25x15 + 25x10 + 15x10) = 2(375 + 250 + 150) = 2(775) = **1,550 cm²**. Wrapping paper: 70 x 50 = **3,500 cm²**. Yes, Maria has enough paper (3,500 > 1,550). Note: In practice, she would need more than the minimum SA due to folding and overlapping, but mathematically the paper area exceeds the surface area.

### Section 4: Volume

**16.** V = 11 x 8 x 5 = **440 cm³**

**17.** V = 3 x 2 x 3/2 = **9 ft³**

**18.** V = 9/2 x 5/2 x 2 = 45/4 x 2 = 90/4 = **22 1/2 cm³**

**19.** 360 = 12 x 6 x h → 360 = 72h → h = **5 in**

### Section 5: Problem Solving

**20a.** V = 4 x 2 x 3/2 = **12 ft³**

**20b.** Jenna paints 4 sides + bottom (not the top).
- Two long sides: 2 x (4 x 1.5) = 2 x 6 = 12 ft²
- Two short sides: 2 x (2 x 1.5) = 2 x 3 = 6 ft²
- Bottom: 4 x 2 = 8 ft²
- Total: 12 + 6 + 8 = **26 ft²**

**20c.**
- Soil cost: 12 ft³ x $5 = **$60**
- Paint: 26 ft² ÷ 40 ft²/can = 0.65, so Jenna needs **1 can** of paint.

## Reteaching Recommendations

### If a student scores below 70% on Section 1 (Area of Polygons):
- Reteach Lessons 1-3 using concrete manipulatives (cut-and-rearrange for parallelograms, double-triangle for triangles, double-trapezoid for trapezoids).
- Provide additional practice with identifying the height vs. slant side.
- Use grid paper for all practice so students can verify by counting squares.

### If a student scores below 70% on Section 2 (Composite Figures):
- Reteach Lesson 4 with heavy emphasis on the decomposition strategy.
- Use colored pencils to outline each sub-shape within the composite figure.
- Practice with grid paper before moving to figures without grids.

### If a student scores below 70% on Section 3 (Nets and Surface Area):
- Return to Lesson 5 with physical nets — cutting, folding, and taping builds spatial reasoning.
- For surface area, require students to draw and label the complete net before calculating.
- Use the table method: list every face, its shape, its dimensions, and its area before summing.

### If a student scores below 70% on Section 4 (Volume):
- Return to Lesson 8 with unit cubes — physically fill rectangular containers.
- Reteach the layered approach: base area x height = volume.
- For fractional edges, review fraction multiplication separately before combining with geometry.

### If a student scores below 70% on Section 5 (Problem Solving):
- Focus on the problem identification skill: "Is this area, surface area, or volume?"
- Practice with a sort activity: give students 10 problems and have them categorize without solving.
- Then practice solving one category at a time before mixing.

### General Reteaching Strategies:
- Pair students who scored Approaching with Proficient peers for collaborative practice.
- Provide formula reference cards with labeled diagrams for students who need memory support.
- Allow additional time on a retake assessment for students who complete reteaching activities.
- Use exit tickets from the original lessons as targeted practice problems.
