# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 4, Lesson 3

## Area of Trapezoids

### Learning Objectives
1. Identify the two bases and the height of a trapezoid.
2. Decompose a trapezoid into familiar shapes (triangles and rectangles) to find its area.
3. Apply the formula A = 1/2 x (b1 + b2) x h to calculate the area of trapezoids.
4. Explain how the trapezoid area formula relates to decomposition.

### Prerequisites
- Calculate the area of parallelograms using A = b x h (Lesson 1)
- Calculate the area of triangles using A = 1/2 x b x h (Lesson 2)
- Identify the perpendicular height of a shape

### Materials
- Pre-cut cardstock trapezoids (two congruent copies per student)
- Grid paper (1 cm)
- Rulers
- Scissors
- Colored pencils

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Display the following problems:**

1. Find the area of a parallelogram with b = 14 cm and h = 6 cm.
2. Find the area of a triangle with b = 10 in and h = 8 in.
3. A triangle has an area of 45 ft² and a base of 15 ft. Find its height.

"Solve these quickly — you will need both the parallelogram and triangle formulas today."

(Answers: 84 cm², 40 in², h = 6 ft)

"We know how to find the area of parallelograms and triangles. Today we tackle trapezoids — shapes with one pair of parallel sides."

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Trapezoid:** A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides.
- **Base 1 (b1) and Base 2 (b2):** The two parallel sides of a trapezoid. They may have different lengths.
- **Height (h):** The perpendicular distance between the two bases.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete Exploration (Decomposition Method):**

Draw a trapezoid on the board with b1 = 10 cm (bottom), b2 = 6 cm (top), and h = 4 cm.

"This is a trapezoid. What makes it different from a parallelogram?"

Students: It only has one pair of parallel sides.

"Right. The top and bottom are parallel, but the left and right sides are not parallel to each other. The two parallel sides are called the bases — base 1 and base 2."

"Let me show you one way to find the area: break it into shapes you already know."

Draw a vertical line from the top-left corner straight down to the base. Draw another from the top-right corner straight down.

"Now I have a rectangle in the middle and two triangles on the sides."

| Piece | Shape | Dimensions | Area |
|-------|-------|-----------|------|
| Left triangle | Triangle | b = 2 cm, h = 4 cm | 1/2 x 2 x 4 = 4 cm² |
| Middle rectangle | Rectangle | 6 cm x 4 cm | 6 x 4 = 24 cm² |
| Right triangle | Triangle | b = 2 cm, h = 4 cm | 1/2 x 2 x 4 = 4 cm² |
| **Total** | | | **32 cm²** |

"This decomposition method always works, but it requires several calculations. Let me show you a faster formula."

**Step 2 — Concrete Exploration (Double-Trapezoid Method):**

"Everyone take your two identical trapezoids. Flip one upside down and place it next to the other so they fit together."

Demonstrate: the two trapezoids form a parallelogram.

"What shape did you make?"

Students: A parallelogram!

"What is the base of this parallelogram?" (b1 + b2 = 10 + 6 = 16 cm)
"What is the height?" (h = 4 cm)
"What is the area of the whole parallelogram?" (16 x 4 = 64 cm²)
"But we used two trapezoids. So one trapezoid is..." (64 ÷ 2 = 32 cm²)

"That matches our decomposition answer. Now let me write this as a formula."

Write on the board: **Area of a trapezoid = 1/2 x (b1 + b2) x h**

**Step 3 — Understanding the Formula:**

"Let me explain each part:
- b1 + b2 adds the two parallel bases together — that gives us the base of the big parallelogram.
- Multiply by h — that gives the area of the big parallelogram.
- Multiply by 1/2 — because the trapezoid is only half of that parallelogram."

"This formula works for every trapezoid, no matter how it is oriented."

### Worked Example A

**Problem:** Find the area of a trapezoid with b1 = 8 in, b2 = 12 in, and h = 5 in.

A = 1/2 x (b1 + b2) x h
A = 1/2 x (8 + 12) x 5
A = 1/2 x 20 x 5
A = 1/2 x 100
A = 50 in²

"Always add the bases first inside the parentheses, then multiply by the height, then take half."

### Worked Example B

**Problem:** A trapezoid has bases of 15 cm and 9 cm, a height of 7 cm, and slant sides of 8 cm and 7.5 cm. What is its area?

"Which measurements do we use? Only the two bases and the height."

A = 1/2 x (15 + 9) x 7
A = 1/2 x 24 x 7
A = 1/2 x 168
A = 84 cm²

"Again, the slant sides are not needed. The height is the perpendicular distance between the two bases."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

### Activity 1 — Decomposition Verification

"For this trapezoid (b1 = 12 cm, b2 = 8 cm, h = 5 cm), find the area two ways:"

**Method 1 — Decompose into rectangle + two triangles:**
- Rectangle: 8 x 5 = 40 cm²
- Left triangle: 1/2 x 2 x 5 = 5 cm²
- Right triangle: 1/2 x 2 x 5 = 5 cm²
- Total: 40 + 5 + 5 = **50 cm²**

**Method 2 — Formula:**
A = 1/2 x (12 + 8) x 5 = 1/2 x 20 x 5 = **50 cm²**

"Both methods give the same answer. The formula is faster, but decomposition helps you understand why the formula works."

### Activity 2 — Practice with Partners

Find the area of each trapezoid.

1. b1 = 6 cm, b2 = 10 cm, h = 4 cm → A = 1/2 x 16 x 4 = **32 cm²**
2. b1 = 9 in, b2 = 15 in, h = 8 in → A = 1/2 x 24 x 8 = **96 in²**
3. b1 = 7 m, b2 = 11 m, h = 6 m → A = 1/2 x 18 x 6 = **54 m²**

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 — Developing

Find the area of each trapezoid.

1. b1 = 5 cm, b2 = 9 cm, h = 4 cm → **A = 28 cm²**
2. b1 = 8 in, b2 = 12 in, h = 6 in → **A = 60 in²**
3. b1 = 10 ft, b2 = 14 ft, h = 3 ft → **A = 36 ft²**
4. b1 = 6 m, b2 = 6 m, h = 5 m → **A = 30 m²**
5. What do you notice about problem 4? What shape is it when both bases are the same length? → **It is a parallelogram (or rectangle). The formula still works.**

### Tier 2 — Proficient

1. b1 = 11 cm, b2 = 17 cm, h = 8 cm → **A = 112 cm²**
2. b1 = 7.5 in, b2 = 12.5 in, h = 6 in → **A = 60 in²**
3. A = 75 ft², b1 = 10 ft, b2 = 15 ft, h = ? → **h = 6 ft**
4. A = 52 m², b1 = 6 m, h = 8 m, b2 = ? → **b2 = 7 m**
5. A table top shaped like a trapezoid has bases of 3 feet and 5 feet and a height of 2.5 feet. What is the area of the table top? → **A = 10 ft²**
6. Decompose a trapezoid with b1 = 14 cm, b2 = 8 cm, h = 6 cm into a rectangle and two triangles. Show the area of each piece and the total. → **Rectangle: 8 x 6 = 48; Two triangles: 2 x 1/2 x 3 x 6 = 18; Total = 66 cm²**

### Tier 3 — Advanced

1. b1 = 13.6 cm, b2 = 8.4 cm, h = 7.5 cm → **A = 82.5 cm²**
2. A trapezoid has an area of 91 in², bases of 8 in and 18 in. What is its height? → **h = 7 in**
3. A trapezoidal garden bed has bases of 6 feet and 10 feet with a height of 4 feet. If each flower needs 2 square feet of space, how many flowers can be planted? → **A = 32 ft²; 32 ÷ 2 = 16 flowers**
4. A trapezoid has b1 = 20 cm and h = 10 cm. If the area is 150 cm², what is b2? → **b2 = 10 cm**
5. Compare the three area formulas you have learned. Write each one and explain how they are all related. Why does the parallelogram formula not have a 1/2? Why does the trapezoid formula add two bases? → **Student should connect: rectangle is a special parallelogram; parallelogram has no 1/2 because it is already a full shape; triangle is half a parallelogram; trapezoid uses (b1+b2) because the two bases create the parallelogram when doubled.**

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Find the area of a trapezoid with b1 = 9 cm, b2 = 13 cm, and h = 6 cm.

**Problem 2:** A trapezoid has an area of 80 in², b1 = 7 in, and b2 = 9 in. What is the height?

**Problem 3:** Name the two methods for finding the area of a trapezoid that we learned today.

---

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. A = 1/2 x (9 + 13) x 6 = 1/2 x 22 x 6 = **66 cm²**
2. 80 = 1/2 x (7 + 9) x h → 80 = 1/2 x 16 x h → 80 = 8h → h = **10 in**
3. **Method 1: Decompose the trapezoid into rectangles and triangles, then add the areas. Method 2: Use the formula A = 1/2 x (b1 + b2) x h.**

## Teacher Notes

- **Two methods, one understanding:** The decomposition method builds understanding; the formula provides efficiency. Allow students to use whichever method they prefer on independent practice, but require them to demonstrate the formula at least twice.
- **Common error — forgetting parentheses:** Students may compute 1/2 x b1 + b2 x h without adding the bases first. Emphasize the order of operations and the meaning: "add the two bases first, because together they make the base of the parallelogram."
- **Special case — equal bases:** If b1 = b2, the formula gives A = b x h, the parallelogram formula. Point this out to reinforce that all these formulas are connected.
- **Concrete models matter:** The double-trapezoid method (two copies forming a parallelogram) is the most powerful conceptual tool in this lesson. If students struggle, return to the physical manipulatives.
- **Preparation for Lesson 4:** Tomorrow students will combine all three area formulas to find the area of composite figures. Ensure students have a solid grasp of all three formulas before moving on. Consider a brief review at the start of Lesson 4.
- **Formative check:** Review exit tickets. If students struggle with Problem 2 (solving for height), plan a brief algebra reteach on isolating variables.
