# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 4, Lesson 2

## Area of Triangles

### Learning Objectives
1. Explain why a triangle is half of a parallelogram.
2. Derive the area formula A = 1/2 x b x h from the parallelogram area formula.
3. Calculate the area of triangles using the formula.
4. Identify the base and corresponding perpendicular height for any triangle orientation.

### Prerequisites
- Calculate the area of parallelograms using A = b x h (Lesson 1)
- Identify perpendicular height versus slant side
- Multiply whole numbers, decimals, and fractions

### Materials
- Pre-cut cardstock triangles (two congruent copies per student, different color)
- Grid paper (1 cm)
- Rulers
- Scissors
- Tape

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Display the following problems:**

1. Find the area of a parallelogram with b = 10 cm and h = 6 cm.
2. Find the area of a parallelogram with b = 7 in and h = 9 in.
3. A parallelogram has an area of 48 ft² and a base of 8 ft. What is its height?

"Solve these three problems to warm up your parallelogram skills. You will need them today."

(Answers: 60 cm², 63 in², h = 6 ft)

"Yesterday we discovered that a parallelogram can be rearranged into a rectangle. Today, we are going to discover something about triangles — using parallelograms."

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Triangle:** A polygon with exactly three sides and three vertices.
- **Base of a triangle (b):** Any side of the triangle chosen as the reference for height.
- **Height of a triangle (h):** The perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite vertex.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete Discovery:**

"Everyone has two identical triangles — one blue and one yellow. Hold them up. Are they the same shape and size?"

Students confirm: Yes.

"Now, try to put them together to make a shape you already know how to find the area of. You can flip, rotate, or slide the triangles. Work with your partner."

Circulate as students experiment. Most will form a parallelogram or rectangle.

"What shape did you make?"

Students: A parallelogram (or rectangle).

"Right! Two identical triangles fit together to make a parallelogram. That means one triangle is exactly half of a parallelogram."

Tape the two triangles together on the board to show the parallelogram clearly.

**Step 2 — Representational Bridge:**

Draw a parallelogram on the board: base = 8 cm, height = 5 cm. Draw the diagonal to split it into two triangles.

"The area of this whole parallelogram is 8 x 5 = 40 cm². Each triangle is half. So the area of one triangle is..."

Students: 20 cm².

"How did you get 20?"

Students: 40 divided by 2, or half of 40.

Write on the board:

**Area of a triangle = 1/2 x b x h**

"This is the formula. The 1/2 is there because a triangle is half of a parallelogram."

**Step 3 — Abstract Formula Work:**

"Let me show you that this works for all triangles, not just right triangles."

Draw three triangles: an acute triangle, a right triangle, and an obtuse triangle. For each, show the base and the perpendicular height (note that for obtuse triangles, the height may fall outside the triangle).

"No matter what triangle you have, the height is always the perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite vertex. For obtuse triangles, you sometimes have to extend the base line to draw the height — and that is okay."

### Worked Example A

**Problem:** Find the area of a triangle with a base of 12 cm and a height of 7 cm.

"What is our formula?"
Students: A = 1/2 x b x h

A = 1/2 x 12 x 7
A = 1/2 x 84
A = 42 cm²

"You can also think of it as: 12 x 7 = 84, then divide by 2 to get 42. Either way works."

### Worked Example B

**Problem:** A triangle has a base of 9 inches, a height of 6 inches, and a slant side of 7.5 inches. What is its area?

"Just like with parallelograms, we have an extra measurement. Which values do we need?"

Students: The base and the height only.

A = 1/2 x 9 x 6
A = 1/2 x 54
A = 27 in²

"We ignore the slant side — it is not the perpendicular height."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

### Activity 1 — From Parallelogram to Triangle

"For each parallelogram below, I will give you the area. Tell me the area of the triangle formed by cutting the parallelogram in half along its diagonal."

1. Parallelogram area = 60 cm² → Triangle area = **30 cm²**
2. Parallelogram area = 84 in² → Triangle area = **42 in²**
3. Parallelogram area = 100 m² → Triangle area = **50 m²**

"Now go the other direction: if a triangle has an area of 36 ft², what is the area of the parallelogram you would make with two copies of it?"

Answer: **72 ft²**

### Activity 2 — Calculate Triangle Areas

Work with a partner to find each area.

1. b = 10 cm, h = 8 cm → A = 1/2 x 10 x 8 = **40 cm²**
2. b = 15 in, h = 6 in → A = 1/2 x 15 x 6 = **45 in²**
3. b = 7 m, h = 12 m → A = 1/2 x 7 x 12 = **42 m²**
4. b = 20 ft, h = 9 ft → A = 1/2 x 20 x 9 = **90 ft²**

"Check your answers with your partner. If you disagree, show each other your work and find the mistake."

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 — Developing

Find the area of each triangle.

1. b = 6 cm, h = 4 cm → **A = 12 cm²**
2. b = 10 in, h = 8 in → **A = 40 in²**
3. b = 14 ft, h = 5 ft → **A = 35 ft²**
4. b = 8 m, h = 10 m → **A = 40 m²**
5. A triangle has a base of 16 cm and a height of 3 cm. What is its area? → **A = 24 cm²**

### Tier 2 — Proficient

Find the area or the missing measurement.

1. b = 13 cm, h = 8 cm → **A = 52 cm²**
2. b = 9.5 in, h = 6 in → **A = 28.5 in²**
3. A = 36 ft², b = 9 ft, h = ? → **h = 8 ft**
4. A = 55 m², h = 10 m, b = ? → **b = 11 m**
5. A triangle has a base of 18 cm, a height of 7 cm, and sides measuring 10 cm and 12 cm. What is its area? → **A = 63 cm²**
6. A triangular garden has a base of 14 feet and a height of 8 feet. If one bag of mulch covers 10 ft², how many bags are needed? → **A = 56 ft²; 56 ÷ 10 = 5.6, so 6 bags**

### Tier 3 — Advanced

1. b = 11.4 cm, h = 7.5 cm → **A = 42.75 cm²**
2. A triangle has an area of 63 in² and a height of 9 in. What is the length of its base? → **b = 14 in**
3. A triangular sail on a boat has a base of 4.5 meters and a height of 6.8 meters. How many square meters of fabric are needed? → **A = 15.3 m²**
4. A parallelogram has a base of 12 cm and a height of 10 cm. A diagonal divides it into two triangles. What is the area of each triangle? Are the two triangles always equal in area? Explain. → **Each = 60 cm². Yes, the diagonal always divides a parallelogram into two congruent triangles.**
5. Draw three different triangles, each with an area of exactly 24 cm², on grid paper. They must have different base and height combinations. → **Possible: b=8 h=6, b=12 h=4, b=6 h=8, b=16 h=3, etc.**

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Find the area of a triangle with b = 14 cm and h = 9 cm.

**Problem 2:** A triangle has an area of 30 in² and a base of 10 in. What is its height?

**Problem 3:** Explain in one or two sentences why the formula for the area of a triangle has a 1/2 in it.

---

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. A = 1/2 x 14 x 9 = **63 cm²**
2. 30 = 1/2 x 10 x h → 30 = 5h → h = **6 in**
3. **The formula has 1/2 because a triangle is exactly half of a parallelogram with the same base and height. Two identical triangles fit together to make a parallelogram, so one triangle is half the area.**

---

## Teacher Notes

- **Key conceptual moment:** The physical activity of putting two congruent triangles together to form a parallelogram is the conceptual anchor for this lesson. If students do not fully understand why the 1/2 appears in the formula, they will struggle to remember it. Spend extra time here if needed.
- **Common error — forgetting the 1/2:** When students forget to halve, have them return to the concrete model. "Did you use the whole parallelogram or just one triangle? If one triangle, you need to halve."
- **Obtuse triangles:** Some students will struggle with height that falls outside the triangle. Use a physical model: hold the triangle with the base at the bottom, then drop a string (with a weight) from the top vertex straight down. The string shows the height even if it does not land inside the triangle.
- **Multiple bases:** Show students that any side of a triangle can serve as the base — the height changes accordingly, but the area stays the same. This flexibility is important for composite figures in Lesson 4.
- **Connection to Lesson 1:** Continuously reinforce that the triangle formula builds on the parallelogram formula. This is the CRA progression in action — each new concept connects to the previous one.
- **Formative check:** If students struggle with Exit Ticket Problem 2 (working backward), plan a brief reteaching on inverse operations before Lesson 3.
