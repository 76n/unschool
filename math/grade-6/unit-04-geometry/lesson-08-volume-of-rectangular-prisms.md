# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 4, Lesson 8

## Volume of Rectangular Prisms

### Learning Objectives
1. Define volume as the amount of space inside a three-dimensional shape, measured in cubic units.
2. Calculate the volume of rectangular prisms using V = l x w x h.
3. Understand the relationship V = B x h, where B is the area of the base.
4. Solve real-world problems involving the volume of rectangular prisms.

### Prerequisites
- Identify rectangular prisms and their dimensions (Lesson 5)
- Calculate the area of rectangles
- Multiply whole numbers and decimals
- Prior exposure to volume with whole-number edges (Grade 5)

### Materials
- Unit cubes (1 cm) — at least 50 per pair
- Small rectangular containers or boxes
- Rulers
- Grid paper

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Display the following:**

1. Find the area of a rectangle that is 5 cm by 3 cm. → 15 cm²
2. What does area measure? → The space inside a flat (2D) shape.
3. If you stacked four layers of that rectangle on top of each other, how many unit cubes would fill the stack?

"For problem 3, think about it — 15 square centimeters on the bottom, stacked 4 layers high. How many cubes?" (Answer: 60 cubes.)

"Today we are measuring volume — the space inside a 3D shape. Instead of counting squares, we count cubes."

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Volume (V):** The amount of space inside a three-dimensional shape, measured in cubic units.
- **Cubic unit:** A cube with edges of 1 unit (e.g., 1 cm³ is a cube that is 1 cm on every edge).
- **Base area (B):** The area of one base of a prism. For a rectangular prism, B = l x w.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Concrete: Filling with Cubes:**

Give each pair of students a small rectangular container and unit cubes.

"Your job is to fill this box completely with unit cubes — no gaps, no stacking above the top. Then count how many cubes you used."

Allow 3-4 minutes. Students fill and count.

"How many cubes fit?" Collect responses. "The number of cubes is the volume of the box."

"Now let me show you a faster way than counting every single cube."

**Step 2 — Layered Counting:**

Build a rectangular prism from cubes: 5 cubes long, 3 cubes wide, 4 cubes tall.

"How many cubes are in the bottom layer?"

Students: 5 x 3 = 15 cubes.

"That bottom layer is like the area of the base — 15 square units. Now, how many layers?" — 4 layers.

"Total cubes: 15 x 4 = 60 cubes = 60 cubic units."

Write on the board:
**Volume = (area of the base) x height**
**V = B x h**

"For a rectangular prism, the base is a rectangle, so B = l x w."

**V = l x w x h**

**Step 3 — Abstract Formula:**

"Both formulas give the same answer. V = l x w x h is the one most people memorize. V = B x h helps you understand why it works — you are stacking layers of the base area."

Draw a rectangular prism with l = 8 cm, w = 5 cm, h = 6 cm.

V = 8 x 5 x 6 = 240 cm³

"The answer is 240 cubic centimeters. Volume always uses cubic units: cm³, in³, ft³, m³. The little '3' reminds us we are measuring three-dimensional space."

**Step 4 — Units:**

"Surface area uses square units because we measure flat surfaces. Volume uses cubic units because we measure space inside. Never mix them up."

| Measurement | What It Measures | Units |
|-------------|-----------------|-------|
| Area | Flat surface (2D) | cm², in², ft² |
| Surface area | Outside of a 3D shape | cm², in², ft² |
| Volume | Space inside a 3D shape | cm³, in³, ft³ |

### Worked Example A

**Problem:** A rectangular aquarium is 60 cm long, 30 cm wide, and 40 cm tall. What is its volume?

V = l x w x h
V = 60 x 30 x 40
V = 72,000 cm³

"The aquarium can hold 72,000 cubic centimeters of water. Fun fact: 1,000 cm³ = 1 liter, so this aquarium holds 72 liters."

### Worked Example B

**Problem:** A shipping container has a volume of 480 ft³. It is 10 ft long and 8 ft wide. How tall is it?

V = l x w x h
480 = 10 x 8 x h
480 = 80h
h = 480 ÷ 80
h = 6 ft

"When you know the volume and two dimensions, divide to find the third."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

### Activity 1 — Cube Building

"Build these rectangular prisms from unit cubes, then verify with the formula."

1. l = 4, w = 3, h = 2 → Count: 24 cubes. Formula: 4 x 3 x 2 = **24 cm³** ✓
2. l = 5, w = 2, h = 3 → Count: 30 cubes. Formula: 5 x 2 x 3 = **30 cm³** ✓

"The formula gives the exact same count. That is because V = l x w x h counts the cubes without having to place them one by one."

### Activity 2 — Formula Practice

Find the volume. Work with your partner.

1. l = 9 cm, w = 7 cm, h = 4 cm → V = **252 cm³**
2. l = 12 in, w = 5 in, h = 8 in → V = **480 in³**
3. V = 360 m³, l = 12 m, w = 6 m, h = ? → h = 360 ÷ 72 = **5 m**

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 — Developing

Find the volume of each rectangular prism.

1. l = 6 cm, w = 4 cm, h = 3 cm → **V = 72 cm³**
2. l = 10 in, w = 5 in, h = 2 in → **V = 100 in³**
3. l = 8 ft, w = 8 ft, h = 8 ft → **V = 512 ft³**
4. l = 15 m, w = 3 m, h = 4 m → **V = 180 m³**
5. What is special about the shape in problem 3? → **It is a cube. All edges are the same length.**

### Tier 2 — Proficient

1. l = 14 cm, w = 9 cm, h = 6 cm → **V = 756 cm³**
2. l = 11.5 in, w = 8 in, h = 5 in → **V = 460 in³**
3. V = 600 cm³, l = 15 cm, w = 8 cm, h = ? → **h = 5 cm**
4. V = 1,080 ft³, l = 12 ft, h = 9 ft, w = ? → **w = 10 ft**
5. A storage locker is 10 ft long, 6 ft wide, and 8 ft tall. How many cubic feet of items can it hold? → **V = 480 ft³**
6. Two boxes: Box A is 12 x 6 x 5 cm. Box B is 10 x 8 x 4 cm. Which holds more? By how much? → **Box A: 360 cm³. Box B: 320 cm³. Box A holds 40 cm³ more.**

### Tier 3 — Advanced

1. l = 18.5 cm, w = 12.4 cm, h = 7.6 cm → **V = 1,744.24 cm³** (accept rounding)
2. A rectangular prism has a volume of 420 in³. List three different sets of whole-number dimensions that give this volume. → **e.g., 10x7x6, 14x5x6, 21x4x5, 20x7x3, etc.**
3. A swimming pool is 25 m long, 10 m wide, and 2 m deep. If 1 m³ = 1,000 liters, how many liters of water does the pool hold? → **V = 500 m³ = 500,000 liters**
4. A cube has a volume of 343 cm³. What is its edge length? What is its surface area? → **Edge = 7 cm (since 7³ = 343). SA = 6 x 49 = 294 cm².**
5. Company A ships in boxes that are 20 x 15 x 10 cm. Company B ships in boxes that are 25 x 12 x 10 cm. Both boxes have the same volume (3,000 cm³). Which box uses less cardboard (has less surface area)? → **Company A SA = 2(300+200+150) = 1,300 cm². Company B SA = 2(300+250+120) = 1,340 cm². Company A uses less cardboard.**

**Problem 3:** What units do you use for volume? Why are they different from area units?

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. V = 13 x 8 x 5 = **520 cm³**
2. 240 = 10 x 6 x h → 240 = 60h → h = **4 in**
3. **Volume uses cubic units (cm³, in³, ft³) because we are measuring three-dimensional space. Area uses square units because it measures two-dimensional space. The exponent matches the number of dimensions.**

## Teacher Notes

- **Cubes build understanding:** The hands-on cube-filling activity is essential. Students who physically build rectangular prisms understand that V = l x w x h is just a shortcut for counting cubes. Do not skip this step.
- **Layer reasoning:** Emphasize V = B x h (base area times height) as the conceptual version. This reasoning extends to all prisms and cylinders in later grades. V = l x w x h is the specific version for rectangular prisms.
- **Common error — confusing SA and V:** Students may write square units for volume or calculate SA when asked for volume. Keep the comparison chart visible. Ask: "Are you measuring the outside or the inside?"
- **Common error — order of multiplication:** Remind students that l x w x h can be multiplied in any order (commutative and associative properties). 8 x 5 x 6 = 5 x 8 x 6 = 6 x 5 x 8 = 240. This is helpful when one pair of factors is easier to multiply mentally.
- **Real-world connections:** Volume is everywhere: fish tanks, swimming pools, shipping containers, refrigerators, suitcases. Ask students to estimate volumes of classroom objects.
- **Capacity connection:** If appropriate, mention that 1 cm³ = 1 milliliter and 1,000 cm³ = 1 liter. This connects volume to capacity, which students encounter in science and daily life.
- **Preparation for Lesson 9:** Tomorrow's lesson extends volume to fractional edge lengths. Students will need their fraction multiplication skills. If fraction multiplication is weak, consider a brief review at the start of Lesson 9.
