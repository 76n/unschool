# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 4, Lesson 1

## Area of Parallelograms

### Learning Objectives
1. Identify the base and perpendicular height of a parallelogram.
2. Derive the area formula for a parallelogram by rearranging it into a rectangle.
3. Calculate the area of parallelograms using A = b x h.
4. Explain why the height must be perpendicular to the base, not the slant side.

### Prerequisites
- Calculate the area of rectangles using A = l x w
- Identify parallel and perpendicular lines
- Multiply whole numbers and decimals

### Materials
- Pre-cut cardstock parallelograms (one per student)
- Scissors (class set)
- Grid paper (1 cm)
- Rulers
- Tape or glue sticks

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Display the following problems:**

1. Find the area of a rectangle that is 8 cm long and 5 cm wide.
2. Find the area of a rectangle that is 12 in long and 3.5 in wide.
3. A rectangular garden is 15 feet by 9 feet. What is its area?

"Take two minutes to solve these three rectangle area problems on your grid paper. These are review — use what you already know about rectangles."

Allow students to share answers briefly. (Answers: 40 cm², 42 in², 135 ft²)

"Today we are going to use what we know about rectangles to figure out the area of a new shape — the parallelogram."

---

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Parallelogram:** A quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides.
- **Base (b):** The side of the parallelogram used as a reference for measuring area. Any side can be chosen as the base.
- **Height (h):** The perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite side. The height forms a 90-degree angle with the base.

### Instruction Notes

Hold up a cardstock parallelogram. "Everyone take out your cardstock parallelogram. Look at it carefully. How is this shape different from a rectangle?"

Allow 2-3 student responses. Guide toward: "The sides are tilted — they are not perpendicular to the base."

"Here is the big question for today: Can we figure out the area of this parallelogram by turning it into a shape we already know?"

**Step 1 — Concrete Exploration:**

"Take your ruler and draw a straight line from the top-left corner straight down to the base. This line should make a right angle — a perfect 90-degree angle — with the base. This line is called the height."

Demonstrate on the document camera or board. Mark the right angle symbol.

"Now, carefully cut along that line with your scissors."

Students cut the parallelogram into two pieces: a right triangle and a trapezoid.

"Take the triangle you just cut off from the left side and move it over to the right side. Tape it onto the right end."

Pause and circulate. Help students who struggle with alignment.

"What shape did you just make?"

Students: A rectangle!

"You turned a parallelogram into a rectangle without adding or removing any paper. That means the parallelogram and the rectangle have the same area."

**Step 2 — Representational Connection:**

Draw a parallelogram on the board with base labeled 10 cm and height labeled 6 cm. Show the cut-and-rearrange process with dotted lines.

"The rectangle we made has the same base and the same height as the parallelogram. So the area of the parallelogram is the same as the area of the rectangle: base times height."

Write on the board: **Area of a parallelogram = b x h**

**Step 3 — Why not the slant side?**

Draw a parallelogram with base = 10 cm, height = 6 cm, and slant side = 7 cm.

"Some people want to use this slant side — the 7 cm — as the height. But watch what happens when we rearrange. The rectangle we make is 10 cm by 6 cm, not 10 cm by 7 cm. The slant side is not the height because it is not perpendicular to the base."

Circle the right angle symbol. "Always look for the 90-degree angle. The height is the perpendicular distance."

### Worked Example A

**Problem:** Find the area of a parallelogram with a base of 9 inches and a height of 4 inches.

"What formula do we use?"
Students: A = b x h

"What is the base?" — 9 inches.
"What is the height?" — 4 inches.

A = 9 x 4
A = 36 square inches

"Notice our answer is in square inches because we are measuring a flat surface — area is always in square units."

### Worked Example B

**Problem:** A parallelogram has a base of 14 cm, a height of 8 cm, and a slant side of 10 cm. What is its area?

"This problem gives us three measurements. Which ones do we need?"

Students: The base and the height.

"Right — we ignore the slant side. The slant side is not perpendicular to the base."

A = b x h
A = 14 x 8
A = 112 cm²

"What if I told you the base was 8 cm and the height was 14 cm instead? Would the area change?"

Students: No — 8 x 14 = 112 too.

"Exactly. You can choose any side as the base as long as you use the matching perpendicular height."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

### Activity 1 — Grid Paper Parallelograms

"On your grid paper, I have drawn three parallelograms. For each one, identify the base and height, then calculate the area."

| Parallelogram | Base | Height | Area |
|--------------|------|--------|------|
| A | 6 cm | 4 cm | 24 cm² |
| B | 8 cm | 3 cm | 24 cm² |
| C | 5 cm | 7 cm | 35 cm² |

"Notice that parallelograms A and B have the same area but different dimensions. Different parallelograms can have the same area — just like different rectangles can."

### Activity 2 — Find the Missing Measurement

Work with a partner to find the missing value:

1. A = ?, b = 11 in, h = 6 in → A = 66 in²
2. A = 72 cm², b = 9 cm, h = ? → h = 8 cm
3. A = 45 ft², b = ?, h = 5 ft → b = 9 ft

"For problems 2 and 3, you have to work backward. If A = b x h, then how do you find h when you know A and b?"

Students: Divide the area by the base.

"Exactly. h = A ÷ b and b = A ÷ h. This is just using your division skills."

## Independent Practice

### Tier 1 — Developing

Find the area of each parallelogram.

1. b = 7 cm, h = 3 cm → **A = 21 cm²**
2. b = 10 in, h = 5 in → **A = 50 in²**
3. b = 12 ft, h = 4 ft → **A = 48 ft²**
4. b = 9 m, h = 8 m → **A = 72 m²**
5. A parallelogram has a base of 6 cm and a height of 11 cm. What is its area? → **A = 66 cm²**

### Tier 2 — Proficient

Find the area or the missing measurement.

1. b = 15 cm, h = 7 cm → **A = 105 cm²**
2. b = 8.5 in, h = 4 in → **A = 34 in²**
3. A = 96 ft², b = 12 ft, h = ? → **h = 8 ft**
4. A = 63 m², h = 7 m, b = ? → **b = 9 m**
5. A parallelogram has a base of 13 cm, a height of 9 cm, and a slant side of 10 cm. What is its area? → **A = 117 cm²**
6. Two parallelograms both have an area of 48 cm². One has a base of 8 cm. The other has a base of 12 cm. What are their heights? → **h = 6 cm; h = 4 cm**

### Tier 3 — Advanced

1. b = 7.5 cm, h = 6.4 cm → **A = 48 cm²**
2. A parallelogram has an area of 84 in² and a height of 10.5 in. What is the length of its base? → **b = 8 in**
3. A parking lot shaped like a parallelogram has a base of 50 meters and a height of 30 meters. Each parking space takes up 12.5 m². How many cars can fit? → **A = 1,500 m²; 1,500 ÷ 12.5 = 120 cars**
4. A parallelogram and a rectangle both have an area of 60 cm². The rectangle is 10 cm by 6 cm. Give two possible base-height pairs for the parallelogram that are different from 10 and 6. → **e.g., b = 12 cm, h = 5 cm; b = 15 cm, h = 4 cm**
5. Explain in your own words why the slant side of a parallelogram is not used as the height when calculating area. Use a diagram to support your answer. → **Student explanation should reference perpendicular distance and the cut-and-rearrange reasoning.**

## Exit Ticket

**Problem 1:** Find the area of a parallelogram with b = 11 cm and h = 7 cm.

**Problem 2:** A parallelogram has an area of 54 in² and a base of 9 in. What is its height?

**Problem 3:** True or false — To find the area of a parallelogram, you multiply the base by the slant side. Explain your answer.

---

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. A = 11 x 7 = **77 cm²**
2. h = 54 ÷ 9 = **6 in**
3. **False.** You multiply the base by the perpendicular height, not the slant side. The height must form a 90-degree angle with the base.

---

## Teacher Notes

- **Timing:** The concrete cut-and-rearrange activity is the heart of this lesson. Allow adequate time for students to physically transform their parallelogram into a rectangle. This hands-on experience is more valuable than extra practice problems.
- **Common error:** Students frequently use the slant side instead of the height. Reinforce the right angle symbol in every diagram. Ask students to trace the height with their finger and say "perpendicular" each time.
- **Extension for early finishers:** Challenge students to draw a parallelogram on grid paper with a specific area (e.g., "Draw a parallelogram with an area of exactly 30 square centimeters"). Multiple solutions are possible.
- **Formative check:** Review exit tickets before Lesson 2. If more than 25% of students confuse the height with the slant side, begin Lesson 2 with a brief reteaching activity using physical parallelograms.
- **Real-world connection:** Show images of parallelogram-shaped objects: certain road signs, some building facades, leaning bookshelves. Ask students to estimate dimensions and calculate approximate areas.
