# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 1, Lesson 8

## Topic
Comparing Rates

## Learning Objectives
- Use unit rates to compare two or more rate situations and determine which is greater, lesser, or more efficient.
- Solve "better buy" problems by comparing unit prices.
- Compare speeds and other rates by converting them to a common unit.

## Prerequisites
- Ability to calculate a unit rate by dividing (Lesson 7).
- Understanding of rates and their units (Lesson 6).
- Decimal division fluency.
- Familiarity with comparison language (greater than, less than, best deal).

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Grocery flyers or printed price lists (for better-buy problems).
- Calculator (optional, for checking decimal division).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 6.RP.2 — Understand the concept of a unit rate a/b associated with a ratio a:b with b not equal to 0, and use rate language in the context of a ratio relationship. 6.RP.3b — Solve unit rate problems including those involving unit pricing and constant speed.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review unit rate calculation and set up a scenario where comparison is needed.

1. "A pack of 8 markers costs $6.40. What is the unit price per marker?" ($0.80)
2. "A pack of 12 markers costs $8.40. What is the unit price per marker?" ($0.70)
3. Ask: "Which pack is the better buy? How do you know?" (The 12-pack, because it costs less per marker.)
4. Say: "Today we will use unit rates to compare all kinds of situations — not just prices, but speeds, productivity, and more."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Better buy** — The option with the lower unit price (less cost per item or per unit of measure).
- **Comparison** — Deciding which rate is greater, lesser, or more efficient by converting both to the same unit.
- **Equivalent basis** — Comparing rates using the same "per one" unit so the comparison is fair.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a concrete model.**

Say: "Imagine two runners. Runner A completes 3 miles in 24 minutes. Runner B completes 5 miles in 35 minutes. Who is faster?"

"You might try to compare 3 miles to 5 miles, but that is not fair — they ran different distances. You might compare 24 minutes to 35 minutes, but that is not fair either — they ran different times."

"The solution: find the unit rate for each and compare."

Runner A: 3 miles / 24 minutes = 0.125 miles per minute.
Runner B: 5 miles / 35 minutes = approximately 0.143 miles per minute.

"Runner B covers more distance per minute, so Runner B is faster."

Alternatively, find minutes per mile:
Runner A: 24 / 3 = 8 minutes per mile.
Runner B: 35 / 5 = 7 minutes per mile.

"Runner B takes fewer minutes per mile, so Runner B is faster." Same conclusion.

**Step 2 — Explain the comparison strategy.**

"To compare two rates:
1. Find the unit rate for each situation.
2. Make sure both unit rates use the same units (e.g., both are dollars per ounce, or both are miles per hour).
3. Compare the numbers."

"For cost comparisons (better buy), the LOWER unit price wins."
"For speed or production comparisons, the HIGHER unit rate (more per unit of time) is faster or more productive."

**Step 3 — Model a better-buy problem.**

"Brand X sells 16 ounces of cereal for $4.80. Brand Y sells 24 ounces of cereal for $6.00. Which is the better buy?"

Brand X: $4.80 / 16 oz = $0.30 per ounce.
Brand Y: $6.00 / 24 oz = $0.25 per ounce.

"Brand Y has the lower unit price ($0.25 < $0.30), so Brand Y is the better buy."

**Step 4 — Address a common misconception.**

"Some students think the bigger package is always the better deal. That is often true, but not always. You MUST calculate the unit price to be sure."

Show a counterexample: "Store A sells 5 apples for $2.00 ($0.40 each). Store B sells 8 apples for $3.60 ($0.45 each). The smaller package from Store A is actually cheaper per apple."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Better buy (unit price comparison).**

"A 20-ounce bottle of shampoo costs $5.00. A 32-ounce bottle costs $7.04. Which is the better buy?"

- 20-oz bottle: $5.00 / 20 = $0.25 per ounce.
- 32-oz bottle: $7.04 / 32 = $0.22 per ounce.
- The 32-ounce bottle is the better buy ($0.22 per ounce < $0.25 per ounce).

**Example B — Speed comparison.**

"Car A travels 270 miles in 5 hours. Car B travels 340 miles in 8 hours. Which car is faster?"

- Car A: 270 / 5 = 54 miles per hour.
- Car B: 340 / 8 = 42.5 miles per hour.
- Car A is faster (54 mph > 42.5 mph).

**Example C — Production rate comparison.**

"Machine P produces 180 parts in 6 hours. Machine Q produces 250 parts in 10 hours. Which machine is more productive?"

- Machine P: 180 / 6 = 30 parts per hour.
- Machine Q: 250 / 10 = 25 parts per hour.
- Machine P is more productive (30 > 25 parts per hour).

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. Juice Box A: 10 juice boxes for $4.50. Juice Box B: 6 juice boxes for $3.00. Which is the better buy?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find the unit price for each. Which is lower?"

2. Swimmer A completes 8 laps in 10 minutes. Swimmer B completes 15 laps in 20 minutes. Who swims faster (more laps per minute)?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find laps per minute for each swimmer."

3. A 3-pound bag of rice costs $2.49. A 10-pound bag costs $7.50. Which is the better buy?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find the cost per pound for each."

4. Two typists are competing. Typist A types 180 words in 4 minutes. Typist B types 275 words in 5 minutes. Who types faster?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find words per minute for each typist."

5. A plumber charges $210 for 3 hours of work. An electrician charges $350 for 5 hours of work. Who charges more per hour?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find the hourly rate for each. Which is higher?"

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### ★ On-Level

1. Store A: 4 notebooks for $6.00. Store B: 7 notebooks for $9.80. Which store has the better deal?

2. Hiker A walks 9 miles in 3 hours. Hiker B walks 14 miles in 4 hours. Who walks faster?

3. A 5-gallon jug of water costs $3.75. A 2-gallon jug costs $1.80. Which is the better buy?

4. Factory A produces 360 cans in 8 hours. Factory B produces 275 cans in 5 hours. Which factory is more productive?

### ★★ Stretch

5. Three brands of peanut butter are on sale:
   - Brand A: 18 ounces for $3.60.
   - Brand B: 28 ounces for $5.04.
   - Brand C: 40 ounces for $8.40.
   Which is the best buy? Rank all three from cheapest to most expensive per ounce.

6. Two delivery trucks are driving to the same city. Truck A has traveled 195 miles in 3 hours. Truck B has traveled 280 miles in 4 hours. If they keep the same speeds, which truck will reach a city 420 miles away first? How much sooner will it arrive?

7. A student reads 42 pages in 1.5 hours. Another student reads 70 pages in 2 hours. Who reads faster? By how many pages per hour?

### ★★★ Challenge

8. Cell phone Plan A costs $45 per month for 3 GB of data. Plan B costs $60 per month for 5 GB of data. Plan C costs $80 per month for 8 GB. Which plan has the lowest cost per GB? If you use 4 GB per month, which plan would you recommend and why?

9. A school is buying T-shirts. Vendor 1 charges $156 for 12 shirts. Vendor 2 charges $247.50 for 18 shirts. Vendor 3 charges $340 for 25 shirts. The school needs exactly 50 shirts. Which vendor should they choose to spend the least money? What would the total cost be?

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. A 6-pack of water bottles costs $2.10. A 10-pack costs $3.00. Which is the better buy? Show your work.

2. Runner A runs 4 miles in 32 minutes. Runner B runs 6 miles in 54 minutes. Who is faster? Explain.

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Practice finding unit rates side by side. Set up a T-chart: Option A on the left, Option B on the right. Walk through the division for each, write the unit rate, and then compare. Emphasize the final step: "Now that you have both unit rates, which is better?" If the student struggles with the division itself, allow a calculator and focus on the comparison reasoning.

### If Mastery Is Met
Present a three-way comparison with one rate that requires decimals to the hundredths place. For example: "Three runners — 7 miles in 52 minutes, 5 miles in 36 minutes, and 9 miles in 68 minutes. Rank them fastest to slowest."

## Extension (Optional)

1. **Grocery Store Challenge:** Using a grocery flyer (or online prices), choose three products that come in different sizes. For each, calculate the unit price for every size option. Create a recommendation chart showing the best buy for each product.

2. **Speed Challenge:** Research the top speed of three different animals (e.g., cheetah, horse, human). Convert each speed to the same unit (miles per hour or feet per second) and rank them. How many times faster is the fastest animal compared to the slowest?

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Comparing rates with different units.** A student might compare "dollars per ounce" for one item with "dollars per bottle" for another. Stress that both unit rates must have the SAME units before comparing.
- **Thinking "bigger number = better buy."** Students may see $0.30 and $0.25 and think $0.30 is better because it is "more." Reinforce: for costs, LOWER is better. For speed/production, HIGHER is better. Always ask: "What does this number mean? Do you want more or less of it?"
- **Dividing in the wrong direction.** If asked for "miles per hour," some students divide hours by miles. Return to the phrase "per one ___" — the word after "per" goes in the divisor.

### Pacing Advice
This lesson is highly engaging because better-buy problems are immediately relevant to students' lives. Use real grocery prices or flyers if possible — students are more motivated by authentic problems. The core skill (finding unit rates) was covered in Lesson 7, so this lesson is primarily about applying that skill in comparison contexts. If the student is confident with unit rates, move quickly to the Stretch and Challenge problems, which involve multi-step reasoning.

4. Typist A: 180 / 4 = 45 words per minute. Typist B: 275 / 5 = 55 words per minute. Typist B types faster.
5. Plumber: $210 / 3 = $70 per hour. Electrician: $350 / 5 = $70 per hour. They charge the same rate per hour.

### Independent Practice

**★ On-Level**
1. Store A: $6.00 / 4 = $1.50 per notebook. Store B: $9.80 / 7 = $1.40 per notebook. Store B is the better deal.
2. Hiker A: 9 / 3 = 3 mph. Hiker B: 14 / 4 = 3.5 mph. Hiker B walks faster.
3. 5-gallon: $3.75 / 5 = $0.75 per gallon. 2-gallon: $1.80 / 2 = $0.90 per gallon. The 5-gallon jug is the better buy.
4. Factory A: 360 / 8 = 45 cans per hour. Factory B: 275 / 5 = 55 cans per hour. Factory B is more productive.

**★★ Stretch**
5. Brand A: $3.60 / 18 = $0.20 per ounce. Brand B: $5.04 / 28 = $0.18 per ounce. Brand C: $8.40 / 40 = $0.21 per ounce. Ranking (cheapest to most expensive per ounce): Brand B ($0.18), Brand A ($0.20), Brand C ($0.21). Brand B is the best buy.
6. Truck A: 195 / 3 = 65 mph. Time for 420 miles: 420 / 65 = 6.46 hours (about 6 hours 28 minutes). Truck B: 280 / 4 = 70 mph. Time for 420 miles: 420 / 70 = 6 hours. Truck B arrives first, about 28 minutes sooner.
7. Student 1: 42 / 1.5 = 28 pages per hour. Student 2: 70 / 2 = 35 pages per hour. Student 2 reads faster by 35 - 28 = 7 pages per hour.

**★★★ Challenge**
8. Plan A: $45 / 3 = $15 per GB. Plan B: $60 / 5 = $12 per GB. Plan C: $80 / 8 = $10 per GB. Plan C has the lowest cost per GB. For 4 GB of usage, Plan B (5 GB for $60) would be a good recommendation because Plan A only offers 3 GB (not enough) and Plan C offers 8 GB for $80 (more data than needed, higher total cost). However, if cost per GB is the only factor and you can use Plan C, it has the best rate. The best practical answer depends on whether the student factors in wasted data vs. pure cost per GB.
9. Vendor 1: $156 / 12 = $13 per shirt. Vendor 2: $247.50 / 18 = $13.75 per shirt. Vendor 3: $340 / 25 = $13.60 per shirt. Vendor 1 has the lowest unit price. For 50 shirts: 50 x $13 = $650. (They would need to order at least 5 orders of 12, but 5 x 12 = 60, which is more than 50. Alternatively, 4 orders = 48, plus they need 2 more. If partial orders are possible, 50 x $13 = $650. If only full-pack orders are allowed, 5 packs of 12 = 60 shirts for $780 from Vendor 1 vs. 2 packs of 25 = 50 shirts for $680 from Vendor 3. Accept either interpretation with valid reasoning.)

11. (8 + 2) x 3 - 4 = 10 x 3 - 4 = 30 - 4 = 26.

### Exit Ticket
1. 6-pack: $2.10 / 6 = $0.35 per bottle. 10-pack: $3.00 / 10 = $0.30 per bottle. The 10-pack is the better buy ($0.30 < $0.35).
