# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 1, Lesson 7

## Topic
Unit Rate

## Learning Objectives
- Define unit rate as a rate where the second quantity is 1 (e.g., "per one").
- Calculate unit rates by dividing both quantities so the denominator (or second term) becomes 1.
- Apply unit rates to real-world contexts, including unit price.

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of what a rate is and how to set one up (Lesson 6).
- Division of whole numbers and decimals.
- Ability to use ratio tables (Lesson 4).
- Understanding of "per" language.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Grocery flyers or printed price tags showing multi-item pricing (e.g., "3 for $5.97").
- Calculator (optional, for checking decimal division).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 6.RP.2 — Understand the concept of a unit rate a/b associated with a ratio a:b with b not equal to 0, and use rate language in the context of a ratio relationship. 6.RP.3b — Solve unit rate problems including those involving unit pricing and constant speed.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Motivate the need for a "per one" rate by showing that comparing rates is hard when the second quantities differ.

1. Present two scenarios:
   - Store A sells 6 apples for $3.
   - Store B sells 10 apples for $4.50.
2. Ask: "Which store has the better deal? How would you figure that out?"
3. Let the student think for a moment. Say: "It is hard to compare because the numbers of apples are different. Wouldn't it be easier if we knew the price for just ONE apple at each store? That is what a unit rate helps us find."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Unit rate** — A rate in which the second quantity (the denominator) is 1. It answers the question "how much per one?"
- **Unit price** — The cost per one item or per one unit of measurement (e.g., $1.50 per pound).
- **Per** — For each one.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Connect to a concrete model.**

Return to the warm-up example. "Store A sells 6 apples for $3. To find the price per ONE apple, we divide."

Write: $3 / 6 apples = $0.50 per apple.

"This is the unit rate — the cost for one apple. We divided the total cost by the number of apples."

Now do Store B: $4.50 / 10 apples = $0.45 per apple.

"Store B charges $0.45 per apple, while Store A charges $0.50 per apple. Store B is the better deal."

**Step 2 — Explain the division process.**

"To find a unit rate, divide both quantities so the second quantity becomes 1."

General formula: If the rate is a per b, the unit rate is (a / b) per 1.

Show with a ratio table:

| Apples | Cost ($) |
|--------|----------|
| 6      | 3.00     |
| 1      | 0.50     |

"We divided both columns by 6: 6 / 6 = 1, and 3.00 / 6 = 0.50."

**Step 3 — Show that either quantity can be "the one."**

"A car travels 150 miles in 3 hours."

- Miles per hour (how far in one hour): 150 / 3 = 50 miles per hour.
- Hours per mile (how long for one mile): 3 / 150 = 0.02 hours per mile.

"Both are unit rates, but miles per hour is much more commonly used. Context tells us which unit rate is more useful."

**Step 4 — Introduce the term "unit price."**

"When the unit rate involves cost, we call it the unit price. It answers 'how much does one item cost?' or 'how much per pound, per gallon, per ounce?' This is what you see on grocery store shelf labels."

Show an example shelf label: "A 20-ounce bottle of juice costs $3.00. Unit price = $3.00 / 20 ounces = $0.15 per ounce."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Finding a unit rate (whole-number result).**

"A factory produces 240 widgets in 8 hours. Find the unit rate."

- Rate: 240 widgets per 8 hours.
- Divide both by 8: 240 / 8 = 30, 8 / 8 = 1.
- Unit rate: 30 widgets per hour.
- Meaning: The factory produces 30 widgets each hour.

**Example B — Finding a unit rate (decimal result).**

"A jogger runs 7 miles in 2 hours. Find the unit rate in miles per hour."

- Rate: 7 miles per 2 hours.
- Divide both by 2: 7 / 2 = 3.5, 2 / 2 = 1.
- Unit rate: 3.5 miles per hour.
- Meaning: The jogger runs 3.5 miles each hour.

**Example C — Finding a unit price.**

"A 12-pack of water bottles costs $4.20. Find the unit price."

- Rate: $4.20 per 12 bottles.
- Divide both by 12: 4.20 / 12 = 0.35, 12 / 12 = 1.
- Unit price: $0.35 per bottle.
- Meaning: Each bottle costs 35 cents.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. A car travels 225 miles on 5 gallons of gas. What is the unit rate in miles per gallon?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Divide the miles by the gallons. What does the answer mean in words?"

2. A 6-pack of yogurt costs $5.10. What is the unit price (cost per yogurt)?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Divide the cost by the number of yogurts."

3. A printer prints 84 pages in 7 minutes. What is the unit rate in pages per minute?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Which quantity should equal 1 — pages or minutes? Minutes, because we want pages PER minute."

4. Marcus reads 45 pages in 3 hours. What is his reading rate in pages per hour? If he keeps this rate, how many pages will he read in 5 hours?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "First find pages per hour. Then multiply by 5."

5. A store sells trail mix at $8 for 2 pounds. What is the unit price per pound? How much would 7 pounds cost?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find the cost per one pound first. Then multiply by 7."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### ★ On-Level

1. A cyclist rides 48 miles in 4 hours. What is the unit rate in miles per hour?

2. A 15-ounce can of soup costs $2.25. What is the unit price per ounce?

3. A machine fills 90 bottles in 6 minutes. What is the unit rate in bottles per minute?

4. A recipe uses 9 cups of flour for 3 batches of cookies. What is the unit rate in cups of flour per batch?

### ★★ Stretch

5. A runner completes 5 laps in 12 minutes. What is the unit rate in laps per minute? What is the unit rate in minutes per lap?

6. A family drives 364 miles and uses 14 gallons of gas. What is the unit rate in miles per gallon? If gas costs $3.50 per gallon, how much did the gas for the trip cost?

7. Two phone plans are available:
   - Plan A: 500 minutes for $40.
   - Plan B: 350 minutes for $28.
   Find the unit price (cost per minute) for each plan. Which plan is cheaper per minute?

### ★★★ Challenge

8. A concert hall sold 1,200 tickets for $21,600. A smaller theater sold 450 tickets for $7,650. Which venue charged more per ticket? By how much?

9. A train travels 312.5 miles in 2.5 hours. What is the unit rate in miles per hour? If the train keeps this speed, how long will it take to travel 500 miles?

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. A package of 8 granola bars costs $6.00. What is the unit price per granola bar?

2. A hose fills a pool at 42 gallons in 6 minutes. What is the unit rate in gallons per minute? How many gallons will flow in 10 minutes?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Use a ratio table to scaffold the division. Set up the rate as a two-row table and divide both columns to get a 1 in the second column. For example: 42 gallons / 6 minutes → table shows 42:6, then 7:1. This visual scaffold helps students who struggle with abstract division.

### If Mastery Is Met
Present: "A car's fuel efficiency is 28 miles per gallon. Gas costs $3.75 per gallon. How much does it cost to drive 140 miles?" This requires chaining two unit rates together (miles/gallon and dollars/gallon).

## Extension (Optional)

1. **Grocery Store Math:** Choose five items from a grocery flyer. For each item, calculate the unit price. Rank them from least to most expensive per unit. Which item is the best value?

2. **Speed Conversions:** A car travels at 60 miles per hour. How many miles per minute is that? How many miles per second? (Hint: There are 60 minutes in an hour and 60 seconds in a minute.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Dividing in the wrong direction.** Students may divide the wrong quantity by the other (e.g., dividing hours by miles instead of miles by hours). Teach the phrase "per one ___" and always identify the "one" unit first. That unit's number goes in the divisor.
- **Rounding too early or incorrectly.** When the division produces a decimal, students may round prematurely. Encourage exact answers unless the problem specifies rounding. For money, always round to two decimal places (cents).
- **Forgetting to include units in the answer.** A bare number like "12" is meaningless as a unit rate. Insist on complete answers: "12 miles per hour" or "$0.75 per bottle."

### Pacing Advice
The concept of unit rate is central to the rest of the unit and to Grade 6 math overall. Spend time ensuring the student understands WHY we divide (to find a "per one" amount) and not just HOW. If the student is comfortable with division, move briskly through Examples A and B and devote more time to the application problems (Guided Practice 4-5), which require using the unit rate as a stepping stone to find other quantities.
