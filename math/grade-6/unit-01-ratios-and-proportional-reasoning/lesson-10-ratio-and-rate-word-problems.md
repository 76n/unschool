# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 1, Lesson 10

## Topic
Ratio and Rate Word Problems

## Learning Objectives
- Solve multi-step word problems involving ratios and rates by selecting an appropriate strategy (ratio table, double number line, unit rate, or equivalent ratios).
- Identify the type of problem (ratio, rate, unit rate, scaling) and choose the most efficient approach.
- Interpret and communicate answers in the context of the original problem.

## Prerequisites
- All skills from Lessons 1-9: writing ratios, equivalent ratios, ratio tables, double number lines, rates, unit rates, comparing rates, and scaling.
- Ability to read multi-step word problems and identify relevant information.
- Multiplication and division of whole numbers and decimals.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.
- Calculator (optional, for checking work on multi-step problems).

**Standards:** 6.RP.3 — Use ratio and rate reasoning to solve real-world and mathematical problems, e.g., by reasoning about tables of equivalent ratios, tape diagrams, double number line diagrams, or equations.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review the strategies learned in the unit and help students think about when to use each one.

1. Present three mini-problems (students do NOT need to solve them — just identify the strategy):
   - (a) "A recipe uses flour and sugar in the ratio 4:1. How much sugar for 20 cups of flour?"
   - (b) "Which is a better deal: 5 pencils for $2.00 or 8 pencils for $3.60?"
   - (c) "A map scale is 1 cm = 15 km. How far is 6 cm on the map?"

2. Ask: "For each problem, what strategy would you use? Equivalent ratios? A ratio table? Unit rate? Why?"

3. Discuss: (a) Equivalent ratios or ratio table. (b) Unit rate comparison. (c) Unit rate (multiply scale by distance).

4. Say: "Today we are pulling all of our strategies together to tackle mixed word problems. The key is to read carefully, identify what you know and what you need to find, and then pick the best tool."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Multi-step problem** — A problem that requires more than one calculation to reach the answer.
- **Strategy selection** — Choosing the most efficient method to solve a problem based on what information is given.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Present a framework for solving word problems with ratios and rates.**

Write this on the board:

**SOLVE Framework:**
1. **S** — State the problem. What are you being asked to find?
2. **O** — Organize the information. What quantities are given? What are their units?
3. **L** — Label the relationship. Is it a ratio? A rate? A unit rate? A scaling problem?
4. **V** — Visualize with a tool. Choose: ratio table, double number line, equivalent ratios, or unit rate calculation.
5. **E** — Execute and explain. Solve, then write your answer in a complete sentence with units.

**Step 2 — Model the framework with a multi-step problem.**

"A school is making trail mix for a field trip. The recipe uses peanuts, raisins, and chocolate chips in the ratio 5:3:2. They need 40 cups of trail mix total. How many cups of each ingredient do they need?"

1. **S:** Find the cups of peanuts, raisins, and chocolate chips.
2. **O:** Ratio is 5:3:2. Total mix = 40 cups.
3. **L:** This is a ratio problem (part-to-whole). Total parts = 5 + 3 + 2 = 10.
4. **V:** Ratio table:

   | Peanuts | Raisins | Chocolate | Total |
   |---------|---------|-----------|-------|
   | 5       | 3       | 2         | 10    |
   | ?       | ?       | ?         | 40    |

   Scale factor: 40 / 10 = 4.

5. **E:** Peanuts: 5 x 4 = 20 cups. Raisins: 3 x 4 = 12 cups. Chocolate chips: 2 x 4 = 8 cups. Check: 20 + 12 + 8 = 40 cups.

Answer: They need 20 cups of peanuts, 12 cups of raisins, and 8 cups of chocolate chips.

**Step 3 — Model a mixed rate-and-ratio problem.**

"Liam bikes at a rate of 12 miles per hour. His friend Nora bikes at 9 miles per hour. They both start biking at the same time. After 3 hours, how much farther has Liam biked than Nora?"

1. **S:** Find the difference in distance after 3 hours.
2. **O:** Liam: 12 mph. Nora: 9 mph. Time: 3 hours.
3. **L:** These are rates (distance per time). Use the unit rate to find total distance.
4. **V:** Liam: 12 x 3 = 36 miles. Nora: 9 x 3 = 27 miles.
5. **E:** Difference: 36 - 27 = 9 miles. After 3 hours, Liam has biked 9 miles farther than Nora.

**Step 4 — Emphasize reading carefully.**

"Many students make errors not because of math, but because they misread the problem. Always re-read the question after solving. Did you answer what was asked? Are your units correct?"

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Ratio problem with a total given.**

"A fruit salad has strawberries and blueberries in the ratio 7:3. If there are 60 pieces of fruit total, how many strawberries are there?"

- Total parts: 7 + 3 = 10.
- Scale factor: 60 / 10 = 6.
- Strawberries: 7 x 6 = 42.
- Answer: There are 42 strawberries.

**Example B — Rate problem requiring multiple steps.**

"A plumber charges $55 per hour and a $75 service fee for each visit. How much does the plumber charge for a 4-hour visit?"

- Hourly charge: $55 x 4 = $220.
- Add service fee: $220 + $75 = $295.
- Answer: The plumber charges $295 for a 4-hour visit.

**Example C — Choosing between strategies.**

"A bakery sells muffins in boxes of 6 for $9 and boxes of 10 for $14. If you need 30 muffins, which option costs less?"

Strategy: Find the cost of 30 muffins using each box size.

Option 1 (boxes of 6): 30 / 6 = 5 boxes. Cost: 5 x $9 = $45.
Option 2 (boxes of 10): 30 / 10 = 3 boxes. Cost: 3 x $14 = $42.

Answer: Buying 3 boxes of 10 costs $42, which is $3 less than 5 boxes of 6 ($45). The boxes of 10 are the better deal.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. A classroom has boys and girls in the ratio 3:5. If there are 32 students total, how many boys are there? How many girls?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Total parts = 3 + 5 = 8. Scale factor = 32 / 8. Then multiply each part."

2. A painter can paint 3 walls in 2 hours. At this rate, how many walls can she paint in 8 hours?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Set up a ratio table: 3 walls per 2 hours. Scale up to 8 hours."

3. A store sells ribbon at $3 for every 2 feet. How much does 7 feet of ribbon cost?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find the unit rate (cost per foot) first, then multiply by 7."

4. Two buses leave a station at the same time. Bus A travels at 45 miles per hour. Bus B travels at 55 miles per hour. After 2 hours, how much farther has Bus B traveled?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Find the distance each bus traveled, then subtract."

5. A recipe makes 16 pancakes using 2 cups of mix. You want to make 40 pancakes. How many cups of mix do you need? If each cup of mix costs $1.25, how much will it cost?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "This is a two-step problem. First find cups of mix, then find cost."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### ★ On-Level

1. Red and blue tiles are used in a mosaic in the ratio 4:7. If there are 44 tiles in total, how many are red?

2. A factory produces 150 widgets in 5 hours. How many widgets does it produce in 12 hours at the same rate?

3. A 15-ounce bottle of juice costs $2.70. How much do 6 bottles cost?

4. A car travels 280 miles on 8 gallons of gas. How far can it travel on 15 gallons?

### ★★ Stretch

5. A school is mixing paint for a mural. They need green paint, which is made by mixing blue and yellow in the ratio 2:5. They need 28 cups of green paint total. How many cups of blue paint and how many cups of yellow paint do they need?

6. Two workers are building a fence. Worker A installs 8 fence posts in 3 hours. Worker B installs 10 fence posts in 4 hours. If they work together for 6 hours, how many fence posts will they install in total? (Hint: find each person's rate, then add them.)

7. A road trip is 450 miles. The family drives at an average speed of 60 miles per hour. Gas costs $3.60 per gallon, and their car gets 30 miles per gallon. How long does the trip take? How much do they spend on gas?

### ★★★ Challenge

8. A pet shelter has cats, dogs, and rabbits in the ratio 4:3:1. There are 15 more cats than rabbits. How many animals are in the shelter in total?

9. Mia is training for a race. In week 1, she runs 3 miles in 27 minutes. In week 4, she runs 5 miles in 40 minutes. Has her speed improved? By how much (in minutes per mile)?

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: comparing fractions with unlike denominators — Grade 5)*

10. Which is greater: 3/5 or 5/8? Show your work.

11. Which is greater: 7/12 or 2/3? Show your work.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. A snack mix contains pretzels and crackers in the ratio 3:2. If you want 25 pieces of snack mix total, how many pretzels do you need?

2. A bike rider travels at 15 miles per hour. A jogger travels at 6 miles per hour. After 2.5 hours, how much farther has the bike rider traveled than the jogger?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Return to the SOLVE framework. Walk through a problem step by step with the student, having them fill in each step. Focus on Step 3 (Label the relationship) — many errors come from misidentifying the type of problem. Provide additional practice with simpler one-step problems before returning to multi-step ones.

### If Mastery Is Met
Present an open-ended challenge: "Create your own multi-step word problem that involves a ratio AND a rate. Solve it yourself, then explain which strategies you used and why."

## Extension (Optional)

1. **Unit 1 Strategy Guide:** Create a one-page "cheat sheet" that lists all the strategies learned in this unit (equivalent ratios, ratio tables, double number lines, unit rates). For each strategy, write when to use it and give one example. This serves as a study tool for the unit assessment.

2. **Real-World Rate Report:** Find a real-world problem that involves rates (e.g., comparing internet plans, cell phone data rates, or sports statistics). Solve it using at least two different strategies and explain which one was more efficient.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Not identifying total parts in a ratio-to-total problem.** When a problem gives a ratio and a total, students often try to divide the total by one of the ratio terms rather than by the sum of all parts. Emphasize: "Add the parts first to find the total parts."
- **Mixing up operations in multi-step problems.** Students may add when they should multiply or forget to do the second step. Encourage them to write out a plan before computing.
- **Answering a different question than what was asked.** A problem may ask "how many MORE" but the student gives the total for one quantity. Stress the final step of re-reading the question and matching the answer to it.

### Pacing Advice
This is a synthesis lesson, so the pace depends heavily on how well the student has internalized Lessons 1-9. If the student handles Guided Practice smoothly, move to Independent Practice and the Challenge problems. If the student struggles with problem selection (which strategy to use), spend more time on the Warm-Up discussion, presenting more problems and having the student just classify them before solving. A second session on this lesson is valuable if the student needs it before the unit assessment.

**★★★ Challenge**
