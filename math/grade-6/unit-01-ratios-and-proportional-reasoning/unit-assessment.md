## Section A — Understanding Ratios (3 items)

**1.** A jar contains 9 red marbles and 6 green marbles. Write the ratio of green marbles to red marbles in all three forms (word form, colon form, and fraction form).

**(Answer: 6 to 9, 6:9, 6/9)**

**2.** A pet store has 8 cats, 5 dogs, and 3 hamsters. Write:
- (a) The ratio of dogs to cats. **(Answer: 5:8)**
- (b) The ratio of hamsters to the total number of animals. **(Answer: 3:16)**
- (c) Is your answer to (b) a part-to-part or part-to-whole ratio? **(Answer: Part-to-whole)**

**3.** Sofia says, "The ratio of apples to oranges is 4:7." Marcus says, "The ratio of oranges to apples is 4:7." Can they both be correct about the same basket of fruit? Explain why or why not.

**(Answer: No, they cannot both be correct about the same basket. If Sofia is correct that apples to oranges is 4:7, then the ratio of oranges to apples must be 7:4, not 4:7. The order of a ratio matters — switching the order changes the ratio. They could only both be correct if there were equal numbers of apples and oranges, but 4:7 is not equal.)**

**Scoring — Section A:**
- Item 1: 1 point (all three forms must be correct; accept unsimplified forms).
- Item 2: 1 point (all three parts must be correct; 1/2 credit if two of three parts are correct).
- Item 3: 1 point (correct answer with valid explanation).

## Section B — Equivalent Ratios (3 items)

**4.** Write three equivalent ratios for 6:5.

**(Answer: Answers will vary. Examples: 12:10, 18:15, 24:20, 30:25. Any ratios produced by multiplying both terms by the same nonzero whole number.)**

**5.** Simplify the ratio 28:42 to simplest form. Show your work.

**(Answer: GCF of 28 and 42 is 14. 28 / 14 = 2, 42 / 14 = 3. Simplest form: 2:3.)**

**6.** Are the ratios 15:20 and 12:16 equivalent? Show how you know.

**(Answer: Yes. 15:20 simplifies to 3:4 (GCF = 5). 12:16 simplifies to 3:4 (GCF = 4). Both simplify to 3:4, so they are equivalent.)**

**Scoring — Section B:**
- Item 4: 1 point (at least three valid equivalent ratios).
- Item 5: 1 point (correct simplest form with work shown).
- Item 6: 1 point (correct answer with valid justification).

## Section C — Ratio Tables and Double Number Lines (3 items)

**7.** Complete the ratio table.

| Pencils | Erasers |
|---------|---------|
| 4       | 3       |
| 8       | ?       |
| ?       | 15      |
| 16      | ?       |

**(Answer: Row 2: 6. Row 3: 20. Row 4: 12.)**

*Scoring detail: 8 pencils → 4 x 2 = 8, so 3 x 2 = 6 erasers. 15 erasers → 3 x 5 = 15, so 4 x 5 = 20 pencils. 16 pencils → 4 x 4 = 16, so 3 x 4 = 12 erasers.*

**8.** The double number line below shows the relationship between cups of lemonade mix and cups of water.

```
Mix (cups):    0     2     4     6     8    10
                |-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
                |-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
Water (cups):  0     5    10    15    20    25
```

Use the double number line to answer:
- (a) How many cups of water do you need for 6 cups of mix? **(Answer: 15 cups)**
- (b) If you have 20 cups of water, how many cups of mix do you need? **(Answer: 8 cups)**

**9.** A ratio table shows the following:

| Hours | Miles |
|-------|-------|
| 3     | 135   |
| 7     | ?     |

Find the missing value. Show your strategy.

**(Answer: Method 1 — Find the unit rate: 135 / 3 = 45 miles per hour. Then 45 x 7 = 315 miles. Method 2 — Find the scale factor from 3 to 7: this is not a whole-number factor, so the unit-rate approach is easier. Answer: 315 miles.)**

**Scoring — Section C:**
- Item 7: 1 point (all three missing values correct; 1/2 credit for two out of three).
- Item 8: 1 point (both parts correct).
- Item 9: 1 point (correct answer with strategy shown).

## Section D — Rates and Unit Rates (4 items)

**10.** Decide whether each of the following is a rate or a same-unit ratio. Write "Rate" or "Ratio" for each.
- (a) 24 pages in 3 minutes. **(Answer: Rate)**
- (b) 14 fiction books and 10 nonfiction books. **(Answer: Ratio)**
- (c) $18 for 4 pounds of meat. **(Answer: Rate)**

**11.** A bakery makes 96 muffins in 8 hours. What is the unit rate? Write your answer in a complete sentence with units.

**(Answer: 96 / 8 = 12 muffins per hour. "The bakery makes 12 muffins per hour.")**

**12.** A 14-ounce bag of chips costs $4.20. What is the unit price per ounce?

**(Answer: $4.20 / 14 = $0.30 per ounce.)**

**13.** Two stores sell the same brand of rice:
- Store A: 5 pounds for $4.25.
- Store B: 8 pounds for $6.40.

Which store has the better deal? Show your work.

**(Answer: Store A: $4.25 / 5 = $0.85 per pound. Store B: $6.40 / 8 = $0.80 per pound. Store B has the better deal because $0.80 per pound is less than $0.85 per pound.)**

**Scoring — Section D:**
- Item 10: 1 point (all three correct; 1/2 credit for two out of three).
- Item 11: 1 point (correct unit rate with units and sentence).
- Item 12: 1 point (correct unit price).
- Item 13: 1 point (correct identification of better deal with work shown).

## Section E — Problem Solving with Ratios and Rates (3 items)

**14.** A trail mix recipe uses nuts, dried fruit, and chocolate chips in the ratio 4:3:1. If you want to make 32 cups of trail mix, how many cups of each ingredient do you need?

**(Answer: Total parts: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8. Scale factor: 32 / 8 = 4. Nuts: 4 x 4 = 16 cups. Dried fruit: 3 x 4 = 12 cups. Chocolate chips: 1 x 4 = 4 cups. Check: 16 + 12 + 4 = 32 cups.)**

**15.** A car travels at a constant speed of 55 miles per hour. A truck travels at a constant speed of 40 miles per hour. They both leave the same town at the same time, traveling in the same direction. After 4 hours, how much farther has the car traveled than the truck?

**(Answer: Car: 55 x 4 = 220 miles. Truck: 40 x 4 = 160 miles. Difference: 220 - 160 = 60 miles. The car has traveled 60 miles farther.)**

**16.** A map uses the scale 2 centimeters = 35 kilometers. On the map, two cities are 9 centimeters apart. What is the actual distance between the cities in kilometers?

**(Answer: Method: Find the unit rate. 35 / 2 = 17.5 km per cm. Then 17.5 x 9 = 157.5 km. The actual distance is 157.5 kilometers.)**

**Scoring — Section E:**
- Item 14: 1 point (all three ingredient amounts correct with work; 1/2 credit if the student finds the correct total parts and scale factor but makes an arithmetic error on one ingredient).
- Item 15: 1 point (correct final answer — the difference — with work shown).
- Item 16: 1 point (correct distance with work shown).

## Error Analysis Guide

| Error Pattern | Example | Likely Cause | Intervention |
|--------------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Writes ratio in wrong order | Writes 9:6 when asked for green to red (which is 6:9) | Does not attend to the order of quantities as stated in the problem | Have student underline each quantity in the question and label "first" and "second." Practice 5 problems focused solely on order. |
| Adds instead of multiplies for equivalent ratios | Writes 6:5, 7:6, 8:7 as "equivalent" ratios | Applies additive reasoning instead of multiplicative reasoning | Return to concrete counters. Build copies of the base group. Show that adding 1 to each changes the relationship. Reinforce with ratio tables. |
| Incomplete ratio table (fills some cells, leaves others blank or wrong) | Correctly fills x2 row but incorrectly fills x5 row | May only see the additive pattern (going one row at a time) and cannot jump rows | Teach the "from first row to any row" multiplication strategy. Practice jumping from row 1 to row 4, row 1 to row 5, etc. |
| Divides in wrong direction for unit rate | Finds 3/135 instead of 135/3 for "miles per hour" | Confuses which quantity is "per one" | Use the sentence "I want ___ per ONE ___." The "one" unit is always the divisor. Practice writing the division before computing. |
| Answers a different question than what was asked | Problem asks "how much farther" but student gives total distance for one vehicle | Does not re-read the question after computing | Require student to re-read the question after solving and write the answer as a sentence that directly addresses the question. |
| Forgets to add all parts for ratio-to-total problems | For ratio 4:3:1, divides total by 4 instead of by 8 | Does not recognize that total parts = sum of all ratio terms | Highlight the word "total" in the problem. Write out: "4 parts + 3 parts + 1 part = 8 parts total." Always compute total parts as a visible first step. |

## Facilitator Observations

During the assessment, note the following to guide future instruction:

- Does the student work confidently or does she/he frequently pause and seem uncertain about where to begin? (May indicate need for more strategy-selection practice.)

- Does the student use scratch work and ratio tables, or does she/he try to solve everything mentally? (Students who skip organizational tools are more prone to errors on multi-step problems.)

- Does the student label units in answers, or are answers bare numbers? (Labeling units indicates deeper conceptual understanding.)

- Does the student check work or re-read problems before moving on? (Self-monitoring is a key metacognitive skill for success in later units.)

- Which sections does the student complete most quickly and most slowly? (Speed patterns can reveal both strengths and areas of uncertainty.)
