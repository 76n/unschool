# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 1, Lesson 2

## Topic
Writing Ratios Three Ways

## Learning Objectives
- Write a ratio in three equivalent forms: a:b, a to b, and a/b.
- Distinguish between part-to-part and part-to-whole ratios.
- Explain why the order of a ratio matters and demonstrate this with examples.

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of what a ratio is (Lesson 1).
- Ability to use "for every" language to describe a ratio.
- Familiarity with fractions as notation (numerator/denominator).
- Comfort with identifying two quantities in a comparison.

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector).
- Counters or small objects in two colors.
- Student worksheet (Guided Practice and Independent Practice problems).
- Index cards or half-sheets for Exit Ticket.

**Standards:** 6.RP.1 — Understand the concept of a ratio and use ratio language to describe a ratio relationship between two quantities.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** Review Lesson 1 and activate thinking about different ways to express the same idea.

1. Show 5 red counters and 8 blue counters. Ask: "What is the ratio of red to blue? Say it using 'for every' language."
2. Ask: "What is the ratio of blue to red? Is it the same or different?"
3. Say: "In Lesson 1, we wrote ratios using the words 'to.' Today we will learn that there are actually three different ways to write the exact same ratio."

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)

### Key Vocabulary
- **Colon notation** — Writing a ratio using a colon, such as 3:5.
- **Word notation** — Writing a ratio using the word "to," such as 3 to 5.
- **Fraction notation** — Writing a ratio as a fraction, such as 3/5.
- **Part-to-part ratio** — A ratio that compares one part of a group to another part of the same group (e.g., red to blue).
- **Part-to-whole ratio** — A ratio that compares one part of a group to the total (e.g., red to all counters).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — Introduce the three ways to write a ratio.**

Place 3 red counters and 7 blue counters on the table. Say: "The ratio of red to blue is 3 to 7. We already know how to write it with words. Here are all three ways to write this same ratio."

Write on the board:
- 3 to 7 (word form)
- 3:7 (colon form)
- 3/7 (fraction form)

Emphasize: "All three of these mean the same thing. They are just different notations — different ways of writing the same comparison."

**Step 2 — Clarify that fraction notation for a ratio is NOT the same as a fraction of a whole.**

This is a critical point. Say: "When we write 3/7 as a ratio, it does NOT mean '3 out of 7 total.' It means '3 compared to 7.' There are 3 red and 7 blue — that is 10 counters total, not 7. The fraction form is just a notation. Be careful not to confuse ratio fraction form with the fraction of the whole group."

**Step 3 — Introduce part-to-part vs. part-to-whole ratios.**

Using the same 3 red and 7 blue counters:
- "The ratio of red to blue is 3:7. This is a part-to-part ratio because we are comparing one part (red) to another part (blue)."
- "The ratio of red to total counters is 3:10. This is a part-to-whole ratio because we are comparing one part (red) to the whole group (all 10 counters)."
- "The ratio of blue to total counters is 7:10. This is also part-to-whole."

Ask: "How do you find the whole? You add the parts together: 3 + 7 = 10."

**Step 4 — Reinforce that order matters.**

Write on the board: "The ratio of red to blue is 3:7. The ratio of blue to red is 7:3."

Ask: "Are 3:7 and 7:3 the same?" (No.) "What changes?" (Which quantity is first.)

Give a real-world example: "A recipe uses 2 cups of flour and 1 cup of sugar. The ratio of flour to sugar is 2:1. The ratio of sugar to flour is 1:2. If you mix these up, your recipe will be wrong."

### Worked Examples

**Example A — Writing a ratio three ways.**

A classroom has 11 boys and 13 girls.

- Ratio of boys to girls:
  - Word form: 11 to 13
  - Colon form: 11:13
  - Fraction form: 11/13
- All three forms describe the same comparison.

**Example B — Identifying part-to-part and part-to-whole.**

A bag has 4 green marbles and 6 yellow marbles.

- Part-to-part (green to yellow): 4:6, or 4 to 6, or 4/6.
- Part-to-part (yellow to green): 6:4, or 6 to 4, or 6/4.
- Part-to-whole (green to total): 4:10, or 4 to 10, or 4/10. (Total = 4 + 6 = 10.)
- Part-to-whole (yellow to total): 6:10, or 6 to 10, or 6/10.

**Example C — Order matters in context.**

A pet store has 9 cats and 5 dogs.

- "What is the ratio of dogs to cats?" We need dogs first: 5:9.
- "What is the ratio of cats to total animals?" We need cats to total: 9:14. (Total = 9 + 5 = 14.)
- Common mistake: writing 9:5 when asked for dogs to cats. Always read the question carefully.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. A fruit bowl has 6 apples and 4 oranges. Write the ratio of apples to oranges in all three forms.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Start with the word form, then translate it to colon form and fraction form."

2. Using the same fruit bowl (6 apples, 4 oranges), write the ratio of oranges to total fruit in all three forms.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "What is the total? Is this part-to-part or part-to-whole?"

3. A trail mix has 10 peanuts, 5 raisins, and 3 chocolate chips. What is the ratio of raisins to peanuts? Write it three ways.
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Which quantity is named first in the question?"

4. Using the same trail mix, what is the ratio of chocolate chips to total pieces? Write it three ways. Is this part-to-part or part-to-whole?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "How do you find the total? Add all three parts together."

5. A parking lot has 15 sedans and 9 trucks. Jayden writes the ratio of trucks to sedans as 15:9. Is Jayden correct? If not, what should the correct ratio be?
   - *Facilitator prompt:* "Look at what the problem asks for first. Does 15 represent trucks or sedans?"

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)

### ★ On-Level

1. A zoo has 8 monkeys and 12 parrots. Write the ratio of monkeys to parrots in all three forms.

2. A box has 7 red crayons and 5 blue crayons. Write the ratio of blue crayons to total crayons three ways. Is this part-to-part or part-to-whole?

3. There are 20 students in a class. 11 are girls and 9 are boys. Write the ratio of boys to girls in colon form.

4. A necklace uses 4 white beads for every 3 black beads. Write the ratio of black beads to white beads in fraction form.

### ★★ Stretch

5. A garden has 15 rose bushes, 10 tulip plants, and 5 daisy plants. Write three different ratios you can find in this garden — at least one must be part-to-whole. Write each in colon form.

6. Samantha says, "The ratio of cats to dogs is 3/5, so 3 out of every 5 animals are cats." Is Samantha correct? Explain why or why not.

7. A recipe calls for 2 eggs, 3 cups of flour, and 1 cup of milk. Write the ratio of flour to total ingredients in all three forms. (Hint: count each item separately — 2 + 3 + 1 = 6 total items.)

### ★★★ Challenge

8. In a school election, the ratio of votes for Candidate A to votes for Candidate B is 7:5. If there were 60 total votes, how many votes did each candidate receive? Explain your reasoning.

9. A shelf has fiction and nonfiction books. The ratio of fiction to total books is 5:8. What is the ratio of fiction to nonfiction? What is the ratio of nonfiction to total books? Explain how you found the nonfiction count.

### Interleaved Review
*(Reviewing: dividing fractions — Grade 5)*

10. Solve: 3/4 / 1/2 = ?

11. Solve: 5/6 / 2/3 = ?

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)

### Exit Ticket

1. A fish tank has 9 goldfish and 6 guppies. Write the ratio of guppies to goldfish in all three forms.

2. Using the same fish tank, what is the ratio of goldfish to total fish? Is this a part-to-part or part-to-whole ratio?

**Scoring:**
- 2 out of 2 correct = Mastery demonstrated.
- 1 out of 2 correct = Partial understanding; review errors.
- 0 out of 2 correct = Reteach needed.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Return to concrete counters. Place two groups and walk through all three notations step by step. Pay special attention to whether the student confuses part-to-part with part-to-whole. If the student writes the ratio in the wrong order, practice circling the first-named quantity in the question and labeling it "first."

### If Mastery Is Met
Have the student write a short paragraph describing a real-world situation that involves both a part-to-part ratio and a part-to-whole ratio. They should write each ratio in all three forms.

## Extension (Optional)

1. **Ratio Reporter:** Imagine you are a sports reporter. Write a short report about a basketball game where Team A scored 48 points and Team B scored 36 points. Use at least three different ratios in your report, written in at least two different forms.

2. **Three-Part Ratios:** Sometimes we compare three things at once, like a paint mixture of 2 parts red, 3 parts blue, and 1 part white, written as 2:3:1. Create your own three-part ratio scenario and explain what each number represents.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

### Common Errors
- **Confusing ratio fraction notation with part-of-a-whole fractions.** Students may see 3/7 and think "3 out of 7 total" rather than "3 compared to 7." Reinforce the difference explicitly and repeatedly. Use the sentence frame: "This ratio means 3 for every 7, NOT 3 out of 7."
- **Switching the order when translating between forms.** A student might correctly say "4 to 6" but then write 6:4 in colon form. Have the student point to the first number as they translate from one form to the next.
- **Forgetting to add all parts for part-to-whole ratios.** When finding the total, students sometimes add only two of three groups. Encourage listing all parts before adding.

### Pacing Advice
The three-notation concept is straightforward for most students, so the real depth in this lesson comes from part-to-part vs. part-to-whole distinctions. If the student handles the three forms quickly, spend more time on Guided Practice problems 4 and 5 and the Stretch/Challenge problems. If the student is confused by fraction notation for ratios, spend extra time on Step 2 of Direct Instruction and use side-by-side examples: "3/10 as a fraction means 3 out of 10. But 3/7 as a ratio means 3 compared to 7."
