# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 5, Lesson 8

## Topic
Box Plots: Five-Number Summary, Creating Box Plots, Interpreting Quartiles and IQR

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Calculate the five-number summary (minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum) for any data set and explain what each component represents.
- Construct a box plot (box-and-whisker plot) from a five-number summary by drawing the box, median line, and whiskers on a number line.
- Interpret a box plot to describe the distribution's center, spread, and shape, including comparing two box plots on the same scale.

## Prerequisites
- Finding the median of odd and even data sets (Lesson 4)
- Finding Q1, Q3, and IQR (Lesson 5)
- Understanding range and IQR as measures of spread (Lesson 5)
- Reading dot plots and histograms (Lessons 6–7)

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Define the five-number summary, teach box plot construction step by step, and develop interpretation skills.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Five-Number Summary:** A set of five values that summarize a data set: Minimum, Q1, Median (Q2), Q3, Maximum.
- **Box Plot (Box-and-Whisker Plot):** A display that shows the five-number summary on a number line. A rectangle (the box) spans from Q1 to Q3, a vertical line inside the box marks the median, and horizontal lines (whiskers) extend from the box to the minimum and maximum.
- **Whisker:** Each horizontal line extending from the edge of the box to the minimum (left whisker) or maximum (right whisker).
- **Interquartile Range (IQR):** The length of the box. IQR = Q3 − Q1. Represents the spread of the middle 50% of the data.
- **Outlier:** A data value that falls more than 1.5 × IQR below Q1 or above Q3. (Introduced conceptually; formal calculation is extension material.)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1 — The five-number summary:**

"Every data set has these five key values:
1. Minimum (Min): The smallest value.
2. First Quartile (Q1): The median of the lower half of the data (25th percentile).
3. Median (Q2): The middle value of the full data set (50th percentile).
4. Third Quartile (Q3): The median of the upper half of the data (75th percentile).
5. Maximum (Max): The largest value.

These five numbers divide the data into four sections, each containing approximately 25% of the data values:
- Section 1: Min to Q1 (lowest 25%)
- Section 2: Q1 to Median (lower-middle 25%)
- Section 3: Median to Q3 (upper-middle 25%)
- Section 4: Q3 to Max (highest 25%)"

**Step 2 — Constructing a box plot:**

1. Calculate the five-number summary.
2. Draw a horizontal number line that spans slightly beyond the minimum and maximum. Choose a scale with equal intervals.
3. Above the number line, draw a small vertical mark at each of the five values.
4. Draw a rectangle (box) with its left edge at Q1 and its right edge at Q3.
5. Draw a vertical line inside the box at the median.
6. Draw a horizontal line (whisker) from the left edge of the box to the minimum.
7. Draw a horizontal line (whisker) from the right edge of the box to the maximum.
8. Label the number line with a title and units.

**Step 3 — Interpreting a box plot:**

- **Center:** The median is shown by the vertical line inside the box.
- **Spread of middle 50%:** The width of the box = IQR = Q3 − Q1.
- **Full spread:** From the left end of the left whisker (Min) to the right end of the right whisker (Max) = Range.
- **Shape:** If the median line is centered in the box, the middle 50% is symmetric. If it is closer to Q1, the data is skewed right (upper half is more spread out). If closer to Q3, the data is skewed left.
- **Whisker length:** A long right whisker means the top 25% of data is widely spread; a long left whisker means the bottom 25% is widely spread.

**Step 4 — What box plots do not show:**

"A box plot summarizes the data but does not show individual values. You cannot see if there are outliers (unless you modify the plot), how many values are in each section, or the exact shape within each quarter. Box plots trade detail for simplicity and comparability."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Five-number summary and box plot construction:**

Number of points scored by a basketball player across 12 games:
8, 14, 22, 17, 5, 19, 11, 25, 13, 20, 16, 9

Step 1: Order the data.
5, 8, 9, 11, 13, 14, 16, 17, 19, 20, 22, 25

Step 2: Find the five-number summary.
- Min = **5**
- Max = **25**
- n = 12 (even); Q2 = average of 6th and 7th values = (14 + 16) ÷ 2 = **15**
- Lower half (first 6): 5, 8, 9, 11, 13, 14 → Q1 = (9 + 11) ÷ 2 = **10**
- Upper half (last 6): 16, 17, 19, 20, 22, 25 → Q3 = (19 + 20) ÷ 2 = **19.5**
- IQR = 19.5 − 10 = **9.5**

Five-number summary: Min = 5, Q1 = 10, Median = 15, Q3 = 19.5, Max = 25

Box plot description:
- Number line from 0 to 30, with tick marks at every 5.
- Box drawn from 10 (Q1) to 19.5 (Q3) — width of 9.5 units.
- Vertical line inside box at 15 (median). The median line is slightly left of center in the box, indicating a slight right skew (the upper 25% from Q3 to 25 is less spread than the segment from median to Q3).
- Left whisker from 10 down to 5 (length 5).
- Right whisker from 19.5 up to 25 (length 5.5).

Interpretation: The typical scoring range is 10–19.5 points (the middle 50%). The median game score is 15 points. The left whisker and right whisker are roughly equal, suggesting a roughly symmetric distribution overall.

**Example 2 — Interpreting a box plot:**

A box plot for daily low temperatures (°F) over one month shows:
Min = 28, Q1 = 38, Median = 44, Q3 = 51, Max = 67

- The box spans from 38 to 51, so the IQR = **13°F**. This means the middle 50% of daily lows ranged over a 13°F span.
- The full range = 67 − 28 = **39°F**.
- The median (44) is slightly closer to Q1 (38) than to Q3 (51), suggesting the upper half of data (44 to 51) is more compressed than the lower half (38 to 44). Actually: Q1 to Median = 44−38 = 6; Median to Q3 = 51−44 = 7. These are close, suggesting the box is roughly symmetric.
- The right whisker spans from 51 to 67 = 16 units; the left whisker spans 38 − 28 = 10 units. The longer right whisker indicates the top 25% of temperatures are more spread out — some unusually warm days.
- 75% of days had lows at or below 51°F (Q3).

**Example 3 — Comparing two box plots:**

Two sixth-grade classes took the same science test. Box plots on the same number line:

Class A: Min = 55, Q1 = 68, Median = 75, Q3 = 82, Max = 95
Class B: Min = 40, Q1 = 58, Median = 72, Q3 = 80, Max = 98

Comparison:
- Class A's median (75) is higher than Class B's median (72) → Class A performed slightly better overall.
- Class A IQR = 82 − 68 = 14; Class B IQR = 80 − 58 = 22 → Class B has more variability in the middle 50%.
- Class A has a smaller range (95 − 55 = 40) than Class B (98 − 40 = 58) → Class B has more spread overall.
- Class B has a low outlier region (minimum of 40, far left whisker) — some students in Class B scored very low.
- Conclusion: Class A performed higher and more consistently. Class B had some very high AND very low performers.

**Exit Ticket:**

> The number of minutes 9 students spent on a science project:
> 45, 60, 30, 75, 55, 80, 50, 65, 40
>
> (a) Find the five-number summary (Min, Q1, Median, Q3, Max).
> (b) Calculate the IQR.
> (c) What percent of students spent between 45 and 70 minutes? (Hint: use the quartile boundaries.)
> (d) One student is listed as spending 120 minutes. Would this change the IQR? Would it change the maximum? Explain.

**Expected Answers:**

Ordered: 30, 40, 45, 50, 55, 60, 65, 75, 80 (n = 9)

(a)
- Min = **30**
- Max = **80**
- Median = 5th value = **55**
- Lower half (exclude 55): 30, 40, 45, 50 → Q1 = (40 + 45) ÷ 2 = **42.5**
- Upper half (exclude 55): 60, 65, 75, 80 → Q3 = (65 + 75) ÷ 2 = **70**
- Five-number summary: 30, 42.5, 55, 70, 80

(b) IQR = 70 − 42.5 = **27.5 minutes**

(c) Q1 = 42.5, Q3 = 70. The interval from Q1 to Q3 contains the middle 50% of students. The interval 45 to 70 is close to Q1 to Q3 but not exactly. Looking at actual data: values between 45 and 70 are: 45, 50, 55, 60, 65 = 5 students. 5/9 ≈ **56%**. (Alternative interpretation using quartiles: Q1 to Q3 contains exactly the middle 50%.)

(d) Adding 120 minutes: the new maximum becomes 120 — this **changes the maximum** (and the range). The IQR is based on Q1 and Q3. With n = 10, the halves shift slightly. New ordered data: 30, 40, 45, 50, 55, 60, 65, 75, 80, 120. New Q2 = (55+60)÷2 = 57.5. Lower half: 30,40,45,50,55 → Q1 = 45. Upper half: 60,65,75,80,120 → Q3 = 75. IQR = 75−45 = 30. So the IQR **does change** (from 27.5 to 30), though the change is moderate. The maximum **does change** significantly (from 80 to 120).

**Mastery = correct five-number summary, correct IQR, correct percent reasoning, and correct explanation about the outlier's effect.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met

### If Mastery Is Met

## Extension (Optional)

**Modified Box Plots with Outlier Detection:**

A value is considered an outlier if it is:
- More than Q3 + 1.5 × IQR (upper fence), or
- Less than Q1 − 1.5 × IQR (lower fence)

Using Example 1 (basketball scoring):
Q1 = 10, Q3 = 19.5, IQR = 9.5
Upper fence = 19.5 + 1.5 × 9.5 = 19.5 + 14.25 = 33.75
Lower fence = 10 − 14.25 = −4.25

No values exceed 33.75 or fall below −4.25, so there are no outliers.

Now try this data set: 12, 15, 18, 20, 21, 22, 23, 55
Q1 = 16.5, Q3 = 22.5, IQR = 6
Upper fence = 22.5 + 9 = 31.5. Is 55 an outlier? Yes — 55 > 31.5.

In a modified box plot, the whisker stops at the highest non-outlier value (23), and the outlier (55) is marked with a separate dot.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- Students frequently confuse "25% of data is below Q1" with "the value at Q1 IS 25." Reinforce: Q1 is a value on the number line, and it means 25% of data falls below it.
- The most important conceptual point of this lesson: the box (Q1 to Q3) always contains the middle 50% of data, regardless of how wide or narrow it looks. The width of the box tells you about variability (wide box = high IQR = more spread in the middle 50%).
- When comparing two box plots (Example 3, Problem 3), require students to comment on BOTH center (median) AND spread (IQR and range). Single-statistic comparisons earn only partial credit.
- Box plots do not show n (the number of data points). Two box plots can look identical even if one has 10 data points and another has 1,000. This is a key limitation to discuss.
- The exit ticket part (d) previews outlier influence — connect to the formal outlier rule in the Extension if time allows.
