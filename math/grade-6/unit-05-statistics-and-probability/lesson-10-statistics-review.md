# Math — Grade 6 — Unit 5, Lesson 10

## Topic
Statistics Review and Real-World Application: Integrated Practice with All Concepts, Real-World Data Analysis

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Apply all unit concepts — statistical questions, measures of center (mean, median, mode), measures of variability (range, IQR), and data displays (dot plot, histogram, box plot) — to analyze a real-world data set from start to finish.
- Interpret statistical summaries and displays to draw conclusions, make comparisons, and support claims with evidence from data.
- Identify statistical questions, choose appropriate measures and displays for a given context, and recognize the effects of outliers on statistical summaries.

## Direct Instruction (10–15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Consolidate the unit's content through a structured walk-through of a complete data analysis cycle.

### Key Vocabulary Review
(Brief review only — students should know all of these from prior lessons)

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Statistical question | A question that anticipates variability in responses |
| Mean | Sum of values ÷ count; non-resistant to outliers |
| Median | Middle value when ordered; resistant to outliers |
| Mode | Most frequent value(s) |
| Range | Max − Min; non-resistant to outliers |
| IQR | Q3 − Q1; resistant to outliers |
| Dot plot | Individual dots above a number line; best for small, narrow data sets |
| Histogram | Bars representing frequency in equal-width bins; best for large or wide data sets |
| Box plot | Five-number summary display; best for comparing distributions |
| Outlier | A value unusually far from the rest of the data |
| Skew | Asymmetry in a distribution; right-skewed means tail goes right (mean > median) |

### The Data Analysis Cycle (Instruction Notes)

"When analyzing real-world data, statisticians follow a cycle:

**Step 1 — Ask a statistical question.** The question must anticipate variability.

**Step 2 — Collect or receive data.** Make sure you understand what was measured and how.

**Step 3 — Organize and display the data.** Choose the appropriate display based on the size of the data set, the type of data, and the question being answered.

**Step 4 — Calculate summary statistics.** Find mean (or median), mode (if meaningful), range, and IQR. Note any outliers.

**Step 5 — Interpret and communicate.** Answer the original question using evidence from the statistics and display. State what the data suggests and any limitations.

Today's lesson uses this full cycle in every problem."

### Full Worked Example — The Weather Study

A group of students recorded the daily high temperature (°F) for two weeks (14 days) in their city during March:

Week 1: 52, 58, 61, 55, 63, 57, 60
Week 2: 65, 70, 68, 74, 66, 72, 69

**Step 1 — Statistical question:** "How do daily high temperatures during March in our city vary, and do they tend to rise from week to week?"

**Step 2 — Data:** 14 values, measured in °F.

**Step 3 — Organize and display:**
All 14 values ordered: 52, 55, 57, 58, 60, 61, 63, 65, 66, 68, 69, 70, 72, 74

For the full data set: a dot plot works (14 values, range ≈ 22 units). For comparing weeks: side-by-side box plots are ideal.

**Step 4 — Summary statistics:**

Full data set (n = 14):
- Mean = (52+55+57+58+60+61+63+65+66+68+69+70+72+74) ÷ 14 = 890 ÷ 14 ≈ 63.6°F
- Median = (63+65)÷2 = 64°F (7th and 8th values)
- Range = 74−52 = 22°F
- Q1: lower half (52,55,57,58,60,61,63) → Q1 = 58°F; Q3: upper half (65,66,68,69,70,72,74) → Q3 = 70°F; IQR = 12°F

Week 1 five-number summary (ordered: 52,55,57,58,60,61,63):
Min=52, Q1=(55+57)÷2=56, Median=58, Q3=(60+61)÷2=60.5, Max=63

Week 2 five-number summary (ordered: 65,66,68,69,70,72,74):
Min=65, Q1=(66+68)÷2=67, Median=69, Q3=(70+72)÷2=71, Max=74

**Step 5 — Interpret:**
- Week 2 median (69°F) is 11 degrees higher than Week 1 median (58°F) — temperatures clearly rose.
- Week 1 IQR = 60.5−56 = 4.5°F (tight cluster). Week 2 IQR = 71−67 = 4°F (equally tight). Both weeks show consistent day-to-day temperatures with little variability.
- No outliers; the distribution across both weeks is approximately uniform (rising trend), not skewed.
- Conclusion: Temperatures in this city rose approximately 11°F from the first to second week of March, with both weeks showing consistent day-to-day temperatures within a 4–5 degree range.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

**Scenario: School Garden Project**

A sixth-grade class planted bean seeds and measured the height (cm) of each plant after 3 weeks. Heights recorded:
12, 18, 15, 20, 14, 17, 16, 22, 13, 19, 15, 18, 21, 16, 14

Work through the full data analysis cycle:

1. Write a statistical question this data could answer.
2. Order the data and create a frequency tally for a dot plot (how many plants at each height from 12 to 22 cm).
3. Find: (a) Mean, (b) Median, (c) Mode, (d) Range, (e) IQR
4. Describe the shape: is the distribution symmetric or skewed? Are there any outliers?
5. Which measure of center — mean or median — better represents the "typical" plant height? Explain.
6. If you wanted to compare these heights to a second class's bean plants, which display would you use? Why?

**Facilitator guidance:**
Ordered: 12, 13, 14, 14, 15, 15, 16, 16, 17, 18, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22 (n=15)
Mean = 250÷15 ≈ 16.7 cm; Median = 8th value = 16 cm; Modes = 14, 15, 16, 18 (all appear twice — multimodal, which is common in small data sets, so "no clear mode" is also an acceptable answer); Range = 22−12 = 10 cm; Q1 = 14 cm (4th value), Q3 = 19 cm (12th value), IQR = 5 cm. Distribution is roughly symmetric, no outliers. Mean and median are very close (16.7 vs. 16) — either is appropriate. For comparison: side-by-side box plots.

## Independent Practice (10–15 minutes)

**The Big Review Problems**

**Problem 1 — Integrated Data Analysis (Weather Data):**

A meteorologist recorded the number of rainy days per month over 12 months:
4, 7, 9, 12, 8, 5, 3, 6, 10, 8, 11, 7

(a) Find the mean, median, and mode. Round to the nearest tenth where needed.
(b) Find the range and IQR.
(c) Is this data skewed right, skewed left, or roughly symmetric? How can you tell from the relationship between mean and median?
(d) Create a dot plot representation: list how many times each value appears (tallied by value).
(e) Would a dot plot or histogram be more appropriate for this data set? Explain.
(f) The meteorologist adds January data for the following year: 2 rainy days. How does this change the mean? Does it change the median? Explain.

**Problem 2 — Comparing Two Groups (Sports Data):**

Points scored per game by two basketball teams across 10 games:

Team Rockets: 58, 72, 65, 80, 61, 74, 68, 77, 63, 70
Team Eagles: 45, 82, 55, 90, 50, 88, 48, 85, 52, 93

(a) Find the five-number summary for each team.
(b) Find the mean and median for each team.
(c) Which team has a higher median? Which has a higher mean?
(d) Which team is more consistent? Use both range and IQR to support your answer.
(e) If you were a scout evaluating both teams, which measure of variability would you use to judge consistency, and why?
(f) Based on this data, describe the box plot comparison. Which team's box would be wider? Which team's median line would be higher?

**Problem 3 — Identifying and Interpreting Displays:**

A histogram of students' reading speeds (words per minute) shows:
- Bin 80–99 wpm: 3 students
- Bin 100–119 wpm: 8 students
- Bin 120–139 wpm: 12 students
- Bin 140–159 wpm: 9 students
- Bin 160–179 wpm: 4 students
- Bin 180–199 wpm: 2 students
- Bin 200–219 wpm: 1 student

(a) How many students were measured in total?
(b) In which bin does the median fall?
(c) Is the distribution symmetric, skewed left, or skewed right? Explain.
(d) Would the mean be higher or lower than the median? Why?
(e) A student proposes using a dot plot instead. Is this a good idea? Why or why not?
(f) What bin width was used? What would happen to the histogram if you doubled the bin width?

**Problem 4 — Outlier Investigation (Science Data):**

A science class measured the time (in seconds) for 10 chemical reactions to complete:
14, 16, 15, 17, 13, 15, 16, 14, 15, 48

(a) Calculate the mean and median with the outlier included.
(b) Remove the outlier and recalculate both.
(c) Which measure changed more? By how much?
(d) The scientist says the 48-second reaction was caused by contaminated equipment. Should this value be included in a report about typical reaction times? Why or why not?
(e) After removing the outlier, is the mean or median more appropriate? Calculate the correct measure.

**Problem 5 (Stretch) — Statistical Reasoning:**

Two doctors are comparing patient wait times (minutes) at their clinics.

Doctor A's data: Min=5, Q1=12, Median=18, Q3=25, Max=35
Doctor B's data: Min=8, Q1=15, Median=22, Q3=40, Max=68

(a) Which doctor has shorter typical wait times? By how much?
(b) Which clinic has more consistent wait times? Use IQR to justify.
(c) 75% of Doctor A's patients wait no longer than how many minutes?
(d) 25% of Doctor B's patients wait more than how many minutes?
(e) A patient asks: "Which doctor has a lower chance of a very long wait?" What does the box plot data tell you, and what is your recommendation?

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)

**Exit Ticket:**

> A survey asked 12 students how many hours they slept last night:
> 7, 8, 6, 9, 7, 8, 10, 7, 6, 8, 9, 5
>
> Answer the following:
> (a) Is "How many hours do sixth graders sleep?" a statistical question? Yes or No — explain briefly.
> (b) Find the median and IQR for this data set.
> (c) The school counselor says "most students sleep between 6.5 and 8.75 hours." Is this claim supported by the statistics? (Hint: what do Q1 and Q3 tell you?)
> (d) Name the best display to present this data to the school's health committee and explain in one sentence why.

**Expected Answers:**

(a) **Yes** — it anticipates variability across many students (different students sleep different amounts).

(b) Ordered: 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 8, 8, 8, 9, 9, 10 (n=12)
    Q2 = (7+8)÷2 = **7.5 hours**
    Lower half (first 6): 5,6,6,7,7,7 → Q1 = (6+7)÷2 = **6.5 hours**
    Upper half (last 6): 8,8,8,9,9,10 → Q3 = (8+9)÷2 = **8.5 hours**
    IQR = 8.5 − 6.5 = **2 hours**

(c) The counselor says "most students sleep between 6.5 and 8.75 hours." Q1 = 6.5 (25th percentile) and Q3 = 8.5 (75th percentile) — the middle 50% of students sleep between 6.5 and 8.5 hours. The counselor's 8.75 upper bound is slightly too high (Q3 = 8.5), but the claim is **approximately supported**: the IQR (middle 50%) ranges from 6.5 to 8.5 hours.

(d) **Box plot** (to show the health committee the median, typical range/IQR, and any outliers in a clean summary) OR **dot plot** (for 12 students with integer values, shows every individual data point). Either is acceptable with a clear reason. The box plot is best for a formal presentation to a committee.

**Mastery = yes to (a) with reason; correct median and IQR in (b); correct Q1/Q3 interpretation in (c); reasonable display choice with justification in (d).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Target the specific gap: if (a) is wrong, revisit Lesson 1. If (b) is wrong, revisit Lessons 4–5. If (c) is wrong, revisit Lesson 8 (quartile interpretation). If (d) is wrong, revisit Lesson 9. Use this exit ticket as a diagnostic tool for unit-assessment preparation.

### If Mastery Is Met
Ask: "How many hours of sleep would be considered unusually low using the outlier rule (Q1 − 1.5 × IQR)? Is the value 5 an outlier by this definition?" (Lower fence = 6.5 − 1.5×2 = 6.5 − 3 = 3.5. Since 5 > 3.5, the value 5 is NOT an outlier by the formal rule — but it is at the low end.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

- Lesson 10 is an integration and consolidation lesson, not a lesson introducing new concepts. The goal is fluency and transfer — students should be able to move between concepts without prompting.
- The guided practice "bean plants" problem is deliberately designed to yield a multimodal data set (many values appear twice). This is intentional: use it to discuss when mode is and is not useful (here, the distribution is more meaningfully described by mean/median than mode).
- Problem 2 (Team Rockets vs. Eagles) produces a high-insight comparison: the Eagles score higher on average but are wildly inconsistent (range 48, IQR large); the Rockets are more consistent (lower range and IQR). This models the kind of reasoning a sports analyst, scientist, or business analyst actually does.
- Problem 4 previews data cleaning — when is it appropriate to remove a value? This is a rich discussion: the outlier in Problem 4 was caused by equipment failure, which is different from legitimate natural variability. Students should think critically about this distinction.
- Use the exit ticket results diagnostically before the unit assessment. If students struggle with parts (b), (c), or (d), offer targeted reteaching on those specific lessons.
