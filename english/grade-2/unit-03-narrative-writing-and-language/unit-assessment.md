# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 3 Assessment

## Unit: Narrative Writing & Language

**Assessment Type:** Summative (end-of-unit)
**Estimated Time:** 35-45 minutes
**Facilitation:** The facilitator reads directions aloud. The student completes written portions independently. Oral items are administered one-on-one. For grammar items, the facilitator may read the sentence aloud while the student follows along.
**Standards Assessed:** W.2.3, L.2.1a, L.2.1b, L.2.2c, L.2.2e

## Scoring Guide

### Part 1: Narrative Elements & Craft (10 points)

**Question 1 (1 point):**
- 1 point: Names at least two characters (Nico and Bella; accept also grandparents or the rabbits)
- 0 points: Cannot identify characters

**Question 2 (1 point):**
- 1 point: Identifies place (hill behind grandparents' farm, hidden garden) AND time (warm Sunday afternoon)
- 0.5 points: Identifies place OR time but not both
- 0 points: Cannot identify the setting

**Question 3 (1 point):**
- 1 point: Lists at least two events (e.g., they climbed the hill and found a wall; they looked over and saw a garden; they went through the gate; they explored the garden)
- 0.5 points: Lists only one event
- 0 points: Cannot identify events

**Question 4 (1 point):**
- 1 point: Describes the ending (they decided to keep the garden their secret) AND gives a reasonable opinion about whether it is satisfying with explanation
- 0.5 points: Describes the ending but does not evaluate it, or evaluates without describing
- 0 points: Cannot describe the ending

**Question 5 (2 points):**
- 2 points: Correctly identifies B as stronger AND gives a clear reason (e.g., "B starts with action and dialogue, which hooks the reader; A is boring and just tells facts about Zoe")
- 1 point: Correctly identifies B as stronger but gives a vague reason
- 0 points: Chooses A, or cannot explain

**Question 6 (1 point):**
- 1 point: Names any valid hook strategy (start with action, start with dialogue, start with a feeling/thought)
- 0 points: Cannot name a strategy

**Question 7 (1 point):**
- 1 point: Adds at least one sensory detail that appeals to sight, sound, taste, smell, or touch (e.g., "The boy ate his warm, cheesy pizza and licked his fingers.")
- 0 points: No sensory detail added or sentence unchanged

**Question 8 (1 point):**
- 1 point: Explains that "show, don't tell" means describing what a character does or looks like instead of just naming the feeling (e.g., "Instead of saying 'she was scared,' you describe what she does, like shaking or hiding")
- 0 points: Cannot explain the concept

**Question 9 (1 point):**
- 1 point: Names at least two of the three strategies (resolve the problem, share the character's feelings, reveal a lesson learned)
- 0.5 points: Names one strategy
- 0 points: Cannot name any

### Part 4: Narrative Writing Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | Mastered (5) | Approaching (3) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Story Structure** (beginning, events, ending) | Story has a clear beginning that introduces characters and setting, sequenced events, and a satisfying ending that wraps up the story | Story has some structure but may be missing a clear beginning or ending, or events may not be fully developed | Story is a collection of sentences without clear beginning, middle, or end; lacks recognizable structure |
| **Beginning & Hook** | Beginning hooks the reader using action, dialogue, or feeling; characters and setting are clearly introduced | Beginning introduces the story but lacks a hook; characters or setting may be vague | No clear beginning; characters and setting are not introduced |
| **Details & Description** | Includes at least two sensory details; demonstrates "show, don't tell" at least once; writing is vivid and engaging | Includes one sensory detail or attempt at showing; some descriptive language present | No sensory details; writing is plain and undeveloped; relies entirely on telling |
| **Language & Grammar** | Uses temporal words to sequence events; grammar is mostly correct (possessives, plurals, pronouns); sentences are complete | Uses some temporal words; a few grammar errors but meaning is clear; most sentences are complete | No temporal words; frequent grammar errors that interfere with meaning; incomplete sentences |

## Reteaching Guide

### If Part 1 (Narrative Elements & Craft) is below 7/10:

**Story elements (Questions 1-4):**
- Return to Lesson 1 and practice identifying elements in familiar stories
- Use the Story Map graphic organizer for any story the student reads

**Beginnings and hooks (Questions 5-6):**
- Return to Lesson 2 and practice writing beginnings using the three hook strategies
- Read the first page of several books together and identify the hook each author uses

**Details and show/tell (Questions 7-8):**
- Return to Lesson 4 and practice the five senses description activity with physical objects
- Do daily "show, don't tell" practice: give a feeling word, have the student describe what it looks like

**Endings (Question 9):**
- Return to Lesson 5 and practice writing endings for incomplete stories
- Post the three ending strategies where the student can see them during writing

---

### If Part 2 (Grammar & Language) is below 11/15:

**Collective nouns (Questions 10-12):**
- Review the collective noun chart from Lesson 6
- Practice through games: "What is a group of birds called?" "What is a group of wolves called?"

**Irregular plurals (Questions 13-16):**
- Create flashcards with singular on one side and plural on the other
- Practice the most common ones daily: child/children, tooth/teeth, mouse/mice, foot/feet, goose/geese
- Use irregular plurals in conversation: "Look, two geese!" "I brushed my teeth."

**Reflexive pronouns (Questions 17-20):**
- Post the reflexive pronoun chart where the student can see it
- Practice one sentence per day using a different reflexive pronoun
- Focus on the four most common: myself, himself, herself, themselves

**Possessives (Questions 21-24):**
- Practice the ownership test daily: "Does this mean 'more than one' or 'belonging to'?"
- Use physical objects: "This is Dad's mug. D-A-D, apostrophe, S."
- Write plural/possessive pairs and have the student sort them

---

### If Part 3 (Spelling & Reference Materials) is below 3/5:

- Continue building and using the personal word wall
- Practice 2-3 tricky words per day using the Try-It, Check-It, Fix-It strategy
- If dictionary skills are weak, practice alphabetical order with letter cards before trying dictionary lookups
- Integrate spelling practice into daily writing: have the student identify one word they are unsure about and look it up

---

### If Part 4 (Narrative Writing) is below 14/20:

- Identify which rubric criterion scored lowest:
  - **Story Structure:** Return to Lessons 1 and 3. Practice planning with the story map before every writing session. Ensure the student always has a plan before picking up the pencil.
  - **Beginning & Hook:** Return to Lesson 2. Practice writing multiple beginnings for the same story. Choose the best one together.
  - **Details & Description:** Return to Lesson 4. Practice the five senses activity with real objects. Read stories together and highlight sensory details.
  - **Language & Grammar:** Return to Lessons 3, 6, 7, 8. Practice temporal words, irregular plurals, reflexive pronouns, and possessives in isolation, then reintegrate into writing.

- For all areas: Have the student write a short narrative (5-8 sentences) with facilitator support using the writing checklist from Lesson 10. Focus on one area of improvement at a time.

### Part 4

**30.** Scored by rubric. See the narrative writing rubric above. A model mastery-level response:

> On Tuesday morning, Aisha was walking to school when she spotted something shiny under the old oak tree by the park. She bent down and picked it up. It was a small golden key with a red ribbon tied around it.
>
> "I wonder what this opens," Aisha said to herself.
>
> First, she looked around the tree to see if someone had dropped it. Nobody was there. Next, she put the key in her pocket and kept walking. All morning at school, she could feel the key poking her leg through her pocket. She could not stop thinking about it.
>
> Then, at recess, Aisha noticed a little wooden box sitting on the edge of the school garden. It had a tiny keyhole. Her heart started beating fast. She pulled the key out of her pocket and slid it into the lock. Click! The box opened.
>
> Inside was a folded piece of paper. Aisha unfolded it carefully. It said: "Congratulations! You found the treasure hunt prize. Bring this note to the library for a free book. — Mrs. Chen."
>
> Finally, Aisha ran to the library. Mrs. Chen smiled and let her pick any book she wanted. Aisha chose a book about animals around the world. She hugged it to her chest and grinned. She learned that being curious and paying attention can lead to wonderful surprises.

This response demonstrates: characters (Aisha, Mrs. Chen), setting (walking to school, school garden, library), a hook (action — spotting something shiny), temporal words (first, next, then, finally), sensory details (shiny key, poking her leg, heart beating fast), a reflexive pronoun (herself), a possessive (Aisha's — implied in "her pocket"), and a satisfying ending (resolved the mystery, showed feelings, revealed a lesson learned).
