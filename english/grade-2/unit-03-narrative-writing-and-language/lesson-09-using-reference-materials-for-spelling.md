# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 3, Lesson 9

## Topic
Using Reference Materials for Spelling

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify three reference strategies for checking spelling: word walls, dictionaries, and asking a knowledgeable person
- Use a simple dictionary or word list to find the correct spelling of a word
- Apply a step-by-step strategy when unsure how to spell a word (try it, check it, fix it)
- Understand that good writers check their spelling — it is a sign of strong writing, not weakness

## Prerequisites
- Ability to spell common high-frequency words (the, they, was, said, because, etc.)
- Ability to use phonetic spelling to attempt unfamiliar words
- Understanding that English spelling is not always predictable from sounds alone
- Writing experience from Lessons 1-8 of this unit

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- A simple children's dictionary (printed or online) — if unavailable, use the word list provided in this lesson
- Index cards and a marker (for building a personal word wall)
- The student's writing from earlier lessons in this unit (for revision practice)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate awareness of tricky spelling and review prior skills.

**Activity: Tricky Spellers**

Say: "You are becoming a great writer! You know how to write beginnings, put events in order, add details, and write endings. You also know about irregular plurals, reflexive pronouns, and possessives. Today we are going to learn one more important skill — what to do when you are not sure how to spell a word."

Write these three words. Ask the student to try to spell each one:

1. "because" (many students spell it "becuz" or "becaus")
2. "friend" (commonly spelled "frend" or "freind")
3. "beautiful" (commonly attempted as "butiful" or "byootiful")

Say: "Raise your hand if any of those felt hard to spell." (The student will likely agree.) "That is totally normal! English has a lot of words that do not sound the way they are spelled. Today we are going to learn what real writers do when they are not sure about a spelling."

**Review:**

4. **Lesson 8 Review:** "Rewrite this using a possessive: 'The hat that belongs to Emma.'" (Emma's hat)
5. **Lesson 6 Review:** "What is the plural of 'goose'?" (geese)

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce three reference strategies for spelling.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Reference materials:** Tools that help you find information, like dictionaries, word lists, and word walls
- **Word wall:** A list of important words posted where you can see them while you write
- **Dictionary:** A book (or online tool) that lists words in alphabetical order and tells you how to spell them, what they mean, and how to say them
- **Phonetic spelling:** Spelling a word the way it sounds — a great first try, but not always correct

### Instruction Notes

Say: "When you come to a word you are not sure how to spell, here are three tools you can use."

Write on paper:

**Three Spelling Tools for Writers**

**Tool 1: Word Wall or Spelling List**

Say: "A word wall is a list of words you use a lot, posted where you can see it while you write. You can look up and find the word quickly."

Show the student a sample word wall (or create one together on a piece of paper):

**Common Tricky Words**
| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| because | friend | said | they |
| people | would | could | should |
| beautiful | favorite | different | together |
| through | thought | enough | again |
| sometimes | always | every | before |

Say: "If you are writing and you need one of these words, you just look at the wall! No guessing."

**Tool 2: Dictionary**

Say: "A dictionary lists EVERY word in alphabetical order. If you know the first few letters of a word, you can look it up."

If you have a children's dictionary, open it and demonstrate:

"Let's say I want to spell the word 'special.' I know it starts with 'sp.' I open the dictionary to the S section, find 'sp' words, and look until I find 'special.' There it is: S-P-E-C-I-A-L."

If no dictionary is available, say: "You can also use an online dictionary or ask a grown-up to help you look it up on a phone or computer."

**Tool 3: Ask Someone**

Say: "The fastest tool of all — ask someone who knows! If a grown-up, older sibling, or teacher is nearby, you can ask: 'How do you spell ___?' Good writers are not embarrassed to ask. Asking shows you care about getting it right."

**The Try-It, Check-It, Fix-It Strategy:**

Say: "Here is a step-by-step plan for when you are not sure about a word."

Write on paper:

1. **TRY IT:** Sound the word out and write your best guess
2. **CHECK IT:** Does it look right? If not sure, use a tool (word wall, dictionary, ask someone)
3. **FIX IT:** If the spelling is wrong, cross it out neatly and write the correct spelling

Say: "Step 1 is important — always TRY first! Sounding it out is a smart strategy. Sometimes you will get it right. And even if you don't, your first try helps you look it up because you will know the first few letters."

### Worked Examples

Say: "Let's practice the Try-It, Check-It, Fix-It strategy."

**Word to spell: "different"**

1. TRY IT: The student writes their best attempt (maybe "difrent" or "diffrent")
2. CHECK IT: Look at the word wall (or dictionary). Find "different." D-I-F-F-E-R-E-N-T.
3. FIX IT: Cross out the first try, write the correct spelling.

Say: "See? You tried, you checked, and you fixed it. That is exactly what good writers do."

**Word to spell: "children"**

1. TRY IT: Student writes their attempt (maybe "childern" or "chilren")
2. CHECK IT: Look at the word wall or dictionary. C-H-I-L-D-R-E-N.
3. FIX IT: Write the correct spelling.

Say: "Notice that 'children' is also an irregular plural from Lesson 6! Spelling and grammar go together."

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice using reference materials with support.

**Activity 1: Build a Personal Word Wall (6 minutes)**

Say: "Let's build YOUR personal word wall. This is a list of words that YOU find tricky. You can keep it in your writing folder and look at it whenever you write."

Give the student 8-10 blank index cards. Together, identify words the student commonly misspells or finds tricky. Write one word per card, spelled correctly, in large clear letters.

**Suggested words** (choose based on what the student actually struggles with):

- because, friend, said, they, would, could, people, beautiful, favorite, different, together, through, thought, again, sometimes, every, really, know, write, right

Organize the cards alphabetically (or by category). Say: "This is YOUR word wall. You made it. Keep it next to you when you write, and you will always have these tricky words handy."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student already spells most of these correctly, focus on more challenging words: enough, special, surprise, already, caught, finally, instead.*

**Activity 2: Dictionary Scavenger Hunt (6 minutes)**

Using a children's dictionary (or the word list provided above as a substitute), have the student look up these five words and write the correct spelling:

1. Look up a word that starts with "thr" and means "the act of thinking." (thought — T-H-O-U-G-H-T)
2. Look up a word that starts with "beau" and means "very pretty." (beautiful — B-E-A-U-T-I-F-U-L)
3. Look up a word that starts with "en" and means "as much as you need." (enough — E-N-O-U-G-H)
4. Look up a word that starts with "fav" and means "the one you like best." (favorite — F-A-V-O-R-I-T-E)
5. Look up a word that starts with "sur" and means "something you did not expect." (surprise — S-U-R-P-R-I-S-E)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles with alphabetical order, help them: "B comes before C. So 'beautiful' comes after 'ask' but before 'cat.' Let's find the B section first." Guide them through the process rather than doing it for them.*

*If no dictionary is available: Say each word, have the student try to spell it, then show them the correct spelling. Discuss what part was tricky.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices spelling strategies independently.

**Part A: Try-It, Check-It, Fix-It (5 minutes)**

For each word below, the student:
1. Tries to spell it on their own
2. Checks it using the word wall, dictionary, or asks the facilitator
3. Fixes it if needed

Words to spell:
1. together
2. different
3. surprise
4. through
5. favorite

### Difficulty Tiers
- ★ Try to spell the five words, then check and fix using the word wall or asking the facilitator
- ★★ Try to spell the five words, check them using a dictionary (without asking), and fix any mistakes
- ★★★ Try to spell the five words, check using a dictionary, fix any mistakes, AND write each word in a sentence

**Part B: Revision Practice (5 minutes)**

Take out a piece of writing from an earlier lesson in this unit (a story beginning from Lesson 2, an event sequence from Lesson 3, or a paragraph from Lesson 4 or 5).

Read through the writing and look for any misspelled words. Use the Try-It, Check-It, Fix-It strategy to correct them.

### Interleaved Review

6. **Lesson 7 Review:** "Fill in the blank with a reflexive pronoun: She made dinner all by ___." (herself)
7. **Lesson 4 Review:** "What does 'show, don't tell' mean?" (Instead of saying how someone feels, describe what they do so the reader can figure out the feeling.)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess whether the student can use reference materials and spelling strategies.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Name two tools a writer can use to check spelling. (Any two: word wall/spelling list, dictionary, asking a knowledgeable person)
2. Spell the word "because." (B-E-C-A-U-S-E)
3. If you try to spell a word and it does not look right, what should you do? (Check it using a reference tool — word wall, dictionary, or ask someone — then fix it.)

**Mastery = Student names at least two spelling tools, spells "because" correctly (or uses a tool to find the correct spelling), and describes the check-and-fix strategy.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Keep the personal word wall visible during all writing time
- Practice 2-3 tricky words per day using the Try-It, Check-It, Fix-It strategy
- Do not penalize spelling during creative writing — keep spelling practice separate from story writing to avoid discouraging the student

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 10 (Writing a Complete Narrative). Preview: "You have learned every tool you need to write an amazing story — beginnings, events, details, endings, grammar, and spelling! Next time, you are going to put it ALL together and write your very own complete narrative!"

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**
- **Word Collector:** Over the next week, whenever you come across a word you do not know how to spell, write it on a card and add it to your personal word wall. See how many words you can collect!
- **Spelling Detective:** Find five tricky words in a book you are reading. Write each one down. What makes the spelling tricky? (Silent letters? Unusual vowel combinations? Letters that do not match the sounds?)
- **Teach-Back:** Choose three words from your word wall and teach the facilitator how to remember the spelling. Create a memory trick for each one. Example: "BECAUSE — Big Elephants Can Always Understand Small Elephants."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson can be completed in one session. The most important takeaway is the Try-It, Check-It, Fix-It strategy and the personal word wall. If time is short, prioritize these over the dictionary scavenger hunt.

**Spelling anxiety:** Some students become so worried about spelling that they avoid writing adventurous words and stick to simple words they know how to spell. This limits their writing. Combat this by separating drafting from editing: "When you are writing your first draft, just get your ideas down. Spell the best you can. We will check and fix spelling AFTER you finish writing." This frees the student to focus on ideas first.

**Phonetic spelling is valuable:** Never dismiss phonetic spelling as "wrong." It shows the student is thinking about sounds, which is an essential skill. Say: "I can read what you wrote! You did a great job sounding it out. The English spelling is a little different — let me show you." Validate the attempt, then teach the correct form.

**Dictionary readiness:** Some second graders are not yet fluent with alphabetical order, which makes dictionary use slow. That is okay. The goal of this lesson is to introduce the dictionary as a TOOL, not to make the student a fluent dictionary user. If alphabetical order is a barrier, focus on the word wall and asking strategies instead.

**Personal word wall maintenance:** Encourage the student to add to their word wall throughout the rest of the year. Every time they learn the correct spelling of a tricky word, they can make a new card. Over time, this becomes a powerful personal reference tool.

**Connection to Lesson 10:** In the next lesson, the student writes a complete narrative. Remind them to use their word wall and the Try-It, Check-It, Fix-It strategy during the editing phase. Spelling accuracy is assessed in the unit assessment, so this lesson directly supports that.

**Digital alternatives:** If the student has access to a tablet or computer, a spell-checker is another valid reference tool. You can mention it: "Spell-checkers are another tool writers use. They underline words that might be misspelled. But you should still know how to check words on your own, because spell-checkers do not catch every mistake."
