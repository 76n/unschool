# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 3, Lesson 6

## Topic
Collective Nouns and Irregular Plural Nouns

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify and use at least five collective nouns (e.g., a flock of birds, a pack of wolves, a bunch of grapes)
- Form irregular plural nouns correctly (e.g., child/children, mouse/mice, tooth/teeth, foot/feet)
- Distinguish between regular plurals (add -s or -es) and irregular plurals
- Use collective nouns and irregular plurals in sentences

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of regular plural nouns (add -s or -es: cat/cats, box/boxes)
- Ability to write complete sentences
- Understanding of what a noun is (a person, place, thing, or animal)

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Index cards (for matching game)
- Crayons or colored pencils (optional, for illustrating collective nouns)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review regular plural nouns and activate thinking about nouns.

Say: "For the next few lessons, we are going to learn special grammar rules that will make your writing better and more correct. Let's start with a quick review."

**Activity: Quick Plural Review**

Say: "You already know how to make most nouns plural — you add -s or -es. Let's practice."

Write each word and have the student write the plural:

1. dog → ___ (dogs)
2. cat → ___ (cats)
3. bus → ___ (buses)
4. wish → ___ (wishes)
5. baby → ___ (babies — change y to i, add -es)

Say: "Great! Those are called REGULAR plurals because they follow a regular pattern. Today we are going to learn about nouns that do NOT follow the rules — tricky plurals! And we will also learn about special group names for animals and things."

**Lesson 3 Review:** "What temporal word would you use to start the LAST event in a story?" (Finally, or last.)

---

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach collective nouns and irregular plural nouns.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Noun:** A word for a person, place, thing, or animal
- **Plural:** More than one of something
- **Regular plural:** A plural formed by adding -s or -es (dogs, boxes, wishes)
- **Irregular plural:** A plural that does NOT follow the -s or -es rule — the word changes in a special way
- **Collective noun:** A special name for a GROUP of people, animals, or things

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1: Collective Nouns (5 minutes)**

Say: "When you see a group of animals together, there is a special name for that group. Instead of saying 'a lot of birds,' you can say 'a FLOCK of birds.' The word 'flock' is a collective noun — it names the whole group."

Write this chart:

**Collective Nouns — Special Group Names**

| Collective Noun | What Group? | Example Sentence |
|----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| a **flock** of | birds | A flock of birds flew over the lake. |
| a **pack** of | wolves / dogs | A pack of wolves howled at the moon. |
| a **herd** of | cows / horses / elephants | A herd of cows grazed in the field. |
| a **school** of | fish | A school of fish swam past the boat. |
| a **bunch** of | grapes / bananas / flowers | She picked a bunch of flowers from the garden. |
| a **team** of | players / people | A team of players ran onto the field. |
| a **family** of | people / animals | A family of ducks waddled across the road. |
| a **pile** of | things (leaves, books, etc.) | There was a pile of leaves in the yard. |
| a **swarm** of | bees / insects | A swarm of bees buzzed near the hive. |

Say: "These words make your writing more interesting and precise. 'A flock of birds' sounds much better than 'a lot of birds,' right? Writers love using these special group names!"

Ask the student: "Can you think of any other group names you have heard?" (Accept any reasonable answers — a class of students, a litter of puppies, a crowd of people, etc.)

**Part 2: Irregular Plural Nouns (7 minutes)**

Say: "Now let's talk about tricky plurals. Most nouns follow the rule — just add -s or -es. But some nouns break the rule completely. The word changes instead of just adding a letter."

Write this chart:

**Irregular Plural Nouns**

| Singular (one) | Plural (more than one) | What Changes? |
|---------------|----------------------|--------------|
| child | **children** | The whole word changes |
| man | **men** | a changes to e |
| woman | **women** | a changes to e |
| mouse | **mice** | -ouse changes to -ice |
| tooth | **teeth** | -oo- changes to -ee- |
| foot | **feet** | -oo- changes to -ee- |
| goose | **geese** | -oo- changes to -ee- |
| person | **people** | The whole word changes |
| leaf | **leaves** | -f changes to -ves |
| wolf | **wolves** | -f changes to -ves |
| knife | **knives** | -fe changes to -ves |

Say: "You do NOT add -s to these words. You do NOT say 'childs' or 'mouses' or 'tooths.' These words have their own special plural forms."

**Special case — words that stay the same:**

| Singular | Plural | Note |
|----------|--------|------|
| fish | **fish** | Same word! (One fish, two fish) |
| sheep | **sheep** | Same word! |
| deer | **deer** | Same word! |

Say: "Some words are the same whether there is one or many! One sheep, ten sheep. One deer, five deer. English can be tricky!"

### Worked Examples

Say: "Let's practice using these in sentences."

Write the sentence with a blank. The student fills in the correct word.

1. "I lost two ___." (teeth — not "tooths")
2. "The ___ were playing on the playground." (children — not "childs")
3. "Three ___ ran across the kitchen floor." (mice — not "mouses")
4. "My ___ hurt after running so far." (feet — not "foots")
5. "A ___ of wolves ran through the forest." (pack — collective noun)

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice collective nouns and irregular plurals with support.

**Activity 1: Collective Noun Sentences (5 minutes)**

Give the student a collective noun. They write a sentence using it correctly.

1. a herd of ___
2. a school of ___
3. a swarm of ___
4. a team of ___

*Facilitator prompt: If the student writes "a herd of dogs," gently correct: "A herd is usually for large animals like cows, horses, or elephants. Dogs travel in a PACK. Let's fix that." Help the student match the right collective noun with the right animal or thing.*

**Activity 2: Singular to Plural Matching (7 minutes)**

Write these singular nouns on one set of index cards and the plurals on another set. Mix them up. The student matches each singular to its plural.

Singular cards: child, mouse, tooth, foot, goose, man, woman, leaf, wolf, sheep

Plural cards: children, mice, teeth, feet, geese, men, women, leaves, wolves, sheep

After matching, have the student pick three pairs and use the PLURAL form in a sentence.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student tries to add -s (e.g., "mouses"), say: "That is what the regular rule would tell us to do! But 'mouse' is an irregular noun — it breaks the rule. The plural of mouse is 'mice.' Some words just have to be memorized." Be patient — these forms take repeated exposure.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices collective and irregular plural nouns independently.

**Part A: Choose the Correct Plural (4 minutes)**

Circle the correct plural form:

1. One goose, two (gooses / **geese**)
2. One woman, three (**women** / womans)
3. One knife, four (**knives** / knifes)
4. One fish, six (**fish** / fishes)
5. One child, many (**children** / childs)

**Part B: Fill in the Blank (3 minutes)**

Use the correct collective noun from the word bank: flock, pack, bunch, herd, school

6. A ___ of elephants walked to the watering hole.
7. I bought a ___ of bananas at the store.
8. A ___ of birds flew south for the winter.
9. A ___ of wolves hunted together.
10. A ___ of fish darted through the coral reef.

**Part C: Write Your Own (3 minutes)**

### Difficulty Tiers
- ★ Write two sentences using irregular plural nouns (choose from the chart)
- ★★ Write two sentences with irregular plurals AND two sentences with collective nouns
- ★★★ Write a short paragraph (3-4 sentences) that includes at least two irregular plurals AND one collective noun, all used in a mini-story

### Interleaved Review

11. **Lesson 4 Review:** Rewrite this sentence by adding a sensory detail: "The dog barked." (Example: "The big brown dog barked so loudly it made me jump.")
12. **Unit 2 Review:** What is the main topic of a text? (The most important thing the text is about.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick assessment of irregular plural and collective noun knowledge.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Write the plural of: child, tooth, mouse. (children, teeth, mice)
2. Use one collective noun in a sentence. (Example: "A flock of birds flew over the park.")

**Mastery = Student correctly forms at least 2 out of 3 irregular plurals AND writes a correct sentence with a collective noun.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Create flashcards with the irregular plurals and practice daily for a few minutes
- Focus on the most common ones first: children, teeth, feet, mice
- Use the irregular plurals in conversation throughout the day: "Look at those geese!" "My feet are cold."

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (Reflexive Pronouns). Preview: "You learned some tricky nouns today! Next time, we are going to learn special pronouns — words like 'myself' and 'himself.'"

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**
- **Collective Noun Illustrations:** Choose three collective nouns and draw a picture of each one (a flock of birds, a school of fish, a pack of wolves). Label each picture.
- **Irregular Plural Memory Game:** Use the matching cards from Guided Practice to play a memory/concentration game. Lay cards face down, flip two at a time, and try to match singular to plural.
- **Silly Story:** Write a short story that uses at least three irregular plural nouns and two collective nouns. Make it funny!

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This is a grammar lesson and tends to move faster than the writing lessons. If the student grasps both concepts in one session, move on. If the student finds irregular plurals particularly confusing, spend a second session with more practice.

**Why collective nouns matter for writing:** Collective nouns enrich vocabulary and make writing more precise. When the student writes their complete narrative in Lesson 10, encourage them to use collective nouns if the opportunity arises (e.g., "a pile of treasure" or "a flock of birds").

**Common errors with irregular plurals:** Students may overgeneralize the regular plural rule and say "mouses," "childs," "tooths." This is a normal developmental stage. Do not treat it as a serious error — simply model the correct form: "Yes! You mean mice. More than one mouse is mice." Repeated exposure is the best remedy.

**Dialect note:** In some dialects, forms like "fishes," "mices," or "peoples" may be used. Respect the student's home language while teaching the standard written forms: "At home, people say it lots of different ways, and that is fine. In our writing, we use these special forms." Never frame the student's home language as wrong.

**Connection to writing:** Remind the student that these grammar skills are TOOLS for their writing, not isolated drills. When they write their complete narrative in Lesson 10, they will have a chance to use collective nouns and irregular plurals naturally.
