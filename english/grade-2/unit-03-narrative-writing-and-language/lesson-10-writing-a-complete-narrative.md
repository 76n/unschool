# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 3, Lesson 10

## Topic
Writing a Complete Narrative — Putting It All Together

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Plan a complete narrative using a story map (characters, setting, events, ending)
- Write a narrative with a strong beginning, sequenced events using temporal words, descriptive details, and a satisfying ending
- Apply grammar and language skills from Lessons 6-9 (collective nouns, irregular plurals, reflexive pronouns, possessives, spelling strategies) in their writing
- Revise and edit their narrative for clarity, detail, and correctness

## Prerequisites
- All skills from Lessons 1-9 of this unit
- Ability to write multiple connected sentences with correct punctuation
- Familiarity with the personal word wall and Try-It, Check-It, Fix-It strategy (Lesson 9)

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and lined paper (several sheets)
- Eraser
- Crayons or colored pencils (for illustrating the final story)
- The student's Temporal Words list (Lesson 3)
- The student's personal word wall (Lesson 9)
- The Reflexive Pronoun chart (Lesson 7)
- A story planning graphic organizer (provided in this lesson)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review all narrative writing skills and build excitement for writing a complete story.

Say: "Today is a big day! You are going to write your very own complete story from beginning to end. You have learned EVERY skill you need. Let's do a quick review to warm up."

**Rapid Review — One Question Per Lesson:**

1. **Lesson 1:** "What are the four ingredients of a good story?" (Characters, setting, events, ending)
2. **Lesson 2:** "Name one hook strategy for a beginning." (Action, dialogue, or feeling)
3. **Lesson 3:** "Name three temporal words." (Any three: first, next, then, after that, later, finally, last)
4. **Lesson 4:** "What does 'show, don't tell' mean?" (Describe what a character does instead of just naming the feeling)
5. **Lesson 5:** "What are three ways to write a satisfying ending?" (Resolve the problem, share feelings, reveal a lesson learned)

Say: "You know all of this! You are ready. Let's write a story!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model the planning process and demonstrate how all skills come together.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Draft:** The first version of a piece of writing — it does not have to be perfect
- **Revise:** To go back and make your writing better by adding details, fixing the order, or making things clearer
- **Edit:** To fix mistakes in spelling, punctuation, and grammar

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Before a writer starts writing, they PLAN. A builder does not start building a house without a blueprint. A writer does not start writing without a plan."

**Step 1: Choose a Topic**

Say: "First, decide what your story will be about. Here are some ideas, or you can come up with your own."

Write the choices on paper:

**Story Ideas:**
- A. A child discovers a secret door in their school.
- B. Two friends have an adventure at the beach.
- C. A pet gets loose and the family has to find it.
- D. A child tries something new and scary for the first time.
- E. Your own idea!

Say: "Pick the idea that makes you the most excited to write. If you are excited about it, your reader will be excited to read it!"

**Step 2: Plan with a Story Map**

Draw this graphic organizer on paper:

```
MY STORY PLAN

Title: _______________________________________

CHARACTERS (Who is in my story?)
Name: _________________ What are they like? _________________
Name: _________________ What are they like? _________________

SETTING (Where and when?)
Where: _______________________
When: ________________________

EVENTS (What happens?)
Beginning (Hook + introduce characters/setting):
_______________________________________________

Event 1: ______________________________________
Event 2: ______________________________________
Event 3: ______________________________________

ENDING (How does it wrap up?)
Problem resolved: _____________________________
Character feels: _______________________________
Lesson learned: _______________________________
```

Say: "Let me show you how I would plan a story."

**Model Planning (use a think-aloud):**

Say: "I am going to plan a story about a boy who tries to bake a cake by himself for his mom's birthday."

Fill in the organizer out loud:

- **Title:** "The Birthday Surprise"
- **Characters:** Eli (a 7-year-old boy, kind, wants to do something special); Mom (loves Eli, does not know about the surprise)
- **Setting:** Eli's kitchen, on a Saturday morning
- **Beginning:** Eli wakes up early, remembers it is Mom's birthday, decides to bake a cake all by himself
- **Event 1:** He finds a recipe but it is confusing — he mixes up tablespoons and cups
- **Event 2:** The batter spills, the kitchen gets messy, he almost gives up
- **Event 3:** He keeps trying, puts the cake in the oven, and it actually comes out okay (a little lopsided)
- **Ending:** Mom wakes up to a messy kitchen but a beautiful lopsided cake. She hugs Eli and says it is the best birthday present. Eli learns that doing something from the heart matters more than doing it perfectly.

Say: "See how the plan makes it easy to write? I know exactly what happens in order. Now it is your turn!"

### Worked Examples

Say: "Let me write the first paragraph of my story so you can see how the plan turns into writing."

Write on paper (the student watches):

> Eli's eyes popped open before the sun was even up. He sat up in bed and grinned. Today was Mom's birthday, and he had a plan. He was going to bake her a cake all by himself.
>
> He tiptoed down to the kitchen in his pajamas and bare feet. The floor was cold. He pulled Mom's big cookbook off the shelf and found a recipe for chocolate cake.

Point out:
- "I used a HOOK — starting with action (eyes popped open) and a feeling (grinned)."
- "I introduced the CHARACTER (Eli) and the SETTING (kitchen, early morning)."
- "I added SENSORY DETAILS (cold floor, bare feet, big cookbook)."
- "I used a POSSESSIVE (Mom's birthday, Mom's cookbook)."

Say: "Your turn. Use your plan and everything you have learned to write your story!"

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Support the student through planning and beginning their draft.

**Activity 1: Plan Your Story (5 minutes)**

Give the student the story planning organizer. Help them fill it in for their chosen story idea.

*Facilitator prompts:*
- "Who are the characters? Tell me about them. What are they like?"
- "Where does this take place? Can you picture it?"
- "What is the problem or interesting thing that happens?"
- "What events happen in order? What is first? Then what?"
- "How does it end? Does the character solve the problem? How do they feel? Do they learn something?"

Help the student fill in the organizer with short notes (not full sentences yet). The plan should take about 5 minutes.

**Activity 2: Write the Beginning (7 minutes)**

Say: "Now let's start writing! Begin with your hook and introduce your characters and setting."

Have the student write the first 3-5 sentences of their story (the beginning).

Read the beginning together and check:
- "Did you introduce the characters?"
- "Did you tell us the setting?"
- "Did you use a hook — action, dialogue, or a feeling?"
- "Can I picture this in my mind? Are there any details you could add?"

Celebrate what is working. Suggest one small improvement if appropriate.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student is stuck staring at the blank page, say: "Tell me out loud what happens first." After they say it, say: "Great! Write that down." Oral rehearsal breaks writer's block.*

*If the student writes "This is a story about..." redirect gently: "That tells us ABOUT the story. Can you start us IN the story instead? What is your character doing right at the beginning?"*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student continues writing their narrative independently.

Say: "Now keep writing! Use your story plan to guide you through the events and the ending. Remember to use:"

Write a checklist on paper for the student to reference:

**My Writing Checklist:**
- [ ] Strong beginning with a hook
- [ ] Characters and setting introduced
- [ ] Events in order with temporal words (first, next, then, finally)
- [ ] Sensory details (what can you see, hear, feel?)
- [ ] Show, don't tell (at least once!)
- [ ] Satisfying ending (resolve the problem, feelings, lesson learned)
- [ ] Correct spelling (use your word wall!)
- [ ] Possessives with apostrophes (if used)
- [ ] Capital letters at the start of sentences
- [ ] End punctuation (. ! ?)

The student writes independently while the facilitator observes and offers gentle support only when asked.

### Difficulty Tiers
- ★ Write a complete narrative with at least 8 sentences that includes a beginning, 2-3 events, and an ending
- ★★ Write a complete narrative with at least 10 sentences that includes a hook, temporal words, at least two sensory details, and a satisfying ending using two ending strategies
- ★★★ Write a complete narrative with at least 12 sentences that includes all checklist items, AND go back and revise by adding one more detail and checking spelling with your word wall

### Interleaved Review
After completing the draft (or as a separate activity if time runs short):

**Grammar Check:** Read through your story and check for:
- Any irregular plurals — are they spelled correctly? (children, not childs; teeth, not tooths)
- Any reflexive pronouns — are they correct? (himself, not hisself; themselves, not theirselves)
- Any possessives — is the apostrophe in the right place?

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess the student's complete narrative against the unit objectives.

**Exit Ticket:**

The student reads their story aloud to the facilitator. Together, go through the checklist:

| Skill | Present? |
|-------|----------|
| Characters introduced | Yes / Not yet |
| Setting described | Yes / Not yet |
| Hook used in the beginning | Yes / Not yet |
| Events sequenced with temporal words | Yes / Not yet |
| Sensory details included | Yes / Not yet |
| Satisfying ending | Yes / Not yet |
| Correct grammar (plurals, pronouns, possessives) | Yes / Not yet |
| Spelling checked | Yes / Not yet |

**Mastery = The narrative includes at least 6 out of 8 checklist items and tells a complete story with a beginning, events, and an ending.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify the specific checklist items that are missing
- Work together to add the missing elements (this may require an additional session)
- If the student wrote very little, do not criticize the length. Ask: "What happens next in your story?" and help them add 2-3 more sentences.
- Before the unit assessment, do one more revision session focusing on the weakest areas.

### If Mastery Is Met
Say: "You wrote a complete narrative! You are an AUTHOR! I am so proud of how you used everything you learned in this unit." Prepare for the unit assessment. The student's narrative from this lesson can serve as a writing sample for the assessment.

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who finish their narrative quickly and with high quality:**
- **Illustrate Your Story:** Draw pictures to go with your story — one for the beginning, one for the middle, and one for the ending. Create a mini picture book.
- **Author's Chair:** Read your story aloud to a family member, friend, or pet. Ask them: "What was your favorite part? What do you want to know more about?" Use their feedback to add a detail or two.
- **Revision Pass:** Go through your story three times:
  - Pass 1: Circle any place you could add a sensory detail. Add one.
  - Pass 2: Underline any "telling" sentences. Can you change one to "showing"?
  - Pass 3: Check all spelling against your word wall. Fix any mistakes.
- **Write a Second Story:** Choose a different story idea from the list and plan a new narrative using your story map. You know the process now — see how quickly you can plan and draft a second story!

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson is designed for TWO sessions. Session 1: Warm-Up, Direct Instruction, Guided Practice (planning and starting the draft). Session 2: Independent Practice (finishing the draft), revision, and Check for Understanding. Do not rush the student through a complete narrative in one sitting — writing stamina at this age is limited.

**The planning step is essential:** Students who skip planning often write themselves into a corner ("I don't know what happens next!"). Insist on completing the story map before writing begins. The five minutes spent planning saves twenty minutes of frustration during writing.

**Revision is a separate step:** After the student finishes their first draft, take a break (even if just five minutes). Then come back and read it together for revision. Fresh eyes catch things that tired eyes miss. Teach the student that EVERY writer revises — it is not a punishment, it is what makes writing great.

**Celebrating the writing:** This is the culminating lesson of the narrative craft sequence. Treat the student's finished story as a real accomplishment. Read it aloud with enthusiasm. Display it. Share it with a family member. Building a writer's identity is as important as building writing skills.

**If the student's story is short:** Some students will write 5-6 sentences and declare "done!" This is normal for second grade. Rather than saying "write more," use specific prompts: "I love this part. Can you tell me one more thing about what the character saw?" "What did the character say to their friend?" Specific prompts produce more writing than general demands for length.

**Handwriting fatigue:** If the student's hand gets tired, offer a short break. Some students may benefit from alternating between writing and drawing (draw a picture of the next scene, then write about it). The goal is the NARRATIVE, not handwriting endurance.

**Grammar integration:** Do not overemphasize grammar during the drafting phase. Let the student focus on storytelling first. Grammar corrections happen during editing. If you notice errors in possessives, irregular plurals, or reflexive pronouns, note them but wait until the editing phase to address them.

## Answer Key

**Warm-Up (Rapid Review):**
1. Characters, setting, events, ending
2. Action, dialogue, or feeling
3. Any three: first, next, then, after that, later, finally, last
4. Describe what a character does instead of just saying the feeling
5. Resolve the problem, share the character's feelings, reveal a lesson learned

**Model Story Plan ("The Birthday Surprise"):**
See the worked example in Direct Instruction. The model demonstrates how to fill in the story planning organizer.

**Model Story Beginning:**
See the two paragraphs in Direct Instruction. The model demonstrates a hook (action — eyes popped open), character introduction (Eli), setting (kitchen, early morning), sensory details (cold floor, bare feet), and possessives (Mom's birthday).

**Student's Narrative:**
Answers will vary. Assess using the checklist in Check for Understanding. A strong narrative will include characters, setting, a hook, sequenced events with temporal words, sensory details, a satisfying ending, and correct grammar/spelling. A complete model narrative at the second-grade level:

> Mia's eyes grew wide when she heard a strange noise in the backyard. She grabbed a flashlight and tiptoed outside. The grass was wet and cold against her bare feet.
>
> First, she walked to the big oak tree. She could hear scratching sounds coming from behind it. "Hello?" she whispered.
>
> Then she peeked around the tree and gasped. A tiny orange kitten was sitting in a pile of leaves, shaking and mewing. Its fur was matted and dirty.
>
> "You poor thing," Mia said softly. She picked the kitten up carefully. It was so light and small that it fit in her two hands. The kitten's little claws poked her palm, but she did not mind.
>
> After that, Mia carried the kitten inside and wrapped it in a warm towel. She gave it a bowl of milk. The kitten lapped it up and then curled into a tiny ball on Mia's lap.
>
> "Can we keep it?" Mia asked her mom.
>
> Her mom looked at the kitten and smiled. "I think it found the right family," she said.
>
> Finally, Mia named the kitten Maple because of the pile of leaves where she found it. Mia learned that sometimes the best things in life come when you least expect them. She told herself she would take care of Maple forever.
