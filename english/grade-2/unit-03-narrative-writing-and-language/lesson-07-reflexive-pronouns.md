# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 3, Lesson 7

## Topic
Reflexive Pronouns — Myself, Himself, Herself, Themselves

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define what a reflexive pronoun is and explain when to use one
- Correctly use the reflexive pronouns: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves
- Choose the correct reflexive pronoun to match the subject of a sentence
- Use reflexive pronouns in their own writing

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of common pronouns (I, you, he, she, it, we, they)
- Ability to write complete sentences
- Understanding of what a noun is (Unit 3, Lesson 6)

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Index cards (for matching activity)
- A small mirror (optional, for the warm-up demonstration)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review pronouns and activate thinking about reflexive forms.

Say: "Last time we learned about collective nouns and irregular plurals. Today we are learning about a special kind of pronoun. But first, let's make sure we remember what regular pronouns are."

**Quick Review: Match the Pronoun**

Write each noun. The student tells you the pronoun that replaces it.

1. Maria → ___ (she)
2. the boys → ___ (they)
3. Dad → ___ (he)
4. the cat → ___ (it)
5. Maria and I → ___ (we)

Say: "Great! Pronouns take the place of nouns so we do not have to say the same name over and over."

**Lesson 6 Review:** "What is the plural of 'tooth'?" (teeth) "And what is a special group name for a bunch of fish swimming together?" (a school of fish)

Say: "Now, look at this sentence: 'I made breakfast all by MYSELF.' See that word 'myself'? That is a special pronoun. Today we are going to learn all about words like 'myself.'"

---

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce reflexive pronouns and teach when to use them.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Pronoun:** A word that takes the place of a noun (I, you, he, she, it, we, they)
- **Reflexive pronoun:** A special pronoun that refers BACK to the subject of the sentence — it ends in -self (one person) or -selves (more than one person)
- **Subject:** The person or thing doing the action in a sentence

### Instruction Notes

Say: "A reflexive pronoun is a pronoun that bounces BACK to the subject — like looking at yourself in a mirror. When I say 'I looked at MYSELF in the mirror,' the word 'myself' bounces back to 'I.' I am the one looking, and I am the one being looked at."

*Optional: If you have a small mirror, hold it up. Say: "When you look in a mirror, you see YOURSELF. The reflection bounces back to YOU. That is what reflexive pronouns do — they bounce back to the person doing the action."*

Write the Reflexive Pronoun Chart:

**Reflexive Pronouns**

| Subject Pronoun | Reflexive Pronoun | Example Sentence |
|----------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| I | **myself** | I made the bed by **myself**. |
| you (one person) | **yourself** | You should be proud of **yourself**. |
| he | **himself** | He taught **himself** to ride a bike. |
| she | **herself** | She poured **herself** a glass of water. |
| it | **itself** | The cat cleaned **itself**. |
| we | **ourselves** | We built the fort by **ourselves**. |
| you (more than one) | **yourselves** | You two should help **yourselves** to some cake. |
| they | **themselves** | They finished the project by **themselves**. |

Say: "Notice the pattern? For one person, the word ends in -SELF. For more than one person, it ends in -SELVES."

Write on paper:
- One person → -**self** (myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself)
- More than one → -**selves** (ourselves, yourselves, themselves)

**When do we use reflexive pronouns?**

Say: "We use a reflexive pronoun when the person doing the action and the person receiving the action are the SAME person."

Write two examples:

1. "Maya helped **herself** to some fruit." (Maya is the one helping. Maya is the one getting the fruit. Same person!)
2. "The dog shook **itself** dry." (The dog is the one shaking. The dog is the one getting dry. Same dog!)

**Common mistakes to avoid:**

Say: "There are some mistakes people sometimes make with reflexive pronouns. Let me show you."

**Wrong:** "Hisself" → **Correct:** "Himself"
**Wrong:** "Theirselves" → **Correct:** "Themselves"

Say: "I know 'hisself' might sound right because we say 'himself' — but the correct word is 'himself.' And the correct word is 'themselves,' not 'theirselves.' These are just tricky words we have to memorize."

### Worked Examples

Say: "Let's practice choosing the right reflexive pronoun."

Write each sentence. The student fills in the blank.

1. "I tied my shoes all by ___." (**myself** — the subject is "I")
2. "The cat licked ___ clean after dinner." (**itself** — the subject is "the cat")
3. "Ben and Sam made lunch by ___." (**themselves** — the subject is "Ben and Sam" = they)
4. "Sarah looked at ___ in the mirror and smiled." (**herself** — the subject is "Sarah" = she)

For each one, ask: "Who is doing the action? That tells us which reflexive pronoun to use."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice choosing and using reflexive pronouns with support.

**Activity 1: Match the Subject to the Reflexive Pronoun (4 minutes)**

Write these on paper. The student draws a line from the subject to the correct reflexive pronoun.

| Subject | | Reflexive Pronoun |
|---|---|---|
| I | | themselves |
| the birds | | herself |
| Grandma | | myself |
| we | | itself |
| the baby kitten | | ourselves |

Answers: I → myself, the birds → themselves, Grandma → herself, we → ourselves, the baby kitten → itself

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles, ask: "What pronoun would replace 'Grandma'?" (she) "So the reflexive pronoun is..." (herself). Help them use the chart as a reference.*

**Activity 2: Fix the Sentence (6 minutes)**

Say: "These sentences have the WRONG reflexive pronoun. Can you fix them?"

1. "Jake made hisself a sandwich." → "Jake made **himself** a sandwich."
2. "The girls did it all by theirselves." → "The girls did it all by **themselves**."
3. "I told yourself to be brave." → "I told **myself** to be brave."
4. "She and I built the fort by ourself." → "She and I built the fort by **ourselves**."
5. "The puppy tired hisself out playing." → "The puppy tired **itself** out playing." (or "himself" if the puppy is referred to as "he")

After fixing each one, have the student read the correct sentence aloud.

*Facilitator prompt: For #3, if the student is confused, explain: "Who is doing the telling? 'I' am. So I am telling — who? — MYSELF. The reflexive pronoun needs to match the person doing the action."*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices reflexive pronouns independently.

**Part A: Choose the Correct Reflexive Pronoun (4 minutes)**

Fill in the blank with the correct reflexive pronoun.

1. We painted the fence by ___. (ourselves)
2. The squirrel buried the nut all by ___. (itself)
3. You should give ___ a pat on the back! (yourself)
4. He accidentally tripped ___. (himself)
5. They cleaned up the mess by ___. (themselves)
6. I reminded ___ to bring my lunch. (myself)

**Part B: Write Sentences (4 minutes)**

### Difficulty Tiers
- ★ Write two sentences, each using a different reflexive pronoun from the chart
- ★★ Write three sentences using three different reflexive pronouns, AND underline the reflexive pronoun in each one
- ★★★ Write a short paragraph (3-4 sentences) about something you did by yourself. Use at least two different reflexive pronouns and include a sensory detail (Lesson 4 skill)

**Part C: Interleaved Review (2 minutes)**

7. **Lesson 6 Review:** Write the plural of these irregular nouns: child, mouse, foot. (children, mice, feet)
8. **Lesson 2 Review:** Name one hook strategy for starting a story. (Action, dialogue, or feeling/thought)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Fill in the blank: "Mia dressed ___ for school this morning." (herself)
2. Fill in the blank: "We grew the tomatoes all by ___." (ourselves)
3. Is this correct or incorrect? "He made hisself a fort." (Incorrect — it should be "himself")

**Mastery = Student answers at least 2 out of 3 correctly.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Post the reflexive pronoun chart in the student's writing area
- Practice daily with one sentence per day: "Can you tell me a sentence using 'myself'? How about 'herself'?"
- Focus on the most common four: myself, himself, herself, themselves
- Use reflexive pronouns in everyday conversation: "Did you brush your teeth by yourself? You did it all by yourself!"

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 8 (Possessives and Apostrophes). Preview: "Great work with reflexive pronouns! Next time, we are going to learn about a tiny but powerful punctuation mark — the apostrophe — and how it shows that something BELONGS to someone."

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**
- **Reflexive Pronoun Brag Page:** Write 4-5 sentences about things you can do all by yourself, using a different reflexive pronoun in each one. Example: "I can tie my shoes by myself. My sister can read chapter books by herself."
- **Story Connection:** Look at the story beginning you wrote in Lesson 2. Can you add a sentence with a reflexive pronoun? For example: "She told herself she could do it."
- **Reflexive Pronoun Comic Strip:** Draw a three-panel comic strip where a character does something by themselves. Write a sentence under each panel using a reflexive pronoun.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This is a straightforward grammar lesson. Most students can complete it in one session. If the student consistently confuses "himself" with "hisself" or "themselves" with "theirselves," plan brief daily practice for the next few days.

**"Hisself" and "theirselves":** These forms are used in many English dialects and are not "wrong" in spoken language — they are logical constructions that follow a pattern (his + self, their + selves). However, standard written English uses "himself" and "themselves." Approach this with respect: "In writing, the special forms are 'himself' and 'themselves.' At home, people say it lots of different ways, and that is okay. For our writing, we will use these forms."

**When NOT to use reflexive pronouns:** Some students may start overusing reflexive pronouns after this lesson (e.g., "My mom and myself went to the store" instead of "My mom and I went to the store"). If you see this, gently correct: "We only use 'myself' when the action bounces back — when you are doing something to yourself. Here, you are just going to the store, so 'I' is the right word."

**Connection to writing:** Reflexive pronouns show up naturally in narratives: "She told herself she could be brave." "He picked himself up off the ground." When the student writes their complete narrative in Lesson 10, watch for opportunities to use reflexive pronouns naturally.

**Growth mindset note:** If the student is frustrated by the non-standard forms, say: "English has so many tricky rules! The fact that you are learning them means you are getting better at writing every day. Even grown-ups get confused by these sometimes."
