# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 3, Lesson 8

## Topic
Possessives and Apostrophes

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain that a possessive noun shows ownership — that something belongs to someone or something
- Form singular possessive nouns by adding an apostrophe and -s (the dog's bone, Sara's book)
- Distinguish between plural nouns (dogs) and possessive nouns (dog's)
- Use possessive nouns correctly in sentences

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of nouns (persons, places, things, animals)
- Understanding of plural nouns, including irregular plurals (Lesson 6)
- Ability to write complete sentences with correct punctuation

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Crayons or colored pencils (for the labeling activity)
- Small classroom or household objects (optional, for the hands-on possessive demonstration)
- Index cards (optional, for the matching game)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review nouns and activate thinking about ownership.

**Activity: Whose Is It?**

Gather a few small objects (a pencil, a book, a cup) or point to items in the room. Hold up each object and ask:

1. "Whose pencil is this?" (It's mine. / It's [student's name]'s pencil.)
2. "Whose book is that?" (It's Mom's book. / It's the teacher's book.)

Say: "When we talk about who something BELONGS to, we are talking about possession — owning something. In writing, we use a special punctuation mark to show that something belongs to someone. It is called an APOSTROPHE."

**Review:**

3. **Lesson 7 Review:** "Fill in the blank: He made lunch all by ___." (himself)
4. **Lesson 6 Review:** "What is the plural of 'child'?" (children)

Say: "Today we are going to learn how to use the apostrophe to show that something belongs to someone."

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach how to form singular possessive nouns using an apostrophe and -s.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Possessive:** Showing that something belongs to someone or something
- **Apostrophe:** A punctuation mark that looks like a small floating comma ( **'** ) — used in possessives to show ownership
- **Singular possessive:** Showing ownership by ONE person, animal, or thing

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1: What is a Possessive? (4 minutes)**

Say: "When something belongs to someone, we can show it in two ways."

Write on paper:

**Long way:** "The bone that belongs to the dog"
**Short way:** "The **dog's** bone"

Say: "See that little mark between the 'g' and the 's' in 'dog's'? That is an apostrophe. It is telling us that the bone BELONGS to the dog."

Write more examples:

| Long Way (Belongs to) | Short Way (Possessive) |
|---|---|
| The book that belongs to Sara | **Sara's** book |
| The tail of the cat | The **cat's** tail |
| The bike that belongs to Dad | **Dad's** bike |
| The roof of the house | The **house's** roof |
| The hat that belongs to the snowman | The **snowman's** hat |

Say: "To make a singular noun possessive, we add an APOSTROPHE and then the letter S. That's it! Apostrophe, then S."

Write the rule:

**Rule: To show that something belongs to ONE person or thing, add 's**
- dog → dog's (the dog's bone)
- Sara → Sara's (Sara's book)
- Mom → Mom's (Mom's car)

**Part 2: Possessive vs. Plural — The Tricky Part (4 minutes)**

Say: "Here is where it gets tricky. The words 'dogs' and 'dog's' SOUND exactly the same when you say them out loud. But they mean very different things!"

Write on paper:

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **dogs** (no apostrophe) | More than one dog (plural) | Three **dogs** played in the park. |
| **dog's** (with apostrophe) | Belonging to one dog (possessive) | The **dog's** collar was red. |

Say: "If you are not sure whether to use an apostrophe, ask yourself: Does this mean 'more than one' or 'belonging to'?"

**The Ownership Test:**

Write: "Can I replace this with 'belonging to ___'?"

- "The dogs ran fast." → Can I say "The belonging to dog ran fast"? NO — this means more than one dog. No apostrophe.
- "The dog's toy is under the bed." → Can I say "The toy belonging to the dog is under the bed"? YES — this means the toy belongs to the dog. Use an apostrophe!

Practice together:

1. "My friends house" → Does the house belong to my friend? YES → "My **friend's** house"
2. "The cats are sleeping" → Does something belong to the cats? NO, this means more than one cat → "The **cats** are sleeping" (no apostrophe)

**Part 3: Names Ending in S (4 minutes)**

Say: "What if someone's name already ends in S? Like 'James' or 'Marcus'?"

Write the rule:

**If a name ends in S, you can add just an apostrophe OR an apostrophe + s. Both are correct!**

- James' backpack OR James's backpack
- Marcus' lunch OR Marcus's lunch

Say: "For now, just add apostrophe + s to every name, and you will be correct: 'James's backpack.' When you are older, you can choose which style you prefer."

### Worked Examples

Say: "Let's practice making words possessive."

Write each phrase. The student rewrites it using a possessive noun.

1. "The hat that belongs to the clown" → "The **clown's** hat"
2. "The garden that belongs to Grandma" → "**Grandma's** garden"
3. "The wings of the butterfly" → "The **butterfly's** wings"
4. "The classroom of the teacher" → "The **teacher's** classroom"

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice forming and using possessives with support.

**Activity 1: Add the Apostrophe (5 minutes)**

Say: "These sentences are missing apostrophes. Read each sentence and add the apostrophe in the right place."

Write each sentence. The student marks where the apostrophe goes.

1. "I borrowed Mias markers." → "I borrowed **Mia's** markers."
2. "The birds nest had three eggs in it." → "The **bird's** nest had three eggs in it."
3. "We played at Nanas house." → "We played at **Nana's** house."
4. "The rabbits ears were soft and floppy." → "The **rabbit's** ears were soft and floppy."
5. "Leos backpack was very heavy." → "**Leo's** backpack was very heavy."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student puts the apostrophe in the wrong place (e.g., "Mias'"), say: "The apostrophe comes RIGHT BEFORE the s. Think of it as: Mia, then apostrophe, then s. Mia's."*

**Activity 2: Plural or Possessive? (7 minutes)**

Say: "For each sentence, tell me if the underlined word is PLURAL (more than one) or POSSESSIVE (belonging to)."

1. "The **dogs** were barking in the yard." (Plural — more than one dog)
2. "The **dog's** water bowl was empty." (Possessive — the bowl belongs to the dog)
3. "Three **birds** sat on the branch." (Plural — more than one bird)
4. "The **bird's** song was beautiful." (Possessive — the song belongs to the bird)
5. "My **sister's** room is messy." (Possessive — the room belongs to my sister)
6. "Both **sisters** helped clean up." (Plural — more than one sister)

After each one, ask: "How do you know? Can you use the ownership test?"

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles, ask for each one: "Does this mean 'more than one' or 'belonging to someone'?" This simple question helps them sort plural from possessive.*

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Rewrite using a possessive: "The bike that belongs to Tom" → ___ (Tom's bike)
2. Is this plural or possessive? "The cat's paw was muddy." ___ (Possessive)
3. Write a sentence using a possessive noun. (Answers will vary.)

**Mastery = Student correctly forms a possessive in #1, correctly identifies possessive in #2, and writes a sentence with a possessive in #3.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice the ownership test daily: "Does this mean 'more than one' or 'belonging to'?"
- Focus on the physical rule: noun + apostrophe + s = belongs to that noun
- Use real objects: "This is Mom's cup. M-O-M, apostrophe, S. It belongs to Mom."
- Revisit before Lesson 10 to ensure the student can use possessives in their narrative writing

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 9 (Using Reference Materials for Spelling). Preview: "You are building your writing toolbox! Next time, we are going to learn what to do when you are not sure how to spell a word — real tools that writers use."

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**
- **Possessive Picture Labels:** Draw a picture of a scene (a park, a classroom, a pet shop). Label at least five things in the picture using possessive nouns (the girl's hat, the dog's leash, the tree's shadow, the teacher's book, the bird's nest).
- **Apostrophe Detective:** Look through a book or magazine. Find three examples of possessive nouns with apostrophes. Write them down and explain what belongs to whom.
- **Possessive vs. Plural Challenge:** Write six sentences — three with plural nouns and three with possessive nouns. Read them to the facilitator and see if they can tell which is which.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson covers singular possessives only. Plural possessives (e.g., "the dogs' bones" — multiple dogs) are not taught in Grade 2 and should not be introduced here. If the student asks about them, say: "Great question! You will learn about that in a later grade. For now, we are focusing on showing that ONE thing owns something."

**The apostrophe placement confusion:** The most common error is putting the apostrophe after the s instead of before it (e.g., writing "dogs'" when they mean "dog's"). Reinforce: "First write the owner's name. Then add the apostrophe. Then add s. Dog → dog' → dog's." A physical, step-by-step approach helps.

**Contractions vs. possessives:** Students may have seen apostrophes in contractions (can't, don't, it's). If they ask, briefly explain: "The apostrophe has two jobs in English. In contractions, it shows that letters are missing (can't = cannot). In possessives, it shows ownership. You will learn more about contractions later. For now, focus on the ownership job." Do NOT introduce "its" vs. "it's" in this lesson — that is a complex exception that will confuse second graders.

**Common error — possessive vs. plural in writing:** After this lesson, watch the student's writing for incorrect apostrophe use (e.g., "The dog's ran fast" or "I have two cat's"). If you see this, gently ask: "Does the dog own something here, or is this more than one dog?" Correcting in context is more effective than isolated drills.

**Connection to narrative writing:** Possessives are essential in stories: "Maya's eyes grew wide." "The dragon's cave was dark." When the student writes their narrative in Lesson 10, encourage natural use of possessives and watch for correct apostrophe placement.
