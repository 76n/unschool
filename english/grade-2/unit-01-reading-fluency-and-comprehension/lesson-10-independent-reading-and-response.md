# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 1, Lesson 10

## Topic
Independent Reading and Response

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read a grade-level passage independently with accuracy and expression
- Apply comprehension strategies learned in this unit: questioning, retelling, character/setting analysis, cause-and-effect connections
- Write a response to a reading passage (2-3 sentences answering a question about the text)
- Self-select a passage and demonstrate reading independence

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-9 (all prior decoding, fluency, and comprehension skills)
- This lesson is a synthesis and application lesson — the student is applying everything they have learned

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper (lined paper recommended for writing)
- All three reading passages provided in this lesson (print or display so the student can choose)
- Fix-Up Strategies card from Lesson 3 (available as a reference but not required)
- A quiet, comfortable reading spot

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review all comprehension strategies and prepare for independent reading.

**Activity: Strategy Review**

Say: "Today is a special day! You are going to read a story all by yourself and show me everything you have learned. Before we start, let's remember all the strategies you know."

Write the strategies as the student names them (prompt if needed):

1. **Decoding:** Sound it out, look for chunks, use what you know about vowel patterns and word parts (Lessons 1-2)
2. **Self-correction:** Does it make sense? Does it sound right? Does it look right? (Lesson 3)
3. **Fluency:** Read smoothly, use punctuation to guide your voice (Lesson 4)
4. **Questioning:** Ask who, what, where, when, why, how (Lesson 5)
5. **Retelling:** Tell the beginning, middle, and end (Lesson 6)
6. **Central message:** What did the character learn? (Lesson 6)
7. **Character and setting:** Who is in the story, where does it happen, describe using evidence (Lesson 7)
8. **Cause and effect:** Why did that happen? What happened because of it? (Lesson 8)
9. **Comparing:** How are two things the same or different? (Lesson 9)

Say: "That is a LOT of strategies! You have come so far in this unit. Today you get to use them all on your own. I believe in you!"

---

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Explain the independent reading process and set expectations.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Independent reading:** Reading on your own, using your strategies when you need them
- **Written response:** Writing your answer to a question about what you read

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Here is how today is going to work:"

**Step 1:** "I am going to show you three passages. You will read the first sentence or two of each one and choose the one that interests you most."

**Step 2:** "You will read your passage by yourself. Use your fix-up strategies if you get stuck on a word. Keep checking: does this make sense?"

**Step 3:** "After reading, you will answer some questions about the story — some out loud and some in writing."

**Step 4:** "For the writing part, you will answer one question by writing 2-3 sentences. I will show you how to write a good response first."

**Model a written response:**

Say: "If someone asks me, 'What was the central message of The Little Ant?' here is how I would write a response:"

Write on paper:

> "The central message of The Little Ant is that working together makes hard things easier. The ant could not carry the crumb by herself, but when her friends helped, they carried it home easily. This story teaches us that teamwork is important."

Point out: "I did three things: (1) I answered the question in my first sentence. (2) I gave evidence from the story. (3) I explained what it means. You do not have to write three perfect sentences — just do your best to answer the question and tell why."

## Guided Practice (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Help the student choose a passage and prepare for independent reading.

**Activity: Passage Preview and Selection**

Present the three passages below. Read the title and first 1-2 sentences of each aloud. Then ask the student: "Which one do you want to read?"

**Option A: The Brave Little Turtle**

> Luna was a small turtle who lived near a pond. She loved to swim, but she had never been to the other side of the pond...

**Option B: The Birthday Surprise**

> Dex woke up on Saturday morning with a big grin. It was his birthday! But when he went downstairs, nobody said "happy birthday"...

**Option C: The Best Fort Ever**

> It had been raining for three days straight. Zara and her cousin Mateo were stuck inside with nothing to do...

After the student chooses, say: "Great choice! Before you read, what do you predict this story will be about? What questions do you have?"

Let the student make a prediction. Then say: "Now read the whole story on your own. Take your time. Use your strategies. I will be right here if you need me, but try to work through it yourself first."

*Facilitator note: Stay nearby but give the student space. Resist the urge to help unless the student has been stuck for 15+ seconds. This is their chance to demonstrate independence.*

## Independent Practice (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads independently and responds to comprehension questions.

### Passage A: The Brave Little Turtle

> Luna was a small turtle who lived near a pond. She loved to swim, but she had never been to the other side of the pond. It looked so far away.
>
> "What if I get tired?" Luna worried. "What if I can't make it?"
>
> Her friend Fern the frog said, "You are a strong swimmer, Luna. Just take it one paddle at a time."
>
> Luna slipped into the water. She swam past the lily pads. She swam past the big rock in the middle. Her legs felt tired, but she kept going. "One more paddle," she told herself. "One more paddle."
>
> At last, Luna reached the other side. She climbed onto a warm, flat rock and looked back across the pond.
>
> "I did it!" she said. "It was far, but I did not give up!"
>
> Fern hopped across the lily pads to join her. "I knew you could do it," Fern said. "You are braver than you think."

### Passage B: The Birthday Surprise

> Dex woke up on Saturday morning with a big grin. It was his birthday! But when he went downstairs, nobody said "happy birthday." Mom was busy in the kitchen. Dad was reading the newspaper. His sister Naya was watching TV.
>
> "Did everyone forget?" Dex thought. His smile faded. He ate his cereal quietly and went to his room.
>
> After lunch, Mom said, "Dex, can you help me get something from the garage?"
>
> Dex trudged to the garage and opened the door. "SURPRISE!" Twenty voices shouted at once. The garage was decorated with balloons and streamers. His friends, his grandparents, and his cousins were all there.
>
> Dex's eyes went wide. "You didn't forget!" he said, laughing.
>
> "Of course not," said Naya, handing him a present. "We were planning this all week. Happy birthday, Dex!"
>
> Dex grinned. It was the best surprise he had ever had.

### Passage C: The Best Fort Ever

> It had been raining for three days straight. Zara and her cousin Mateo were stuck inside with nothing to do. They had already played every board game and watched their favorite movie twice.
>
> "I'm bored," said Mateo, flopping on the couch.
>
> Zara looked around the room. She saw blankets, chairs, pillows, and a pile of books. An idea popped into her head. "Let's build a fort!" she said.
>
> They draped blankets over the chairs. They stacked pillows to make walls. They used clothespins to hold everything together. It took a while, but finally, they crawled inside.
>
> "This is amazing," Mateo whispered. The fort was dark and cozy. They brought in flashlights and books and snacks.
>
> "I don't even want it to stop raining," said Zara. "This is better than going outside."
>
> Mateo laughed. "We should build a fort every rainy day."
>
> From inside the fort, they could hear the rain tapping on the windows. It was the perfect sound for reading.

### Comprehension Questions (for whichever passage the student chose)

**Oral Questions (discuss with facilitator):**

1. **Retelling:** Can you tell me what happened in the beginning, middle, and end of the story?
2. **Character:** Who is the main character? Describe them using evidence from the story.
3. **Setting:** Where and when does the story take place? How does the setting matter?
4. **Cause and effect:** Name one cause-and-effect connection in the story.

**Written Response (student writes 2-3 sentences):**

5. **Central message:** What is the central message or lesson of this story? Write 2-3 sentences explaining the message and what in the story helped you figure it out.

### Difficulty Tiers
- ★ Answer oral questions 1-4 with facilitator support. For question 5, say the answer aloud and the facilitator writes it down, or the student writes 1 sentence with a sentence starter: "The message of this story is ___."
- ★★ Answer oral questions 1-4 independently. Write 2 sentences for question 5.
- ★★★ Answer oral questions 1-4 with detailed, evidence-based responses. Write 3 sentences for question 5, including specific text evidence. Then write one additional sentence about which comprehension strategy helped you the most today and why.

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess the student's independent reading and comprehension.

**Assessment Rubric:**

Use this rubric to evaluate the student's overall performance:

| Skill | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Reading accuracy** | Reads with few errors; self-corrects when errors occur | Some errors; occasional self-correction | Many errors; rarely self-corrects |
| **Fluency** | Reads smoothly with expression and appropriate pacing | Mostly smooth; some choppy sections | Word-by-word reading; flat expression |
| **Retelling** | Retells beginning, middle, and end with key details | Retells most of the story but misses some key events | Retelling is incomplete or out of order |
| **Comprehension questions** | Answers oral questions accurately using text evidence | Answers most questions but some responses lack evidence | Struggles to answer questions or answers are not connected to the text |
| **Written response** | Writes 2-3 sentences with a clear answer, evidence, and explanation | Writes 1-2 sentences with a partial answer | Writing is unclear or does not address the question |

**Mastery = Score of at least 2 in every category.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which specific skills need more practice (decoding? fluency? comprehension? writing?)
- If the passage was too difficult, the student may need more time with decoding (Lessons 1-2) before attempting independent reading
- For comprehension gaps, return to the specific lesson (5-8) that addresses the weak area
- For writing gaps, practice oral responses first, then use sentence frames to scaffold written responses
- Consider re-doing this lesson with a different passage after targeted practice

### If Mastery Is Met
The student is ready for the Unit Assessment. Celebrate their growth! Say: "You have learned so many strategies in this unit. You can decode tricky words, read with expression, ask questions, retell stories, analyze characters, find cause and effect, and compare texts. That is incredible growth!"

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate strong independent reading:**
- **Read Another Passage:** The student reads one of the other two passages they did not choose and compares it to their chosen passage using a Venn diagram (Lesson 9 skill).
- **Book Recommendation:** The student writes 3-4 sentences recommending their chosen passage to a friend. Include: what the story is about, who would like it, and why.
- **Author's Chair:** The student reads their written response aloud, as if they were a reading expert sharing their thinking with the class.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson is longer than previous lessons because it includes independent reading time. If the student needs more time for reading or writing, it is fine to extend to 40-45 minutes or split into two sessions (Session A: reading and oral questions; Session B: written response).

**Critical note — this is a synthesis lesson, not a teaching lesson:** The purpose of Lesson 10 is to give the student an opportunity to apply what they have learned, not to teach new skills. Resist the urge to reteach during independent reading. If the student struggles, note the area for reteaching later but let them work through it as independently as possible.

**The power of choice:** Letting the student choose their passage is intentional. Choice increases motivation and engagement. Do not steer the student toward a particular passage — let them pick based on interest.

**Supporting the written response:** Writing is often the hardest part for second graders. The key scaffolds are:
1. Talk first, write second — have the student say their answer aloud before writing
2. Sentence starters: "The message of this story is ___. I know because ___."
3. Focus on ideas, not spelling — if the student has great ideas but messy spelling, praise the ideas. Spelling can be cleaned up later.

**Growth mindset note:** This is a great moment to reflect on growth. Say: "Remember when we started this unit? You have learned how to read bigger words, read with expression, ask questions about stories, retell them, describe characters, find cause and effect, and compare texts. Look how much you have grown as a reader!"

## Answer Key

**Passage A: The Brave Little Turtle**

1. **Retelling:** Beginning — Luna wanted to swim across the pond but was scared she could not make it. Middle — Fern encouraged her, and Luna swam across, telling herself "one more paddle" when she got tired. End — Luna reached the other side and was proud she did not give up.
2. **Character:** Luna is brave (she did something that scared her), determined (she kept going when tired), and a bit nervous at first (she worried about getting tired).
3. **Setting:** A pond. The setting matters because the pond being far across is what creates the challenge.
4. **Cause and effect:** Luna was tired, but she kept telling herself "one more paddle," so she kept going. / Fern encouraged Luna, so Luna tried to swim across.
5. **Central message:** The story teaches that you should try hard things even if they scare you. Luna was afraid to swim across the pond, but she did not give up and made it. The message is that you are braver and stronger than you think.

**Passage B: The Birthday Surprise**

1. **Retelling:** Beginning — Dex woke up on his birthday but nobody said happy birthday. Middle — Dex thought everyone forgot and felt sad. End — Mom asked him to go to the garage, and his family and friends surprised him with a party.
2. **Character:** Dex is emotional (his smile faded when he thought they forgot, but then he was overjoyed), loved by his family (they planned a surprise all week). Naya is thoughtful (she handed him a present and explained they had been planning).
3. **Setting:** Dex's house on a Saturday morning (his birthday). The garage is where the surprise happens.
4. **Cause and effect:** Nobody said happy birthday, so Dex thought everyone forgot and felt sad. / The family was planning a surprise all week, so they acted normal to keep the secret.
5. **Central message:** Things are not always what they seem — sometimes people are doing something kind that you cannot see yet. Dex thought his family forgot, but they were actually planning a wonderful surprise. The message is to have trust and patience.

**Passage C: The Best Fort Ever**

1. **Retelling:** Beginning — Zara and Mateo were bored because it had been raining for three days. Middle — Zara had the idea to build a fort using blankets, chairs, pillows, and clothespins. End — They crawled inside with flashlights, books, and snacks and decided rainy days were actually great.
2. **Character:** Zara is creative (she looked at blankets and chairs and came up with the fort idea) and optimistic (she turned a boring day into something fun). Mateo is easygoing and appreciative ("This is amazing").
3. **Setting:** Inside a house on a rainy day. The rainy-day setting causes the problem (boredom) and the solution (building an indoor fort).
4. **Cause and effect:** It rained for three days, so they were stuck inside and bored. / They were bored, so Zara came up with the idea to build a fort. / They built the fort, so they had fun and did not want the rain to stop.
5. **Central message:** You can use your creativity to make the best of a bad situation. Zara and Mateo were bored, but instead of staying bored, they used what they had to build something wonderful. The message is that a little creativity can turn a bad day into a great one.
