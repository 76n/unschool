# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 1, Lesson 3

## Topic
Reading Accuracy and Self-Correction Strategies

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Use at least three strategies when encountering an unfamiliar word (sound it out, look for chunks, reread, use picture clues, check for meaning)
- Self-monitor while reading by asking: "Did that make sense? Did that sound right? Did that look right?"
- Self-correct errors independently when prompted

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: Advanced Vowel Patterns
- Lesson 2: Multisyllable Decoding
- Ability to read simple sentences

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Reading passages provided in this lesson (print or display)
- A bookmark or index card (to track lines while reading)
- "Fix-Up Strategies" reference card (created during the lesson)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review decoding skills from Lessons 1-2 and Grade 1 phonics.

**Activity: Word Ladder**

Say: "Let's warm up our reading brains with some words. Read each word as fast as you can."

Write these words one at a time:

1. **chart** (Lesson 1 review: ar)
2. **charting** (Lesson 2 review: suffix -ing)
3. **recount** (Lesson 2 review: prefix re-)
4. **flower** (Lesson 1 review: ow diphthong)
5. **bedroom** (Lesson 2 review: compound word)
6. **train** (Grade 1 review: ai vowel team)

Say: "You are reading bigger words now! Today we are going to practice what to do when you get to a word you do not know."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach five fix-up strategies for reading accuracy and introduce self-monitoring.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Accuracy:** Reading the words correctly — saying the right word
- **Self-correct:** When you notice you made a mistake and fix it on your own
- **Self-monitor:** Checking yourself while you read to make sure it makes sense

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the problem (2 minutes)**

Say: "Even great readers come to words they do not know. That is totally normal! What matters is what you do when you get stuck. Good readers have a toolkit of strategies. Today we are going to build your toolkit."

**Step 2: Teach five fix-up strategies (6 minutes)**

Create a "Fix-Up Strategies" reference card together. Write each strategy on an index card or piece of paper that the student can keep:

**My Fix-Up Strategies**

1. **Sound it out.** Look at each letter or letter group and blend the sounds together. Use what you know about vowel patterns and word parts.

2. **Look for chunks you know.** Do you see a small word inside the big word? A prefix or suffix? A familiar pattern?

3. **Reread the sentence.** Go back to the beginning of the sentence and read it again. Sometimes the other words give you clues.

4. **Use picture clues.** If there is a picture, look at it. What is happening? What word would make sense?

5. **Ask: Does it make sense?** After you try a word, check — does that word make sense in the sentence? Does it sound right? Does it match the letters you see?

For each strategy, model with an example:

Write: "The bird sat on a **branch** of the old tree."

Say: "Pretend I do not know the word 'branch.' Watch me use my strategies."
- "Let me sound it out: /b/ /r/ /a/ /n/ /ch/... branch!"
- "Let me check — does 'branch' make sense? A bird sitting on a branch of a tree? Yes!"

Write: "She was **unhappy** about the rain."

Say: "Pretend I do not know this word. Let me look for chunks I know: I see *un-* and *happy*. Un-happy. Unhappy! Does it make sense? She was unhappy about the rain? Yes!"

**Step 3: Introduce self-monitoring (2 minutes)**

Say: "Good readers are always checking themselves. After you read a sentence, ask yourself three questions:"

Write on paper:
- **Does it make sense?** (Do the ideas make sense?)
- **Does it sound right?** (Does it sound like the way people talk?)
- **Does it look right?** (Do the letters match what I said?)

Say: "If the answer to any of these is 'no,' go back and try again. That is called self-correcting, and it is one of the smartest things a reader can do."

### Worked Examples

Write this sentence: "The cat jumped over the **fence** and ran away."

Say: "Let me pretend I read this as: 'The cat jumped over the *face* and ran away.' Hmm, let me check — does it make sense? The cat jumped over the face? That does not make sense. Let me look at the word again. F-E-N-C-E. Oh! That is *fence*, not *face*. 'The cat jumped over the fence and ran away.' Now it makes sense!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice using fix-up strategies with short passages.

**Activity 1: Strategy Practice with Sentences (5 minutes)**

Read these sentences with the student. For each underlined word, the student should read it, then tell you which strategy they used. If they read it easily, still ask: "How did you know that word?"

1. The farmer drove his **tractor** down the dirt road.
2. We need to **repack** our bags before the trip.
3. The little turtle moved **slowly** along the path.
4. A **thunderstorm** woke us up last night.
5. The puppy was **playful** and full of energy.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student reads a word incorrectly and does not notice, ask: "Does that make sense in the sentence? Let's read it again." Guide them to self-correct rather than telling them the right word.*

*If the student struggles significantly: Point to the word and say: "Let's try our strategies. Can you sound it out? Do you see any chunks you know?" Walk through the strategies together.*

**Activity 2: Reading a Short Passage (5 minutes)**

Say: "Now let's practice reading a short passage. Remember to use your strategies if you get stuck, and keep checking — does it make sense?"

**Passage: A Rainy Morning**

> Ben looked out the window. Dark clouds filled the sky. Raindrops started to fall, slowly at first, then faster and faster. Ben felt unhappy. He wanted to play outside.
>
> "We can play inside," said his sister, Joy. "Let's build a fort!"
>
> Ben smiled. He grabbed some blankets and pillows. They worked together to build the best fort ever. By the time the rain stopped, they did not even want to go outside anymore.

Have the student read the passage aloud. Note any errors, but do not correct every mistake immediately. After a sentence with an error, pause and ask: "Did everything sound right in that sentence?" Give the student a chance to find and fix the error.

After reading, ask: "Were there any tricky words? What did you do when you got to them?"

*Facilitator prompt: If the student makes more than 5 errors in this passage, it may be too difficult. Simplify by reading it aloud first (echo reading), then having the student read it.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices reading with accuracy and self-correction independently.

**Activity: Read and Check**

The student reads the following passage independently. After reading, they answer the questions below.

**Passage: The Garden Helper**

> Marta helped her grandpa in the garden every Saturday. She liked to dig in the dirt and water the flowers. Her favorite part was picking the ripe tomatoes.
>
> One morning, Marta found a small green frog sitting on a leaf. "Hello, little frog!" she said softly. The frog blinked its big eyes and jumped into the garden.
>
> "That frog is helpful," said Grandpa. "Frogs eat the bugs that hurt our plants."
>
> Marta grinned. "Then the frog is our garden helper, just like me!"

After reading, the student answers these questions orally or in writing:

1. Were there any words you got stuck on? What did you do?
2. Read the passage one more time. Did it sound smoother the second time?

### Difficulty Tiers
- ★ Read the passage with the facilitator nearby, asking for help on tricky words and using the Fix-Up Strategies card as a reference
- ★★ Read the passage independently, using the Fix-Up Strategies card if needed, and tell the facilitator about any words that were tricky
- ★★★ Read the passage independently without the strategies card, self-correct any errors, and then read it a second time even more smoothly

### Interleaved Review
After the passage, have the student read these review words:

3. **barnyard** (Lessons 1-2: r-controlled + compound word)
4. **joyful** (Lessons 1-2: diphthong + suffix)
5. **sweet** (Grade 1 review: ee vowel team)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess whether the student can use fix-up strategies and self-correct.

**Exit Ticket:**

**Part 1:** Ask the student: "Name three things you can do when you get stuck on a word." (Student should name at least 3 of the 5 strategies: sound it out, look for chunks, reread the sentence, use picture clues, check if it makes sense.)

**Part 2:** Write this sentence with a tricky word:

"The bird perched on the highest branch and chirped a cheerful song."

Have the student read it aloud. Observe:
- Does the student attempt unfamiliar words using a strategy (rather than just skipping or guessing wildly)?
- If the student makes an error, do they notice and try to self-correct?

**Mastery = Student names at least 3 strategies AND reads the sentence with no more than 1 uncorrected error.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Keep the Fix-Up Strategies card visible during all reading practice
- Before moving to Lesson 4, spend an extra session reading short passages together and practicing the strategies
- Focus on one strategy at a time — start with "sound it out" and "look for chunks" since those are the most concrete
- If the student consistently does not notice errors, practice the self-monitoring questions: read a sentence, intentionally make a mistake, and have the student catch it

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Reading Rate and Expression) next session. Continue to reinforce self-monitoring habits during all reading activities.

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate strong accuracy and self-correction:**
- **Error Detective:** The facilitator reads a passage aloud and intentionally makes 3-4 errors (word substitutions that change the meaning). The student listens and catches the errors. This builds listening comprehension and self-monitoring awareness.
- **Strategy Journal:** After independent reading, the student writes down one tricky word they encountered and which strategy they used to figure it out.
- **Teach the Strategies:** The student explains the five fix-up strategies to a stuffed animal, sibling, or family member. Teaching reinforces learning.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson is about building habits, not perfection. The student will not master self-correction in one session. The goal is for the student to know the strategies exist and to begin using them consciously. This will develop over the entire unit (and year).

**Critical facilitator skill — wait time:** When the student encounters an unfamiliar word, resist the urge to immediately tell them the word. Wait 5-10 seconds. Often the student will try a strategy on their own. If they are truly stuck after trying, step in with a prompt: "Try sounding it out" or "Do you see any parts you know?" Only provide the word as a last resort.

**Common issue — over-reliance on one strategy:** Some students will only want to sound out words letter by letter. Others will guess based on the first letter. Encourage the student to have multiple strategies: "Sounding it out is one strategy. What else could you try?" The self-monitoring questions help students evaluate whether their attempt worked, which motivates trying other strategies.

**Common issue — not noticing errors:** Many second graders read right through errors without noticing. This is because they are focused on decoding and not yet monitoring for meaning. The self-monitoring questions ("Does it make sense?") need to become a habit. Ask these questions frequently — eventually the student will internalize them.

**Growth mindset note:** Say: "Getting stuck on a word does not mean you are a bad reader. It means you found a new word to learn! Every reader gets stuck sometimes. What makes you a great reader is that you know what to do when it happens."

**Note on the passages:** The two passages in this lesson ("A Rainy Morning" and "The Garden Helper") are intentionally simple and engaging, with a mix of familiar and slightly challenging words. They will be referenced again in later comprehension lessons, so keep copies available.
