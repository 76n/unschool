# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 1, Lesson 5

## Topic
Asking and Answering Questions: Who, What, Where, When, Why, How

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Ask who, what, where, when, why, and how questions about a text
- Answer questions about a text by referring back to the story
- Use a graphic organizer to record questions and answers about a reading passage
- Apply the before/during/after reading strategy (predict, check, recall)

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-4 (decoding, accuracy, and fluency skills)
- Ability to read a short passage at grade level with reasonable accuracy

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Reading passage provided in this lesson (print or display)
- Question words reference card (created during the lesson)
- Graphic organizer template (drawn during the lesson or pre-drawn)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review fluency and decoding, and activate prior knowledge about asking questions.

**Activity: Fluency Review + Question Words**

Have the student read these sentences aloud with expression (fluency review from Lesson 4):

1. "Where did you put the paintbrush?" asked Ella.
2. "I left it on the porch!" said James.

Then say: "You just read a question. Ella asked 'where' something was. Today we are going to learn about the different kinds of questions we can ask when we read a story. Asking questions is one of the most powerful things a reader can do!"

Write the six question words in a column: **Who, What, Where, When, Why, How**

Say: "These are the question words. Let's talk about what each one asks."

- **Who** — asks about a person or animal
- **What** — asks about a thing or an event (what happened)
- **Where** — asks about a place
- **When** — asks about a time
- **Why** — asks about a reason
- **How** — asks about the way something happens

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the before/during/after reading strategy and how to ask and answer questions.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Comprehension:** Understanding what you read — knowing what the story is about and what it means
- **Predict:** To make a guess about what will happen before you read
- **Question words:** Who, what, where, when, why, how — words that help us ask questions to understand a story

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the Before/During/After strategy (3 minutes)**

Say: "Good readers think before, during, and after they read. Here is what that looks like:"

Write on paper:

**Before Reading:**
- Look at the title and any pictures
- Predict: "What do I think this story will be about?"
- Wonder: "What questions do I already have?"

**During Reading:**
- Check: "Does this match what I predicted?"
- Ask: "Do I understand what is happening? Who is this about? What just happened?"

**After Reading:**
- Recall: "What was the story about?"
- Answer: "Can I answer the questions — who, what, where, when, why, how?"

**Step 2: Model asking and answering questions (7 minutes)**

Say: "I am going to read a very short passage, and I will show you how I ask questions."

Write or display this short text:

> **The New Puppy**
>
> On Saturday morning, Kai and his mom went to the animal shelter. Kai wanted a puppy more than anything. He looked at all the dogs. One small brown puppy wagged its tail and licked Kai's hand. "That's the one," Kai whispered. Mom smiled and nodded.

**Before reading:** "The title says 'The New Puppy.' I predict this story is about someone getting a new puppy. I wonder — who gets the puppy? Where do they get it?"

**During reading:** Read the passage aloud. After the first sentence, pause: "Who is in the story? Kai and his mom. Where did they go? The animal shelter. When? Saturday morning."

After the second sentence: "What did Kai want? A puppy."

After the rest: "What happened? A brown puppy licked Kai's hand. What did Kai say? 'That's the one.' What did Mom do? She smiled and nodded."

**After reading:** "Now let me answer all the question words:"

| Question Word | Question | Answer |
|---------------|----------|--------|
| **Who** | Who is the story about? | Kai and his mom |
| **What** | What happened? | Kai chose a puppy at the animal shelter |
| **Where** | Where did they go? | The animal shelter |
| **When** | When did this happen? | Saturday morning |
| **Why** | Why did they go to the shelter? | Because Kai wanted a puppy |
| **How** | How did Kai choose his puppy? | The puppy wagged its tail and licked his hand |

### Worked Examples

Point to the passage and say: "Notice how I found the answers IN the story. I did not make them up. When someone asks you a question about a story, you can go back to the story and find the answer. Let me show you."

Ask: "Where did Kai and his mom go?" Point to the first sentence. "It says right here — 'went to the animal shelter.' The answer is in the text."

Ask: "Why did the puppy stand out to Kai?" Point to the relevant sentence. "It says 'one small brown puppy wagged its tail and licked Kai's hand.' I can use that to answer the question."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice asking and answering questions together with a new passage.

**Activity: Read and Question Together**

Say: "Now we are going to read a new story together. This time, YOU are going to ask the questions."

**Before reading:** Show the student the title. Ask: "What do you predict this story will be about? What questions do you have?"

**Passage: The Big Storm**

> It was a warm afternoon in June. Anna and her dad were playing catch in the backyard. Suddenly, the sky turned dark. Thunder rumbled in the distance.
>
> "We need to go inside," said Dad. "A storm is coming."
>
> They hurried into the house. Rain began to pour down. Lightning flashed across the sky. Anna felt a little scared.
>
> "It's okay," said Dad. "Storms are loud, but we are safe inside. Let's read a book together while we wait."
>
> Anna picked her favorite book. They sat on the couch and read until the storm passed. When the sun came out again, Anna saw a beautiful rainbow.
>
> "That was worth the wait!" she said.

**During reading:** Read the passage together (choral reading or taking turns). After each paragraph, pause and ask the student to tell you one thing they noticed or one question they have.

**After reading:** Create a graphic organizer together. Draw a simple chart on paper:

| Question Word | My Question | Answer from the Story |
|---------------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Who** | | |
| **What** | | |
| **Where** | | |
| **When** | | |
| **Why** | | |
| **How** | | |

Say: "Let's fill this in together. For 'Who,' can you ask a question that starts with 'who'?"

Help the student generate questions and find answers in the text. Guide them to point to the part of the text where they found each answer.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles to form questions, offer a starter: "Who was in the story?" or "Who said 'We need to go inside'?" Then let the student try the next one more independently.*

*If the student struggles to find answers: Say: "Let's look back at the story. Can you find where it tells us [the answer]?" Point to the relevant paragraph.*

**Expected answers (allow for variation):**

| Question Word | Sample Question | Answer |
|---------------|----------------|--------|
| **Who** | Who is the story about? | Anna and her dad |
| **What** | What happened? | A storm came while they were playing, so they went inside and read a book |
| **Where** | Where were Anna and Dad playing? | In the backyard |
| **When** | When did the story take place? | A warm afternoon in June |
| **Why** | Why did they go inside? | Because a storm was coming |
| **How** | How did Anna feel better? | Dad said they were safe and they read a book together |

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads a passage and answers questions independently.

**Activity: Read and Respond**

The student reads the following passage independently and then answers the questions below. They may look back at the passage to find answers.

**Passage: The Sharing Tree**

> In the park near Lily's house, there was a very old oak tree. It was the biggest tree in the whole park. Lily called it "the sharing tree" because everyone in the neighborhood shared it.
>
> In the spring, birds built nests in its branches. In the summer, children played in its shade. In the fall, squirrels collected acorns under it. In the winter, it stood tall and strong, even without its leaves.
>
> One windy day in March, a big branch broke off the tree. Lily was worried. "Is the tree going to be okay?" she asked her mom.
>
> "Trees are strong," said Mom. "It will grow new branches. Just wait and see."
>
> Lily visited the tree every week. By summer, she could see tiny new branches starting to grow. She smiled. The sharing tree was going to be just fine.

**Questions:**

1. **Who** is the main character in this story? ___
2. **What** did Lily call the oak tree? ___
3. **Where** is the tree located? ___
4. **When** did the big branch break? ___
5. **Why** was Lily worried? ___
6. **How** did Lily know the tree would be okay? ___

### Difficulty Tiers
- ★ Answer the 6 questions above. The student may answer orally and the facilitator writes down the answers.
- ★★ Answer the 6 questions in writing (words or short phrases are fine). Then write one question of your own about the story.
- ★★★ Answer the 6 questions in complete sentences. Then write two questions of your own and answer them.

### Interleaved Review
After completing the questions, have the student read these review words:

7. **neighborhood** (Lesson 2: multisyllable decoding)
8. **worried** (Lesson 2: suffix -ed, with spelling change)
9. **branches** (Lesson 1: review of consonant digraph + decoding)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess whether the student can ask and answer questions about a text.

**Exit Ticket:**

Read this very short text to the student (or have them read it):

> Tom found a shiny gold coin on the sidewalk after school on Tuesday. He picked it up and showed it to his friend Maria. "Maybe it is a lucky coin!" said Maria.

Ask:
1. **Who** found the coin? (Tom)
2. **Where** did he find it? (On the sidewalk)
3. **When** did he find it? (After school on Tuesday)
4. **What** did Maria say about it? (Maybe it is a lucky coin)

**Mastery = Student answers at least 3 out of 4 questions correctly, referring to the text (not making up answers).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice with very short texts (2-3 sentences) and one question at a time
- Use the question words reference card during reading
- Before asking questions, have the student retell what happened in the passage — this ensures they understood the basic content before moving to question-answering
- Highlight key words in the text that connect to each question word

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 6 (Retelling and Central Message) next session. Continue to ask who/what/where/when/why/how questions during all reading activities.

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate strong question-asking skills:**
- **Question Creator:** The student reads a short passage and writes 3-4 questions about it (without seeing pre-written questions). Then the facilitator answers the questions and the student checks whether the answers are correct using the text.
- **Question Game:** Take turns asking each other questions about a passage. If the answerer gets it right, they earn a point. If they need to look back at the text, they can — but encourage answering from memory first.
- **Real-World Questions:** Practice the before/during/after strategy with a real book. Before reading a page, the student predicts and wonders. After the page, they ask and answer one question.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This is the first explicit comprehension lesson in the unit. Take your time. If the student is still working on fluency (Lessons 1-4 skills), it is okay to spend extra time on fluency warm-ups before diving into comprehension work.

**Common issue — answering from imagination instead of text:** Second graders sometimes answer questions based on what they think should happen rather than what the text actually says. When this happens, gently say: "That is a great idea! But let's check — does the story tell us that? Let's look back and find where it says the answer." Teaching students to go back to the text is one of the most important comprehension habits to build.

**Common issue — "why" and "how" questions are harder:** Who, what, where, and when questions have answers that are usually stated directly in the text. Why and how questions often require the student to think a little more — the answer may be implied rather than stated. Be patient with these. Help the student connect clues: "The story says Anna felt scared. Then Dad said they were safe. What did they do? Read a book. So how did Anna feel better? Dad helped her feel safe, and they read together."

**Common issue — the student does not know how to form a question:** Some students can answer questions but struggle to create them. Practice with a simple frame: "Who ___?" "What ___?" "Where ___?" Sentence starters help: "Who is the story about?" "What happened when ___?" "Where did ___ go?"

**Growth mindset note:** Say: "Asking questions is what scientists, detectives, and great readers all do. When you ask questions about a story, you are thinking deeply. That is exactly what strong readers do."
