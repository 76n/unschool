# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 1, Lesson 8

## Topic
Connecting Events in a Story: Cause and Effect

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain how events in a story connect to one another (one event leads to the next)
- Identify cause and effect relationships in a story
- Recognize signal words that show cause and effect (because, so, then, after, since)
- Sequence events from a story in the correct order

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 6: Retelling and Central Message (beginning, middle, end)
- Lesson 7: Character and Setting
- Ability to retell a story in order

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Reading passage provided in this lesson (print or display)
- Index cards or strips of paper (for sequencing activity)
- Scissors (optional, for cut-and-sort sequencing)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review character/setting skills and activate thinking about cause and effect.

**Activity: Everyday Cause and Effect**

Say: "Before we read today, let's think about something called cause and effect. A cause is WHY something happens. An effect is WHAT happens."

Give everyday examples and ask the student to identify the cause and effect:

1. "I left my ice cream in the sun. It melted." — What is the cause? (Left ice cream in the sun.) What is the effect? (It melted.)
2. "It rained all night. The ground was wet this morning." — What is the cause? (It rained.) What is the effect? (The ground was wet.)
3. "The dog barked loudly. The cat ran under the bed." — What is the cause? (The dog barked.) What is the effect? (The cat ran under the bed.)

Say: "Cause and effect happen in stories too! Today we are going to practice finding how one event in a story causes another event to happen."

**Decoding Review:** Have the student read: **because, suddenly, afterward** (multisyllable words from Lesson 2 strategies)

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach cause and effect in stories and introduce signal words.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Cause:** The reason something happens — WHY it happens
- **Effect:** What happens as a result — WHAT happens because of the cause
- **Signal words:** Words that help you see the connection between events (because, so, then, after, since, when)
- **Sequence:** The order events happen in — first, next, then, finally

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Cause and effect in stories (4 minutes)**

Say: "In stories, events do not just happen randomly. One event causes the next event. It is like a chain."

Draw a simple cause-and-effect chain:

```
[Cause] → [Effect/Cause] → [Effect/Cause] → [Effect]
```

Say: "The first event causes the second. The second causes the third. And so on. This is how a story moves forward."

Model with a familiar story:

"Think about the story of 'Two Cats and a Blanket' from Lesson 6:"

- **Event 1:** Both cats wanted the blanket. → **This caused:** They pulled on it.
- **Event 2:** They pulled on the blanket. → **This caused:** The blanket ripped.
- **Event 3:** The blanket ripped. → **This caused:** Both cats were cold.
- **Event 4:** Both cats were cold. → **This caused:** They decided to share.

Say: "See how each event leads to the next? That is cause and effect!"

**Step 2: Signal words (3 minutes)**

Say: "Authors use special words to show cause and effect. These are called signal words. When you see these words, they are clues that a cause and effect is happening."

Write the signal words:

| Signal Word | How It Works | Example |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| **because** | Connects cause to effect | The ground was wet **because** it rained. |
| **so** | The cause happened, **so** the effect followed | It rained, **so** the ground was wet. |
| **then** | One thing happened, **then** the next happened | It rained. **Then** the ground got wet. |
| **after** | **After** the cause, the effect happened | **After** it rained, the ground was wet. |
| **since** | **Since** the cause happened, the effect followed | **Since** it rained, we stayed inside. |
| **when** | **When** the cause happened, the effect happened | **When** it rained, the ground got wet. |

**Step 3: Practice finding signal words (3 minutes)**

Write these sentences. Have the student find the signal word and identify the cause and effect:

1. "Mia studied hard, **so** she did well on the test."
   - Signal word: so. Cause: Mia studied hard. Effect: She did well on the test.

2. "The flowers grew tall **because** they got lots of sunlight."
   - Signal word: because. Cause: They got lots of sunlight. Effect: The flowers grew tall.

3. "**After** the bell rang, the children ran outside."
   - Signal word: after. Cause: The bell rang. Effect: The children ran outside.

### Worked Examples

Write: "Jack forgot his umbrella, so he got wet in the rain."

Say: "Let me find the signal word — 'so.' What is the cause? Jack forgot his umbrella. What is the effect? He got wet. The signal word 'so' connects them."

Write: "Because the kitten was tired, it curled up and fell asleep."

Say: "Signal word: 'because.' The cause is that the kitten was tired. The effect is that it curled up and fell asleep."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice identifying cause and effect and sequencing events in a story.

**Activity 1: Read and Find Connections (5 minutes)**

Read this passage together:

**Passage: The Garden Disaster**

> Tomas planted sunflower seeds in his garden. He watered them every day and made sure they got plenty of sun. After two weeks, tiny green sprouts poked out of the soil. Tomas was so proud.
>
> Then one morning, Tomas saw that a rabbit had gotten into the garden. It had nibbled on several of his little plants! The stems were bent and the leaves were chewed.
>
> Tomas felt upset, but his dad had an idea. "Let's build a small fence around the garden," Dad said. "That will keep the rabbits out."
>
> They spent the afternoon building a fence from sticks and string. It was not fancy, but it worked. The rabbit could not get in anymore.
>
> Over the next few weeks, the sunflowers grew taller and taller. By the end of summer, they were as tall as Tomas! He picked one and gave it to his dad.
>
> "Thank you for helping me save my garden," Tomas said.

After reading, work through cause and effect together:

Ask: "What caused the sprouts to grow?" (Tomas planted seeds, watered them, and gave them sun.)

Ask: "What happened because the rabbit got in?" (It nibbled the plants — the stems were bent and leaves were chewed. Tomas felt upset.)

Ask: "What did Tomas and his dad do because of the rabbit problem?" (They built a fence.)

Ask: "What happened after they built the fence?" (The rabbit could not get in, and the sunflowers grew tall.)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles, point to specific sentences and say: "This event happened. What happened next? Did the first event cause the second one?"*

**Activity 2: Sequencing (5 minutes)**

Write these events on separate index cards (or strips of paper) in mixed-up order. Have the student put them in the correct sequence:

- Tomas planted sunflower seeds.
- Tiny green sprouts grew.
- A rabbit nibbled the plants.
- Tomas and his dad built a fence.
- The sunflowers grew as tall as Tomas.
- Tomas gave a sunflower to his dad.

After sorting, ask: "Can you point to a place where one event CAUSED the next?" Have the student draw arrows between cause-and-effect pairs.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student has trouble sequencing, ask: "Which event happened first? What happened because of that?" Work through one pair at a time.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student identifies cause-and-effect relationships and sequences events independently.

**Activity: Read and Respond**

The student reads the following passage independently and completes the activities below.

**Passage: The Missing Key**

> One morning, Rosa could not find her house key. She looked in her backpack. She looked on the kitchen counter. She looked in her coat pockets. It was not anywhere.
>
> "Oh no," Rosa said. "I must have dropped it on the way home from school yesterday."
>
> Rosa and her mom walked along the sidewalk, retracing her steps. They looked carefully on the ground. After ten minutes, Rosa spotted something shiny under a bush near the school.
>
> "There it is!" she cried. She picked up the key and held it tight.
>
> "Let's get you a keychain," said Mom. "If your key is on a bright keychain, it will be easier to find in your backpack and harder to lose."
>
> That afternoon, they went to the store. Rosa chose a bright orange keychain shaped like a star. She clipped her key onto it right away.
>
> "I will never lose my key again," Rosa said with a grin.

**Part 1: Cause and Effect Matching**

Draw lines to match each cause with its effect:

| Cause | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| Rosa dropped her key on the way home | They retraced her steps to school |
| Rosa could not find her key at home | Rosa spotted it under a bush |
| They looked carefully along the sidewalk | She could not find it the next morning |
| Mom suggested a keychain | Rosa chose a bright orange star keychain |

**Part 2: Sequencing**

Number these events in the order they happened (1-5):

___ Rosa and her mom walked to school looking for the key.
___ Rosa could not find her key in the morning.
___ Rosa chose a bright orange keychain at the store.
___ Rosa found the shiny key under a bush.
___ Rosa dropped her key on the way home from school.

**Part 3: Signal Words**

Find one signal word in the passage and write the sentence it appears in. Identify the cause and effect.

### Difficulty Tiers
- ★ Complete Part 1 (cause-effect matching) and Part 2 (sequencing) with facilitator help as needed. Answer orally.
- ★★ Complete Parts 1, 2, and 3 in writing independently.
- ★★★ Complete all three parts AND write 2-3 sentences explaining how the events in the story connect to each other. Use at least one signal word (because, so, then, after) in your sentences.

### Interleaved Review
After completing the activity, ask these review questions:
- **Character (Lesson 7):** What word would you use to describe Rosa? Why? (Responsible — she cared about finding her key; determined — she kept looking; smart — she took steps to prevent losing it again.)
- **Retelling (Lesson 6):** Can you retell this story in three sentences — beginning, middle, end?

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess whether the student can identify cause and effect in a story.

**Exit Ticket:**

Read this short text to the student (or have them read it):

> The wind blew hard and knocked over the flower pot on the porch. Dirt spilled everywhere. Mom asked Leo to help clean it up. Because they worked together, the porch was clean in just five minutes.

Ask:
1. What caused the flower pot to fall over? (The wind blew hard.)
2. What happened after the pot fell? (Dirt spilled everywhere.)
3. Find the signal word in the last sentence. What is the cause and effect? (Signal word: "because." Cause: They worked together. Effect: The porch was clean in five minutes.)

**Mastery = Student answers at least 2 out of 3 correctly, including identifying at least one cause-and-effect pair.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to everyday cause and effect examples (not from stories) and practice identifying cause and effect in real life
- Use the signal words as a reference — have the student look for those words in sentences as a starting point
- Work with very short texts (2-3 sentences) with explicit cause-and-effect relationships before returning to longer passages
- Practice the language: "_____ happened because _____" and "_____ happened, so _____"

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 9 (Comparing Texts) next session. Continue to point out cause-and-effect connections during all future reading.

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate strong cause-and-effect understanding:**
- **Chain Reaction Story:** Create an original cause-and-effect chain. The student writes 4-5 events where each one causes the next. Example: "The alarm did not go off, so I woke up late. Because I woke up late, I missed the bus. Since I missed the bus..."
- **What If?** Go back to the Garden Disaster story. Ask: "What if Tomas and his dad did NOT build a fence? What do you think would have happened?" This practices prediction based on cause-and-effect reasoning.
- **Cause-and-Effect Hunt:** Find cause-and-effect relationships in a book the student is reading independently. Write down two examples with signal words.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** Cause and effect is a critical comprehension skill that extends well beyond reading — it is how we understand the world. If the student needs extra time, spend two sessions on this lesson. Use Session A for the concept and guided practice, and Session B for the independent practice and extension.

**Common issue — confusing sequence with cause:** Students may think that because Event B came after Event A, Event A caused Event B. Help them test this: "Did the first event MAKE the second one happen? Or did they just happen one after another?" Example: "Tomas ate breakfast. Then he went to school." Eating breakfast did not cause him to go to school — those events are sequential but not causal.

**Common issue — difficulty with "why" questions:** Cause-and-effect questions are essentially "why" questions, which are harder than "who/what/where/when." If the student struggles, rephrase: "What MADE this happen?" is sometimes easier than "WHY did this happen?"

**Common issue — missing implicit cause-and-effect:** Sometimes the cause or effect is not stated directly. In "The Garden Disaster," the text does not explicitly say "the fence kept the rabbit out, which caused the sunflowers to grow." The student has to connect these events. If they struggle with implicit connections, make them explicit: "The fence kept the rabbit out. Because of that, the plants were safe. Because the plants were safe, they grew tall."

**Growth mindset note:** Say: "Finding cause and effect is like being a detective — you are figuring out WHY things happen in the story. Every time you ask 'why?' or 'what happened because of that?', you are thinking deeply about the story."
