# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 1 Assessment

## Unit: Reading Fluency & Comprehension

**Assessment Type:** Summative (end-of-unit)
**Estimated Time:** 35-45 minutes
**Facilitation:** The facilitator administers Part 1 (decoding) and Part 2 (fluency) individually, then the student completes Part 3 (comprehension) independently or with the facilitator reading questions aloud.

## Part 2: Fluency (9 points)

**Instructions to facilitator:** Have the student read the passage below aloud. Use the rubric to score accuracy, rate, and expression. Do NOT pre-read the passage to the student — this should be a cold read.

### Fluency Passage: The Painting

> Ava loved to paint. Every afternoon, she sat at the kitchen table with her brushes and her paints. She painted flowers, trees, birds, and sometimes even her cat, Whiskers.
>
> One day, Ava tried to paint the sunset. She mixed red and orange together, but it did not look right. She tried again with yellow and pink. Still not right.
>
> "I can't get the colors to match!" she said, feeling frustrated.
>
> Her older brother, James, walked by. "Can I see?" he asked. He looked at the painting, then looked out the window at the sky.
>
> "It's beautiful," he said. "It doesn't have to look exactly like the real sunset. It looks like YOUR sunset, and that is what makes it special."
>
> Ava looked at her painting again. James was right. The colors were bright and bold. It was her own sunset, and she loved it.

**Word count:** 152 words

### Fluency Rubric

| Skill | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Accuracy** | Reads with 95%+ accuracy (no more than 7 errors in this passage); self-corrects most errors | Reads with 90-94% accuracy (8-15 errors); some self-correction | Reads with less than 90% accuracy (16+ errors); rarely self-corrects |
| **Rate** | Reads at a natural, conversational pace; smooth phrasing; groups words meaningfully | Mostly smooth but with some hesitations, word-by-word sections, or rushing | Reads word by word throughout, or so fast that meaning is lost |
| **Expression** | Voice changes for dialogue, questions, and exclamations; pauses at punctuation; sounds like natural speech | Some expression present but inconsistent; some punctuation awareness | Reads in a flat, monotone voice with no expression changes |

## Part 3: Comprehension (20 points)

**Instructions to facilitator:** The student reads the passage below independently. If the student's decoding is not yet strong enough to read independently, the facilitator may read the passage aloud while the student follows along. The student then answers the questions. Questions may be answered in writing or orally (facilitator records oral answers). For written answers, focus on the quality of the ideas, not spelling or grammar.

### Reading Passage: The River Crossing

> One warm spring morning, a mother duck led her five ducklings down to the river. The ducklings had never been in the water before. Four of them jumped in right away, splashing and paddling happily. But the smallest duckling, Pip, stood on the bank and shivered.
>
> "Come on, Pip!" called his brothers and sisters. "The water is fun!"
>
> Pip looked at the river. It was wide and the current was moving fast. "What if I get swept away?" he said in a small voice.
>
> Mother Duck swam back to the bank. "Watch me first," she said kindly. She swam in a slow circle near the shore. "See? Stay close to the edge where the water is calm. Use your feet to paddle. You were born to swim, Pip."
>
> Pip put one foot in the water. It was cold but not too bad. He put his other foot in. Then, slowly, he pushed off from the bank. His little feet started paddling, and suddenly — he was swimming!
>
> "I'm doing it!" Pip quacked.
>
> His brothers and sisters cheered. Mother Duck swam beside him. "I told you," she said. "You were born for this."
>
> Pip paddled around and around, getting braver with every stroke. By the time the sun was high in the sky, he was the last one to leave the water.
>
> "Can we come back tomorrow?" he asked.
>
> Mother Duck smiled. "Every day," she said.

**Word count:** 218 words

## Scoring Guide

### Part 3 Detailed Scoring

**Question 1 (2 points):**
- 2 points: Names at least two characters (Pip, Mother Duck, brothers/sisters, the ducklings)
- 1 point: Names only one character
- 0 points: Cannot identify any characters

**Question 2 (2 points):**
- 2 points: Identifies both where (a river/river bank) AND when (a warm spring morning)
- 1 point: Identifies where OR when but not both
- 0 points: Cannot identify the setting

**Question 3 (3 points):**
- 3 points: Retells all three parts with key details (beginning: ducklings go to river, Pip is scared; middle: Mother Duck shows Pip how to swim; end: Pip swims and loves it)
- 2 points: Retells two of three parts accurately
- 1 point: Retells only one part or retelling is out of order
- 0 points: Cannot retell the story

**Question 4 (3 points):**
- 3 points: Identifies a clear central message AND explains it with reference to the story (e.g., "You should try new things even when they are scary, because you might discover you are good at them. Pip was scared of the water but he ended up loving swimming.")
- 2 points: Identifies a reasonable central message but without strong explanation or text connection
- 1 point: States the topic ("it's about a duck") rather than the message, or gives a very vague message ("be brave")
- 0 points: Cannot identify a message

**Question 5 (2 points):**
- 2 points: Describes Pip at the beginning AND end with text evidence (e.g., "At the beginning Pip was scared — he shivered and said 'what if I get swept away.' At the end he was brave and happy — he was the last one to leave the water.")
- 1 point: Describes Pip at one point but not the change, or describes without evidence
- 0 points: Cannot describe the character

**Question 6 (2 points):**
- 2 points: Explains how the river creates the challenge/conflict (e.g., "The river is important because it was wide and the current was fast, which made Pip scared. Without the river, there would be no story because there would be nothing for Pip to be afraid of.")
- 1 point: Identifies the setting but does not explain its importance
- 0 points: Cannot connect setting to the story

**Question 7 (2 points):**
- 2 points: Identifies the cause with text evidence (the river was wide and the current was fast; Pip was afraid of being swept away)
- 1 point: General answer without text evidence ("he was scared")
- 0 points: Cannot identify the cause

**Question 8 (2 points):**
- 2 points: Identifies the effect clearly (Pip felt brave enough to try; he put his feet in and started swimming)
- 1 point: Partial answer
- 0 points: Cannot identify the effect

**Question 9 (4 points):**
- 4 points: Writes 2-3 sentences that (a) describe a personal experience, (b) connect it to Pip's experience, and (c) show reflection or insight
- 3 points: Writes 2-3 sentences with a personal connection but limited reflection
- 2 points: Writes 1-2 sentences with a basic connection
- 1 point: Writes something but does not connect to the story
- 0 points: No response or completely off-topic

## Mastery Thresholds

| Level | Score Range | Percentage | Meaning | Action |
|-------|-----------|------------|---------|--------|
| **Mastered** | 33-39 points | 85-100% | Student has strong command of Unit 1 skills | Advance to Unit 2; include Unit 1 skills in spaced review |
| **Approaching** | 27-32 points | 70-84% | Partial understanding with some gaps | Identify specific weak areas; provide targeted reteaching on those skills before advancing |
| **Developing** | Below 27 points | Below 70% | Significant gaps remain | Return to specific lessons for reteaching; reassess before advancing |

### Reteaching Recommendations by Skill Area

**If Part 1 (Decoding) is below 4/10:**
- Return to Lessons 1-2 for intensive phonics practice
- Create a daily word reading routine with r-controlled vowels, diphthongs, and multisyllable words
- The student may need additional Grade 1 phonics review before progressing

**If Part 2 (Fluency) is below 5/9:**
- Build in daily repeated reading practice (re-read short passages 3-4 times)
- Use echo reading for expression modeling
- If accuracy is the main issue, return to decoding (Lessons 1-2). Fluency builds on accuracy.
- If rate/expression is the issue, focus on punctuation awareness and repeated reading (Lesson 4)

**If Part 3 (Comprehension) is below 14/20:**
- Identify which question types the student missed:
  - Questions 1-2 (who/what/where/when): Reteach Lesson 5, focusing on going back to the text to find answers
  - Questions 3-4 (retelling/central message): Reteach Lesson 6, using simpler texts
  - Questions 5-6 (character/setting): Reteach Lesson 7, focusing on text evidence
  - Questions 7-8 (cause/effect): Reteach Lesson 8, using everyday cause-and-effect examples first
  - Question 9 (written response): Practice oral responses first, then scaffold with sentence frames

**If the student scores Mastered overall but has one weak area:**
- Address the weak area with targeted practice during warm-ups in Unit 2
- Include the weak skill in spaced review activities

### Part 3: Comprehension

**1.** Characters: Pip (the smallest duckling), Mother Duck, Pip's four brothers and sisters (the other ducklings). Award full credit for naming at least two.

**2.** Setting: A river (or river bank), on a warm spring morning.

**3.** Retelling:
- **Beginning:** A mother duck brought her five ducklings to the river for the first time. Four ducklings jumped right in, but the smallest duckling, Pip, was scared and would not get in the water.
- **Middle:** Mother Duck came back to the bank and showed Pip how to swim near the shore where the water was calm. She encouraged him and told him he was born to swim. Pip slowly put his feet in and started paddling.
- **End:** Pip discovered he loved swimming! He swam around and around and was the last one to leave the water. He asked if they could come back the next day.

**4.** Central message: You should try new things even when they scare you, because you might discover you are really good at them (or you might discover you love them). Pip learned that he was a strong swimmer even though he was afraid at first. / It is okay to be scared, but do not let fear stop you from trying. Mother Duck helped Pip take it one step at a time, and he ended up loving the water.

**5.** At the beginning, Pip was scared and timid — he shivered on the bank and said "What if I get swept away?" in a small voice. At the end, Pip was brave and enthusiastic — he was the last one to leave the water and asked to come back the next day. He changed from being the most fearful to being the most eager.

**6.** The river is important because it creates the challenge in the story. The river was wide and the current was fast, which is what scared Pip. If there were no river, Pip would not have had to face his fear. The setting also provides the solution — the calm water near the shore is where Pip was able to learn safely.

**7.** Pip did not want to get in because the river was wide, the current was moving fast, and he was afraid of being swept away. He had never been in the water before, which made it even scarier.

**8.** Because Mother Duck showed Pip how to swim near the calm shore, Pip felt brave enough to try. He put his feet in, pushed off from the bank, and started swimming. Without her showing him, he might have stayed on the bank.

**9.** Answers will vary. Award points based on the rubric criteria: personal experience, connection to Pip's experience, and reflection/insight. Example: "I was scared to ride my bike without training wheels. My dad ran beside me, just like Mother Duck swam beside Pip. When I finally did it, I felt so proud, just like Pip did when he started swimming."
