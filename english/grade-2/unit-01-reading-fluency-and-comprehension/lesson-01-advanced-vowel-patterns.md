# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 1, Lesson 1

## Topic
Advanced Vowel Patterns: R-Controlled Vowels and Diphthongs

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read words with r-controlled vowels (ar, er, ir, or, ur) accurately
- Read words with diphthongs (oi/oy, ou/ow) accurately
- Sort words by vowel pattern
- Decode multisyllabic words containing these patterns

## Prerequisites
- Knowledge of short and long vowel sounds
- Ability to read one-syllable CVC words (cat, sit, hop)
- Familiarity with common vowel teams from Grade 1 (ai, ay, ee, ea, oa)

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Index cards or small pieces of paper (for word sort activity)
- Word lists provided in this lesson (print or write out before the session)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review Grade 1 vowel patterns and activate prior knowledge.

**Activity: Quick Vowel Review**

Say to the student: "Before we learn some new vowel sounds today, let's warm up with sounds we already know."

Write these words one at a time. Ask the student to read each word:

1. **rain** (ai vowel team)
2. **boat** (oa vowel team)
3. **feet** (ee vowel team)
4. **play** (ay vowel team)
5. **read** (ea vowel team)

If the student reads them all correctly, say: "You remember your vowel teams from last year! Today we are going to learn some new vowel patterns that will help you read even more words."

If the student struggles, briefly review: "When two vowels are together, the first one usually says its name. Let's try again."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce r-controlled vowels and diphthongs as new vowel patterns.

### Key Vocabulary
- **R-controlled vowel:** When the letter *r* comes after a vowel, it changes the vowel sound — the *r* "bosses" the vowel around
- **Diphthong:** Two vowels that blend together to make a new, sliding sound (your mouth moves while you say it)

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1: R-Controlled Vowels (5 minutes)**

Say: "You know that vowels can make short sounds and long sounds. But when the letter *r* comes right after a vowel, something interesting happens — the *r* changes the sound! We call these 'bossy r' vowels."

Write each pattern and example word. Say the sound, then the word. Have the student repeat:

| Pattern | Sound | Example Words |
|---------|-------|---------------|
| **ar** | /ar/ as in "car" | car, star, farm, park |
| **er** | /er/ as in "her" | her, fern, after, water |
| **ir** | /er/ as in "bird" | bird, girl, first, stir |
| **or** | /or/ as in "corn" | corn, horn, storm, fort |
| **ur** | /er/ as in "burn" | burn, turn, nurse, hurt |

Say: "Notice that *er*, *ir*, and *ur* all make the same sound! That can be tricky, but the more you read, the more you will remember which spelling goes with which word."

**Part 2: Diphthongs (5 minutes)**

Say: "Now let's learn another kind of vowel pattern. These are called diphthongs — that is a big word that means the sound slides from one vowel to another. Listen carefully and watch my mouth."

Write each pattern and example word:

| Pattern | Sound | Example Words |
|---------|-------|---------------|
| **oi** | /oy/ as in "coin" | coin, oil, join, point |
| **oy** | /oy/ as in "boy" | boy, toy, joy, enjoy |
| **ou** | /ow/ as in "cloud" | cloud, house, loud, mouth |
| **ow** | /ow/ as in "cow" | cow, town, down, brown |

Say: "Notice that *oi* and *oy* make the same sound. And *ou* and *ow* make the same sound. Just like *er*, *ir*, and *ur* — different spellings can make the same sound!"

**Important note for the facilitator:** The pattern *ow* can also say /oh/ as in "snow" and "grow." Acknowledge this if the student brings it up: "Great thinking! The letters *ow* can make two different sounds — /ow/ as in 'cow' and /oh/ as in 'snow.' Today we are focusing on the /ow/ sound."

### Worked Examples

Write the word **barnyard** on paper.

Say: "Let's try reading a bigger word together. I see two parts: *barn* and *yard*. I know *ar* says /ar/. So *barn* is /barn/. And *yard* — I see *ar* again — /yard/. The word is *barnyard*!"

Write the word **joyful** on paper.

Say: "Here is another one. I see *joy* — that has the /oy/ diphthong — and *ful*. Put them together: *joyful*. It means full of joy!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice reading words with the new patterns with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Read the Words (4 minutes)**

Present these words one at a time. Have the student read each one. If they struggle, help them find the vowel pattern first.

1. shark
2. nurse
3. point
4. flower
5. thirty
6. storm
7. enjoy
8. church
9. around
10. morning

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles, say: "Can you find the vowel pattern in this word? Underline it. Now what sound does that pattern make? Good — now read the whole word."*

*If the student struggles with identifying the pattern: Cover part of the word and reveal it in chunks. For example, for "flower," cover "er" and show "flow," then reveal the whole word.*

**Activity 2: Word Sort (6 minutes)**

Write these 12 words on index cards (or small pieces of paper), one word per card:

star, bird, house, toy, corn, turn, coin, brown, park, girl, loud, joy

Create column headers on paper:

| ar | ir/ur | or | oi/oy | ou/ow |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Say: "Let's sort these words by their vowel pattern. Read each word, find the vowel pattern, and put it in the right column."

Work through the first two together, then let the student continue. Help as needed.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student places a word in the wrong column, ask: "What vowel sound do you hear in that word? Which column has words with the same sound?" Guide them to self-correct.*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads and sorts words independently to build fluency with the new patterns.

**Activity: Read, Sort, and Write**

Have the student read each word below, identify the vowel pattern, and write it in the correct column on their paper. Then have them write one more word of their own for each column (they can use words from the lesson or think of new ones).

### Words to Sort

1. card
2. dirt
3. order
4. round
5. enjoy
6. purse
7. start
8. boil
9. tower
10. short

### Difficulty Tiers
- ★ Sort the 10 words above into the correct vowel pattern columns (with the column headers visible as a reference)
- ★★ Sort the 10 words AND write one additional word for each vowel pattern column
- ★★★ Sort the 10 words, write one additional word for each column, AND use three of the words in sentences

### Interleaved Review
After completing the sort, have the student read these Grade 1 review words:

11. beach (ea)
12. train (ai)
13. goat (oa)

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess whether the student can read words with r-controlled vowels and diphthongs.

**Exit Ticket:**

Have the student read these six words aloud (they have not seen these specific words in today's lesson):

1. sharp (ar)
2. skirt (ir)
3. border (or + er)
4. coil (oi)
5. clown (ow)
6. purple (ur)

**Mastery = Student reads at least 5 out of 6 words correctly.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which pattern(s) the student struggled with
- Make flashcards for those patterns and practice daily for 2-3 minutes
- Before the next lesson, do a brief review of the difficult pattern(s) using 5-6 additional words
- Repeat the word sort activity, focusing on the tricky pattern(s)

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 2 (Multisyllable Decoding) next session. Continue to include r-controlled vowels and diphthongs in warm-up reviews in future lessons.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who master the patterns quickly:**
- **Word Hunt:** Look through a book or around the house for words with r-controlled vowels and diphthongs. Write down five words you find and sort them.
- **Silly Sentences:** Write a sentence that uses as many r-controlled vowel words as possible. Example: "The girl in a purple shirt turned to stir the corn on the farm."
- **Pattern Flip:** The student writes a word with one pattern (e.g., "barn") and then changes the vowel pattern to make a new word or nonsense word (e.g., "bern," "born," "burn"). Discuss which ones are real words.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** If the student is solid on r-controlled vowels but struggles with diphthongs (or vice versa), you can split this lesson into two sessions — one for each pattern type.

**Common confusion — ow:** The letters *ow* can say /ow/ (cow) or /oh/ (snow). If the student reads "cow" as "koh," say: "That is one sound *ow* can make. In this word, it makes the other sound — /ow/. Try it." Over time, context and familiarity help students choose the right pronunciation.

**Common confusion — er/ir/ur:** These three patterns all make the same sound, which is confusing for spelling. For reading, the student just needs to know that any of these patterns says /er/. For spelling, they will need more time and practice in later units. For now, focus on reading, not spelling.

**Growth mindset note:** If the student feels overwhelmed by all the patterns, say: "There are a lot of vowel patterns in English! No one learns them all in one day. Every time you read, your brain gets better at recognizing these patterns. You are building your word power right now."

**Connection to comprehension:** Strong decoding frees up brainpower for understanding. The work in this lesson and the next directly supports the comprehension skills in Lessons 5-10. Help the student see the connection: "The better you get at reading words quickly, the more you can focus on understanding the story."
