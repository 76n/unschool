# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 1, Lesson 2

## Topic
Multisyllable Decoding: Compound Words, Prefixes, and Suffixes

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Break compound words into two smaller words and read them
- Identify and read words with common prefixes (un-, re-)
- Identify and read words with common suffixes (-ing, -ed, -ly, -ful)
- Apply the VCCV syllable division pattern to decode two-syllable words

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: Advanced Vowel Patterns (r-controlled vowels and diphthongs)
- Ability to read one-syllable words fluently
- Basic understanding that words are made up of parts

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Index cards or small pieces of paper (for word building)
- Scissors (optional, for cutting words apart)

**Purpose:** Review Lesson 1 vowel patterns and Grade 1 phonics skills.

**Activity: Vowel Pattern Quick Read**

Write these words one at a time. Ask the student to read each word and identify the vowel pattern:

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach three strategies for decoding multisyllabic words.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Compound word:** A word made by putting two smaller words together (e.g., sun + flower = sunflower)
- **Prefix:** A word part added to the beginning of a word that changes its meaning
- **Suffix:** A word part added to the end of a word that changes its meaning
- **Syllable:** A word part with one vowel sound — like a "beat" in a word

### Instruction Notes

**Strategy 1: Compound Words (3 minutes)**

Say: "Some big words are really just two small words stuck together. If you can read the small words, you can read the big word!"

Write these compound words. Cover the second part, have the student read the first part, then reveal the second part:

| Word 1 | + | Word 2 | = | Compound Word |
|--------|---|--------|---|---------------|
| sun | + | light | = | **sunlight** |
| rain | + | coat | = | **raincoat** |
| bed | + | room | = | **bedroom** |
| play | + | ground | = | **playground** |
| cup | + | cake | = | **cupcake** |

Say: "When you see a long word, always check — is it two words put together?"

**Strategy 2: Prefixes (3 minutes)**

Say: "Sometimes a small part is added to the beginning of a word to change its meaning. These are called prefixes."

Write the prefix, its meaning, and examples:

**un- means "not" or "the opposite of":**
- happy → **unhappy** (not happy)
- do → **undo** (the opposite of do)
- kind → **unkind** (not kind)
- tie → **untie** (the opposite of tie)

**re- means "again":**
- read → **reread** (read again)
- do → **redo** (do again)
- fill → **refill** (fill again)
- play → **replay** (play again)

Say: "When you see *un-* or *re-* at the beginning of a word, cover it up. Read the base word first. Then put the prefix back on."

**Strategy 3: Suffixes (3 minutes)**

Say: "Small parts can also be added to the end of a word. These are called suffixes."

Write the suffix, its meaning, and examples:

**-ing (happening now):** jump → **jumping**, read → **reading**, play → **playing**

**-ed (happened in the past):** jump → **jumped**, play → **played**, want → **wanted**

**-ly (in a ___ way):** slow → **slowly**, quick → **quickly**, soft → **softly**

**-ful (full of):** help → **helpful**, joy → **joyful**, care → **careful**

Say: "When you see a suffix at the end of a word, cover it up. Read the base word first. Then add the suffix."

**Strategy 4: VCCV Syllable Division (3 minutes)**

Say: "For some two-syllable words, there is a trick. When you see two consonants in the middle of a word between two vowels, you can split the word between those consonants."

Write the pattern: **V-C-C-V → split between the two C's**

Examples (draw a line between the syllables):

- nap | kin → **napkin** (a is the first vowel, i is the second vowel, split between p and k)
- bas | ket → **basket**
- rab | bit → **rabbit**
- pup | pet → **puppet**
- gar | den → **garden**

Say: "Find the two consonants in the middle. Split between them. Read each part. Put them together!"

### Worked Examples

Write the word **uncomfortable** on paper.

Say: "This is a really big word! But let's break it apart. I see the prefix *un-* at the beginning. I see the suffix *-able* at the end. The base word in the middle is *comfort.* So the word is *un-comfort-able*: uncomfortable!"

Write the word **replaying** on paper.

Say: "I see the prefix *re-* and the suffix *-ing*. The base word is *play*. So the word is *re-play-ing*: replaying!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice breaking apart multisyllabic words with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Break It Apart (5 minutes)**

Present these words one at a time. For each word, ask the student: "What parts do you see? Can you break this word apart and then read it?"

1. **sunshine** (compound: sun + shine)
2. **unkind** (prefix: un + kind)
3. **reading** (suffix: read + ing)
4. **hopeful** (suffix: hope + ful)
5. **redo** (prefix: re + do)
6. **baseball** (compound: base + ball)
7. **quickly** (suffix: quick + ly)
8. **unpack** (prefix: un + pack)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles, ask: "Do you see a small word hiding inside this big word?" or "Do you see un- or re- at the beginning?" or "Do you see -ing, -ed, -ly, or -ful at the end?"*

*If the student struggles with recognizing the base word after removing affixes: Write the base word alone first, let them read it, then add the prefix or suffix back.*

**Activity 2: VCCV Practice (5 minutes)**

Write these words. Help the student find the two consonants in the middle and draw a line to split the word:

1. **kitten** (kit | ten)
2. **number** (num | ber)
3. **picnic** (pic | nic)
4. **winter** (win | ter)
5. **happen** (hap | pen)

For each word, say: "Find the two vowels. Find the two consonants between them. Split between the consonants. Read each part. Put it together!"

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles with finding the vowels, underline the vowels first in a different color, then find the consonants between them.*

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess whether the student can decode multisyllabic words using the strategies taught.

**Exit Ticket:**

Have the student read these six words aloud. They have not practiced these specific words today:

1. **doorbell** (compound word)
2. **rewrite** (prefix: re-)
3. **thankful** (suffix: -ful)
4. **sitting** (suffix: -ing, with doubled consonant)
5. **unlock** (prefix: un-)
6. **mitten** (VCCV pattern)

For each word, ask: "How did you figure that word out?" (The student should be able to name the strategy: "I saw two words," "I found the prefix," etc.)

**Mastery = Student reads at least 5 out of 6 words correctly AND can explain the strategy for at least 3 of them.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which strategy the student needs more practice with (compound words, prefixes, suffixes, or VCCV)
- Create a practice set of 8-10 words using that strategy
- Practice for 5 minutes at the start of the next session before moving on
- If the student struggles with all strategies, spend an extra session on this lesson

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (Reading Accuracy and Self-Correction) next session. Continue to include multisyllabic words in warm-up reviews.

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who master the strategies quickly:**
- **Compound Word Brainstorm:** How many compound words can you think of in 2 minutes? Write them all down. (Examples: football, airplane, homework, birthday, popcorn...)
- **Prefix/Suffix Swap:** Start with a base word like "play." How many new words can you make by adding prefixes and suffixes? (play, replay, playing, played, playful, replaying, replayed...)
- **Word Builder:** The student creates new words by combining base words with different prefixes and suffixes. Which combinations make real words? Which make silly nonsense words?

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson covers a lot of ground. If the student needs more time, split it into two sessions: Session A covers compound words and prefixes/suffixes, Session B covers VCCV division and combined practice.

**Common issue — doubled consonants with suffixes:** When -ing or -ed is added to a short vowel word, the final consonant is often doubled (sit → sitting, hop → hopped). The student does not need to know the spelling rule yet, but they should recognize that "tt" in "sitting" still makes one /t/ sound. If they try to read "sit-ting" as two separate /t/ sounds, gently model the correct blending.

**Common issue — past tense -ed:** The suffix -ed makes three different sounds: /d/ (played), /t/ (jumped), /id/ (wanted). The student may try to always pronounce it as "ed." When this happens, say: "Let's try all three sounds and see which one sounds like a real word you have heard before." This self-correction through oral language is the most effective approach.

**Growth mindset note:** Say to the student: "Big words are not harder words — they are just words with more parts. When you know how to break a word apart, you can read almost anything. You are becoming a word detective!"

**Connection to fluency:** Decoding multisyllabic words quickly is essential for reading fluency. The strategies learned in this lesson will be applied immediately in Lesson 3 and throughout the rest of the unit.
