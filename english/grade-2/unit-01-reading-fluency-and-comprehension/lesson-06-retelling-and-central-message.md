# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 1, Lesson 6

## Topic
Retelling a Story and Identifying the Central Message

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Retell a story using beginning, middle, and end
- Identify the central message or lesson of a story
- Answer the question "What did the character learn?" to determine the message
- Use a sequencing graphic organizer to organize a retelling

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 5: Asking and Answering Questions (who, what, where, when, why, how)
- Ability to read a short passage and answer basic comprehension questions

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Crayons or colored pencils (optional, for illustrating)
- Reading passage provided in this lesson (print or display)
- Scissors (optional, for sequencing cut-and-sort activity)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review question-answering skills from Lesson 5 and activate retelling skills.

**Activity: Quick Recall**

Say: "Remember the story from last lesson about the sharing tree? Without looking at the story, can you tell me..."

1. **Who** was the story about? (Lily)
2. **What** happened to the tree? (A big branch broke off)
3. **How** did the story end? (The tree grew new branches and was going to be fine)

If the student remembers, say: "Great! You just retold part of the story from memory. Today we are going to learn how to retell a whole story from beginning to end."

If the student does not remember, briefly recap the story and say: "That is okay! Today we are going to learn a strategy that helps you remember stories better."

**Decoding Review:** Have the student read these words quickly:
- **carefully** (Lessons 1-2: suffix -ful, suffix -ly)
- **restarted** (Lesson 2: prefix re-, suffix -ed)

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach retelling structure and how to find the central message.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Retell:** To tell a story again in your own words, including the important parts
- **Beginning:** The start of the story — who is in it, where it happens, what the problem or situation is
- **Middle:** The main events — what happens, how the character tries to solve the problem
- **End:** How the story finishes — how the problem is solved, how the character feels
- **Central message:** The lesson or big idea the story teaches — what the character (and the reader) can learn

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Teach the B-M-E structure (4 minutes)**

Say: "Every story has three parts: a beginning, a middle, and an end. When you retell a story, you tell the most important parts in order."

Draw a simple three-box graphic organizer on paper:

```
[ Beginning ]  →  [ Middle ]  →  [ End ]
```

Say: "Let me show you with a story you probably know."

Use a familiar example (adapt if needed):

"Think about the story of the three little pigs."

- **Beginning:** Three little pigs each built a house — one of straw, one of sticks, one of bricks. A wolf came.
- **Middle:** The wolf blew down the straw house and the stick house. Those pigs ran to the brick house.
- **End:** The wolf could not blow down the brick house. The pigs were safe.

Say: "I did not tell every single detail. I told the most important parts. That is what retelling is — the big, important parts in order."

**Step 2: Teach the central message (3 minutes)**

Say: "The central message is the lesson the story teaches. It is the big idea — what the character learned, and what we can learn from the story."

Ask: "In the three little pigs, what is the lesson?" (Guide toward: It is worth it to do things the right way, even if it takes more time and work.)

Say: "To find the central message, ask yourself these questions:"
- "What did the character learn?"
- "How did the character change from the beginning to the end?"
- "What would I learn if this happened to me?"

Write these on paper as a reference.

**Step 3: Model with a short passage (3 minutes)**

Read this short passage aloud:

> **The Messy Desk**
>
> Ryan's desk was always messy. Papers, crayons, and books were piled everywhere. One day, he could not find his homework. He looked and looked, but it was lost in the mess.
>
> His teacher said, "Ryan, if you keep your desk tidy, you will always know where things are."
>
> That afternoon, Ryan cleaned his whole desk. He put papers in one pile, crayons in a cup, and books in a stack. The next morning, he found his homework right away.
>
> "That was easy!" Ryan said with a smile.

Model the retelling:

- **Beginning:** "Ryan's desk was always messy, and one day he could not find his homework."
- **Middle:** "His teacher told him to keep his desk tidy. Ryan cleaned his whole desk and organized everything."
- **End:** "The next morning, Ryan found his homework right away and was happy."

Model finding the central message: "What did Ryan learn? He learned that keeping things organized makes life easier. The central message is: staying organized helps you find what you need."

### Worked Examples

Say: "Notice that I did not retell every word. I used my own words and only included the important parts. And to find the message, I asked: 'What did Ryan learn?' That question is your best tool for finding the central message."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice retelling and finding the central message together.

**Activity: Read, Retell, and Find the Message**

Say: "Now let's try one together. We will read a story, retell it using beginning, middle, and end, and find the central message."

**Passage: The Talent Show**

> Maya wanted to sing in the school talent show, but she was very nervous. Her hands shook and her voice wobbled when she thought about standing on stage.
>
> "I can't do it," Maya told her grandma. "Everyone will laugh at me."
>
> "Nonsense," said Grandma. "Let's practice together every day this week."
>
> So they did. Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday — every day after school, Maya sang her song while Grandma listened. Each day, her voice got a little stronger and a little steadier.
>
> On Saturday, Maya walked onto the stage. Her heart was beating fast. She took a deep breath and started to sing. Her voice filled the room, clear and strong. When she finished, everyone clapped.
>
> Maya ran to Grandma and hugged her. "I did it!" she said.
>
> "You sure did," said Grandma. "Practice makes all the difference."

**Step 1: Retell together**

Draw the B-M-E organizer. Say: "Let's fill this in together."

Ask: "What happened in the beginning?" Help the student identify: Maya wanted to sing in the talent show but was nervous.

Ask: "What happened in the middle?" Help the student identify: Grandma helped her practice every day, and Maya got better.

Ask: "What happened at the end?" Help the student identify: Maya sang on stage, everyone clapped, and she was proud.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student includes too many details, say: "That is all true! But for a retelling, we want just the most important parts. What is the one big thing that happened in the middle?"*

*If the student skips important events: Ask guiding questions: "What did Grandma do? How did that help Maya?"*

**Step 2: Find the central message**

Ask: "What did Maya learn?"

Guide the student: "At the beginning, Maya said 'I can't do it.' At the end, she said 'I did it!' What changed? What helped her?"

Expected answer (in student's words): Practice helps you get better / If you practice, you can do hard things / Do not give up when something is hard.

Say: "That is the central message! The story teaches us that practicing helps us get better, even when something feels scary at first."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student retells a story and identifies the central message independently.

**Activity: Read, Retell, and Write**

The student reads the following passage independently, then completes the activities below.

**Passage: Two Cats and a Blanket**

> Pepper and Ginger were two cats who lived in the same house. One cold night, there was only one warm blanket on the couch. Pepper sat on one end. Ginger sat on the other end.
>
> "This is MY blanket," said Pepper, pulling it toward herself.
>
> "No, it is MY blanket," said Ginger, pulling it back.
>
> They pulled and pulled. Suddenly — RIP! The blanket tore right in half. Now neither cat had a warm blanket. They both shivered in the cold.
>
> "This was silly," said Pepper. "If we had shared, we both could have been warm."
>
> "You are right," said Ginger. "Let's put the two halves together and share."
>
> They pushed the two pieces close together and curled up side by side. It was not perfect, but it was warm enough. And from that night on, Pepper and Ginger always shared the blanket.

**Part 1: Retell the story** — Fill in the B-M-E organizer (the student draws or uses the one below):

- **Beginning:** ___
- **Middle:** ___
- **End:** ___

**Part 2: Central message** — Answer these questions:
1. What did Pepper and Ginger learn?
2. What is the central message (lesson) of this story?

**Part 3 (if time permits):** Write 1-2 sentences retelling the story in your own words.

### Difficulty Tiers
- ★ Retell the story orally to the facilitator using the B-M-E structure. Answer the central message questions orally.
- ★★ Write the B-M-E retelling in words or short phrases. Write the central message in one sentence.
- ★★★ Write the B-M-E retelling in complete sentences. Write the central message AND explain how you figured it out ("I know this because...").

### Interleaved Review
After completing the retelling, have the student answer these review questions (from Lesson 5):
- **Who** are the two characters? (Pepper and Ginger)
- **Where** does the story take place? (In a house, on a couch)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess whether the student can retell a story and identify the central message.

**Exit Ticket:**

Tell the student this very short story (or have them read it):

> Sam wanted the last cookie, but so did his sister Mia. They argued about it. Then Sam had an idea. He broke the cookie in half and gave one piece to Mia. They both smiled.

Ask:
1. "Retell this story — what happened in the beginning, middle, and end?"
2. "What is the central message or lesson?"

**Mastery = Student retells with all three parts (beginning, middle, end) in order AND identifies a reasonable central message (e.g., sharing is better than fighting, sharing makes both people happy).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice retelling with very familiar stories (fairy tales, stories the student knows well) — familiarity with the content lets the student focus on the retelling structure
- Use visual support: draw three boxes and have the student draw what happened in each (beginning, middle, end), then tell the story from the pictures
- For central message difficulty, always start with: "What did the character learn?" If the student says "I don't know," prompt: "How was the character different at the end compared to the beginning?"
- Repeat this lesson with a different short passage before moving on

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (Character and Setting) next session. Continue to ask "Can you retell that story?" and "What was the lesson?" during all future reading activities.

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who retell and identify messages easily:**
- **Message Match:** Think of another story (one you have read or a movie you have seen) that has the same message as "Two Cats and a Blanket." What is the same about the messages?
- **Draw the Story:** Draw three pictures (beginning, middle, end) and write a sentence under each picture. This creates a visual retelling.
- **Create Your Own:** Think of a central message (like "be kind to others" or "don't give up"). Make up a very short story (3-4 sentences) that teaches that message.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** Retelling and central message are two related but distinct skills. If the student needs more time, split this lesson into two sessions: Session A focuses on retelling (B-M-E), and Session B focuses on central message. Retelling is the foundation — the student needs to be able to retell before they can identify the message.

**Common issue — retelling every detail:** Some students retell by recounting every single sentence. This is actually a positive sign — it shows they understood the story. Gently guide them toward summarizing: "You remembered all of that! Now, can you tell me just the three most important parts?" Over time, they will learn to distinguish main events from details.

**Common issue — confusing the topic with the central message:** Students may say the message of "Two Cats and a Blanket" is "two cats and a blanket" or "sharing a blanket." Clarify: "That is what the story is about (the topic). The central message is what the story teaches us. What lesson can we learn?" The distinction between topic and message takes repeated practice.

**Common issue — stating the message too broadly or too narrowly:** A student might say "be nice" (too broad) or "share blankets" (too narrow). Both are reasonable attempts. Help them refine: "Be nice is a great idea! Can you be more specific about what kind of 'nice'? What did the cats learn to do?" Guide toward: "Sharing with others is better than fighting over things."

**Growth mindset note:** Say: "Retelling a story is like being a storyteller yourself! Every time you practice retelling, you get better at remembering important parts. And when you find the message of a story, you are thinking like a really deep reader."

## Answer Key

**Independent Practice:**

**Part 1 — B-M-E Retelling:**
- **Beginning:** Pepper and Ginger both wanted the only warm blanket. They each sat on one end of it.
- **Middle:** They pulled the blanket back and forth and it ripped in half. Now neither cat had a warm blanket and they were cold.
- **End:** They decided to share by putting the two halves together and curling up side by side. From then on, they always shared.

**Part 2 — Central Message:**
1. Pepper and Ginger learned that sharing is better than fighting. When they fought over the blanket, they both lost. When they shared, they were both warm.
2. Central message: Sharing with others is better than fighting over something. When you share, everyone benefits. (Accept any reasonable version of this idea.)

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Retelling: Beginning — Sam and Mia both wanted the last cookie and argued. Middle — Sam had an idea to break the cookie in half. End — He gave one piece to Mia and they both smiled.
2. Central message: Sharing is a good way to solve a problem. / When you share, everyone is happy. (Accept reasonable variations.)
