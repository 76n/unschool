# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 2, Lesson 5

> **Methodology Reference:** [Teaching Methodology Framework](../../../methodology/teaching-methodology-framework.md)

## Topic
Introduction to Opinion Writing

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define an opinion and distinguish it from a fact
- Identify the four parts of an opinion piece: topic sentence stating an opinion, first reason, second reason, and closing sentence
- Use the Opinion Writing Template to plan an opinion piece orally
- Write a short opinion piece (4-6 sentences) with a clear opinion, two reasons, and a closing

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of how reasons support points in informational text (Lesson 3 of this unit)
- Ability to identify an author's point and reasons (Lesson 3 of this unit)
- Basic sentence-writing skills (can write complete sentences with a capital letter and ending punctuation)
- Familiarity with the point-reason connection

## Materials Needed
- Opinion Writing Template (printed or drawn on paper — see template in instruction section)
- Opinion Writing Anchor Chart (blank poster or whiteboard space)
- "Fact or Opinion?" sorting cards (8-10 index cards with pre-written statements)
- Lined writing paper
- Pencils and an eraser
- Colored pencils for illustrating (optional)
- Model opinion piece poster or chart paper (for the teacher-written model)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Help the student understand the difference between a fact and an opinion, which is essential before they begin writing their own opinions.

**Activity: Fact or Opinion?**

Say: *"Before we start writing today, we need to understand something important. There are two kinds of statements people make: facts and opinions. A fact is something that is TRUE and can be proven. An opinion is what someone THINKS or BELIEVES — other people might think differently."*

Read each statement aloud. The student responds "Fact!" or "Opinion!" after each one.

1. "The sun is a star." (Fact)
2. "Pizza is the best food in the world." (Opinion)
3. "Dogs have four legs." (Fact)
4. "Summer is the most fun season." (Opinion)
5. "There are seven days in a week." (Fact)
6. "Chocolate ice cream tastes better than vanilla." (Opinion)

After the activity, say: *"Did you notice something? The opinions used words like 'best,' 'most fun,' and 'better.' Those are clue words that tell us someone is sharing what they THINK, not what is always true for everyone. Today, we are going to learn how to write our own opinions — and how to give REASONS to make our opinions strong, just like the authors we studied in Lesson 3."*

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the structure of an opinion piece and introduce the Opinion Writing Template, then model writing an opinion piece together from start to finish.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Opinion** — What someone thinks or believes; not everyone may agree
- **Fact** — Something that is true and can be proven
- **Topic sentence** — The first sentence of a piece of writing that tells the reader what the writing is about; in opinion writing, the topic sentence states the opinion
- **Reason** — An explanation for WHY you hold your opinion; reasons support your opinion
- **Closing sentence** — The last sentence that wraps up the writing and restates the opinion in a different way
- **Opinion Writing Template** — A planning tool with four parts: opinion, reason 1, reason 2, closing

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Connect to what students already know (2 minutes)**

Say: *"In Lesson 3, we learned that authors make points and give reasons to support those points. Remember the recycling passage? The author's point was that recycling is important, and then the author gave three reasons why."*

Point to the block tower analogy from Lesson 3 (if still visible): *"The point was on top, and the reasons held it up. Today, YOU are going to be the author! You are going to write YOUR point — your opinion — and give reasons to hold it up."*

**Step 2: Introduce the four parts of an opinion piece (3 minutes)**

Say: *"An opinion piece has four parts. Let me show you."*

Write the Opinion Writing Template on the board:

---

**Opinion Writing Template**

1. **Topic Sentence (My Opinion):** I think ___ because...
2. **Reason 1:** One reason is...
3. **Reason 2:** Another reason is...
4. **Closing Sentence:** That is why I think ___.

---

Say: *"Part 1 is the topic sentence. This is where you tell the reader your opinion right away. You do not hide it or save it for the end. You say it up front: 'I think ___ because...'*"

Say: *"Parts 2 and 3 are your reasons. Each reason explains WHY you hold your opinion. These are the blocks that hold up your opinion. You need at least two reasons to make your opinion strong."*

Say: *"Part 4 is the closing sentence. This is where you wrap up your writing and remind the reader of your opinion. You say it again, but in a slightly different way."*

**Step 3: Model writing an opinion piece together (5-6 minutes)**

Say: *"Now I am going to write an opinion piece, and I want you to help me. We are going to write about this topic: What is the best part of the school day?"*

Say: *"I think recess is the best part of the school day. That is my opinion. Not everyone will agree — some people might think lunch or reading time is the best. But I have reasons for my opinion. Let me use the template."*

**Model — think aloud as you write each part on the chart paper:**

**Part 1 — Topic Sentence:**

Say: *"First, I write my topic sentence. I need to state my opinion clearly."*

Write: **I think recess is the best part of the school day because it is fun and it helps students feel their best.**

Say: *"See? I told the reader exactly what I think. I said 'I think' so they know it is my opinion, and I gave a preview of why."*

**Part 2 — Reason 1:**

Say: *"Now I need my first reason. WHY do I think recess is the best? One reason is that recess gives students a chance to run, play, and use their energy. Let me write that."*

Write: **One reason is that recess gives students a chance to run, play, and use their energy. After sitting in a classroom, kids need to move their bodies. Running around at recess makes us feel happy and awake.**

Say: *"Notice that I did not just say 'Recess is fun.' I explained WHY it is fun — kids get to run and play and use their energy. Good reasons give details."*

**Part 3 — Reason 2:**

Say: *"Now I need my second reason. Another reason I think recess is the best is that playing with friends at recess helps students learn to share and take turns."*

Write: **Another reason is that recess helps students learn to share and take turns. When kids play games together, they practice being kind and working as a team. These are important skills that we use every day.**

Say: *"My second reason is different from my first. It gives a NEW explanation for my opinion. I should not say the same thing twice."*

**Part 4 — Closing Sentence:**

Say: *"Finally, I wrap it up. I remind the reader of my opinion, but I say it in a different way."*

Write: **That is why I believe recess is the most important part of the school day. It keeps us active and teaches us how to be good friends.**

Say: *"Look! I restated my opinion — but instead of saying 'the best,' I said 'the most important.' I also reminded the reader of my two reasons. That is a strong closing."*

Read the full piece aloud from start to finish. Then say: *"Let's count the parts. Topic sentence with my opinion? Yes. Reason 1? Yes. Reason 2? Yes. Closing that restates the opinion? Yes. Four parts — we have a complete opinion piece!"*

### Worked Examples

**Worked Example 1: A Complete Opinion Piece**

Read this opinion piece aloud:

> I think cats are the best pets because they are easy to care for. One reason is that cats clean themselves, so you do not have to give them baths very often. Another reason is that cats do not need to be taken on walks — they are happy playing inside the house. That is why I think cats make wonderful pets for families.

Say: *"Let me find the four parts. The topic sentence is 'I think cats are the best pets because they are easy to care for.' That states the opinion. Reason 1 is that cats clean themselves. Reason 2 is that cats do not need walks. The closing is 'That is why I think cats make wonderful pets for families.' All four parts are here, and each reason explains WHY cats are the best pets."*

**Worked Example 2: An Opinion Piece with a Missing Part**

Read this piece aloud:

> I think summer is the best season. One reason is that you get to swim in the pool. Another reason is that you do not have to wake up early for school.

Say: *"Let me check the parts. Topic sentence stating the opinion? Yes — 'I think summer is the best season.' Reason 1? Yes — swimming in the pool. Reason 2? Yes — not waking up early. But wait... where is the closing sentence? There is no sentence at the end that wraps it up and restates the opinion. This piece is missing its closing. It needs one more sentence, like: 'That is why summer is my favorite time of year.' Without the closing, the piece feels unfinished — like a song that stops before the last note."*

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** The student plans and orally rehearses an opinion piece using the Opinion Writing Template, then begins writing with facilitator support.

**Activity: Plan and Write — "My Favorite ___"**

**Step 1: Choose a topic (2 minutes)**

Say: *"Now it is your turn to write an opinion piece! First, you need to pick a topic. Here are some choices. You can also pick your own topic."*

Offer these options:
- My favorite animal is ___ because...
- I think ___ is the best game to play because...
- The best season is ___ because...
- My favorite book is ___ because...

Allow the student to choose. If they struggle to decide, help them pick one and say: *"Remember, there is no wrong answer — it is YOUR opinion!"*

**Step 2: Oral rehearsal using the template (3-4 minutes)**

Say: *"Before we write anything down, let's practice saying it out loud. I will hold up the template, and you fill in each part by talking."*

Guide the student through each part orally:

**Part 1:** *"Tell me your opinion. Start with 'I think...' What do you think and why?"*

If the student says something vague like "I like dogs," prompt: *"That is great! Now add 'because' and give a preview. Why do you think dogs are the best?"*

**Part 2:** *"Now give me your first reason. Start with 'One reason is...' Why do you hold this opinion?"*

If the reason is thin (e.g., "because they are cool"), prompt: *"What makes them cool? Can you tell me more about that?"*

**Part 3:** *"Now give me a DIFFERENT reason. Start with 'Another reason is...' Think of something new."*

If the student repeats their first reason, say: *"That is similar to your first reason. Can you think of a different reason — something else that supports your opinion?"*

**Part 4:** *"Now wrap it up. Start with 'That is why I think...' Restate your opinion in a slightly different way."*

Say: *"Excellent! You just planned your whole opinion piece by talking it through. Now let's write it down."*

**Step 3: Writing with support (4-5 minutes)**

Give the student lined writing paper and the Opinion Writing Template as a reference.

Say: *"Write your opinion piece using the same words you practiced out loud. I will be right here if you need help."*

As the student writes, offer support:
- If they forget what to write: *"Look at the template. What part comes next?"*
- If they struggle with spelling: Help with the word or say *"Sound it out — what letter do you hear first?"* (Do not let spelling frustration stop the flow of ideas.)
- If they write very short reasons: *"Can you add one more sentence that tells me more about that reason?"*
- If they skip the closing: *"You are almost done! You need one more sentence to wrap it up. Start with 'That is why I think...'"*

When the student finishes, read the piece aloud together. Celebrate the accomplishment!

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students demonstrate their ability to write an opinion piece independently or strengthen their piece through revision.

### Difficulty Tiers

**★ (On-level):**
Re-read the opinion piece you wrote during Guided Practice. Check it against this checklist:
- [ ] My topic sentence states my opinion clearly.
- [ ] I have at least two reasons that are DIFFERENT from each other.
- [ ] Each reason explains WHY I hold my opinion.
- [ ] My closing sentence restates my opinion.
- [ ] Each sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with punctuation.

If any part is missing, add it now. Then draw a picture that goes with your opinion piece.

**★★ (Stretch):**
Complete the On-level checklist, then:
1. Add a THIRD reason to your opinion piece. Write it between Reason 2 and the closing sentence. Start with "Also,..." or "A third reason is..."
2. Write one sentence explaining what the difference is between a fact and an opinion. Give one example of each.

**★★★ (Challenge):**
Complete the On-level and Stretch tasks, then:
3. Write a completely NEW opinion piece on a different topic from the one you chose in Guided Practice. Use the Opinion Writing Template. Your new piece should have:
   - A topic sentence stating your opinion
   - Two or three reasons
   - A closing sentence
   - At least 5 sentences total

### Interleaved Review
**Review from Lesson 4 — Asking Questions:**
4. Imagine someone is going to read YOUR opinion piece. Write one question they might ask BEFORE reading it (based on the title or topic) and one question they might ask AFTER reading it.

## Check for Understanding (3-5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment to determine whether the student understands opinion writing structure.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. What is the difference between a fact and an opinion? Write one sentence for each. (2 points — 1 for correct definition of fact, 1 for correct definition of opinion)

2. Read this opinion piece. Identify the four parts by labeling them.

> I think dogs are better pets than fish because dogs can play with you. One reason is that dogs love to play fetch and go on walks with their owners. Another reason is that dogs can learn tricks like sit, shake, and roll over. That is why I believe dogs are the best pets you can have.

Label: Topic Sentence / Reason 1 / Reason 2 / Closing Sentence (2 points — 0.5 points for each correct label)

3. A student writes: "Vanilla ice cream is white." Is this a fact or an opinion? Another student writes: "Vanilla ice cream is the best flavor." Is this a fact or an opinion? (1 point — 0.5 for each)

**Mastery = 4 out of 5 points**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the Opinion Writing Template and review each part with a simple example, having the student verbally identify each part
- Use the "Fact or Opinion?" sorting activity again with new statements to reinforce the distinction
- Co-write another opinion piece on a fun topic (e.g., "The best color is ___"), with the facilitator writing Part 1 and Part 4 and the student writing Parts 2 and 3
- Repeat the assessment the following day before moving to Lesson 6

### If Mastery Is Met
- The student is ready to move to Lesson 6 (next lesson in the unit, covering informative/explanatory writing)
- Encourage the student to share their opinion piece with a family member and explain the four parts
- The student can begin collecting opinion ideas in a "Writer's Notebook" for future writing assignments

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Opinion Gallery Walk:** The student writes 2-3 short opinion pieces on different topics and displays them. A family member or peer reads each one and writes a short response: "I agree because..." or "I disagree because..." This introduces the idea that opinions can be discussed and debated respectfully.
- **Opinion Piece About a Book:** The student writes an opinion piece about a book they recently read: "I think [book title] is a great book because..." with two reasons. This connects opinion writing to reading.
- **Stronger Reasons Challenge:** Provide the student with a weak opinion piece (e.g., "I think pizza is the best food. One reason is because I like it. Another reason is because it is good.") and ask them to rewrite it with STRONGER, more specific reasons.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- **Pacing:** The oral rehearsal step in Guided Practice is critical. Many second graders can SAY their opinion piece more fluently than they can write it. By rehearsing orally first, they have a mental draft ready when they sit down to write. Do not skip this step.
- **Spelling support:** At this stage, prioritize IDEAS over spelling accuracy. If a student stops writing because they cannot spell a word, offer the spelling quickly or say "Write it the best you can — we will fix spelling later." The goal of this lesson is opinion writing structure, not spelling.
- **Connection to Lesson 3:** This lesson is the mirror image of Lesson 3. In Lesson 3, students were readers identifying an author's point and reasons. Now they are writers creating their own point (opinion) and reasons. Reference the recycling passage and the block tower analogy to help students see the connection.
- **Common misconception — Opinion vs. preference:** Students may confuse a simple preference ("I like blue") with an opinion they can support with reasons ("I think blue is the best color for a bedroom because it is calming and reminds me of the sky"). Coach them to choose opinions they can EXPLAIN with reasons.
- **Common misconception — Reasons that are not reasons:** Students may write "I think pizza is the best because I like it" and consider "because I like it" a reason. Gently redirect: *"Saying you like it just repeats your opinion. A reason tells us WHY you like it. What about pizza makes it your favorite?"*
- **The Opinion Writing Template** is designed to be a scaffold that the student will eventually outgrow. In later lessons, students will learn to write opinion pieces with more complex structures, transitional words, and more than two reasons.
- **Connection to standards:** This lesson directly addresses W.2.1 (write opinion pieces in which they introduce the topic or book they are writing about, state an opinion, supply reasons that support the opinion, use linking words to connect opinion and reasons, and provide a concluding statement or section). It also builds on RI.2.8 by having students apply the point-reason structure they identified as readers.

## Answer Key

### Guided Practice — Sample Opinion Piece (Student Output Will Vary)

**Sample — "My Favorite Animal"**

> I think dogs are the best animals because they are friendly and fun. One reason is that dogs wag their tails and get excited when you come home, which makes you feel happy. Another reason is that you can play fetch and go on walks with dogs, so they help you get exercise and have fun outside. That is why I think dogs are the most wonderful animals in the world.

**Checklist verification:**
- Topic sentence states opinion: Yes ("I think dogs are the best animals")
- Two different reasons: Yes (Reason 1 = friendly and make you happy; Reason 2 = play and exercise)
- Each reason explains WHY: Yes
- Closing restates opinion: Yes ("dogs are the most wonderful animals")
- Capitals and punctuation: Yes

### Independent Practice

**★ On-level:**
Student reviews their own piece against the checklist. Accept any complete piece with all four parts present, correct capitalization, and end punctuation. Drawing should relate to the opinion topic.

**★★ Stretch:**

1. Accept any third reason that is distinct from the first two. Example: "Also, dogs are very loyal and will always stay by your side, even when you are sad."
2. Accept a correct distinction. Example: "A fact is something that is true and can be proven, like 'Dogs have four legs.' An opinion is what someone thinks, like 'Dogs are the best pets.'"

**★★★ Challenge:**

3. Accept any complete opinion piece on a new topic that includes: topic sentence with opinion, two or three reasons, closing sentence, at least 5 sentences total.

**Interleaved Review:**

4. Accept any reasonable before-reading and after-reading questions. Example:
   - Before: "What opinion does this writer have about dogs?" (Predicting question based on the topic.)
   - After: "Do I agree with the writer's opinion? Why or why not?" (Reflecting question.)

### Exit Ticket

1. A fact is something that is true and can be proven. (1 point) An opinion is what someone thinks or believes — other people might think differently. (1 point)

2. Labels:
   - "I think dogs are better pets than fish because dogs can play with you." = **Topic Sentence** (0.5 points)
   - "One reason is that dogs love to play fetch and go on walks with their owners." = **Reason 1** (0.5 points)
   - "Another reason is that dogs can learn tricks like sit, shake, and roll over." = **Reason 2** (0.5 points)
   - "That is why I believe dogs are the best pets you can have." = **Closing Sentence** (0.5 points)
   (Total: 2 points)

3. "Vanilla ice cream is white" = Fact (0.5 points). "Vanilla ice cream is the best flavor" = Opinion (0.5 points). (Total: 1 point)
