# Unit Assessment — Grade 2 ELA, Unit 2: Informational Text & Writing

**Grade:** 2
**Unit:** 2 — Informational Text & Writing
**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** One-on-one with facilitator (oral reading, oral responses, and written composition)
**Estimated Duration:** 30-35 minutes
**Materials:** Printed copy of the assessment passage, pencil, lined paper for writing, the student's revised writing piece from the unit (for Section F)

---

## Assessment Overview

This assessment covers all Unit 2 learning objectives:

| Section | Skill Assessed | Lesson Source | Items |
|---------|---------------|---------------|-------|
| A | Informational text features | Lesson 1 | 4 |
| B | Main topic and key details | Lesson 2 | 4 |
| C | How reasons support points | Lesson 3 | 3 |

| E | Opinion writing | Lessons 5-6 | 5 |

| G | Revision and editing | Lesson 9 | 2 |
| -- | **Total** | -- | **25** |

**Mastery threshold:** 85% overall (21 of 25 points) AND at least 1 correct item in each section.

---

## Administration Instructions

Sections A-D use an informational passage that the student reads (or listens to). Sections E-F require the student to write. Section G uses a provided sample passage for revision and editing.

For Sections A-D: the student may read the passage independently or have it read aloud, depending on reading level. Questions are asked orally; the student responds orally.

For Sections E-F: the student writes independently. Allow 10-15 minutes total for both writing tasks. The student may use their writing templates from the unit as reference (opinion template and informative template).

There are no time limits on individual items. If the student is stuck on a comprehension question for more than 15 seconds, say: "Let's try the next one." For writing, if the student has not started after 2 minutes, offer a topic suggestion.

Before beginning, say: "Today we're going to read a text, answer some questions, and do some writing. You've been doing all of these things in our lessons — just do your best!"

## Assessment Passage

Read or have the student read the following passage:

---

**Why Trees Are Important**

## What Trees Give Us

Trees are one of the most important living things on Earth. They give us many things we need every day.

Trees make the **oxygen** we breathe. People and animals need oxygen to live. Without trees, we would not have enough clean air.

Trees also give us food. **Apples**, oranges, cherries, and nuts all grow on trees. Many of the foods we eat every day come from trees.

## How Trees Help Animals

Many animals need trees to survive. Birds build their **nests** in tree branches. Squirrels store their nuts in holes in tree trunks. Owls sleep in trees during the day. Even tiny insects live under tree **bark**.

## Why We Should Protect Trees

Some people cut down too many trees. This is a problem because animals lose their homes and we lose clean air. That is why it is important to plant new trees and take care of the ones we have.

*Caption: A tall oak tree in a green park. Many birds sit in its branches.*

*Glossary: Oxygen — a gas in the air that people and animals breathe to stay alive. Bark — the hard outer covering of a tree trunk and branches.*

## Section A: Informational Text Features (Lesson 1)

**A1.** "This text has headings — the big titles above some paragraphs. Can you point to one heading and tell me what it says?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Points to and reads any heading ("What Trees Give Us," "How Trees Help Animals," or "Why We Should Protect Trees") | 1 point |
| Cannot identify a heading | 0 points |

**A2.** "There are some words in bold (darker/thicker print). Can you find one bold word and tell me why the author might have made it bold?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Finds a bold word (oxygen, apples, nests, bark) AND gives a reasonable explanation (it's an important word / it's a vocabulary word / so you notice it / it's in the glossary) | 1 point |
| Finds a bold word but cannot explain why it's bold, or cannot find any bold word | 0.5 points |
| Cannot find bold words | 0 points |

**A3.** "At the bottom of the text, there is a caption. What does the caption tell us?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Describes the picture that would go with the text (a tall oak tree with birds) or explains that a caption tells about a picture | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**A4.** "There is also a glossary at the bottom. What is a glossary for?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| It tells you what words mean / it defines words / it explains hard words | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

---

## Section B: Main Topic and Key Details (Lesson 2)

**B1.** "What is this text mostly about? What is the main topic?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Trees / why trees are important / what trees do for us | 1 point |
| A specific detail (e.g., "birds live in trees") rather than the overall topic | 0.5 points |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**B2.** "Tell me one thing trees give us, according to the text."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Oxygen / clean air / food (apples, oranges, cherries, nuts) | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**B3.** "Tell me one way trees help animals."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Birds build nests in them / squirrels store nuts in them / owls sleep in them / insects live under the bark (any detail from that section) | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**B4.** "The text has three sections with headings. Each section has a different part of the main topic. Can you tell me what each section is about?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Accurately describes 2-3 sections (what trees give us, how they help animals, why we should protect them) | 1 point |
| Describes only 1 section or gives vague descriptions | 0.5 points |
| Cannot describe any sections | 0 points |

---

## Section C: How Reasons Support Points (Lesson 3)

**C1.** "The author says trees are important. What is one REASON the author gives to support that point?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Trees make oxygen / trees give us food / trees help animals (any reason from the text) | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**C2.** "The author says we should protect trees. What REASON does the author give for why cutting down trees is a problem?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Animals lose their homes / we lose clean air (either reason from the text) | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**C3.** "Do the reasons the author gives make you think trees really are important? Why or why not?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Gives a thoughtful response that shows evaluation of the author's reasoning (e.g., "Yes, because we need air to breathe" or "Yes, because animals need places to live") | 1 point |
| Says "yes" or "no" without any reasoning | 0.5 points |
| No response | 0 points |

---

## Section D: Questioning Strategies (Lesson 4)

**D1.** "Before we read this text, if you only saw the title 'Why Trees Are Important,' what question would you want to ask? What would you wonder?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Any relevant question (e.g., "Why are trees important?" "What do trees do?" "How do trees help us?") | 1 point |
| No question or an irrelevant question | 0 points |

**D2.** "What is one question you still have AFTER reading this text — something you're curious about that the text didn't answer?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Any relevant, thoughtful question (e.g., "How long does it take a tree to grow?" "What kinds of trees give the most oxygen?" "How many trees are cut down each year?") | 1 point |
| No question or simply restates information from the text | 0 points |

**D3.** "The text says one ladybug... wait — the text says one tree makes oxygen. What would happen if there were no trees at all?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Shows inferential thinking: we wouldn't have enough clean air / animals wouldn't have homes / we wouldn't have tree-grown food (any reasonable inference based on text information) | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

---

## Section E: Opinion Writing (Lessons 5-6)

Say: "Now I'd like you to write a short opinion piece. An opinion piece tells what YOU think and gives REASONS why."

**Prompt:** "Do you think it is important to protect trees? Write your opinion and give at least two reasons."

Allow 5-8 minutes. The student may refer to the text they just read for ideas.

Score the writing on these criteria:

**E1. Opinion statement:** Does the writing include a clear opinion?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| States a clear opinion (e.g., "I think we should protect trees" or "Yes, trees are important and we should protect them") | 1 point |
| Opinion is implied but not clearly stated | 0.5 points |
| No opinion stated | 0 points |

**E2. Reason 1:** Does the writing include at least one reason?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Provides a clear reason supporting the opinion (e.g., "because they give us clean air") | 1 point |
| Reason is vague or unrelated | 0.5 points |
| No reason provided | 0 points |

**E3. Reason 2:** Does the writing include a second reason?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Provides a second, distinct reason | 1 point |
| Second reason is the same as the first, restated | 0.5 points |
| No second reason | 0 points |

**E4. Closing:** Does the writing include a closing sentence?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Includes a closing that wraps up the piece (e.g., "That is why I think we should take care of trees.") | 1 point |
| Writing just stops after the last reason with no closing | 0 points |

**E5. Organization:** Is the writing organized and easy to follow?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Writing flows logically: opinion → reasons → closing | 1 point |
| Writing includes the elements but they are out of order or hard to follow | 0.5 points |
| Writing is disorganized or incoherent | 0 points |

---

## Section F: Informative Writing (Lessons 7-8)

Say: "Now write a short informative piece. An informative piece teaches the reader FACTS about a topic."

**Prompt:** "Write 3-4 sentences teaching someone about trees. Use facts you learned from the text."

Allow 5-7 minutes.

**F1. Topic sentence:** Does the writing have a clear topic?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Opens with a sentence that introduces the topic (e.g., "Trees are very important" or "Trees help people and animals") | 1 point |
| Jumps into facts without introducing the topic | 0.5 points |
| No clear topic | 0 points |

**F2. Facts:** Does the writing include at least 2 facts?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Includes 2+ accurate facts from the text or general knowledge | 1 point |
| Includes only 1 fact | 0.5 points |
| No facts, or facts are inaccurate | 0 points |

**F3. Informative tone:** Does the writing teach rather than tell a story or share an opinion?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Writing is informative in nature — presents facts, not a story or opinion | 1 point |
| Writing mixes informative and narrative/opinion elements | 0.5 points |
| Writing is primarily narrative or opinion rather than informative | 0 points |

**F4. Closing:** Does the writing have a closing?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Includes a closing sentence (e.g., "Now you know some reasons why trees are important!") | 1 point |
| No closing — writing just stops | 0 points |

## Section G: Revision and Editing (Lesson 9)

Show the student this sample passage with errors:

---

*my favrit animal is a dog. dogs are fun to play with they can run fast and catch balls. dogs are also soft and they like to sit in your lap i think evryone should have a dog*

---

**G1. Editing:** "This writing has some mistakes. Can you find and fix at least 3 mistakes?" (Errors: lowercase "my" at start, "favrit" spelling, missing period after "with," lowercase "they" after missing period, missing period after "lap," lowercase "i," "evryone" spelling — 7 total errors)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Identifies and corrects at least 3 errors | 1 point |
| Identifies 1-2 errors | 0.5 points |
| Cannot identify any errors | 0 points |

**G2. Revision:** "Can you think of one way to make this writing BETTER — not just fix mistakes, but make it more interesting or more detailed?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Suggests a meaningful improvement (e.g., add more details about what dogs do, give a specific example, describe what kind of dog, add a better closing) | 1 point |
| Suggests a surface change that doesn't improve quality | 0.5 points |
| Cannot suggest any improvement | 0 points |

## Rubric and Mastery Determination

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Next Steps |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Mastered** | 21-25 (85-100%) with at least 1 correct in each section | Solid informational text reading and foundational writing skills | Advance to Unit 3 (Narrative Writing & Language). Continue daily reading of fiction and nonfiction. |
| **Approaching** | 18-20 (70-84%) | Most skills developing; specific areas need reinforcement | Identify weak sections and reteach. The student may begin Unit 3 while continuing to practice weak areas. |
| **Developing** | Below 18 (<70%) | Significant gaps in reading comprehension or writing | Return to weakest section and reteach. Do not advance to Unit 3 until "Approaching" level. |

## Section-Specific Reteaching Guide

| If the student struggles in... | What it tells you | Reteach with... |
|-------------------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Section A (Text Features)** | Student does not use nonfiction navigation tools | Return to Lesson 1. Do a "text feature walk" before every nonfiction reading. Create a Text Features anchor chart. Practice identifying features in magazines, websites, and nonfiction books. |
| **Section B (Main Topic/Details)** | Cannot distinguish main topic from details | Return to Lesson 2. Use the "one sentence" strategy and main topic/details graphic organizer with every informational text. Practice with shorter, simpler passages first. |
| **Section C (Reasons/Points)** | Cannot identify how authors support their claims | Return to Lesson 3. Practice with everyday arguments: "I think you should eat breakfast because..." Have the student identify the point and reasons in simple persuasive sentences before tackling full texts. |
| **Section D (Questioning)** | Cannot generate meaningful questions about text | Return to Lesson 4. Model questioning with think-alouds. Use question stems: "I wonder why..." "How does..." "What would happen if..." Practice generating questions before, during, and after reading daily. |
| **Section E (Opinion Writing)** | Cannot write a structured opinion piece | Return to Lessons 5-6. Use the opinion writing template as a scaffold. Practice oral opinion-giving before writing. Start with fill-in-the-blank templates and gradually remove scaffolding. |
| **Section F (Informative Writing)** | Cannot write a fact-based informative piece | Return to Lessons 7-8. Distinguish clearly between "teaching facts" and "telling a story." Use the frame "Did you know that..." for each sentence. Provide facts for the student to work with rather than requiring them to generate facts from memory. |
| **Section G (Revision/Editing)** | Cannot identify errors or suggest improvements | Return to Lesson 9. Practice editing with single sentences first before full passages. Use editing checklists with one item at a time. For revision, model specific improvements rather than asking open-ended "how could you make it better?" |

## Facilitator Observations (Record During Assessment)

- Does the student use text features to navigate the passage, or read straight through ignoring them?
- Does the student refer back to the text when answering questions?
- Does the student show different comfort levels with reading comprehension vs. writing?
- How does the student approach the writing tasks — does the student plan before writing, or start immediately?
- Does the student's opinion writing include genuine reasoning, or formulaic filler?
- Does the student's informative writing sound like teaching, or does it slide into storytelling?
- Can the student identify their own errors when re-reading, or only errors in others' writing?

These observations inform differentiation for Unit 3 and help identify whether challenges are in reading comprehension, writing composition, writing mechanics, or metacognitive awareness.
