# English Language Arts — Grade 2 — Unit 2, Lesson 6

## Topic
Writing Opinions with Reasons

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Write a complete opinion piece that includes a topic sentence, two supporting reasons with details, and a closing sentence
- Use linking words (because, also, another reason) to connect opinions to reasons
- Read their writing aloud to check that it makes sense
- Distinguish between an opinion statement and a supporting reason

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 5: Introduction to Opinion Writing (opinion vs. fact, opinion writing structure, sentence starters)
- Ability to write complete sentences with correct capitalization and end punctuation
- Understanding that an opinion is what you think or feel, and a reason is WHY you think or feel that way

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and lined paper (or writing journal)
- Opinion Writing Template (provided in this lesson)
- Model opinion piece (provided in this lesson)
- Eraser
- Colored pencils or crayons (optional, for illustration)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review the opinion writing structure from Lesson 5 and activate thinking about personal preferences.

**Activity: Opinion Quick-Talk**

Say: "In our last lesson, we learned about opinion writing. Let's warm up our opinion-thinking muscles!"

Ask the student each question and have them answer in a complete sentence that includes the word "because":

1. "What is your favorite food?" (Example: "My favorite food is spaghetti because it tastes really good.")
2. "What is the best game to play?" (Example: "The best game to play is tag because you get to run fast.")
3. "What is the best time of day?" (Example: "The best time of day is morning because everything feels fresh.")

After each answer, say: "Great! You just gave me an opinion AND a reason. The opinion is what you think. The reason is the 'because' part — it tells me WHY you think that."

Then review the four parts of an opinion piece from Lesson 5 by asking:

Say: "Remember our opinion writing recipe from last time? Can you tell me the four parts?"

Guide the student to recall:
1. Topic sentence with your opinion
2. First reason with a detail
3. Second reason with a detail
4. Closing sentence

Say: "Today, you are going to write a complete opinion piece using all four parts. You are going to be a real author!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Study a model opinion piece, identify its parts, and prepare to write an original piece.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Opinion piece:** A type of writing where the author shares what they think or believe and gives reasons why
- **Topic sentence:** The first sentence that tells the reader what the piece is about and states the author's opinion
- **Supporting reason:** A reason that explains WHY the author holds their opinion
- **Detail:** Extra information that makes a reason stronger or more interesting
- **Closing sentence:** The last sentence that wraps up the piece and reminds the reader of the opinion
- **Linking words:** Words that connect ideas, such as "because," "also," "another reason," and "for example"

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Read and study the model opinion piece (5 minutes)**

Say: "Before we write our own opinion piece, let's look at one that is already finished. I want you to read this and notice how the author put it together."

Read the following model opinion piece aloud together (facilitator reads first, then student reads):

---

**The Best Season**

*I think fall is the best season of all.* One reason I think this is because the leaves change to beautiful colors like red, orange, and yellow. It is like a painting outside my window. Another reason fall is the best is because you get to pick apples and pumpkins. My family goes to a farm every year, and we always have so much fun. *Fall is the best season, and I hope everyone gets to enjoy it!*

---

After reading, go through the piece part by part:

Say: "Let's find the four parts of an opinion piece in this writing."

Point to the first sentence: "What is the topic sentence?" (*I think fall is the best season of all.*) "What opinion does the author share?" (Fall is the best season.)

Point to the second and third sentences: "What is the first reason?" (The leaves change to beautiful colors.) "Is there a detail that makes the reason stronger?" (Yes — like red, orange, and yellow. It is like a painting outside my window.)

Point to the fourth and fifth sentences: "What is the second reason?" (You get to pick apples and pumpkins.) "Is there a detail?" (Yes — My family goes to a farm every year, and we always have so much fun.)

Point to the last sentence: "What is the closing sentence?" (*Fall is the best season, and I hope everyone gets to enjoy it!*) "Does it remind us of the opinion?" (Yes.)

Say: "Notice the linking words: 'One reason I think this is because' and 'Another reason.' Those words connect the opinion to the reasons. They are like bridges between ideas."

**Step 2: Introduce the topic choices and plan (5 minutes)**

Say: "Now it is your turn! You get to choose what to write about. Here are your two choices:"

Write on paper:
- **Choice A:** What is the best pet?
- **Choice B:** What is the best season?

Say: "Pick the one you have the most to say about. Which one makes you think of reasons right away?"

After the student chooses, help them plan by filling out a simple planning chart verbally first:

| Part | What will you write? |
|------|---------------------|
| My opinion: | I think ___ is the best ___ |
| Reason 1: | Because... |
| Detail for Reason 1: | (something extra about that reason) |
| Reason 2: | Also / Another reason... |
| Detail for Reason 2: | (something extra about that reason) |
| Closing: | That is why ___ is the best ___ |

Have the student say each part out loud before writing anything. This oral rehearsal is essential — it separates the thinking from the physical act of writing.

Say: "Tell me your whole opinion piece out loud, from start to finish, before you write a single word."

Listen to the student's oral version. If a reason is weak or missing a detail, coach them:
- "That's a great reason! Can you tell me more about it? Give me a detail."
- "Why do you think that? Tell me more."

### Worked Examples

**Worked Example 1: Best Pet (facilitator models thinking aloud)**

Say: "Let me show you how I would plan if I chose 'best pet.' Watch how I think through each part."

"My opinion is: I think dogs are the best pet. Now I need a reason. Why do I think dogs are the best? Because dogs are always happy to see you when you come home. That's my first reason. Let me add a detail: They wag their tails and jump up and down. Now I need a second reason. Another reason dogs are the best pet is because you can take them on walks and play fetch outside. Detail: My neighbor's dog loves to catch a ball at the park. Now I need a closing: Dogs are the best pet because they love you and are always ready to play."

Write the complete piece on paper for the student to see:

*I think dogs are the best pet. One reason is because dogs are always happy to see you when you come home. They wag their tails and jump up and down. Another reason dogs are the best pet is because you can take them on walks and play fetch outside. My neighbor's dog loves to catch a ball at the park. Dogs are the best pet because they love you and are always ready to play!*

**Worked Example 2: Best Season (facilitator models a different approach)**

Say: "Let me try the season topic too, but I'll pick a different season than the model piece."

"My opinion: I think summer is the best season. Reason 1: because you don't have to wake up early for school. Detail: You can stay up late and sleep in. Reason 2: Another reason is because you can swim and go to the beach. Detail: Last summer, I went to the lake and it was so cool and refreshing. Closing: Summer is my favorite season and I wish it would last forever!"

Write the complete piece:

*I think summer is the best season. One reason is because you don't have to wake up early for school. You can stay up late and sleep in! Another reason summer is the best is because you can swim and go to the beach. Last summer, I went to the lake and it was so cool and refreshing. Summer is my favorite season, and I wish it would last forever!*

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Support the student through drafting their opinion piece step by step using the Opinion Writing Template.

**Activity: Draft Your Opinion Piece**

Provide the student with the Opinion Writing Template (write this on lined paper or have the student copy it):

**Opinion Writing Template**

1. **Topic sentence (state your opinion):**
   I think _________________________________________________.

2. **First reason with detail:**
   One reason is because ______________________________________.
   _________________________________________________________.

3. **Second reason with detail:**
   Another reason is __________________________________________.
   _________________________________________________________.

4. **Closing sentence:**
   That is why _______________________________________________.

Guide the student through each section:

**Section 1 — Topic Sentence:**
Say: "Start with your topic sentence. Remember, this tells the reader your opinion right away. Use 'I think' or 'I believe' or 'The best ___ is ___.' Write it now."

If the student struggles, offer these starters:
- "I think ___ is the best ___."
- "In my opinion, ___ is the best ___."
- "The best ___ in the whole world is ___."

**Section 2 — First Reason:**
Say: "Now write your first reason. Start with 'One reason is because...' or 'First, I think this because...' Then add a detail — one more sentence that tells me more about that reason."

If the student writes only the reason without a detail, prompt: "That's a strong reason. Now, can you tell me one more thing about it? Paint a picture with your words so I can really understand."

**Section 3 — Second Reason:**
Say: "Now write your second reason. Start with 'Another reason is...' or 'Also...' Don't forget to add a detail after your reason."

If the student repeats their first reason in different words, say: "That sounds a lot like your first reason. Let's think of something different. What is a SECOND reason, a totally new one, why you think ___?"

**Section 4 — Closing:**
Say: "Finish with a closing sentence. This reminds the reader of your opinion. You can say 'That is why I think ___' or 'Now you know why ___ is the best ___' or try your own ending."

**After writing — Read aloud:**
Say: "Great work! Now read your whole piece to me from start to finish. Read it like you're telling a friend. Listen to yourself — does every part make sense?"

As the student reads aloud, listen for:
- Does the topic sentence clearly state an opinion?
- Does each reason actually support the opinion?
- Are there details after each reason?
- Does the closing wrap things up?

If something is unclear, say: "I noticed something. When you said ___, I wasn't sure what you meant. Can you say it a different way?" Help the student revise that sentence verbally, then write the revised version.

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** The student strengthens their opinion piece by adding to it and practices previously learned skills.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
The student re-reads their completed opinion piece and does the following:
1. Circle all the linking words (because, also, another reason, one reason).
2. Underline the topic sentence in one color and the closing sentence in another color.
3. Add one more detail to either Reason 1 or Reason 2 (one extra sentence).
4. Draw a small picture that goes with their opinion at the bottom of the page.

**Stretch:**
All of the above, plus:
5. Write a third reason with a detail: "One more reason is ___. ___."
6. Add an exclamation sentence somewhere in the piece to show strong feeling (e.g., "That is amazing!" or "Everyone should try it!").

**Challenge:**
All of the above, plus:
7. Write a NEW opinion piece about the other topic they did NOT choose (if they wrote about best pet, now write about best season, or vice versa). This second piece should be at least 5 sentences long.
8. After writing both pieces, tell the facilitator: "Which piece do you think is stronger? Why?"

### Interleaved Review

While the student works on independent practice, incorporate a brief review of skills from earlier lessons:

**Informational Text Review (from Lessons 1-4):**
Ask the student these questions orally while they take a break from writing:
1. "What are two text features you might find in an informational book?" (Headings, bold words, captions, table of contents, glossary — any two)
2. "What is the difference between a main topic and a key detail?" (The main topic is what the whole text is about; a key detail is one piece of information that tells more about the main topic)
3. "If an author says 'Recycling is important,' what would be a reason that supports that point?" (Accept any reasonable reason, such as: "Because it keeps trash out of the ocean" or "Because we can reuse things instead of throwing them away")

## Check for Understanding (3-5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment to determine if the student has met the lesson objectives.

**Exit Ticket:**

The student answers these questions independently (orally or in writing):

1. **Read this sentence: "I think cats are the best pet." Is this an opinion or a fact? How do you know?**
   (Answer: It is an opinion because it tells what someone thinks. You cannot prove it is true for everyone.)

2. **Read this sentence: "Cats are the best pet because they are soft and quiet." What is the reason in this sentence?**
   (Answer: The reason is "because they are soft and quiet.")

3. **What are the four parts of an opinion piece?**
   (Answer: Topic sentence with opinion, first reason with detail, second reason with detail, closing sentence.)

4. **Look at your opinion piece. Read your closing sentence to me. Does it remind the reader of your opinion?**
   (Answer: Student reads their closing sentence. It should connect back to the opinion stated in the topic sentence.)

**Scoring:**
- Question 1: 1 point (identifies opinion and explains why)
- Question 2: 1 point (identifies the reason)
- Question 3: 1 point (names all four parts — accept reasonable wording)
- Question 4: 1 point (reads a closing sentence that connects to the opinion)

**Mastery = 3 out of 4 points, AND the student has produced a complete opinion piece with all four required parts (topic sentence, two reasons, closing).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student cannot identify opinion vs. fact: Return to Lesson 5 and practice sorting opinion and fact statements with 10 additional examples.
- If the student's piece is missing reasons: Do a "reason hunt" together. Read the model piece again and circle every reason. Then go back to the student's piece and add reasons using the sentence starter "One reason is because..."
- If the student cannot produce a closing: Provide three closing sentence options and have the student pick the one that best fits their piece. Then practice writing their own.
- Re-attempt the exit ticket after remediation.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Celebrate the student's completed opinion piece. Say: "You are an opinion writer! You told the reader what you think AND you told them why. That is exactly what real authors do."
- Save the opinion piece in the student's writing folder — it will be used again in Lesson 9 (Revision and Editing) and Lesson 10 (Publishing and Sharing).
- Move to Lesson 7: Introduction to Informative Writing.

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Opinion Poster:** The student creates a poster for their opinion. The poster includes: the opinion in large letters at the top, the two reasons written below, and an illustration. Display the poster in the learning space.
- **Opinion Debate:** The facilitator takes the opposite opinion (e.g., if the student says dogs are the best pet, the facilitator argues for cats). Each side gives reasons. This builds oral argumentation skills.
- **Opinion Letter:** The student writes a short letter to a family member sharing their opinion. The letter format adds a real audience and purpose: "Dear Grandma, I think ___ is the best ___. Here is why..."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pedagogical context:** This lesson moves from the introduction of opinion writing structure (Lesson 5) to full, independent production. The key shift is from recognizing and labeling parts of an opinion piece to actually generating them. This is a significant cognitive step — the student must hold their opinion in mind, think of supporting reasons, add details, and organize everything into a coherent piece. Oral rehearsal before writing is critical at this stage.

**Pacing notes:** This lesson may take two sessions. If so, use the first session for the Warm-Up, Direct Instruction, and Guided Practice (the student completes their draft). Use the second session for Independent Practice, the Exit Ticket, and any revision. Do not rush the drafting process — it is more important that the student produces a thoughtful, complete piece than a quick one.

**Common issues:**
- **"I can't think of reasons."** Have the student close their eyes and picture the thing they are writing about. "Think about your favorite pet. What do you see? What do you do with it? How does it make you feel?" Each answer can become a reason.
- **Short pieces:** If the student writes only 3 sentences, celebrate what they wrote and add one sentence at a time. "You have your opinion and one reason. That's great. Now let's add one more reason. Just one."
- **Copying the model:** Some students may try to copy the model piece word for word. If this happens, say: "The model piece is about fall. Your piece is about YOUR opinion. You can use the same pattern but with YOUR ideas." Point to each section of the template and have them fill it in with original content.
- **Spelling anxiety:** Remind the student that this is a draft. "Spell it the best you can. We'll fix spelling later in Lesson 9 when we learn about editing. Right now, the most important thing is getting your ideas on paper."

This lesson aligns with standard **W.2.1** (Write opinion pieces with a topic, opinion, reasons, and a closing) and builds toward the revision work in Lesson 9 and publication in Lesson 10.
