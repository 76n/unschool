# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 1, Lesson 3

## Topic
Describing Characters in Depth

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Describe a character's traits using specific details from the text (thoughts, words, and actions)
- Distinguish between character traits (lasting qualities) and character feelings (temporary emotions)
- Organize character evidence into a character trait web

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of theme and how events build toward theme (Lessons 1-2)
- Ability to describe characters and explain how their actions contribute to events (RL.3.3)
- Ability to refer to the text when answering questions (RL.3.1)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard or chart paper and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils and colored pencils
- Sticky notes (2-3 per student)
- Printed or projected reading passages (provided below)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate thinking about what makes a person who they are and bridge to the concept of character traits.

**Activity: Describe Someone You Know**

Say: "Think about someone you know well — a family member, a friend, a neighbor. If I asked you to describe that person, what would you say? I do not mean what they look like. I mean what kind of person they are. Write down three words that describe that person on your sticky note."

Allow 60 seconds for writing. Then:

Say: "Who wants to share? Tell me one word you wrote and why you chose it."

Call on 2-3 students. After each, ask: "How do you know that person is [trait]? What have they done or said that shows you?"

Say: "You just did something really important. You described someone using their qualities — what we call traits — and you backed it up with evidence. That is exactly what we are going to do today with characters in stories. We are going to describe characters in depth using specific details from the text."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the difference between traits and feelings, introduce the character trait web, and model how to find text evidence for character traits.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Character trait:** A lasting quality that describes what kind of person a character is (e.g., brave, generous, stubborn, curious). Traits do not usually change from paragraph to paragraph.
- **Character feeling/emotion:** A temporary emotional state that changes as events happen (e.g., scared, happy, frustrated, relieved). Feelings come and go throughout a story.
- **Text evidence:** The specific words, actions, thoughts, or dialogue from the story that prove your answer.
- **Character trait web:** A graphic organizer with the character's name in the center and trait bubbles connected to evidence bubbles around it.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Before we start reading, I need to teach you something important. A trait and a feeling are not the same thing."

Write on the board:

| Traits (Lasting) | Feelings (Temporary) |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| kind | happy |
| determined | frustrated |
| honest | nervous |
| selfish | excited |

Say: "Traits stick with a character throughout the story. They are part of who the character is. Feelings change depending on what is happening. A character can be brave — that is a trait — and still feel scared in a moment. Being scared does not mean they are not brave. Does that make sense?"

Say: "When we describe a character in depth, we focus on traits. And we prove those traits with evidence. The best evidence comes from three places."

Write on the board:
1. **What the character DOES** (actions)
2. **What the character SAYS** (dialogue)
3. **What the character THINKS** (thoughts)

Say: "Let me show you how this works. I am going to read a passage and build a character trait web."

### Worked Examples

**Passage: "New Neighbors"**

> The moving truck arrived next door on a Saturday morning. Amira watched from the window as a family carried boxes inside. A girl about her age struggled to lift a heavy box from the truck.
>
> Amira pulled on her shoes and ran outside. "Need help?" she asked.
>
> The girl looked surprised. "Really? Thanks!"
>
> Together they carried the box to the front porch. "I am Amira. I live right there," Amira said, pointing to her house.
>
> "I am Sophie. We just moved from Ohio."
>
> "That is really far," Amira said. "You must miss your friends."
>
> Sophie's eyes got watery. "Yeah. A lot."
>
> Amira thought for a second. "Well, you already have one friend here. Do you want to come over for lunch after you finish unpacking?"
>
> Sophie smiled for the first time. "I would love that."

Say: "Let me build a character trait web for Amira."

Draw a circle in the center of the board and write **AMIRA**. Then draw three bubbles around it.

Say: "What is one trait I can use to describe Amira? Let me look at what she does, says, and thinks."

Bubble 1: **Kind / Helpful**
- Evidence: She saw Sophie struggling with a box and ran outside to help without being asked.
- Evidence: She invited Sophie over for lunch.

Bubble 2: **Thoughtful / Empathetic**
- Evidence: She said "You must miss your friends," showing she was thinking about how Sophie felt.
- Evidence: She thought for a second before responding to Sophie's sadness, then offered friendship.

Say: "See how each trait has evidence from the text? I did not just say Amira is kind — I proved it with her actions and words. That is what 'describing a character in depth' means."

Say: "Now notice — Sophie's eyes got watery. Is 'sad' a trait or a feeling?"

Students should answer: A feeling.

Say: "Right. Sophie feels sad in that moment, but that does not mean sadness is her trait. She might be cheerful in the next chapter. Feelings change. Traits last."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students work with the teacher to build a character trait web for a new passage.

Say: "Let us try this together. I will read a passage, and we will build a character trait web as a team. Draw a circle in your notebook and get ready to add trait bubbles."

**Passage: "The Science Fair"**

> Diego had been working on his science fair project for three weeks. He had tested his hypothesis four different times, restarting each time something went wrong. His notebook was filled with charts, measurements, and notes in tiny handwriting.
>
> On the night before the fair, his little sister, Luna, accidentally knocked his display board off the table. The board bent, and one of his graphs ripped.
>
> Diego's face turned red. He clenched his fists. But then he took a deep breath.
>
> "Luna, you need to be more careful," he said. His voice was firm but calm.
>
> Luna started to cry. "I am so sorry!"
>
> "It is okay," Diego said, even though his heart was pounding. "I can fix it."
>
> He spent the next two hours carefully repairing the board, remaking the graph from his notebook, and even adding a new section he had not thought of before. When he finished, the board looked better than the original.
>
> At the science fair, Diego won second place. "Not bad for a board that got destroyed the night before," he told his mom, grinning.

Say: "Write the name DIEGO in the center circle. Now, let us find traits. Talk to your partner: What is one word that describes what kind of person Diego is? Use his actions, words, or thoughts as proof."

Allow 90 seconds. Then collect responses:

Guide students toward:
- **Hardworking / Determined:** He worked for three weeks, restarted his experiment four times, has notebooks full of careful notes, and spent two hours repairing his board.
- **Patient / Self-controlled:** When Luna broke his board, he was angry (red face, clenched fists) but chose to take a deep breath and speak calmly instead of yelling.
- **Positive / Resilient:** He fixed the board and even made it better. He joked about it afterward ("Not bad for a board that got destroyed").

Say: "Now here is a key question. When Diego's face turned red and he clenched his fists — is 'angry' a trait or a feeling?"

Students should say: A feeling.

Say: "Exactly. He felt angry for a moment, but his trait is patient or self-controlled because of what he chose to do with that anger. That is the difference."

Have students complete their character trait webs with at least two traits and two pieces of evidence per trait.

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently analyze a character using a trait web and text evidence.

**Passage: "The Recital"**

> Hannah had practiced her violin piece every single day for two months. She played it in the morning before school. She played it after dinner. She even played it softly on weekends so her parents could sleep in. Her teacher, Ms. Patel, said she was one of the hardest-working students she had ever taught.
>
> The day of the recital, Hannah peeked through the curtain at the audience. The auditorium was packed. Her hands started to shake.
>
> "I cannot do this," she whispered to the boy next to her, Tomás.
>
> "You will be amazing," Tomás said. "You practice more than anyone."
>
> When Hannah's name was called, she walked to the center of the stage. Her legs felt like jelly. She lifted her violin, placed the bow on the strings, and paused. The room was silent.
>
> Then she began to play. At first her notes wobbled. But after the first few measures, something clicked. Her fingers found the right places. The music flowed. She stopped thinking about the audience and just played.
>
> When she finished, the applause filled the room. Hannah smiled, her cheeks pink, and took a bow. Backstage, she found Tomás.
>
> "Thank you for what you said," she told him. "It helped."
>
> "You did that all on your own," Tomás said.

**Directions:** Answer the following questions in complete sentences.

1. Create a character trait web for Hannah. Include at least two traits with two pieces of text evidence for each trait.
2. At the recital, Hannah felt nervous (her hands shook, her legs felt like jelly). Is "nervous" a character trait or a character feeling? Explain your answer.
3. How does Hannah's dialogue with Tomás at the end of the passage support one of her traits?
4. Choose one of Hannah's traits and explain how that trait helped her succeed at the recital.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Answer questions 1-4 as written.
**Stretch:** After completing questions 1-4, create a character trait web for Tomás using the limited evidence in the passage. What traits can you identify for him even though he is not the main character?
**Challenge:** After completing all questions, write a paragraph comparing Hannah and Diego (from Guided Practice). What trait do they share? How do their stories show that trait in different ways?

### Interleaved Review
5. (Lesson 1-2 review) What is the theme of "The Recital"? Write it as a full sentence and list one piece of evidence.
6. (Grade 3 review — RL.3.1) What did Ms. Patel say about Hannah? Answer using the exact words from the text.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Read the following mini-passage:

> Malik found a twenty-dollar bill on the playground. He looked around. No one was watching. He thought about keeping it, but then he remembered that someone had probably lost it and felt sad. He brought the money to the school office and told the secretary where he had found it.

1. Name one character trait that describes Malik.
2. What evidence from the text supports that trait? (Give one specific detail.)
3. Is "tempted" (when Malik thought about keeping the money) a trait or a feeling? Explain.

**Expected Answers:**
1. Honest (or responsible, or ethical, or caring). Accept any reasonable trait supported by evidence.
2. Evidence: He brought the money to the school office instead of keeping it. OR He thought about how the person who lost it probably felt sad.
3. "Tempted" is a feeling, not a trait. It is a temporary emotion Malik experienced in that moment. His trait is honesty, which is shown by his decision to return the money even though he was tempted to keep it.

**Mastery = Student names an appropriate trait (not a feeling), provides text evidence, and correctly distinguishes a trait from a feeling.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the trait vs. feeling chart from Direct Instruction with the student.
- Give the student three short scenarios and ask them to label each character description as a trait or a feeling. Practice until the student can explain the difference.
- Provide a word bank of common traits (brave, kind, lazy, honest, stubborn, curious, generous) for the student to use in the next lesson.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 4, where they will analyze how characters respond to challenges and examine character motivation.
- Encourage the student to create character trait webs for characters in their independent reading.

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Trait Trading Cards:** The student creates a "trading card" for a character from today's lesson or from a book they are reading. The front has the character's name and an illustration; the back has two traits with evidence.
2. **Trait vs. Feeling Sort:** The student receives a list of 12 words and sorts them into "Trait" and "Feeling" categories, then writes a sentence using each trait word to describe a character.
3. **Character Interview:** The student writes 5 interview questions they would ask Hannah (or Diego) and then writes the character's answers based on what they know from the text.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- **Trait vs. feeling confusion:** This is the most common struggle in this lesson. Keep returning to the anchor question: "Is this something that lasts, or is it something that changes?" If a student says a character is "sad," ask: "Is this person always sad, or are they sad right now because of what happened?"
- **Evidence depth:** Fourth graders often give vague evidence ("she was nice"). Push for specificity: "Where exactly in the text do you see that? Can you point to the sentence?"
- **Character trait web:** Keep the format simple — name in the center, 2-3 trait bubbles, each with 1-2 evidence lines. Students can add more traits as they grow more skilled, but starting with 2 keeps it manageable.
- **Anchor chart update:** Add a "Character Analysis" anchor chart to the wall with the three evidence sources (actions, words, thoughts) and the trait vs. feeling distinction.
- **Connection to theme:** Point out that character traits often connect to the theme. A character who is determined usually appears in a story whose theme is about perseverance. This connection deepens in Lesson 4.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. **Character Trait Web for Hannah:**
   - Trait 1: **Hardworking / Dedicated** — Evidence: She practiced every day for two months, played morning and evening, and Ms. Patel called her one of the hardest-working students.
   - Trait 2: **Brave / Courageous** — Evidence: Even though she was so nervous her hands were shaking and her legs felt like jelly, she still walked on stage and played. She pushed through her fear.
   - Trait 3 (optional): **Grateful / Humble** — Evidence: She thanked Tomás for encouraging her backstage, showing she appreciated his support.

2. "Nervous" is a character feeling, not a trait. It is a temporary emotion Hannah experienced because of the recital. Her traits are hardworking and brave — those describe who she is as a person, not just how she felt in one moment.

3. When Hannah thanks Tomás and says "It helped," this shows she is grateful and humble. She does not take all the credit — she acknowledges that his encouragement mattered. This supports the trait of being grateful or humble.

4. Example: Hannah's trait of being hardworking helped her succeed because all her months of practice meant her fingers knew the music even when she was nervous. After the first few measures, "something clicked" and "the music flowed" — that happened because she had practiced so much.

### Stretch Tier
Tomás traits: **Supportive / Encouraging** — He told Hannah "You will be amazing" and "You practice more than anyone" when she was nervous. **Honest / Humble** — He said "You did that all on your own," giving Hannah credit instead of taking it.

### Interleaved Review
5. Theme: Hard work and perseverance help you succeed even when you are afraid. Evidence: Hannah practiced for two months and was able to perform well despite being very nervous because of all her preparation.
6. Ms. Patel said Hannah "was one of the hardest-working students she had ever taught."

### Exit Ticket
See expected answers above.
