# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 1: Reading Literature

## Facilitator Guide

### Unit Overview
Unit 1 introduces fourth graders to four foundational literary analysis skills: determining theme, describing characters and settings in depth, comparing points of view, and recognizing structural differences among poems, drama, and prose. Over ten lessons, students move from identifying what a story is really about (theme) to examining how authors build characters, settings, and events with specific details, to understanding how the narrator's perspective shapes a story, and finally to comparing how the same content can look and feel different depending on the literary form. The unit culminates in a celebration lesson where students apply all four skill areas to a self-selected text.

### Standards Addressed
| Standard | Description | Lessons |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| RL.4.2 | Determine a theme of a story, drama, or poem from details in the text; summarize the text | 1, 2, 10 |

| RL.4.6 | Compare and contrast the point of view from which different stories are narrated, including the difference between first- and third-person narrations | 6, 7, 10 |
| RL.4.5 | Explain major differences between poems, drama, and prose, and refer to structural elements of each when writing or speaking about a text | 8, 9, 10 |

### Prerequisite Skills (Grade 3)
Students entering this unit should be able to:
- Ask and answer questions about a text, referring explicitly to the text as the basis for answers (RL.3.1)
- Describe characters in a story and explain how their actions contribute to the sequence of events (RL.3.3)
- Determine the meaning of words and phrases as they are used in a text (RL.3.4)

If students struggle with these prerequisite skills, use the interleaved review sections in each lesson to reinforce them before moving forward.

### Facilitation Tips
- **Anchor charts:** Create a running anchor chart for each major concept (Theme, Character Analysis, Point of View, Literary Structure). Add to it after each lesson so students can reference prior learning.
- **Think-alouds:** Model your own thinking process frequently. Fourth graders benefit from hearing how an experienced reader reasons through text.
- **Turn and talk:** Before cold-calling, give students 30-60 seconds to share ideas with a partner. This builds confidence and surfaces misconceptions early.
- **Flexible grouping:** Use exit ticket data to form small re-teaching groups. Students who do not meet mastery on a given lesson should receive targeted support before moving on.
- **Passage familiarity:** Preview all passages before the lesson. Some contain emotional content (e.g., characters facing loss or fear) that may benefit from a brief content introduction.

### Common Misconceptions
| Misconception | Correction |
|---------------|------------|
| Theme is the same as topic | Topic is a single word or phrase (e.g., "friendship"); theme is a complete message or lesson about the topic (e.g., "True friends stick together even when things are hard"). |
| A character trait is the same as a feeling | Traits are lasting qualities (brave, generous); feelings change throughout a story (scared, happy). Both matter, but they are different. |
| First person means the story is true | First person is a narrative choice; fiction can be told in first person. The "I" narrator is a character, not necessarily the author. |
| Poems must rhyme | Many poems use free verse. Poems are defined by their use of line breaks, stanzas, and compressed language, not by rhyme alone. |

### Assessment Guidance
- **Formative:** Use the exit ticket in each lesson to gauge daily mastery. Students scoring below the stated mastery threshold should receive re-teaching before the next lesson.
- **Summative:** The unit assessment includes five passages covering all four standards. Administer it after Lesson 10. Students should score at least 70% overall and demonstrate competency in each standard.
- **Portfolio option:** If your program uses portfolios, include the student's Lesson 10 culminating project and the unit assessment as evidence of growth.

### Differentiation at a Glance
- **Below level:** Provide pre-reading vocabulary support; allow paired reading; use the "On-level" tier in independent practice.
- **On level:** Follow lesson as written; use the "On-level" and "Stretch" tiers.
- **Above level:** Assign "Challenge" tier activities; encourage deeper analysis and cross-text connections.

### Connection to Future Units
The skills developed in this unit — theme identification, character and setting analysis, point of view, and structural analysis — will be applied and extended in Unit 2 (Reading Informational Text), where students will compare how similar skills operate in nonfiction, and in Unit 3 (Writing), where students will use their understanding of literary structure to craft their own narratives.
