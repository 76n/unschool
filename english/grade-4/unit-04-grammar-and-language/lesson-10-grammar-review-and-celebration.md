# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Topic
Grammar Review and Celebration

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Demonstrate mastery of all five grammar standards covered in Unit 4
- Apply relative pronouns, relative adverbs, progressive tenses, adjective order, precise word choice, and capitalization in context
- Celebrate grammar learning through engaging review activities

## Prerequisites
- Completion of Lessons 1-9 in Unit 4
- Familiarity with all five standards: L.4.1a, L.4.1b, L.4.1d, L.4.3a, L.4.2a

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Index cards (for Grammar Challenge cards)
- Timer or stopwatch
- Small prizes or certificates (optional, for celebration)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Get students excited about reviewing everything they have learned and build confidence before the unit assessment.

**Activity: Grammar Bingo Recall**

Say: "We have learned a lot of grammar skills in this unit. I am going to call out a grammar term, and I want you to give me a quick example in your notebook. Write fast — this is just for fun, not for a grade."

Call out the following, giving about 30 seconds each:
1. "Write a sentence using the relative pronoun 'who.'"
2. "Write a sentence in the past progressive tense."
3. "Write two adjectives in the correct order before the word 'car.'"
4. "Write a sentence using formal language."
5. "Write a sentence with a proper noun that needs a capital letter."

Say: "Fantastic! You just used skills from five different lessons in under three minutes. That tells me you have learned a lot. Today is our review and celebration day. We are going to play some grammar games, practice our skills, and get ready for the unit assessment."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Provide a quick summary review of all five standards before practice activities.

### Key Vocabulary
This lesson reviews all key vocabulary from Lessons 1-9. No new terms are introduced.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Let us do a lightning review of everything we learned in Unit 4. I will put the key ideas on the board, and you can copy them into your notebooks as a study guide."

**Standard 1: Relative Pronouns and Adverbs (L.4.1a) — Lessons 1-3**

Write on the board:
- **Relative pronouns for people:** who (subject), whom (object), whose (possession)
- **Relative pronouns for things:** which (bonus info, commas), that (essential info, no commas)
- **Relative adverbs:** where (place), when (time), why (reason)
- Purpose: Connect clauses and add information to nouns

Say: "Remember the he/him trick for who vs. whom. Remember the comma rule for which vs. that. Remember place/time/reason for where/when/why."

**Standard 2: Progressive Verb Tenses (L.4.1b) — Lessons 4-5**

Write on the board:
- **Present progressive:** am/is/are + verb-ing (happening now)
- **Past progressive:** was/were + verb-ing (was happening at a past time)
- **Future progressive:** will be + verb-ing (will be happening at a future time)
- Spelling rules: drop silent e, double final consonant (CVC)

Say: "Progressive means 'in progress' — the action is ongoing at a specific time."

**Standard 3: Ordering Adjectives (L.4.1d) — Lesson 6**

Write on the board:
- **OSASCOMP:** Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Color, Origin, Material, Purpose
- Use the sound test if unsure

Say: "Most of the time, two or three adjectives are enough. Put them in OSASCOMP order."

**Standard 4: Precise Word Choice and Register (L.4.3a) — Lessons 7-8**

Write on the board:
- Replace vague words (nice, good, bad, said, went) with precise ones
- Formal language: no contractions, no slang, polished tone — for school and professional settings
- Informal language: contractions, casual tone — for friends and personal writing

Say: "Choose words that paint a clear picture and match your audience."

**Standard 5: Capitalization (L.4.2a) — Lesson 9**

Write on the board:
- Capitalize: proper nouns, titles before names, geographical names, historical periods/events, organizations, titles of works
- Do NOT capitalize: common nouns, titles used alone, general descriptions

Say: "If it is the specific name of something, capitalize it. If it is a general word, do not."

### Worked Examples

Say: "Let me show you one combined example that uses multiple skills at once."

Write on the board: *rewrite this sentence with correct grammar and capitalization, and replace any vague words:*

Original: "the man who we met at the museum in washington was real nice and he was talking about the civil war."

Say: "Let us fix this step by step."
- Capitalization: The, Washington, Civil War
- Relative pronoun: "who we met" — we met him, so it should be "whom we met"
- Precise words: "real nice" → "incredibly knowledgeable"
- Progressive tense: "was talking" is already past progressive — correct!

Revised: *The man whom we met at the museum in Washington was incredibly knowledgeable, and he was talking about the Civil War.*

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students apply all skills in a game-like review format.

Say: "We are going to play Grammar Challenge. I will read a question, and you write the answer in your notebook. After each question, we will check the answer together."

**Round 1: Relative Pronouns and Adverbs**

1. Fill in the blank: The librarian ______ helped me find the book was very patient. (who / whom)
2. Combine using "which" or "that": The painting won first prize. The painting hangs in the hallway.
3. Fill in the blank: Saturday is the day ______ we go to the farmers market. (where / when / why)

**Round 2: Progressive Tenses**

4. Change to present progressive: The students study for the test.
5. Change to past progressive: The rain fell all afternoon.
6. Change to future progressive: We will eat dinner at six o'clock.

**Round 3: Adjective Order**

7. Put in order: (old, beautiful, wooden) rocking chair → a ______ ______ ______ rocking chair
8. Fix the order: She wore a cotton long white dress.

**Round 4: Precise Words and Register**

9. Replace the vague word: The sunset was **nice**.
10. Rewrite in formal language: That science project was super cool and I loved it.

**Round 5: Capitalization**

11. Correct the capitalization: we read the book island of the blue dolphins by scott o'dell in november.
12. Should "president" be capitalized? "The president of the garden club spoke at the meeting."

Say: "Great work! Let us go through the answers together."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students complete a mixed-skills review independently, simulating assessment conditions.

**Part A: Mixed Grammar Practice (one item per standard)**

1. **(L.4.1a)** Combine using a relative pronoun or adverb: The scientist discovered a new insect. The scientist's lab is at the university.
2. **(L.4.1b)** Write a sentence about cooking in the future progressive tense.
3. **(L.4.1d)** Arrange the adjectives correctly: (Italian, delicious, small) restaurant → a ______ ______ ______ restaurant
4. **(L.4.3a)** Revise for precise word choice: "The thing in the sky was big and bright."
5. **(L.4.2a)** Correct the capitalization: last spring, our family visited the grand canyon and hiked the bright angel trail.

**Part B: Error Hunt — Find and fix ALL the errors in this paragraph.**

"Yesterday at four o'clock, me and my friends was playing soccer in lincoln park. the girl which scored the winning goal is elena, who's team always wins. we was real happy about the game. the park where the game was at is on maple street near jefferson elementary school."

Errors to find:
- "me and my friends" → "my friends and I"
- "was playing" → "were playing" (plural subject)
- "lincoln park" → "Lincoln Park"
- "the girl which" → "the girl who" (people use who, not which)
- "who's team" → "whose team" (possessive, not contraction)
- "we was" → "we were"
- "real happy" → "really happy" or a more precise word like "thrilled"
- "the park where the game was at" → "the park where the game took place" (remove dangling preposition)
- "maple street" → "Maple Street"
- "jefferson elementary school" → "Jefferson Elementary School"

**Part C: Grammar Showcase — Write a short paragraph (4-5 sentences) about a school event, real or imagined. Include at least one example of each:**
- A relative pronoun or adverb
- A progressive tense verb
- Two or more correctly ordered adjectives
- At least one precise word (underline it)
- Correct capitalization throughout

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A and B.
**Stretch:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts. In Part C, include all five relative pronouns (who, whom, whose, which, that) across your paragraph.

### Interleaved Review
13. Write the past tense of these irregular verbs: think, bring, teach. *(Grade 3 review: L.3.1d)*
14. Add commas and quotation marks: The coach shouted Great job everyone! *(Grade 3 review: L.3.2c)*

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Fill in the relative pronoun: The firefighter ______ rescued the kitten received a medal. (who / whom / whose)
2. Change to past progressive: The bird sang in the tree.
3. Correct all errors: "we read a good french book called the little prince at oak hill school last tuesday."

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which standard(s) the student missed and provide targeted review before the unit assessment
- Have the student review the anchor charts and their study guide notes
- Provide 2-3 additional practice items for the missed standard

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is well-prepared for the unit assessment
- Celebrate their growth in grammar skills throughout the unit

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Grammar Awards:** Create "Grammar Awards" for classmates or fictional characters. Example: "The Best Use of a Relative Pronoun Award goes to the student who always combines sentences smoothly." Each award must use correct grammar.
2. **Grammar Comic Strip:** Create a four-panel comic strip where each panel demonstrates a different grammar skill from the unit. Include speech bubbles with correct grammar.
3. **Teach a Younger Student:** Write a one-page "grammar guide" for a second or third grader explaining one of the five skills in simple language. Include examples and a practice question.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This is a review lesson, so the pace should be lively and encouraging. Use the game format to keep energy high.
- Pay attention to which standards students struggle with most during the review. Use this data to plan any last-minute reteaching before the unit assessment.
- The Error Hunt paragraph in Part B is intentionally loaded with multiple error types. Some students may not find all errors — that is fine. The goal is to practice applying multiple grammar lenses at once.
- The Grammar Showcase (Part C) is an excellent formative assessment. If a student can produce a paragraph that demonstrates all five skills correctly, they are ready for the unit assessment.
- Consider letting students share their Grammar Showcase paragraphs with a partner or the group. Public sharing builds confidence and creates a sense of celebration.
- If time permits, end with a brief celebration: acknowledge growth, hand out certificates, or simply say: "You came into this unit knowing basic grammar. Now you can use relative pronouns, progressive tenses, adjective order, precise words, and advanced capitalization. That is real progress."
