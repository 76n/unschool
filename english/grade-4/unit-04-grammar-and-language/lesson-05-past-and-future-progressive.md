# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 5

## Topic
Past Progressive and Future Progressive Tenses

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Form and use the past progressive tense (was/were + verb-ing)
- Form and use the future progressive tense (will be + verb-ing)
- Distinguish among present, past, and future progressive tenses

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of present progressive tense from Lesson 4
- Ability to form the -ing form of verbs (spelling rules from Lesson 4)
- Familiarity with simple past and simple future tenses

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Anchor chart from Lesson 4 (add past and future progressive formulas)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review present progressive and lead students to wonder about progressive actions in the past and future.

**Activity: When Was the Action Happening?**

Write on the board:
1. I am eating breakfast.
2. I was eating breakfast when the phone rang.
3. At this time tomorrow, I will be eating breakfast.

Say: "Read all three sentences. What do they have in common?" Allow responses.

Say: "All three sentences use a form of 'eat' with -ing. They all show an action in progress. But they are happening at different times. Sentence 1 is right now — present progressive. Sentence 2 is in the past. Sentence 3 is in the future. Today we are going to learn how to form the past progressive and the future progressive."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the formulas for past progressive and future progressive and when each is used.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Past progressive tense:** Shows an action that was in progress at a specific time in the past. Formula: was/were + verb-ing.
- **Future progressive tense:** Shows an action that will be in progress at a specific time in the future. Formula: will be + verb-ing.
- **Was:** Helping verb for past progressive with I, he, she, it, and singular nouns.
- **Were:** Helping verb for past progressive with we, you, they, and plural nouns.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Yesterday we learned the present progressive tense: am/is/are + verb-ing. Today we are adding two more progressive tenses."

Write on the board:
| Tense | Formula | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Present Progressive | am/is/are + verb-ing | She is reading a book. |
| Past Progressive | was/were + verb-ing | She was reading a book. |
| Future Progressive | will be + verb-ing | She will be reading a book. |

**Teaching PAST PROGRESSIVE:**

Say: "The past progressive tense shows an action that was happening at a certain time in the past. It was already in progress — not finished yet at that moment."

Write on the board: *At eight o'clock last night, I was watching a movie with my family.*

Say: "If you picture a timeline, at eight o'clock the movie watching was already going on. It was in the middle of happening."

Say: "We use 'was' with I, he, she, and it. We use 'were' with we, you, and they."

Write on the board:
- I **was** sleeping.
- He **was** sleeping.
- We **were** sleeping.
- They **were** sleeping.

Say: "The past progressive is often used when one action was already happening and then something else interrupted it."

Write on the board: *The students were taking a test when the fire alarm rang.*

Say: "The test-taking was in progress. Then the fire alarm interrupted it."

**Teaching FUTURE PROGRESSIVE:**

Say: "The future progressive tense shows an action that will be in progress at a specific time in the future."

Write on the board: *At noon tomorrow, we will be eating lunch in the cafeteria.*

Say: "If you imagine tomorrow at noon, the eating will already be in progress. The formula is simple — it is the same for every subject."

Write on the board:
- I **will be** waiting.
- She **will be** waiting.
- They **will be** waiting.

Say: "Notice that 'will be' stays the same no matter who the subject is. That makes future progressive the easiest one to form!"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (past progressive):**
- Simple past: The dog barked at the mail carrier.
- Past progressive: *The dog was barking at the mail carrier when we opened the door.*
- Explanation: "The barking was already in progress. Subject is 'the dog' (it), so we use 'was.'"

**Example 2 (past progressive):**
- Simple past: The kids played soccer.
- Past progressive: *The kids were playing soccer all afternoon.*
- Explanation: "The playing was ongoing. Subject is 'the kids' (they), so we use 'were.'"

**Example 3 (future progressive):**
- Simple future: We will study for the science test.
- Future progressive: *We will be studying for the science test after dinner tonight.*
- Explanation: "The studying will be in progress at that future time. Use 'will be' + studying."

**Example 4 (future progressive):**
- Simple future: The train will arrive at the station.
- Future progressive: *By five o'clock, the train will be arriving at the station.*
- Explanation: "At five o'clock, the arrival process will be in progress."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice forming past and future progressive with facilitator support.

Say: "Let us practice both tenses. Write each answer in your notebook."

**Part A: Rewrite each sentence in the past progressive tense.**

1. The baby cried during the movie. → The baby ______ during the movie.
2. We hiked through the forest. → We ______ through the forest when it started to rain.
3. My brother built a model airplane. → My brother ______ a model airplane all evening.
4. The chef prepared dinner. → The chef ______ dinner when the guests arrived.
5. I read my favorite comic book. → I ______ my favorite comic book before bed.

Say: "For number 1, the subject is 'the baby' — that is like 'it,' so we use 'was.' Cry becomes crying — just add -ing. The baby was crying during the movie."

**Part B: Rewrite each sentence in the future progressive tense.**

6. The parade will start at ten o'clock. → At ten o'clock, the parade ______.
7. She will practice the piano after school. → After school, she ______ the piano.
8. They will travel across the country. → This summer, they ______ across the country.

Say: "For number 6, future progressive is always 'will be' + verb-ing. The parade will be starting at ten o'clock."

**Part C: Identify the tense — present, past, or future progressive.**

9. The monkeys are swinging from the branches.
10. Last night at seven, we were eating tacos for dinner.
11. This time next week, I will be swimming in the ocean.
12. The teacher was explaining the assignment when the bell rang.

---

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently form and use all three progressive tenses.

**Part A: Change each sentence to the progressive tense indicated in parentheses.**

1. The wind blows through the trees. (present progressive)
2. The students worked on their group project. (past progressive)
3. We will explore the tide pools at the beach. (future progressive)
4. She dances in the talent show. (present progressive)
5. The painter decorated the gym for the school dance. (past progressive)

**Part B: Fill in the blank with the correct progressive form of the verb.**

6. Right now, the kittens ______ with a ball of yarn. (play — present progressive)
7. Yesterday at this time, I ______ my science poster. (finish — past progressive)
8. Tomorrow morning, the team ______ for the big game. (warm up — future progressive)
9. When the lights went out, we ______ a board game. (play — past progressive)
10. Next Saturday, my family ______ to the state fair. (drive — future progressive)

**Part C: Fix the errors in these progressive sentences.**

11. They was running laps around the track yesterday. (Error: was should be were)
12. I will be swim in the pool this afternoon. (Error: swim should be swimming)
13. She are painting a picture of the sunset. (Error: are should be is)

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A and B.
**Stretch:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts, then write three sentences about a school event: one about what was happening (past progressive), one about what is happening (present progressive), and one about what will be happening (future progressive).

### Interleaved Review
14. Combine using a relative pronoun: The roller coaster is the tallest ride. The roller coaster was built last year. *(Review: Lesson 2)*
15. Choose the correct relative adverb: Recess is the time ______ we play capture the flag. (where / when / why) *(Review: Lesson 3)*

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Change to past progressive: The owl hooted in the tree. (At midnight, the owl ______)
2. Change to future progressive: We will rehearse the school play. (At four o'clock, we ______)
3. Label the tense: "The squirrels were gathering acorns when the storm began." (present / past / future progressive)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the three formulas side by side on the anchor chart
- Practice matching subjects to was/were for past progressive
- Provide additional sentences to convert, one tense at a time

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student moves to Challenge tier in Lesson 6
- Ask the student to write a journal entry about their day using all three progressive tenses

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Progressive Timeline:** Draw a timeline with "past," "now," and "future." Write two progressive-tense sentences for each section of the timeline, all about a single event like a class field trip.
2. **Story Interruptions:** Write five sentences using past progressive where one action is interrupted by another. Example: "I was brushing my teeth when I heard a strange noise outside."
3. **Progressive Tense Reporter:** Pretend you are a news reporter. Write a short news report using all three progressive tenses. Example: "Earlier today, firefighters were battling a blaze on Oak Street. Right now, investigators are searching for the cause. By this evening, crews will be clearing the debris."

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The most common error with past progressive is using "was" with plural subjects (e.g., "They was running"). Reinforce: was = singular (I, he, she, it), were = plural (we, you, they).
- Future progressive is the easiest to form because "will be" does not change. Students usually master it quickly.
- The concept of an action "in progress at a specific time" is central to all progressive tenses. Use timeline drawings on the board to visualize this — draw an arrow for the ongoing action and a dot for the specific time point.
- Some students may ask about "was going to" vs. "will be." Clarify that "was going to" is a different construction (past intention) and is not the same as future progressive.
- If students are ready, briefly mention that the past progressive is often used with "when" or "while" to show two simultaneous past actions: "While I was reading, my brother was playing drums." This is not required for mastery but builds awareness.
- This lesson completes the progressive verb tenses standard (L.4.1b). Students will review progressive tenses throughout the remaining lessons via interleaved review.
