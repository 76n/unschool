# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 3

## Topic
Relative Adverbs: Where, When, Why

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define relative adverb and explain how it differs from a relative pronoun
- Use where, when, and why to introduce relative clauses
- Combine two sentences into one using a relative adverb

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of relative pronouns who, whom, whose, which, and that (Lessons 1-2)
- Ability to identify nouns related to places, times, and reasons
- Experience combining sentences with relative pronouns

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Anchor chart paper for relative adverbs reference

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review relative pronouns and introduce the concept that some connecting words are adverbs, not pronouns.

**Activity: Pronoun or Not?**

Write on the board:
1. The park where we play soccer has a new fountain.
2. The girl who sits next to me likes drawing cats.

Say: "Look at both sentences. In sentence 2, we know 'who' is a relative pronoun — it stands in for a person. But look at sentence 1. The word 'where' is not replacing a noun. It is telling us something about a place. 'Where' is not a pronoun — it is an adverb."

Say: "Today we are going to learn about three relative adverbs: where, when, and why. These words do the same job as relative pronouns — they connect clauses — but instead of replacing nouns, they tell us about places, times, and reasons."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students how where, when, and why function as relative adverbs that introduce clauses.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Relative adverb:** A word that introduces a clause and tells about a place, time, or reason. The three relative adverbs are where, when, and why.
- **Where:** Introduces a clause about a place.
- **When:** Introduces a clause about a time.
- **Why:** Introduces a clause about a reason.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Relative adverbs work just like relative pronouns — they connect a main sentence to extra information. The difference is what kind of information they add."

Write on the board:
- **Where** → place
- **When** → time
- **Why** → reason

**Teaching WHERE:**

Say: "We use 'where' to give information about a place."

Write on the board: *The bakery where my mom works smells like cinnamon every morning.*

Say: "'Where my mom works' tells us about the bakery — it tells us which bakery by describing a place. The word 'where' connects the clause to the noun 'bakery.'"

**Teaching WHEN:**

Say: "We use 'when' to give information about a time."

Write on the board: *Summer is the season when fireflies light up the backyard.*

Say: "'When fireflies light up the backyard' tells us about summer — it tells us about that time. The word 'when' connects the clause to the noun 'season.'"

**Teaching WHY:**

Say: "We use 'why' to give information about a reason."

Write on the board: *Nobody knows the reason why the cafeteria stopped serving tacos.*

Say: "'Why the cafeteria stopped serving tacos' tells us about the reason. The word 'why' connects the clause to the noun 'reason.' Notice that 'why' almost always follows the word 'reason.'"

Say: "Here is a helpful pattern: If the noun is a place, try 'where.' If the noun is a time, try 'when.' If the noun is 'reason,' try 'why.'"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (where):**
- Sentence A: The library is very quiet.
- Sentence B: We study at the library.
- Combined: *The library where we study is very quiet.*
- Explanation: "The library is a place, so we use 'where' to connect the clause."

**Example 2 (when):**
- Sentence A: Winter is my favorite time of year.
- Sentence B: We get to build snow forts in winter.
- Combined: *Winter is my favorite time of year when we get to build snow forts.*
- Explanation: "Winter is a time, so we use 'when.'"

**Example 3 (why):**
- Sentence A: I understand the reason now.
- Sentence B: You were late for that reason.
- Combined: *I understand the reason why you were late.*
- Explanation: "'Reason' is the noun, and we are explaining it, so we use 'why.'"

**Example 4 (where):**
- Sentence A: The corner store has the best popsicles.
- Sentence B: My grandpa buys his newspaper at the corner store.
- Combined: *The corner store where my grandpa buys his newspaper has the best popsicles.*
- Explanation: "The corner store is a place — use 'where.'"

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice selecting and using relative adverbs with facilitator support.

Say: "Let us practice together. Write each answer in your notebook."

**Part A: Choose the correct relative adverb (where, when, or why).**

1. The playground ______ we have recess has a new climbing wall.
2. Morning is the time ______ the birds sing the loudest.
3. Can you tell me the reason ______ the game was canceled?
4. The museum ______ the dinosaur skeletons are displayed is downtown.
5. Saturday is the day ______ our family goes to the farmers market.

Say: "For number 1, ask: Is the playground a place, a time, or a reason? It is a place, so we use 'where.' The playground where we have recess has a new climbing wall."

Walk through each item, having students identify the noun as a place, time, or reason.

**Part B: Combine the sentences using where, when, or why.**

6. The beach is my favorite vacation spot. We collect seashells at the beach.
7. I finally know the reason. The dog keeps barking for that reason.
8. Autumn is a beautiful season. The leaves change color in autumn.

Say: "For number 6, 'beach' is a place, so we use 'where': The beach where we collect seashells is my favorite vacation spot."

**Part C: Is it a relative pronoun or a relative adverb? Label each underlined word.**

9. The teacher **who** organized the field trip is Mr. Garcia.
10. The park **where** we had the picnic has a pond with ducks.
11. The recipe **that** my grandmother uses calls for cinnamon.
12. Friday is the day **when** we have art class.

Say: "For number 9, 'who' replaces a noun — it stands in for 'the teacher.' That makes it a relative pronoun. For number 10, 'where' tells about a place. It does not replace a noun — that makes it a relative adverb."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently use relative adverbs to connect clauses.

**Part A: Fill in where, when, or why.**

1. The hospital ______ my aunt works is on Maple Street.
2. Do you remember the year ______ we moved to this neighborhood?
3. She explained the reason ______ she chose that book for her report.
4. The farm ______ we picked strawberries also has a corn maze.
5. Bedtime is the hour ______ I read my favorite chapter books.

**Part B: Combine each pair into one sentence using a relative adverb.**

6. The theater shows old movies on weekends. My cousins and I go to the theater.
7. January is a cold month. Ponds freeze over in January.
8. I do not understand the reason. The electricity went out for some reason.

**Part C: Choose the correct word — relative pronoun or relative adverb.**

9. The cabin ______ we stayed last summer had no electricity. (where / which)
10. The girl ______ backpack is purple is my best friend. (whose / where)
11. That was the moment ______ everything changed. (who / when)

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A and B.
**Stretch:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts, then write a short paragraph (4-5 sentences) about your neighborhood or school using at least one where, one when, and one why.

### Interleaved Review
12. Choose the correct relative pronoun: The magician ______ we saw at the fair made a rabbit disappear. (who / whom) *(Review: Lesson 1)*
13. Add commas if needed: The Great Wall of China ______ is over 13,000 miles long can be seen from nearby hills. *(Review: Lesson 2 — which/that punctuation)*

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Fill in the blank: The restaurant ______ we eat breakfast every Sunday has the best pancakes.
2. Combine these sentences: Spring is a wonderful season. Flowers bloom in spring.
3. Is "where" a relative pronoun or a relative adverb?

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the place/time/reason categorization with the student
- Provide additional noun-sorting practice: Is this a place, time, or reason?
- Create a reference card with where = place, when = time, why = reason

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student moves to Challenge tier in Lesson 4
- Encourage student to use relative adverbs in their next writing assignment

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Adverb Map:** Draw a map of your school or neighborhood. Label five places and write a sentence about each one using "where." Example: "The cafeteria where we eat lunch is next to the gym."
2. **Timeline Sentences:** Create a timeline of five important events in your life. Write a sentence about each one using "when." Example: "Second grade was the year when I learned to ride a bike."
3. **Relative Word Sort:** Write ten sentences on index cards, some with relative pronouns and some with relative adverbs. Sort them into two piles and explain your reasoning to a partner.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Students sometimes confuse "where," "when," and "why" as question words with their use as relative adverbs. Point out that in questions, these words start the sentence ("Where is the library?"), but as relative adverbs, they appear in the middle of a sentence connecting a clause to a noun.
- "Why" is the least commonly used relative adverb and almost always follows the word "reason." Some style guides suggest dropping "why" after "reason" (e.g., "the reason he left" instead of "the reason why he left"). Both forms are acceptable at this level.
- If students ask whether to use commas with relative adverbs, follow the same essential vs. bonus test from Lesson 2. In most cases at this level, relative adverb clauses are restrictive (essential) and do not need commas.
- This lesson completes the relative pronouns and relative adverbs standard (L.4.1a). Students will review these skills throughout the rest of the unit via interleaved review items.
- Consider creating a combined anchor chart showing all relative pronouns (who, whom, whose, which, that) and all relative adverbs (where, when, why) side by side.
