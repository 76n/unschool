# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 4

## Topic
Present Progressive Tense

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define present progressive tense and explain when it is used
- Form the present progressive using am/is/are + the -ing form of a verb
- Distinguish between simple present tense and present progressive tense

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of simple present tense (e.g., I walk, she runs)
- Ability to form regular and irregular verbs in present and past tense (Grade 3, L.3.1d)
- Familiarity with subject-verb agreement (I am, she is, they are)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Anchor chart paper for progressive tense formula

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Help students notice the difference between an action that happens regularly and an action happening right now.

**Activity: Right Now vs. Every Day**

Say: "I am going to say two sentences. Listen carefully and tell me which one describes something happening right now, at this very moment."

Read aloud:
- Pair 1: "Marcus plays basketball." vs. "Marcus is playing basketball."
- Pair 2: "The cat sleeps on the couch." vs. "The cat is sleeping on the couch."

Say: "Which sentence in each pair sounds like the action is happening right now?" Allow responses.

Say: "The sentences with 'is playing' and 'is sleeping' describe actions happening at this very moment. That is called the present progressive tense. The other sentences — 'plays' and 'sleeps' — describe things that happen regularly or generally. Today, we are going to learn how to form and use the present progressive tense."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students the formula for present progressive and when to use it.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Progressive tense:** A verb tense that shows an action is in progress — it is ongoing or continuing.
- **Present progressive tense:** Shows an action that is happening right now, at this moment. Formula: am/is/are + verb-ing.
- **Helping verb:** A verb that comes before the main verb to help show tense. In present progressive, the helping verbs are am, is, and are.
- **-ing form:** The form of a verb made by adding -ing to the base (e.g., run → running, eat → eating).

### Instruction Notes

Say: "The present progressive tense tells us that something is happening right now, as we speak. To form it, we need two parts."

Write on the board:
**Present Progressive = am / is / are + verb-ing**

Say: "The first part is a helping verb — am, is, or are. Which one we use depends on the subject."

Write on the board:
- I → **am** (I am reading.)
- He / She / It → **is** (She is reading.)
- We / You / They → **are** (They are reading.)

Say: "The second part is the main verb with -ing added to the end. Let us look at some spelling rules for adding -ing."

**Spelling Rules for -ing:**

Write on the board:
1. Most verbs: Just add -ing. (walk → walking, read → reading, play → playing)
2. Verbs ending in silent e: Drop the e, add -ing. (make → making, ride → riding, write → writing)
3. Short verbs ending in consonant-vowel-consonant: Double the last letter, add -ing. (run → running, sit → sitting, swim → swimming)

Say: "Now let us talk about when we use present progressive versus simple present."

Write on the board:
- **Simple present:** I eat lunch at noon. (This happens regularly.)
- **Present progressive:** I am eating lunch right now. (This is happening at this moment.)

Say: "The simple present is for habits, routines, and general truths. The present progressive is for actions happening right now or around the current time."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:**
- Base sentence: The dog chases the squirrel.
- Present progressive: *The dog is chasing the squirrel.*
- Explanation: "The subject is 'the dog' (it), so we use 'is.' Chase ends in silent e, so we drop the e and add -ing: chasing."

**Example 2:**
- Base sentence: We study for the spelling test.
- Present progressive: *We are studying for the spelling test.*
- Explanation: "The subject is 'we,' so we use 'are.' Study becomes studying — just add -ing."

**Example 3:**
- Base sentence: I swim in the lake.
- Present progressive: *I am swimming in the lake.*
- Explanation: "The subject is 'I,' so we use 'am.' Swim ends in consonant-vowel-consonant, so we double the m: swimming."

**Example 4:**
- Base sentence: The birds sing in the tree.
- Present progressive: *The birds are singing in the tree.*
- Explanation: "The subject is 'the birds' (they), so we use 'are.' Sing becomes singing."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice forming present progressive with facilitator support.

Say: "Let us practice forming the present progressive tense. Write each answer in your notebook."

**Part A: Rewrite each sentence in the present progressive tense.**

1. The children play in the park. → The children ______ in the park.
2. My sister bakes cookies. → My sister ______ cookies.
3. I draw a picture of my cat. → I ______ a picture of my cat.
4. The rain falls on the roof. → The rain ______ on the roof.
5. We plan a surprise party. → We ______ a surprise party.

Say: "For number 1, the subject is 'the children' — that is like 'they,' so we use 'are.' Play just gets -ing: playing. The children are playing in the park."

Say: "For number 2, the subject is 'my sister' — that is like 'she,' so we use 'is.' Bake ends in silent e, so we drop the e: baking. My sister is baking cookies."

Walk through each item, emphasizing the helping verb choice and -ing spelling.

**Part B: Is it simple present or present progressive? Label each sentence.**

6. The squirrel hides acorns in the yard.
7. My dad is washing the car right now.
8. We eat dinner at six o'clock every night.
9. Look — the baby is crawling across the floor!
10. The Earth orbits the sun.

Say: "For number 6, 'hides' is a simple present verb — no helping verb, no -ing. This is simple present — something the squirrel does regularly. For number 7, 'is washing' has a helping verb and -ing. The words 'right now' confirm it is present progressive."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently form and use present progressive tense.

**Part A: Change each sentence from simple present to present progressive.**

1. The teacher explains the math problem.
2. My friends ride their bikes to school.
3. The chef chops vegetables for the soup.
4. I write a letter to my pen pal.
5. The snow melts on the sidewalk.

**Part B: Fill in the blank with the correct present progressive form of the verb in parentheses.**

6. Right now, the students ______ a science experiment. (conduct)
7. My little brother ______ his shoes for the first time. (tie)
8. The orchestra ______ for the spring concert. (practice)
9. I ______ for my lost library book. (search)
10. The sun ______ behind the mountains. (set)

**Part C: Fix the errors in these present progressive sentences.**

11. The girls is dancing on the stage. (Error: subject-verb agreement)
12. My dog is runing around the backyard. (Error: spelling of -ing form)
13. We am building a treehouse. (Error: wrong helping verb)

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A and B.
**Stretch:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts, then write a "snapshot paragraph" of 4-5 sentences describing what is happening in your classroom right now using present progressive. Every sentence must use a different verb.

### Interleaved Review
14. Fill in the correct relative adverb: The park ______ we fly kites has a hill with a great view. (where / when / why) *(Review: Lesson 3)*
15. Choose the correct relative pronoun: The book ______ I read last week was about a brave knight. (which / that) *(Review: Lesson 2)*

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Change to present progressive: The cat climbs the tree.
2. Fill in the blank: Right now, my mom ______ dinner for the whole family. (cook)
3. Which sentence is present progressive? (a) She walks to school. (b) She is walking to school.

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the formula: am/is/are + verb-ing
- Practice matching subjects to helping verbs (I = am, he/she/it = is, we/you/they = are)
- Provide a list of five verbs and have the student write the -ing form of each

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student moves to Challenge tier in Lesson 5
- Ask the student to find three present progressive sentences in a book

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Action Snapshot:** Look out the window or around the room. Write ten sentences describing what you see happening right now, all in present progressive tense.
2. **Progressive Story Starter:** Begin a story with: "It is a rainy afternoon, and everyone in the neighborhood is doing something different." Continue the story using present progressive for every verb.
3. **Simple vs. Progressive Sort:** Write ten sentences on index cards — five in simple present and five in present progressive. Trade with a partner and sort each other's cards.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The most common errors with present progressive are (1) choosing the wrong helping verb (e.g., "I is running") and (2) misspelling the -ing form (e.g., "runing" instead of "running," "makeing" instead of "making"). Address both explicitly.
- Students who are English language learners may overuse the progressive tense for habitual actions (e.g., "I am going to school every day" instead of "I go to school every day"). Gently correct this by emphasizing the "right now" meaning.
- Some students may ask about verbs that are not usually used in progressive form (stative verbs like "know," "believe," "own"). At this level, simply say: "Some verbs describe states, not actions. We do not usually say 'I am knowing the answer.' We say 'I know the answer.' These verbs describe how things are, not what is happening."
- The -ing spelling rules are important to teach explicitly. Consider posting them on an anchor chart that students can reference during writing time.
- This lesson introduces only the present progressive. Past and future progressive will follow in Lesson 5.
