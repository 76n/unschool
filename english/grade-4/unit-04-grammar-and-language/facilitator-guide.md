# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 4: Grammar and Language

## Facilitator Guide

### Unit Overview
Unit 4 focuses on grammar and language conventions that strengthen students' writing and speaking. Students learn to use relative pronouns and relative adverbs to combine sentences and add detail, form and use all three progressive verb tenses, order adjectives according to conventional English patterns, choose precise words and phrases for effect, and apply correct capitalization rules. The unit culminates with a review celebration and a comprehensive assessment covering all five standards.

### Standards Addressed
- **L.4.1a** — Use relative pronouns (who, whom, whose, which, that) and relative adverbs (where, when, why)
- **L.4.1b** — Form and use the progressive verb tenses (present, past, future)
- **L.4.1d** — Order adjectives within sentences according to conventional patterns
- **L.4.3a** — Choose words and phrases to convey ideas precisely
- **L.4.2a** — Use correct capitalization

### Prerequisite Skills (Grade 3)
- Form and use regular and irregular verbs (L.3.1d)
- Form and use comparative and superlative adjectives (L.3.1g)
- Use commas and quotation marks in dialogue (L.3.2c)
- Use dictionaries and glossaries to determine word meanings (L.3.4d)

### Facilitator Tips
- **Anchor charts:** Create and display anchor charts for relative pronouns, relative adverbs, progressive tense formulas, and adjective order. Leave them posted throughout the unit so students can reference them.
- **Consistent terminology:** Use the terms "relative pronoun," "relative clause," "progressive tense," and "adjective order" consistently. Introduce kid-friendly definitions first, then layer in formal grammar vocabulary.
- **Sentence combining:** Lessons 1-3 focus heavily on combining sentences. Model the thinking process aloud, showing students how two short sentences become one complex sentence with a relative pronoun or adverb.
- **Progressive tense formulas:** In Lessons 4-5, write the formulas on the board (am/is/are + -ing, was/were + -ing, will be + -ing). Let students refer to these while practicing.
- **Adjective order:** Lesson 6 uses the mnemonic OSASCOMP (Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Color, Origin, Material, Purpose). Consider making a poster or providing bookmarks with this pattern.
- **Word choice lessons:** Lessons 7-8 connect grammar to writing. Encourage students to apply precise word choice and audience awareness in all their writing going forward.
- **Interleaved review:** Every lesson includes 1-2 review items from previous lessons or Grade 3 skills. Use these to keep earlier concepts fresh and identify students who need reteaching.
- **Error correction:** Many practice sections include sentences with errors. Normalize mistakes as learning opportunities. Say: "Finding errors is how we train our grammar brains!"
- **Differentiation:** Each lesson includes On-level, Stretch, and Challenge tiers. Use exit ticket data to assign tiers for the following lesson's independent practice.

### Assessment Guidance
- **Formative:** Use daily exit tickets to monitor mastery. Students who score below mastery criteria should receive targeted reteaching before moving on.
- **Summative:** The unit assessment covers all five standards with a mix of multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, error correction, and short constructed response items. Mastery = 80% overall with at least one correct item per standard.
- **Reteaching:** If more than a third of students miss items for a particular standard, consider a mini-lesson review before proceeding to Unit 5.

### Common Misconceptions
- Students often confuse "who" (subject) and "whom" (object). Use the he/him substitution trick: if "him" works, use "whom."
- Students may struggle to distinguish "which" (nonrestrictive) from "that" (restrictive). Use the comma clue: "which" clauses have commas, "that" clauses do not.
- Progressive tenses can be confused with simple tenses. Emphasize that progressive shows an action in progress at a specific time.
- Adjective order feels intuitive to native speakers but is hard to articulate. Focus on the sound test: "Does it sound right?"
- Students sometimes over-capitalize common nouns or forget to capitalize proper adjectives (e.g., "american" instead of "American").
