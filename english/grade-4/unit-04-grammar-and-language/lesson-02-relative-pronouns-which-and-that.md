# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 2

## Topic
Relative Pronouns: Which and That

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Use which and that as relative pronouns to add information about things
- Distinguish between restrictive clauses (that) and nonrestrictive clauses (which)
- Correctly punctuate sentences using which (with commas) and that (without commas)

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of who, whom, and whose from Lesson 1
- Ability to identify nouns and distinguish people from things
- Familiarity with commas in sentences

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Two different colored highlighters (optional, for marking clauses)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review Lesson 1 and transition from pronouns for people to pronouns for things.

**Activity: People or Things?**

Say: "Yesterday we learned three relative pronouns — who, whom, and whose — and we said they are used for people. But what about things? What if I want to give extra information about an object, a place, or an animal?"

Write on the board:
1. The bicycle has a flat tire. The bicycle belongs to Marcus.
2. The book was exciting. I read the book last weekend.

Say: "Can we use 'who' here? No — a bicycle and a book are not people. Today we are learning two relative pronouns that work for things: which and that."

Ask: "Can anyone guess how we might combine the first pair?" Allow responses.

Say: "Great thinking! You might say 'The bicycle that belongs to Marcus has a flat tire.' Let us learn exactly when to use 'that' and when to use 'which.'"

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students the difference between that (restrictive) and which (nonrestrictive) and how to punctuate each correctly.

### Key Vocabulary
- **That (relative pronoun):** Introduces a clause that is essential to the meaning of the sentence. Without it, you would not know which thing the sentence is about. No commas.
- **Which (relative pronoun):** Introduces a clause that adds extra, non-essential information. The sentence still makes sense without it. Set off by commas.
- **Restrictive clause:** A clause that restricts or narrows down which thing you mean. Uses "that." Essential information.
- **Nonrestrictive clause:** A clause that adds bonus information. Uses "which." Non-essential information.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Both 'that' and 'which' connect extra information to things. The difference is whether the information is essential or just a bonus."

**Teaching THAT (restrictive):**

Write on the board: *The shoes that have red laces are mine.*

Say: "Imagine there are many pairs of shoes. Which ones are mine? The ones that have red laces. If I take away 'that have red laces,' you would not know which shoes I mean. The clause is essential. When the clause is essential, we use 'that' and we do NOT use commas."

**Teaching WHICH (nonrestrictive):**

Write on the board: *My new sneakers, which have red laces, are very comfortable.*

Say: "Here, you already know which sneakers I mean — my new sneakers. The part about the red laces is just bonus information, like a fun fact. I could remove it and the sentence still makes perfect sense: 'My new sneakers are very comfortable.' When the clause is just bonus information, we use 'which' and we DO use commas."

Say: "Here is an easy way to remember: THAT is a must-have. WHICH is a nice-to-have. And commas are the signal — 'which' always gets commas, 'that' never does."

Write the rule on the board:
- **THAT** = essential information, NO commas
- **WHICH** = bonus information, YES commas

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (that):**
- Sentence A: The sandwich tasted delicious.
- Sentence B: My dad made the sandwich.
- Combined: *The sandwich that my dad made tasted delicious.*
- Explanation: "Which sandwich? The one my dad made. The clause is essential to identify which sandwich, so we use 'that' with no commas."

**Example 2 (which):**
- Sentence A: The school library has over three thousand books.
- Sentence B: The school library was built five years ago.
- Combined: *The school library, which was built five years ago, has over three thousand books.*
- Explanation: "There is only one school library, so we already know which one. The building date is bonus info, so we use 'which' with commas."

**Example 3 (that):**
- Sentence A: The trail is steep and rocky.
- Sentence B: The trail leads to the waterfall.
- Combined: *The trail that leads to the waterfall is steep and rocky.*
- Explanation: "There might be many trails. Which one is steep? The one that leads to the waterfall. Essential info — use 'that.'"

**Example 4 (which):**
- Sentence A: My bicycle is in the garage.
- Sentence B: My bicycle has a silver bell.
- Combined: *My bicycle, which has a silver bell, is in the garage.*
- Explanation: "I only have one bicycle. The silver bell is bonus detail — use 'which' with commas."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice choosing between which and that and punctuating correctly.

Say: "Let us practice together. I will read each item, and you write the answer in your notebook."

**Part A: Choose "which" or "that" and add commas if needed.**

1. The movie ______ we watched on Friday was a comedy.
2. The Eiffel Tower ______ is located in Paris attracts millions of visitors.
3. The jacket ______ has the broken zipper needs to be repaired.
4. Our class pet ______ is a hamster named Peanut escaped from its cage yesterday.
5. The cupcakes ______ have sprinkles on them are for the bake sale.

Say: "For number 1, ask: Is there only one movie, or are we narrowing down which one? We are saying it was 'the movie we watched on Friday' — that narrows it down, so we use 'that' and no commas. The movie that we watched on Friday was a comedy."

Say: "For number 2, there is only one Eiffel Tower, so the location information is bonus. We use 'which' with commas: The Eiffel Tower, which is located in Paris, attracts millions of visitors."

Continue through items 3-5, discussing each.

**Part B: Combine the sentences using which or that.**

6. The backpack is too heavy. The backpack is full of textbooks.
7. The Grand Canyon is in Arizona. The Grand Canyon is one of the Seven Natural Wonders.
8. The app helps you learn Spanish. My sister downloaded the app.

Say: "For number 6, think: Are there many backpacks and we need to identify which one? Yes — the one that is full of textbooks. So: The backpack that is full of textbooks is too heavy."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently apply their understanding of which and that.

**Part A: Fill in "which" or "that" and add commas where needed.**

1. The planet ______ is closest to the sun is Mercury.
2. Mount Everest ______ is the tallest mountain in the world is in Nepal.
3. The cookies ______ you baked yesterday are already gone.
4. Our school bus ______ is bright yellow picks us up at seven thirty.
5. The instrument ______ makes the highest sound in the orchestra is the piccolo.

**Part B: Combine each pair using which or that. Punctuate correctly.**

6. The volcano erupted last year. The volcano is on a remote island.
7. The Statue of Liberty was a gift from France. The Statue of Liberty stands in New York Harbor.
8. The store sells the best ice cream. The store opened last month.

**Part C: Fix the punctuation errors.**

9. The homework, that is due tomorrow, covers fractions. (Error: should use "that" without commas, or "which" with commas depending on meaning — here "that" is wrong with commas)
10. The Amazon River which is the longest river in South America flows through Brazil. (Error: missing commas around "which" clause)

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A and B.
**Stretch:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts, then write two original sentences — one using "that" (no commas) and one using "which" (with commas) — about your favorite place.

### Interleaved Review
11. Fill in the correct relative pronoun: The teacher ______ helped me after school was Mrs. Johnson. (who / whom / whose) *(Review: Lesson 1)*
12. Write the comparative and superlative forms of "happy": happy, ______, ______. *(Grade 3 review: L.3.1g — comparative and superlative adjectives)*

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Fill in the blank and add commas if needed: The Nile River ______ is in Africa is one of the longest rivers in the world.
2. Fill in the blank: The song ______ she sang at the talent show was beautiful.
3. True or False: "That" clauses need commas around them.

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the essential vs. bonus information distinction with simple examples
- Practice the comma test: "Can I remove this clause and still know what the sentence is about?"
- Provide a reference card: THAT = essential, no commas; WHICH = bonus, commas

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student moves to Challenge tier in Lesson 3
- Encourage student to notice "which" and "that" in their reading

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Clause Detective:** Find three sentences in a book or article that use "which" or "that." Copy them and label each clause as essential (restrictive) or bonus (nonrestrictive).
2. **Sentence Expansion:** Take five simple sentences about animals and expand each one by adding a "which" or "that" clause. Example: "The cheetah can run fast" becomes "The cheetah, which is the fastest land animal, can run fast."
3. **Which vs. That Quiz:** Write a five-question quiz about "which" and "that" to give to a classmate. Include an answer key.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The which/that distinction is one of the trickier grammar concepts at this level. Many adult writers struggle with it. Be patient and emphasize the essential vs. bonus test.
- In everyday speech, "that" is often used in place of "which" even for nonrestrictive clauses. Acknowledge this but teach the formal distinction for writing.
- The comma is the key visual cue. If students can remember "which gets commas, that does not," they will get most sentences right.
- Some students may ask about using "which" without commas in British English. Acknowledge that British English sometimes uses "which" for both types but explain that American English follows the that/which distinction.
- For Part C, item 9 has two possible corrections. The most likely intended meaning is that the homework narrows down which homework (essential), so removing the commas and keeping "that" is the expected answer. Accept either correction if the student explains their reasoning.
