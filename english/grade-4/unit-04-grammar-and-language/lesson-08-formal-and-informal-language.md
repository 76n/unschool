# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 8

## Topic
Formal and Informal Language

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Distinguish between formal and informal language
- Identify situations where formal or informal language is appropriate
- Rewrite sentences to shift between formal and informal registers

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of precise word choice from Lesson 7
- Ability to write complete, grammatically correct sentences
- Experience with different types of writing (letters, stories, reports)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Sample texts: a friendly letter and a business letter (written on the board or printed)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Help students notice that we naturally change how we speak depending on the situation.

**Activity: Two Ways to Ask**

Say: "Imagine you want a cookie. How would you ask your best friend? Now imagine you are at a fancy dinner at a restaurant. How would you ask the server for a dessert?"

Allow students to respond. Write their examples on the board.

Say: "You probably said something like 'Hey, can I have a cookie?' to your friend and 'Excuse me, may I please have a dessert?' to the server. We change how we talk depending on who we are talking to and where we are. The words we choose, the way we put sentences together — it all shifts."

Write on the board:
- **Informal:** Hey, can I have a cookie?
- **Formal:** Excuse me, may I please have a dessert?

Say: "Today we are going to learn about two types of language — formal and informal — and when to use each one."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students the characteristics of formal and informal language and when each is appropriate.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Formal language:** Language that follows all grammar rules, uses complete sentences, avoids slang, and sounds polished and professional. Used in school reports, business letters, speeches, and with authority figures.
- **Informal language:** Language that is relaxed and casual. May include contractions, slang, sentence fragments, and a conversational tone. Used with friends, family, and in casual writing like text messages or journal entries.
- **Register:** The level of formality in language. Writers and speakers shift their register to match the audience and purpose.
- **Audience:** The person or people who will read or hear your words. Your audience helps you decide which register to use.
- **Slang:** Very casual words or phrases used in everyday conversation but not appropriate in formal writing (e.g., "cool," "awesome," "gonna").

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Think of language like clothing. You wear different clothes for different occasions. You would not wear pajamas to a job interview, and you would not wear a suit and tie to a pool party. Language works the same way."

**Characteristics of Formal Language:**

Write on the board:
- Uses complete sentences
- Avoids contractions (writes "do not" instead of "don't")
- Avoids slang and casual expressions
- Uses precise vocabulary
- Sounds polished and respectful
- Often uses third person (he, she, they) rather than first person (I)

Say: "When do we use formal language? Here are some examples."

Write on the board:
- A research report for school
- A letter to the principal
- A speech at an assembly
- An application or form
- Talking to a teacher, doctor, or other adult in a professional setting

**Characteristics of Informal Language:**

Write on the board:
- May use contractions (can't, won't, I'm)
- May include slang or casual expressions
- Can use sentence fragments
- Often uses first and second person (I, you, we)
- Has a friendly, conversational tone
- May include humor or personal opinions

Say: "When do we use informal language?"

Write on the board:
- A letter or message to a friend
- A journal or diary entry
- A conversation with a classmate
- A friendly email to a family member
- A creative story with dialogue

**Comparing the Two:**

Write on the board side by side:

| Informal | Formal |
|----------|--------|
| Hey, what's up? | Hello, how are you today? |
| That movie was awesome! | That film was truly impressive. |
| I gotta finish my homework. | I need to complete my homework. |
| Can you help me out? | Would you be willing to assist me? |
| The experiment was super cool. | The experiment produced remarkable results. |

Say: "Notice that the ideas are the same, but the word choices and sentence structures are different. Neither one is wrong — they just fit different situations."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1: Informal to Formal**
- Informal: "Yo, the field trip was so fun! We got to see a ton of animals."
- Formal: "The field trip was very enjoyable. We had the opportunity to observe many different animal species."
- Explanation: "We removed 'Yo' and 'so fun,' replaced 'a ton of' with 'many different,' and made the tone more polished."

**Example 2: Formal to Informal**
- Formal: "I am writing to request permission to use the gymnasium for our after-school club meeting."
- Informal: "Hey, can we use the gym for our club meeting after school?"
- Explanation: "We made it conversational, used a contraction, and simplified the vocabulary."

**Example 3: Identifying Audience**
- Sentence: "I would like to express my gratitude for the generous donation to our school library."
- Audience: This sounds like it is written to a community member or donor — formal.
- Rewritten for a friend: "Thanks so much for the books you gave to our library! That was really cool of you."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice identifying and shifting between formal and informal language.

Say: "Let us practice together. Write your answers in your notebook."

**Part A: Label each sentence as formal or informal.**

1. Dear Ms. Rivera, I am writing to invite you to our class presentation on Friday.
2. Hey Sam, wanna come over after school?
3. The results of the survey indicate that most students prefer pizza for lunch.
4. OMG, that science experiment was the best thing ever!
5. It would be greatly appreciated if you could respond by Thursday.

Say: "For number 1, this has a greeting with a title (Ms. Rivera), a complete sentence with no contractions, and a polished tone. This is formal."

Say: "For number 2, it starts with 'Hey,' uses 'wanna' (a slang contraction), and sounds like a friend talking. This is informal."

Walk through each item.

**Part B: Rewrite each informal sentence in formal language.**

6. That book was super boring and I didn't like it at all.
7. Can I borrow your pencil real quick?
8. The food at the party was totally amazing.

Say: "For number 6, let us think about what makes this informal: 'super boring,' 'didn't,' and the casual tone. A formal version might be: 'I did not find the book engaging and would not recommend it.' We removed the slang and the contraction."

**Part C: Rewrite each formal sentence in informal language.**

9. I respectfully request that you consider my application for the student council position.
10. The presentation was well-organized and demonstrated a thorough understanding of the topic.

---

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently identify audience, register, and shift between formal and informal language.

**Part A: Label each sentence as formal or informal.**

1. The committee has decided to postpone the event until further notice.
2. Guess what? We're going to the beach this weekend!
3. I would be honored to participate in the school's annual spelling competition.
4. That math test was crazy hard.
5. Please accept my sincere apology for the inconvenience.

**Part B: Rewrite each sentence in the register indicated.**

6. "The concert was lots of fun and the band was awesome!" → Rewrite in formal language.
7. "I wish to express my disappointment regarding the cancellation of the art program." → Rewrite in informal language.
8. "Yo, that was the coolest thing I ever saw!" → Rewrite in formal language.
9. "The library will be closed for renovations beginning next Monday." → Rewrite in informal language.

**Part C: Read each situation. Write one sentence using the appropriate register (formal or informal).**

10. You are writing a thank-you note to a neighbor who helped you find your lost cat.
11. You are texting a friend about your weekend plans.
12. You are writing the opening of a research report about dolphins.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A and B.
**Stretch:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts, then write two short letters about the same topic (e.g., asking to start a school garden) — one to a friend and one to the principal. Each should be 3-4 sentences using the appropriate register.

### Interleaved Review
13. Replace the vague word with a precise one: The dog **went** down the street. *(Review: Lesson 7)*
14. Put the adjectives in the correct order: (silver, tiny, antique) watch → a ______ ______ ______ watch *(Review: Lesson 6)*

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Label as formal or informal: "Would you kindly hold the door open for me?"
2. Rewrite in formal language: "The pizza here is the best ever!"
3. Name one situation where you would use formal language.

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the formal vs. informal characteristics chart
- Practice with more side-by-side comparisons
- Ask the student to think about "who is listening" before choosing words

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student moves to Challenge tier in Lesson 9
- Encourage the student to notice formal and informal language in books, signs, and conversations

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Register Flip:** Take a paragraph from a textbook (formal) and rewrite it as if you are telling the same information to a friend. Then take a casual email and rewrite it as a formal letter.
2. **Audience Detective:** Collect three different types of writing (a news article, a friend's note, a menu). For each one, identify the audience and whether the register is formal, informal, or somewhere in between.
3. **Formal/Informal Dialogue:** Write a short skit where a character accidentally uses formal language with friends and informal language with the principal. Make it humorous to show why matching register to audience matters.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Students at this age are naturally more comfortable with informal language. The challenge is helping them produce formal language, not recognize it. Spend more practice time on converting informal to formal.
- Avoid framing informal language as "wrong" or "bad." Both registers are valid — the key is knowing when each one fits. Say: "Informal language is not wrong. It is just the right language for casual situations, not for a school report."
- Some students may bring up texting language (LOL, BRB, u instead of you). Acknowledge that text shortcuts exist but explain that they are a very informal register — fine for personal messages but never for school writing.
- Contractions are a helpful litmus test: formal writing avoids them, informal writing uses them freely. This is an easy rule for students to remember and apply.
- This lesson pairs with Lesson 7 (precise word choice) to complete the L.4.3a standard. Together they teach students that how you say something matters as much as what you say.
- If time allows, discuss how register can vary even within formal writing — a letter to a teacher is formal but might be friendlier than a letter to a company.
