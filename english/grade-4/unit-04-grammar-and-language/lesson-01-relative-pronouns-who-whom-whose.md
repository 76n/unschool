# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 1

## Topic
Relative Pronouns: Who, Whom, Whose

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define relative pronoun and explain its purpose in a sentence
- Use who, whom, and whose correctly to connect clauses
- Combine two simple sentences into one sentence using a relative pronoun

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of subject and object in a sentence
- Ability to identify nouns and pronouns
- Experience forming complete sentences with correct subject-verb agreement

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Index cards (6 per student for sorting activity)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about pronouns and introduce the idea of connecting sentences.

**Activity: Two-Sentence Puzzle**

Say: "I am going to put two sentences on the board. Your job is to figure out how to combine them into one smooth sentence."

Write on the board:
1. The girl won the spelling bee. The girl practiced every night.
2. My neighbor is very kind. My neighbor helped us carry groceries.

Say: "Turn to a partner and try to combine each pair into one sentence. You have two minutes."

After sharing, say: "You probably said something like 'The girl who won the spelling bee practiced every night.' That little word 'who' is doing something special — it is connecting two ideas together. Today we are going to learn about words called relative pronouns that do exactly this job."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students what relative pronouns are and how who, whom, and whose function in sentences.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Relative pronoun:** A word that connects a clause to a noun. It "relates" extra information back to a person or thing.
- **Clause:** A group of words that has a subject and a verb.
- **Who:** A relative pronoun used as the subject of the clause (the person doing the action).
- **Whom:** A relative pronoun used as the object of the clause (the person receiving the action).
- **Whose:** A relative pronoun that shows ownership or possession.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "A relative pronoun is like a bridge. It connects a main sentence to extra information about a noun. Today we are focusing on three relative pronouns that are used for people: who, whom, and whose."

**Teaching WHO:**

Say: "We use 'who' when the pronoun is the subject — the person doing the action in the extra information."

Write on the board: *The artist who painted that mural lives on my street.*

Say: "In this sentence, 'who' refers back to 'the artist.' The artist is doing the action of painting. Because the person is doing the action, we use 'who.'"

**Teaching WHOM:**

Say: "We use 'whom' when the pronoun is the object — the person receiving the action."

Write on the board: *The coach whom we admire taught us a new drill.*

Say: "Here, 'whom' refers back to 'the coach.' But notice — the coach is not doing the admiring. We are doing the admiring. The coach is receiving the action, so we use 'whom.'"

Say: "Here is a trick to remember: Try replacing the pronoun with 'he' or 'him.' If 'he' sounds right, use 'who.' If 'him' sounds right, use 'whom.' He = who. Him = whom. Notice they both end in 'm'!"

Write on the board: *He painted the mural → who. We admire him → whom.*

**Teaching WHOSE:**

Say: "We use 'whose' to show that something belongs to someone."

Write on the board: *The student whose backpack is red forgot her lunch.*

Say: "'Whose' tells us that the backpack belongs to the student. It shows possession, just like 'his' or 'her,' but it connects a clause."

### Worked Examples

Say: "Let me show you how to combine two sentences using each pronoun."

**Example 1 (who):**
- Sentence A: The firefighter rescued the cat.
- Sentence B: The firefighter lives next door.
- Combined: *The firefighter who lives next door rescued the cat.*
- Explanation: "The firefighter is doing the action of living, so we use 'who.'"

**Example 2 (whom):**
- Sentence A: The author wrote ten books.
- Sentence B: I met the author at the library.
- Combined: *The author whom I met at the library wrote ten books.*
- Explanation: "I met the author — the author is receiving the action, so we use 'whom.'"

**Example 3 (whose):**
- Sentence A: The boy found a twenty-dollar bill.
- Sentence B: The boy's dog is named Biscuit.
- Combined: *The boy whose dog is named Biscuit found a twenty-dollar bill.*
- Explanation: "The dog belongs to the boy, so we use 'whose' to show possession."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice identifying and using who, whom, and whose with facilitator support.

Say: "Now it is your turn. We will work through these together. Write each answer in your notebook."

**Part A: Choose the correct relative pronoun.**

1. The teacher ______ gave us extra recess was Mrs. Patel.  (who / whom / whose)
2. The dentist ______ I visited yesterday was very gentle.  (who / whom / whose)
3. The girl ______ painting won first place smiled proudly.  (who / whom / whose)
4. The pilot ______ flew the plane waved at the crowd.  (who / whom / whose)
5. The friend ______ you invited to the party just arrived.  (who / whom / whose)

Say: "For number 1, think: Is the teacher doing the action or receiving it? The teacher gave us extra recess — the teacher is doing the giving, so we use 'who.'"

Walk through each item, asking students to apply the he/him trick before revealing the answer.

**Part B: Combine the sentences using who, whom, or whose.**

6. The librarian helps us find books. The librarian knows every shelf in the library.
7. My uncle tells the best stories. We visited my uncle last weekend.
8. The swimmer broke the school record. The swimmer's goggles are bright green.

Say: "For number 6, ask yourself: Which person are we giving extra information about? The librarian. Is the librarian doing the action? Yes — she knows every shelf. So we use 'who.' The librarian who knows every shelf in the library helps us find books."

---

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students apply their understanding of who, whom, and whose independently.

**Part A: Fill in the blank with who, whom, or whose.**

1. The scientist ______ discovered the new species wrote a book about it.
2. The musician ______ we heard at the concert played the violin beautifully.
3. The boy ______ skateboard has lightning bolts on it just did a kickflip.
4. People ______ recycle help protect the environment.
5. The veterinarian ______ our family trusts takes care of our dog, Maple.

**Part B: Combine each pair into one sentence using a relative pronoun.**

6. The chef made the best pizza I have ever tasted. The chef trained in Italy.
7. The crossing guard keeps us safe every morning. We wave to the crossing guard.
8. The girl won the science fair. The girl's project was about volcanoes.

**Part C: Fix the error in each sentence.**

9. The bus driver whom drives us to school is always cheerful. (Error: whom should be who)
10. The student who I borrowed the pencil from forgot to sharpen it. (Error: who should be whom)

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A and B.
**Stretch:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts, then write three original sentences — one using who, one using whom, and one using whose — about people in your school or community.

### Interleaved Review
11. Write the past tense of these irregular verbs: run, sing, catch. *(Grade 3 review: L.3.1d — irregular verbs)*
12. Add commas and quotation marks to this sentence: Mom said Please set the table for dinner. *(Grade 3 review: L.3.2c — commas and quotation marks in dialogue)*

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Fill in the blank: The astronaut ______ walked on the Moon trained for years. (who / whom / whose)
2. Fill in the blank: The neighbor ______ lawn has a garden gnome invited us to a barbecue. (who / whom / whose)
3. Combine: The zookeeper feeds the penguins. We talked to the zookeeper.

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the he/him substitution trick with the student one-on-one
- Provide additional sentence pairs for combining practice
- Create a reference card with who = subject (he), whom = object (him), whose = ownership

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student moves to Challenge tier in Lesson 2
- Encourage student to look for relative pronouns in independent reading

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Relative Pronoun Hunt:** Find five sentences that use who, whom, or whose in a book, magazine, or article. Copy them into your notebook and underline the relative pronoun.
2. **Interview Sentences:** Write five sentences about a family member or friend using who, whom, or whose. Example: "My grandmother, who grew up on a farm, makes the best apple pie."
3. **Pronoun Poster:** Create a mini-poster showing the difference between who, whom, and whose with your own example sentences and illustrations.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- "Whom" is the most challenging pronoun for students at this age. Do not expect full mastery immediately; it will be reinforced throughout the unit.
- The he/him trick is the single most effective tool for who vs. whom. Practice it repeatedly.
- Some students may ask about "that" as a relative pronoun for people (e.g., "The boy that won"). Acknowledge this is common in everyday speech but tell them that in formal writing, "who" is preferred for people. "That" will be covered in Lesson 2 for things.
- If students confuse "whose" with "who's" (the contraction for "who is"), clarify that "whose" shows ownership and "who's" means "who is."
- Encourage students to read their combined sentences aloud. If a sentence sounds awkward, it may need restructuring.
