# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 9

## Topic
Capitalization Rules

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Apply capitalization rules to proper nouns, titles, geographical names, historical periods, and organizations
- Identify and correct capitalization errors in sentences
- Distinguish between common nouns (no capital) and proper nouns (capital)

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of the difference between common and proper nouns
- Basic capitalization skills (beginning of sentences, the pronoun I, names of people)
- Familiarity with commas and punctuation in sentences

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Anchor chart paper for capitalization rules reference

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about capitalization and reveal that there are more rules than students may realize.

**Activity: Capital or Not?**

Write on the board (without capital letters — all lowercase):
1. mount everest
2. the teacher
3. pacific ocean
4. a holiday
5. martin luther king jr. day
6. the red cross

Say: "Look at these words and phrases. Raise your hand if you think the first one should be capitalized." Go through each item.

Say: "Some of these were easy — you know names of people need capitals. But what about 'pacific ocean'? Or 'the red cross'? Or 'martin luther king jr. day'? Today we are going to review and extend our capitalization rules so you can handle all of these with confidence."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach and review capitalization rules for proper nouns, titles, geographical names, historical periods, and organizations.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Proper noun:** The specific name of a particular person, place, thing, or idea. Always capitalized.
- **Common noun:** A general word for a person, place, thing, or idea. Not capitalized unless it starts a sentence.
- **Title:** A word that comes before a person's name (Dr., President, Captain) or the name of a book, movie, or work of art.
- **Geographical name:** The name of a specific place on Earth — a city, state, country, continent, ocean, mountain, river, etc.
- **Historical period/event:** The name of a specific time period or event in history (the Middle Ages, the American Revolution).

### Instruction Notes

Say: "The golden rule of capitalization is: Capitalize proper nouns — the specific names of things. Do not capitalize common nouns — the general words for things."

Write on the board:

**Rule 1: People's Names and Titles**
- Capitalize a person's full name: *Rosa Parks, Albert Einstein*
- Capitalize titles before names: *Dr. Patel, President Lincoln, Captain Hernandez*
- Do NOT capitalize titles used alone: *The doctor will see you now. The president gave a speech.*

Write examples:
- Correct: *We met Mayor Johnson at the parade.*
- Correct: *The mayor was at the parade.*

**Rule 2: Geographical Names**
- Capitalize specific places: cities, states, countries, continents, oceans, mountains, rivers, lakes
- Do NOT capitalize general geographical words

Write examples:
- Correct: *She visited the Rocky Mountains last summer.*
- Correct: *She visited the mountains last summer.*
- Correct: *We swam in Lake Michigan.*
- Correct: *We swam in the lake.*

**Rule 3: Historical Periods and Events**
- Capitalize names of specific historical periods and events

Write examples:
- Correct: *The Ancient Egyptians built the pyramids.*
- Correct: *The American Revolution began in 1775.*
- Correct: *The Middle Ages lasted for about a thousand years.*

**Rule 4: Organizations and Institutions**
- Capitalize the names of specific organizations, schools, teams, and companies

Write examples:
- Correct: *My sister volunteers for the American Red Cross.*
- Correct: *He goes to Lincoln Elementary School.*
- Correct: *The Chicago Cubs won the game.*
- Incorrect: *He goes to the Elementary School down the street.* (This is a general reference, not a specific name.)

**Rule 5: Titles of Works**
- Capitalize the first word and all important words in titles of books, movies, songs, and poems
- Do NOT capitalize small words like a, an, the, and, but, or, in, of, to (unless they are the first word)

Write examples:
- Correct: *Charlotte's Web*
- Correct: *The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe*
- Correct: *Bridge to Terabithia*

Say: "Notice that 'the' and 'to' are not capitalized in the middle of titles, but 'The' at the beginning of a title is capitalized because it is the first word."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:**
- Incorrect: *My family visited the grand canyon in arizona last july.*
- Correct: *My family visited the Grand Canyon in Arizona last July.*
- Explanation: "Grand Canyon is a specific place (geographical name), Arizona is a state, and July is a month — all proper nouns."

**Example 2:**
- Incorrect: *doctor sanchez works at mercy general hospital.*
- Correct: *Doctor Sanchez works at Mercy General Hospital.*
- Explanation: "Doctor is a title before a name, Sanchez is a person's name, and Mercy General Hospital is the name of a specific organization."

**Example 3:**
- Incorrect: *we read the tale of despereaux in our Book Club.*
- Correct: *We read The Tale of Despereaux in our book club.*
- Explanation: "'We' starts the sentence. The book title capitalizes all important words. 'Book club' is not a proper noun — it is a general description."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice identifying and correcting capitalization errors with facilitator support.

Say: "Let us practice fixing capitalization errors. Rewrite each sentence correctly in your notebook."

**Part A: Correct the capitalization errors in each sentence.**

1. my friend jenna moved to austin, texas, last september.
2. we learned about the civil war in social studies class.
3. the principal, mrs. yamamoto, announced that lincoln elementary school won an award.
4. have you ever read the book where the wild things are by maurice sendak?
5. the mississippi river flows through ten states before reaching the gulf of mexico.

Say: "For number 1, let us go through word by word. 'My' starts the sentence — capitalize it. 'Jenna' is a person's name. 'Austin' is a city. 'Texas' is a state. 'September' is a month. So: My friend Jenna moved to Austin, Texas, last September."

Walk through each item, asking students to identify why each word does or does not need a capital.

**Part B: Decide whether the underlined word should be capitalized. Write yes or no.**

6. We visited a **museum** in Washington, D.C.
7. She wants to join the **Girl Scouts** next year.
8. My **uncle** makes the best barbecue.
9. The **Pacific Ocean** is the largest ocean on Earth.
10. He is the best **mayor** our town has ever had.

Say: "For number 6, 'museum' is a common noun — it is not the specific name of the museum. So no, do not capitalize it. If it said 'the National Air and Space Museum,' then we would capitalize it."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently apply capitalization rules.

**Part A: Rewrite each sentence with correct capitalization.**

1. last summer, we traveled to yellowstone national park in wyoming.
2. my teacher, mr. brooks, grew up in seoul, south korea.
3. the declaration of independence was signed in philadelphia in 1776.
4. our basketball team, the westside wolves, plays at jefferson middle school.
5. i just finished reading harry potter and the sorcerer's stone by j.k. rowling.

**Part B: Find and correct the capitalization error(s) in each sentence.**

6. My family celebrates thanksgiving with my Grandparents in ohio.
7. The amazon Rainforest is in south america.
8. We watched a movie called finding nemo on friday Night.
9. The President of our school's Student Council is named hector Ruiz.
10. The nile river flows through egypt and into the mediterranean sea.

**Part C: Write a sentence for each capitalization rule. Use correct capitalization.**

11. Write a sentence that includes a person's name and title.
12. Write a sentence that includes a geographical name (a specific place).
13. Write a sentence that includes the title of a book or movie.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A and B.
**Stretch:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts, then write a travel postcard (5-6 sentences) to a friend describing a real or imaginary trip. Include at least one example of each capitalization rule (person's name/title, geographical name, historical reference, organization, and title of a work).

### Interleaved Review
14. Label as formal or informal: "We would like to extend our sincere thanks for your generous contribution." *(Review: Lesson 8)*
15. Choose the correct word: The building ______ was damaged in the storm has been repaired. (which / that) *(Review: Lesson 2)*

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Correct the capitalization: we visited the statue of liberty in new york city.
2. Should "doctor" be capitalized in this sentence? "The doctor said I should drink more water."
3. Correct the capitalization in this book title: the chronicles of narnia

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the five rules with the student, one at a time
- Provide a capitalization checklist for the student to use when editing
- Practice with additional sentences, focusing on the rule the student missed

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student moves to Challenge tier in Lesson 10
- Ask the student to proofread a paragraph from their own writing for capitalization

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Capitalization Scavenger Hunt:** Look through a newspaper, magazine, or book page. Find and list five examples of proper nouns that follow the capitalization rules learned today. Write the rule each example follows.
2. **Error-Filled Paragraph:** Write a paragraph about a famous person or place with at least eight capitalization errors. Exchange with a partner and see if they can find and fix all the errors.
3. **Proper Noun Map:** Draw a map (real or imaginary) with at least ten labeled places. Make sure every place name is capitalized correctly. Include cities, rivers, mountains, and countries.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Capitalization is a skill that students have been building since kindergarten. This lesson extends and formalizes rules they may already follow intuitively. The new content for fourth grade includes historical periods, organizations, and more nuanced title capitalization.
- The most common errors are: (1) capitalizing common nouns that seem important (e.g., "My Teacher," "the School"), (2) not capitalizing proper adjectives (e.g., "american" instead of "American"), and (3) inconsistent title capitalization.
- For title capitalization, the simplified rule at this level is: capitalize the first word and all "important" words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs). Do not capitalize short prepositions, articles, or conjunctions unless they are the first word. Different style guides have slightly different rules — keep it simple for fourth graders.
- If students ask about capitalizing seasons (spring, summer, fall, winter), note that seasons are not capitalized unless they are part of a proper noun (e.g., "Spring Festival").
- Days of the week and months of the year are capitalized, but students sometimes forget this. Reinforce if needed.
- This lesson addresses L.4.2a. It connects to all writing tasks — remind students that correct capitalization is expected in every piece of writing they produce.
