# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 4, Lesson 6

## Topic
Ordering Adjectives Within Sentences

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify the conventional order of adjectives in English (opinion, size, age, shape, color, origin, material, purpose)
- Arrange multiple adjectives in the correct order within a sentence
- Use the "sound test" to check whether adjective order sounds natural

## Prerequisites
- Ability to identify adjectives in a sentence
- Experience using comparative and superlative adjectives (Grade 3, L.3.1g)
- Understanding of how adjectives modify nouns

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Index cards or sentence strips (for sorting activity)
- Anchor chart paper for OSASCOMP reference

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Help students notice that adjective order matters and that some orders "sound right" while others sound strange.

**Activity: Which Sounds Right?**

Say: "I am going to read two versions of the same sentence. Tell me which one sounds better."

Read aloud:
- Pair 1: "She wore a red big hat." vs. "She wore a big red hat."
- Pair 2: "He found an old tiny wooden box." vs. "He found a tiny old wooden box."
- Pair 3: "They adopted a fluffy little white kitten." vs. "They adopted a white little fluffy kitten."

Say: "Most of you probably picked 'a big red hat,' 'a tiny old wooden box,' and 'a fluffy little white kitten.' Those sound right, do they not? But why? Is there a rule for the order adjectives go in?"

Say: "The answer is yes! English has a specific order for adjectives, and today we are going to learn it. Once you know the pattern, you will always know which order sounds right."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the conventional adjective order pattern and how to apply it.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Adjective order:** The conventional sequence in which adjectives appear before a noun in English.
- **Opinion adjective:** Describes what someone thinks or feels about the noun (beautiful, ugly, delicious, boring).
- **Size adjective:** Describes how big or small (huge, tiny, tall, short).
- **Age adjective:** Describes how old or new (ancient, young, new, old).
- **Shape adjective:** Describes the form (round, square, flat, narrow).
- **Color adjective:** Describes the hue (red, blue, golden, dark).
- **Origin adjective:** Describes where something is from (American, Italian, African, local).
- **Material adjective:** Describes what something is made of (wooden, metal, silk, cotton).
- **Purpose adjective:** Describes what something is used for (sleeping as in "sleeping bag," running as in "running shoes").

### Instruction Notes

Say: "In English, when we use more than one adjective before a noun, they follow a specific order. Here is the pattern."

Write on the board:
**O - S - A - S - C - O - M - P**
1. **O**pinion (lovely, terrible, delicious)
2. **S**ize (big, small, enormous)
3. **A**ge (old, new, ancient, young)
4. **S**hape (round, square, flat)
5. **C**olor (blue, green, spotted)
6. **O**rigin (Japanese, Mexican, southern)
7. **M**aterial (wooden, glass, leather)
8. **P**urpose (cooking, sleeping, racing)

Say: "You can remember this with the word OSASCOMP. Let me say it with you: O-S-A-S-C-O-M-P."

Say: "You do not usually use all eight types at once — that would be a very long description! Most sentences use two or three adjectives. The pattern tells you which type comes first."

Write on the board: *a beautiful small old round blue Italian leather racing car*

Say: "That sentence uses all eight types. You would probably never write a sentence like that, but it shows the order. In real writing, you might say 'a beautiful old Italian racing car' — and that follows the same pattern: opinion, age, origin, purpose."

Say: "The good news is that you already know most of this pattern. It is the natural way English sounds. Today's lesson helps you understand why it sounds that way and gives you a tool to check your writing."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:**
- Adjectives: wooden, large, old
- Noun: chest
- Order: large (size) → old (age) → wooden (material)
- Sentence: *We found a large old wooden chest in the attic.*

**Example 2:**
- Adjectives: blue, beautiful, silk
- Noun: scarf
- Order: beautiful (opinion) → blue (color) → silk (material)
- Sentence: *My grandmother gave me a beautiful blue silk scarf.*

**Example 3:**
- Adjectives: round, tiny, green
- Noun: beads
- Order: tiny (size) → round (shape) → green (color)
- Sentence: *The necklace was made of tiny round green beads.*

**Example 4:**
- Adjectives: Japanese, new, exciting
- Noun: video game
- Order: exciting (opinion) → new (age) → Japanese (origin)
- Sentence: *My cousin showed me an exciting new Japanese video game.*

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice ordering adjectives with facilitator support.

Say: "Let us practice putting adjectives in the right order. Write each answer in your notebook."

**Part A: Put the adjectives in the correct order before the noun.**

1. (red, big) ball → a ______ ______ ball
2. (cotton, old, soft) blanket → a ______ ______ ______ blanket
3. (Mexican, delicious) food → ______ ______ food
4. (square, brown, small) box → a ______ ______ ______ box
5. (metal, shiny, new) lunchbox → a ______ ______ ______ lunchbox

Say: "For number 1, we have 'red' and 'big.' Red is a color and big is a size. In OSASCOMP, size comes before color: a big red ball."

Say: "For number 2, we have 'cotton,' 'old,' and 'soft.' Soft is an opinion — it describes how it feels. Old is age. Cotton is material. Opinion, then age, then material: a soft old cotton blanket."

Walk through each item, having students identify the category of each adjective.

**Part B: Rewrite each sentence with the adjectives in the correct order.**

6. She bought a leather brown new jacket.
7. We saw a Chinese ancient beautiful vase at the museum.
8. He carries a green big canvas backpack to school.

Say: "For number 6, let us sort the adjectives. 'Leather' is material. 'Brown' is color. 'New' is age. The order should be: new (age) → brown (color) → leather (material). She bought a new brown leather jacket."

**Part C: Which sentence has the adjectives in the correct order?**

9. (a) I found a flat small gray stone. (b) I found a small flat gray stone.
10. (a) They live in a lovely old brick house. (b) They live in an old lovely brick house.

---

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently order adjectives in sentences.

**Part A: Arrange the adjectives in the correct order and write the full sentence.**

1. (plastic, yellow, little) duck → The ______ ______ ______ ______ floated in the bathtub.
2. (French, old, gorgeous) painting → We admired the ______ ______ ______ ______.
3. (striped, long, wool) scarf → She wrapped a ______ ______ ______ ______ around her neck.
4. (young, talented, Korean) pianist → The ______ ______ ______ ______ played beautifully.
5. (rusty, enormous, steel) bridge → The ______ ______ ______ ______ stretched across the river.

**Part B: Fix the adjective order in each sentence.**

6. He wore a silk blue fancy tie to the wedding.
7. We ate a Greek warm wonderful salad for lunch.
8. My sister has a sleeping purple new bag for camp.

**Part C: Add two adjectives to each sentence. Make sure they are in the correct order.**

9. The ______ ______ dog chased the ball across the yard. (Choose from: spotted, big, or your own)
10. We moved into a ______ ______ house on Oak Street. (Choose from: red, cozy, or your own)

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A and B.
**Stretch:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts, then write a descriptive paragraph about your dream bedroom using at least five sentences. Each sentence must include two or more correctly ordered adjectives.

### Interleaved Review
11. Change to past progressive: The squirrel buried acorns in the yard. *(Review: Lesson 5)*
12. Fill in the correct relative pronoun: The woman ______ car broke down asked us for directions. (who / whose / whom) *(Review: Lesson 1)*

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Put in order: (wooden, old, small) table → a ______ ______ ______ table
2. Fix this sentence: She picked a red beautiful round apple from the tree.
3. What type of adjective comes first — size or color?

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review OSASCOMP with physical cards — write each adjective on a card and have the student physically sort them
- Practice the sound test: Read both orders aloud and ask which sounds more natural
- Provide a bookmark or reference card with the OSASCOMP order

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student moves to Challenge tier in Lesson 7
- Ask the student to revise a paragraph from earlier writing, checking adjective order

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Adjective Stacking Challenge:** Start with a simple noun like "cat." Add one adjective at a time, following the correct order, until you have at least four adjectives. Example: cat → fluffy cat → small fluffy cat → adorable small fluffy cat.
2. **Description Game:** Describe an object in the room using three or more adjectives in the correct order. Your partner has to guess what you are describing. Example: "I am looking at a large flat white rectangular object." (Answer: the whiteboard.)
3. **Silly Descriptions:** Write five silly but grammatically correct descriptions using three or more adjectives. Example: "The gorgeous tiny ancient square purple Martian rubber cooking hat sat on the shelf." Check that every adjective follows OSASCOMP order.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Adjective order is one of the most intuitive grammar rules for native English speakers — most people follow it without knowing the rule exists. The goal of this lesson is to make the implicit rule explicit.
- Non-native English speakers may find this lesson particularly valuable, as adjective order is one of the most difficult patterns to acquire naturally.
- If students get overwhelmed by eight categories, simplify to the most common: opinion, size, age, color, material. Shape, origin, and purpose can be taught as extensions.
- The "sound test" is a valid strategy. If students can read both orders aloud and pick the one that sounds natural, they are demonstrating competence even without memorizing OSASCOMP.
- When two adjectives are the same type (e.g., two colors: "a red and blue striped shirt"), they are joined with "and" or a comma. This is different from the ordering rule and can be mentioned briefly if students ask.
- Avoid requiring students to use all eight adjective types in a single sentence. Two or three is realistic and sufficient for demonstrating mastery.
