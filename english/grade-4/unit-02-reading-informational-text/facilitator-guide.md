# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 2: Reading Informational Text

## Facilitator Guide

### Unit Overview
Unit 2 builds students' ability to read, analyze, and interpret informational texts across a range of formats and purposes. Students deepen their Grade 3 foundation in identifying main ideas and supporting details, then extend to summarizing, explaining complex events and concepts, comparing firsthand and secondhand accounts, and interpreting visual information such as charts, graphs, diagrams, timelines, and maps. By the end of this unit, students will confidently approach any informational text, extract meaning, and connect information across text and visual elements.

### Standards Addressed
| Standard | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| RI.4.2 | Determine the main idea of a text and explain how it is supported by key details; summarize the text |
| RI.4.3 | Explain events, procedures, ideas, or concepts in a historical, scientific, or technical text, including what happened and why, based on specific information in the text |
| RI.4.6 | Compare and contrast a firsthand and secondhand account of the same event or topic; describe the differences in focus and the information provided |
| RI.4.7 | Interpret information presented visually, orally, or quantitatively (e.g., in charts, graphs, diagrams, timelines, animations, or interactive elements on web pages) and explain how the information contributes to an understanding of the text in which it appears |

### Prerequisites (Grade 3 Skills)
Students should be able to:
- Determine the main idea of a text and recount key details (RI.3.2)
- Describe the relationship between events, ideas, or concepts in a text (RI.3.3)
- Use text features and search tools to locate information (RI.3.5)
- Identify basic text structures and describe logical connections (RI.3.8)

### Materials Needed

### Instructional Tips
1. **Activate prior knowledge.** Begin each lesson by connecting to what students learned in Grade 3 about main ideas, details, and text features. Students benefit from hearing explicit bridges between old and new skills.
2. **Use the facilitator scripts.** The Say: "..." prompts are designed for direct, clear instruction. Read or paraphrase them as written to maintain consistency across facilitators.
3. **Model think-alouds.** During Direct Instruction and Guided Practice, demonstrate your own thought process aloud. Show students how a skilled reader approaches an informational passage.
4. **Check for understanding frequently.** Do not wait until the exit ticket. Use thumbs-up/thumbs-down, turn-and-talk, and quick whip-arounds during guided practice.
5. **Differentiate with the difficulty tiers.** Each lesson offers On-level, Stretch, and Challenge tiers. Assign tiers based on exit ticket data from prior lessons, not fixed groupings.
6. **Emphasize text evidence.** A recurring theme in Grade 4 is supporting answers with specific details from the text. Reinforce this expectation in every lesson.
7. **Build vocabulary deliberately.** Each lesson introduces key vocabulary. Post new terms on a word wall and revisit them in subsequent lessons.

### Common Misconceptions
- Students may confuse the **topic** (what the text is about in one or two words) with the **main idea** (the most important point the author makes about the topic). Address this explicitly in Lesson 1.
- Students may include personal opinions in summaries. Lesson 2 directly teaches how to keep summaries objective.
- Students may assume firsthand accounts are always more "true" than secondhand accounts. Lessons 5–7 help students see that each type has strengths and limitations.
- Students may read charts and graphs without connecting the data to the accompanying text. Lessons 8–9 teach students to move between visual and written information.

### Assessment Guidance
- **Formative:** Use exit tickets after every lesson. Review results before the next lesson and reteach as needed.
- **Summative:** The Unit Assessment includes two extended passages with visual elements, covering all four standards. Mastery is 80% or higher overall, with at least one correct response per standard.
- **Remediation:** If a student scores below mastery on a specific standard, revisit the corresponding lessons and provide additional practice passages at the On-level tier before reassessing.

### Cross-Curricular Connections
- **Science:** Lessons 4, 8, and 9 use science-related passages. Coordinate with science instruction on topics like the water cycle, animal adaptations, or weather.
- **Social Studies:** Lessons 3, 5, 6, and 7 use historical topics. Align with social studies units on exploration, community history, or historical events.
- **Math:** Lessons 8 and 9 reinforce graph and data interpretation skills that support math standards on data analysis.
