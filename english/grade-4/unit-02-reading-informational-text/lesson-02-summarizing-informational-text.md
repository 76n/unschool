# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 2, Lesson 2

## Topic
Summarizing Informational Text

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Write a summary of an informational text that includes the main idea and key details
- Distinguish between important details and interesting but less important details
- Keep personal opinions out of a summary

## Prerequisites
- Ability to identify the main idea and key details in an informational passage (Lesson 1)
- Understanding of the difference between topic and main idea
- Ability to write complete sentences

## Materials Needed
- Printed or projected lesson passages
- Summary Frame graphic organizer (Main Idea + 3 Key Details + Summary sentence starters)
- Whiteboard and markers
- Pencils and paper for student responses
- Highlighters (optional)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review main idea and key details from Lesson 1 and introduce the concept of summarizing.

**Activity: Too Much or Just Right?**

Say: "Yesterday we practiced finding the main idea and key details. Today we are going to use those skills to write summaries. But first, let me show you what a summary is NOT."

Read aloud the following two descriptions of the same text:

Version 1 (too long): "The passage talks about how earthworms tunnel through the ground and that makes passageways and those passageways let air and water reach plant roots and the tunnels also loosen the soil and that is good for roots and also earthworms eat dead leaves and break them down into nutrients and farmers call them nature's plows and I think earthworms are really cool."

Version 2 (just right): "The passage explains that earthworms help keep soil healthy. They create tunnels that let air and water reach roots, loosen soil, and turn dead leaves into nutrients for plants."

Say: "Which version is a better summary? Why?"

Allow students to respond. Guide them to notice that Version 1 retells every detail and adds a personal opinion, while Version 2 captures the main idea and only the most important details in a few sentences.

Say: "A summary is a short retelling that captures the main idea and key details. It should be much shorter than the original text, and it should never include our own opinions."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students a clear process for writing an informational text summary.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Summary:** A short retelling of a text that includes only the main idea and the most important details
- **Paraphrase:** To restate information in your own words
- **Objective:** Based on facts, not personal feelings or opinions
- **Subjective:** Based on personal feelings or opinions (what we leave OUT of a summary)

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Today I am going to teach you the four steps to writing a strong summary. We will call it the SKIP method, because when we summarize, we skip over small details and opinions."

Write on the board:
- **S** — State the main idea
- **K** — Keep only key details (2–3 most important ones)
- **I** — In your own words (paraphrase, do not copy)
- **P** — Pull out opinions (no personal feelings)

Say: "Let me show you how this works. I am going to read a passage and write a summary using SKIP."

### Worked Examples

**Passage A: "The Amazing Octopus"**

> The octopus is one of the most intelligent creatures in the ocean. Scientists have observed octopuses solving puzzles, opening jars, and even escaping from their tanks in aquariums. An octopus has a large brain compared to its body size, and each of its eight arms has its own cluster of nerve cells that can taste and feel independently. Octopuses can also change the color and texture of their skin in less than a second, helping them hide from predators or sneak up on prey. Some species squirt dark ink into the water to confuse attackers while they make a quick escape. With these remarkable abilities, the octopus continues to surprise researchers who study ocean life.

Say: "Step S — State the main idea. What is this passage mostly telling us? The first sentence says the octopus is one of the most intelligent creatures in the ocean. The last sentence says it continues to surprise researchers. The main idea is: The octopus is a remarkably intelligent ocean creature."

Say: "Step K — Keep only key details. There are many details here. I need to pick the two or three most important ones."

Write on the board:
- Octopuses solve puzzles, open jars, and escape tanks.
- Each arm has nerve cells that can taste and feel on their own.
- They change color and texture to hide or hunt.
- Some squirt ink to confuse predators.

Say: "These are all interesting, but which ones best support the main idea about intelligence? I will keep: solving puzzles and escaping tanks (shows intelligence), arms with independent nerve cells (shows brain power), and changing color instantly (shows a remarkable ability). The ink detail is interesting but less central."

Say: "Step I — In your own words. I need to paraphrase, not copy the author's exact sentences."

Say: "Step P — Pull out opinions. Do I have any personal opinions? No, I will make sure I do not add any."

Write the summary on the board:

"The octopus is one of the smartest animals in the sea. Scientists have watched octopuses solve puzzles and escape from tanks, which shows their problem-solving skills. Each of their eight arms can taste and feel on its own, and octopuses can change their skin color and texture almost instantly to hide from danger. These abilities make the octopus a fascinating subject for researchers."

Say: "Notice that my summary is much shorter than the original passage. I included the main idea and three key details, I used my own words, and I did not add any personal opinions like 'I think octopuses are cool.'"

## Guided Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice summarizing with facilitator support using the SKIP method.

Say: "Let us try one together. I will read the passage, and then we will work through SKIP step by step."

**Passage B: "Why Do Leaves Change Color?"**

> In many parts of the United States, tree leaves change from green to brilliant shades of red, orange, and yellow each autumn. This color change happens because of changes in sunlight and temperature. During spring and summer, leaves are green because they contain a pigment called chlorophyll, which helps them absorb sunlight to make food for the tree. As the days get shorter and cooler in autumn, trees stop producing chlorophyll. Without the green chlorophyll, other pigments that were always in the leaf become visible. Yellow and orange colors come from pigments called carotenoids, while red and purple shades are produced by pigments called anthocyanins. Once the leaves have fully changed color, they dry out, fall from the tree, and become part of the soil below.

Say: "Step S — What is the main idea? Talk to a partner."

Allow 30 seconds. Call on a student.

Expected: "Leaves change color in autumn because of changes in sunlight and temperature that affect pigments in the leaves."

Say: "Good. Step K — What are the two or three most important details? Write them down."

Allow 1 minute. Discuss as a group.

Expected key details:
- Chlorophyll makes leaves green in spring and summer.
- Trees stop producing chlorophyll when days get shorter and cooler.
- Other pigments (carotenoids and anthocyanins) become visible without chlorophyll.

Say: "Step I — Now, with your partner, write a 2–3 sentence summary in your own words. Remember Step P — no opinions!"

Allow 2–3 minutes. Circulate and support.

Call on a student to share. Model example:

"Leaves change color in autumn because trees stop making the green pigment chlorophyll when the days get shorter and cooler. Without chlorophyll, other pigments like yellow carotenoids and red anthocyanins show through. Eventually the colorful leaves dry out and fall to the ground."

Say: "Let us check our summary. Did we state the main idea? Yes. Did we include key details? Yes, two or three. Did we use our own words? Yes. Did we leave out opinions? Yes. Great job."

## Independent Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently summarize an informational passage.

**Passage C: "The Golden Gate Bridge"**

> The Golden Gate Bridge in San Francisco, California, is one of the most famous bridges in the world. When it opened on May 27, 1937, it was the longest suspension bridge ever built, stretching 4,200 feet across the Golden Gate Strait. The bridge connects the city of San Francisco to Marin County to the north. Building the bridge was dangerous and difficult. Workers had to deal with strong winds, thick fog, and powerful ocean currents. A safety net was hung beneath the bridge during construction, and it saved the lives of 19 workers who fell. The bridge is painted a reddish-orange color called "International Orange," which was chosen because it is visible in San Francisco's frequent fog. Today, about 40 million vehicles cross the Golden Gate Bridge each year, and it remains one of the most photographed structures on Earth.

**Questions:**
1. What is the main idea of this passage?
2. List three key details that support the main idea.
3. Which of the following details is interesting but LEAST important for a summary?
   a) The bridge opened on May 27, 1937.
   b) A safety net saved 19 workers who fell.
   c) The bridge is painted International Orange for visibility in fog.
   d) About 40 million vehicles cross it each year.
4. Write a 2–3 sentence summary of this passage using the SKIP method. Remember: main idea, key details, your own words, no opinions.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Answer questions 1–4 as written above.
**Stretch:** Answer all four questions, and then explain why the detail you chose in question 3 is the least important for a summary.
**Challenge:** Answer all questions. Then rewrite your summary to be exactly two sentences long while still capturing the main idea and at least two key details.

### Interleaved Review
5. (Lesson 1 review) Reread the passage. What is the difference between the topic and the main idea? Write the topic in one or two words, and then write the main idea as a complete sentence.
6. (Grade 3 review — RI.3.3) The passage describes several challenges of building the bridge. Name two challenges and explain how builders dealt with one of them.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Read the following short passage:

> Honeybees live in large groups called colonies. A single colony can have as many as 60,000 bees. Each bee has a specific job. The queen bee lays eggs. Worker bees collect nectar from flowers and turn it into honey. Guard bees protect the entrance to the hive. By working together, the colony stays healthy and strong.

1. What is the main idea? (Expected: Honeybees live in colonies where each bee has a specific job, and they work together to keep the colony healthy.)
2. Write a one- or two-sentence summary of this passage. (Expected: A honeybee colony can have up to 60,000 bees, and each bee plays a specific role. The queen lays eggs, workers make honey, and guards protect the hive, all working together to keep the colony strong.)
3. A student wrote this summary: "Honeybees are so cool because they work together and I think everyone should learn about them." What is wrong with this summary? (Expected: It includes personal opinions — "so cool" and "I think everyone should learn about them" — instead of sticking to facts from the text.)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct, with the summary written objectively in the student's own words**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the SKIP method step by step using the warm-up passage
- Have the student highlight the main idea in one color and key details in another before attempting to write the summary
- Provide sentence starters: "This passage is mainly about ___. One important detail is ___. Another detail is ___."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 3 on explaining events in history
- Challenge the student to summarize a passage from their independent reading book

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Summary Shrink:** Give students a passage and ask them to write a summary in exactly 25 words. Then have them shrink it to 15 words. Discuss what had to be cut and why.
2. **Summary vs. Opinion:** Give students three written responses to the same passage. One is an objective summary, one includes opinions, and one is just a list of random details. Students identify which is the best summary and explain why.
3. **Summarize the News:** Students bring in (or are given) a short news article appropriate for their age. They use the SKIP method to write a summary and share it with the class.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The biggest challenge in this lesson is helping students distinguish between important details and merely interesting details. Use the question: "If I removed this detail, would the summary still make sense?" If yes, it may not be essential.
- Watch for students who copy sentences directly from the passage. Reinforce paraphrasing by having them close the text after reading and tell you what it said before writing.
- Some students may write summaries that are too short (just the main idea) or too long (retelling every detail). Use the guideline: a summary should be about one-quarter to one-third the length of the original passage.
- If students add opinions, gently point them out and ask: "Did the author say that, or is that what you think?" This builds awareness of the difference between objective and subjective statements.
- The SKIP method is a scaffold. As students gain confidence, they may not need to follow each step explicitly, but it provides a reliable structure for struggling learners.

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice (Passage B):**
- Main Idea: Leaves change color in autumn because trees stop producing chlorophyll when days get shorter and cooler, allowing other pigments to show.
- Key Details: Chlorophyll makes leaves green; trees stop making chlorophyll in autumn; carotenoids (yellow/orange) and anthocyanins (red/purple) become visible.
- Sample Summary: "Leaves change color in autumn because trees stop making the green pigment chlorophyll when days get shorter and cooler. Without chlorophyll, other pigments — yellow carotenoids and red anthocyanins — become visible before the leaves dry out and fall."

**Independent Practice (Passage C):**
1. Main Idea: The Golden Gate Bridge is one of the most famous bridges in the world, known for its impressive construction and lasting importance.
2. Key Details: (any three) It was the longest suspension bridge when it opened in 1937; it stretches 4,200 feet across the Golden Gate Strait; building it was dangerous due to wind, fog, and currents; a safety net saved 19 workers; it is painted International Orange for fog visibility; about 40 million vehicles cross it each year.
3. Answer: b) A safety net saved 19 workers who fell. (While interesting, this detail about the construction process is the least central to a summary about the bridge's fame and importance. Accept other answers with strong reasoning.)
4. Sample Summary: "The Golden Gate Bridge, which opened in 1937 in San Francisco, was the longest suspension bridge in the world at the time. It was difficult to build because of strong winds and fog, and its reddish-orange color was chosen to stand out in foggy weather."
5. (Review) Topic: The Golden Gate Bridge. Main Idea: The Golden Gate Bridge is one of the most famous bridges in the world. (Accept reasonable variations.)
6. (Review) Challenges: strong winds, thick fog, powerful ocean currents. Builders dealt with the danger of falling by hanging a safety net beneath the bridge, which saved 19 workers.

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Honeybees live in colonies where each bee has a specific job, and by working together, the colony stays healthy and strong.
2. Sample: "A honeybee colony can include up to 60,000 bees, each with a different role such as laying eggs, making honey, or guarding the hive. Working together keeps the colony healthy."
3. The summary includes personal opinions ("so cool" and "I think everyone should learn about them") instead of sticking to facts from the text. A good summary is objective.
