# English Language Arts — Grade 4 — Unit 2, Lesson 3

## Topic
Explaining Events in History

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain a historical event using specific details from an informational text
- Identify the sequence of events using signal words (first, then, next, finally, after, before)
- Describe cause-and-effect relationships within a historical text

## Prerequisites
- Ability to identify the main idea and key details (Lesson 1)
- Ability to summarize an informational text (Lesson 2)
- Understanding of basic sequence (beginning, middle, end) from Grade 3 (RI.3.3)

## Materials Needed
- Printed or projected lesson passages
- Sequence graphic organizer (boxes connected by arrows)
- Cause-and-Effect T-chart
- Whiteboard and markers
- Pencils and paper for student responses

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about sequence and introduce the idea of explaining historical events.

**Activity: What Happened and Why?**

Say: "Today we are going to read about real events from history and practice explaining what happened and why. Before we start, let me see how well you remember sequence words."

Write the following scrambled events on the board:
- The colonists threw 342 chests of tea into Boston Harbor.
- The British government placed a tax on tea.
- The colonists dressed up as Mohawk Indians to disguise themselves.
- The colonists were angry about being taxed without having a voice in the decision.

Say: "These events describe something called the Boston Tea Party. Can you put them in the order they happened? Talk to a partner."

Allow 1 minute. Then arrange together:
1. The British government placed a tax on tea.
2. The colonists were angry about being taxed without having a voice in the decision.
3. The colonists dressed up as Mohawk Indians to disguise themselves.
4. The colonists threw 342 chests of tea into Boston Harbor.

Say: "Good. Notice that some of these events are about what happened, and others explain why it happened. Today we are going to learn how to find both in a text."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students how to identify sequence and cause-effect relationships in historical texts.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Sequence:** The order in which events happen
- **Signal words:** Words that show the order of events or connections between them (first, next, then, after, because, as a result, due to)
- **Cause:** The reason something happened (why)
- **Effect:** What happened as a result (what)
- **Historical text:** A text that describes events from the past

### Instruction Notes

Say: "When we read about events in history, we need to understand two things: the order events happened in, and why they happened. Sequence tells us the order. Cause and effect tells us the why."

Write on the board:
- **Sequence signal words:** first, second, next, then, after, before, finally, later, meanwhile, during
- **Cause-effect signal words:** because, since, so, therefore, as a result, due to, this led to, consequently

Say: "Let me show you how to find these in a real passage about history."

### Worked Examples

**Passage A: "The Great Fire of London"**

> In the early morning of September 2, 1666, a small fire broke out in a bakery on Pudding Lane in London. The bakery was owned by Thomas Farinor, the king's baker. At first, the fire seemed like it could be controlled. However, London had experienced a long, dry summer, and the wooden buildings were packed tightly together along narrow streets. Strong winds quickly spread the flames from one building to the next. By the end of the first day, more than 300 houses had burned. The fire raged for four days, destroying over 13,000 homes, 87 churches, and much of the city's center. Finally, on September 5, the winds died down and firefighters were able to stop the blaze by pulling down buildings to create gaps the fire could not cross. After the Great Fire, London was rebuilt with wider streets and buildings made of brick and stone instead of wood, so a fire of that size could never spread so easily again.

Say: "Let me walk through this passage step by step. First, I am going to find the sequence of events."

Write on the board:
1. September 2, 1666 — A fire broke out in a bakery on Pudding Lane.
2. Strong winds spread the fire quickly through tightly packed wooden buildings.
3. By the end of the first day, over 300 houses had burned.
4. The fire raged for four days, destroying 13,000 homes and 87 churches.
5. September 5 — Winds died down and firefighters stopped the fire by pulling down buildings.
6. After the fire, London was rebuilt with wider streets and brick buildings.

Say: "I found signal words like 'at first,' 'by the end of the first day,' 'for four days,' and 'finally' to help me put events in order."

Say: "Now let me find cause-and-effect relationships."

Write a T-chart:
| Cause (Why) | Effect (What Happened) |
|---|---|
| London had a long, dry summer | The wooden buildings were dry and burned easily |
| Buildings were packed tightly on narrow streets | Fire spread quickly from building to building |
| Strong winds blew | Flames jumped from one building to the next |
| Winds died down | Firefighters could stop the blaze |
| The Great Fire destroyed much of London | The city was rebuilt with wider streets and brick buildings |

Say: "See how the passage gives us reasons for what happened? The fire was not just bad luck — the dry summer, the crowded wooden buildings, and the strong winds all caused it to spread. And the effect of the fire was that London changed the way it was built."

## Guided Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice identifying sequence and cause-effect in a historical passage with facilitator support.

Say: "Now let us try one together. I will read the passage, and you will help me find the sequence and the causes and effects."

**Passage B: "The Pony Express"**

> Before telephones and the internet existed, sending a message across the United States took a very long time. In the 1850s, mail traveling by stagecoach from Missouri to California took about 25 days. People wanted a faster way to communicate, so in April 1860, a new mail delivery service called the Pony Express began operating. Young riders on fast horses carried mailbags across nearly 2,000 miles of plains, deserts, and mountains. The route was divided into sections, and relay stations were set up every 10 to 15 miles. When a rider reached a station, he would quickly jump onto a fresh horse and keep going. Each rider covered about 75 miles before passing the mail to the next rider. Because of this relay system, the Pony Express could deliver a letter from Missouri to California in just 10 days. However, the Pony Express lasted only 18 months. In October 1861, the transcontinental telegraph was completed, allowing people to send messages across the country in just minutes. As a result, the Pony Express was no longer needed and shut down.

Say: "Let us start with sequence. What happened first, next, and so on? Work with a partner to list at least four events in order."

Allow 2 minutes. Then discuss as a class:
1. In the 1850s, mail by stagecoach took about 25 days from Missouri to California.
2. In April 1860, the Pony Express began operating.
3. Riders carried mail on horseback using relay stations every 10–15 miles.
4. Letters could be delivered in just 10 days.
5. In October 1861, the transcontinental telegraph was completed.
6. The Pony Express shut down.

Say: "What signal words helped you? I heard 'before,' 'in April 1860,' 'in October 1861,' and 'as a result.' Good."

Say: "Now let us find cause-and-effect relationships. With your partner, find at least two."

Allow 1–2 minutes. Discuss:
- Cause: People wanted faster communication. Effect: The Pony Express was created.
- Cause: Relay stations with fresh horses were set up. Effect: Mail could travel 2,000 miles in 10 days.
- Cause: The transcontinental telegraph was completed. Effect: The Pony Express was no longer needed and shut down.

Say: "Excellent. Now I want you to use these details to explain the Pony Express to me in three or four sentences, as if you were telling someone who has never heard of it. Include what happened and why."

Allow 2 minutes for students to write. Call on a volunteer.

Sample response: "In the 1850s, it took 25 days to send mail from Missouri to California by stagecoach. Because people wanted faster communication, the Pony Express started in 1860. Riders on horseback carried mail using relay stations and could deliver a letter in just 10 days. However, the Pony Express ended after only 18 months because the telegraph allowed people to send messages in minutes."

## Independent Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently read a historical passage and explain events using sequence and cause-effect.

**Passage C: "The Dust Bowl"**

> During the 1930s, a terrible disaster struck the Great Plains of the United States. For years, farmers had plowed up the natural grasses that held the soil in place so they could plant wheat and other crops. Then, beginning in 1931, a severe drought hit the region. Without rain, the crops dried up and died. Without the natural grasses or living crops to hold it down, the dry topsoil turned to dust. Strong winds swept up the loose soil and created enormous dust storms that could darken the sky for hours. Some storms were so large they were called "black blizzards," and dust traveled as far as New York City and Washington, D.C. Thousands of families lost their farms because nothing would grow in the ruined soil. Many of these families packed up their belongings and moved west to California, hoping to find work and start over. The Dust Bowl lasted nearly a decade. It finally ended in the late 1930s when rain returned and the government taught farmers new methods of soil conservation, such as planting rows of trees to block the wind and rotating crops to keep the soil healthy.

**Questions:**
1. List five events from the passage in the order they happened.
2. Identify two cause-and-effect relationships in the passage. Write each one using this format: "Because ___, ___ happened."
3. What signal words or phrases in the passage helped you understand the sequence of events?
4. Using details from the text, explain in three to four sentences what the Dust Bowl was and why it happened.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Answer questions 1–4 as written above.
**Stretch:** Answer all four questions. For question 4, also explain how the Dust Bowl ended and what changes were made to prevent it from happening again.
**Challenge:** Answer all questions. Then write a paragraph explaining what lessons people can learn from the Dust Bowl about taking care of the environment. Use at least two details from the passage.

### Interleaved Review
5. (Lesson 1 review) What is the main idea of this passage? Write it as one complete sentence.
6. (Lesson 2 review) Write a two-sentence summary of this passage using the SKIP method.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Read the following short passage:

> In 1848, a man named James Marshall discovered gold at Sutter's Mill in California. News of the discovery spread quickly across the country. As a result, thousands of people rushed to California in 1849, hoping to find gold and become rich. These people were called "forty-niners." Because so many people arrived, California's population grew rapidly, and it became a state in 1850.

1. List three events in the order they happened. (Expected: Gold was discovered at Sutter's Mill in 1848; thousands of people rushed to California in 1849; California became a state in 1850.)
2. Identify one cause-and-effect relationship. (Expected: Because gold was discovered, thousands of people rushed to California. OR Because so many people arrived, California became a state in 1850.)
3. What signal word in the passage shows a cause-and-effect connection? (Expected: "as a result" or "because")

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct, with events in proper sequence and cause-effect clearly stated**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the signal word lists from Direct Instruction
- Reread Passage A together and use the sequence graphic organizer step by step
- Practice finding cause-effect with simple two-sentence pairs before returning to full passages

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 4 on explaining scientific concepts
- Encourage the student to look for sequence and cause-effect in their social studies textbook

## Extension (Optional)
1. **History Detective:** Give students a short passage about a different historical event (the invention of the telephone, the first airplane flight). Have them create a sequence chart and a cause-effect T-chart.
2. **Timeline Maker:** Students create a simple timeline of the events in Passage C (The Dust Bowl) with dates and brief descriptions.
3. **Cause-Effect Chain:** Students create a visual chain showing how one event in the Dust Bowl led to the next (plowing up grass led to exposed soil, drought led to dead crops, wind led to dust storms, dust storms led to farm failures, farm failures led to migration).

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Students at this age may have limited background knowledge about historical events. Provide brief context before reading (e.g., "The Great Plains is a large, flat area in the middle of the United States where many people farmed").
- Cause and effect can be tricky because one event can have multiple causes, and one cause can lead to multiple effects. The Dust Bowl passage is a good example — the disaster was caused by both farming practices AND drought.
- Some students confuse correlation with causation. If a student says "The Dust Bowl happened because people moved to California," gently redirect: "Did people move before or after the Dust Bowl? Was moving the cause or the effect?"
- Encourage students to use the signal word lists as a reference tool. Over time, they will internalize these words and recognize them automatically.
- If students struggle with writing explanations, let them explain orally first, then write down what they said.

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice (Passage B):**
- Sequence: (1) 1850s — stagecoach mail took 25 days; (2) April 1860 — Pony Express began; (3) Riders used relay stations; (4) Mail delivered in 10 days; (5) October 1861 — telegraph completed; (6) Pony Express shut down.
- Cause-Effect: Because people wanted faster communication, the Pony Express was created. Because the telegraph was completed, the Pony Express was no longer needed. Because of the relay system, mail could be delivered in 10 days.

**Independent Practice (Passage C):**
1. Sequence: (1) Farmers plowed up natural grasses to plant crops; (2) A severe drought began in 1931; (3) Crops died and soil turned to dust; (4) Strong winds created enormous dust storms; (5) Thousands of families lost farms and moved west; (6) Rain returned in the late 1930s and new farming methods were introduced.
2. Cause-Effect: Because farmers plowed up the natural grasses, the soil had nothing to hold it in place. Because a severe drought hit and crops died, the dry topsoil turned to dust. (Accept other valid pairings.)
3. Signal words: "for years," "then," "beginning in 1931," "without," "finally," "in the late 1930s," "when."
4. Sample explanation: "The Dust Bowl was a disaster that struck the Great Plains during the 1930s. Farmers had plowed up the natural grasses that protected the soil. When a severe drought killed the crops, the exposed soil turned to dust and strong winds created enormous 'black blizzards.' Thousands of families lost their farms and moved west to California."
5. (Review) Main idea: The Dust Bowl was a terrible disaster caused by poor farming practices and drought that destroyed farms across the Great Plains during the 1930s.
6. (Review) Sample summary: "During the 1930s, farmers on the Great Plains suffered a disaster called the Dust Bowl after drought and years of plowing up natural grasses turned the soil to dust. Massive dust storms destroyed farms, forcing thousands of families to move west until rain and new farming methods ended the crisis."

**Exit Ticket:**
1. (1) Gold discovered at Sutter's Mill in 1848; (2) Thousands rushed to California in 1849; (3) California became a state in 1850.
2. Because gold was discovered, thousands of people rushed to California. OR Because so many people arrived, California became a state in 1850.
3. "As a result" or "because."
