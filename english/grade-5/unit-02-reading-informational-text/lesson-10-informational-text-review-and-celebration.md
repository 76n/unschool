# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 2, Lesson 10

## Topic
Informational Text Review and Celebration

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Apply all four unit standards (RI.5.1, RI.5.2, RI.5.3, RI.5.9) to a set of informational texts
- Demonstrate confidence in quoting, identifying main ideas, explaining relationships, and synthesizing sources
- Reflect on their growth as informational text readers throughout the unit

## Prerequisites
- Completion of Lessons 1-9 in this unit
- Familiarity with all four standards: RI.5.1, RI.5.2, RI.5.3, RI.5.9

## Materials Needed

- All graphic organizers from the unit (Quote Evidence Chart, Inference Chart, Multiple Main Ideas Map, Cause-and-Effect Chain, Text Integration Matrix)
- Whiteboard and markers

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model how all four skills work together when reading informational text.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Review:** No new vocabulary in this lesson. This section reviews key terms from the unit.
- **Quote accurately:** Use the author's exact words inside quotation marks (RI.5.1)
- **Inference:** A conclusion drawn from text evidence plus background knowledge (RI.5.1)
- **Multiple main ideas:** Two or more central points in a single text (RI.5.2)
- **Summarize:** Restate the most important ideas concisely in your own words (RI.5.2)
- **Cause and effect:** A relationship where one event leads to or causes another (RI.5.3)
- **Synthesize:** Combine information from multiple sources into a new understanding (RI.5.9)

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Today I want to show you that these four skills are not separate -- they work together every time you read informational text. A strong reader quotes the text to support their inferences, identifies multiple main ideas to write a good summary, explains cause-and-effect relationships, and synthesizes information across sources. Let me show you with a quick example."

### Worked Examples

**Passage A: "The Power of Vaccines"**

> Vaccines are one of the most important medical inventions in history. A vaccine works by introducing a weakened or inactive form of a germ into the body. The immune system recognizes the germ as a threat and produces antibodies to fight it. If the person is later exposed to the real germ, their immune system already knows how to defeat it quickly. Before vaccines existed, diseases like smallpox, polio, and measles killed or disabled millions of people every year. Smallpox alone is estimated to have killed 300 million people in the twentieth century before a global vaccination campaign eradicated the disease in 1980. Today, vaccines prevent an estimated 4 to 5 million deaths worldwide each year.

Say: "Watch how I use all four skills with this one passage."

Say: "RI.5.1 -- Quoting: If someone asks me how vaccines work, I can quote the text: 'A vaccine works by introducing a weakened or inactive form of a germ into the body.'"

Say: "RI.5.1 -- Inference: The text says smallpox killed 300 million people but was eradicated by vaccination. I can infer that vaccines are most effective when given to large populations, because it took a 'global vaccination campaign' to wipe out the disease."

Say: "RI.5.2 -- Main Ideas: This passage has two main ideas: (1) Vaccines work by training the immune system to fight germs, and (2) Vaccines have saved millions of lives by preventing deadly diseases."

Say: "RI.5.3 -- Cause and Effect: Because scientists developed a global vaccination campaign, smallpox was eradicated in 1980. The cause was the campaign; the effect was eradication."

Say: "And if I had a second text about vaccine hesitancy or new vaccine research, I could use RI.5.9 to synthesize the two sources into a more complete understanding."

Say: "See how all the skills connect? They are tools in your reading toolkit, and today you get to use them all."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice applying all four skills to a paired set of passages with facilitator support.

Say: "Let us work through two passages together, applying all four skills."

**Text 1: "The Invention of the Printing Press"**

> In 1440, a German inventor named Johannes Gutenberg developed the printing press, a machine that would transform the way information was shared across the world. Before Gutenberg's invention, books in Europe were copied by hand, a process that could take months for a single volume. As a result, books were extremely rare and expensive, and only the wealthiest people and religious institutions owned them. Gutenberg's printing press used movable metal type -- individual letters that could be arranged, inked, and pressed onto paper to create pages quickly. His first major printed work, the Gutenberg Bible, was completed around 1455. Within fifty years of its invention, the printing press had produced an estimated 20 million volumes across Europe.

**Text 2: "How the Printing Press Changed Society"**

> The printing press had a profound impact on European society. Because books could now be produced quickly and cheaply, literacy rates began to rise as more people gained access to written material. New ideas in science, philosophy, and religion spread rapidly across the continent, fueling movements like the Renaissance and the Protestant Reformation. The printing press also standardized languages, as printed books established common spellings and grammar rules that had varied widely in handwritten manuscripts. Historians often describe the printing press as one of the most important inventions in human history because it democratized knowledge -- making it available to ordinary people, not just the elite.

Say: "Let us apply each skill."

Say: "RI.5.1 -- Quote a sentence from Text 1 that explains why books were rare before the printing press."

Allow 30 seconds. Call on a student.

Expected: "Before Gutenberg's invention, books in Europe were copied by hand, a process that could take months for a single volume."

Say: "RI.5.2 -- What are the main ideas of Text 2? It is a single passage, but does it have more than one main idea?"

Allow 30 seconds for partner discussion. Call on students.

Expected: Main Idea 1: The printing press made books more accessible, increasing literacy. Main Idea 2: The spread of printed books fueled major intellectual and social movements.

Say: "RI.5.3 -- What is the cause-and-effect relationship between the printing press and literacy? Use a signal word."

Expected: Because books could be produced quickly and cheaply, more people gained access to written material. As a result, literacy rates began to rise.

Say: "RI.5.9 -- Using information from BOTH texts, explain in 2-3 sentences why the printing press was important. Your answer should include at least one fact from each text."

Allow 2 minutes. Call on a student.

Sample: "Before the printing press, books were copied by hand and only wealthy people could afford them. Gutenberg's invention in 1440 allowed books to be produced quickly and cheaply, leading to increased literacy and the rapid spread of new ideas that fueled the Renaissance and the Reformation."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently apply all four unit skills to a set of passages.

**Text 1: "What Are Wetlands?"**

> Wetlands are areas where water covers the soil or is present near the surface for all or part of the year. They include marshes, swamps, bogs, and the edges of lakes and rivers. Wetlands may not look impressive, but they perform essential functions for the environment. They act as natural sponges, absorbing excess rainwater and slowly releasing it, which helps prevent flooding in nearby communities. Wetlands also filter pollutants from water as it passes through the soil and plants, improving the quality of water that flows into rivers, lakes, and underground aquifers. In addition, wetlands are among the most productive ecosystems on Earth, providing habitat for a remarkable variety of birds, fish, amphibians, and insects.

**Text 2: "The Disappearance of Wetlands"**

> Despite their importance, wetlands have been disappearing at an alarming rate. Since the 1700s, more than half of the world's wetlands have been drained or filled in to make room for agriculture, housing, and commercial development. In the United States alone, approximately 53 percent of the original wetlands have been lost. The destruction of wetlands has had measurable consequences. Communities that have lost nearby wetlands experience more severe flooding because the natural sponge that absorbed excess water is gone. Water quality has declined in many areas because the natural filtration system provided by wetlands has been removed. Many species that depend on wetland habitat, including numerous bird and amphibian species, have seen their populations decline.

**Directions:**

1. (RI.5.1 -- Quoting) Quote one sentence from Text 1 that explains how wetlands help prevent flooding.
2. (RI.5.1 -- Inference) Based on Text 2, what can you infer about the future of wetlands if current trends continue? Support your inference with a quote.
3. (RI.5.2 -- Main Ideas) What are the two main ideas of Text 1? Write each as a complete sentence.
4. (RI.5.3 -- Cause and Effect) Using Text 2, explain the cause-and-effect relationship between wetland destruction and flooding. Use at least one signal word.
5. (RI.5.9 -- Synthesis) Write a 3-4 sentence synthesis paragraph about wetlands using information from both texts. Include at least one quote from each text.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Answer questions 1-5 as written.
**Stretch:** Answer all five questions, ensuring every response includes an accurate quote with quotation marks.
**Challenge:** Answer all five questions, then write a 2-sentence conclusion explaining why protecting wetlands should be a priority. Use evidence from both texts to support your conclusion.

### Interleaved Review
6. (Grade 4 review -- RI.4.3) According to Text 2, what three types of development have caused wetlands to disappear?
7. (Grade 4 review -- RI.4.2) Write a one-sentence summary of Text 2 that captures its main idea and one key detail.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

> The Great Wall of China, one of the most famous structures on Earth, was built over many centuries to protect China from invasions by northern nomadic groups. The earliest sections were constructed as far back as the 7th century BC, and the most well-known portions were built during the Ming Dynasty (1368-1644). The wall stretches approximately 13,000 miles across northern China.

1. (RI.5.1) Quote the sentence that tells you when the most famous parts of the wall were built.
2. (RI.5.2) What is the main idea of this passage?
3. (RI.5.3) What is the relationship between the threat of invasion and the building of the wall?

**Expected Answers:**
1. "The most well-known portions were built during the Ming Dynasty (1368-1644)."
2. The Great Wall of China was built over many centuries to protect the country from northern invasions. (Accept reasonable variations.)
3. Because China faced the threat of invasion from northern nomadic groups, the government built the Great Wall as a defense. The threat of invasion was the cause, and the construction of the wall was the effect.

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct, demonstrating accurate quoting, main idea identification, and relationship explanation**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which specific skill the student is struggling with and revisit the corresponding lesson (Lessons 1-2 for RI.5.1, Lessons 3-5 for RI.5.2, Lessons 6-7 for RI.5.3, Lessons 8-9 for RI.5.9)
- Provide a short, simple passage and walk through all four skills step by step
- Allow the student to use graphic organizers as scaffolding

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for the Unit Assessment
- Celebrate their growth and encourage confidence

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson is designed as both a review and a celebration. Keep the tone positive and encouraging. Students should feel proud of the skills they have developed over the past nine lessons.
- If you notice that the class struggles with a particular standard during the independent practice, use the remaining time to provide a brief reteach before the unit assessment.

- Celebrate completion of the unit. Whether you use certificates, stickers, or simple verbal recognition, acknowledging students' hard work builds motivation for the assessment and future units.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. (RI.5.1) "They act as natural sponges, absorbing excess rainwater and slowly releasing it, which helps prevent flooding in nearby communities."
2. (RI.5.1 -- Inference) If current trends continue, more wetlands will likely be destroyed, leading to increased flooding, worse water quality, and greater loss of wildlife habitat. The text states that "more than half of the world's wetlands have been drained or filled in" and this destruction "has had measurable consequences," suggesting the problems will worsen if the trend is not reversed.
3. (RI.5.2) Main Idea 1: Wetlands perform essential functions for the environment, including flood prevention and water filtration. Main Idea 2: Wetlands are among the most productive ecosystems on Earth, providing habitat for a remarkable variety of species. (Accept reasonable variations.)
4. (RI.5.3) Because wetlands have been drained or filled in for development, communities that lost their nearby wetlands now experience more severe flooding. The wetlands used to act as "natural sponges, absorbing excess rainwater," but once the wetlands were destroyed, that natural protection was removed.
5. (RI.5.9) Sample synthesis: "Wetlands are vital ecosystems that 'act as natural sponges, absorbing excess rainwater' and providing habitat for countless species. However, 'more than half of the world's wetlands have been drained or filled in' for agriculture, housing, and commercial development. The destruction of wetlands has led to increased flooding, declining water quality, and loss of wildlife habitat. Protecting remaining wetlands is essential for both environmental health and human safety."

### Interleaved Review
6. According to Text 2, wetlands have been drained or filled in to make room for (1) agriculture, (2) housing, and (3) commercial development.
7. Summary: Wetlands have been disappearing at an alarming rate due to human development, with measurable consequences including more severe flooding, declining water quality, and declining wildlife populations. (Accept reasonable variations.)
