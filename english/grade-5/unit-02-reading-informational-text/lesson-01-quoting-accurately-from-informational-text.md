# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 2, Lesson 1

## Topic
Quoting Accurately from Informational Text

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Locate specific evidence in an informational text that answers a given question
- Use quotation marks to quote accurately from a text when explaining what it says explicitly
- Distinguish between quoting (using the author's exact words) and paraphrasing (restating in one's own words)

## Prerequisites
- Ability to refer to details and examples in a text when explaining what the text says explicitly (RI.4.1)
- Understanding of quotation marks and their use in writing
- Ability to read Grade 5 level informational text independently

## Materials Needed
- Printed or projected lesson passages
- Quote Evidence Chart graphic organizer (three columns: Question, Exact Quote, Page/Paragraph)
- Whiteboard and markers
- Pencils and paper for student responses
- Highlighters (recommended)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about using text evidence and introduce the concept of quoting accurately.

**Activity: Exact Words Challenge**

Say: "Last year in fourth grade, you practiced referring to details and examples from the text to support your answers. This year, we are going to take that skill to the next level. In fifth grade, you need to quote accurately -- that means using the author's exact words and putting them in quotation marks."

Write on the board:
- The text says that volcanoes are dangerous.
- The text says, "Volcanoes can erupt without warning, sending clouds of ash and rivers of lava across the landscape."

Say: "Look at these two sentences. Both are about volcanoes, but which one gives you a better picture of what the author actually wrote?"

Allow students to respond.

Say: "The second one is much stronger because it uses the author's exact words. That is what we mean by quoting accurately. Today, you are going to learn how to find the best quotes and use them correctly."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students the process of locating evidence, selecting a relevant quote, and formatting it with quotation marks.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Quote:** The exact words from a text, placed inside quotation marks
- **Evidence:** Information from the text that supports or proves a point
- **Explicitly:** Stated directly and clearly in the text, not hidden or implied
- **Paraphrase:** Restating an idea from the text in your own words, without quotation marks

### Instruction Notes

Say: "When we quote from a text, we follow three steps. I call them the Quote It Right steps."

Write on the board:
1. Read the question carefully -- what is it asking?
2. Search the text for the exact words that answer the question.
3. Copy the words exactly, put quotation marks around them, and name where you found them.

Say: "Let me show you how this works. Watch how I use these three steps with a real passage."

### Worked Examples

**Passage A: "The Giant Sequoias"**

> Giant sequoias are among the largest living things on Earth. These massive trees grow naturally only in a narrow band on the western slopes of the Sierra Nevada mountains in California. The largest giant sequoia, known as General Sherman, stands 275 feet tall and measures over 36 feet across at its base. Scientists estimate that General Sherman weighs approximately 2.7 million pounds. Despite their enormous size, giant sequoias begin life as tiny seeds no bigger than a flake of oatmeal. A single sequoia cone, which is only about the size of a chicken egg, can hold more than 200 seeds. Giant sequoias can live for more than 3,000 years, making them some of the oldest living organisms on the planet. Their thick, reddish bark -- sometimes more than two feet thick -- helps protect them from fire, insects, and disease.

Say: "Here is my question: How large is the General Sherman tree? Let me work through the three steps."

Say: "Step 1 -- I read the question carefully. It asks about the size of the General Sherman tree specifically. So I need to find facts about its size."

Say: "Step 2 -- I search the text. I see this sentence: 'The largest giant sequoia, known as General Sherman, stands 275 feet tall and measures over 36 feet across at its base.' That answers my question with specific numbers."

Say: "Step 3 -- I copy the exact words and put quotation marks around them."

Write on the board:
According to the passage, General Sherman "stands 275 feet tall and measures over 36 feet across at its base."

Say: "Notice that I used the author's exact words inside the quotation marks. I did not change any words. I also introduced the quote by writing 'According to the passage.' That tells the reader where the information comes from."

Say: "Now let me show you the difference between a quote and a paraphrase."

Write on the board:
- Quote: The passage states that General Sherman "stands 275 feet tall and measures over 36 feet across at its base."
- Paraphrase: The passage explains that the General Sherman tree is very tall and wide.

Say: "The quote gives the exact words and exact numbers. The paraphrase puts the idea in my own words but loses the precise details. In fifth grade, when a question asks you to quote from the text, you must use the exact words with quotation marks."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice locating evidence and quoting accurately with facilitator support.

Say: "Now it is your turn. I am going to read a new passage, and we will practice quoting together."

**Passage B: "The Water Underground"**

> Beneath the surface of the Earth lies a hidden world of water. Groundwater -- water that fills the cracks and spaces in underground rock and soil -- makes up about 30 percent of all the freshwater on the planet. This water collects in underground layers of rock called aquifers. An aquifer acts like a giant underground sponge, holding water in its tiny pores and cracks. People have depended on groundwater for thousands of years, digging wells to reach the water stored below. Today, groundwater supplies drinking water to nearly half the population of the United States. However, groundwater is not an unlimited resource. In many areas, people are pumping water out of aquifers faster than rain can refill them. Scientists warn that some of the world's largest aquifers could be seriously depleted within the next fifty years if current usage rates continue.

Say: "Here is question 1: What percentage of the world's freshwater is groundwater? Search the passage and find the exact words that answer this question. Highlight or underline them."

Allow 30 seconds. Call on a student.

Expected response: "Groundwater ... makes up about 30 percent of all the freshwater on the planet."

Say: "Now write your answer as a complete sentence that includes the quote with quotation marks."

Allow 1 minute. Call on a student.

Expected response: According to the passage, groundwater "makes up about 30 percent of all the freshwater on the planet."

Say: "Excellent. Now question 2: What is an aquifer? Find the exact words in the text."

Allow 30 seconds. Call on a student.

Expected response: "An aquifer acts like a giant underground sponge, holding water in its tiny pores and cracks."

Say: "Good. Now question 3: What warning do scientists give about aquifers? Quote the text."

Allow 30 seconds. Call on a student.

Expected response: Scientists warn that "some of the world's largest aquifers could be seriously depleted within the next fifty years if current usage rates continue."

Say: "Notice how each answer uses the author's exact words inside quotation marks. You are not changing words, adding words, or leaving out important parts. That is accurate quoting."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently locate evidence and quote accurately from a new passage.

**Passage C: "The Remarkable Octopus"**

> The octopus is one of the most intelligent creatures in the ocean. Scientists have observed octopuses solving puzzles, opening jars, and even escaping from aquarium tanks by squeezing through tiny gaps. An octopus has no bones in its body, which allows it to fit through any opening larger than its beak -- the only hard part of its body. This ability has earned the octopus a reputation as a master escape artist. Beyond their intelligence, octopuses are known for their incredible ability to change color and texture in less than a second. Specialized cells in their skin called chromatophores expand and contract to produce a wide range of colors and patterns. An octopus uses this skill for camouflage, blending in with rocks, coral, and sand to hide from predators. Some species can even mimic the appearance and movements of other sea creatures, such as lionfish and flatfish, to avoid being attacked. Octopuses also have three hearts and blue blood, which helps them survive in the cold, low-oxygen waters of the deep ocean.

**Directions:**

1. According to the passage, what evidence shows that octopuses are intelligent? Quote the text.
2. Why can an octopus fit through very small openings? Quote the text to support your answer.
3. How does an octopus change color? Quote the specific sentence that explains this.
4. What is one reason an octopus changes color? Quote the text.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Answer questions 1-3 using accurate quotes with quotation marks.
**Stretch:** Answer all four questions using accurate quotes with quotation marks and introduce each quote with a signal phrase (e.g., "The passage states," "According to the author").
**Challenge:** Answer all four questions, then write a short paragraph (3-4 sentences) about octopuses that includes at least two accurate quotes from the passage.

### Interleaved Review
5. (Grade 4 review -- RI.4.2) Reread the passage. What is the main idea? Write it as a complete sentence.
6. (Grade 4 review -- RI.4.3) The passage describes several abilities of the octopus. List three of these abilities in the order they appear in the passage.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

> The Arctic fox is perfectly adapted to survive in one of the coldest environments on Earth. Its thick, white winter coat provides both warmth and camouflage against the snow. In summer, the fur changes to brown or gray, helping the fox blend in with rocks and soil. The Arctic fox's short ears and small body reduce heat loss, allowing it to withstand temperatures as low as minus 58 degrees Fahrenheit.

1. What does the Arctic fox's winter coat provide? Quote the text.
2. How cold can the Arctic fox survive? Quote the exact words from the passage.

**Expected Answers:**
1. The Arctic fox's winter coat provides "both warmth and camouflage against the snow."
2. The Arctic fox can withstand "temperatures as low as minus 58 degrees Fahrenheit."

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 correct, with exact words quoted and quotation marks used**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the Quote It Right steps using Passage A from Direct Instruction
- Provide a short passage (3-4 sentences) and guide the student through locating and copying a quote word by word
- Practice the physical act of adding quotation marks to written responses

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 2 on making inferences from informational text
- Encourage the student to practice quoting when discussing books at home

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Quote Collector:** Give students a short informational article. Have them create a "quote collection" of five important quotes, each introduced with a different signal phrase (e.g., "The author explains," "According to the text," "The passage reveals").
2. **Quote vs. Paraphrase Sort:** Provide five statements about a passage -- some are accurate quotes and some are paraphrases. Students sort them into two columns and add quotation marks to the quotes.
3. **Quote It Right Game:** Students work in pairs. One student asks a question about the passage, and the other must answer using an accurate quote within 60 seconds.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The most common error is students paraphrasing instead of quoting. Watch for responses that capture the idea but change the wording. Gently redirect by asking, "Are those the author's exact words?"
- Some students will quote too much text -- entire sentences or paragraphs when only a phrase is needed. Teach them to find the smallest, most relevant portion that answers the question.
- Introduce signal phrases (According to the text, The author states, The passage says) as a way to smoothly incorporate quotes. This prepares students for more sophisticated writing in later grades.
- If students struggle with the mechanics of quotation marks, spend a few extra minutes modeling where to place them and remind students that only the author's exact words go inside the marks.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. The passage states that "Scientists have observed octopuses solving puzzles, opening jars, and even escaping from aquarium tanks by squeezing through tiny gaps."
2. According to the passage, "An octopus has no bones in its body, which allows it to fit through any opening larger than its beak -- the only hard part of its body."
3. The passage explains, "Specialized cells in their skin called chromatophores expand and contract to produce a wide range of colors and patterns."
4. According to the text, "An octopus uses this skill for camouflage, blending in with rocks, coral, and sand to hide from predators." (Also accept the quote about mimicking other creatures.)

### Interleaved Review
5. Main idea: The octopus is one of the most intelligent and remarkable creatures in the ocean, with unique abilities including problem-solving, color-changing, and physical flexibility. (Accept reasonable variations that capture both intelligence and physical abilities.)
6. Three abilities in order: (1) solving puzzles, opening jars, and escaping tanks; (2) fitting through tiny openings because they have no bones; (3) changing color and texture using chromatophores. (Also accept mimicking other creatures and having three hearts/blue blood.)

See Expected Answers above.
