# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 2, Lesson 8

## Topic
Integrating Information from Two Texts

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read two texts on the same topic and identify what information each text provides
- Determine what is shared by both texts and what is unique to each
- Combine information from both texts to build a more complete understanding of a topic

## Prerequisites
- Ability to identify main ideas and key details in informational text (Lessons 3-4, RI.5.2)
- Ability to summarize informational text (Lesson 5, RI.5.2)
- Ability to quote accurately from text (Lesson 1, RI.5.1)

## Materials Needed
- Printed or projected lesson passages (two passages per section)
- Text Integration Matrix graphic organizer (three columns: Text 1 Only, Both Texts, Text 2 Only)
- Whiteboard and markers
- Pencils and paper for student responses
- Highlighters in two colors (one per text)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the concept that different texts on the same topic can each provide different information.

**Activity: Two Friends, One Trip**

Say: "Imagine two of your friends went to the same amusement park on the same day. When they tell you about it, do they describe exactly the same things?"

Allow students to respond.

Say: "Probably not. One friend might talk about the roller coasters. The other might focus on the food and the games. Together, their stories give you a much fuller picture of what the park was like than either story alone."

Say: "The same thing happens with informational text. Two authors can write about the same topic, but each one includes different information, different details, and sometimes different perspectives. When you read both texts and put the information together, you build a more complete understanding of the topic. This is called integrating information, and it is one of the most important reading skills you will learn this year."

Write on the board:
**Integration** = Combining information from multiple sources to understand a topic more completely.

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students a strategy for reading two texts on the same topic and integrating the information.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Integrate:** To combine information from different sources into a unified understanding
- **Source:** A text, article, or document that provides information
- **Overlapping information:** Facts or ideas that appear in both texts
- **Unique information:** Facts or ideas that appear in only one text

### Instruction Notes

Say: "When you read two texts on the same topic, here is a three-step process I call Read, Sort, Combine."

Write on the board:
1. **Read** both texts carefully. Note the topic and main ideas of each.
2. **Sort** the information. What does Text 1 say that Text 2 does not? What does Text 2 say that Text 1 does not? What do both texts say?
3. **Combine** the information to explain the topic more fully than either text could alone.

Say: "Step 2 is where the Text Integration Matrix comes in. Let me show you."

### Worked Examples

**Text 1: "Mars: The Red Planet"**

> Mars is the fourth planet from the sun and is often called the Red Planet because of the iron oxide, or rust, that covers its surface. Mars is about half the size of Earth and has a much thinner atmosphere, which is made up almost entirely of carbon dioxide. Temperatures on Mars can drop to minus 195 degrees Fahrenheit at the poles. Despite its harsh conditions, Mars has features that resemble those on Earth, including polar ice caps, canyons, and extinct volcanoes. The largest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons, stands on Mars at a height of nearly 72,000 feet -- almost three times the height of Mount Everest.

**Text 2: "Exploring Mars"**

> For decades, scientists have sent spacecraft to Mars to learn more about the planet and to search for signs of past or present life. The first successful Mars landing was NASA's Viking 1 in 1976, which sent back the first photographs from the Martian surface. Since then, several rovers have explored the planet, including Curiosity, which landed in 2012, and Perseverance, which arrived in 2021. These rovers have discovered evidence that liquid water once flowed on the surface of Mars, including dried-up riverbeds and minerals that can only form in the presence of water. Scientists believe that where there was water, there may have been life. Future missions may send humans to Mars, with some estimates placing the first crewed mission as early as the 2030s.

Say: "Both texts are about Mars, but they focus on different aspects. Let me sort the information."

Draw the Text Integration Matrix on the board:

| Text 1 Only | Both Texts | Text 2 Only |
|-------------|------------|-------------|
| Mars is half the size of Earth | Mars is a planet in our solar system | Viking 1 landed in 1976 |
| Atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide | Mars has harsh conditions | Curiosity and Perseverance rovers |
| Temperatures drop to -195°F | Mars has features of interest to scientists | Evidence of past water on Mars |
| Olympus Mons is the largest volcano | | Signs of possible past life |
| Iron oxide gives it the red color | | Future human missions possible |

Say: "Now I combine the information. Watch how I write about Mars using facts from BOTH texts."

Write on the board:
"Mars, the fourth planet from the sun, is a harsh but fascinating world. Its thin carbon dioxide atmosphere and extreme temperatures -- as low as minus 195 degrees Fahrenheit -- make it very different from Earth, yet Mars has Earth-like features including polar ice caps and the solar system's largest volcano, Olympus Mons. Scientists have sent multiple rovers to explore the planet, and these missions have discovered evidence that liquid water once flowed on Mars. This discovery is significant because scientists believe that where there was water, there may have been life."

Say: "Notice that my combined summary uses information from Text 1 (physical features, temperature, atmosphere) and Text 2 (rovers, water evidence, possibility of life). Neither text alone tells the full story. Together, they give a more complete picture."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice reading two texts and integrating information with facilitator support.

Say: "Now let us try this together with two new texts."

**Text 1: "What Are Hurricanes?"**

> A hurricane is a powerful tropical storm with sustained winds of at least 74 miles per hour. Hurricanes form over warm ocean water near the equator, where the heat and moisture provide the energy the storm needs to grow. As warm, moist air rises from the ocean surface, cooler air rushes in to replace it. This cycle creates a spinning system of clouds and wind that can grow hundreds of miles wide. Hurricanes are classified on the Saffir-Simpson scale from Category 1 (weakest) to Category 5 (strongest), with Category 5 storms producing winds exceeding 157 miles per hour.

**Text 2: "Hurricane Preparedness"**

> Hurricanes cause billions of dollars in damage each year and are among the deadliest natural disasters in the United States. Storm surge -- the wall of ocean water pushed ashore by a hurricane's winds -- is responsible for the majority of hurricane-related deaths. In 2005, Hurricane Katrina's storm surge reached 28 feet in some areas along the Gulf Coast, devastating entire communities. Today, the National Hurricane Center uses satellites and computer models to track hurricanes days before they make landfall, giving communities time to prepare. Emergency managers recommend that families in hurricane-prone areas create emergency kits, develop evacuation plans, and stay informed through official weather updates.

Say: "What is the topic of both texts?"

Allow students to respond. (Expected: Hurricanes.)

Say: "Now work with a partner to fill in the Text Integration Matrix. What is in Text 1 only? What is in Text 2 only? What do both texts share?"

Allow 3-4 minutes. Then discuss as a class.

Expected matrix:
- Text 1 Only: How hurricanes form (warm ocean water, rising air cycle); Saffir-Simpson scale; wind speed categories
- Both Texts: Hurricanes are powerful storms; hurricanes have strong winds
- Text 2 Only: Hurricane damage and deaths; storm surge; Hurricane Katrina (2005); tracking with satellites; preparedness recommendations

Say: "Now, using information from both texts, write 2-3 sentences that explain what hurricanes are and why they are dangerous. Your response must include at least one fact from each text."

Allow 2-3 minutes. Call on a student.

Sample: "Hurricanes are powerful storms that form over warm ocean water and can produce winds exceeding 157 miles per hour. They are extremely dangerous because storm surge -- the wall of water pushed ashore by the wind -- causes the majority of hurricane-related deaths."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently read two texts and integrate information.

**Text 1: "The Amazon Rain Forest: Earth's Green Lung"**

> The Amazon rain forest is the largest tropical rain forest on Earth, covering approximately 2.1 million square miles across nine South American countries. Often called the "lungs of the planet," the Amazon produces about 20 percent of the world's oxygen through the photosynthesis of its enormous number of trees and plants. The forest is also one of the most biodiverse places on Earth, home to an estimated 10 percent of all known species, including more than 40,000 plant species, 1,300 bird species, and 3,000 types of fish. Many of these species are found nowhere else in the world. The Amazon River, which flows through the forest, carries more water than any other river and accounts for roughly 20 percent of all river water that flows into the world's oceans.

**Text 2: "Threats to the Amazon"**

> The Amazon rain forest faces serious threats from human activity. Deforestation -- the clearing of forest for farming, ranching, and logging -- has destroyed approximately 17 percent of the Amazon over the past fifty years. Cattle ranching is the single largest driver of deforestation, as farmers burn sections of forest to create grazing land. When trees are cut down or burned, the carbon stored in their trunks and leaves is released into the atmosphere as carbon dioxide, contributing to climate change. Deforestation also threatens the survival of thousands of plant and animal species that depend on the forest for habitat. Indigenous communities that have lived in the Amazon for thousands of years are losing their ancestral lands. Conservation organizations and some governments are working to slow deforestation through protected areas, sustainable farming practices, and international agreements.

**Directions:**

1. What is the topic of both texts?
2. Fill in a Text Integration Matrix: list at least two pieces of information unique to Text 1, two unique to Text 2, and one piece of information shared by both.
3. Write a 3-4 sentence combined summary that uses information from BOTH texts. Include at least one quote from each text.
4. What does reading both texts help you understand about the Amazon that reading only one text would not?

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Answer questions 1-3.
**Stretch:** Answer all four questions with accurate quotes from both texts.
**Challenge:** Answer all four questions, then write a 4-5 sentence paragraph explaining the relationship between the Amazon's importance (Text 1) and the threats it faces (Text 2). Use evidence from both texts.

### Interleaved Review
5. (Grade 4 review -- RI.4.2) What is the main idea of Text 2? Write it as a complete sentence.
6. (Grade 4 review -- RI.4.3) According to Text 2, explain the cause-and-effect chain that links deforestation to climate change.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

**Text A:**
> Honeybees communicate the location of food sources through a movement called the waggle dance. The direction of the dance shows other bees which way to fly, and the speed tells them how far away the food is.

**Text B:**
> Honeybee populations have declined sharply in recent years due to pesticides, habitat loss, and disease. Since bees pollinate about one-third of the food humans eat, this decline threatens global food production.

Using information from BOTH texts, write 2 sentences about honeybees.

**Expected Answers:**
Sample: "Honeybees are sophisticated communicators that use a waggle dance to share the exact location of food sources with other bees. However, honeybee populations are declining due to pesticides, habitat loss, and disease, which threatens global food production since bees pollinate about one-third of the food humans eat." (Accept any response that accurately combines information from both texts.)

**Mastery = Response includes at least one accurate fact from each text, combined into a coherent statement**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the Mars example and walk through the Read, Sort, Combine steps one at a time
- Provide two very short texts (2-3 sentences each) on a familiar topic and guide the student through filling in the matrix
- Start by asking: "What does Text 1 tell you? What does Text 2 tell you? What do you know when you put them together?"

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 9 on synthesizing multiple sources
- Encourage the student to compare two articles or books on the same topic during independent reading

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Two-Source Report:** Students choose a topic they are interested in, find two short articles about it, and write a paragraph that integrates information from both sources.
2. **Information Scavenger Hunt:** Give students three short texts on the same topic. Students identify one fact that appears in all three texts, one that appears in only two, and one that is unique to each.
3. **Source Comparison Poster:** Students create a visual display comparing two texts on the same topic, showing overlapping and unique information with a Venn diagram.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Students may struggle to move beyond simply alternating facts from each text. True integration means weaving the information together into a cohesive understanding, not just listing facts from Text 1 and then from Text 2.
- Color-coded highlighting is very effective for this lesson. Have students highlight Text 1 facts in one color and Text 2 facts in another. This helps them visually see where information overlaps and where it is unique.
- The Text Integration Matrix is a critical scaffold. Students should use it for every paired text activity until they can integrate information mentally.
- This lesson introduces RI.5.9 with two texts. Lesson 9 extends to three or more sources and deeper synthesis. Make sure students are comfortable with two-text integration before moving forward.
- Remind students that integrating does not mean one text is better or more important. Both texts contribute to a fuller understanding.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. The topic of both texts is the Amazon rain forest.
2. Text Integration Matrix:
   - Text 1 Only: Produces about 20% of world's oxygen; home to 10% of all known species; the Amazon River carries more water than any other river; 40,000+ plant species, 1,300+ bird species, 3,000+ fish species.
   - Both Texts: The Amazon is extremely important to life on Earth; it contains enormous biodiversity; it is a massive rain forest in South America.
   - Text 2 Only: 17% of the Amazon has been deforested; cattle ranching is the leading cause; deforestation releases carbon dioxide; indigenous communities are losing land; conservation efforts are underway.
3. Sample summary: "The Amazon rain forest is the largest tropical forest on Earth, producing 'about 20 percent of the world's oxygen' and housing an estimated 10 percent of all known species. However, this vital ecosystem faces serious threats, as 'approximately 17 percent of the Amazon' has been destroyed by deforestation, largely driven by cattle ranching. Conservation groups are working to protect the remaining forest through sustainable practices and international agreements."
4. Reading both texts shows that the Amazon is not only extremely important (Text 1 explains its role in oxygen production, biodiversity, and water systems) but also in serious danger (Text 2 explains the threats of deforestation, climate change, and habitat loss). Reading only Text 1 might leave you unaware of the threats; reading only Text 2 might not fully convey why the Amazon is so important.

### Interleaved Review
5. Main idea of Text 2: The Amazon rain forest faces serious threats from human activities, especially deforestation driven by cattle ranching. (Accept reasonable variations.)
6. Cause-and-effect chain: When farmers clear forest for ranching, the trees are cut down or burned. Because trees store carbon in their trunks and leaves, cutting or burning them releases this carbon into the atmosphere as carbon dioxide. This additional carbon dioxide in the atmosphere contributes to climate change.
