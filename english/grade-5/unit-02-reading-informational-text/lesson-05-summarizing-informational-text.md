# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 2, Lesson 5

## Topic
Summarizing Informational Text

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Write a summary that includes two or more main ideas and key supporting details
- Distinguish between a summary and a retelling (summaries are shorter and more focused)
- Keep summaries objective by excluding personal opinions

## Prerequisites
- Ability to identify two or more main ideas in a text (Lesson 3, RI.5.2)
- Ability to explain how details support main ideas (Lesson 4, RI.5.2)
- Ability to write a summary of a text with one main idea (Grade 4, RI.4.2)

## Materials Needed
- Printed or projected lesson passages
- Summary Planning Sheet graphic organizer (boxes for Main Idea 1, Main Idea 2, Key Details for each, and a Final Summary space)
- Whiteboard and markers
- Pencils and paper for student responses
- Highlighters in two colors

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review what students know about summarizing from Grade 4 and introduce the challenge of summarizing a text with multiple main ideas.

**Activity: Too Much, Too Little, Just Right**

Say: "In fourth grade, you learned how to summarize informational text. A summary is a short version of the text that includes only the most important information. Today we are going to level up. In fifth grade, your summaries need to capture two or more main ideas -- not just one."

Write on the board three summaries of an imaginary passage about rain forests:

A: "Rain forests are cool."
B: "Rain forests are dense, warm, wet forests found near the equator. They are home to jaguars, toucans, frogs, snakes, monkeys, and hundreds of kinds of insects. It rains almost every day. The trees are tall. There are different layers of the forest. Some plants grow on other plants. Scientists study rain forests."
C: "Rain forests are dense, warm ecosystems near the equator that contain an enormous diversity of plant and animal life. They also play a vital role in regulating Earth's climate by absorbing carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen."

Say: "Which summary is too little information, which is too much, and which is just right?"

Allow students to respond.

Say: "A is too little -- it is just an opinion, not a summary. B is too much -- it retells almost everything instead of focusing on the big ideas. C is just right -- it captures two main ideas (biodiversity and climate regulation) with enough detail to be clear but short enough to be a true summary. That is what we are aiming for today."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students a strategy for writing a multi-main-idea summary that is concise, objective, and complete.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Summary:** A short restatement of the most important ideas and details in a text, written in the reader's own words
- **Objective:** Based on facts, without personal opinions or feelings
- **Concise:** Using as few words as possible while still being complete and clear
- **Retelling:** Restating most or all of what happened in a text, including minor details (longer than a summary)

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Here is a four-step strategy for writing a strong summary. I call it the SMID method."

Write on the board:
- **S** = State the topic
- **M** = Main ideas (identify two or more)
- **I** = Important details (one or two per main idea)
- **D** = Draft and check (write the summary, then check: Is it objective? Is it concise? Does it include all main ideas?)

Say: "A good Grade 5 summary should be 3-5 sentences long and cover all the main ideas without including personal opinions or minor details. Let me show you."

### Worked Examples

**Passage A: "The Two Sides of Fire"**

> Fire is one of the most destructive forces in nature. Wildfires can burn millions of acres of forest, destroy homes and communities, and cause the deaths of both wildlife and humans. In 2020 alone, wildfires burned over 10 million acres across the United States. The intense heat of a wildfire can reach temperatures above 1,400 degrees Fahrenheit, hot enough to melt aluminum. Smoke from large wildfires can travel hundreds of miles, causing dangerous air quality in cities far from the fire itself.
>
> However, fire is also a natural and necessary part of many ecosystems. Many plant species depend on fire to reproduce. The seeds of the lodgepole pine, for example, are sealed inside cones that only open when exposed to the extreme heat of a fire. After a wildfire clears away dead brush and thick undergrowth, sunlight can reach the forest floor for the first time in years, allowing new plants to sprout. Fire also returns nutrients to the soil as burned plant material breaks down. Forest managers sometimes set controlled burns on purpose to maintain healthy forests and prevent the buildup of dead wood that could fuel a larger, more dangerous wildfire.

Say: "Let me use the SMID method."

Write on the board:
- S (Topic): Fire
- M (Main Ideas): (1) Fire is one of the most destructive forces in nature. (2) Fire is a natural and necessary part of many ecosystems.
- I (Important Details): (1) Wildfires burn millions of acres and destroy homes. (2) Many plants depend on fire to reproduce; fire returns nutrients to soil.
- D (Draft):

Say: "Now I combine these into a concise, objective summary."

Write on the board:
"Fire is both a destructive force and a necessary part of nature. Wildfires can burn millions of acres, destroy homes, and create dangerous air conditions across wide areas. At the same time, fire plays a vital role in many ecosystems by helping certain plants reproduce, returning nutrients to the soil, and clearing dead brush so new growth can begin."

Say: "Let me check my summary. Is it objective? Yes -- I did not say 'I think fire is scary.' Is it concise? Yes -- it is three sentences instead of repeating every fact. Does it include both main ideas? Yes -- destruction and necessity. That is a strong summary."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice writing a summary with facilitator support.

Say: "Let us try the SMID method together."

**Passage B: "Wind Energy: Promise and Problems"**

> Wind energy is one of the fastest-growing sources of electricity in the world. Wind turbines convert the energy of moving air into electrical power without burning fossil fuels or producing harmful pollution. A single large wind turbine can generate enough electricity to power more than 1,500 homes. Wind farms -- groups of turbines built in windy areas -- now operate in all 50 states. The United States generates approximately 10 percent of its total electricity from wind power, and that number continues to grow each year.
>
> Despite its benefits, wind energy faces several significant challenges. Wind turbines only generate power when the wind is blowing, which means energy production can be unpredictable. Storing wind energy for calm days remains expensive and technologically difficult. Wind turbines have also been criticized for their impact on wildlife, particularly birds and bats that can be killed by the spinning blades. Some communities oppose the construction of wind farms near their homes, citing noise and visual impact. Engineers and scientists are working to solve these problems, developing quieter turbines, better energy storage systems, and designs that are safer for wildlife.

Say: "Step S -- what is the topic?"

Allow students to respond. (Expected: Wind energy.)

Say: "Step M -- what are the two main ideas? Turn and talk."

Allow 30 seconds. Call on students.

Expected: (1) Wind energy is one of the fastest-growing and most promising sources of electricity. (2) Wind energy faces several significant challenges.

Say: "Step I -- what are the most important details for each? Choose one or two per main idea."

Allow 1 minute. Call on students.

Expected: (1) Turbines produce electricity without pollution; US generates about 10% from wind. (2) Turbines only work when wind blows; storage is expensive; turbines can harm wildlife.

Say: "Step D -- now draft your summary. Write 3-5 sentences that cover both main ideas with key details. Keep it objective and concise. You have 3 minutes."

Allow 3 minutes. Call on a student to share.

Sample summary: "Wind energy is one of the fastest-growing sources of electricity in the world. Wind turbines generate power without burning fossil fuels, and the United States already produces about 10 percent of its electricity from wind. However, wind energy also faces challenges, including unpredictable production when the wind is not blowing, expensive energy storage, and the risk of harm to birds and bats."

Say: "Great summary. It covers both main ideas, includes key details, and stays objective. Notice that it does not include opinions like 'I think wind energy is great' or minor details like the exact number of homes one turbine can power."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently write a summary of a passage with multiple main ideas.

**Passage C: "The Deep Ocean: Unknown and Essential"**

> The deep ocean remains one of the least explored places on Earth. Scientists estimate that more than 80 percent of the ocean floor has never been mapped, observed, or explored by humans. The deepest part of the ocean, the Mariana Trench, plunges nearly 36,000 feet below the surface -- deeper than Mount Everest is tall. Reaching these extreme depths requires specialized submersibles that can withstand crushing pressures of more than 16,000 pounds per square inch. Fewer people have visited the bottom of the Mariana Trench than have walked on the moon. New species are discovered in the deep ocean every year, many of which look unlike any creatures found in shallow waters. Some deep-sea fish produce their own light through a process called bioluminescence, while others survive near volcanic vents where temperatures can exceed 700 degrees Fahrenheit.
>
> Despite being so remote, the deep ocean plays a crucial role in sustaining life on the planet's surface. Deep ocean currents act as a global conveyor belt, circulating cold, nutrient-rich water from the depths to the surface, where it supports the growth of phytoplankton -- tiny organisms that produce more than half of the world's oxygen. The deep ocean also absorbs enormous amounts of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, helping to regulate global temperatures. Scientists warn that human activities such as deep-sea mining, pollution, and climate change could disrupt these vital processes, with consequences that would affect every living thing on Earth.

**Directions:**

1. Use the SMID method to plan your summary. Identify the topic, two main ideas, and key details on your Summary Planning Sheet.
2. Write a 3-5 sentence summary that includes both main ideas and at least one key detail for each. Keep your summary objective and concise.
3. Reread your summary and check: Did you include both main ideas? Is it objective? Is it concise?

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete steps 1-3 as described.
**Stretch:** Complete steps 1-3, then quote one sentence from the passage that best captures each main idea.
**Challenge:** Complete all steps above, then write a second version of your summary that is only 2 sentences long. Which version is better, and why?

### Interleaved Review
4. (Grade 4 review -- RI.4.1) Quote one sentence from the passage that supports the idea that the deep ocean is difficult to explore.
5. (Grade 4 review -- RI.4.3) According to the passage, what happens when deep ocean currents bring cold water to the surface? Explain the process using details from the text.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

> The Amazon River is the largest river in the world by volume, carrying more water than any other river on Earth. It flows through six countries in South America and stretches more than 4,000 miles from its source in the Andes Mountains to the Atlantic Ocean. The Amazon basin is home to the world's largest tropical rain forest, which contains approximately 10 percent of all species on the planet.
>
> The Amazon River also supports millions of people who live along its banks. Fishing communities depend on the river for food and income, and the river serves as the main transportation route through the dense rain forest, where roads are scarce.

Write a 2-3 sentence summary that includes both main ideas.

**Expected Answers:**
Sample summary: "The Amazon River is the largest river in the world by volume, flowing more than 4,000 miles through South America and supporting the world's largest tropical rain forest. The river is also essential to millions of people who depend on it for food, income, and transportation." (Accept any summary that captures both main ideas -- the Amazon's size/ecological importance and its importance to human communities -- without personal opinions.)

**Mastery = Summary includes both main ideas, at least one key detail, and no personal opinions**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the SMID method step by step using Passage A from Direct Instruction
- Help the student identify the two main ideas first, then guide them to select one key detail for each
- Provide the sentence frame: "This passage is about ___. The first main idea is ___. The second main idea is ___."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 6 on relationships between individuals and events
- Encourage the student to practice summarizing chapters in nonfiction books at home

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Summary Shrink:** Give students a passage and ask them to write three summaries: one in 5 sentences, one in 3 sentences, and one in 1 sentence. Discuss what must be cut at each level and what must be kept.
2. **Summary Swap:** Students write a 4-sentence summary of a passage. Partners read each other's summaries and check whether both main ideas are included. They suggest improvements.
3. **Summary vs. Retelling:** Provide a passage and two written responses -- one is a good summary and one is a retelling. Students identify which is which and explain the differences.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The most common error is writing a retelling instead of a summary. If a student's summary is longer than 5 sentences, it probably includes too many minor details. Help them ask, "Is this detail essential to understanding the main idea?"
- Some students include opinions in their summaries (e.g., "I think this is really interesting"). Remind them that a summary should only contain information from the text.
- The SMID method (State, Main ideas, Important details, Draft and check) provides a clear structure. Post it in the classroom for reference.
- This lesson is the capstone of the RI.5.2 sequence (Lessons 3-5). Students who master summarizing with multiple main ideas are well prepared for more complex analytical work in Lessons 6-10.
- Encourage students to write summaries in their own words. If they are copying entire sentences, remind them that quoting is for evidence, while summarizing is for capturing the big picture.
