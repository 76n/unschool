# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 2: Reading Informational Text

## Facilitator Guide

### Unit Overview
Unit 2 develops students' ability to read, analyze, and synthesize informational texts at the fifth-grade level. Building on the Grade 4 foundation in main idea identification, summarizing, and comparing accounts, students now learn to quote accurately from text, identify multiple main ideas within a single passage, explain complex relationships between individuals and events, and integrate information across multiple sources on the same topic. By the end of this unit, students will approach informational reading as active, critical thinkers who can support their analysis with precise textual evidence.

### Standards Addressed
| Standard | Description | Lessons |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| RI.5.1 | Quote accurately from a text when explaining what the text says explicitly and when drawing inferences from the text | 1, 2, 10 |
| RI.5.2 | Determine two or more main ideas of a text and explain how they are supported by key details; summarize the text | 3, 4, 5, 10 |
| RI.5.3 | Explain the relationships or interactions between two or more individuals, events, ideas, or concepts in a historical, scientific, or technical text based on specific information in the text | 6, 7, 10 |
| RI.5.9 | Integrate information from several texts on the same topic in order to write or speak about the subject knowledgeably | 8, 9, 10 |

### Prerequisite Skills (Grade 4)
Students should be able to:
- Determine the main idea of a text and explain how it is supported by key details; summarize the text (RI.4.2)
- Explain events, procedures, ideas, or concepts in a historical, scientific, or technical text (RI.4.3)
- Refer to details and examples in a text when explaining what the text says explicitly and when drawing inferences (RI.4.1)
- Compare and contrast a firsthand and secondhand account of the same event or topic (RI.4.6)
- Interpret information presented visually, orally, or quantitatively (RI.4.7)

### Facilitation Tips
- **Reinforce accurate quoting early and often.** RI.5.1 is a foundational skill for the entire unit. From Lesson 1 onward, require students to use quotation marks and cite specific phrases when supporting their answers. This habit will serve them across all standards.
- **Model think-alouds extensively.** During Direct Instruction, make your thinking visible. Show students how you locate evidence, determine which details matter, and decide between explicit statements and inferences.
- **Use color-coded annotation.** Encourage students to highlight in one color for main ideas and another for supporting details. When working with multiple texts (Lessons 8-9), assign a different color to each source so students can visually track where information comes from.
- **Build stamina with passage length.** Grade 5 passages are longer and more complex than Grade 4. If students struggle with the full passages, break them into paragraphs and work through one at a time before asking students to consider the whole text.
- **Connect standards explicitly.** Help students see that quoting accurately (RI.5.1) is not a standalone skill -- it is the tool they use when identifying main ideas (RI.5.2), explaining relationships (RI.5.3), and integrating sources (RI.5.9).
- **Use the difficulty tiers strategically.** Assign tiers based on exit ticket performance from the previous lesson, not fixed ability groups. Students should move between tiers fluidly.
- **Celebrate growth.** Lesson 10 is designed as a review and celebration. Use it to help students see how far they have come and to build confidence before the unit assessment.

### Common Misconceptions
| Misconception | Correction |
|---------------|------------|
| Students paraphrase instead of quoting accurately | Teach that RI.5.1 requires the exact words from the text inside quotation marks. Model the difference between paraphrasing and quoting, and show when each is appropriate. |
| Students think a text can only have one main idea | Grade 5 introduces the concept of multiple main ideas. Use passages with distinct sections or paragraphs, each with its own central point, to show that a longer text can carry more than one main idea. |
| Students confuse a detail with a main idea | Reinforce the "umbrella" test: a main idea is broad enough to cover multiple details. If a statement is too specific to cover the whole passage or section, it is a detail, not a main idea. |
| Students copy large chunks of text instead of selecting precise quotes | Teach students to identify the shortest, most relevant phrase that supports their point. Practice trimming quotes to only the essential words. |
| Students treat integration of sources as simply listing information from each text | True integration means combining, comparing, and connecting information across sources. Teach students to look for what sources share, where they differ, and what each adds uniquely. |

### Assessment Guidance
- **Formative:** Use exit tickets after every lesson. Review results before the next lesson and reteach as needed. Pay particular attention to whether students are quoting accurately (RI.5.1) since this skill underpins all other standards in the unit.
- **Summative:** The Unit Assessment includes five informational passages (including a paired set for RI.5.9) with approximately 24 questions covering all four standards. Mastery is 80% or higher overall, with at least one correct response per standard.
- **Remediation:** If a student scores below mastery on a specific standard, revisit the corresponding lessons. For RI.5.1, return to Lessons 1-2. For RI.5.2, return to Lessons 3-5. For RI.5.3, return to Lessons 6-7. For RI.5.9, return to Lessons 8-9.

### Differentiation at a Glance
- **Below grade level:** Provide shorter passages (100-150 words). Pre-teach vocabulary before each lesson. Use sentence frames for written responses (e.g., "According to the text, '___' which shows that ___"). Allow students to work with a partner during independent practice.
- **On grade level:** Use the lesson passages and questions as written. Students complete the On-level tier of independent practice. Encourage students to answer in complete sentences using textual evidence.
- **Above grade level:** Assign the Challenge tier of independent practice. Encourage students to find additional connections between passages and prior knowledge. Have students write their own informational paragraphs with multiple main ideas for a partner to analyze.

### Connection to Future Units
The skills developed in this unit form the backbone of analytical reading throughout Grade 5 and beyond. Accurate quoting (RI.5.1) and evidence-based reasoning carry directly into Units 3 and 4, where students will analyze text structure and author's purpose. The ability to identify multiple main ideas and summarize (RI.5.2) prepares students for the increasingly complex texts they will encounter in Grade 6, where they must trace arguments and evaluate claims. Explaining relationships (RI.5.3) develops the kind of analytical thinking required for middle school science and social studies reading. Most importantly, integrating multiple sources (RI.5.9) introduces students to the research and synthesis skills they will refine throughout their academic careers.
