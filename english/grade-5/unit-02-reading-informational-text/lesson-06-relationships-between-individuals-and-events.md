# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 2, Lesson 6

## Topic
Relationships Between Individuals and Events

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify relationships between two or more individuals, events, or ideas in an informational text
- Explain how individuals influenced events or how events influenced individuals, using specific evidence
- Use signal words and phrases to describe relationships (e.g., because, as a result, led to, influenced)

## Prerequisites
- Ability to explain events, procedures, and concepts in informational text (RI.4.3)
- Understanding of cause and effect from narrative text (Grade 4)
- Ability to quote accurately from text (Lesson 1, RI.5.1)

## Materials Needed
- Printed or projected lesson passages
- Relationship Map graphic organizer (boxes for individuals/events connected by labeled arrows showing the relationship)
- Whiteboard and markers
- Pencils and paper for student responses
- Highlighters

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about connections between people and events and introduce the concept of relationships in informational text.

**Activity: Connections in History**

Say: "Think about something you learned in social studies. Can you think of a person who changed history? What did they do, and what happened because of it?"

Allow 1-2 students to share briefly.

Say: "When we read informational text, especially about history and science, we see these connections everywhere. A person takes an action, and that action leads to an event. An event happens, and it changes how people think or what they do next. These are called relationships -- the connections between individuals, events, ideas, and concepts."

Write on the board:
- Individual --> Event: A person's actions lead to an event.
- Event --> Individual: An event changes a person or what they do.
- Event --> Event: One event causes or leads to another event.
- Idea --> Idea: One concept connects to or influences another concept.

Say: "In fourth grade, you explained what happened and why in informational text. In fifth grade, you need to explain the relationships between multiple people, events, or ideas. Today, we start with relationships between individuals and events."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students to identify and explain relationships between individuals and events in historical text.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Relationship:** The connection or interaction between two or more people, events, ideas, or concepts
- **Influence:** To have an effect on something; to cause a change
- **Interaction:** The way two or more things affect each other
- **Signal words:** Words that show connections between ideas (because, therefore, as a result, led to, due to, consequently)

### Instruction Notes

Say: "When we read about people and events in informational text, we need to ask three questions."

Write on the board:
1. Who are the key individuals?
2. What are the key events?
3. How are the individuals and events connected?

Say: "The third question is the most important. It is not enough to list people and events -- you need to explain the relationship between them. Signal words help us describe these connections. Let me show you."

### Worked Examples

**Passage A: "Rachel Carson and the Environmental Movement"**

> In 1962, a marine biologist named Rachel Carson published a book called Silent Spring. The book presented detailed scientific evidence that pesticides, particularly DDT, were causing serious harm to wildlife and the environment. Carson described how DDT traveled through the food chain, accumulating in the tissues of birds and causing their eggshells to become so thin that they broke before chicks could hatch. The chemical industry attacked Carson fiercely, calling her an alarmist and questioning her credentials. Despite the criticism, Silent Spring captured the attention of the American public and government officials. President John F. Kennedy ordered a special committee to investigate Carson's claims, and the committee's report confirmed her findings. Public awareness of environmental dangers grew rapidly, and in 1970, the United States government established the Environmental Protection Agency. Two years later, DDT was banned for agricultural use in the United States. Rachel Carson did not live to see these changes -- she died of cancer in 1964 -- but her work is widely credited with launching the modern environmental movement.

Say: "Let me identify the individuals and events, then map their relationships."

Write on the board:
- Individual: Rachel Carson (marine biologist)
- Event 1: Publication of Silent Spring (1962)
- Event 2: The chemical industry attacked Carson
- Individual: President Kennedy
- Event 3: Kennedy ordered an investigation; the committee confirmed Carson's findings
- Event 4: The EPA was established (1970)
- Event 5: DDT was banned (1972)

Say: "Now let me explain the relationships."

Write on the board:
- Rachel Carson's research led to the publication of Silent Spring, which exposed the dangers of DDT.
- The book influenced President Kennedy to order an investigation.
- The investigation's findings resulted in growing public awareness.
- As a result of this awareness, the government established the EPA and banned DDT.
- Carson's work launched the modern environmental movement.

Say: "Notice how I used signal words -- 'led to,' 'influenced,' 'resulted in,' 'as a result.' These words show the connections between the people and events. Carson did not just write a book. Her book started a chain of events that changed the country."

Draw a Relationship Map on the board with arrows:
Carson's research --> Silent Spring published --> Chemical industry attacks / Kennedy investigates --> Investigation confirms findings --> Public awareness grows --> EPA established / DDT banned

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice identifying and explaining relationships between individuals and events with facilitator support.

Say: "Let us work through a new passage together. As I read, listen for the individuals, the events, and the connections between them."

**Passage B: "The Wright Brothers and the Age of Flight"**

> Orville and Wilbur Wright grew up in Dayton, Ohio, where they developed an early fascination with mechanical things. As young men, they ran a bicycle shop, where they gained practical skills in building and repairing machines. In the late 1890s, the brothers became captivated by the idea of human flight after reading about the experiments of a German glider pilot named Otto Lilienthal, who died in a gliding accident in 1896. Lilienthal's death did not discourage the Wrights -- it motivated them to find a safer way to fly. The brothers spent years studying how birds controlled their flight, and they built a small wind tunnel to test different wing shapes. On December 17, 1903, at Kitty Hawk, North Carolina, Orville piloted the first powered airplane flight in history, covering 120 feet in 12 seconds. The achievement received little attention at first, but within a decade, airplanes were being used for mail delivery, military purposes, and eventually passenger travel. The Wright brothers' invention transformed transportation and connected the world in ways that had never been possible before.

Say: "Who are the key individuals in this passage?"

Allow students to respond. (Expected: Orville and Wilbur Wright, Otto Lilienthal.)

Say: "What are the key events? Turn and talk with a partner and list at least three."

Allow 1 minute. Call on students.

Expected events: Lilienthal's gliding experiments and death; the Wrights studied flight and built a wind tunnel; the first powered flight at Kitty Hawk (1903); airplanes used for mail, military, and passengers.

Say: "Now the big question: how are the individuals and events connected? I want you to write at least two relationship statements using signal words. Work with your partner."

Allow 2-3 minutes. Call on students.

Expected responses:
- Otto Lilienthal's death motivated the Wright brothers to find a safer way to achieve human flight.
- The Wright brothers' experience building bicycles gave them the practical skills that enabled them to build an airplane.
- The Wrights' successful flight at Kitty Hawk led to the development of airplanes for mail, military, and passenger use.
- As a result of the Wright brothers' invention, transportation was transformed and the world became more connected.

Say: "Excellent work. Notice that each statement explains how one person or event connects to another. You are not just listing facts -- you are explaining relationships."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently identify and explain relationships between individuals, events, and ideas.

**Passage C: "Alexander Fleming and the Discovery of Penicillin"**

> In 1928, a Scottish scientist named Alexander Fleming made a discovery that would change medicine forever. Fleming had been studying bacteria in his laboratory at St. Mary's Hospital in London when he left for a two-week vacation. When he returned, he noticed that one of his petri dishes had been contaminated by a mold. Instead of throwing it away, Fleming examined the dish closely and observed something remarkable: the bacteria near the mold had been destroyed. The mold, which belonged to a type called Penicillium, was producing a substance that killed bacteria. Fleming published his findings, but he lacked the resources to develop the substance into a usable medicine. More than a decade later, two other scientists, Howard Florey and Ernst Boris Chain, read Fleming's research and began working to purify penicillin and produce it in large quantities. Their efforts succeeded just in time for World War II, when penicillin was used to treat infected wounds and saved the lives of thousands of soldiers. By the end of the war, penicillin was being mass-produced and was available to the general public. The discovery of penicillin led to the development of many other antibiotics and is credited with saving more than 200 million lives worldwide since its introduction.

**Directions:**

1. List the key individuals in this passage.
2. List four key events in the order they occurred.
3. Explain the relationship between Alexander Fleming and the discovery of penicillin. Use at least one signal word (because, led to, as a result, etc.).
4. Explain the relationship between Florey and Chain's work and the impact of penicillin during World War II. Use signal words and quote at least one detail from the text.
5. How did one individual's work influence another individual's work in this passage? Explain with evidence.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Answer questions 1-4.
**Stretch:** Answer all five questions with accurate quotes and signal words in every response.
**Challenge:** Answer all five questions, then draw a Relationship Map showing all the connections between the individuals and events. Include labeled arrows.

### Interleaved Review
6. (Grade 4 review -- RI.4.2) What is the main idea of this passage? Write it as a complete sentence.
7. (Grade 4 review -- RI.4.3) Explain the steps that led from Fleming's contaminated petri dish to penicillin being available to the general public. List at least four steps in order.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

> In 1955, Rosa Parks refused to give up her seat on a city bus in Montgomery, Alabama, an act that violated the city's segregation laws. Her arrest sparked the Montgomery Bus Boycott, during which African Americans refused to ride city buses for 381 days. The boycott was led by a young minister named Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and brought national attention to the civil rights movement. The boycott ended when the Supreme Court ruled that bus segregation was unconstitutional.

1. Name one relationship between an individual and an event in this passage.
2. Use a signal word to explain the connection.

**Expected Answers:**
1. Any of: Rosa Parks's refusal to give up her seat led to the Montgomery Bus Boycott. OR Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. led the boycott, which brought national attention to the civil rights movement. OR The boycott resulted in the Supreme Court ruling against bus segregation.
2. Signal word used correctly (led to, sparked, resulted in, as a result, because of, etc.).

**Mastery = One correct relationship identified with a signal word that accurately describes the connection**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to Passage A and walk through the Relationship Map step by step
- Provide a simple two-sentence passage (e.g., "Thomas Edison invented the lightbulb. As a result, homes could be lit with electricity.") and ask the student to identify the individual, event, and relationship
- Practice signal words with fill-in-the-blank exercises

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 7 on cause-and-effect in scientific text
- Encourage the student to look for relationships between people and events in their social studies reading

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Relationship Chain:** Students read a longer historical passage and create a chain of connected events, showing how one individual's action led to an event, which influenced another individual, and so on.
2. **Historical Connections:** Students choose two historical figures they have studied and write a paragraph explaining the relationship between them and the events they influenced.
3. **What If?** Students choose one individual from a passage and write 3-4 sentences explaining what might have been different if that person had not taken the action described. This encourages deeper thinking about the significance of individual-event relationships.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Students often list individuals and events without explaining the connections. Emphasize that the relationship is what matters -- the "how" and "why" between people and events, not just the "who" and "what."
- Signal words are essential scaffolding for this skill. Post a list of signal words in the classroom and encourage students to use them in every response.
- This lesson focuses on historical text because relationships between individuals and events are often clearest in historical contexts. Lesson 7 extends this skill to scientific text with cause-and-effect relationships.
- Some students may struggle with passages that have multiple interconnected relationships. Encourage them to start with the simplest connection and then add complexity.
- The Relationship Map graphic organizer is a powerful tool. Model it on the board and have students create their own for each passage.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. Key individuals: Alexander Fleming, Howard Florey, Ernst Boris Chain.
2. Key events in order: (1) Fleming discovered that Penicillium mold killed bacteria (1928). (2) Fleming published his findings but could not develop a usable medicine. (3) Florey and Chain purified penicillin and produced it in large quantities. (4) Penicillin was used during World War II to treat infected wounds and save thousands of soldiers' lives.
3. Alexander Fleming's accidental discovery of mold killing bacteria led to the identification of penicillin. Because Fleming examined his contaminated petri dish closely instead of throwing it away, he found that the Penicillium mold "was producing a substance that killed bacteria," which started the chain of events that changed medicine forever.
4. Florey and Chain's work purifying penicillin resulted in the medicine being available just in time for World War II. As a result, "penicillin was used to treat infected wounds and saved the lives of thousands of soldiers." Without their efforts to produce penicillin in large quantities, the medicine would not have been available during the war.
5. Fleming's published research influenced Florey and Chain's work. The passage states that "Florey and Ernst Boris Chain read Fleming's research and began working to purify penicillin." Fleming's initial discovery provided the foundation, and Florey and Chain built on it to create a usable medicine. One individual's work directly enabled and inspired the other individuals' contributions.

### Interleaved Review
6. Main idea: Alexander Fleming's accidental discovery of penicillin, developed further by Florey and Chain, revolutionized medicine and has saved more than 200 million lives. (Accept reasonable variations.)
7. Steps in order: (1) Fleming left a petri dish unattended during vacation. (2) Mold contaminated the dish and killed the bacteria near it. (3) Fleming identified the mold as Penicillium and published his findings. (4) Florey and Chain read Fleming's research and worked to purify penicillin. (5) Penicillin was produced in large quantities during World War II. (6) After the war, penicillin became available to the general public.

See Expected Answers above.
