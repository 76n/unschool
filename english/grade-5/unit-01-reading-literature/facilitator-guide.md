# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 1: Reading Literature

## Facilitator Guide

### Unit Overview
Unit 1 introduces fifth graders to four core literary analysis skills: quoting accurately from text, determining theme through character responses and poetry, comparing and contrasting characters, settings, and events, and analyzing how visual and multimedia elements contribute to literary meaning. Over ten lessons, students progress from precise text citation and inference to thematic analysis, cross-element comparison, and multimedia interpretation. The unit culminates in a celebration lesson where students apply all four skill areas to demonstrate their growth as close readers.

### Standards Addressed
| Standard | Description | Lessons |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| RL.5.1 | Quote accurately when explaining what the text says explicitly and when drawing inferences from the text | 1, 2, 10 |
| RL.5.2 | Determine a theme of a story, drama, or poem from details in the text, including how characters respond to challenges or how the speaker reflects upon a topic; summarize the text | 3, 4, 5, 10 |
| RL.5.3 | Compare and contrast two or more characters, settings, or events in a story or drama, drawing on specific details in the text | 6, 7, 10 |
| RL.5.7 | Analyze how visual and multimedia elements contribute to the meaning, tone, or beauty of a text (e.g., graphic novel, multimedia presentation of fiction, folktale, myth, poem) | 8, 9, 10 |

### Prerequisite Skills (Grade 4)
Students entering this unit should be able to:
- Determine a theme of a story, drama, or poem from details in the text and summarize the text (RL.4.2)
- Describe in depth a character, setting, or event in a story or drama, drawing on specific details in the text (RL.4.3)
- Refer to details and examples in a text when explaining what the text says explicitly and when drawing inferences from the text (RL.4.1)
- Compare and contrast the point of view from which different stories are narrated (RL.4.6)

If students struggle with these prerequisite skills, use the interleaved review sections in each lesson to reinforce them before moving forward.

### Facilitation Tips
- **Quotation anchor chart:** From Lesson 1 onward, maintain a chart showing proper quotation formatting. Fifth graders must learn to integrate quotes smoothly into their written responses.
- **Think-alouds:** Model your own inference process frequently. Fifth graders benefit from hearing how an experienced reader moves from explicit details to logical conclusions.
- **Turn and talk:** Before cold-calling, give students 30-60 seconds to share ideas with a partner. This builds confidence and surfaces misconceptions early.
- **Flexible grouping:** Use exit ticket data to form small re-teaching groups. Students who do not meet mastery on a given lesson should receive targeted support before moving on.
- **Passage familiarity:** Preview all passages before the lesson. Some contain emotional content (e.g., characters facing loss, frustration, or fear) that may benefit from a brief content introduction.
- **Comparison organizers:** For Lessons 6 and 7, prepare Venn diagram templates or T-charts in advance to scaffold the comparison process.
- **Visual literacy vocabulary:** For Lessons 8 and 9, pre-teach terms such as panel, frame, color palette, and layout so students can discuss visual elements precisely.

### Common Misconceptions
| Misconception | Correction |
|---------------|------------|
| Quoting means copying any sentence from the text | Quoting means selecting specific words or sentences that directly support the point being made, then placing them in quotation marks with proper attribution. |
| An inference is just a guess | An inference is a logical conclusion based on text evidence combined with what the reader already knows. It must be supported by the text, not invented. |
| Theme is the same as the plot summary | The plot is what happens in the story; the theme is the deeper message or lesson that the events reveal. Theme answers "What does this story teach us?" |
| Comparing means only finding similarities | Comparing involves both similarities AND differences. Students should look for ways elements are alike and ways they are different. |
| Visual elements are just decorations | In graphic novels, illustrated texts, and multimedia presentations, visual elements carry meaning — they can establish tone, reveal character emotions, advance the plot, and add layers that words alone do not convey. |

### Assessment Guidance
- **Formative:** Use the exit ticket in each lesson to gauge daily mastery. Students scoring below the stated mastery threshold should receive re-teaching before the next lesson.
- **Summative:** The unit assessment includes five passages covering all four standards. Administer it after Lesson 10. Students should score at least 70% overall and demonstrate competency in each standard (at least 50% per standard).
- **Portfolio option:** If your program uses portfolios, include the student's Lesson 10 culminating project and the unit assessment as evidence of growth.

### Differentiation at a Glance
- **Below level:** Provide pre-reading vocabulary support; allow paired reading; use the "On-level" tier in independent practice; offer sentence frames for quotation integration (e.g., "According to the text, '___.'").
- **On level:** Follow lesson as written; use the "On-level" and "Stretch" tiers.
- **Above level:** Assign "Challenge" tier activities; encourage deeper analysis, cross-text connections, and original written responses that synthesize multiple standards.

### Connection to Future Units
The skills developed in this unit — accurate quoting, thematic analysis, cross-element comparison, and visual/multimedia literacy — will be applied and extended in Unit 2 (Reading Informational Text), where students will use evidence-based citation and comparison skills with nonfiction texts, and in Unit 3 (Writing), where students will incorporate textual evidence and thematic analysis into their own compositions. The multimedia analysis skills from Lessons 8-9 will also support later work with digital texts and media literacy.
