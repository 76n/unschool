# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 1, Lesson 6

## Topic
Comparing and Contrasting Characters

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Compare and contrast two characters in a story by identifying their similarities and differences
- Draw on specific details from the text to support comparisons
- Organize character comparisons using a structured format (Venn diagram or T-chart)

## Prerequisites
- Ability to describe characters in depth using specific text details (RL.4.3)
- Ability to distinguish between character traits and character feelings (RL.4.3)
- Ability to quote accurately from text (Lessons 1-2, RL.5.1)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard or chart paper and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils and colored pencils
- Sticky notes (for annotation)
- Printed or projected reading passages (provided below)
- Venn diagram templates or blank paper for drawing Venn diagrams

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about comparing and contrasting using familiar, everyday examples.

**Activity: Two Friends, Same and Different**

Say: "Think about two people you know — maybe two friends, two family members, or two classmates. How are they similar? How are they different? Turn to your partner and share one similarity and one difference."

Allow 60 seconds for partner sharing.

Say: "When we notice how two people are alike and how they are different, we are comparing and contrasting. Today, we are going to use that same skill with characters in stories. But in fifth grade, we cannot just say 'they are different.' We have to use specific details from the text to prove our comparisons. That means quoting the exact words the author uses."

Write on the board:
- **Compare = How are they the SAME?**
- **Contrast = How are they DIFFERENT?**
- **Support every comparison with TEXT EVIDENCE.**

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students a systematic approach to comparing and contrasting characters using specific text details.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Compare:** To identify ways two or more things are similar or alike.
- **Contrast:** To identify ways two or more things are different.
- **Character trait:** A lasting quality of a person's personality (e.g., brave, generous, stubborn). Traits are different from temporary feelings.
- **Text evidence:** Specific words, actions, dialogue, or descriptions from the passage that support a claim.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "In fourth grade, you learned to describe one character in depth. In fifth grade, we take it further — we compare and contrast two or more characters within the same story. This means looking at two characters side by side and analyzing their similarities and differences."

Write on the board:
**What to Compare and Contrast:**
- Traits (What kind of people are they?)
- Motivations (What do they want?)
- Responses to challenges (How do they handle problems?)
- Actions (What do they do?)
- Relationships (How do they interact with others?)

Say: "We can organize our thinking using a Venn diagram or a T-chart. Let me model this."

### Worked Examples

**Passage: "Two Sisters"**

> Lucia and Marisol were sisters, but you would never guess it by watching them. Lucia woke at dawn every morning, made her bed with sharp corners, and lined up her shoes by color in the closet. Her homework was always finished the night before, and her backpack was packed and zipped by eight o'clock.
>
> Marisol, on the other hand, treated mornings like a battle she was losing. She hit the snooze button three times, left her blankets in a tangled heap, and stuffed her homework into her bag while running out the door. Her shoes were wherever she had kicked them off the night before.
>
> But when their neighbor Mrs. Pak slipped on the icy sidewalk and could not carry her groceries home, both sisters dropped what they were doing and ran to help. Lucia organized the bags by weight so nothing would break. Marisol held Mrs. Pak's arm and told jokes to make her laugh as they walked.
>
> "Those girls," Mrs. Pak told their mother later. "Different as night and day, but both with hearts of gold."

Say: "Let me compare and contrast Lucia and Marisol."

Draw a Venn diagram on the board:

**Lucia (only):**
- Organized and structured: "made her bed with sharp corners," "lined up her shoes by color"
- Prepares in advance: "homework was always finished the night before"
- Helps in a practical, organized way: "organized the bags by weight"

**Marisol (only):**
- Disorganized and spontaneous: "hit the snooze button three times," "stuffed her homework into her bag while running"
- Acts in the moment: "treated mornings like a battle she was losing"
- Helps in an emotional, personal way: "held Mrs. Pak's arm and told jokes to make her laugh"

**Both:**
- Caring and generous: "both sisters dropped what they were doing and ran to help"
- "Hearts of gold" (Mrs. Pak's description)
- Responded immediately when someone needed them

Say: "See how the sisters are very different in their daily habits and personalities, but similar in what matters most — they both care about others and act when someone needs help. And I used specific details from the text to prove every point."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice comparing and contrasting characters with teacher support.

Say: "Now let us try a new passage together. As I read, pay attention to the two main characters and start thinking about how they are alike and different."

**Passage: "The Race"**

> Owen and Destiny had been best friends since kindergarten, even though they approached almost everything in opposite ways. When the school announced a charity fun run, they both signed up immediately — but for completely different reasons.
>
> Owen trained every day for two weeks. He ran laps around the track after school, timed himself with a stopwatch, and studied the race course map. "I want to finish in the top ten," he told Destiny. "I have been working too hard not to."
>
> Destiny did not train at all. She spent the weeks before the race making posters, convincing other students to sign up, and organizing a bake sale to raise extra money for the charity. "I do not care about my time," she said. "I care about how much money we raise."
>
> On race day, Owen crossed the finish line eighth out of fifty runners. He pumped his fist in the air and checked his time — a personal best. Destiny finished somewhere in the middle, walking the last quarter mile with a group of first graders who were tired and wanted to quit. She cheered them across the finish line, and they cheered right back.
>
> At the awards ceremony, Owen received a ribbon for placing in the top ten. Destiny received the Spirit Award for raising the most money and encouraging the most participants. They looked at each other's awards and grinned.
>
> "You destroyed that race," Destiny said.
>
> "You destroyed the fundraiser," Owen said.
>
> They bumped fists. Different goals, same team.

Say: "Let us build a comparison together. I will draw a Venn diagram on the board. What goes on Owen's side only?"

Guide students to identify:
- Competitive and goal-driven: "trained every day," "timed himself," "I want to finish in the top ten"
- Focused on personal achievement
- Prepared through physical training

Say: "What goes on Destiny's side only?"

Guide students to identify:
- Community-focused and encouraging: "making posters, convincing other students to sign up"
- Focused on helping others: "walked the last quarter mile with a group of first graders"
- Prepared by organizing and rallying people

Say: "What goes in the middle — what do they share?"

Guide students to identify:
- Both signed up immediately — enthusiastic and willing
- Both successful in their own way (Owen earned a ribbon, Destiny earned the Spirit Award)
- Both supportive of each other ("You destroyed that race" / "You destroyed the fundraiser")
- Both care about the event, just in different ways

Say: "Now write two sentences comparing and two sentences contrasting them. Use quotes from the text."

**Model response:** Owen and Destiny are similar because they both signed up for the fun run "immediately" and both earned recognition — Owen for speed and Destiny for spirit. They are also alike in how they support each other, as shown when Destiny says, "You destroyed that race" and Owen responds, "You destroyed the fundraiser." However, they differ in their motivations: Owen is competitive and trained physically, saying, "I want to finish in the top ten," while Destiny focused on community, saying, "I do not care about my time. I care about how much money we raise." Their approaches to race day also differ — Owen ran for a personal best while Destiny walked with struggling first graders to encourage them.

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently compare and contrast two characters using text evidence.

**Passage: "The Storm"**

> When the power went out during the thunderstorm, Noor and her brother Idris reacted in completely different ways.
>
> Noor immediately took charge. She found the flashlight in the kitchen drawer, gathered candles from the dining room, and set them up around the living room in a careful circle. She checked the weather report on her phone and announced, "The storm should pass in about two hours. We have water in the fridge and crackers in the pantry."
>
> Idris sat on the couch with his knees pulled up to his chest. Every time thunder cracked, he flinched. "What if the power does not come back?" he asked. "What if the roof leaks? What about the food in the freezer?"
>
> "One thing at a time," Noor said calmly. She sat down next to him and handed him a candle. "Hold this. You are in charge of the light."
>
> Idris took the candle. His hands were shaking, but having a job seemed to settle him. He held the candle steady and watched the flame.
>
> After a while, the thunder moved farther away. Idris said, "I know I worry too much."
>
> Noor bumped his shoulder. "You do not worry too much. You just think of things other people do not. That is why you are the one who reminded Dad to buy batteries last week. Without you, we would not even have this flashlight."
>
> Idris almost smiled. "I guess worrying is useful sometimes."
>
> "Sometimes," Noor said. "And being calm is useful sometimes too. Good thing there are two of us."

**Directions:** Answer the following questions in complete sentences. Use accurate quotes from the passage.

1. Create a Venn diagram (or written comparison) showing how Noor and Idris are similar and different. Include at least two items on each side and at least two in the middle.
2. How does Noor respond to the storm? Quote at least one piece of evidence.
3. How does Idris respond to the storm? Quote at least one piece of evidence.
4. What does Noor's comment at the end — "Good thing there are two of us" — suggest about the value of their different personalities?

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Answer questions 1-4 as written.
**Stretch:** After answering questions 1-4, write a paragraph explaining which character you are more like — Noor or Idris — and why. Use details from both the passage and your own experience.
**Challenge:** After answering all questions, compare Noor and Idris to Lucia and Marisol from the Worked Example. How are the two pairs of siblings similar in the way their differences work together?

### Interleaved Review
5. (Grade 4 review — RL.4.2) What is the theme of this passage? Write it as a complete sentence.
6. (Grade 4 review — RL.4.6) This passage is written in third person. How can you tell? Name one thing the narrator can do that a first-person narrator (like Noor or Idris) could not.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Read the following mini-passage:

> At the school bake sale, Amir brought a tray of perfectly decorated cupcakes. Each one had a tiny flower made from frosting. Jun brought a plate of cookies that were slightly lopsided and different sizes. "They are homemade," Jun said with a shrug. Amir's cupcakes sold out first, but Jun's cookies got the most compliments because people said they tasted "like something my grandma would make."

1. Name one way Amir and Jun are similar.
2. Name one way they are different. Use a quote from the passage.

**Expected Answers:**
1. They are similar because they both participated in the bake sale and both contributed baked goods. (Accept: Both had something positive about their offering.)
2. They are different in their approach: Amir focused on appearance with "perfectly decorated cupcakes" and "a tiny flower made from frosting," while Jun's cookies were "slightly lopsided and different sizes" but earned compliments for tasting "like something my grandma would make." Amir's emphasis was on looks; Jun's strength was flavor and homemade quality.

**Mastery = Student identifies at least one valid similarity and one valid difference, with the difference supported by a text quote.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the Venn diagram structure using a simple, familiar example (e.g., comparing two animals or two foods).
- Have the student reread the exit ticket and highlight details about Amir in one color and details about Jun in another.
- Provide a sentence frame: "[Character A] is ___, as shown by '___,' while [Character B] is ___, as shown by '___.' They are both ___."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 7, which extends comparison to settings and events.
- Encourage the student to compare two characters in their independent reading book.

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Character Comparison Poster:** The student creates a poster comparing two characters from a book they are reading. The poster includes a Venn diagram, two quotes per character, and a sentence explaining what the comparison reveals.
2. **Same Challenge, Different Response:** The student writes a short scene (5-8 sentences) where two characters face the same problem but respond in opposite ways. Then they write one sentence explaining what each response reveals about the character.
3. **Real-World Comparison:** The student compares two real people they admire (family members, historical figures, etc.) using the same structure: traits, motivations, and responses to challenges.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- **Most common mistake:** Students will list traits for each character separately without connecting them. Redirect: "You described each character well, but now put them side by side. What is similar? What is different?"
- **Venn diagrams:** Some students may put too many items in the "Both" section and not enough in the individual sections, or vice versa. Encourage balance.
- **Trait vs. feeling reminder:** From Grade 4, students learned that traits are lasting qualities while feelings are temporary. If students say "Idris is scared," ask: "Is scared a trait or a feeling? What is his lasting trait?"
- **Pacing note:** The Guided Practice passage is rich enough for extended discussion. If time allows, spend a few extra minutes having students share their written comparison sentences.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. **Venn Diagram:**
   - **Noor only:** Calm and take-charge; found the flashlight, set up candles, checked the weather report. Responds to stress by taking practical action. The text says she "immediately took charge."
   - **Idris only:** Anxious and cautious; "sat on the couch with his knees pulled up to his chest" and "every time thunder cracked, he flinched." Responds to stress by worrying and asking questions.
   - **Both:** Care about each other; both are useful in their own way — Noor provides calm leadership, Idris's worrying led to preparedness (he reminded Dad to buy batteries). Both siblings appreciate the other's personality.
2. **Noor's response:** Noor responds to the storm by taking practical action. The text says she "immediately took charge," found the flashlight, set up candles, and reassured Idris by saying, "One thing at a time." She also gave Idris a job to help him feel useful.
3. **Idris's response:** Idris responds with anxiety. He "sat on the couch with his knees pulled up to his chest" and asked worried questions like, "What if the power does not come back? What if the roof leaks?" However, once Noor gave him a job (holding the candle), "having a job seemed to settle him."
4. **Noor's comment:** Noor's statement "Good thing there are two of us" suggests that their different personalities complement each other. Noor's calmness helps the family stay focused during a crisis, while Idris's tendency to worry means he thinks ahead and prepares for problems (like reminding their father to buy batteries). Together, they cover what the other person misses, making them stronger as a pair than either would be alone.

### Interleaved Review
5. (RL.4.2) Theme: People with different strengths and temperaments are valuable in different ways, and working together allows each person's strengths to fill in for the other's weaknesses. (Accept reasonable variations about the value of different personalities or complementary strengths.)
6. (RL.4.6) The passage is in third person because the narrator refers to both characters by name ("Noor" and "Idris") and uses pronouns like "she" and "he" rather than "I." A third-person narrator can describe both characters' actions and reactions, while a first-person narrator (like Noor) could only describe her own thoughts and would have to guess at what Idris was thinking or feeling internally.
