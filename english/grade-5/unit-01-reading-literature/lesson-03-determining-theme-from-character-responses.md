# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 1, Lesson 3

## Topic
Determining Theme from Character Responses

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify how a character responds to a challenge or conflict in a story
- Explain how the character's response reveals the theme of the text
- Support the identified theme with accurate quotes from the passage

## Prerequisites
- Ability to quote accurately from text and draw inferences (Lessons 1-2, RL.5.1)
- Understanding of theme as a message or lesson expressed in a complete sentence (RL.4.2)
- Ability to distinguish theme from topic

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard or chart paper and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Index cards (for sorting activity)
- Printed or projected reading passages (provided below)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect the concept of challenge and response to students' own experiences before applying it to literature.

**Activity: How Would You Respond?**

Say: "Imagine this situation: You have been practicing for a spelling bee for weeks. On the day of the competition, you misspell your word in the second round and have to sit down while others keep going. How would you respond? What would you do next?"

Allow 60 seconds for partner sharing.

Say: "Some of you said you would feel disappointed but try again next year. Some said you might feel embarrassed. The way you respond to a challenge tells people a lot about who you are. In stories, it works the same way. When a character faces a challenge, the way they respond reveals the theme — the deeper message — of the story. Today, we are going to learn how to trace that connection."

Write on the board: **Character's Challenge + Character's Response = Theme**

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students to trace the path from a character's challenge through their response to the theme the author is communicating.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Challenge:** A problem, conflict, or difficult situation a character faces in the story.
- **Response:** How the character reacts to, deals with, or overcomes the challenge — through actions, words, thoughts, or decisions.
- **Theme:** The big message or lesson the author communicates through the story. In fifth grade, theme is often revealed by how characters respond to challenges.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "In fourth grade, you learned to find the theme of a story by looking at what happens and what the characters learn. In fifth grade, we go deeper. We look specifically at the challenges characters face and how they respond. The response is where the theme lives."

Write on the board:
**Three Steps to Find Theme from Character Response:**
1. Identify the character's challenge — what problem do they face?
2. Track the character's response — what do they do, say, think, or decide?
3. State the theme — what lesson does the character's response teach the reader?

Say: "Let me model this with a passage."

### Worked Examples

**Passage: "The Tryout"**

> Malik had been counting down the days until basketball tryouts. He practiced layups in his driveway every evening and watched videos of professional players to study their footwork. On tryout day, he arrived early, laced up his shoes, and felt ready.
>
> But during the scrimmage, things fell apart. Malik missed three shots in a row. He tripped over his own feet going for a rebound. The coach's whistle blew, and the older kids on the court seemed to move twice as fast as he did.
>
> When the results were posted the next day, Malik's name was not on the list. He stood in the hallway staring at the paper until his friend Jerome found him.
>
> "You okay?" Jerome asked.
>
> Malik took a slow breath. "Not really. But I know what I need to work on now. I could not keep up with the speed out there. So I am going to train harder and try again in the spring."
>
> Jerome raised his eyebrows. "Most people would just quit."
>
> "I am not most people," Malik said. And the next morning, he was back in the driveway, practicing before the sun came up.

Say: "Let me use our three steps."

Write on the board:
1. **Challenge:** Malik tried out for basketball and did not make the team.
2. **Response:** Instead of quitting, Malik identified what he needed to improve — his speed — and committed to training harder for the spring tryout. The text says, "I know what I need to work on now" and he was "back in the driveway, practicing before the sun came up."
3. **Theme:** When you face failure, learning from it and trying again is more valuable than giving up.

Say: "Notice how I quoted Malik's exact words and actions to support the theme. The theme comes directly from how he chose to respond. If Malik had quit, the story would have a completely different theme."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice identifying challenge, response, and theme with teacher support.

Say: "Let us work through a new passage together. As I read, jot down: What is the challenge? How does the character respond?"

**Passage: "The Group Project"**

> Sana was assigned to a group project with three other students. She was excited at first — until the first meeting. Diego spent the whole time drawing in his notebook. Fatima said she was too busy with soccer to do her part. And Brianna argued with every idea Sana suggested.
>
> That night, Sana complained to her older sister, Layla. "They are not doing anything. I should just do the whole project myself so it is done right."
>
> Layla looked up from her homework. "You could do that. But then you would be exhausted, and they would not learn anything. What if you tried a different approach?"
>
> The next day, Sana came to the meeting with a plan. She asked Diego to design the poster since he was already an amazing artist. She suggested Fatima handle the research since she could do it on her phone between soccer practices. And she invited Brianna to lead the presentation, since Brianna clearly had strong opinions and was not afraid to speak up.
>
> To Sana's surprise, everyone agreed. Diego's poster was the best one in the class. Fatima found facts no one else had thought of. And Brianna delivered the presentation with confidence and energy.
>
> "That actually worked," Sana told Layla afterward.
>
> "Of course it did," Layla said. "You stopped trying to do everything yourself and started figuring out what everyone was good at."

Say: "Step 1 — what is the challenge?"

Expected: Sana's group members were not cooperating, and she was frustrated.

Say: "Step 2 — how does Sana respond? Find the key moment."

Expected: Instead of doing the project alone, Sana assigned each person a task that matched their strengths — Diego designed, Fatima researched, Brianna presented.

Say: "Good. Now quote the text. What exact words show us her response?"

Guide students to: "She asked Diego to design the poster since he was already an amazing artist" and "she invited Brianna to lead the presentation, since Brianna clearly had strong opinions and was not afraid to speak up."

Say: "Step 3 — what theme does Sana's response reveal?"

Guide toward: **A good leader finds and uses each person's strengths rather than trying to do everything alone.** (Accept variations about teamwork, leadership, or recognizing others' abilities.)

Say: "See how the theme comes from the response, not just from the problem? If Sana had done the project alone, the theme would be different."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently trace the challenge-response-theme path and support their answers with quotes.

**Passage: "The New Neighborhood"**

> When Jin's family moved from the city to a small town, everything felt wrong. The streets were too quiet. The nearest store was a twenty-minute drive. And at school, everyone already had their friend groups. Jin ate lunch alone for the first three days, sitting at the end of a long table and pretending to read.
>
> On Thursday, a girl named Rosa sat down across from him. "You are in my math class," she said. "I am Rosa."
>
> "Jin," he said, barely looking up.
>
> Rosa did not seem bothered by his short answer. "A bunch of us play soccer in the park after school. You should come."
>
> Jin almost said no. He was not good at soccer. He did not know anyone. And part of him just wanted to go home and be miserable in peace. But another part of him remembered what his mother had said before the move: "New places only stay new if you refuse to let them become familiar."
>
> "Okay," Jin said. "I will come."
>
> That afternoon, Jin missed the ball more than he kicked it. He did not score a single goal. But Rosa high-fived him anyway, and a boy named Marcus showed him a trick for trapping the ball with his foot. By the time the sun started setting, Jin had learned four names, laughed until his sides hurt, and forgotten, at least for an hour, that everything felt wrong.

**Directions:** Answer the following questions in complete sentences. Use accurate quotes from the passage.

1. What is the main challenge Jin faces in this passage?
2. Describe Jin's response to the challenge. How does he change from the beginning of the passage to the end?
3. Quote at least two pieces of evidence that show Jin's response to the challenge.
4. What is the theme of this passage? Write it as a complete sentence. Explain how Jin's response reveals this theme.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Answer questions 1-4 as written.
**Stretch:** After answering questions 1-4, explain what the story's theme would have been if Jin had said "no" to Rosa's invitation. How would a different response change the message?
**Challenge:** After answering all questions, write about a character from a book you have read who responded to a challenge in a way that revealed a theme. Identify the challenge, response, and theme, using the same three-step process.

### Interleaved Review
5. (Grade 4 review — RL.4.1) What did Jin's mother say to him before the move? Copy the exact quote from the passage.
6. (Grade 4 review — RL.4.3) Describe Rosa using one character trait. Provide specific evidence from the text to support your answer.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Read the following mini-passage:

> Ava's science experiment failed in front of the whole class. The volcano she built did not erupt. She felt her face burn with embarrassment. But instead of sitting down, she said, "I think the baking soda ratio was off. Can I try again with a different measurement?" The teacher smiled and handed her a new cup of baking soda.

1. What is Ava's challenge?
2. How does she respond?
3. What theme does her response suggest? Write it as a complete sentence.

**Expected Answers:**
1. Ava's volcano experiment failed in front of the class, and she felt embarrassed.
2. Instead of giving up, Ava identified what went wrong ("the baking soda ratio was off") and asked to try again with a different approach.
3. Theme: When something goes wrong, figuring out what failed and trying again is braver than giving up. (Accept reasonable variations about resilience, learning from mistakes, or problem-solving.)

**Mastery = Student correctly identifies the challenge, describes the response, and states a theme as a complete sentence connected to the character's response.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the three-step formula (Challenge + Response = Theme) using a familiar example.
- Have the student retell a fairy tale and identify the character's challenge and response before attempting to state the theme.
- Provide a sentence frame: "When [character] faced [challenge], they [response], which teaches us that ___."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 4, which applies theme identification to poetry.
- Encourage the student to track character challenges and responses in their independent reading.

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Challenge-Response Chart:** The student selects a book they have read and creates a three-column chart (Challenge | Response | Theme) for the main character, filling in at least two rows for different challenges in the story.
2. **Rewrite the Response:** Give the student a short passage. They rewrite the ending so the character responds differently to the challenge, then explain how the new response changes the theme.
3. **Real-Life Themes:** The student writes a short paragraph about a real challenge they or someone they know faced, describes the response, and identifies the "theme" or lesson learned.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- **Most common mistake:** Students will identify the challenge but skip straight to the theme without tracking the response. Redirect: "Before you state the theme, tell me exactly what the character DID. How did they respond?"
- **Anchor chart:** Add a new section to the class anchor chart: "Finding Theme Through Character Responses" with the three-step formula and an example from today's lesson.
- **Connecting to Lesson 1-2:** Remind students that they still need to quote accurately when supporting their theme statement. The skills from Lessons 1 and 2 carry forward into every lesson this unit.
- **Pacing note:** The Guided Practice passage has multiple characters, which can slow discussion. If time is tight, focus the conversation on Sana's response rather than detailing each group member's contribution.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. **Jin's challenge:** Jin's family moved from the city to a small town where everything felt unfamiliar and lonely. He had no friends and "ate lunch alone for the first three days, sitting at the end of a long table and pretending to read."
2. **Jin's response:** At first, Jin withdrew and isolated himself. But when Rosa invited him to play soccer, he overcame his reluctance and said yes, even though "he was not good at soccer" and "did not know anyone." By the end, he had "learned four names, laughed until his sides hurt, and forgotten, at least for an hour, that everything felt wrong." He went from isolating himself to opening up and trying.
3. **Two quotes showing his response:**
   - Jin almost turned Rosa down, but he remembered his mother's words: "New places only stay new if you refuse to let them become familiar." This motivated him to say, "Okay. I will come."
   - At the soccer game, even though "Jin missed the ball more than he kicked it" and "did not score a single goal," he stayed and connected with the other kids, showing he was willing to be imperfect in order to belong.
4. **Theme:** Being open to new experiences and new people, even when it feels uncomfortable, is the first step toward feeling at home in an unfamiliar place. Jin's response reveals this theme because his choice to accept Rosa's invitation — despite wanting to "go home and be miserable in peace" — led to laughter, new friendships, and a moment where the new place started feeling less wrong.

### Interleaved Review
5. (RL.4.1) Jin's mother said, "New places only stay new if you refuse to let them become familiar."
6. (RL.4.3) Rosa is friendly and welcoming. She sat down across from Jin at lunch even though he was a stranger who "barely" looked up, and she invited him to play soccer with her group without hesitation. She also "did not seem bothered by his short answer" and high-fived him at the soccer game even though he missed the ball more than he kicked it.
