# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 1, Lesson 5

## Topic
Summarizing Stories and Drama

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Summarize a story or drama by identifying the key characters, setting, problem, major events, and resolution
- Distinguish between a summary (brief retelling of key events) and a complete retelling (every detail)
- Include the theme when summarizing, connecting it to the events of the text

## Prerequisites
- Ability to determine theme from character responses and poetry (Lessons 3-4, RL.5.2)
- Ability to quote accurately from text (Lessons 1-2, RL.5.1)
- Understanding of story elements: character, setting, problem, events, resolution (RL.4.3)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard or chart paper and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Printed or projected reading passages (provided below)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Help students understand the difference between retelling every detail and creating a focused summary.

**Activity: The Movie Review Challenge**

Say: "Imagine your friend asks you what a movie was about. You have two choices. Choice A: You tell them every single thing that happened, scene by scene, for twenty minutes. Choice B: You give them the main idea in about thirty seconds — the important characters, the main problem, and how it ends. Which choice does your friend actually want?"

Allow students to respond.

Say: "Most of the time, your friend wants Choice B — the summary. A summary captures the most important parts and leaves out the small details. Today, we are going to practice writing strong summaries of stories and drama. A fifth-grade summary also includes the theme — the big message the text communicates."

Write on the board: **Summary = Key Characters + Setting + Problem + Major Events + Resolution + Theme**

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach a clear strategy for summarizing fiction and drama, including how to weave in theme.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Summary:** A brief retelling of a text that includes only the most important information — key characters, setting, central problem, major events, resolution, and theme.
- **Retelling:** Describing everything that happens in a text in order, including minor details. A retelling is longer and less focused than a summary.
- **Resolution:** How the main problem or conflict is solved or addressed by the end of the story.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "A good summary answers five questions in just a few sentences."

Write on the board:
**The Summary Framework:**
1. WHO are the key characters?
2. WHERE and WHEN does the story take place?
3. WHAT is the main problem?
4. HOW do the characters deal with it? (major events only)
5. HOW does it end, and WHAT is the theme?

Say: "The hardest part of summarizing is deciding what to leave OUT. A detail belongs in the summary only if removing it would make the summary confusing or incomplete. Let me show you."

### Worked Examples

**Passage: "The Lighthouse Keeper"**

> Twelve-year-old Cora lived on a small rocky island off the coast of Maine with her father, who was the lighthouse keeper. Every evening, her father climbed the iron spiral staircase to the top of the lighthouse and lit the great lamp. The beam swept across the dark ocean, warning ships away from the rocks.
>
> One November night, a terrible storm rolled in. The wind howled so loudly that Cora could feel the cottage walls shaking. Her father put on his rain slicker and headed for the lighthouse as usual, but halfway across the yard, he slipped on the wet stones and fell hard. Cora heard him cry out.
>
> She ran outside and found him sitting on the ground, gripping his ankle. "I think it is broken," he said through clenched teeth. "Cora, the lamp. You have to light it. If the ships cannot see the light, they will hit the rocks."
>
> Cora had watched her father light the lamp hundreds of times, but she had never done it herself. Her hands shook as she climbed the narrow staircase. The wind battered the glass windows at the top. She struck the match three times before it caught. When the flame finally touched the wick, the great lamp blazed to life, and the beam cut through the storm like a golden arm reaching across the water.
>
> She stayed at the top all night, tending the flame, watching the ships pass safely in the distance. When the sun finally rose, the storm had cleared. Cora climbed down the stairs on trembling legs and found her father waiting at the bottom, propped against the wall with his injured ankle wrapped in a towel.
>
> "You did it," he said softly.
>
> Cora nodded. She was exhausted, but for the first time, the lighthouse felt like hers too.

Say: "Now, a weak summary would retell every detail: 'Cora lived on an island. Her father was the lighthouse keeper. Every evening he climbed the stairs. One night there was a storm...' That is too long. It includes minor details."

Say: "A strong summary uses our framework."

Write on the board:
**Summary:** Cora, a twelve-year-old girl, lives on a small island with her father, who keeps the lighthouse. During a severe storm, her father injures his ankle and cannot light the lamp. Cora must climb the lighthouse and light it herself for the first time. Despite her fear, she succeeds and tends the flame through the night, keeping ships safe. The theme is that when others depend on you, rising to the challenge reveals strength you did not know you had.

Say: "Notice what I left out: the iron staircase, the rain slicker, the match striking three times. Those are vivid details, but the summary works without them. I kept only the information needed to understand the story's core."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice summarizing a drama scene with teacher support.

Say: "Now let us summarize a drama scene together. Remember, drama has characters, dialogue, and stage directions but no narrator. We still use the same framework."

**Drama: "The Talent Show"**

> SCENE: A school auditorium backstage. ELENA stands holding a guitar. Her hands are visibly shaking. TOMÁS, her older brother, stands next to her.
>
> TOMÁS: You have practiced this song a hundred times. You know it by heart.
>
> ELENA: (peering through the curtain at the audience) That is a LOT of people, Tomás. I did not think there would be this many.
>
> TOMÁS: It does not matter how many people are out there. When you play, you are not playing for them. You are playing for you. Remember why you started learning guitar in the first place.
>
> ELENA: (quietly) Because of Abuela's song. The one she used to hum when she was cooking.
>
> TOMÁS: (nodding) Right. You taught yourself guitar so you could play that song. That is what tonight is about. Not the audience. Not the judges. Just the song.
>
> ELENA: (taking a deep breath, standing straighter) Okay. Just the song.
>
> (An ANNOUNCER's voice is heard offstage: "Next up — Elena Morales!")
>
> ELENA: (stepping toward the curtain, then stopping) Tomás? What if I mess up?
>
> TOMÁS: (smiling) Then you will keep playing. That is what musicians do.
>
> (ELENA nods, grips her guitar, and walks through the curtain. The sound of a single guitar note rings out, clear and strong.)

Say: "Let us use our framework. Who are the key characters?"

Expected: Elena (the main character) and her brother Tomás.

Say: "Where and when does it take place?"

Expected: Backstage at a school talent show auditorium.

Say: "What is the main problem?"

Expected: Elena is terrified about performing in front of a large audience.

Say: "How does she deal with it?"

Expected: Tomás reminds her that she learned guitar to play her grandmother's song, and that the performance is about the song, not the audience. Elena takes a deep breath and decides to go onstage.

Say: "How does it end, and what is the theme?"

Expected: Elena walks through the curtain and plays. The theme is that remembering why you started something can give you the courage to keep going, even when you are afraid.

Say: "Now put it all together in 3-4 sentences." Guide students to write a summary.

**Model summary:** In this drama scene, Elena is backstage at a talent show, paralyzed with stage fright before her guitar performance. Her brother Tomás calms her by reminding her that she learned guitar to play their grandmother's song, not to impress the audience. Elena takes a deep breath and walks onstage, playing her first note "clear and strong." The theme is that reconnecting with your original purpose can give you courage when fear threatens to hold you back.

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently summarize a story, including theme.

**Passage: "The Ice"**

> Every winter, the pond behind Adaeze's house froze solid enough to skate on. Adaeze had been skating since she was four years old. She could glide backwards, spin in circles, and stop on a dime. On the ice, she felt free.
>
> This winter, her cousin Kwame came to visit from Atlanta. He had never seen a frozen pond before, let alone skated on one. When Adaeze handed him a pair of skates, he looked at them like she had handed him a pair of snakes.
>
> "Just hold onto my arm," Adaeze said. "I will not let you fall."
>
> Kwame wobbled onto the ice and immediately grabbed the nearest tree branch hanging over the edge. His legs slid in opposite directions. "This is impossible," he said.
>
> "It is not impossible. It is just new," Adaeze said. She held out both hands. "Trust me."
>
> For the next hour, Adaeze skated slowly beside Kwame, holding his arm, encouraging him every time he took a step. When he fell — and he fell a lot — she helped him up without laughing. She showed him how to bend his knees and push off with the inside of his foot.
>
> By the end of the afternoon, Kwame could glide forward for about ten feet without holding on to anything. It was not graceful. His arms windmilled, and his ankles wobbled. But he was smiling.
>
> "Same time tomorrow?" Kwame asked.
>
> Adaeze grinned. "Same time tomorrow."

**Directions:** Answer the following questions in complete sentences.

1. Write a summary of this passage in 4-5 sentences. Include the key characters, setting, problem, major events, resolution, and theme. Do NOT retell every detail.
2. List two details from the passage that you left OUT of your summary. Explain why you left them out.
3. What is the theme of this passage? Write it as a complete sentence and explain how the characters' actions reveal it.
4. Quote one sentence from the passage that best supports the theme.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Answer questions 1-4 as written.
**Stretch:** After answering questions 1-4, rewrite your summary as if you were telling a friend about the story in just two sentences. What did you have to cut? What did you keep?
**Challenge:** After answering all questions, write a summary of the drama scene from Guided Practice ("The Talent Show") without looking at the model summary. Then compare your version to the model. What did you include that the model did not? What did the model include that you missed?

### Interleaved Review
5. (Grade 4 review — RL.4.3) Describe Adaeze using two character traits. For each, provide specific evidence from the text.
6. (Grade 4 review — RL.4.1) What did Adaeze say when Kwame called skating "impossible"? Quote her exact words.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Read the following mini-passage:

> Maya wanted to enter the school writing contest but was afraid her story was not good enough. Her teacher, Mr. Kim, read her draft and told her it had a strong beginning and an interesting main character. He suggested she revise the ending. Maya rewrote the ending three times before submitting. She did not win first place, but the judges wrote "beautifully told" on her entry. Maya taped the comment to her desk.

1. Write a 2-3 sentence summary of this passage that includes the problem, resolution, and theme.

**Expected Answers:**
1. Maya was afraid to enter the writing contest because she doubted her story. After her teacher encouraged her and suggested revisions, she rewrote the ending multiple times and submitted it. Although she did not win first place, the judges praised her writing, and the experience taught her that improvement and effort matter more than winning. (Accept reasonable variations that capture the problem, resolution, and a valid theme.)

**Mastery = Student writes a focused summary (not a retelling of every detail) that includes the problem, resolution, and a theme statement.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the Summary Framework (Who, Where/When, What problem, How dealt with, How ends/theme).
- Have the student retell the exit ticket passage aloud, then ask: "Which of those details are the MOST important? Cross out the rest."
- Provide a sentence frame: "[Character] faced [problem]. They [major action]. In the end, [resolution], which shows that [theme]."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 6, which focuses on comparing and contrasting characters.
- Encourage the student to practice summarizing chapters in their independent reading using the framework.

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Summary Shrink:** The student writes a 5-sentence summary of a familiar story, then rewrites it in 3 sentences, then in 1 sentence. They reflect on what had to be cut each time and why.
2. **Drama vs. Story Summary:** The student picks one passage from today's lesson (the story or the drama) and writes a summary. Then they explain: Was the story or the drama easier to summarize? Why?
3. **Summary Swap:** Two students each summarize a different story from their independent reading. They swap summaries and try to guess the theme based only on the other student's summary.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- **Most common mistake:** Students will retell every event instead of summarizing. The key correction is asking: "If you removed that sentence from your summary, would the summary still make sense? If yes, you do not need it."
- **Including theme:** Students may write a clean summary but forget the theme. Remind them: "A fifth-grade summary always ends with the theme. What is the author's message?"
- **Drama summarizing:** When summarizing drama, students sometimes quote dialogue extensively. Redirect: "You are not copying the script. Describe what happens and what the characters decide."
- **Pacing note:** If students finish the Independent Practice quickly, have them compare their summary to a partner's and discuss what each person included or excluded.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. **Summary:** Adaeze, an experienced skater, tries to teach her visiting cousin Kwame how to ice skate on the frozen pond behind her house. Kwame has never skated before and struggles badly, falling repeatedly and calling it impossible. Adaeze patiently guides him, holding his arm and encouraging him without laughing at his mistakes. By the end of the afternoon, Kwame can glide a short distance on his own and asks to come back the next day. The theme is that patience, encouragement, and trust help people overcome fears and try new things.
2. **Two details left out (any two acceptable):**
   - "She could glide backwards, spin in circles, and stop on a dime" — This shows Adaeze's skill but is not essential to the story's core problem and resolution.
   - "He looked at them like she had handed him a pair of snakes" — This is a vivid comparison but is a minor detail about Kwame's initial reaction.
   - "She showed him how to bend his knees and push off with the inside of his foot" — This is a specific teaching detail but is covered by the broader idea that she guided him patiently.
   - "His arms windmilled, and his ankles wobbled" — This describes his clumsy skating vividly but is not needed for the summary.
3. **Theme:** Patience, encouragement, and trust can help someone overcome their fear of something new. Adaeze's patient teaching and refusal to laugh at Kwame's mistakes made him feel safe enough to keep trying, and Kwame's willingness to trust Adaeze allowed him to improve. Their actions together show that learning happens best when one person is willing to teach gently and the other is willing to keep trying.
4. **Best quote for theme:** "It is not impossible. It is just new" — this captures the idea that what seems impossible is really just unfamiliar, and with patience and support, it can be overcome. (Also accept: "Trust me" or "Same time tomorrow?")

### Interleaved Review
5. (RL.4.3) Adaeze is patient — she skated "slowly beside Kwame" for an entire hour, holding his arm and "encouraging him every time he took a step." She is also kind and supportive — "When he fell — and he fell a lot — she helped him up without laughing," showing she cared about his feelings and did not want to embarrass him.
6. (RL.4.1) When Kwame said skating was impossible, Adaeze said, "It is not impossible. It is just new."
