# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 1, Lesson 9

## Topic
Multimedia and Literary Meaning

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify multimedia elements (audio, animation, video, interactive features) that can accompany literary texts
- Analyze how multimedia elements contribute to the meaning, tone, or beauty of a literary text
- Compare the experience of reading a text alone to experiencing it with multimedia elements

## Prerequisites
- Ability to analyze visual elements in illustrated texts (Lesson 8, RL.5.7)
- Understanding of tone, mood, and atmosphere in literature (Lessons 7-8)
- Ability to quote accurately from text and support claims with evidence (Lessons 1-2, RL.5.1)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard or chart paper and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Pencils
- Printed or projected reading passages (provided below)
- Optional: multimedia projector or screen for demonstrating audio/visual elements
- Optional: speakers for playing music or sound effects (if available)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Help students recognize how audio and multimedia elements shape their experience of stories.

**Activity: The Soundtrack Test**

Say: "Think about a movie you have watched. Now imagine watching the exact same movie, but with all the music and sound effects removed. No dramatic music during the scary parts. No cheerful tune when something good happens. No thunder sounds during the storm. Just silence and dialogue. Would the movie feel the same?"

Allow students to respond.

Say: "Most of you said no — and you are right. Music, sound effects, and other multimedia elements change how we experience a story. They add emotion, create atmosphere, and guide our feelings. The same thing happens in literature when stories are presented as multimedia — with audio, animation, video, or interactive features. In Lesson 8, we analyzed visual elements like illustrations and graphic novel panels. Today, we are expanding to include all kinds of multimedia."

Write on the board:
**Multimedia elements in literature:** music, sound effects, voice narration, animation, video, interactive features, background sounds

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach students to identify and analyze how multimedia elements enhance literary texts.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Multimedia:** A combination of different content forms — text, audio, images, video, animation — used together to tell a story or communicate information.
- **Sound design:** The use of music, sound effects, and ambient sounds to create mood and atmosphere.
- **Narration:** A spoken reading of a text, often with vocal expression that conveys emotion and tone.
- **Animation:** Moving images that bring illustrations to life, adding motion and time to static pictures.
- **Ambient sound:** Background sounds that create a sense of place (e.g., rain, birdsong, city traffic).

### Instruction Notes

Say: "In Lesson 8, we learned to analyze illustrations and graphic novel panels. Today, we are thinking about what happens when a literary text is presented with sound, movement, and other multimedia elements. Think about audiobooks, animated short films based on stories, interactive e-books, and multimedia poetry presentations."

Write on the board:
**Questions to Ask About Multimedia Elements:**
1. What multimedia elements are present? (music, narration, animation, sound effects, etc.)
2. What mood or feeling do they create?
3. How do they change the reader's or viewer's experience compared to reading the text alone?
4. What do they ADD to the meaning, tone, or beauty of the text?

Say: "Just like visual elements, multimedia elements are not just extra. When done well, they are part of the storytelling. Let me show you how to analyze them."

### Worked Examples

Say: "I am going to describe two versions of the same poem — one read silently from a page, and one presented as a multimedia performance. We will analyze what the multimedia adds."

**Poem: "Night Market"**

> The lanterns sway above the crowd,
> red and gold against the dark.
> Smoke rises from the grill,
> carrying the smell of spiced meat
> and roasted corn.
>
> A woman calls out prices
> in a voice like a song.
> Children dart between the stalls,
> laughing, grabbing, pointing
> at everything that glitters.
>
> My grandmother holds my hand.
> Her grip is warm and sure.
> She leans down and whispers,
> "This is where I grew up.
> Every sound you hear tonight
> is a sound I have carried with me
> my whole life."

Say: "If you read this poem on the page, you experience it through the words — the imagery of lanterns, smoke, the grandmother's whisper. Now imagine this same poem presented as a multimedia piece."

Write on the board:
**Multimedia Version of "Night Market":**
- **Narration:** A young person's voice reads the poem slowly, with pauses between stanzas. During the grandmother's whispered lines, the voice drops to a near-whisper.
- **Ambient sound:** Background audio plays crowd murmur, sizzling from a grill, distant laughter, and the clinking of metal utensils.
- **Music:** A soft, traditional melody plays on a stringed instrument, growing slightly louder during the grandmother's speech.
- **Visual animation:** Warm-toned illustrations of swaying lanterns, drifting smoke, and a crowded market slowly pan across the screen. When the grandmother speaks, the animation shifts to a close-up of two hands clasped together.

Say: "Let me analyze what the multimedia adds using our four questions."

Write on the board:
1. **Elements present:** Narration, ambient sound, music, animation.
2. **Mood created:** Warm, nostalgic, intimate. The crowd sounds make the market feel alive. The soft music creates tenderness. The whispered narration at the end creates closeness.
3. **How does it change the experience?** Reading the poem on the page, you imagine the sounds and sights. In the multimedia version, you actually hear the crowd, the sizzle, the music. The poem becomes immersive — you feel like you are standing in the market.
4. **What does multimedia ADD?** The ambient sounds make the grandmother's words more powerful — when she says "every sound you hear tonight is a sound I have carried with me my whole life," the reader has actually heard those sounds. The music adds an emotional layer, and the close-up of the clasped hands visually emphasizes the bond between grandmother and grandchild. The multimedia version adds sensory depth and emotional intensity that the text alone can only suggest.

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice analyzing the impact of multimedia elements on a literary text.

Say: "Now let us analyze a different scenario together. I am going to describe a folktale presented in two formats, and we will compare them."

**Folktale Summary: "The Tiger's Whisker" (Korean Folktale)**

> A woman named Yun-Ok needs a tiger's whisker to make a potion that will heal her husband's troubled mind. To get the whisker, she must earn the trust of a wild tiger living on the mountain. Every night for six months, she climbs the mountain and leaves food for the tiger, coming closer each time. Eventually, the tiger lets her approach. She carefully plucks one whisker. But when she brings it to the healer, the healer throws the whisker into the fire and says, "You do not need the potion. You already know the cure. The patience you showed the tiger is the same patience your husband needs."

**Format A: Text Only (in a printed book)**
The folktale is written in five paragraphs with no illustrations or audio.

**Format B: Animated Short Film**
> - **Opening:** Dark mountain at night, lit only by moonlight. Wind sounds. Yun-Ok is shown climbing, her lantern a tiny warm dot against the dark landscape.
> - **Tiger scenes:** The tiger is animated with glowing amber eyes in the darkness. Each visit, Yun-Ok is shown a little closer. The music shifts from tense (low drums) to calm (soft flute) as the tiger begins to trust her.
> - **Whisker scene:** Close-up of Yun-Ok's hand reaching slowly toward the tiger's face. The tiger's breath is visible in the cold air. Silence — no music, no sound effects — for five full seconds before she plucks the whisker.
> - **Final scene:** The healer throws the whisker into the fire. The flame flares bright orange. The music swells. The healer's words appear as text on screen, then slowly fade as Yun-Ok's face is shown — understanding dawning in her eyes.

Say: "What does the animated version add to the meaning of this story? Let us discuss."

Guide students to identify:
- **Music shifting from tense to calm:** shows the relationship between Yun-Ok and the tiger changing over time, which the text describes but the music lets us feel.
- **Five seconds of silence before plucking the whisker:** creates suspense and emphasizes the courage of the moment. In the written version, this moment is just one sentence.
- **The fire flaring when the healer burns the whisker:** visually dramatizes the shock — Yun-Ok (and the viewer) expected the whisker to be the solution. The bright flame makes the moment feel like a turning point.
- **Yun-Ok's face showing "understanding dawning":** the animated version shows her emotional reaction, which the text does not describe. The viewer sees the theme land on her face.

Say: "Write two sentences comparing the two formats. What does the animated version do that the text-only version cannot?"

Expected response: The animated version uses music, silence, and close-up animation to create emotional impact that the text can only describe. For example, the five seconds of silence before Yun-Ok plucks the whisker builds suspense that a written sentence cannot replicate, and seeing her face as she understands the healer's lesson allows the viewer to experience the theme directly rather than imagining it.

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently analyze how multimedia elements would contribute to a literary text.

**Passage: "The Kite Festival" (Folktale)**

> Long ago, in a village where the wind blew strong from the mountains, there lived a girl named Lien who made kites from bamboo and rice paper. She painted each kite with a different story — a dragon on one, a river on another, a field of stars on a third.
>
> Every spring, the village held a kite festival. Whoever flew the most beautiful kite won the honor of painting the village gate for the year. Lien's older brother, Duc, had won three years in a row. His kites were perfectly symmetrical, with bold colors and sharp lines.
>
> This year, Lien entered the festival for the first time. Her kite was different from her brother's. It was not perfectly symmetrical. The bamboo frame curved like a bird's wing, and the rice paper was thin enough to let sunlight through. She had painted a single golden fish on it, with scales so delicate they seemed to shimmer.
>
> When the wind caught Lien's kite, something magical happened. The sunlight passed through the thin rice paper, and the golden fish seemed to glow against the sky. The kite did not fly straight and rigid like Duc's. It danced — swooping and curving, as if the fish were swimming through the air.
>
> The judges looked up in silence. Then, one by one, the villagers began to point and smile.
>
> Duc walked over to his sister. "Your fish is flying," he said quietly. And then he smiled too.

**Directions:** Imagine this folktale is being adapted into a multimedia presentation. Answer the following questions in complete sentences.

1. Describe three multimedia elements you would add to this presentation (e.g., music, animation, sound effects, narration style). For each, explain what it would contribute to the meaning, tone, or beauty of the story.
2. Which moment in the story would benefit MOST from multimedia elements? Explain why, using a quote from the text.
3. Compare reading this story on the page to experiencing it as a multimedia presentation. What can the multimedia version do that the text cannot? What can the text do that multimedia cannot?
4. How would you use sound (music or silence) during the moment when "the judges looked up in silence"? Explain your choice.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Answer questions 1-4 as written.
**Stretch:** After answering questions 1-4, choose one passage from an earlier lesson in this unit and describe how it could be adapted as a multimedia presentation. Name two specific multimedia elements and explain what they would add.
**Challenge:** After answering all questions, write a paragraph arguing whether this particular folktale is better as a text-only story or as a multimedia presentation. Use specific evidence to support your position.

### Interleaved Review
5. (Grade 4 review — RL.4.2) What is the theme of this folktale? Write it as a complete sentence.
6. (Grade 4 review — RL.4.3) Compare Lien's kite to Duc's kite. Name one similarity and one difference.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Read the following:

> A poem about a thunderstorm is performed as a multimedia piece. The narration starts quiet and calm. As the poem describes the storm building, drums begin to play softly in the background, gradually getting louder. When the poem reaches the line "and then the sky broke open," a crash of thunder sound effect plays, and the screen flashes white for one second before returning to the image of rain.

1. Name one multimedia element used and explain what it adds to the poem.
2. Would the line "and then the sky broke open" feel the same without the thunder sound effect and white flash? Why or why not?

**Expected Answers:**
1. The drums gradually getting louder mirror the storm building in the poem, adding tension and anticipation that the words alone describe but the sound makes the audience feel physically. (Also accept: the thunder sound effect at "the sky broke open" creates a dramatic, startling moment that makes the poem's climax visceral; or the white flash visually represents the lightning, adding a sudden visual impact.)
2. No, the line would not feel the same. On the page, "and then the sky broke open" is a powerful image, but the reader experiences it calmly as text. With the thunder sound effect and white flash, the audience is startled — they experience the storm breaking open, not just reading about it. The multimedia elements make the audience feel the poem rather than just understand it.

**Mastery = Student identifies a multimedia element, explains what it adds to meaning or tone, and demonstrates understanding that multimedia changes the audience's experience.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the four multimedia analysis questions with a simple example (e.g., a scary movie scene with and without music).
- Have the student describe their favorite song and explain how the music makes them feel, then connect that idea to how music works in literary presentations.
- Provide a sentence frame: "The [multimedia element] adds ___ to the story by making the audience feel ___, which they would not experience with text alone."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 10, the Reading Literature Celebration.
- Encourage the student to think about multimedia elements the next time they watch a movie, listen to an audiobook, or use an interactive reading app.

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Multimedia Proposal:** The student selects a favorite poem or short story and writes a one-page "producer's proposal" describing how they would adapt it as a multimedia piece. They include specific decisions about narration, music, sound effects, and visual elements, with explanations for each choice.
2. **Audiobook Analysis:** The student listens to a chapter of an audiobook and writes a paragraph about how the narrator's voice (pace, tone, volume) affects the experience compared to reading silently.
3. **Sound Mapping:** The student reads a passage from this unit and creates a "sound map" — listing every moment where a sound effect, piece of music, or ambient noise could be added, and explaining what each would contribute.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- **Adapting without technology:** This lesson is designed to work even without a projector, speakers, or internet access. All multimedia elements are described in text so students can analyze them through reading. However, if you have access to a video clip of a poem performance or an animated folktale, showing even 2-3 minutes of actual multimedia significantly strengthens the lesson.
- **Most common mistake:** Students may focus only on whether multimedia elements are "cool" rather than analyzing what they add to meaning. Redirect: "It is exciting, but tell me specifically what it adds to the STORY. How does it help you understand the characters, the theme, or the mood?"
- **Connecting to Lesson 8:** Emphasize that visual analysis (Lesson 8) and multimedia analysis (this lesson) use the same thinking: What choices were made? What do they add? The difference is that multimedia includes sound, motion, and time — not just static images.
- **Pacing note:** The Guided Practice involves two formats to compare, which can be time-consuming. If needed, focus the comparison on two specific moments rather than the entire folktale.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. **Three multimedia elements (answers will vary; sample response):**
   - **Music:** A soft, traditional flute melody playing during the opening description of Lien painting her kites. This would create a calm, focused atmosphere and set the cultural context, contributing to the beauty of the story.
   - **Animation:** When the kite flies, animate the golden fish so that it "shimmers" and seems to swim through the sky. The text says the fish's "scales so delicate they seemed to shimmer" — animation could make this literally visible, adding wonder and magic to the moment.
   - **Sound effects:** The sound of wind — soft at first, then building as Lien's kite takes flight. When "the wind caught Lien's kite," the wind sound would swell, marking the turning point and making the audience feel the power of the moment.
2. **Best moment for multimedia:** The moment when Lien's kite flies and the fish appears to glow. The text says, "The sunlight passed through the thin rice paper, and the golden fish seemed to glow against the sky. The kite did not fly straight and rigid like Duc's. It danced — swooping and curving, as if the fish were swimming through the air." This moment depends on visual beauty and movement, which words describe beautifully but animation could bring to life — the audience could see the glow, the dance, the swimming fish.
3. **Text vs. multimedia comparison:** The multimedia version could show the fish glowing, let the audience hear the wind and the crowd's silence, and animate the kite's dance — making the experience sensory and immersive. However, the text version has its own strengths: phrases like "as if the fish were swimming through the air" invite the reader to imagine the scene in their own way, and the simplicity of Duc's response — "Your fish is flying" — is intimate and powerful on the page in a way that an actor's delivery might change.
4. **Sound during the judges' silence:** During this moment, I would use actual silence — remove all music and ambient sound for 3-4 seconds. The absence of sound after the music and wind effects would create a powerful contrast, making the audience feel the weight of the moment. The silence would mirror the judges' reaction and build suspense before the villagers begin to "point and smile." Silence can be the most powerful sound in multimedia because it forces the audience to pay attention.

### Interleaved Review
5. (RL.4.2) Theme: True beauty comes from uniqueness and creativity, not from perfection or doing things the expected way. (Accept variations about the value of originality, expressing your own vision, or beauty being found in imperfection.)
6. (RL.4.3) Similarity: Both Lien and Duc make kites for the festival and both care about their craft. Difference: Duc's kites are "perfectly symmetrical, with bold colors and sharp lines" — precise and controlled — while Lien's kite has a frame that "curved like a bird's wing" and rice paper thin enough to let sunlight through, making it delicate and unconventional.
