# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 3, Lesson 10

## Topic
Writing Celebration and Portfolio

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Select their strongest writing piece from the unit and articulate why it represents their best work
- Write a reflective statement describing their growth as a writer during the unit
- Present their writing to an audience and give specific, constructive feedback to peers

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-9: Completed drafts in at least two of the three writing modes (opinion, informative, narrative)
- Lesson 9: Experience with the revision and editing process and peer conferencing
- At least one revised and edited final draft ready for the portfolio

## Materials Needed
- Students' writing folders with all drafts from the unit (rough drafts and revised versions)
- Portfolio folders or covers (construction paper, stapled booklets, or binders)
- Lined writing paper for the reflection
- Sticky notes for audience feedback during presentations
- Timer or clock
- Celebration materials (optional): snacks, tablecloths, soft music, display boards
- Presentation sign-up sheet (described below)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Set a celebratory tone and help students reflect on their writing journey.

**Activity: Then and Now**

Say: "Before we celebrate, let's look back. Take out your writing folder and find the VERY FIRST thing you wrote in this unit — your opinion statement from Lesson 1 or your earliest draft."

Give students one minute to reread their first piece silently.

Say: "Now look at your most recent revised draft. I want you to turn to a partner and complete this sentence: 'At the beginning of this unit, my writing _______. Now, my writing _______.'"

Allow 2 minutes for partner sharing. Then ask 3-4 volunteers to share with the whole class.

Say: "Every single one of you has grown as a writer over these ten lessons. Today we celebrate that growth. You will choose your strongest piece, reflect on what you learned, and share your writing with an audience. This is YOUR day."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Guide students through portfolio assembly and teach them how to write a meaningful reflection.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Portfolio:** A curated collection of a writer's work that demonstrates their skills and growth
- **Reflection:** A piece of writing in which the author thinks about what they learned, how they grew, and what they want to improve
- **Author's chair:** A tradition where a writer reads their work aloud to an audience and receives feedback
- **Craft move:** A specific technique or decision a writer makes to strengthen their writing (e.g., using a transitional clause, adding sensory details, choosing precise vocabulary)

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1: Portfolio Assembly**

Say: "A writing portfolio is not just a folder of papers. It is a curated collection — that means you CHOOSE what goes in and you explain WHY."

Write on the board:

**Portfolio Contents:**
1. **Selected piece** — Your strongest, most polished writing from the unit (opinion, informative, or narrative)
2. **First draft** — The original draft of that same piece (so the reader can see your revision process)
3. **Reflection** — A written statement about your growth (we will write this together)
4. **Bonus piece (optional)** — A second piece from a different genre, if you completed more than one

Say: "You are going to choose ONE piece as your showcase piece. This is the one you will present during our celebration. Choose the piece you are most proud of — the one that shows your best writing."

**Part 2: Writing the Reflection**

Say: "The reflection is the most important part of the portfolio because it shows that you understand your own learning. Let me model one."

Write on chart paper:

> "When I started this unit, I thought opinion writing was just about saying what I believed. Now I understand that a strong opinion piece needs an arguable statement, logically ordered reasons, and specific evidence connected by an explanation. The craft move I am most proud of is my use of transitional clauses. In my first draft, every transition was a single word like 'also' or 'next.' After Lesson 7, I revised my opinion piece and replaced those with clauses like 'Because the research clearly demonstrates...' and 'Although some people might argue...' This change made my writing sound more sophisticated and easier to follow. One area I still want to improve is my conclusions — I tend to restate my introduction instead of offering a new insight. In future writing, I will practice ending with a call to action or a thought-provoking question."

Point out the structure:

**Reflection Formula:**
1. **Then:** What did you think/know about writing BEFORE the unit?
2. **Now:** What do you understand about writing NOW?
3. **Craft move:** What specific technique are you most proud of? Give an example from your writing.
4. **Next step:** What do you still want to improve?

### Worked Examples / Model Texts

Display a second model reflection to show students a different approach:

> "My favorite piece in this portfolio is my narrative about finding a time capsule under the playground. I chose this piece because it shows how much I have grown with dialogue and pacing. In my first draft, the scene where Aiden opens the locker was only two sentences long. After Lesson 6, I learned to EXPAND important moments with sensory details and dialogue. In my revised version, the same scene is a full paragraph with the sound of the locker clicking open, the smell of old paper, and a conversation between Aiden and the custodian. I also improved at using transitions — in my narrative, I used clause transitions like 'Before he could respond' and 'As he unfolded the paper' to keep the story flowing. Something I want to work on next is varying my sentence lengths. My teacher pointed out that many of my sentences start with a character name and a verb. I want to practice starting sentences in different ways to make my writing more interesting to read."

Ask: "What makes this reflection strong?" Guide students to notice:
- It names a specific piece and explains WHY it was chosen
- It gives a concrete BEFORE and AFTER example
- It names a specific craft move (expanding with sensory details and dialogue)
- It honestly identifies an area for growth

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students write their reflections and prepare for the celebration.

**Activity: Draft Your Reflection and Prepare to Present**

**Step 1 — Reflection Writing (7 minutes):**

Students write their reflection using the formula:
1. **Then:** What did you know about writing before this unit?
2. **Now:** What do you understand now?
3. **Craft move:** What specific technique are you most proud of? Include an example.
4. **Next step:** What do you want to improve?

Minimum length: 5-7 sentences.

Circulate and support students who struggle to identify their growth. Prompt with:
- "Look at your first draft and your revised draft. What is different?"
- "Which lesson changed the way you write? What did you learn?"
- "Read your partner's sticky note from Lesson 9. What did they compliment?"

**Step 2 — Presentation Preparation (5 minutes):**

Each student selects a 1-2 paragraph excerpt from their showcase piece to read aloud. Students should:
- Practice reading the excerpt once silently, once in a whisper
- Mark any words they want to emphasize or pauses they want to take
- Prepare a one-sentence introduction: "The piece I chose is my [genre] about [topic]. I am proud of it because [reason]."

---

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** The Writing Celebration — students present their work and give feedback.

**Activity: Author's Chair Celebration**

Set up the classroom in a circle or U-shape. Place a special chair at the front (the Author's Chair).

**Presentation Protocol:**
1. The author introduces their piece (one sentence).
2. The author reads their selected excerpt aloud.
3. Two audience members share specific compliments using the sentence frame: "I noticed that you _______, and it was effective because _______."
4. The author says "Thank you" and returns to their seat.

Each presentation should take approximately 2 minutes. In a class of 20-25 students, choose 5-6 presenters for the Author's Chair (rotate alphabetically or by volunteer). Students who do not present at the Author's Chair participate in a simultaneous **Gallery Walk** activity:

**Gallery Walk (for students not presenting at the Author's Chair):**
- Place showcase pieces on desks or display boards around the room
- Students walk around reading each other's work silently
- On a sticky note, each reader writes one specific compliment and places it on the piece
- Compliments must name a specific craft move (not just "good job"): "Your transitional clause 'Although the evidence seemed to point in one direction...' made your counterargument really smooth."

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Present a 1-2 paragraph excerpt and write one sticky note compliment for a peer.
**Stretch:** Present a longer excerpt (3+ paragraphs) and write three sticky note compliments for three different peers, each naming a different craft move.
**Challenge:** Present their excerpt, write three peer compliments, AND write a "Future Writing Goals" statement (3-4 sentences) describing two specific skills they want to develop in the next unit, with a plan for how they will practice.

### Interleaved Review
1. **Revision review (W.5.1-4):** In one sentence, explain the difference between revision and editing. (Expected: Revision improves the content, organization, and clarity of a piece, while editing corrects grammar, spelling, and punctuation errors.)
2. **Writing mode review (W.5.1-3):** Name the three writing modes we studied in this unit and the purpose of each. (Expected: Opinion writing persuades the reader; informative writing explains a topic; narrative writing tells a story about real or imagined experiences.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Complete the following sentence with a specific example from your own writing:

"The most important thing I learned about writing in this unit is _______, and I know this because in my writing I _______."

**Expected Response:**
A statement that names a specific skill and connects it to a concrete example from the student's own work. Example: "The most important thing I learned about writing in this unit is how to use transitional clauses to connect ideas, and I know this because in my opinion piece I changed 'Also, recess helps focus' to 'Because students need time to recharge their brains, recess directly improves afternoon focus.'"

**Mastery = The response names a specific writing skill and provides a concrete example from the student's own writing.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Conference with the student and walk through their writing folder together, pointing out specific improvements
- Offer a choice board: "Which skill did you practice? (a) transitions, (b) sensory details, (c) evidence and explanation, (d) audience awareness, (e) revision." Have the student select one and find an example in their writing.

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student has demonstrated metacognitive awareness and is well prepared for future writing units
- Encourage the student to set a specific writing goal for the next unit and record it in their writing journal

## Extension (Optional)
- **Letter to a future student:** Write a letter to a student who will take this writing unit next year. Give them three specific pieces of advice based on what you learned. Use examples from your own experience.
- **Genre comparison essay:** Write a short essay (2-3 paragraphs) comparing two of the three writing modes (opinion, informative, narrative). How are they similar? How are they different? Which one was most challenging for you and why?
- **Writing goals vision board:** Create a visual representation of your writing goals for the rest of the year. Include specific skills you want to develop, genres you want to try, and topics you want to explore.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The Writing Celebration should feel like an EVENT, not just another lesson. Small touches matter: arrange desks differently, play quiet background music, bring in snacks or juice, display student work on bulletin boards. Students who feel their writing is valued will be more motivated to write in the future.
- The reflection is the most cognitively demanding part of this lesson. Some students will struggle to articulate their growth. This is normal. Use prompting questions during conferences and allow students to dictate their reflections verbally if writing is a barrier.
- Sticky note compliments must be SPECIFIC. Before the Gallery Walk, review what specific feedback looks like: "I noticed you used the metaphor 'the memory sat in his stomach like a stone' and it really helped me feel Marcus's nervousness" vs. "Good story." Post examples of strong vs. weak compliments.
- If time is short, the Author's Chair and Gallery Walk can be split across two class periods. The reflection writing and portfolio assembly can happen in one session, and the celebration in the next.
- Collect portfolios at the end of the celebration. These are valuable assessment artifacts that show student growth over the entire unit. Consider sending them home with a parent letter explaining what the portfolio represents.
- Some students may feel uncomfortable presenting. Offer alternatives: they can read to a partner instead of the whole class, or they can have a friend read their piece for them. The goal is celebration, not anxiety.

## Rubric / Answer Key

| Criteria | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Proficient (3) | Advanced (4) |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|---------------|
| Portfolio Selection | No piece is selected, or the student cannot explain why they chose the piece | A piece is selected but the explanation is vague ("I liked it" or "It was easy") | A piece is selected with a clear, specific explanation of why it represents the student's best work | The selection is thoughtful and insightful; the student articulates how the piece demonstrates multiple skills and shows growth |
| Reflection Quality | No reflection is written, or the reflection is one vague sentence | A reflection is written but is general; it does not name specific skills or provide examples | The reflection follows the formula (Then/Now/Craft Move/Next Step) with specific examples from the student's writing | The reflection demonstrates deep self-awareness; the student analyzes their growth with precision, provides compelling before-and-after examples, and sets thoughtful goals |
| Presentation | Student does not present or reads without preparation | Student reads their excerpt but lacks confidence; no introduction is given | Student introduces their piece, reads clearly, and engages the audience | Student presents with confidence, expression, and poise; the introduction contextualizes the piece and the reading brings the writing to life |
| Peer Feedback | No peer feedback is given, or feedback is vague ("Good job," "I liked it") | Peer feedback is given but is general; it does not name a specific craft move | Peer feedback names a specific craft move and explains why it was effective | Peer feedback is insightful and detailed; it names specific craft moves, explains their impact, and may suggest ways to build on the strengths |
