# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 2

## Topic
Correlative Conjunctions: Both/And and Not Only/But Also

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Use both/and correctly to connect two equal elements in a sentence
- Use not only/but also correctly to add emphasis when connecting two elements
- Distinguish between all four correlative conjunction pairs studied so far (either/or, neither/nor, both/and, not only/but also)

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of either/or and neither/nor from Lesson 1
- Knowledge of coordinating conjunctions from Grade 4
- Ability to identify parallel sentence elements (subjects, verbs, phrases)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Correlative conjunction reference chart (add both/and and not only/but also to Lesson 1 chart)
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Index cards with all four conjunction pairs
- Colored pencils for marking conjunction pairs

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review Lesson 1 correlative conjunctions and activate prior knowledge.

**Activity: Pair Match Relay**

Write the following on the board with the second conjunction missing:

1. Either the hawk _______ the eagle soared over the canyon.
2. Neither the rain _______ the wind stopped the soccer game.
3. _______ you clean your room _______ you lose your screen time.

Say: "Fill in the missing conjunction for each sentence. Remember — correlative conjunctions come in fixed pairs!"

Give students 2 minutes. Review answers together: (1) or, (2) nor, (3) Either/or.

Say: "Excellent! Today we are adding two more pairs to our collection: *both/and* and *not only/but also*."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce both/and and not only/but also with explicit instruction, rules, and examples.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Both/and:** A conjunction pair used to show that two things are equally true or included
- **Not only/but also:** A conjunction pair used to emphasize that two things are true, with extra emphasis on the second item
- **Parallel structure:** When the elements joined by correlative conjunctions have the same grammatical form

### Instruction Notes

Say: "We already know either/or and neither/nor. Today we add two more pairs."

Write on the board:
- **both . . . and** (two things are equally true)
- **not only . . . but also** (two things are true, with emphasis)

Say: "Let us start with *both/and*. We use this pair when two things are equally true or equally included."

Write: **Both the guitar and the drums are fun to play.**

Say: "Notice I am saying the guitar IS fun AND the drums ARE fun. Both things are true. The word *both* comes before the first item, and *and* comes before the second."

Say: "Now let us look at *not only/but also*. This pair is special because it adds emphasis. It says: 'This first thing is true — and you might be surprised, but this second thing is ALSO true!'"

Write: **She is not only a talented singer but also an amazing dancer.**

Say: "I am emphasizing that being a singer is impressive, and being a dancer makes it even more impressive. The *not only* comes before the first element, and *but also* comes before the second."

**Rule — Parallel Structure:** Write on the board: "The elements after each conjunction must match in form."

- Correct: "Both **running** and **swimming** are good exercise." (gerund + gerund)
- Incorrect: "Both **running** and **to swim** are good exercise." (gerund + infinitive — mismatch!)

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (both/and — subjects):**
Write: **Both the librarian and the teacher recommended that book.**

Say: "Two people did the same action. I connected two subjects: *the librarian* and *the teacher*. They match — both are noun phrases."

**Example 2 (both/and — adjectives):**
Write: **The experiment was both fascinating and surprising.**

Say: "Here I connected two adjectives describing the experiment. *Fascinating* and *surprising* are both adjectives, so the structure is parallel."

**Example 3 (not only/but also — verb phrases):**
Write: **The dog not only fetched the ball but also brought back the stick.**

Say: "I am emphasizing two actions the dog did. *Fetched the ball* and *brought back the stick* are both verb phrases — they match."

**Example 4 (not only/but also — nouns):**
Write: **The science fair featured not only robots but also volcanoes.**

Say: "Two things at the science fair. I put *not only* before *robots* and *but also* before *volcanoes*. Both are plural nouns — parallel structure!"

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice using both/and and not only/but also with teacher support before working independently.

**Exercise A: Fill in the blanks.**

1. _______ the cake _______ the cookies were delicious at the bake sale.
2. The roller coaster was _______ exciting _______ a little scary.
3. He _______ finished his homework _______ cleaned his room before dinner.
4. The museum has _______ dinosaur fossils _______ space exhibits.

Work through item 1 together. Then have students try items 2-4 in pairs before reviewing.

**Answers:** (1) Both/and, (2) not only/but also (or both/and), (3) not only/but also, (4) both/and (or not only/but also).

**Exercise B: Choose the correct correlative conjunction pair.**

Say: "For each sentence, decide which pair fits best: either/or, neither/nor, both/and, or not only/but also."

5. _______ lions _______ tigers live in the wild in Africa. (both/and)
6. _______ the principal _______ the vice principal was available for a meeting. (neither/nor)
7. You may bring _______ a pencil _______ a pen to the test. (either/or)
8. The relay team _______ won the race _______ set a new school record! (not only/but also)

Discuss as a class why each pair was chosen.

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students demonstrate mastery of both/and and not only/but also while distinguishing among all four pairs studied.

**Directions:** Complete each item as directed.

1. Write a sentence using *both/and* about two of your favorite hobbies.
2. Write a sentence using *not only/but also* about something impressive a friend has done.
3. Fill in the blanks: The zoo has _______ penguins _______ polar bears in the Arctic exhibit.
4. Fill in the blanks: The magician _______ pulled a rabbit from the hat _______ made a dove appear.
5. Correct the error: "She plays both soccer or basketball." Write the correct sentence.
6. Correct the error: "He not only sings and also plays the piano." Write the correct sentence.
7. Choose the best pair and complete: "_______ the rain _______ the cold could keep them from the championship game." (neither/nor)
8. Choose the best pair and complete: "_______ we leave at noon _______ we leave at one, we will arrive on time." (either/or or whether/or)
9. Combine using both/and: "Elephants are strong. Elephants are intelligent."
10. Combine using not only/but also: "The new park has a playground. The new park has a splash pad."

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-8.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-10.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-10, plus write a 4-sentence paragraph about your favorite school subject using at least three different correlative conjunction pairs.

### Interleaved Review
11. **L.4.1a (relative pronouns):** Choose the correct relative pronoun: "The student _______ (who / which) won the spelling bee practiced every day."
12. **L.4.3a (precise words):** Replace the underlined word with a more precise one: "The dog went across the yard." (e.g., sprinted, dashed, trotted)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Write a sentence using *both/and* about two things you learned in school this week.
2. Write a sentence using *not only/but also* about a person you admire.

**Expected Answers:**
1. Answers vary. Example: "This week I learned about both fractions and correlative conjunctions."
2. Answers vary. Example: "My mom is not only a great cook but also a wonderful storyteller."

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 items correct, with properly paired conjunctions and parallel structure in the connected elements.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the four correlative conjunction pairs using a reference chart
- Practice sorting sentences by conjunction type with index card activities
- Focus on the matching/parallel structure requirement using simple noun + noun examples first
- Provide sentence frames: "Both _______ and _______ are _______."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 3 on verb tense consistency
- Encourage the student to use correlative conjunctions in their next writing assignment

## Extension (Optional)
- **All Four Pairs Story:** Write a short story (6-8 sentences) that uses all four correlative conjunction pairs at least once. Underline each pair.
- **Conjunction Comparison:** Explain in writing: What is the difference between "Both the dog and the cat are friendly" and "Not only the dog but also the cat is friendly"? When would you use each?
- **Real-World Hunt:** Find examples of correlative conjunctions in a newspaper article, advertisement, or website. Record the sentences and identify which pair is used.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Students often confuse *both/and* with *not only/but also*. Emphasize that *both/and* treats items equally, while *not only/but also* creates a sense of surprise or added emphasis on the second item.
- Parallel structure is the most common error. Students may write: "She both likes reading and to play soccer." Correct form: "She likes both reading and playing soccer." The conjunction pair should be placed so that the elements after each word are grammatically parallel.
- The phrase "not only/but also" can feel awkward for some students. Let them practice saying sentences aloud before writing them.
- Some students may encounter "whether/or" in their reading. Briefly acknowledge it: "Whether you study or you guess, you should always try your best." It will appear in later grades in more depth.
