# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 8

## Topic
Expanding Sentences for Detail and Interest

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Expand a simple sentence by adding descriptive details (where, when, how, why)
- Use adjectives, adverbs, prepositional phrases, and appositives to add meaning and interest
- Evaluate whether added details improve or clutter a sentence

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of adjectives and adverbs from Grade 3-4
- Knowledge of prepositional phrases (in, on, under, beside, through, etc.)
- Ability to write complete, grammatically correct sentences (L.4.3a)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- "Bare Bones" sentence strips (simple sentences printed on strips of paper)
- Colored pencils (different colors for each type of expansion)
- Mentor sentence examples from published texts (optional)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show students the difference between a bare sentence and an expanded one.

**Activity: Bare Bones vs. Full Picture**

Write two sentences on the board:

**Sentence A:** "The dog ran."

**Sentence B:** "The muddy golden retriever ran joyfully across the freshly mowed lawn, chasing a bright red frisbee."

Say: "Both sentences are about a dog running. Which one paints a picture in your mind? Which one would you rather read?"

Allow students to respond. Then say: "Sentence B gives us so much more — we can see the dog, feel the energy, and picture the scene. Today we are going to learn how to take a simple sentence and expand it into something vivid and interesting, like a writer who paints with words."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach specific strategies for expanding sentences while maintaining clarity.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Sentence expansion:** Adding words, phrases, or clauses to a simple sentence to provide more detail, meaning, or interest
- **Prepositional phrase:** A group of words that begins with a preposition and adds information about where, when, or how (e.g., "under the bridge," "during the storm")
- **Appositive:** A noun or noun phrase that renames or explains another noun (e.g., "Ms. Rivera, our science teacher, designed the experiment")

### Instruction Notes

Say: "There are four main questions we can ask to expand a sentence. I call them the Expansion Questions."

Write on the board:
1. **WHERE?** (Add a place or location)
2. **WHEN?** (Add a time)
3. **HOW?** (Add a description of the manner)
4. **WHY?** (Add a reason or cause)

Say: "Let us start with a bare sentence and expand it step by step."

Write: **"The bird sang."**

Say: "Let me ask the expansion questions one at a time."

- WHERE? → "The bird sang **in the tall oak tree**."
- WHEN? → "**Every morning**, the bird sang in the tall oak tree."
- HOW? → "Every morning, the bird sang **sweetly** in the tall oak tree."
- WHY? → "Every morning, the bird sang sweetly in the tall oak tree, **greeting the neighborhood with its cheerful melody**."

Say: "Look how much richer that sentence is now! But notice — I did not just dump random words in. Every detail I added serves a purpose. It helps the reader see, hear, or understand more."

**Tools for Expansion:**

Write on the board:
- **Adjectives** (describe nouns): the *tall* oak tree, the *cheerful* melody
- **Adverbs** (describe verbs): sang *sweetly*
- **Prepositional phrases** (tell where/when): *in the tall oak tree*, *every morning*
- **Appositives** (rename a noun): "Ms. Rivera, *our science teacher*, explained the experiment."
- **Participial phrases** (add action detail): "The bird, *perched on the highest branch*, sang sweetly."

**Warning:** Say: "More is not always better. A sentence can become too long and confusing. Good writers add details that matter and cut details that do not. Listen to this."

Write: "The very small tiny little brown bird, which was really quite a cute bird, sang a song that was nice and pretty in a tree that was tall and had green leaves on a morning that was sunny and warm."

Say: "This is overloaded! The details repeat and do not add meaning. Compare it to our earlier version. Which is better? Quality over quantity."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Adding where and when:**
Base: "The children played."
Expanded: "**After school**, the children played **in the sprinklers on the front lawn**."

Say: "I added when (*after school*) and where (*in the sprinklers on the front lawn*). Now we can picture the scene."

**Example 2 — Adding an appositive:**
Base: "Dr. Patel explained the experiment."
Expanded: "Dr. Patel, **a marine biologist from the university**, explained the experiment."

Say: "The appositive tells us who Dr. Patel is. It adds meaning without making the sentence awkward."

**Example 3 — Adding how and why:**
Base: "The team celebrated."
Expanded: "The team celebrated **wildly**, **hugging and high-fiving after their first championship victory**."

Say: "I added how they celebrated (*wildly, hugging and high-fiving*) and why (*after their first championship victory*). The reader feels the excitement."

**Example 4 — Balanced expansion:**
Base: "Rain fell."
Expanded: "**Throughout the long afternoon**, heavy rain fell **steadily on the tin roof of the old barn**, **creating a soothing rhythm that lulled the kittens to sleep**."

Say: "Notice the balance — when, what kind of rain, where, and what happened as a result. Each detail adds something new."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice expanding sentences with teacher guidance and peer feedback.

**Exercise A: Expand these sentences by adding at least two details.**

1. "The cat slept." (Add where and how)
2. "Students read." (Add when and where)
3. "The storm arrived." (Add when and a descriptive detail)
4. "The chef cooked." (Add what, where, and how)

Model item 1: "The fluffy cat slept peacefully on the warm windowsill." (Added how: peacefully, where: on the warm windowsill.)

Have students expand items 2-4, then share with a partner. Select 2-3 strong examples to share with the class.

**Exercise B: Is this expansion effective? Why or why not?**

5. Base: "The boy smiled." Expanded: "The boy smiled happily with a happy smile on his happy face."
   (Not effective — "happy" is repeated three times; the expansion does not add new information.)

6. Base: "The river flowed." Expanded: "The crystal-clear river flowed gently through the shadowy canyon, carrying autumn leaves on its surface."
   (Effective — each detail adds a new sensory element.)

Discuss as a class what makes an expansion effective vs. cluttered.

---

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently expand sentences and evaluate their own expansions.

**Directions:** Expand each base sentence by adding at least two meaningful details. Then complete the remaining items.

1. Base: "The dog barked."
2. Base: "Lightning flashed."
3. Base: "The girl laughed."
4. Base: "Fish swam."
5. Base: "The bell rang."
6. Expand this sentence by adding an appositive: "Coach Williams organized the tournament."
7. Expand this sentence by adding a prepositional phrase and an adverb: "The squirrel climbed."
8. Take this overloaded sentence and revise it so it is expanded but not cluttered: "The very big huge enormous elephant that was gray and wrinkly walked slowly and heavily and loudly through the very tall green grass on a hot sunny warm day."

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-7.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-8.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-8, plus find a paragraph in a book you are reading. Copy one well-expanded sentence and explain what details the author added and why they are effective.

### Interleaved Review
9. **L.4.1d (ordering adjectives):** Expand this sentence by adding three adjectives in the correct order: "She found a _______ _______ _______ stone on the beach."
10. **L.4.1b (progressive tenses):** Expand this sentence and use the present progressive tense: "The students work." → Write an expanded version in present progressive.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Expand this sentence by adding at least three meaningful details: "The bird flew."

**Expected Answers:**
Answers vary. Example: "Early in the morning, the bright blue jay flew gracefully over the rooftops, searching for its breakfast."

**Mastery = the expanded sentence includes at least 3 details (where, when, how, why, descriptive adjectives/adverbs), is grammatically correct, and the details add genuine meaning rather than being repetitive or cluttered.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Revisit the four Expansion Questions (where, when, how, why) and use them as a checklist
- Provide sentence frames: "[When], the [adjective] [noun] [verb + adverb] [where], [why/result]."
- Practice expanding with one detail at a time before combining multiple details
- Use visual prompts (pictures) to inspire descriptive additions

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 9 on combining and reducing sentences
- Challenge the student to apply sentence expansion during their next writing workshop draft

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Before and After Gallery:** Write five "bare bones" sentences on index cards. On the back, write an expanded version. Display them as a classroom gallery. Classmates can try expanding the front before flipping to see the back.
- **Mentor Sentence Study:** Choose three sentences from a published author you admire. Identify what details the author added to make the sentence vivid. Write your own sentence modeled after the author's style.
- **Expansion Relay:** In groups of four, start with a bare sentence. Each person adds one detail, passing the sentence around the group. Read the final expanded sentence aloud. Does it work? Could anything be cut?

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Emphasize that sentence expansion is a revision tool. Students do not need to write expanded sentences on the first draft — they can go back and add details during revision.
- The biggest pitfall is over-expansion. Teach students to read their expanded sentences aloud. If they run out of breath or lose track of the meaning, the sentence is probably too long and should be split or trimmed.
- Appositives are a powerful expansion tool but may be new to some students. Keep appositive practice simple: "Name, description, continued sentence." Example: "Mr. Torres, the music teacher, plays the guitar."
- Connect this lesson to writing: strong descriptive writing is the result of thoughtful sentence expansion. Narrative writing, in particular, benefits from sensory details added through expansion.
- Some students confuse expanding with "making longer." Redirect by asking: "Does your added detail help the reader see, hear, feel, or understand something new? If not, cut it."
