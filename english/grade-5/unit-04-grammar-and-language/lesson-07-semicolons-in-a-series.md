# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 7

## Topic
Semicolons in a Series with Internal Commas

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain why semicolons are needed when items in a series already contain commas
- Use semicolons correctly to separate items in a complex series
- Distinguish between simple series (commas only) and complex series (semicolons needed)

## Prerequisites
- Mastery of commas in a series from Lesson 6
- Understanding of what a semicolon looks like and its basic purpose
- Ability to identify items in a list within a sentence

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Printed or projected example sentences
- Two colors of highlighters (one for commas, one for semicolons)
- Index cards with sample series items for sorting activity

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Demonstrate why commas alone can create confusion in certain series.

**Activity: The Confusing List**

Write on the board WITHOUT semicolons:

"On our road trip, we stopped in Austin, Texas, Denver, Colorado, and Portland, Oregon."

Say: "Read this sentence. How many places did we visit? Is it three places? Six places? It is hard to tell!"

Give students 1 minute to discuss with a partner. Then say: "We visited three cities. But because each city name already has a comma (between the city and the state), using commas to separate the items makes the sentence confusing. Today we are going to learn a punctuation mark that solves this problem: the semicolon."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the rule for using semicolons in a series when items contain internal commas.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Semicolon (;):** A punctuation mark that is stronger than a comma but weaker than a period; used to separate items in a complex series
- **Internal commas:** Commas that appear within the items of a series, not between items
- **Complex series:** A series where one or more items already contain commas, requiring semicolons to separate the items clearly

### Instruction Notes

Say: "You already know that a semicolon looks like a period sitting on top of a comma. In a series, we use semicolons instead of commas when the items themselves already have commas inside them."

**The Rule:** Write on the board: "When items in a series contain internal commas, use semicolons to separate the items."

Return to the warm-up example and fix it:

**Incorrect:** "On our road trip, we stopped in Austin, Texas, Denver, Colorado, and Portland, Oregon."

**Correct:** "On our road trip, we stopped in Austin, Texas; Denver, Colorado; and Portland, Oregon."

Say: "Now it is perfectly clear: three cities. The semicolons separate the three big items, and the commas stay inside each item to separate city from state."

Say: "Think of it this way: Commas are the small dividers within each item. Semicolons are the big dividers between items."

Write on the board:
```
Item 1: Austin, Texas
   ;
Item 2: Denver, Colorado
   ;
Item 3: and Portland, Oregon
```

Say: "Notice we still use *and* before the last item, just like in a regular series. The semicolons simply replace the commas that would normally go between items."

**When do you NOT need semicolons?**

Say: "If your list items do not have internal commas, just use regular commas."

Write: "We visited Texas, Colorado, and Oregon." (Simple series — commas are fine.)
Write: "We visited Austin, Texas; Denver, Colorado; and Portland, Oregon." (Complex series — semicolons needed.)

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Cities and states:**
Write: "My cousins live in Miami, Florida; Chicago, Illinois; and Seattle, Washington."

Say: "Three items, each with a city-state comma inside. Semicolons keep the items clear."

**Example 2 — Dates:**
Write: "The tests are scheduled for Monday, October 5; Wednesday, October 7; and Friday, October 9."

Say: "Each date has an internal comma between the day and the date number. Semicolons separate the three dates."

**Example 3 — Items with descriptions:**
Write: "For the party, we need balloons, which should be blue; streamers, which should be silver; and a cake, which should be chocolate."

Say: "Each item has a description set off by a comma. The semicolons show where one item ends and the next begins."

**Example 4 — People with titles:**
Write: "The judges were Ms. Park, the art teacher; Mr. Davis, the principal; and Coach Ramirez, the PE teacher."

Say: "Each person has a title after a comma. Without semicolons, you could not tell where one judge's entry ends and the next begins."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice using semicolons in complex series with teacher support.

**Exercise A: Add semicolons and commas correctly.**

1. The relay team includes Zoe from Team A Marcus from Team B and Lily from Team C.
2. The museum trip stops are the Hall of Dinosaurs on the first floor the Space Exhibit on the second floor and the Ocean Gallery in the basement.
3. Our family reunion was attended by Aunt Rosa from Dallas Texas Uncle Jim from Portland Oregon and Grandpa Lou from Boise Idaho.

Work through item 1 together:
"The relay team includes Zoe, from Team A; Marcus, from Team B; and Lily, from Team C."

Have students work on items 2-3 in pairs, then review.

**Answers:**
1. Zoe, from Team A; Marcus, from Team B; and Lily, from Team C.
2. the Hall of Dinosaurs, on the first floor; the Space Exhibit, on the second floor; and the Ocean Gallery, in the basement.
3. Aunt Rosa, from Dallas, Texas; Uncle Jim, from Portland, Oregon; and Grandpa Lou, from Boise, Idaho.

**Exercise B: Decide — commas or semicolons?**

4. "We packed sandwiches fruit and water for the hike." (Commas — no internal commas)
5. "The winners were Aiden who placed first Sofia who placed second and Noah who placed third." (Semicolons — internal commas after names)
6. "Her favorite colors are blue green and purple." (Commas — no internal commas)

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently apply semicolon and comma rules in series.

**Directions:** Complete each item as directed.

1. Add correct punctuation (commas and/or semicolons): "We have lived in Tampa Florida Nashville Tennessee and Reno Nevada."
2. Add correct punctuation: "The snack options are apples with peanut butter crackers with cheese and celery with hummus."
3. Add correct punctuation: "My three best friends are Jada who loves soccer Ethan who loves drawing and Mia who loves reading."
4. Add correct punctuation: "The concert dates are Saturday March 12 Saturday March 19 and Saturday March 26."
5. Write a sentence listing three cities and their states, using semicolons correctly.
6. Write a sentence listing three people and their roles (e.g., teacher, coach), using semicolons correctly.
7. Decide: Does this sentence need semicolons or just commas? Add the correct punctuation. "The backpack contained a notebook a water bottle and a calculator."
8. Decide: Does this sentence need semicolons or just commas? Add the correct punctuation. "For dinner we had salad with ranch dressing pasta with marinara sauce and bread with garlic butter."

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-6.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-8.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-8, plus write a 3-sentence paragraph about a vacation that includes at least one simple series (commas) and one complex series (semicolons).

### Interleaved Review
9. **L.4.1a (relative adverbs):** Complete with the correct relative adverb: "The park _______ (where / when / why) we play soccer has a brand-new field."
10. **L.4.3a (precise words):** Replace the vague word: "The food at the restaurant was good." Use a more specific adjective (e.g., delicious, flavorful, savory).

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Add correct punctuation: "The field day teams are the Lions from Room 201 the Eagles from Room 203 and the Bears from Room 205."
2. Does this sentence need semicolons or just commas? "We bought pencils erasers and notebooks at the store."

**Expected Answers:**
1. "The field day teams are the Lions, from Room 201; the Eagles, from Room 203; and the Bears, from Room 205."
2. Just commas: "We bought pencils, erasers, and notebooks at the store." (No internal commas, so semicolons are not needed.)

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 items correct, with appropriate use of semicolons vs. commas.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the core principle: "Semicolons are needed when items already have commas inside them."
- Use a visual sorting activity: give students sentence cards and have them sort into "commas only" and "semicolons needed" piles
- Practice with city/state pairs first, since that pattern is the most intuitive
- Draw bracket diagrams showing each "big item" and the "small commas" inside

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 8 on expanding sentences
- Encourage the student to notice semicolons in published writing (textbooks, articles)

## Extension (Optional)
- **Travel Brochure:** Create a travel brochure that lists five destinations, each with a city and state (or city and country). Write a sentence using semicolons to list all five destinations.
- **Class Directory:** Write a sentence listing five classmates and one fact about each, using semicolons to separate entries. Example: "In our group, there is Kai, who plays piano; Nora, who writes stories;..."
- **Punctuation Explanation:** Write a brief paragraph explaining the difference between commas in a series and semicolons in a series, as if you were teaching a younger student.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Semicolons in a series can feel advanced for fifth graders, but the concept is logical once students grasp the "commas inside the items" principle. Use lots of visual examples.
- The most common error is using semicolons in a simple series that does not need them. Reinforce: "Check first — do any of your items already have commas? If not, just use commas."
- City/state pairs and dates are the most natural and frequent contexts where this rule applies. Focus on these patterns before moving to more complex examples (items with descriptions, appositives).
- Some students may try to use semicolons to join independent clauses (another valid use of semicolons). Acknowledge this use briefly: "Yes, semicolons can also join two complete sentences, but today we are focusing on their use in a series."
- If you have students who are still struggling with basic comma use in a series (Lesson 6), address that first before adding the semicolon layer.
