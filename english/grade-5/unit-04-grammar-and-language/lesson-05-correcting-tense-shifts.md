# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 5

## Topic
Correcting Inappropriate Tense Shifts

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify inappropriate verb tense shifts in sentences and paragraphs
- Correct tense shifts by choosing the appropriate tense for the context
- Edit a passage for tense consistency while preserving appropriate shifts

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of verb tense consistency (Lesson 3)
- Ability to use tense for sequence and condition (Lesson 4)
- Familiarity with past, present, future, and progressive tenses

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Colored pencils or highlighters (for underlining verbs)
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Printed editing passages (or projected versions)
- Verb tense reference chart from Lessons 3-4

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Sharpen students' ability to spot tense errors quickly.

**Activity: Tense Error Speed Round**

Say: "I am going to show you five sentences one at a time. Give me a thumbs up if the tense is consistent, or a thumbs down if there is an error. If you spot an error, call out the correction."

Display one at a time:

1. "The cat slept on the couch and purrs softly." (Thumbs down — purrs → purred)
2. "We will pack our bags and drive to the campsite." (Thumbs up)
3. "She opens her lunchbox and found a surprise note." (Thumbs down — found → finds, or opens → opened)
4. "The birds were singing while the sun was rising." (Thumbs up)
5. "I studied all night and will pass the test yesterday." (Thumbs down — will pass → passed)

Say: "Excellent detective work! Today, we are going to practice editing longer passages — finding and fixing tense errors like a real editor."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach a systematic strategy for identifying and correcting tense shifts in longer passages.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Editing for tense:** The process of reading a passage specifically to check that verb tenses are consistent and appropriate
- **Base tense:** The main tense used throughout a piece of writing
- **Intentional shift:** A tense change that is necessary because the time frame genuinely changes

### Instruction Notes

Say: "When you edit a paragraph or a whole essay for tense, follow these three steps."

Write the **Tense Editing Strategy** on the board:

**Step 1: Identify the base tense.** Read the first sentence or two. What tense is the author using? This is the base tense for the passage.

**Step 2: Underline every verb.** Go through the passage and underline or circle each verb.

**Step 3: Check each verb.** Ask: Does this verb match the base tense? If not, did the time genuinely change? If the time changed, the shift is fine. If it did not, correct the verb.

Say: "Let me model this with a paragraph."

Write on the board:

*"Last Saturday, our family went to the farmers' market. Mom picks out fresh tomatoes and peppers. Dad bought a loaf of bread from the bakery stand. We ate samples of cheese and then walks to the car with our bags full."*

Say: "Step 1: What is the base tense? The first sentence says *Last Saturday* and uses *went* — past tense. That is our base tense."

Say: "Step 2: Let me underline all the verbs: *went, picks, bought, ate, walks*."

Say: "Step 3: Check each one. *Went* — past tense, matches. *Picks* — present tense. Did the time change? No, we are still at the market last Saturday. This is an error. Change to *picked*. *Bought* — past tense, matches. *Ate* — past tense, matches. *Walks* — present tense. Same time frame. Error. Change to *walked*."

Write the corrected version:

*"Last Saturday, our family went to the farmers' market. Mom **picked** out fresh tomatoes and peppers. Dad bought a loaf of bread from the bakery stand. We ate samples of cheese and then **walked** to the car with our bags full."*

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Short passage with one error:**
Write: "Every morning, the crossing guard stands at the corner. She waved to the students and helps them cross safely."

Say: "Base tense: present (*stands*, *helps*). The error: *waved* is past tense. It should be *waves*."

Correct: "Every morning, the crossing guard stands at the corner. She **waves** to the students and helps them cross safely."

**Example 2 — Passage with an appropriate shift:**
Write: "The ancient Egyptians built the pyramids thousands of years ago. Today, tourists from around the world visit them."

Say: "Base tense starts as past (*built*), then shifts to present (*visit*). Is this appropriate? Yes — *thousands of years ago* is past, *today* is present. The time genuinely changed. No correction needed."

**Example 3 — Passage with multiple errors:**
Write: "The explorers packed their supplies and set off into the jungle. They hike for hours through thick vines. A colorful parrot flies overhead. By nightfall, they had reached the river and set up camp."

Say: "Base tense: past (*packed, set off*). Errors: *hike* (should be *hiked*), *flies* (should be *flew*). *Had reached* is past perfect — showing an event completed by nightfall. That is fine."

Correct: "The explorers packed their supplies and set off into the jungle. They **hiked** for hours through thick vines. A colorful parrot **flew** overhead. By nightfall, they had reached the river and set up camp."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice the three-step tense editing strategy with teacher support.

**Exercise A: Edit the paragraph.**

Say: "Use the three-step strategy. First, identify the base tense. Then underline all verbs. Then check each one."

*"Our class science experiment was amazing. First, we pour vinegar into a bottle. Then we added baking soda and watch the volcano erupt. Foam spills everywhere, and everyone cheered. Ms. Rivera smiled and tells us to clean up."*

Have students work in pairs for 3 minutes, then review as a class.

**Corrected version:**
*"Our class science experiment was amazing. First, we **poured** vinegar into a bottle. Then we added baking soda and **watched** the volcano erupt. Foam **spilled** everywhere, and everyone cheered. Ms. Rivera smiled and **told** us to clean up."*

Base tense: past. Errors: pour → poured, watch → watched, spills → spilled, tells → told.

**Exercise B: Decide — correct or leave?**

1. "Last year I played trumpet, but now I play saxophone." (Leave — appropriate shift)
2. "The runners stretched before the race and then runs five laps." (Correct: runs → ran)
3. "If it snows tomorrow, the school will close early." (Leave — condition sentence)
4. "The pioneers traveled west. They build cabins and plant crops." (Correct: build → built, plant → planted)

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently apply the tense editing strategy to sentences and passages.

**Part 1: Find and correct the tense error in each sentence.**

1. "The magician pulled a rabbit from his hat and waves it at the audience."
2. "Every afternoon, the dog waits at the door and barked when its owner arrived."
3. "We finished our science project and then present it to the class."
4. "The roller coaster climbed to the top and then races down the track."
5. "She was reading her book when the phone will ring."

**Part 2: Edit the passage. Find and correct all tense errors. There are four errors.**

6. *"The day of the big game finally arrived. The team runs onto the field, and the crowd cheered loudly. The quarterback throws a perfect pass, and the receiver caught it near the end zone. The team celebrates their victory late into the evening."*

**Part 3: Write your own.**

7. Write a 3-sentence paragraph about a trip to a restaurant. Use past tense consistently.
8. Write a 3-sentence paragraph about your daily routine. Use present tense consistently.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-7.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-8.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-8, plus write a 5-sentence paragraph about a memorable day. Include one appropriate tense shift and underline it, explaining why the shift is acceptable.

### Interleaved Review
9. **L.4.3a (precise words):** Replace the vague word with a more precise one: "The athlete moved quickly down the track." (e.g., sprinted, dashed, raced)
10. **L.4.1d (ordering adjectives):** Rewrite with adjectives in the correct order: "She bought a wooden large rectangular cutting board."

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Find and correct the tense errors in this passage (there are two):

*"The students gathered in the auditorium for the assembly. The principal walks to the microphone and welcomed everyone. She announced the winners of the reading challenge and gives out the prizes."*

**Expected Answers:**
- walks → **walked**
- gives → **gave**

Corrected: *"The students gathered in the auditorium for the assembly. The principal **walked** to the microphone and welcomed everyone. She announced the winners of the reading challenge and **gave** out the prizes."*

**Mastery = both errors identified and corrected.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the three-step strategy and practice it with the student on simpler, two-sentence passages
- Have the student color-code verbs by tense (blue = past, green = present, red = future) to make inconsistencies visually obvious
- Provide a checklist card with the three editing steps for the student to keep at their desk
- Practice oral correction before written: read sentences aloud and ask "Does that sound right?"

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 6 on commas in a series
- Encourage the student to apply the three-step tense editing strategy to their own writing during revision time

## Extension (Optional)
- **Peer Editing Workshop:** Exchange a recent writing assignment with a partner. Use the three-step tense editing strategy to check your partner's work for tense consistency. Write feedback notes.
- **Intentional Shift Story:** Write a story that takes place in two different time periods (e.g., a memory and the present). Use appropriate tense shifts to move between the two time periods. Underline each shift and label it.
- **Error Hunt Challenge:** The teacher provides a passage with six hidden tense errors. Students race to find and correct all six. First student to find all errors wins.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson focuses on the editing and correction skill, which is slightly different from the production skill in Lessons 3-4. Editing requires reading critically and checking existing text, which uses a different cognitive process than writing from scratch.
- Students who rush through reading often miss tense errors. Encourage them to read slowly and point to each verb with their finger or pencil.
- Some students may "correct" appropriate tense shifts. Remind them to always ask: "Did the time change?" If yes, the shift may be intentional and correct.
- For struggling students, start with passages that have only one error so they can build confidence before tackling multi-error passages.
- This is the final lesson in the verb tense sequence (L.5.1d). Consider a brief cumulative review of Lessons 3-5 before moving to the punctuation lessons.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice — Part 1
1. waves → **waved** ("The magician pulled a rabbit from his hat and waved it at the audience.")
2. barked → **barks**, arrived → **arrives** ("Every afternoon, the dog waits at the door and barks when its owner arrives.") Base tense is present ("every afternoon").
3. present → **presented** ("We finished our science project and then presented it to the class.")
4. races → **raced** ("The roller coaster climbed to the top and then raced down the track.")
5. will ring → **rang** ("She was reading her book when the phone rang.")

### Independent Practice — Part 2
6. Corrected passage: *"The day of the big game finally arrived. The team **ran** onto the field, and the crowd cheered loudly. The quarterback **threw** a perfect pass, and the receiver caught it near the end zone. The team **celebrated** their victory late into the evening."*

Errors: runs → ran, throws → threw, celebrates → celebrated. (Note: There are three tense errors, not four, unless the student also identifies a fourth. Accept three corrections.)

### Independent Practice — Part 3
7. Answers vary. Example: "Last Friday, my family went to an Italian restaurant downtown. We ordered pasta and garlic bread. The food tasted delicious, and we left very full."
8. Answers vary. Example: "Every morning, I wake up at seven o'clock. I eat cereal for breakfast and pack my lunch. Then I walk to the bus stop with my neighbor."

### Interleaved Review
9. Answers vary. Acceptable: sprinted, dashed, raced, bolted, flew (any precise verb replacing "moved quickly")
10. "She bought a **large, rectangular, wooden** cutting board." (size, shape, material)

See Expected Answers above.
