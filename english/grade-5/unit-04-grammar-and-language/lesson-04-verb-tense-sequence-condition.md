# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 4

## Topic
Using Verb Tense to Show Sequence and Condition

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Use verb tense purposefully to show the order (sequence) of events
- Use verb tense to express conditions (if/then relationships)
- Choose appropriate tenses to convey various times, sequences, states, and conditions

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of past, present, and future tenses (Lesson 3)
- Ability to identify consistent vs. inconsistent tense use (Lesson 3)
- Familiarity with progressive tenses from Grade 4 (L.4.1b)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Verb tense timeline chart from Lesson 3
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Sequence signal word cards (before, after, while, by the time, when)
- Condition signal word cards (if, unless, when, as long as)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review tense consistency and introduce the idea that tense can tell a story about time.

**Activity: Time Travel Sentences**

Write on the board:
- "Before I _______ (eat) breakfast, I _______ (brush) my teeth."

Say: "Can you fill in these blanks? But here is the tricky part — both actions happen in the past, but one happens BEFORE the other. How do we show that with verb tense?"

Give students 1-2 minutes to try. Then discuss: "Before I ate breakfast, I brushed my teeth." Both past tense — the word "before" signals the order.

Say: "Today we are going to learn how verb tense helps us show the ORDER of events and how to talk about CONDITIONS — things that depend on something else happening."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach how verb tense conveys sequence (order of events) and conditions (if/then situations).

### Key Vocabulary
- **Sequence:** The order in which events happen (first, then, next, finally)
- **Condition:** A situation that must be true for something else to happen (if/then)
- **Past perfect:** A tense that shows one past action happened before another (had + past participle: "had eaten")
- **Signal words:** Words that tell the reader about time order or conditions (before, after, if, when, unless)

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1: Tense and Sequence**

Say: "Sometimes we need to show that one event happened before another. Simple past tense can do this when we use signal words."

Write: **After the bell rang, the students rushed to the cafeteria.**

Say: "Both *rang* and *rushed* are past tense. The word *after* tells us the order: first the bell rang, then the students rushed."

Say: "But sometimes we use the past perfect tense — *had* plus the past participle — to make the order even clearer."

Write: **By the time we arrived, the movie had already started.**

Say: "*Had started* is past perfect. It tells us the movie started FIRST, and we arrived SECOND. The past perfect (*had started*) marks the earlier event."

**Part 2: Tense and Condition**

Say: "We also use verb tense to talk about conditions — things that depend on something else."

Write: **If it rains tomorrow, we will stay inside for recess.**

Say: "Notice the pattern: *If* + present tense (*rains*), then future tense (*will stay*). This is a common pattern for conditions about the future."

Write: **If you practice every day, you become a better musician.**

Say: "Here we have present tense in both parts. This is for general truths — things that are always true."

Write: **If she had studied harder, she would have passed the test.**

Say: "This talks about a condition in the past that did NOT happen. She did not study harder, so she did not pass. We use *had* + past participle and *would have* + past participle."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Sequence with signal words:**
Write: "Before the storm arrived, the farmers had gathered all the crops."

Say: "Two past events. The gathering happened FIRST. The storm arrived SECOND. *Had gathered* (past perfect) marks the earlier event. *Arrived* (simple past) is the later event."

**Example 2 — Sequence with while:**
Write: "While the teacher was explaining the lesson, the students were taking notes."

Say: "Both actions happen at the same time in the past. *Was explaining* and *were taking* are both past progressive. Using the same tense shows these events are simultaneous."

**Example 3 — Future condition:**
Write: "If you finish your homework early, you can play outside."

Say: "The condition is *finish your homework early* (present tense). The result is *can play outside*. Present tense in the *if* clause, modal verb in the main clause."

**Example 4 — General truth condition:**
Write: "When water reaches 100 degrees Celsius, it boils."

Say: "This is always true — a scientific fact. Both verbs are present tense because this is a general condition, not a one-time event."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice choosing the correct tense for sequence and condition sentences.

**Exercise A: Choose the correct verb form for sequence.**

1. By the time the bus came, we (wait / had waited) for twenty minutes.
2. After she (finished / had finished) her chores, Mia went to the park.
3. The game (started / had started) before we found our seats.
4. While Dad (cooked / was cooking) dinner, I (set / was setting) the table.

Work through item 1 together: "had waited" — the waiting happened first, the bus came second.

**Answers:** (1) had waited, (2) finished or had finished (both acceptable — "after" signals order), (3) had started, (4) was cooking / was setting.

**Exercise B: Choose the correct verb form for conditions.**

5. If the temperature drops below freezing, the pond (freezes / will freeze).
6. If you (mixed / mix) red and blue paint, you get purple.
7. Unless we (leave / will leave) now, we (are / will be) late.
8. If she (had practiced / practiced) more, she (would have won / will win) the race.

Work through items 5-6 together. Have students complete 7-8 independently, then review.

**Answers:** (5) will freeze (specific future condition), (6) mix / get (general truth), (7) leave / will be, (8) had practiced / would have won.

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently use verb tense to convey sequence and conditions.

**Directions:** Complete each item as directed.

1. Fill in the correct tense: "Before the concert began, the musicians (tune) _______ their instruments."
2. Fill in the correct tense: "By the time I woke up, my sister (leave) _______ for school already."
3. Fill in the correct tense: "If you (water) _______ the plant regularly, it (grow) _______ tall."
4. Fill in the correct tense: "While the team (practice) _______, dark clouds (gather) _______ overhead."
5. Write a sentence using "after" to show two events in sequence. Use past tense.
6. Write a sentence using "by the time" with the past perfect tense.
7. Write a sentence using "if" to describe a future condition.
8. Write a sentence using "while" to show two actions happening at the same time.
9. Write a sentence using "if" to describe a general truth about nature or science.
10. Correct this sentence: "If she will study tonight, she will do well on the test tomorrow."

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-8.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-10.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-10, plus write a 4-sentence story about a surprise birthday party. Use at least one sequence sentence (with past perfect) and one condition sentence.

### Interleaved Review
11. **L.4.1b (progressive tenses):** Write the past progressive form of "swim": "The dolphins _______ in the warm water."
12. **L.4.1a (relative pronouns):** Complete with a relative pronoun: "The book _______ I borrowed from the library was fascinating."

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Fill in the correct verb forms: "By the time the firefighters arrived, the neighbors (already / put out) _______ the small fire."
2. Complete the condition: "If it (snow) _______ tonight, school (be) _______ canceled tomorrow."

**Expected Answers:**
1. had already put out
2. snows / will be

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 items correct with appropriate tense forms.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the verb tense timeline and physically place events on it to show order
- Practice with just two tenses at a time (e.g., simple past and past perfect only)
- Use sentence frames: "By the time _______, _______ had already _______."
- Focus on signal words as clues to choosing the right tense

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 5 on correcting inappropriate tense shifts
- Challenge the student to find sequence and condition sentences in their reading

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Timeline Story:** Create a timeline of five events from your week. Write a paragraph that uses at least two past perfect sentences to show the order of events.
- **If/Then Chain:** Write a chain of condition sentences where the result of one becomes the condition of the next: "If I finish my homework, I can go outside. If I go outside, I will ride my bike. If I ride my bike..."
- **Science Connection:** Write three condition sentences about scientific facts using "if/when" and present tense (e.g., "When ice melts, it becomes water.").

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Past perfect (had + past participle) is new for many fifth graders. Do not expect perfection on the first day. The goal is recognition and basic use; proficiency develops over multiple exposures.
- Some students may struggle to see why past perfect is necessary when signal words like "before" and "after" already indicate order. Explain that past perfect provides extra clarity, especially in complex passages where the order might otherwise be confusing.
- For condition sentences, focus on the two most common patterns at this level: (1) If + present, will + base verb (future conditions), and (2) If + present, present (general truths). The past unreal condition (if + had + past participle, would have + past participle) is introduced for exposure but should not be assessed heavily.
- Signal word lists are very helpful. Consider posting them in the classroom: Sequence words (before, after, by the time, while, when, already, first, then) and Condition words (if, unless, when, as long as).

3. "If you **water** the plant regularly, it **grows** (or **will grow**) tall."
4. "While the team **was practicing**, dark clouds **were gathering** overhead."

9. Answers vary. Example: "If you heat water to 100 degrees, it boils."
