# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 9

## Topic
Combining and Reducing Sentences

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Combine two or more short, choppy sentences into one clear, fluent sentence
- Reduce wordy or repetitive sentences by eliminating unnecessary words
- Choose the best combining or reducing strategy based on the relationship between ideas

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of sentence expansion from Lesson 8
- Knowledge of conjunctions (coordinating and correlative) from Lessons 1-2
- Ability to identify subjects, verbs, and objects in sentences

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- "Choppy Paragraph" handout (printed or projected)
- Colored pencils for marking shared elements between sentences
- Sentence strip cards for physical combining activity

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Help students hear the difference between choppy writing and fluent writing.

**Activity: Choppy vs. Smooth**

Read aloud (or have a student read) the following two versions:

**Version A (choppy):** "I have a dog. My dog is brown. My dog is fluffy. My dog likes to play. My dog likes to run. My dog likes to swim."

**Version B (smooth):** "I have a fluffy brown dog who loves to play, run, and swim."

Say: "Which version sounds better? Version A has six sentences that all repeat *my dog*. Version B says the same thing in one smooth sentence. Today we are going to learn how to combine choppy sentences into smooth ones and how to reduce wordy sentences by cutting unnecessary words."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach strategies for combining and reducing sentences to improve clarity and style.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Sentence combining:** Joining two or more short sentences into one longer, more effective sentence
- **Sentence reducing:** Removing unnecessary or repeated words to make a sentence more concise
- **Choppy writing:** Writing that uses many short, repetitive sentences, making it sound abrupt and disconnected

### Instruction Notes

**Part 1: Combining Sentences**

Say: "There are several ways to combine sentences. The method you choose depends on how the ideas relate to each other."

**Strategy 1: Combine with a conjunction.**

Write:
- "The game was exciting." + "The game was close."
- Combined: "The game was exciting **and** close."

Say: "When two sentences share the same subject and verb, we can combine the different parts with a conjunction."

**Strategy 2: Combine with a correlative conjunction.**

Write:
- "The pizza was delicious." + "The garlic bread was delicious."
- Combined: "**Both** the pizza **and** the garlic bread were delicious."

Say: "We can use the correlative conjunction pairs we learned in Lessons 1-2!"

**Strategy 3: Combine by using an appositive.**

Write:
- "Ms. Chang teaches fourth grade." + "Ms. Chang is my neighbor."
- Combined: "Ms. Chang, **my neighbor**, teaches fourth grade."

Say: "When one sentence gives extra information about a noun in the other sentence, we can turn it into an appositive."

**Strategy 4: Combine by using a subordinating conjunction or relative pronoun.**

Write:
- "The players practiced hard." + "They wanted to win the tournament."
- Combined: "The players practiced hard **because** they wanted to win the tournament."

Say: "When one sentence gives the reason, time, or condition for the other, use a subordinating word like *because, when, after, although, since, who, which*."

**Part 2: Reducing Sentences**

Say: "Sometimes a sentence is not short and choppy — it is the opposite. It is too long and wordy. We reduce it by cutting words that do not add meaning."

**Strategy: Cut repeated or unnecessary words.**

Write: "The reason why she was late was because of the fact that her alarm did not go off in the morning."

Say: "This sentence is stuffed with filler. Let me reduce it."

Reduced: "She was late because her alarm did not go off."

Say: "Same meaning, half the words. I cut *the reason why*, *was because of the fact that*, and *in the morning* (already implied by 'alarm')."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Combining with a conjunction:**
- "The cake was chocolate." + "The frosting was vanilla."
- Combined: "The cake was chocolate, **but** the frosting was vanilla."

**Example 2 — Combining with a relative pronoun:**
- "The library has a new reading corner." + "The reading corner is cozy and quiet."
- Combined: "The library has a new reading corner **that is cozy and quiet**."

**Example 3 — Combining with a series:**
- "We saw dolphins." + "We saw sea turtles." + "We saw jellyfish."
- Combined: "We saw dolphins, sea turtles, and jellyfish."

Say: "When multiple sentences list items, combining them into a series is the simplest and most elegant solution."

**Example 4 — Reducing a wordy sentence:**
- Wordy: "In my personal opinion, I think that the movie was really very entertaining and fun to watch."
- Reduced: "I think the movie was entertaining and fun."

Say: "I cut *in my personal opinion* (redundant with *I think*), *really very* (filler), and *to watch* (implied). The meaning is the same."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice combining and reducing sentences with teacher support.

**Exercise A: Combine each pair (or group) into one sentence.**

1. "The sunset was beautiful." + "The sunset was colorful."
2. "Jayla finished her book." + "She started a new one."
3. "Mr. Kim is our math teacher." + "Mr. Kim coaches the chess club."
4. "We went to the park." + "We played soccer." + "We had a picnic." + "We flew kites."

Work through item 1 together: "The sunset was beautiful and colorful."

Have students work on items 2-4 in pairs, then share.

**Possible Answers:**
1. "The sunset was beautiful and colorful."
2. "After Jayla finished her book, she started a new one." (Or: "Jayla finished her book and started a new one.")
3. "Mr. Kim, our math teacher, also coaches the chess club."
4. "We went to the park, where we played soccer, had a picnic, and flew kites."

**Exercise B: Reduce these wordy sentences.**

5. "Due to the fact that it was raining outside, we had to stay inside the building for recess."
6. "The small little kitten was very extremely playful and active in nature."

**Possible Answers:**
5. "Because it was raining, we stayed inside for recess."
6. "The little kitten was extremely playful."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently combine and reduce sentences to improve writing fluency.

**Part 1: Combine each group of sentences into one clear sentence.**

1. "The ocean was calm." + "The ocean was warm." + "The ocean was clear."
2. "Liam plays basketball." + "He is on the school team."
3. "The test was hard." + "Everyone passed it."
4. "We planted tomatoes." + "We planted peppers." + "We planted sunflowers in the school garden."
5. "The robot moved forward." + "The robot turned left." + "The robot picked up the ball."

**Part 2: Reduce each wordy sentence.**

6. "What I want to say is that the book was the most interesting book I have ever read in my entire life."
7. "She walked slowly at a slow pace toward the big large enormous door."
8. "The basic fundamental reason for the cancellation of the game was that it was raining."

**Part 3: Revise the choppy paragraph.**

9. Rewrite this paragraph by combining and reducing sentences:

*"The fair was fun. The fair was crowded. There were rides. There were games. There was food. I rode the Ferris wheel. My brother rode the Ferris wheel. We ate cotton candy. We ate funnel cake. We ate caramel apples. We had a great time. We were tired when we got home."*

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-8.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-9.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-9, plus take a paragraph from your own writing and revise it by combining at least two sentences and reducing at least one wordy sentence.

### Interleaved Review
10. **L.4.2a (capitalization):** Correct: "next monday, our class is visiting the national air and space museum."
11. **L.4.1a (relative pronouns):** Combine using a relative pronoun: "The artist painted a mural." + "The mural shows the history of our town."

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Combine: "The puppy chewed the shoe." + "The puppy hid under the bed."
2. Reduce: "In my opinion, I personally believe that recess should be longer in length."

**Expected Answers:**
1. "The puppy chewed the shoe and hid under the bed." (Also accept: "The puppy chewed the shoe and then hid under the bed." or "After the puppy chewed the shoe, it hid under the bed.")
2. "I believe recess should be longer." (Or: "I think recess should be longer.")

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 items correct, with the combined sentence being grammatically correct and the reduced sentence retaining the original meaning in fewer words.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Start with the simplest combining strategy: joining with "and"
- Use physical sentence strips that students can literally cut and rearrange
- For reducing, have students cross out one word at a time and re-read to see if the meaning changes; if not, keep it crossed out
- Provide a checklist of "filler words" to look for: "the reason why," "due to the fact that," "in my opinion I think," "very very," "really"

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 10, the Grammar Review and Celebration
- Encourage the student to apply combining and reducing during writing revision time

## Extension (Optional)
- **Revision Relay:** In groups, each student writes three short, choppy sentences on a topic. Pass to the next student, who must combine them into one fluid sentence. Compare results.
- **Wordcount Challenge:** Take a 100-word paragraph and rewrite it in 60 words or fewer without losing any important information. Count your words carefully.
- **Style Study:** Find a paragraph from a favorite author. Identify one sentence where the author effectively combined ideas. Explain how the combination improves the writing compared to if the ideas had been in separate sentences.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Combining and reducing are revision skills. Students should practice them during the editing/revision phase of the writing process, not necessarily during drafting. During drafting, it is fine to write choppy sentences and clean them up later.
- The most common combining error is creating run-on sentences. Remind students that combining does not mean joining everything with "and" — they should use a variety of strategies (conjunctions, relative pronouns, appositives, series).
- When reducing, some students worry about cutting too much. Reassure them: if the meaning stays the same, the cut is good. If meaning is lost, put the words back.
- This lesson connects directly to the writing process. Consider scheduling a writing workshop session after this lesson where students apply combining and reducing to a current draft.
- Combining sentences is also an excellent way to practice commas in a series (Lesson 6) and correlative conjunctions (Lessons 1-2). Look for opportunities to reinforce those skills.
