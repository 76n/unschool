# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Topic
Grammar Review and Celebration

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Demonstrate proficiency across all four unit standards (L.5.1e, L.5.1d, L.5.2a, L.5.3a)
- Apply grammar skills in an integrated, authentic writing context
- Reflect on their growth as grammar-aware writers

## Prerequisites
- Completion of Lessons 1-9
- Familiarity with correlative conjunctions, verb tense consistency, punctuation in a series, and sentence manipulation

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- "Grammar Challenge" cards (prepared sets of review questions)
- Timer (for timed review activities)
- Colored pencils
- Certificates or small rewards for celebration (optional)
- Index cards for the Grammar Gallery activity

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Energize students and review all four grammar concepts from the unit.

**Activity: Four Corners Quick Review**

Label four areas of the room (or four sections of the board):
- Corner 1: Correlative Conjunctions
- Corner 2: Verb Tense
- Corner 3: Punctuation in a Series
- Corner 4: Sentence Expanding/Combining

Read the following sentences and ask students to identify which grammar skill is being demonstrated. Students point to the correct corner (or hold up 1-4 fingers).

1. "Both the coach and the players celebrated the victory." (Corner 1 — correlative conjunctions)
2. "The hikers packed their bags, filled their water bottles, and laced up their boots." (Corner 3 — commas in a series)
3. "Yesterday the sun shone brightly, but today it is raining." (Corner 2 — verb tense)
4. "The small gray kitten, barely eight weeks old, pounced playfully on a crinkled ball of paper." (Corner 4 — sentence expanding)

Say: "You have learned so much in this unit! Today we are going to review all four skills, practice them together, and celebrate how far you have come as grammar experts."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Provide a focused review of key rules and strategies from each standard.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Correlative conjunctions:** Either/or, neither/nor, both/and, not only/but also — always in fixed pairs with parallel structure
- **Verb tense consistency:** Keep the same tense unless the time genuinely changes
- **Series punctuation:** Use commas to separate three or more items; use semicolons when items have internal commas
- **Sentence manipulation:** Expand for detail, combine for fluency, reduce for conciseness

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Let me do a speed review of each skill. Follow along and take notes if you want."

**Skill 1: Correlative Conjunctions (L.5.1e)**

Write: "Fixed pairs: either/or, neither/nor, both/and, not only/but also, whether/or."

Say: "Remember — never mix pairs. And the elements after each conjunction must be parallel."

Quick check: "Either she sings or she dances." (Parallel — verb phrase + verb phrase.)

**Skill 2: Verb Tense Consistency (L.5.1d)**

Write: "Same time = same tense. Time changes = tense may change."

Say: "Use the three-step strategy: (1) find the base tense, (2) underline verbs, (3) check each one."

Quick check: "She opened the door and sees a cat." Error: sees → saw.

**Skill 3: Series Punctuation (L.5.2a)**

Write: "Three+ items → commas + Oxford comma. Internal commas → semicolons."

Say: "Always use the Oxford comma. Use semicolons only when items already have commas inside them."

Quick check: "We visited Austin Texas and Denver Colorado." Fix: "We visited Austin, Texas; and Denver, Colorado."

**Skill 4: Sentence Expanding, Combining, and Reducing (L.5.3a)**

Write: "Expand for detail. Combine for fluency. Reduce for conciseness."

Say: "Ask the expansion questions: where, when, how, why. Combine with conjunctions, appositives, relative pronouns, or series. Reduce by cutting filler and repetition."

Quick check: Combine: "The dog ran." + "The dog jumped." → "The dog ran and jumped."

### Worked Examples

**Integrated Example:** Say: "Now let me show you how these skills work together in real writing."

Write this weak paragraph on the board:

*"Yesterday we went to the aquarium. We saw sharks. We saw dolphins. We saw jellyfish. The dolphins do tricks. They jump through hoops. They splash the audience. Neither the sharks or the jellyfish did tricks. It was fun. It was exciting."*

Say: "Let me revise this using everything we have learned."

Revised: *"Yesterday, we went to the aquarium and saw sharks, dolphins, and jellyfish. The dolphins performed amazing tricks — they jumped through hoops and splashed the audience. Neither the sharks nor the jellyfish performed tricks, but they were fascinating to watch. The trip was both fun and exciting."*

Point out: "I combined sentences into a series (L.5.2a), kept the past tense consistent (L.5.1d), fixed *neither/or* to *neither/nor* and used *both/and* (L.5.1e), and expanded and combined for style (L.5.3a)."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students apply all four skills in a collaborative review activity.

**Activity: Grammar Challenge Relay**

Divide students into pairs or small groups. Present the following items one at a time. Teams discuss and write their answer. After each item, review the correct answer.

**Round 1 — Correlative Conjunctions:**
1. Correct this sentence: "Neither the library or the computer lab is open today."
2. Write a sentence using *not only/but also* about a classmate.

**Round 2 — Verb Tense:**
3. Find and fix the error: "The explorers packed their supplies and trek through the jungle for three days."
4. Write a sentence with an appropriate tense shift using a time signal word.

**Round 3 — Series Punctuation:**
5. Add punctuation: "The relay teams include runners from Springfield Illinois jumpers from Dayton Ohio and throwers from Gary Indiana."
6. Add punctuation: "She packed a raincoat a hat sunglasses and sunscreen."

**Round 4 — Sentence Manipulation:**
7. Combine: "The movie was long." + "The movie was boring." + "We left early."
8. Expand: "The cat sat." Add at least three details.

**Answers:**
1. "Neither the library **nor** the computer lab is open today."
2. Answers vary. Example: "Aisha is not only a fast runner but also an excellent student."
3. trek → **trekked**
4. Answers vary. Example: "Last year I played clarinet, but now I am learning the trumpet."
5. "The relay teams include runners from Springfield, Illinois; jumpers from Dayton, Ohio; and throwers from Gary, Indiana."
6. "She packed a raincoat, a hat, sunglasses, and sunscreen."
7. "The movie was long and boring, so we left early."
8. Answers vary. Example: "The sleepy orange tabby cat sat contentedly on the warm windowsill, watching the birds flutter in the garden below."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students demonstrate integrated mastery across all four standards.

**Directions:** Complete each item. This is your final practice before the Unit Assessment.

**Correlative Conjunctions (L.5.1e):**
1. Fill in the correct pair: "_______ the bus _______ the subway goes directly to the stadium."
2. Write a sentence using *whether/or* about a decision you need to make.

**Verb Tense (L.5.1d):**
3. Find and fix the error: "The artist sketched the landscape and then paints it with watercolors."
4. Write two sentences: one about something that happened yesterday and one about what is happening right now. Make sure the tense shift is appropriate.

**Series Punctuation (L.5.2a):**
5. Add punctuation: "The recipe requires flour sugar butter eggs and vanilla extract."
6. Add punctuation: "Our group includes Maria the team captain Jake the scorekeeper and Sofia the timekeeper."

**Sentence Manipulation (L.5.3a):**
7. Combine these three sentences into one: "The thunderstorm was loud." + "The thunderstorm was sudden." + "It scared the horses in the barn."
8. Expand this sentence with at least three meaningful details: "The child ran."
9. Reduce this wordy sentence: "In my personal opinion, I think that it is really truly important and necessary for students to get enough sleep at nighttime."

**Integrated Challenge:**
10. Write a 4-5 sentence paragraph about a school field trip (real or imaginary). Your paragraph must include: one correlative conjunction pair, consistent verb tense, at least one series with correct punctuation, and at least one well-expanded sentence.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-8.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-10.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-10, and in item 10, also include a sentence that uses semicolons in a series.

### Interleaved Review
11. **L.4.1b (progressive tenses):** Write the future progressive: "At this time tomorrow, we _______ (fly) to California."
12. **L.4.3a (precise words):** Replace vague language: "The building was really big and nice." → Use precise adjectives.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Correct this sentence (it has TWO errors — one tense and one conjunction): "Neither the rain or the wind stopped the game, and the players runs onto the field."
2. Combine and punctuate: "We visited the zoo." + "We visited the museum." + "We visited the park."

**Expected Answers:**
1. "Neither the rain **nor** the wind stopped the game, and the players **ran** onto the field."
2. "We visited the zoo, the museum, and the park."

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 items correct with all errors fixed and proper punctuation.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which specific standard the student struggles with and revisit the corresponding lesson
- Provide a one-page grammar reference sheet covering all four skills
- Allow extra practice with targeted exercises from the lesson where the gap exists
- Consider delaying the Unit Assessment by one session to allow for re-teaching

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for the Unit Assessment
- Celebrate their growth! See the Celebration section below.

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Grammar Gallery Walk:** Each student writes a paragraph that demonstrates all four grammar skills. Post paragraphs around the room. Students walk around with a checklist, identifying where each skill appears in their classmates' writing.
- **Grammar Awards:** Create fun award categories: "Correlative Conjunction Champion," "Tense Master," "Punctuation Pro," "Sentence Stylist." Students nominate themselves or peers for each category.
- **Teaching Moment:** Pair students and have each one "teach" a grammar concept to their partner using examples. Teaching deepens understanding.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson is designed to be both a review and a celebration. Keep the energy positive. Students should feel proud of what they have learned, not anxious about the upcoming assessment.
- The Grammar Challenge Relay in Guided Practice can be adapted: use it as a competition with points, a collaborative game, or a self-paced station rotation depending on your class dynamics.
- If you have time, consider a brief "Grammar Superlatives" activity at the end: "Most improved," "Best editor," "Creative sentence master." This builds community and positive associations with grammar.
- Use the Independent Practice results as a diagnostic. If most students struggle with one particular standard, consider a brief targeted review before administering the Unit Assessment.
- The integrated paragraph (item 10) is the most authentic and important task in this practice. It mirrors how students will actually use these skills in real writing. Give this item extra attention during review.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. **Neither** the bus **nor** the subway goes directly to the stadium. (Or: **Either** the bus **or** the subway...)
2. Answers vary. Example: "I cannot decide whether to join the art club or the drama club."
3. paints → **painted** ("The artist sketched the landscape and then painted it with watercolors.")
4. Answers vary. Example: "Yesterday I finished reading a mystery novel. Right now, I am starting a new science fiction book."
5. "The recipe requires flour, sugar, butter, eggs, and vanilla extract."
6. "Our group includes Maria, the team captain; Jake, the scorekeeper; and Sofia, the timekeeper."
7. "The sudden, loud thunderstorm scared the horses in the barn." (Or: "The thunderstorm was loud and sudden, and it scared the horses in the barn.")
8. Answers vary. Example: "The excited child ran barefoot across the warm sandy beach toward the crashing waves, laughing with delight."
9. "Students need enough sleep." (Or: "I think students need enough sleep.")
10. Answers vary. Example: "Last month, our class took a field trip to the natural history museum downtown. We explored the dinosaur exhibit, the gemstone gallery, and the ocean life hall. Not only did we see a real T. rex skeleton, but we also watched a documentary about volcanoes. Our guide, a friendly scientist named Dr. Rao, answered all our questions with enthusiasm. Both the teachers and the students agreed it was the best field trip of the year."

### Interleaved Review
11. "At this time tomorrow, we **will be flying** to California."
12. Answers vary. Example: "The skyscraper was towering and elegant." (Or: "The massive glass skyscraper gleamed in the sunlight.")

See Expected Answers above.
