# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 6

## Topic
Commas in a Series

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define a series in grammar as a list of three or more items in a sentence
- Use commas correctly to separate items in a series of words, phrases, or clauses
- Apply the Oxford comma (serial comma) before the coordinating conjunction in a series

## Prerequisites
- Basic comma usage from Grade 3-4 (commas in addresses, dates, greetings)
- Understanding of coordinating conjunctions (and, or, but)
- Ability to identify nouns, verbs, adjectives, and phrases in sentences

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Printed or projected example sentences
- Comma cards (small cards with commas printed on them for physical placement activity)
- Grocery list or packing list examples

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect the grammar concept to students' everyday experiences with lists.

**Activity: The Rapid List**

Say: "Imagine you are going on a camping trip. In your notebook, write a sentence that lists at least three things you would pack. You have 1 minute."

After 1 minute, call on 3 students to share. Write one example on the board without commas:

"I would pack a tent a sleeping bag a flashlight and snacks."

Say: "Is this easy to read? Not really! How do we know where one item ends and the next begins? That is exactly what commas do in a series — they separate items so the reader can tell them apart. Today we are going to master this skill."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the rules for using commas to separate items in a series, including the Oxford comma.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Series:** A list of three or more items in a sentence, separated by commas
- **Oxford comma (serial comma):** The comma placed before the coordinating conjunction (and, or) in a series of three or more items
- **Items in a series:** Can be single words, phrases, or short clauses

### Instruction Notes

Say: "A series is a list of three or more items in a sentence. We use commas to separate each item."

**Rule 1:** Write on the board: "Place a comma after each item in a series EXCEPT the last item, which is preceded by a conjunction (and, or)."

Write: **I enjoy soccer, basketball, and swimming.**

Say: "There are three items: soccer, basketball, and swimming. I place a comma after *soccer* and after *basketball*. The word *and* comes before the last item. Notice I also put a comma before *and* — this is called the Oxford comma or serial comma."

**Rule 2: Always use the Oxford comma.**

Say: "Some writers leave out the comma before *and*, but in school and in careful writing, we always include it. Here is why."

Write: **I admire my parents, Batman and Wonder Woman.**

Say: "Without the Oxford comma, it sounds like my parents ARE Batman and Wonder Woman! Now watch."

Write: **I admire my parents, Batman, and Wonder Woman.**

Say: "With the Oxford comma, it is clear: three separate items. The Oxford comma prevents confusion."

**Rule 3: Items can be words, phrases, or clauses.**

Write (words): **The flag is red, white, and blue.**
Write (phrases): **The cat hid under the bed, behind the couch, and inside the closet.**
Write (clauses): **She woke up early, she ate breakfast quickly, and she ran to catch the bus.**

Say: "Whether you are listing single words, phrases, or short clauses, the comma rule is the same: comma after each item, with an Oxford comma before the conjunction."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Nouns in a series:**
Write: "For the science project, we need glue, scissors, poster board, and markers."

Say: "Four items. Commas after *glue*, *scissors*, and *poster board*. Oxford comma before *and markers*."

**Example 2 — Verbs in a series:**
Write: "The puppy ran, jumped, and rolled in the grass."

Say: "Three actions. Commas after *ran* and *jumped*. Oxford comma before *and rolled*."

**Example 3 — Adjectives in a series:**
Write: "The sunset was orange, pink, purple, and gold."

Say: "Four colors. Commas separate each one, with an Oxford comma before *and gold*."

**Example 4 — Phrases in a series:**
Write: "We looked for the lost kitten under the porch, behind the garage, and across the street."

Say: "Three phrases telling where we looked. Each phrase gets a comma, with an Oxford comma before *and across the street*."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice inserting commas in a series with teacher guidance.

**Exercise A: Add commas to these sentences.**

1. The recipe calls for flour sugar eggs and butter.
2. My favorite subjects are math science reading and art.
3. The astronaut floated through the cabin checked the instruments and radioed mission control.
4. You can choose a pencil a pen or a crayon for the activity.

Work through item 1 together: "The recipe calls for flour**,** sugar**,** eggs**,** and butter."

Have students complete items 2-4 independently, then compare with a partner.

**Answers:**
1. flour**,** sugar**,** eggs**,** and butter
2. math**,** science**,** reading**,** and art
3. floated through the cabin**,** checked the instruments**,** and radioed mission control
4. a pencil**,** a pen**,** or a crayon

**Exercise B: Write sentences with a series.**

5. Write a sentence listing three things you see in the classroom right now.
6. Write a sentence listing four activities you enjoy on weekends.
7. Write a sentence listing three places you would like to visit.

Have 2-3 students share each sentence. Check for correct comma placement and the Oxford comma.

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students demonstrate mastery of comma use in a series across various contexts.

**Directions:** Complete each item as directed.

1. Add commas: "The garden had roses tulips daisies and sunflowers."
2. Add commas: "Before the trip we packed our bags checked the weather and loaded the car."
3. Add commas: "The sandwich had turkey lettuce tomato and cheese."
4. Add commas: "She is kind funny generous and brave."
5. Write a sentence listing three sports that use a ball.
6. Write a sentence listing four things you might find at the beach.
7. Write a sentence listing three actions a chef does in the kitchen.
8. Correct this sentence: "I need to buy apples, oranges and bananas and grapes." (Fix the missing Oxford comma and clarify the list.)
9. Write a sentence listing three phrases that describe where animals live (e.g., "in the ocean, in the forest, and on the plains").
10. Explain in one or two sentences: Why is the Oxford comma important? Give an example.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-8.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-10.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-10, plus write a 4-sentence paragraph about a perfect day that includes at least two sentences with items in a series. Use the Oxford comma correctly in both.

### Interleaved Review
11. **L.4.1b (progressive tenses):** Choose the correct form: "Tomorrow at noon, we _______ (will be eating / were eating) lunch in the cafeteria."
12. **L.4.2a (capitalization):** Correct the capitalization: "my friend carlos lives on maple street in chicago, illinois."

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Add commas to this sentence: "We saw dolphins sea turtles stingrays and jellyfish at the aquarium."
2. Write one sentence listing three things you ate today, using commas correctly.

**Expected Answers:**
1. "We saw dolphins**,** sea turtles**,** stingrays**,** and jellyfish at the aquarium."
2. Answers vary. Example: "Today I ate cereal**,** a turkey sandwich**,** and an apple."

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 items correct with all commas in the right places, including the Oxford comma.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the basic rule: count the items, then place a comma after each item except the last (which gets a conjunction)
- Use physical comma cards: write list items on separate cards and have the student physically place comma cards between them
- Practice with very simple three-item lists before progressing to four or more items
- Read sentences aloud, pausing at each comma, to build an auditory sense of where commas belong

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 7 on semicolons in a series with internal commas
- Encourage the student to check their own writing for correct comma use in series

## Extension (Optional)
- **Menu Creator:** Design a restaurant menu with at least four categories (appetizers, main courses, sides, desserts). Write a sentence describing each category using a series with commas. Example: "Our appetizers include bruschetta, mozzarella sticks, and garden salad."
- **Comma Debate:** Research the Oxford comma debate. Some style guides require it; others do not. Write a short paragraph arguing for or against always using the Oxford comma.
- **List Poem:** Write a poem where each line contains a series of three or more items. Use commas correctly. Example: "In my pocket I keep a penny, a pebble, and a dream."

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The Oxford comma is a point of genuine debate in professional writing. For this curriculum, we teach students to always use it because it prevents ambiguity and is required in most academic writing.
- Students sometimes place a comma before the first item ("I like, soccer, basketball, and swimming"). Remind them that commas go BETWEEN items, not before the first one.
- Another common error is forgetting that a series requires three or more items. Two items joined by "and" do not need a comma: "I like soccer and basketball" (no comma needed).
- Watch for students who list two-word items and get confused about where commas go: "peanut butter, jelly, and bread" — the comma goes after the complete item "peanut butter," not after "peanut."
- This lesson pairs well with writing workshop activities where students are revising drafts that contain lists.
