# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 3

## Topic
Verb Tense Consistency

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify the six basic verb tenses (past, present, future, past progressive, present progressive, future progressive)
- Recognize when verb tense is consistent within a sentence or passage
- Identify and explain inappropriate verb tense shifts

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of past, present, and future tenses from Grade 3-4
- Ability to form and use progressive verb tenses (L.4.1b)
- Familiarity with regular and irregular verb forms

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Verb tense timeline chart (posted or projected)
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Colored pencils (one color for past tense verbs, one for present, one for future)
- Sample paragraph with tense shifts (projected or printed)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review verb tenses and activate awareness of tense as a time-signaling tool.

**Activity: Tense Sort**

Write these six verbs on the board:
- jumped, is running, will cook, played, eats, was singing

Say: "Sort these verbs into three groups: past, present, and future. Write them in your notebook under the correct heading."

Give students 2 minutes. Then review:
- Past: jumped, played, was singing
- Present: is running, eats
- Future: will cook

Say: "Great! When we write, we choose a tense and usually stick with it. Today we are going to learn why staying consistent with our verb tense matters — and what happens when we accidentally shift."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the concept of tense consistency and help students recognize when a shift is inappropriate.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Verb tense:** The form of a verb that tells when an action happens (past, present, or future)
- **Tense consistency:** Keeping the same verb tense throughout a sentence or passage when the time frame does not change
- **Tense shift:** A change from one verb tense to another; appropriate when the time changes, inappropriate when it does not

### Instruction Notes

Say: "When we write about something that happened in the past, we use past tense verbs. When we write about something happening right now, we use present tense. The key rule is: Do not switch tenses unless the time actually changes."

Write on the board:

**Consistent (correct):** "The dog chased the squirrel and barked at the tree."

Say: "Both verbs — *chased* and *barked* — are past tense. The actions happened at the same time in the past. This is consistent."

**Inconsistent (incorrect):** "The dog chased the squirrel and barks at the tree."

Say: "Here we have *chased* (past) and *barks* (present). Did the time change? No — both actions happened during the same event. This is an inappropriate tense shift. We need to fix it."

**When tense shifts ARE appropriate:**

Write: "Yesterday I walked to school, but today I am riding the bus."

Say: "Here the tense changes from past (*walked*) to present progressive (*am riding*), but that is correct because the TIME actually changed — from yesterday to today. The tense shift matches the time shift."

Draw or display a timeline on the board:
```
PAST ←——————— PRESENT ——————→ FUTURE
walked, ran    walk, run       will walk, will run
was walking    am walking      will be walking
```

Say: "When you write, ask yourself: Did the time change? If yes, you may shift tense. If no, keep it consistent."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Finding an error:**
Write: "Maria studied for the test and passes with a perfect score."

Say: "Let me check this sentence. *Studied* is past tense. *Passes* is present tense. Did the time change? No — both actions are about the same past event. I need to change *passes* to *passed*."

Correct: "Maria studied for the test and **passed** with a perfect score."

**Example 2 — Consistent passage:**
Write: "Every morning, the birds sing outside my window. They hop along the branches and chirp their favorite songs."

Say: "All the verbs — *sing*, *hop*, *chirp* — are present tense. The time frame is the same: every morning, regularly. This is consistent. No changes needed."

**Example 3 — Appropriate shift:**
Write: "Last summer we visited the Grand Canyon. Next summer, we will visit Yellowstone."

Say: "We went from *visited* (past) to *will visit* (future). Is this an error? No! The time genuinely changed from last summer to next summer. This shift is appropriate."

**Example 4 — Multiple errors:**
Write: "The team practiced all week. On game day, they play their best and won the championship."

Say: "Let me check each verb. *Practiced* — past. *Play* — present. *Won* — past. The time frame is the same series of events in the past. *Play* should be *played*."

Correct: "The team practiced all week. On game day, they **played** their best and won the championship."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice identifying and correcting tense shifts with teacher guidance.

**Exercise A: Identify the error and correct it.**

1. "The cat climbed the tree and then jumps to the roof."
2. "We are eating lunch when the fire alarm rings."
3. "Jayla finished her painting and shows it to the class."
4. "Every day, the bus arrives at eight o'clock and the students walked to their classrooms."

Work through item 1 together: *jumps* should be *jumped* (past tense to match *climbed*).

Have students work on items 2-4 in pairs, then review as a class.

**Answers:**
1. jumps → jumped
2. rings → rang (to match "are eating" — both past progressive context) OR are eating → were eating (to match "rings" if present tense intended). Accept either correction if the student explains their reasoning.
3. shows → showed
4. walked → walk (to match present tense "arrives" and "every day")

**Exercise B: Is the tense shift appropriate or inappropriate?**

5. "I studied French last year, and now I am learning Spanish." (Appropriate — time changed)
6. "She opened the door and sees a package on the step." (Inappropriate — same past event)
7. "The dinosaurs lived millions of years ago, but today scientists study their bones." (Appropriate — time changed)

---

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently identify, correct, and maintain verb tense consistency.

**Directions:** For items 1-6, find and correct the tense error. For items 7-10, write sentences with consistent tense.

1. "The hikers climbed the mountain and enjoy the view from the top."
2. "My brother is building a model airplane and painted it blue."
3. "The students will perform in the talent show and received a standing ovation."
4. "Coach Davis blew the whistle, and the swimmers dive into the pool."
5. "Every Saturday, we visit Grandma and baked cookies together."
6. "The astronaut floated in space and takes photographs of Earth."
7. Write two sentences in past tense about a fun day you had.
8. Write two sentences in present tense about your morning routine.
9. Write two sentences that contain an appropriate tense shift (the time genuinely changes).
10. Write a short passage (3 sentences) about a school event. Keep the tense consistent.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-8.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-10.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-10, then find and correct all tense errors in this paragraph: "Yesterday, the class goes on a field trip to the science museum. We see amazing exhibits about space. The guide explained how rockets work and shows us a model. Everyone has a great time."

### Interleaved Review
11. **L.4.2a (capitalization):** Correct the capitalization errors: "last tuesday, we visited the lincoln memorial in washington, d.c."
12. **L.4.1d (ordering adjectives):** Put the adjectives in the correct order: "He found a _______ coin." (old, small, round, silver)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Find and correct the tense error: "The dog ran across the yard and catches the frisbee."
2. Is this tense shift appropriate or inappropriate? "I read that book last month, and now I am reading the sequel."

**Expected Answers:**
1. catches → caught ("The dog ran across the yard and caught the frisbee.")
2. Appropriate — the time changes from last month to now.

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 items correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Revisit the verb tense timeline; have the student place verbs on the timeline to visualize consistency
- Use color-coding: highlight all verbs in a passage and check whether they are the same color (tense)
- Practice with simpler two-verb sentences before moving to paragraphs
- Ask the guiding question: "Did the time change? If not, the tense should stay the same."

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for Lesson 4, which explores using tense to show sequence and condition
- Encourage the student to check their own writing for tense consistency during revision

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Tense Detective:** Take a paragraph from your independent reading book and identify all the verbs. Are they consistent? If there are shifts, explain whether each shift is appropriate or inappropriate.
- **Before and After:** Write a 4-sentence paragraph about "My life last year vs. my life now." Use past tense for last year and present tense for now. Make sure each shift is appropriate.
- **Peer Editor:** Exchange a piece of writing with a classmate. Use a colored pencil to circle any verbs that seem inconsistent with the rest of the passage.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The most common student error is mixing past and present tense within a narrative. Encourage students to decide on a "base tense" before they begin writing and to check all verbs against it during revision.
- Some students may have difficulty with irregular past tense forms (e.g., *run/ran*, *see/saw*, *catch/caught*). If verb form errors are frequent, consider a brief mini-lesson on common irregular verbs.
- When discussing appropriate shifts, help students recognize signal words: "yesterday," "now," "tomorrow," "last week," "currently," "next year." These words cue the reader that a time change is happening.
- Be careful not to overcorrect. If a student writes a passage with a genuine time shift and appropriately changes tense, affirm that choice even if the passage would also work in a single tense.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. enjoy → **enjoyed** ("The hikers climbed the mountain and enjoyed the view from the top.")
2. painted → **is painting** ("My brother is building a model airplane and is painting it blue.") Also accept: is building → built, if student makes both past.
3. received → **will receive** ("The students will perform in the talent show and will receive a standing ovation.")
4. dive → **dived** or **dove** ("Coach Davis blew the whistle, and the swimmers dove into the pool.")
5. baked → **bake** ("Every Saturday, we visit Grandma and bake cookies together.")
6. takes → **took** ("The astronaut floated in space and took photographs of Earth.")
7. Answers vary. Example: "Last weekend, I went to the water park with my cousins. We raced down the giant slide and splashed into the pool."
8. Answers vary. Example: "Every morning, I brush my teeth and eat breakfast. Then I pack my backpack and walk to school."
9. Answers vary. Example: "Last year I played soccer, but this year I am trying basketball. I enjoyed soccer, and I think I will enjoy basketball too."
10. Answers vary. Check that all verbs in the passage are in the same tense unless a time shift is present.

**Challenge paragraph corrected:** "Yesterday, the class **went** on a field trip to the science museum. We **saw** amazing exhibits about space. The guide explained how rockets work and **showed** us a model. Everyone **had** a great time."
