# English Language Arts — Grade 5 — Unit 4, Lesson 1

## Topic
Correlative Conjunctions: Either/Or and Neither/Nor

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define correlative conjunctions and explain that they come in fixed pairs
- Use either/or correctly in sentences to present two choices
- Use neither/nor correctly in sentences to negate two options

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of coordinating conjunctions (and, but, or, so) from Grade 4
- Ability to identify subjects and verbs in a sentence (L.4.1b)
- Familiarity with sentence structure and complete sentences

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers
- Printed or projected example sentences
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Index cards with either/or and neither/nor printed on them
- Colored pencils (two colors for marking conjunction pairs)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of conjunctions and introduce the concept of paired words.

**Activity: Conjunction Quick-Think**

Say: "Think of a conjunction you already know. Write one sentence using that conjunction in your notebook."

Give students 1-2 minutes to write. Then call on 3-4 students to share. Write their sentences on the board, underlining the conjunction in each.

Say: "Great! You already know conjunctions like *and*, *but*, and *or*. Today we are going to learn about a special kind of conjunction that always travels in pairs — like best friends who never go anywhere alone."

Write on the board: **Correlative Conjunctions = conjunctions that work in pairs**

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the correlative conjunction pairs either/or and neither/nor with explicit rules and examples.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Correlative conjunctions:** A pair of conjunctions that work together to connect equal parts of a sentence (e.g., either/or, neither/nor)
- **Either/or:** A conjunction pair used to present two choices or possibilities
- **Neither/nor:** A conjunction pair used to negate or reject two options

### Instruction Notes

Say: "A correlative conjunction is a pair of words that work together. You cannot use one without the other. Think of them like a lock and a key — they only work as a set."

Write on the board:
- **either . . . or** (two choices)
- **neither . . . nor** (two things that are not true)

Say: "Let us start with *either/or*. We use this pair when we want to show that there are two choices."

Write on the board: **Either we play soccer or we play basketball at recess.**

Say: "Notice that *either* comes before the first choice, and *or* comes before the second choice. Both choices are the same kind of phrase — in this case, 'we play soccer' and 'we play basketball.' The parts after *either* and *or* should match in structure."

Say: "Now let us look at *neither/nor*. We use this pair when we want to say that two things are both not true."

Write on the board: **Neither the cat nor the dog wanted to go outside in the rain.**

Say: "Here, *neither* comes before the first thing being rejected, and *nor* comes before the second. We are saying the cat did not want to go out AND the dog did not want to go out."

**Important Rule:** Write on the board: "Never mix pairs! Do NOT write *neither/or* or *either/nor*. The pairs are fixed."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (either/or — choices):**
Say: "Watch how I think through this. I want to say that for our field trip, we have two options: the zoo or the aquarium."

Write: **Either we visit the zoo or we visit the aquarium for our field trip.**

Say: "I put *either* before the first option and *or* before the second. Both parts have the same structure: 'we visit [place].'"

**Example 2 (either/or — subjects):**
Write: **Either Maya or Jordan will present the science project first.**

Say: "Here, I am connecting two subjects — Maya and Jordan. Only one of them will go first. Notice that both parts connected are single names. They match."

**Example 3 (neither/nor — negating two things):**
Write: **Neither the library nor the gym is open on Sunday.**

Say: "I am saying the library is NOT open and the gym is NOT open. I used *neither* before the first item and *nor* before the second. When both subjects are singular, the verb matches the subject closer to it — *gym is*."

**Example 4 (neither/nor — actions):**
Write: **The puppy neither barked nor whined during the thunderstorm.**

Say: "Here I am negating two actions. The puppy did not bark and did not whine. Notice *neither* comes before the first verb and *nor* before the second."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice using either/or and neither/nor with teacher guidance and immediate feedback.

Say: "Now it is your turn. I will put some sentences on the board. Let us fill in the correct correlative conjunctions together."

**Exercise A: Fill in the blanks with either/or or neither/nor.**

1. _______ the red team _______ the blue team has scored a goal yet.
2. You may choose _______ the chocolate cake _______ the vanilla cupcake for dessert.
3. _______ Aiden _______ his sister remembered to bring an umbrella.
4. We can _______ walk to school _______ ride our bikes tomorrow.
5. _______ the math test _______ the spelling quiz was difficult.

Work through items 1-2 together on the board. For items 3-5, have students write their answers independently, then compare with a partner before reviewing as a class.

**Exercise B: Rewrite using correlative conjunctions.**

Say: "Now let us take regular sentences and rewrite them using either/or or neither/nor."

6. The dolphins did not jump. The seals did not jump. (Use neither/nor)
7. We could go hiking. We could go swimming. (Use either/or)
8. The pizza was not too hot. The pizza was not too cold. (Use neither/nor)

Model item 6 on the board: "Neither the dolphins nor the seals jumped."

Have students work on items 7-8, then share.

---

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students demonstrate ability to use either/or and neither/nor independently in a variety of sentence contexts.

**Directions:** Complete each item. Use either/or or neither/nor as indicated.

1. Write a sentence using *either/or* about choosing between two sports.
2. Write a sentence using *neither/nor* about two foods you do not like.
3. Fill in the blanks: _______ the teacher _______ the students wanted to cancel the field trip.
4. Fill in the blanks: You can sit _______ at the front table _______ at the back table.
5. Correct this sentence: "Either the dog nor the cat is allowed on the couch." Write it correctly.
6. Correct this sentence: "Neither the sun or the moon was visible." Write it correctly.
7. Combine into one sentence using neither/nor: "Marcus did not finish his homework. Priya did not finish her homework."
8. Combine into one sentence using either/or: "We will have tacos for dinner. We will have pasta for dinner."
9. Write a sentence using *either/or* about two places you might visit this summer.
10. Write a sentence using *neither/nor* about two subjects at school.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-8.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-10.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-10, plus write a short paragraph (3-4 sentences) that uses both either/or and neither/nor at least once each.

### Interleaved Review
11. **L.4.1b (progressive tenses):** Choose the correct verb form: "Right now, the students _______ (are reading / were reading) their library books."
12. **L.4.1d (ordering adjectives):** Put the adjectives in the correct order: "She wore a _______ dress." (silk, beautiful, long)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Complete the sentence with the correct pair: "_______ the bus _______ the train goes directly to the museum."
2. Write one sentence using *neither/nor* about two animals.

**Expected Answers:**
1. Neither the bus nor the train goes directly to the museum. (Or: Either the bus or the train goes directly to the museum. — accept either pair if the sentence makes logical sense.)
2. Answers vary. Example: "Neither the parrot nor the hamster escaped from its cage."

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 items correct with properly paired conjunctions and logical sentence meaning.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the concept of fixed pairs using the "lock and key" analogy
- Provide a reference card listing the pairs: either/or, neither/nor
- Have the student sort sentence cards into "either/or" and "neither/nor" categories
- Practice orally before returning to written exercises

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready to proceed to Lesson 2, which introduces both/and and not only/but also
- Encourage the student to look for correlative conjunctions in their independent reading

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Conjunction Hunt:** Find three examples of either/or or neither/nor in a book, magazine, or online article. Copy the sentences and underline the conjunction pairs.
- **Sentence Challenge:** Write a short dialogue between two characters that uses either/or at least twice and neither/nor at least twice.
- **Grammar Detective:** Look at a piece of your own writing and find a place where you could revise a sentence using a correlative conjunction. Rewrite it and explain why the revision improves the sentence.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Students commonly write "neither/or" instead of "neither/nor." Emphasize the fixed-pair rule repeatedly and post a reference chart.
- When using neither/nor with two subjects, subject-verb agreement follows the "nearest subject" rule. For example: "Neither the cats nor the dog *is* hungry" (dog is nearest, so singular verb). This is a subtlety worth mentioning but not belaboring at Grade 5.
- Some students may ask about "whether/or." Acknowledge it as another correlative conjunction pair that will appear later but is not the focus today.
- If students struggle with the concept of correlative conjunctions, start with physical manipulatives: write each word on an index card and have students physically match the pairs.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. Answers vary. Example: "Either we play tennis or we play volleyball after school."
2. Answers vary. Example: "I like neither broccoli nor mushrooms."
3. **Neither** the teacher **nor** the students wanted to cancel the field trip.
4. You can sit **either** at the front table **or** at the back table.
5. Corrected: "Either the dog **or** the cat is allowed on the couch."
6. Corrected: "Neither the sun **nor** the moon was visible."
7. "Neither Marcus nor Priya finished their homework." (Also accept: "Neither Marcus nor Priya finished his or her homework.")
8. "Either we will have tacos or we will have pasta for dinner." (Also accept: "We will have either tacos or pasta for dinner.")
9. Answers vary. Example: "This summer, I will visit either the beach or the mountains."
10. Answers vary. Example: "Neither math nor science is hard for me."

12. **beautiful, long, silk** dress (opinion, size, material)
