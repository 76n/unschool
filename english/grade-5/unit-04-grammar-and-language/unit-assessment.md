## Answer Key and Scoring Guide

### Section A: Correlative Conjunctions (L.5.1e)

**1.** Answer: **c) Both the cat and the dog like baths.**
- (a) is wrong: neither/or is an incorrect pair (should be neither/nor)
- (b) is wrong: either/nor is an incorrect pair (should be either/or)
- (d) is wrong: not only/and also is an incorrect pair (should be not only/but also)

**2.** Answer: **Whether** you study for the test **or** you review your notes, you should be well prepared.
- Also accept: **Either** ... **or** (if the student explains that the meaning slightly changes but the grammar is correct)

**3.** Answer: "Neither the principal **nor** the teachers knew about the surprise assembly."
- The error is "or" — it should be "nor" to complete the neither/nor pair.

**4.** Answer: **both** model volcanoes **and** homemade robots. OR **not only** model volcanoes **but also** homemade robots.
- Accept either correct pair. Deduct credit if the pair is mismatched.

**5.** Answer: **c) She likes not only to read but also to play soccer.**
- This is the only option with parallel structure: "to read" and "to play soccer" are both infinitive phrases.
- (a) "to read" / "playing soccer" — not parallel
- (b) "reading" / "playing soccer" — this is actually parallel in form but the placement of "not only" is awkward; accept if student selects this with justification
- (d) "reading" / "to play" — not parallel

**6.** Answers vary. Award 1 point if the sentence: uses neither/nor correctly, the pair is not mismatched, and the sentence is about two weather conditions.
- Example: "Neither the heavy rain nor the strong wind kept us from walking to school."

### Section B: Verb Tense Consistency (L.5.1d)

**7.** Answer: swims → **swam** ("The campers hiked to the lake and then swam to the other side.")

**8.** Answer: woke → **wakes** ("Every morning, the rooster crows at dawn and the farmer wakes up.")
- Base tense is present (signal: "every morning," "crows").

**9.** Answer: **c) We packed our lunches and walked to the park.**
- Both verbs are past tense — consistent.

**10.** Answer: **Appropriate.** The tense shift is appropriate because the time genuinely changes from "last year" (past) to "now" (present). The signal words "last year" and "now" indicate a real time change.
- Award 1 point for correctly identifying the shift as appropriate AND providing a brief explanation that mentions the time change.

**11.** Answer: stands → **stood**, hands → **handed**
- Corrected passage: "The students walked into the gymnasium for the assembly. The principal **stood** at the microphone and welcomed everyone. She announced the winners and **handed** out the trophies."
- Award 1 point only if BOTH errors are corrected. Award 0.5 if only one is found.

**12.** Answers vary. Award 1 point if: both sentences are about a past event, verb tenses are consistent (all past), and the sentences are grammatically correct.
- Example: "Last summer, my family drove to the beach for vacation. We built sandcastles and swam in the ocean every day."

### Section C: Punctuation in a Series (L.5.2a)

**13.** Answer: "The trail mix contained almonds**,** raisins**,** chocolate chips**,** and pretzels."
- Award 1 point for all three commas correctly placed, including the Oxford comma.

**14.** Answer: "Before the race**,** the runners stretched their legs**,** drank water**,** and adjusted their shoes."
- Note: The first comma is after the introductory phrase "Before the race." The remaining commas separate items in the series. Award 1 point for all commas correct.

**15.** Answer: **c) We need paint, brushes, and paper.**
- (a) has unnecessary commas before the first item and after "and"
- (b) has no commas at all
- (d) is missing the Oxford comma before "and"

**16.** Answer: "The debate team members are Olivia, from Lincoln Middle School; Tyler, from Washington Middle School; and Ava, from Jefferson Middle School."
- Semicolons are needed because each item contains an internal comma (name + school). Award 1 point for correct semicolons and commas.

**17.** Answer: Commas only. "My favorite desserts are brownies, ice cream cake, and apple pie."
- No internal commas exist within the items, so semicolons are not needed. Note: "ice cream cake" is a single item (a type of cake). Award 1 point for correct commas and for NOT using unnecessary semicolons.

**18.** Answers vary. Award 1 point if the sentence: lists four activities, uses commas between all items, and includes the Oxford comma before the conjunction.
- Example: "I enjoy reading, hiking, playing basketball, and baking cookies."

### Section D: Expanding, Combining, and Reducing Sentences (L.5.3a)

**19.** Answers vary. Award 1 point if the two sentences are combined into one grammatically correct sentence.
- Example: "The library has a new section filled with graphic novels."
- Also accept: "The library has a new section that is filled with graphic novels."

**20.** Answers vary. Award 1 point if all three actions are combined into one sentence with a series.
- Example: "The chef chopped the onions, sliced the peppers, and diced the tomatoes."

**21.** Answers vary. Award 1 point if the expanded sentence: adds at least three meaningful details, is grammatically correct, and the details are not repetitive or redundant.
- Example: "Early each morning, the bright red cardinal sang a cheerful melody from the highest branch of the maple tree outside my bedroom window."

**22.** Answers vary. Award 1 point if the sentence is shorter than the original and retains the same meaning.
- Example: "Because it was raining, the students stayed inside for recess."
- Also accept: "The students stayed inside for recess because of the rain."

**23.** Answers vary. Award 1 point if the sentence correctly uses an appositive.
- Example: "Dr. Martinez, a famous astronaut, spoke at the assembly."
- Also accept: "Dr. Martinez, who is a famous astronaut, spoke at the assembly." (relative clause rather than appositive — still credit-worthy as it combines the sentences effectively)

**24.** Answers vary. Award 1 point if the revised paragraph: is 2-3 sentences (not 8), combines the choppy sentences effectively, retains all the key information (big, beautiful park; pond; ducks swimming and quacking; watched and fed them), and is grammatically correct.
- Example: "The big, beautiful park had a peaceful pond where ducks swam and quacked happily. We watched them glide across the water and fed them pieces of bread."
