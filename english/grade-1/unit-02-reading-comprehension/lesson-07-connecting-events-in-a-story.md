# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 2, Lesson 7

## Topic
Connecting Events in a Story (Cause and Effect)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define cause as "what happened first" or "the reason" and effect as "what happened next" or "the result"
- Identify at least one cause-and-effect pair in a fiction passage
- Use the word "because" to connect a cause and an effect in a complete sentence

## Prerequisites
- Ability to ask and answer questions about a text using question words (Lessons 1-2)
- Ability to find key details in fiction (Lesson 3)
- Understanding of main topic and supporting details (Lessons 5-6)

## Materials Needed
- Printed or displayed copy of the passage "Max Forgets His Lunch"
- Cause and Effect T-chart (two columns: "Cause — Why did it happen?" and "Effect — What happened?")
- Sentence strips or paper strips for matching activity
- Pencils or crayons
- Whiteboard or chart paper and markers

## Standards Alignment
- **RL.1.1:** Ask and answer questions about key details in a text.
- **RI.1.3:** Describe the connection between two individuals, events, ideas, or pieces of information in a text.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge by connecting everyday experiences to the concept of cause and effect.

**Activity: Why Did That Happen?**

Say to students:
> "I want to tell you about something that happened to me. I left my window open last night. When I woke up, my papers were all over the floor! The wind blew them everywhere."

Ask: "Why were my papers on the floor?" (Because the wind blew them. / Because the window was open.)

Say:
> "Right! The window was open, so the wind blew my papers. One thing CAUSED another thing to happen. Today we are going to learn about CAUSE and EFFECT. A cause is WHY something happens. An effect is WHAT happens because of it."

Do one more quick example:
> "Think about this: It started to rain, so I opened my umbrella. What is the cause — the reason? (It rained.) What is the effect — what happened? (I opened my umbrella.)"

Have students give a thumbs up if they can think of a time something happened BECAUSE of something else. Call on 1-2 students to share.

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach cause and effect explicitly and model identifying cause-and-effect connections in a fiction passage.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Cause:** The reason something happens; what happens first that makes something else happen.
- **Effect:** What happens as a result; what happens because of the cause.
- **Because:** A word that connects an effect to its cause. "I was wet BECAUSE it rained."
- **Connection:** How two events or ideas are linked together.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Teach cause and effect (3 minutes)**

Draw a simple T-chart on the board:

| Cause (Why did it happen?) | Effect (What happened?) |
|---|---|

Say:
> "When we read stories, things happen for a reason. The CAUSE is the reason. The EFFECT is what happens because of the cause. We can use the word BECAUSE to connect them. Listen: 'Sam was hungry BECAUSE he did not eat breakfast.' The cause is: he did not eat breakfast. The effect is: Sam was hungry."

Write this example in the T-chart:
- Cause: Sam did not eat breakfast.
- Effect: Sam was hungry.

Say:
> "Here is a trick. If you hear the word BECAUSE, the cause comes AFTER because. 'Sam was hungry BECAUSE he did not eat breakfast.' What comes after because? 'He did not eat breakfast' — that is the cause!"

**Step 2: Read the passage aloud (3 minutes)**

Say:
> "Now let's read a story and look for cause and effect. Listen carefully for things that happen and WHY they happen."

Read the following passage aloud (display it for students to see):

> **Max Forgets His Lunch**
>
> It was a sunny morning. Max was in a big rush to get to school. He grabbed his backpack and ran out the door. But Max forgot to put his lunch bag in his backpack! At lunchtime, Max looked in his backpack. His lunch was not there. Max felt very sad. His friend Lily saw that Max had no food. Lily shared half of her sandwich with Max. Max smiled and said, "Thank you, Lily! You are a good friend." After school, Max put a note on the door so he would not forget his lunch again.

**Step 3: Model identifying cause and effect (4 minutes)**

Say:
> "Let's find cause and effect in this story. I will ask myself: 'What happened, and why did it happen?'"

> "First, I notice that Max forgot his lunch bag. Why did that happen? The story says he was in a big rush. So the CAUSE is: Max was in a big rush. The EFFECT is: He forgot his lunch bag. Let me write that on our chart."

Write on the T-chart:
- Cause: Max was in a big rush.
- Effect: He forgot his lunch bag.

> "Now I can use the word BECAUSE: 'Max forgot his lunch bag BECAUSE he was in a big rush.' See how BECAUSE connects them?"

### Worked Examples

**Worked Example 1: Why did Max feel sad?**

Say:
> "Let me find another cause and effect. I see that Max felt very sad. Why did he feel sad? Let me look back. The story says his lunch was not in his backpack. So the cause is: His lunch was not there. The effect is: Max felt sad."

Write on the T-chart:
- Cause: His lunch was not in his backpack.
- Effect: Max felt sad.

Say:
> "I can say: 'Max felt sad BECAUSE his lunch was not in his backpack.' The word BECAUSE helps me connect the cause and the effect."

**Worked Example 2: Why did Lily share her sandwich?**

Say:
> "Here is one more. Lily shared half of her sandwich with Max. Why did she do that? The story says she saw that Max had no food. So the cause is: Max had no food. The effect is: Lily shared her sandwich."

Write on the T-chart:
- Cause: Max had no food.
- Effect: Lily shared her sandwich.

Say:
> "I can say: 'Lily shared her sandwich BECAUSE Max had no food.' Now we have found three cause-and-effect pairs in this story! Cause and effect helps us understand WHY things happen in a story."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice identifying cause and effect with facilitator support.

**Activity: Cause and Effect Detectives**

Give each student a copy of the passage and a blank T-chart (Cause / Effect).

Say:
> "Now it is your turn to be a Cause and Effect Detective! I am going to read a part of the story, and I want you to help me find the cause and the effect."

**Prompt 1:** Re-read: "After school, Max put a note on the door so he would not forget his lunch again."

Ask: "What did Max do? (He put a note on the door.) Why did he do it? (So he would not forget his lunch again.) What is the cause?" Guide students to see: The cause is that Max forgot his lunch. The effect is that he put a note on the door.

Have students write this pair on their T-chart.

**Prompt 2:** Re-read: "Max smiled and said, 'Thank you, Lily!'"

Ask: "Why did Max smile? What caused him to smile?" (Lily shared her sandwich with him.)

Have students write this pair on their T-chart:
- Cause: Lily shared her sandwich.
- Effect: Max smiled and said thank you.

**Prompt 3:** Ask students to use the word "because" to say one cause-and-effect pair out loud. Provide the sentence frame on the board:

> "___ happened BECAUSE ___."

Call on 3-4 students to share their sentences.

**Facilitator Prompts:**
- "What happened in this part of the story?"
- "WHY did that happen? What caused it?"
- "Can you use the word BECAUSE to connect them?"
- "Point to the part of the story that tells you why."

**Supporting Struggling Students:**
- Use physical cause-and-effect cards: write causes on one color of card and effects on another. Have the student match them.
- Act it out: "Pretend you forgot your lunch. How would you feel? Why?"
- Simplify: Focus on just one cause-and-effect pair and practice saying it with "because" multiple times.

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently identify cause and effect and use "because" in their writing.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
Read (or listen to) each pair below. Draw a line to match the cause to the effect.

| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|
| It started to rain. | The flowers grew tall. |
| The sun came out. | We went inside. |
| The plant got lots of water. | The snow melted. |

Then write one sentence using "because":
> "___ BECAUSE ___."

**Stretch:**
Complete the matching activity above. Then go back to the story "Max Forgets His Lunch" and find one cause-and-effect pair that was NOT discussed in class. Write it on the T-chart and write a "because" sentence about it.

**Challenge:**
Complete the matching activity. Then write your own short cause-and-effect story (3-4 sentences) about a character who forgets something. Include at least one cause and one effect. Underline the cause and circle the effect.

### Interleaved Review
After completing the cause-and-effect activity, students answer one review question:
- "What is the main topic of the story 'Max Forgets His Lunch'? What is it mostly about?" (Review of Lesson 5: main topic — a boy who forgets his lunch and his friend helps him.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment.

**Exit Ticket:**

Listen to (or read) this sentence:
> "The cat knocked over the glass BECAUSE it jumped on the table."

1. What is the cause? (The cat jumped on the table.)
2. What is the effect? (The glass got knocked over.)
3. Fill in the blank: A cause tells us ___ something happened. (why)
4. Finish this sentence: "I wore my coat BECAUSE ___." (Accept any logical answer, e.g., "it was cold outside.")

**Mastery = 3 out of 4 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the warm-up examples and re-teach cause and effect using simple real-life situations.
- Use hand motions: push one hand forward (cause) and then show the other hand moving (effect) to physically demonstrate how one event leads to another.
- Practice with 2-3 very simple oral cause-and-effect sentences before returning to written work.
- Provide the sentence frame "This happened BECAUSE that happened" and fill it in together with simple scenarios.

### If Mastery Is Met
- The student is ready for Lesson 8, which applies connection-making skills to informational text.
- Encourage the student to notice cause and effect in stories they read or hear at home.

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Cause-and-Effect Chain:** Create a chain of events. Start with one cause and show how it leads to multiple effects: "Max was in a rush -> he forgot his lunch -> he felt sad -> Lily helped him -> he was happy -> he made a note for next time."
- **Draw It:** Students fold a paper in half. On the left side, they draw the cause. On the right side, they draw the effect. They write a "because" sentence under their drawing.
- **Story Retell:** Students retell the story of Max to a partner, making sure to explain WHY each event happened.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- **Pacing:** Cause and effect can be an abstract concept for first graders. Spend extra time on the warm-up real-life examples if students seem confused. Making the concept concrete with physical demonstrations (e.g., pushing a block and watching it fall) can be very effective.
- **Common Misconceptions:**
  - Students may confuse the order. Remind them: the cause is the REASON (it often happens first), and the effect is the RESULT (it often happens second).
  - Students may think "because" always comes in the middle of a sentence. Show them it can also start a sentence: "Because it rained, we went inside."
- **The Word "Because":** This is the anchor word for this lesson. Write it on a word wall card. Students at this age may spell it as "becuz" or "becus" — accept phonetic spelling and gently model the correct spelling.
- **Preparation:** Pre-make matching cards if using the physical matching variation for struggling students.
- **Connection to Standards:** This lesson addresses RL.1.1 (asking and answering questions about key details — specifically "why" questions) and lays groundwork for RI.1.3 (describing connections between events), which will be extended to informational text in Lesson 8.
- **Methodology Note:** The use of a T-chart graphic organizer provides a visual scaffold that makes the abstract relationship between cause and effect concrete and visible. See the Teaching Methodology Framework for more on graphic organizers as scaffolding tools.

## Answer Key

### Guided Practice — T-Chart Entries
Students should have at least two of the following on their T-charts:

| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|
| Max was in a big rush. | He forgot his lunch bag. |
| His lunch was not in his backpack. | Max felt sad. |
| Max had no food. | Lily shared her sandwich. |
| Lily shared her sandwich. | Max smiled and said thank you. |
| Max forgot his lunch. | He put a note on the door. |

### Independent Practice — Matching Activity

| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|
| It started to rain. | We went inside. |
| The sun came out. | The snow melted. |
| The plant got lots of water. | The flowers grew tall. |

**Sample "because" sentences:**
- "We went inside because it started to rain."
- "The snow melted because the sun came out."
- "The flowers grew tall because the plant got lots of water."

### Independent Practice — Stretch
Any cause-and-effect pair from the story not previously discussed is acceptable. For example:
- Cause: Max ran out the door in a rush. Effect: He forgot to put his lunch bag in his backpack.

### Interleaved Review
- Main topic: The story is mostly about a boy named Max who forgets his lunch and his friend Lily helps him. (Accept any reasonable version of this.)

### Exit Ticket
1. The cat jumped on the table.
2. The glass got knocked over.
3. why
4. Accept any logical completion (e.g., "it was cold," "it was snowing," "it was windy").
