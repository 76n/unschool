# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 2, Lesson 8

## Topic
Connecting Ideas in Informational Text

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify sequence words (first, then, next, last) in an informational text
- Describe the connections between ideas or steps in a process described in nonfiction
- Retell the steps of a process in the correct order using sequence words

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of main topic and supporting details (Lessons 5-6)
- Understanding of cause and effect in fiction (Lesson 7)
- Ability to find key details in informational text (Lesson 4)

## Materials Needed
- Printed or displayed copy of the passage "From Caterpillar to Butterfly"
- Sequence strip template (four numbered boxes in a row, or stacked vertically)
- Scissors and glue (for the cut-and-paste sequencing variation)
- Pencils or crayons
- Whiteboard or chart paper and markers
- Index cards or sentence strips with sequence words: FIRST, THEN, NEXT, LAST

## Standards Alignment
- **RI.1.2:** Identify the main topic and retell key details of a text.
- **RI.1.3:** Describe the connection between two individuals, events, ideas, or pieces of information in a text.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of sequence and order by connecting to daily routines.

**Activity: My Morning Steps**

Say to students:
> "Every morning, you do things in a certain ORDER. You do one thing FIRST, THEN you do something else, THEN something else. Let me tell you what I do in the morning."

Model:
> "FIRST, I wake up. THEN, I brush my teeth. NEXT, I eat breakfast. LAST, I leave for school."

Hold up or point to the sequence word cards (FIRST, THEN, NEXT, LAST) as you say each step.

Say:
> "Did you hear those special words? FIRST, THEN, NEXT, LAST. These words tell us the ORDER things happen. They help us know what comes before and what comes after."

Ask 1-2 students: "What do YOU do first in the morning? Then what?" Have students share using the words first, then, next.

Say:
> "Today we are going to read an informational text that tells us how something happens step by step. We will use sequence words to understand the connections between the ideas."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach how informational texts connect ideas through sequence and model identifying these connections.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Sequence:** The order in which things happen; first, second, third, and so on.
- **Sequence words:** Words that tell us the order: first, then, next, after, last, finally.
- **Process:** A set of steps that happen in order to make or do something.
- **Connection:** How ideas are linked or related to each other.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce idea connections in informational text (3 minutes)**

Say:
> "In Lesson 7, we learned about cause and effect in stories. One thing CAUSED another thing to happen. Informational texts also connect ideas, but sometimes in a different way. Sometimes informational texts tell us about a PROCESS — steps that happen in order. The ideas are connected because one step leads to the next step."

Write on the board:
> **Ways informational texts connect ideas:**
> 1. Sequence (order): First → Then → Next → Last
> 2. Cause and effect: This happened, so that happened

Say:
> "Today we will focus on SEQUENCE — the order of steps. When a text tells us how something happens or how to do something, it uses sequence. The steps are connected because you need one step before the next one can happen."

**Step 2: Read the passage aloud (3 minutes)**

Say:
> "Now I am going to read a passage about how a caterpillar becomes a butterfly. This is a real process that happens in nature! Listen for the ORDER of the steps. Listen for words like first, then, next, and last."

Read the following passage aloud (display it for students to see):

> **From Caterpillar to Butterfly**
>
> A butterfly goes through big changes as it grows. First, a mother butterfly lays tiny eggs on a leaf. Then, a small caterpillar hatches from the egg. The caterpillar eats lots of leaves and grows bigger and bigger. Next, the caterpillar makes a hard shell around its body called a chrysalis. Inside the chrysalis, the caterpillar changes in an amazing way. After some time, the chrysalis opens. Last, a beautiful butterfly comes out! It spreads its wings and flies away. This process is called metamorphosis.

**Step 3: Model identifying the sequence and connections (4 minutes)**

Say:
> "Let me find the steps in this process. I will look for the sequence words to help me."

Draw four large numbered boxes on the board (1, 2, 3, 4).

> "I see the word FIRST: 'First, a mother butterfly lays tiny eggs on a leaf.' This is step 1."

Write in box 1: "A mother butterfly lays eggs on a leaf."

> "I see the word THEN: 'Then, a small caterpillar hatches from the egg.' This is step 2. This step is connected to step 1 because the egg has to come BEFORE the caterpillar can hatch."

Write in box 2: "A caterpillar hatches from the egg."

> "Now I see NEXT: 'Next, the caterpillar makes a hard shell around its body called a chrysalis.' This is step 3. This step is connected to step 2 because the caterpillar has to grow before it can make a chrysalis."

Write in box 3: "The caterpillar makes a chrysalis."

> "And I see LAST: 'Last, a beautiful butterfly comes out!' This is step 4. This step is connected to step 3 because the butterfly changes inside the chrysalis before it comes out."

Write in box 4: "A butterfly comes out."

### Worked Examples

**Worked Example 1: Explaining a connection between two steps**

Say:
> "Let me explain the connection between step 2 and step 3. In step 2, the caterpillar hatches and eats lots of leaves. In step 3, it makes a chrysalis. These steps are connected because the caterpillar NEEDS to eat and grow big BEFORE it can make its chrysalis. It could not skip step 2 and go right to step 3! The eating and growing leads to the next step."

Write on the board: "The caterpillar eats and grows → Then it makes a chrysalis."

**Worked Example 2: Using sequence words to retell**

Say:
> "Now I will retell the process using my own words and sequence words: FIRST, the butterfly lays eggs. THEN, a caterpillar comes out and eats leaves. NEXT, the caterpillar makes a chrysalis. LAST, a butterfly comes out of the chrysalis. Each step connects to the next step because one must happen before the other can."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice identifying and ordering the sequence of ideas with facilitator support.

**Activity: Sequence Sort**

Give each student a copy of the passage and a sequence strip template (four boxes labeled 1, 2, 3, 4).

**Option A (Writing):** Students write one step in each box.
**Option B (Cut and Paste):** Give students pre-made sentence strips with the four steps. Students cut them out and glue them in the correct order.

Pre-made sentence strips for Option B:
- A mother butterfly lays eggs on a leaf.
- A caterpillar hatches from the egg and eats leaves.
- The caterpillar makes a chrysalis.
- A butterfly comes out and flies away.

Say:
> "Now it is your turn! Use the passage to fill in the four steps in order. Remember to look for the sequence words: first, then, next, last. They will help you find the right order."

Give students 4-5 minutes to work. Circulate and support.

**Facilitator Prompts:**
- "Can you find the word FIRST in the passage? What happens first?"
- "What comes after that? Look for the word THEN."
- "Point to the sequence word in this sentence."
- "Could step 3 happen before step 2? Why or why not?"
- "How are these two steps connected? Does one need to happen before the other?"

**Supporting Struggling Students:**
- Read the passage aloud again, pausing at each sequence word and asking: "What happens here?"
- Use fingers to count the steps physically: "Hold up one finger — step 1 is..."
- Focus on just two steps first and their connection: "What happens first? What happens next?"
- Let students draw pictures of each step instead of writing.

After 4-5 minutes, review as a group. Read the steps in order together. Ask:
> "Why do these steps need to go in this order? Could the butterfly come out before the caterpillar makes a chrysalis?" (No! The steps are connected — one leads to the next.)

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently identify sequence and connections in a new short text.

Read the following passage aloud, then have students work independently. Display the passage for students to reference.

> **How to Plant a Seed**
>
> Do you want to grow a plant? It is easy! First, get a small pot and fill it with dirt. Then, use your finger to make a little hole in the dirt. Next, drop a seed into the hole and cover it with dirt. Last, give the seed some water. Put the pot in a sunny spot. If you water it every day, a little plant will grow!

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
- Fill in the sequence strip (4 boxes) with the four steps to plant a seed.
- Circle the sequence words in the passage (first, then, next, last).

**Stretch:**
- Complete the sequence strip.
- Circle the sequence words.
- Answer this question: "Why do you need to make a hole BEFORE you drop in the seed? How are these steps connected?" Write one sentence to explain.

**Challenge:**
- Complete the sequence strip and circle the sequence words.
- Answer the connection question above.
- Write a short set of directions (3-4 steps) for something you know how to do (e.g., how to make a sandwich, how to brush your teeth). Use the sequence words: first, then, next, last.

### Interleaved Review
After completing the sequence activity, students answer one review question:
- "Look at the passage 'How to Plant a Seed.' What is the main topic? Name one supporting detail." (Review of Lessons 5-6.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Put these steps in order by writing 1, 2, 3:
   ___ A butterfly comes out.
   ___ A caterpillar hatches.
   ___ The caterpillar makes a chrysalis.

2. Which word tells us what comes at the start? (Circle one: LAST / FIRST / NEXT)

3. Why does the caterpillar need to eat and grow BEFORE it makes a chrysalis? (Accept: because it needs to be big enough / because eating comes first / because that is the order it happens.)

4. Name one sequence word. (Accept: first, then, next, after, last, finally.)

**Mastery = 3 out of 4 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the morning routine example from the warm-up. Practice putting 3 simple daily steps in order using sequence words.
- Use picture cards showing a familiar process (e.g., making a peanut butter sandwich) and have the student put them in order.
- Re-read the caterpillar passage together, pausing at each sequence word and physically holding up a finger for each step.
- Focus on the question: "Could this step happen before that step? Why or why not?" to build the concept of connected steps.

### If Mastery Is Met
- The student is ready for Lesson 9, which teaches comparing fiction and informational text.
- Encourage the student to look for sequence words in books they read at home and share what process or steps they found.

## Extension (Optional)
- **Draw the Life Cycle:** Students draw the four stages of a butterfly's life cycle and label each stage with a sequence word (first, then, next, last).
- **Act It Out:** Students act out the butterfly life cycle in groups. One student curls up as an egg, then pretends to munch leaves as a caterpillar, then holds very still inside a "chrysalis" (crouching with arms wrapped around), then spreads arms wide as a butterfly.
- **Find a Process:** Students look through a classroom informational book and find a page that describes a process or steps. They share the steps with a partner using sequence words.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- **Pacing:** Sequence is often more intuitive for first graders than cause and effect because they experience daily routines with clear order. Use this familiarity to your advantage during the warm-up and throughout the lesson.
- **Common Misconceptions:**
  - Students may think they can only find connections if the text uses the exact words "first, then, next, last." Explain that sometimes the order is shown just by the way sentences are written, even without those signal words.
  - Some students may struggle to explain WHY steps are connected. Use the prompt: "Could you do step 3 without doing step 2 first?" This helps them see the dependency.
- **Preparation:** Pre-cut sentence strips if using the cut-and-paste variation. Have glue sticks ready. Having sequence word cards posted on the board or word wall is helpful for reference.
- **Vocabulary Note:** The word "metamorphosis" appears in the passage. Tell students: "This is a big word that means a big change. You do not need to remember this word, but it is fun to say! It means the change from caterpillar to butterfly." Clap it out: met-a-MOR-pho-sis.
- **Connection to Standards:** This lesson directly addresses RI.1.3 (describing connections between ideas in a text). The focus on sequence builds toward the skill of retelling events or processes in order, which supports RI.1.2.
- **Methodology Note:** The cut-and-paste sequencing activity provides a kinesthetic scaffold for students who learn best through hands-on manipulation. See the Teaching Methodology Framework for more on multi-modal learning approaches.

4. A butterfly comes out and flies away.

### Independent Practice — "How to Plant a Seed"

**Sequence strip:**
1. Get a pot and fill it with dirt.
2. Make a little hole in the dirt.
3. Drop a seed in and cover it with dirt.
4. Give the seed water.

**Sequence words in the passage:** first, then, next, last

**Stretch — Connection question:**
Sample answer: "You need to make a hole first so the seed has a place to go. If you do not make a hole, the seed will sit on top of the dirt." (Accept any response that shows understanding that the hole needs to exist before the seed can go in it.)

**Challenge — Sample student-written directions:**
"How to Brush Your Teeth: First, put toothpaste on your brush. Then, brush your teeth up and down. Next, brush your tongue. Last, rinse your mouth with water." (Accept any set of steps with sequence words.)

### Interleaved Review
- **Main topic:** How to plant a seed (or growing a plant).
- **Supporting detail (any of the following):** You need a pot and dirt. You make a hole. You drop a seed in. You water it. You put it in a sunny spot.

### Exit Ticket
1. Correct order:
   - **1** — A caterpillar hatches.
   - **2** — The caterpillar makes a chrysalis.
   - **3** — A butterfly comes out.

2. FIRST

3. Accept: Because the caterpillar needs to eat and grow big first. / Because it has to be big before it can make a chrysalis. / Because that is the order it happens in nature.

4. Accept any one: first, then, next, after, last, finally.
