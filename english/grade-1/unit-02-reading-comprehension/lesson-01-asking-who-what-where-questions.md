# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 2, Lesson 1

## Topic
Asking Who, What, and Where Questions

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify the three question words: who, what, and where
- Ask who, what, and where questions about a short fiction passage
- Answer who, what, and where questions about a short fiction passage using evidence from the text

## Prerequisites
- Ability to decode Grade 1 connected text with at least 90% accuracy (Unit 1)
- Familiarity with the concepts of characters, setting, and events from Kindergarten
- Ability to read and track print left to right

## Materials Needed
- 3 index cards or strips of paper with the words WHO, WHAT, and WHERE written in large letters
- The reading passage "Mia's Lost Bunny" (provided below — print or write it for the student)
- Paper and pencil
- Crayons or colored pencils (optional, for illustrating)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about stories and build curiosity about asking questions.

**Activity: Question Game**
Say to the student: "Today we are going to learn about asking questions. A question is something you ask when you want to find something out. Questions end with a question mark — this little curly mark with a dot." Write a question mark on paper and show it.

"Let's play a game. I am going to tell you a very, very short story. Just one sentence. Then I will ask you some questions about it."

Say: "A boy named Sam ate a sandwich at school."

Ask:
- "Who ate the sandwich?" (Sam)
- "What did Sam eat?" (A sandwich)
- "Where did Sam eat it?" (At school)

After the student answers, say: "You just answered three questions! You used three special question words — who, what, and where. Those are the words we are going to learn about today."

*Facilitator note: If the student struggles to answer, that is okay. This is just an introduction. Rephrase if needed: "The sentence said 'A boy named Sam.' So who ate the sandwich? Sam did!"*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the three question words — who, what, and where — and what kind of information each one asks for.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Question:** Something you ask to find out information. Questions end with a question mark (?).
- **Who:** A question word that asks about a person or animal.
- **What:** A question word that asks about a thing, an action, or something that happened.
- **Where:** A question word that asks about a place.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the three question words.**
Hold up (or point to) each question word card as you teach it.

Say: "When we read a story, we want to understand it. One of the best ways to understand a story is to ask questions. Today we are going to learn three question words."

Hold up the WHO card.
"WHO asks about a person or an animal. When someone asks a 'who' question, the answer is always a person or an animal. Like: 'Who is your best friend?' The answer is a person."

Hold up the WHAT card.
"WHAT asks about a thing or something that happened. When someone asks a 'what' question, the answer is a thing or an event. Like: 'What is your favorite food?' The answer is a thing — maybe pizza or apples."

Hold up the WHERE card.
"WHERE asks about a place. When someone asks a 'where' question, the answer is always a place. Like: 'Where do you sleep?' The answer is a place — your bedroom."

**Step 2: Practice matching questions to question words.**
Say each question below. Ask the student to hold up (or point to) the correct question word card.

- "Who is your teacher?" (WHO — the answer is a person)
- "What color is the sky?" (WHAT — the answer is a thing)
- "Where do fish live?" (WHERE — the answer is a place)
- "Who has a fluffy tail — a dog or a fish?" (WHO — the answer is an animal)
- "What do you eat for breakfast?" (WHAT — the answer is a thing)
- "Where do you go to play outside?" (WHERE — the answer is a place)

**Step 3: Read the passage together.**
Say: "Now we are going to read a short story. After we read it, we are going to ask and answer who, what, and where questions about it. Let's read it together."

Read the following passage aloud with the student. You may take turns reading sentences or read it together in unison.

---

**Mia's Lost Bunny**

Mia had a small stuffed bunny. She loved that bunny a lot. One day, Mia went to the park with her dad. She set her bunny on a bench. Then she ran to the swings. When it was time to go, Mia looked for her bunny. It was not on the bench! Mia was sad. Her dad helped her look. They found the bunny under the bench in the grass.

---

After reading, say: "Good reading! Now let's ask some questions about this story."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1: A "who" question.**
Say: "Let's ask a 'who' question. Who is this story about?"

Model thinking aloud: "Let me look at the story. The first sentence says, 'Mia had a small stuffed bunny.' And the rest of the story is about Mia and what she did. So the answer is: This story is about Mia."

Write on paper:
- Question: Who is the story about?
- Answer: Mia.

Say: "See how the question starts with 'who' — and the answer is a person? That is how 'who' questions work."

**Example 2: A "where" question.**
Say: "Now let's ask a 'where' question. Where did Mia go?"

Model thinking aloud: "I need to look for a place in the story. The story says, 'Mia went to the park with her dad.' The park is a place. So the answer is: Mia went to the park."

Write on paper:
- Question: Where did Mia go?
- Answer: The park.

Say: "The question starts with 'where' — and the answer is a place. That is how 'where' questions work."

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice asking and answering who, what, and where questions about the passage with facilitator support.

**Activity: Question Detectives**
Say: "Now it is your turn to be a Question Detective! I will ask you some questions about the story, and you will figure out the answer by looking at the story."

**Round 1 — Facilitator asks, student answers (5 minutes):**

Ask these questions one at a time. After each question, prompt the student to look back at the text.

1. "What did Mia have?" (A small stuffed bunny.)
   - If the student struggles: "Look at the very first sentence. What did Mia have?"

2. "Who went to the park with Mia?" (Her dad.)
   - If the student struggles: "The story says Mia went to the park with someone. Read this sentence again: 'Mia went to the park with her dad.'"

3. "Where did Mia set her bunny?" (On a bench.)
   - If the student struggles: "Find the sentence that tells where Mia put her bunny. It says 'She set her bunny on a ___.'"

4. "What did Mia do after she set her bunny down?" (She ran to the swings.)
   - If the student struggles: "Read the sentence right after the bench sentence. What did Mia do?"

5. "Where did they find the bunny?" (Under the bench in the grass.)
   - If the student struggles: "Look at the last sentence. Where was the bunny?"

For each answer, have the student point to the part of the text that gives the answer. Say: "Good! Show me where in the story you found that answer." This builds the habit of going back to the text.

**Round 2 — Student asks, facilitator answers (5 minutes):**

Say: "Now you get to be the teacher! Use the question word cards to help you. Ask ME a who, what, or where question about the story. Then I will answer it."

Help the student form questions. If the student struggles to create a question, offer a sentence starter:
- "Who ___?"
- "What ___?"
- "Where ___?"

For example, the student might ask:
- "Who helped Mia look?" (Her dad.)
- "What was Mia looking for?" (Her bunny.)
- "Where did Mia run to?" (The swings.)

Praise the student for each question: "That was a great 'who' question!" or "Excellent 'where' question — and you used the text to make it!"

*Facilitator note: Some students find it much harder to ASK questions than to ANSWER them. This is normal. If the student cannot form a question, give them a complete question and have them read it to you. The goal is familiarity with the question words, not perfection in question formation.*

---

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student independently answers who, what, and where questions in writing (or orally if writing is a barrier).

### Difficulty Tiers

**★ (On-level):**
Write or dictate the answer to each question about the story "Mia's Lost Bunny."

1. Who lost a toy? _______________
2. What toy did Mia lose? _______________
3. Where did Mia and her dad go? _______________
4. What happened to the bunny? (It was lost / It was not on the bench.)

**★★ (Stretch):**
Answer the On-level questions PLUS these:

5. Where did Mia find the bunny? _______________
6. Who helped Mia? _______________
7. Write your own "what" question about the story: _______________

**★★★ (Challenge):**
Answer all the above questions PLUS:

8. Write one "who" question, one "what" question, and one "where" question about the story. Then write the answer to each one.
9. Draw a picture of your favorite part of the story. Write one sentence about your picture using the word "who," "what," or "where."

### Interleaved Review
After finishing the comprehension questions, have the student complete these brief review items:
- Read these words aloud: bench, park, stuffed, bunny, grass (decoding review from Unit 1)
- "What vowel sound do you hear in the word 'lost'?" (/o/ — short vowel review from Unit 1, Lesson 1)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment to determine if the student understands who, what, and where questions.

**Exit Ticket:**

Read this sentence to the student (or have them read it): "A cat sat on a mat in the kitchen."

1. Ask a "who" question about this sentence. (Acceptable: "Who sat on the mat?" or similar)
2. Ask a "what" question about this sentence. (Acceptable: "What did the cat sit on?" or similar)
3. Ask a "where" question about this sentence. (Acceptable: "Where was the mat?" or "Where did the cat sit?" or similar)
4. Answer this question: "Who sat on the mat?" (A cat.)

**Mastery = Student correctly answers item 4 AND produces at least 2 of 3 questions (items 1-3) using the correct question word.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the three question words using the cards. Point to each card and re-teach what kind of answer each one expects: who = person/animal, what = thing/event, where = place.
- Practice with 3-4 additional simple sentences (e.g., "Tom played ball at the beach." "A bird flew to the tree."). Ask one who, one what, and one where question for each.
- Repeat this lesson with a different short passage in the next session before moving to Lesson 2.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 2: Asking When, Why, and How Questions. Continue to reinforce who, what, and where questions in warm-ups for future lessons. Encourage the student to ask these questions during any reading throughout the day.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**
- **Question Word Hunt:** Give the student a picture book or a page from any text. Challenge them to write (or say) one who question, one what question, and one where question about it. Then answer their own questions.
- **Story Swap:** The student tells YOU a short story (real or made up). Then you ask who, what, and where questions about it — and the student answers. This reverses the roles and deepens understanding.
- **Question Word Sort:** Write 6 questions on strips of paper (2 who, 2 what, 2 where). Mix them up. The student sorts them under the correct question word card.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pedagogical context:** This lesson addresses standard RL.1.1 — asking and answering questions about key details in a text. The ability to formulate and answer basic comprehension questions is the foundation for all higher-order reading skills. Students who learn to ask questions as they read become active, engaged readers rather than passive decoders.

**Why start with who, what, and where:** These three question words have concrete, text-explicit answers. "Who" is always a name or character. "What" is always a thing or event. "Where" is always a place. The student can point to the answer in the text. This makes them easier than when, why, and how (which are introduced in Lesson 2 and often require inference).

**Pacing notes:** This lesson is designed for approximately 35 minutes. If the student grasps the question words quickly, the Guided Practice Round 2 (student asks questions) can be shortened and time can be spent on the Extension activities. If the student needs more time, prioritize Round 1 (answering questions) and the Exit Ticket — question-asking is a skill that will continue to develop across the unit.

**Common issues:**
- Students may answer questions without looking at the text. Always redirect: "That might be right — but let's check. Can you find it in the story?"
- Some students will give one-word answers. Encourage full sentences: "Can you say it in a whole sentence? 'Mia went to the _____.'"
- The word "what" can be confusing because it asks about many different things (objects, actions, events). If the student is confused, simplify: "'What' asks about things and stuff that happens."

**Connection to the methodology framework:** This lesson follows the I Do / We Do / You Do gradual release model with explicit vocabulary instruction, worked examples, and tiered independent practice as defined in the [Teaching Methodology Framework](../../../methodology/teaching-methodology-framework.md).

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice — Round 1:**
1. What did Mia have? — A small stuffed bunny.
2. Who went to the park with Mia? — Her dad.
3. Where did Mia set her bunny? — On a bench.
4. What did Mia do after she set her bunny down? — She ran to the swings.
5. Where did they find the bunny? — Under the bench in the grass.

**Independent Practice — On-level (★):**
1. Who lost a toy? — Mia.
2. What toy did Mia lose? — A stuffed bunny (or a small stuffed bunny).
3. Where did Mia and her dad go? — The park.
4. What happened to the bunny? — It was lost. / It was not on the bench. / Mia could not find it.

**Independent Practice — Stretch (★★):**
5. Where did Mia find the bunny? — Under the bench in the grass.
6. Who helped Mia? — Her dad.
7. (Student-generated "what" question — answers will vary. Accept any reasonable question that starts with "what" and relates to the passage.)

**Independent Practice — Challenge (★★★):**
8. (Student-generated questions — answers will vary. Check that each question uses the correct question word and the answer is supported by the text.)
9. (Student drawing and sentence — accept any response that connects to the story and attempts to use a question word or relates to a who/what/where concept.)

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Acceptable "who" question: "Who sat on the mat?" (Answer: A cat.)
2. Acceptable "what" question: "What did the cat sit on?" (Answer: A mat.)
3. Acceptable "where" question: "Where did the cat sit?" or "Where was the mat?" (Answer: In the kitchen. / On a mat in the kitchen.)
4. Who sat on the mat? — A cat.

**Interleaved Review:**
- Decoding: bench, park, stuffed, bunny, grass (student reads aloud — check for accuracy)
- Vowel sound in "lost": /o/ (short o)
