# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 3, Lesson 3

## Topic
Opinion Writing — Introduction (Stating an Opinion with "I think... because...")

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define an opinion as something you think or believe that not everyone agrees with
- Write an opinion sentence using the frame "I think ___ because ___"
- Distinguish between an opinion and a fact

## Prerequisites
- Ability to name three types of writing: opinion, informative, narrative (Lesson 1)
- Ability to write a complete sentence with a capital letter and end punctuation (Lesson 2)

## Materials Needed
- Chart paper or whiteboard
- The "Three Types of Writing" anchor chart from Lesson 1
- Pencil and lined paper
- Crayons for illustrating
- 10 index cards with statements written on them (see Guided Practice)
- Red crayon or marker (for marking opinions)

**Prepare before the lesson:**
Write each statement on a separate index card:
1. "Pizza is the best food." (opinion)
2. "Pizza has cheese on it." (fact)
3. "Dogs are better than cats." (opinion)
4. "Dogs have four legs." (fact)
5. "Summer is the most fun season." (opinion)
6. "Summer comes after spring." (fact)
7. "Red is the prettiest color." (opinion)
8. "A stop sign is red." (fact)
9. "Recess is the best part of school." (opinion)
10. "Recess is a time to play outside." (fact)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Get the student thinking about their personal preferences and opinions.

**Activity: This or That**
Play a quick "this or that" game. Ask the student to choose and explain briefly:

- "Cats or dogs?" (Student picks one.)
- "Summer or winter?"
- "Apples or bananas?"
- "Playing inside or playing outside?"
- "Morning or nighttime?"

After each choice, ask: "Why?" Listen to their reason.

Then say: "You just told me a bunch of OPINIONS! An opinion is what you think. And you gave me reasons for your opinions. Today we are going to learn how to write those opinions down so other people can read them."

*Facilitator note: This warm-up generates enthusiasm and shows the student that they already have lots of opinions worth sharing.*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the student how to identify and write an opinion statement with a reason.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Opinion:** What you think, believe, or feel about something. Not everyone will agree.
- **Fact:** Something that is true and can be checked. Everyone agrees because it can be proven.
- **Reason:** The "why" behind your opinion — why you think what you think.
- **Opinion writing:** Writing that shares what you think and tells the reader WHY you think it.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Review what an opinion is.**
Point to the Opinion column on the anchor chart from Lesson 1. "Remember — opinion writing tells what you THINK. Let me show you the difference between an opinion and a fact."

Write on chart paper:
- "Ice cream is cold." — "Can we check this? Yes. Everyone agrees. This is a FACT."
- "Ice cream is the best dessert." — "Does everyone agree? No! Some people like cake better. This is an OPINION."

"The trick is: can people disagree? If yes, it is an opinion. If everyone agrees because you can prove it, it is a fact."

**Step 2: Introduce the opinion sentence frame.**
Write on chart paper in large letters:

**I think ___ because ___.**

"This is your secret writing tool for opinion writing. You fill in two blanks:"
- "After 'I think,' you write your opinion — what you believe."
- "After 'because,' you write your REASON — why you believe it."

**Step 3: Model writing an opinion sentence.**
"Watch me use the frame. My favorite animal is a penguin. Let me write my opinion."

Think aloud as you write on chart paper:
"I think penguins are the best animals because they are funny when they waddle."

Point to each part:
- "I think" — signals this is my opinion
- "penguins are the best animals" — my opinion
- "because" — here comes the reason
- "they are funny when they waddle" — my reason

"Let me do the four-finger check from Lesson 2: Subject? Yes — 'I.' Verb? Yes — 'think.' Capital letter? Yes — the I. End punctuation? Yes — period. Complete sentence!"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Opinion about a food.
- "I think apples are the best fruit because they are sweet and crunchy."
- Opinion: apples are the best fruit
- Reason: they are sweet and crunchy

**Example 2:** Opinion about an activity.
- "I think swimming is the most fun because I like splashing in the water."
- Opinion: swimming is the most fun
- Reason: I like splashing in the water

**Example 3 (student-generated):**
Ask the student: "What is your favorite game to play?" Wait for their answer. Then say: "Now let's turn that into an opinion sentence together." Guide them to fill in the frame:
- "I think ___ is the best game because ___."

Write their sentence on the chart paper together.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice sorting opinions from facts, then write opinion sentences with support.

**Activity 1: Fact or Opinion? (5 minutes)**
Spread out the 10 prepared index cards face up. Create two labels: "OPINION" and "FACT."

Read each card aloud with the student. For each one, ask: "Could someone disagree with this, or does everyone agree because it is true?"

Have the student sort each card under the correct label.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student places a card incorrectly, try this test: "If I said the opposite, would that make sense? Let me try: 'Pizza does NOT have cheese on it.' That does not make sense — so it must be a fact. 'Pizza is NOT the best food.' That makes sense — someone could think that. So it is an opinion."*

**Activity 2: Write Two Opinion Sentences Together (5 minutes)**
Help the student write two opinion sentences using the frame. Offer these topics, or let the student choose:

- Favorite color: "I think ___ is the best color because ___."
- Favorite season: "I think ___ is the best season because ___."
- Favorite book or show: "I think ___ is the best [book/show] because ___."

The student says the sentence aloud first, then writes it. You help with spelling only if the student asks or gets stuck on a high-frequency word. Accept invented spelling.

After each sentence, do the four-finger check together.

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student writes their own opinion sentences independently.

**Activity: My Opinion Page**
The student picks two topics from the list below (or thinks of their own) and writes one opinion sentence for each, using the "I think ___ because ___" frame.

Topics to choose from:
- The best animal
- The best food for lunch
- The best thing to do on a rainy day
- The best toy or game
- The best place to go

The student writes the sentence, then draws a picture to go with each opinion.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level (2 opinion sentences):**
Write two opinion sentences using the "I think ___ because ___" frame. Draw a picture for each.

**Stretch (3 opinion sentences):**
Write three opinion sentences. At least one must use a different starter instead of "I think" — try "My favorite ___ is ___ because ___" or "I believe ___ because ___."

**Challenge (3 opinion sentences + fact comparison):**
Write three opinion sentences. Then, for one of them, write a FACT about the same topic underneath. Example:
- Opinion: "I think cats are the best pets because they are soft."
- Fact: "Cats can see in the dark."

### Interleaved Review
- "Read your sentences back to me. Did you do the four-finger check?" (Lesson 2 review — complete sentences)
- "Point to the word 'because' in your sentence. How many syllables does 'because' have?" (Phonics/syllable review — 2 syllables: be-cause)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can write an opinion with a reason and distinguish opinion from fact.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What is an opinion?" (Answer: What you think or believe; something not everyone agrees with)
2. "Is this an opinion or a fact? 'Bananas are yellow.'" (Answer: Fact)
3. "Is this an opinion or a fact? 'Bananas are the yummiest fruit.'" (Answer: Opinion)
4. "Tell me an opinion sentence about school using 'I think... because...'" (Answer: Any opinion about school with a reason, e.g., "I think art class is the best because I like to paint.")

**Mastery = Student correctly answers at least 3 of 4 items, including producing an opinion sentence with a reason (#4).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the fact vs. opinion sorting. Practice with 5 more pairs.
- If the student can identify opinions but cannot write one, use dictation: the student says the sentence and you write it, then the student copies it.
- Provide the sentence frame on a card that the student keeps at their writing spot for reference.
- Repeat this lesson next session before moving to Lesson 4.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Opinion Writing — Adding Reasons and a Closing). Save the student's opinion writing from today in their writing folder.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who write opinion sentences easily:**
- Write an opinion sentence about a book they read in Unit 2. "I think [book title] is a good book because ___."
- Interview a family member: "What is your favorite ___? Why?" Write down their opinion using the frame.
- Play "Convince Me": the student writes an opinion and reads it aloud, trying to convince you to agree. You playfully say, "I'm not sure — give me another reason!" They add more reasons.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The opinion-fact distinction is the conceptual heart of this lesson.** First graders often believe their opinions are facts because their opinions feel true to them. This is completely normal and age-appropriate. Be patient. Use lots of examples. The key question is always: "Could someone disagree?"

**The "I think... because..." frame is deliberately formulaic.** At this stage, the student needs a reliable structure to lean on. Creativity and variety come later. For now, the frame gives the student confidence that they CAN write an opinion piece. It removes the "what do I write?" paralysis.

**Accept invented spelling throughout.** If the student writes "I think dogs ar the best becuz they ar fun," that is a fully successful opinion sentence. The opinion is clear, the reason is given, and the sentence has a capital and a period. Mission accomplished.

**If the student wants to write about something controversial or silly,** let them. "I think homework is bad because it takes too long" is a perfectly valid opinion sentence. So is "I think pickles are gross because they are squishy." The goal is to get the student writing opinions with reasons, not to police the content.

**Save all writing from this lesson.** The student may choose one of these pieces to revise in Lesson 9 or share in Lesson 10.
