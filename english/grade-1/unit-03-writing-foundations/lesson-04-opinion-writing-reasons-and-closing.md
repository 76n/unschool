# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 3, Lesson 4

## Topic
Opinion Writing — Adding Reasons and a Closing

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Write an opinion piece with three parts: an opinion statement, at least one reason, and a closing sentence
- Use the word "because" to connect an opinion to a reason
- Write a closing sentence that restates or wraps up the opinion

## Prerequisites
- Ability to write an opinion sentence using "I think ___ because ___" (Lesson 3)
- Ability to distinguish between opinions and facts (Lesson 3)
- Ability to write complete sentences with capital letters and end punctuation (Lesson 2)

## Materials Needed
- Chart paper or whiteboard
- Pencil and lined paper
- The "Three Types of Writing" anchor chart from Lesson 1
- The student's opinion sentences from Lesson 3 (in their writing folder)
- Crayons for illustrating
- 3 different colored markers (for highlighting the three parts of an opinion piece)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review opinion sentences from Lesson 3 and activate thinking about adding more detail.

**Activity: Read and Expand**
Have the student pull out one of their opinion sentences from Lesson 3. Read it aloud together.

Then ask: "This is a great opinion sentence. But what if someone reads it and says, 'I want to know more about WHY you think that.' How could we add more?"

Listen to the student's ideas. Then say: "Today we are going to turn one opinion sentence into a whole opinion piece — with three parts. By the end of today, you will write like a real author!"

*Facilitator note: Connecting to their own prior work shows continuity and makes the task feel manageable — they are building on something they already did, not starting from scratch.*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the three-part structure of an opinion piece and model writing one.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Opinion piece:** A short piece of writing that shares your opinion, gives reasons, and has a closing.
- **Reason:** A "because" statement that tells the reader WHY you have your opinion.
- **Closing sentence:** The last sentence of a piece of writing that wraps it up — like saying goodbye to the reader.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the three-part structure.**
Create an anchor chart titled "Opinion Writing" with three boxes:

| Part | What It Does | Sentence Starters |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Opinion statement | Tells the reader what you think | "I think..." "I believe..." "My favorite... is..." |
| 2. Reason(s) | Tells the reader WHY you think it | "I think this because..." "One reason is..." "Also..." |
| 3. Closing sentence | Wraps it up — reminds the reader of your opinion | "That is why I think..." "This is why... is the best." "Everyone should try..." |

"An opinion piece is like a little sandwich. The bread on top is your opinion. The filling in the middle is your reasons. The bread on the bottom is your closing."

**Step 2: Model writing a full opinion piece.**
Say: "I am going to write an opinion piece about my favorite season. Watch me."

Think aloud and write on chart paper, one part at a time:

*Part 1 — Opinion statement:*
"I think fall is the best season."

"That is my opinion. Now I need to tell the reader WHY."

*Part 2 — Reasons:*
"I think this because the leaves turn pretty colors. Also, I get to jump in leaf piles."

"I gave TWO reasons! The reader now knows why I think fall is the best."

*Part 3 — Closing sentence:*
"That is why fall is my favorite season."

"My closing sentence wraps it up. It reminds the reader of my opinion one more time."

Read the whole piece aloud together:
> "I think fall is the best season. I think this because the leaves turn pretty colors. Also, I get to jump in leaf piles. That is why fall is my favorite season."

**Step 3: Color-code the three parts.**
Use three different colored markers to underline each part:
- Color 1: Opinion statement
- Color 2: Reasons
- Color 3: Closing sentence

"See? Three parts — opinion, reasons, closing. Every opinion piece has all three."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1: Opinion piece about a food.**
> I think grapes are the best snack. I think this because they are sweet and juicy. Also, they are easy to eat. That is why grapes are the best snack.

- Opinion: grapes are the best snack
- Reason 1: sweet and juicy
- Reason 2: easy to eat
- Closing: restates the opinion

**Example 2: Opinion piece about an activity.**
> I believe riding bikes is the most fun thing to do. One reason is that you go really fast. Also, you can ride with your friends. This is why riding bikes is the best.

- Opinion: riding bikes is the most fun
- Reason 1: you go really fast
- Reason 2: ride with friends
- Closing: restates the opinion

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Co-write an opinion piece with the student.

**Activity: Write One Together**
Choose a topic with the student. Offer these options:
- The best pet
- The best thing to do at recess
- The best bedtime snack

Say: "Let's write this together. I will help you, but your ideas go on the paper."

**Step 1:** "What is your opinion about [topic]?" The student says their opinion. Write the opinion statement together on paper. Check: does it start with "I think" or a similar opener? Capital letter? Period?

**Step 2:** "Now, WHY do you think that? Give me a reason." Write the reason sentence. Then ask: "Can you think of one MORE reason?" Write the second reason. Use "Also" or "Another reason is" to start the second reason.

**Step 3:** "Now let's wrap it up. How should we end this piece? Let's remind the reader of our opinion." Write the closing sentence. Model if needed: "That is why I think ___ is ___."

Read the whole piece aloud together. Count the parts on three fingers: "Opinion — reasons — closing. We did it!"

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles with reasons, ask concrete questions: "What does it look like? How does it make you feel? What can you do with it?" These prompts help generate reasons.*

*Facilitator prompt: If the student gives a reason that is actually another opinion ("because it is the best"), gently redirect: "That tells me you like it, but WHY do you like it? What ABOUT it do you like?"*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student writes their own complete opinion piece.

**Activity: My Opinion Piece**
The student chooses a topic and writes a full opinion piece with all three parts: opinion statement, at least one reason, and a closing sentence.

Topics to choose from (or the student's own topic):
- The best animal in the world
- The best thing about [their name]'s family
- The best day of the week
- The best place to visit

The student may draw a picture to go with their writing.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level (3 sentences):**
Write an opinion piece with exactly 3 sentences: one opinion, one reason, one closing. Use the sentence starters from the anchor chart.

Example:
> I think dogs are the best pets. I think this because they play with you. That is why dogs are the best.

**Stretch (4 sentences):**
Write an opinion piece with 4 sentences: one opinion, TWO reasons, one closing. Use "Also" to start the second reason.

Example:
> I think Saturday is the best day. I think this because there is no school. Also, I can play all day. That is why Saturday is the best day.

**Challenge (5+ sentences):**
Write an opinion piece with at least 5 sentences: one opinion, TWO or more reasons with detail, one closing. Add a detail to at least one reason.

Example:
> I believe the park is the best place to visit. One reason is that there are big slides and swings. I always go on the swings first because I can go so high. Also, there is a pond with ducks. This is why the park is my favorite place.

### Interleaved Review
- "Is your opinion piece an opinion, informative, or narrative?" (Lesson 1 review — it is opinion!)
- "Do the four-finger check on each of your sentences." (Lesson 2 review — complete sentences)
- "Read your piece aloud. Does it make sense?" (Unit 2 comprehension skill — monitoring meaning)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can write a structured opinion piece.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What are the three parts of an opinion piece?" (Answer: Opinion statement, reason(s), closing sentence)
2. "Listen to this opinion piece. What part is missing? 'I think cats are great. They are soft and they purr.'" (Answer: Missing a closing sentence)
3. "Can you add a closing sentence to fix it?" (Answer: Any appropriate closing, e.g., "That is why I think cats are great.")

**Mastery = Student names all three parts AND identifies and fixes the missing part.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Revisit the anchor chart. Point to each part and have the student name it.
- Read the model opinion piece aloud together and have the student point to each part.
- Co-write one more opinion piece together with extra scaffolding: you write the frame, the student fills in the blanks.
- Repeat this lesson next session before moving to Lesson 5.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 5 (Informative Writing — Introduction). Save the student's opinion piece in their writing folder. They may choose this piece for revision in Lesson 9 or sharing in Lesson 10.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who write opinion pieces easily:**
- Write a second opinion piece on a DIFFERENT topic.
- Write an opinion piece trying to convince a family member of something. Read it to them and see if it works!
- Create a "Top 3" list — write one opinion sentence with a reason for each of the student's top 3 favorites in any category (top 3 foods, top 3 animals, etc.).

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson completes the opinion writing mini-unit.** The student now has the tools to produce a basic opinion piece. It will not be polished — and that is the point. The goal is structure, not perfection.

**The most common struggle in this lesson** is the closing sentence. Students often do not understand why they need one — they feel like the writing is "done" after giving reasons. Explain it like this: "A closing sentence is like waving goodbye. You do not just walk away from someone — you say goodbye first. Your closing sentence says goodbye to the reader."

**Another common issue: repeating the opinion as the reason.** "I think pizza is the best because it is the best." Coach the student to think about specific details: "What does pizza TASTE like? What do you like about EATING it? What is ON the pizza that you love?" Sensory and concrete prompts work well for generating real reasons.

**The writing folder is important.** By the end of this unit, the student will have at least one opinion piece, one informative piece, and one narrative. In Lesson 10, they will choose their favorite to share. Keeping all pieces in one folder makes this easy.

**Accept invented spelling.** "I thik dogs ar the best becuz they ar luveng" is a beautiful opinion piece with a clear opinion, a reason (loving), and proper sentence structure. Do not let spelling concerns overshadow the real achievement.

## Answer Key

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Opinion statement, reason(s), closing sentence
2. Missing a closing sentence
3. Any appropriate closing, e.g., "That is why I think cats are great." or "This is why cats are the best."
