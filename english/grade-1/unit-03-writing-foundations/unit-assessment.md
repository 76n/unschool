# Unit Assessment — Grade 1 ELA, Unit 3: Writing Foundations

**Grade:** 1
**Unit:** 3 — Writing Foundations
**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** One-on-one with facilitator (oral responses and writing samples)
**Estimated Duration:** 30-40 minutes (may be split into two sessions if needed)
**Materials:** Pencil, lined paper (at least 4 sheets), eraser, crayons (optional for illustrations), the Three Types of Writing anchor chart (remove during assessment unless student is at Developing level)

---

## Assessment Overview

This assessment covers all Unit 3 learning objectives:

| Section | Skill Assessed | Lesson Source | Items |
|---------|---------------|---------------|-------|
| A | Identifying writing types (opinion, informative, narrative) | Lesson 1 | 4 |
| B | Writing complete sentences (subject, verb, capitals, end marks) | Lesson 2 | 5 |
| C | Opinion writing (opinion, reason, closing) | Lessons 3-4 | 5 |
| D | Informative writing (topic sentence, facts with details, closing) | Lessons 5-6 | 5 |
| E | Narrative writing (beginning, middle, end with sequence words) | Lessons 7-8 | 4 |
| F | Revision (identifying and fixing errors, adding details) | Lesson 9 | 2 |
| -- | **Total** | -- | **25** |

**Mastery threshold:** 85% overall (21 of 25 points) AND at least 1 correct item in each section.

---

## Administration Instructions

This assessment includes both oral questions and writing tasks. For the oral sections (A and parts of B), read each question aloud and record the student's response. For the writing sections (C, D, E, and F), the student writes on lined paper while you observe.

There are no time limits on individual items. If the student is stuck on a writing task for more than 3 minutes, say: "That's okay. Do your best and let's move on."

Accept invented spelling throughout. Spelling accuracy is NOT assessed in this unit. Evaluate the writing for structure, content, and basic conventions (capitals and end punctuation) only.

Before beginning, say: "Today I want to see how much you have learned about writing. You will answer some questions and do some writing. There are no tricks — just show me what you know. You can take your time."

Provide the student with sharpened pencils, an eraser, and lined paper. Allow the student to draw illustrations only after all writing is complete, if time permits.

If the student becomes fatigued, take a short break or split the assessment across two sessions (Sections A-C in session one, Sections D-F in session two).

## Section B: Writing Complete Sentences (Lesson 2)

This section combines oral questions and a writing task.

**B1.** (Oral) "What two parts does every sentence need?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| A who/what (subject) AND a does/is (verb) — or equivalent explanation | 1 point |
| Names only one part or gives an unclear answer | 0.5 points |
| Cannot name either part | 0 points |

**B2.** (Oral) "What does every sentence start with?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| A capital letter | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**B3.** (Oral) "What goes at the end of every sentence?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| A period, question mark, or exclamation point (names at least one) | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**B4.** (Written) Write this on a piece of paper and show it to the student: "my bird sings songs"

Ask: "Is this a complete sentence? What needs to be fixed?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Identifies that it needs a capital M and a period (fixes both) | 1 point |
| Fixes one of the two errors | 0.5 points |
| Cannot identify any errors | 0 points |

**B5.** (Written) Write this and show it to the student: "Runs very fast."

Ask: "Is this a complete sentence? If not, fix it by writing a complete sentence."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Identifies that it is missing a subject/who and writes a complete sentence (e.g., "The dog runs very fast.") | 1 point |
| Identifies the problem but cannot fix it | 0.5 points |
| Says it is a complete sentence | 0 points |

## Section C: Opinion Writing (Lessons 3-4)

**C1.** (Oral) "What is an opinion?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| What you think or believe / something not everyone agrees with / your idea about something | 1 point |
| Partially correct (e.g., "What you like") | 0.5 points |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**C2.** (Oral) "Is this a fact or an opinion? 'Strawberries are red.'"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Fact | 1 point |
| Incorrect | 0 points |

**C3.** (Oral) "Is this a fact or an opinion? 'Strawberries are the most delicious fruit.'"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Opinion | 1 point |
| Incorrect | 0 points |

**C4.** (Written) "Write an opinion piece about your favorite animal. Remember to include: your opinion, a reason, and a closing sentence."

Give the student lined paper. Allow 5-7 minutes.

Score using this rubric:

| Component | Score |
|-----------|-------|
| Includes a clear opinion statement (e.g., "I think ___ is the best...") | 0 or 0.5 or 1 |
| Includes at least one reason with "because" or equivalent | 0 or 0.5 or 1 |

**Scoring detail for C4:**

| Opinion statement | Score |
|---|---|
| Clear opinion with "I think" / "I believe" / "My favorite" or similar | 1 point |
| Opinion is present but unclear or blended with reason | 0.5 points |
| No opinion statement | 0 points |

| Reason and closing | Score |
|---|---|
| At least one reason AND a closing sentence | 1 point |
| Has a reason but no closing, OR has a closing but no real reason | 0.5 points |
| No reason and no closing | 0 points |

*Note: Do NOT deduct points for spelling. Evaluate structure only. An opinion piece that reads "I thik dogs ar the best becuz they ar nice. That is wy I lik dogs." earns full marks — opinion stated, reason given, closing present.*

## Section D: Informative Writing (Lessons 5-6)

**D1.** (Oral) "What does informative writing do?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Teaches the reader something true / shares facts / gives information | 1 point |
| Partially correct (e.g., "Tells you stuff") | 0.5 points |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**D2.** (Oral) "What is a topic sentence?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| The first sentence that tells the reader what the writing is about / introduces the topic | 1 point |
| Partially correct (e.g., "The first sentence") | 0.5 points |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**D3.** (Oral) "Listen to this fact: 'Birds have wings.' How could you add a detail to make it more interesting?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Adds any relevant detail (e.g., "Birds have strong wings that help them fly." or "Birds have colorful wings.") | 1 point |
| Repeats the fact without adding a detail | 0 points |

**D4.** (Written) "Write an informative piece about a topic you know well. Remember to include: a topic sentence, at least two facts, and a closing sentence."

Give the student lined paper. Allow 5-7 minutes.

Score using this rubric:

| Component | Score |
|-----------|-------|
| Includes a topic sentence that introduces the subject | 0 or 0.5 or 1 |
| Includes at least two facts and a closing sentence | 0 or 0.5 or 1 |

**Scoring detail for D4:**

| Topic sentence | Score |
|---|---|
| Clear topic sentence that names the subject | 1 point |
| Jumps into facts without a topic sentence, or topic sentence is vague | 0.5 points |
| No topic sentence | 0 points |

| Facts and closing | Score |
|---|---|
| At least 2 facts (true statements) AND a closing sentence | 1 point |
| Has 2 facts but no closing, OR has 1 fact and a closing | 0.5 points |
| Has fewer than 2 facts and no closing | 0 points |

*Note: Facts must be TRUE statements. If the student writes an opinion as a fact (e.g., "Dogs are the best"), do not count it as a fact. However, accept approximations of truth that show the right intent (e.g., "Dogs can run really really fast" — this is a fact, even if imprecise).*

## Section E: Narrative Writing (Lessons 7-8)

**E1.** (Oral) "What are the three parts of a story?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Beginning, middle, and end | 1 point |
| Names 2 of 3 parts | 0.5 points |
| Names fewer than 2 parts | 0 points |

**E2.** (Oral) "Name two sequence words that help tell a story in order."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Names 2 or more: first, then, next, after that, last, finally | 1 point |
| Names only 1 | 0.5 points |
| Cannot name any | 0 points |

**E3.** (Oral) Read these three sentences in scrambled order. Ask the student to put them in the correct sequence.

"Last, we ate the sandwiches. First, we got the bread and peanut butter out. Then, we made the sandwiches."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correct order: First we got the bread and peanut butter. Then we made the sandwiches. Last we ate them. | 1 point |
| Gets 2 of 3 in correct position | 0.5 points |
| Cannot sequence them | 0 points |

**E4.** (Written) "Write a short story about something that happened — real or made up. Remember to include: a beginning (who and what), a middle (what happens), and an end (how it finishes). Try to use sequence words!"

Give the student lined paper. Allow 5-7 minutes.

Score using this rubric:

| Component | Score |
|-----------|-------|
| Has a beginning (character + situation), middle (events), and end (resolution or conclusion) with at least one sequence word | 0 or 0.5 or 1 or 2 |

**Scoring detail for E4:**

| Narrative structure | Score |
|---|---|
| Clear beginning (character + situation), middle (at least one event), and end (conclusion or feeling), with at least one sequence word | 2 points |
| Has all three parts but no sequence words, OR has two clear parts with sequence words | 1.5 points |
| Has two of three parts (e.g., beginning and middle but no end) | 1 point |
| Has only one part or writing does not tell a story | 0.5 points |
| No narrative produced | 0 points |

*Note: The narrative is worth 2 points because it assesses both structure AND sequencing. Accept any story length — even 3 sentences can earn full marks if they clearly represent beginning, middle, and end with a sequence word.*

## Section F: Revision (Lesson 9)

**F1.** (Written) Show the student this piece of writing (write it on paper):

> "i like to play at the park. the swings are fun i also like the slide"

Ask: "This piece of writing has some things that need to be fixed. Use the revision checklist to find and fix as many things as you can."

| Revision Made | Score |
|---|---|
| Capitalizes "I" at the beginning of the first sentence | 0.5 points |
| Capitalizes "The" at the beginning of the second sentence | included above |
| Adds a period after "fun" (recognizes two sentences run together) | 0.5 points |
| Adds a period at the end | included above |

**Scoring detail for F1:**

| Revisions | Score |
|---|---|
| Fixes at least 3 errors (both missing capitals, at least 1 missing period or sentence separation) | 1 point |
| Fixes 2 errors | 0.5 points |
| Fixes 0-1 errors | 0 points |

The full list of errors: (1) lowercase "i" should be "I", (2) lowercase "the" should be "The", (3) missing period after "fun" (or recognizing that "the swings are fun i also like the slide" is a run-on), (4) missing period at the end.

**F2.** (Written/Oral) Show the student this sentence: "I have a dog."

Ask: "Can you add a detail to make this sentence more interesting? Write the new sentence."

| Response | Score |
|---|---|
| Adds any relevant detail (e.g., "I have a big brown dog." or "I have a dog named Buddy." or "I have a fluffy dog that loves to play.") | 1 point |
| Repeats the sentence without adding a detail, or changes it in a way that loses the original meaning | 0 points |

## Rubric and Mastery Determination

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Next Steps |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Mastered** | 21-25 (85-100%) with at least 1 correct in each section | Solid writing foundations across all three writing types | Advance to Unit 4. Continue daily writing practice. Celebrate growth! |
| **Approaching** | 18-20 (70-84%) | Most writing skills are developing; specific areas need reinforcement | Identify weak sections and reteach (1-2 sessions each). The student may begin Unit 4 while continuing targeted writing practice. |
| **Developing** | Below 18 (<70%) | Significant writing gaps | Return to the weakest section and reteach from that lesson. Increase daily writing time. Do not advance to Unit 4 until "Approaching" level. |

## Section-Specific Reteaching Guide

| If the student struggles in... | What it tells you | Reteach with... |
|-------------------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Section A (Writing Types)** | Cannot distinguish opinion, informative, and narrative writing | Return to Lesson 1. Re-create the anchor chart with the student. Practice sorting 10-15 example sentences into three categories. Use the key words: opinion = "I think," informative = "facts/true," narrative = "story/something happened." |
| **Section B (Complete Sentences)** | Cannot write or identify complete sentences | Return to Lesson 2. Practice the four-finger check with 10 sentences (some complete, some not). Focus on whichever part is weakest: if subject/verb awareness is missing, use the sentence puzzle cards; if conventions (capitals/periods) are missing, practice circling them in printed text. |
| **Section C (Opinion Writing)** | Cannot write an opinion with a reason and closing | Return to Lessons 3-4. Start with the fact-vs-opinion sort to ensure the student knows what an opinion is. Then practice with the "I think ___ because ___" frame. The frame removes the burden of generating the sentence structure so the student can focus on content. Add the closing last. |
| **Section D (Informative Writing)** | Cannot write an informative piece with topic sentence, facts, and closing | Return to Lessons 5-6. Use the oral brainstorm strategy from Lesson 5 (student talks about what they know, facilitator writes facts on cards) to generate content before writing. Practice the topic sentence frame: "[Topic] is/are ___." If the student mixes in opinions, revisit the fact-vs-opinion sort. |
| **Section E (Narrative Writing)** | Cannot write a story with beginning, middle, and end | Return to Lessons 7-8. Use picture sequencing to rebuild the concept of story order. Practice with the physical cards (Beginning, Middle, End). Focus first on oral storytelling — the student tells a story in three parts before writing. Introduce sequence words one at a time: start with just "first" and "then" before adding more. |
| **Section F (Revision)** | Cannot identify errors or add details | Return to Lesson 9. Practice only the capitals-and-periods check with simple sentences. For adding details, use the oral exercise: facilitator says a plain sentence, student adds a detail, then switch. Keep the revision checklist visible at all times during writing. |

## Detailed Rubric by Skill

### Writing Type Identification (Section A)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Identifies writing types | Correctly identifies all three types when given examples | Correctly identifies 2 of 3 types; confuses one pair (usually opinion/informative) | Cannot reliably identify any writing type; guesses randomly |

### Sentence Construction (Section B)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Understands sentence parts | Explains that sentences need a subject and verb; identifies and fixes incomplete sentences | Knows sentences need "something" but cannot articulate subject/verb; can fix some errors | Cannot explain what makes a sentence complete; does not recognize incomplete sentences |
| Uses conventions | Consistently uses capital letters and end punctuation | Uses capitals and end marks sometimes; misses 1-2 per piece | Rarely or never uses capitals and end punctuation |

### Opinion Writing (Section C)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| States an opinion | Clear opinion statement using "I think" or equivalent | Opinion is present but unclear or blended with reasons | Cannot state an opinion or confuses it with a fact |
| Provides a reason | Gives at least one specific reason connected to the opinion with "because" | Attempts a reason but it is vague ("because it is good") or disconnected | No reason given, or reason is just a restatement of the opinion |
| Includes a closing | Closing sentence wraps up the piece and echoes the opinion | Attempts a closing but it is abrupt or unrelated ("The end.") | No closing sentence |

### Informative Writing (Section D)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Introduces the topic | Clear topic sentence that names the subject | Jumps into facts without a clear topic sentence | No topic sentence; the reader cannot tell what the piece is about |
| Provides facts | At least 2 true facts with at least one detail | Provides 1-2 facts but without details, or mixes in opinions | Cannot produce facts about a topic; writing contains only opinions or unrelated statements |
| Includes a closing | Closing sentence wraps up the informative piece | Attempts a closing but it is vague | No closing sentence |

### Narrative Writing (Section E)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Story structure | Clear beginning (character + situation), middle (events), and end (resolution/feeling) | Has 2 of 3 parts clearly; one part is missing or very weak | Writing does not have a recognizable story structure; events are random or absent |
| Sequencing | Uses at least 2 sequence words correctly; events are in logical order | Uses 1 sequence word; events are mostly in order | No sequence words; events are out of order or the reader cannot follow the story |

### Revision Skills (Section F)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Identifies and fixes errors | Finds and fixes 3+ errors in a given piece (capitals, periods, run-ons) | Finds and fixes 1-2 errors | Cannot identify errors in writing |
| Adds details | Adds a relevant, specific detail to improve a sentence | Adds a word but it does not meaningfully improve the sentence | Cannot add a detail; repeats the original sentence |

## Facilitator Observations (Record During Assessment)

- Which writing type does the student seem most confident with (opinion, informative, or narrative)?
- Does the student plan orally before writing, or dive in without planning?
- How long does the student take to generate ideas? Is idea generation or physical writing the bottleneck?
- Does the student re-read their writing spontaneously, or only when prompted?
- Does the student use the revision checklist independently, or need reminders?
- How is the student's handwriting? Is letter formation slowing down the writing process?
- Does the student show enthusiasm for writing, or resistance and avoidance?
- What topics does the student gravitate toward? (This information helps personalize future writing assignments.)

These observations inform differentiation for Unit 4 and help determine whether the student's challenges are in idea generation, sentence construction, conventions, or physical writing stamina.

## Writing Sample Evaluation Notes

When scoring the three writing samples (C4, D4, E4), keep these principles in mind:

1. **Invented spelling is expected and acceptable.** Do not deduct any points for spelling. A student who writes "becuz" for "because" or "animuls" for "animals" is demonstrating phonics application. This is a writing structure assessment, not a spelling test.

2. **Short pieces can earn full marks.** A 3-sentence opinion piece with a clear opinion, reason, and closing earns full points, even though it is brief. Length is not a criterion.

3. **Ideas matter more than execution.** If the student's sentence is grammatically imperfect but the idea is clear and the structure is correct (e.g., "Dogs best becuz they nice. Thats why I lik dogs."), give credit for structure.

4. **Read the student's work aloud to them** if their handwriting makes it hard to evaluate. Ask: "Is this what you meant to write?" Their oral clarification can help you accurately score the piece.

5. **Note strengths alongside weaknesses.** Every student will have something to celebrate in their writing — a vivid detail, a strong opinion, an interesting story idea. Document these strengths to build on in Unit 4.
