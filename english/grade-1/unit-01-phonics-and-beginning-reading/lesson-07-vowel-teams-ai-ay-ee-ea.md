# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 7

## Topic
Vowel Teams: ai, ay, ee, ea

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain that a vowel team is two vowels that work together to make one long vowel sound
- Read at least 10 words containing ai, ay, ee, or ea vowel teams
- Identify the position pattern: ai usually appears in the middle of a word; ay usually appears at the end
- Sort words by vowel team

## Prerequisites
- Short vowel CVC words (Lesson 1)
- Long vowel CVCe pattern (Lesson 6 mastery)
- Understanding of "long vowel = vowel says its name"

## Materials Needed
- Index cards with vowel teams written on them: ai, ay, ee, ea
- Word cards (prepare before lesson — see word list below)
- Paper and pencil
- Crayons or colored pencils (to underline vowel teams)
- Four sorting labels: "ai," "ay," "ee," "ea"

**Word cards to prepare:**
rain, tail, mail, paid, wait, pain, main, play, day, say, may, stay, way, hay, pay, tree, see, bee, seed, feet, need, keep, week, read, eat, sea, bead, team, meat, leaf, bean, seat, heat

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review silent e pattern and prior phonics skills.

**Spaced Review:**
1. Read these CVCe words: "lake, bite, home, cute" (Lesson 6 review)
2. Read these blend words: "drum, clap, fast, milk" (Lessons 4-5 review)
3. Read these digraph words: "shop, then, which" (Lessons 2-3 review)
4. "What rule did you learn about silent e?" (When a word ends in e, the vowel says its name / long sound)

**Bridge to new learning:**
"You learned that silent e is one way to make a vowel say its long sound. Today you'll learn another way — vowel TEAMS! When two vowels sit right next to each other, they often work together to make one long vowel sound. There's even a famous saying about it."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the concept of vowel teams, focusing on ai/ay (long a) and ee/ea (long e).

### Key Vocabulary
- **Vowel team:** Two vowel letters that sit next to each other and work together to make one sound. Usually, they make a long vowel sound.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the concept with the mnemonic.**
"There's a saying that helps you remember vowel teams: 'When two vowels go walking, the first one does the talking.' That means the first vowel says its name (long sound), and the second vowel is quiet."

"This works for MANY vowel teams, but not all. We'll learn the ones it works for today."

**Step 2: Introduce ai and ay (long a teams).**
Write "ai" on a card. "These two vowels — a and i — make the long a sound: /ay/."

Write "rain." Point to the ai: "See the ai in the middle? It says /ay/. /r/... /ay/... /n/... rain!"

Write "ay" on a card. "These two vowels — a and y — also make the long a sound: /ay/."

Write "play." Point to the ay: "The ay says /ay/. /pl/... /ay/... play!"

"Here's a helpful pattern: ai usually comes in the MIDDLE of a word (rain, tail, mail). ay usually comes at the END of a word (play, day, say). This is not a rule you have to memorize — it will start to feel natural as you read more."

**Step 3: Introduce ee and ea (long e teams).**
Write "ee" on a card. "Two e's together make the long e sound: /ee/."

Write "tree." Point to the ee: "/tr/... /ee/... tree!"

Write "ea" on a card. "E and a together also make the long e sound: /ee/."

Write "read." Point to the ea: "/r/... /ee/... /d/... read!"

**Step 4: Important caveat about ea.**
"Most of the time, ea says /ee/ — like in 'read,' 'eat,' and 'sea.' But sometimes ea says /e/ — like in 'bread' or 'head.' You'll learn those tricky ones later. For today, when you see ea, try /ee/ first."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Read the word "tail."
- "I see a-i in the middle. That's a vowel team! It says /ay/."
- "/t/... /ay/... /l/... tail!"

**Example 2:** Read the word "say."
- "I see a-y at the end. Vowel team! It says /ay/."
- "/s/... /ay/... say!"

**Example 3:** Read the word "seed."
- "I see e-e in the middle. Vowel team! It says /ee/."
- "/s/... /ee/... /d/... seed!"

**Example 4:** Read the word "eat."
- "I see e-a. Vowel team! It says /ee/."
- "/ee/... /t/... eat!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice reading vowel team words with support.

**Activity 1: Read ai and ay Words (3 minutes)**
Show word cards: rain, mail, wait, play, day, stay.

For each: "Find the vowel team. What sound does it make? Read the word."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student reads "rain" as "ran" (treats each vowel separately), underline the ai: "These two vowels are a team. They make one sound together: /ay/. Try again."*

*If the student confuses ai with the 'ay' sound in CVC words: remind them that there is no silent e here. "This word doesn't use silent e to make the long a. It uses a vowel team instead — ai. Both ways make the same /ay/ sound!"*

**Activity 2: Read ee and ea Words (3 minutes)**
Show word cards: tree, see, keep, read, eat, leaf.

Same process: find the vowel team, say the sound, read the word.

*If the student struggles with ea: "Remember, e and a together usually say /ee/. Like in 'eat' — something you do with food. /ee/ /t/... eat!"*

**Activity 3: Mixed Vowel Team Sort (4 minutes)**
Place four sorting labels: ai, ay, ee, ea.

Give the student these 8 word cards: rain, play, seed, meat, tail, say, feet, bean.

"Read each word and sort it under the right vowel team."

*If the student is unsure which team a word has: "Look at the vowel letters. What two vowels do you see next to each other? That's your vowel team." Point to the letters.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads, sorts, and writes vowel team words independently.

**★ On-level (10 words):**
Sort these words under ai, ay, ee, or ea:
mail, day, tree, eat, pain, stay, need, sea, wait, may

Write these 3 words from dictation:
1. "rain" (r-a-i-n)
2. "play" (p-l-a-y)
3. "seed" (s-e-e-d)

**★★ Stretch (5 additional words + writing):**
Add to the sort: main, hay, week, team, paid

Write 2 more from dictation:
4. "feet" (f-e-e-t)
5. "tail" (t-a-i-l)

**★★★ Challenge:**
Write two sentences — one using ai or ay words, one using ee or ea words.
Examples: "The rain fell on the train all day." / "I can see the green tree and the bee."

Then: "You know two ways to make the long a sound — silent e (like 'lake') and vowel teams (like 'rain' and 'play'). Can you think of one word for each way?"

### Interleaved Review
- Read these CVCe words: "rope, fine, tube" (Lesson 6 review)
- Read these ending blend words: "best, pink, lamp" (Lesson 5 review)
- "What digraph do you hear in 'ship'?" (sh)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can read and identify vowel team words.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Read these words aloud: **rain, play, tree, meat, wait, keep** (Student reads all 6)
2. "What vowel team do you see in the word 'tail'?" (ai)
3. "What long vowel sound do ee and ea make?" (/ee/ — long e)

**Mastery = Student reads at least 5 of 6 words correctly AND answers at least 1 of the 2 identification questions correctly.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Focus on one vowel team pair at a time. Start with ai/ay (long a) until confident, then add ee/ea (long e).
- Use color coding: underline all vowel teams in one color to help the student see them as units.
- Practice with word families: rain, main, pain, gain; day, play, say, stay; tree, free, see, bee; eat, meat, seat, heat.
- Repeat this lesson next session.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 8 (Vowel Teams: oa, ow, igh). Continue to include ai/ay and ee/ea words in warm-ups.

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who master these vowel teams quickly:**
- Vowel team word hunt: look through a book or around the room for words with ai, ay, ee, or ea.
- Compare long vowel strategies: "There are now TWO ways you know to make a long a: silent e (cape, lake) and vowel teams (rain, play). Can you find both kinds in this list: tape, rain, make, day, bake, stay?"
- Write a silly rhyming poem using vowel team words: "I see a tree by the sea. A bee flew free to me."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**"When two vowels go walking, the first one does the talking"** is a helpful mnemonic but is NOT universally true. Research shows it works about 45% of the time across all vowel pairs. However, for the specific teams taught in this lesson (ai, ay, ee, ea), it works very reliably. Use it as a helpful starting point, not an absolute rule.

**ai vs. ay position pattern:** The generalization that ai appears mid-word and ay appears at the end is quite reliable. English words rarely end in the letter i (we use y instead). This is a spelling convention students will internalize naturally, but it is worth mentioning so they are not confused about why there are two spellings for the same sound.

**ea has two common pronunciations.** In this lesson, we teach ea = /ee/ (long e), which is the most frequent pronunciation. The student will eventually encounter ea = /e/ (short e) in words like "bread," "head," "dead," "read" (past tense). Do not introduce this complication today. If a student happens to read "read" and asks about it, simply say: "That's a tricky word — 'ea' usually says /ee/, but in a few words it says /e/. You'll learn those later."

**Connection to silent e:** Students may wonder why English has multiple ways to spell the same sound. This is a great opportunity: "English borrowed words from lots of different languages, and each language had its own way of spelling sounds. That's why we have 'lake,' 'rain,' AND 'play' — they all have the long a sound, but spelled differently. It can be confusing, but you're figuring it out!"

**Growth mindset note:** "Vowel teams are one of the trickiest parts of learning to read in English. If this feels hard, you are in good company — every reader has to learn these patterns. The fact that you're working through them shows real strength."
