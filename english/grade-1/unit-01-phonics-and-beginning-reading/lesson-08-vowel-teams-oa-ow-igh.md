# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 8

## Topic
Vowel Teams: oa, ow, igh

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read words with the oa vowel team (long o, as in "boat")
- Read words with the ow vowel team when it makes the long o sound (as in "snow")
- Read words with the igh pattern (long i, as in "light")
- Distinguish between all vowel teams learned so far (ai, ay, ee, ea, oa, ow, igh)

## Prerequisites
- Vowel teams ai, ay, ee, ea (Lesson 7 mastery)
- Long vowel CVCe pattern (Lesson 6)
- CVC short vowel words (Lesson 1)

## Materials Needed
- Index cards with "oa," "ow," and "igh" written on them
- Vowel team cards from Lesson 7: ai, ay, ee, ea
- Word cards (prepare before lesson — see word list below)
- Paper and pencil
- Crayons (for underlining vowel patterns)

**Word cards to prepare:**
boat, coat, goat, road, load, soap, foam, oak, moan, toad, snow, low, bow, row, grow, show, slow, flow, glow, know, light, night, right, sight, bright, fight, might, high, sigh, tight

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review vowel teams from Lesson 7 and prior phonics patterns.

**Spaced Review:**
1. Read these vowel team words: "rain, play, tree, meat" (Lesson 7 review)
2. Read these CVCe words: "kite, hope, cave" (Lesson 6 review)
3. Read these blend/digraph words: "trip, shelf, think" (Lessons 2-5 review)
4. "What vowel team do you see in 'seed'?" (ee) "What long vowel sound does it make?" (/ee/)

**Bridge to new learning:**
"You already know four vowel teams that make long a and long e. Today you'll learn three more patterns that make long o and long i. After today, you'll know vowel teams for three different long vowel sounds — that's amazing!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce oa (long o), ow (long o), and igh (long i).

### Key Vocabulary
- **oa:** A vowel team where o and a work together to make the long o sound (/oh/).
- **ow:** Can make the long o sound (/oh/) in some words (like "snow"). Note: ow can also make the /ow/ sound as in "cow" — that is a different pattern taught later.
- **igh:** A three-letter team that makes the long i sound (/iy/). The g and h are silent — only the i "talks."

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce oa (long o).**
Hold up the "oa" card. "O and a together make the long o sound: /oh/. Remember: the first vowel does the talking — the o says its name."

Write "boat." "/b/... /oh/... /t/... boat!"

Try another: "coat." "/k/... /oh/... /t/... coat!"

"The oa team usually appears in the MIDDLE of a word — just like ai for long a."

**Step 2: Introduce ow (long o).**
Hold up the "ow" card. "O and w can also make the long o sound: /oh/."

Write "snow." "/sn/... /oh/... snow!"

Write "grow." "/gr/... /oh/... grow!"

"The ow team often appears at the END of a word — just like ay for long a. See the pattern? Oa in the middle, ow at the end."

"Now here is something important: ow can make TWO different sounds. In 'snow,' it says /oh/. But in 'cow,' it says /ow/. For now, when you see ow, try /oh/ first. If the word doesn't sound right, try /ow/. You'll learn more about this later."

**Step 3: Introduce igh (long i).**
Hold up the "igh" card. "This one is special — it has THREE letters: i, g, h. But the g and h are completely silent! Only the i talks, and it says its long sound: /iy/."

Write "light." "I see l at the beginning: /l/. Then igh: /iy/. Then t: /t/. /l/... /iy/... /t/... light!"

Write "night." "/n/... /iy/... /t/... night!"

"Think of it this way: the g and h are like bodyguards for the i. They are there, but they don't say anything. The i says /iy/ all by itself."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Read the word "road."
- "I see o-a in the middle. That's the oa vowel team: /oh/."
- "/r/... /oh/... /d/... road!"

**Example 2:** Read the word "show."
- "I see o-w at the end. That's ow making the long o sound: /oh/."
- "/sh/... /oh/... show! (And look — 'show' starts with the sh digraph too!)"

**Example 3:** Read the word "bright."
- "I see i-g-h — that's the igh pattern: /iy/."
- "/br/... /iy/... /t/... bright! (And 'bright' starts with the br blend!)"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice reading oa, ow, and igh words with support.

**Activity 1: Read oa Words (3 minutes)**
Show word cards: goat, soap, load, oak, toad.

For each: "Find the vowel team. What sound does it make? Read the word."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student reads "goat" as "got" (uses the short o), point to the oa: "I see two vowels together — o and a. They're a team. The o says its name: /oh/. Try again."*

**Activity 2: Read ow and igh Words (3 minutes)**
Show word cards: slow, glow, know, light, fight, sight.

For ow words: "What sound does ow make here?" (/oh/) "Read the word."
For igh words: "Remember, the g and h are silent. What does the i say?" (/iy/) "Read the word."

*If the student says /ow/ (as in "cow") for "snow": "Good try! Ow can make that sound too. But in this word, try /oh/ instead. Does 'snow' — /sn/ /oh/ — sound like a word you know?"*

*If the student tries to sound out the g and h in "igh": "The g and h are silent helpers in this pattern. Only the i makes a sound: /iy/. Skip the g and h."*

**Activity 3: Vowel Team Identification (4 minutes)**
Show these mixed word cards. Student reads the word and names the vowel team:
- rain (ai)
- snow (ow)
- tree (ee)
- goat (oa)
- light (igh)
- play (ay)
- meat (ea)
- night (igh)

"You know SEVEN vowel patterns now! That's incredible."

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads, sorts, and writes words with all vowel teams learned so far.

**★ On-level (10 words):**
Sort these words by vowel team (oa, ow, or igh):
boat, snow, night, coat, grow, light, road, slow, right, soap

Write these 3 words from dictation:
1. "boat" (b-o-a-t)
2. "snow" (s-n-o-w)
3. "light" (l-i-g-h-t)

**★★ Stretch (5 additional words):**
Add to the sort: foam, glow, bright, toad, know

Write 2 more from dictation:
4. "goat" (g-o-a-t)
5. "night" (n-i-g-h-t)

**★★★ Challenge:**
Write a sentence using at least one oa, one ow, and one igh word. Example: "The goat stood in the snow on a bright night."

Then: "You now know THREE ways to make the long o sound: silent e (hope, note), oa (boat, road), and ow (snow, grow). Write one word for each way."

### Interleaved Review
- Read these ai/ay/ee/ea words: "tail, day, see, bean" (Lesson 7 review)
- Read these CVCe words: "made, time, cute" (Lesson 6 review)
- "What ending blend do you hear in 'fast'?" (-st)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can read oa, ow, and igh words.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Read these words aloud: **coat, show, night, road, bright, flow** (Student reads all 6)
2. "What vowel team do you see in 'goat'?" (oa) "What sound does it make?" (/oh/)
3. "In the word 'light,' which letters are silent?" (g and h)

**Mastery = Student reads at least 5 of 6 words correctly AND answers at least 1 identification question correctly.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Focus on one pattern at a time. Start with oa (it follows the familiar "two vowels, first one talks" rule). Once solid, add ow. Save igh for last (it is the most unusual pattern).
- For igh: create a visual reminder card with "igh = long i" and a picture of a lightbulb (light). Post it where the student can see it during practice.
- Use word families for repetition: boat, coat, goat, moat; snow, grow, flow, blow; light, night, right, sight.
- Repeat this lesson next session.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 9 (Reading Connected Text). Continue to include all vowel team words in warm-ups.

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who master these vowel teams quickly:**
- Long vowel comparison: make three columns for long o (CVCe, oa, ow) and sort these words: hope, boat, snow, note, road, grow, bone, foam, slow.
- Make three columns for long i (CVCe, igh) and sort: kite, light, fine, night, ride, bright, time, sight.
- Write a nighttime story using ow and igh words: "At night, the bright light of the moon made the snow glow."
- Advanced reading: try words that combine vowel teams with blends or digraphs: "throat," "shine," "trail," "stream."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**ow has two pronunciations.** This is a genuine challenge. In "snow," "low," and "grow," ow says /oh/ (long o). In "cow," "how," and "now," ow says /ow/ (a diphthong). This lesson teaches only the /oh/ pronunciation. Tell the student: "If you try /oh/ and it doesn't sound like a word you know, try /ow/ instead." The other pronunciation will be taught formally in a later unit. Most students figure this out from context surprisingly quickly.

**igh is unusual** because it has three letters, two of which are silent. Students may wonder why English does this. A brief, honest answer: "A long time ago, English DID pronounce the 'gh.' The word 'night' used to sound like 'nikht.' Over hundreds of years, people stopped saying the 'gh,' but the spelling stayed. Languages change, but spellings sometimes don't keep up!" This is optional — share only if the student is curious.

**know is a tricky word.** It starts with a silent k AND has the ow vowel team. If "know" appears and the student reads it as "now" or "kuh-now," address each piece: "The k at the beginning is silent (just like in 'knee' and 'knife'). And the ow says /oh/. So: /n/... /oh/... know." Silent k is not formally taught in this unit, so treat "know" as a bonus word if it comes up.

**Connecting all long vowel patterns:** By this point, the student knows multiple ways to make long vowels. This is a strength, not a confusion. Encourage the student: "You have so many tools for reading now. Different words use different patterns, and you are learning to recognize all of them."

**Growth mindset note:** "English spelling can seem really strange sometimes. But every pattern you learn is a tool in your reading toolbox. Professional readers — authors, teachers, scientists — all learned the same patterns you're learning right now."
