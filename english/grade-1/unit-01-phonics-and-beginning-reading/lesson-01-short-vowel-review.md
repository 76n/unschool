# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 1

## Topic
Short Vowel Review: CVC Words

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify and produce the short sound for each vowel (a, e, i, o, u)
- Read at least 10 CVC words accurately
- Sort CVC words by their vowel sound

## Prerequisites
- Letter-name and letter-sound knowledge for all 26 letters (Kindergarten phonics)
- Ability to blend three sounds orally (e.g., /c/ /a/ /t/ = "cat")

## Materials Needed
- Letter cards or magnetic letters (a-z)
- 20 index cards with CVC words written on them (see word list below)
- 5 sorting labels: "a," "e," "i," "o," "u" (write on index cards or paper)
- Paper and pencil
- Crayons (optional, for coloring vowels)

**Word cards to prepare before the lesson:**
cat, hat, map, sad, bag, hen, red, pet, bed, leg, pig, sit, lid, zip, dig, hot, pot, log, mop, cup, bug, nut, run, sun, tub

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate kindergarten knowledge and assess baseline phonics skills.

**Activity: Alphabet Sound Sprint**
Say to the student: "Let's warm up our sound brains! I'm going to point to a letter, and you tell me the sound it makes. Ready?"

Show these letters in random order (write them on cards or paper): b, m, s, t, r, a, e, i, o, u, f, n, p, d, g

For consonants, the student says the sound (e.g., b = /b/).
For vowels, the student says the short sound (e.g., a = /a/ as in "apple").

Then: "Now let's do something fun. I'll say three sounds, and you put them together into a word."
- /d/ /o/ /g/ (dog)
- /s/ /i/ /t/ (sit)
- /r/ /u/ /n/ (run)

*Facilitator note: This warm-up tells you where the student stands. If they struggle with letter sounds, spend extra time reviewing before the main lesson. If they blend easily, they are ready.*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review and solidify the five short vowel sounds in CVC words.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Vowel:** The letters a, e, i, o, u. Every word needs at least one vowel.
- **Consonant:** All the other letters that are not vowels.
- **CVC word:** A word with a consonant, then a vowel, then a consonant — like "cat" (c-a-t) or "dog" (d-o-g).
- **Short vowel:** The sound a vowel makes in a CVC word. It is the "short" or "quick" sound.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the five short vowels with key words.**
Write each vowel on a card with a picture cue:
- **a** — apple (/a/ as in "apple")
- **e** — egg (/e/ as in "egg")
- **i** — itch (/i/ as in "itch")
- **o** — octopus (/o/ as in "octopus")
- **u** — umbrella (/u/ as in "umbrella")

Say each vowel sound and have the student repeat. Point to the key word picture for each.

"These are the five vowels. They are special because every single word has at least one of them. Today we are going to listen for these sounds in the middle of words."

**Step 2: Model reading a CVC word.**
Write "cat" on a card or paper. Point to each letter:
"Watch me read this word. I look at the first letter: /k/. Then the vowel in the middle: /a/. Then the last letter: /t/. Now I blend them together: /k/... /a/... /t/... cat!"

**Step 3: Model with a different vowel.**
Write "cup." Point to each letter:
"/k/... /u/... /p/... cup! The vowel in the middle is u, and it says /u/."

"Notice — 'cat' has /a/ in the middle. 'Cup' has /u/ in the middle. The vowel in the middle is what makes these words different."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Read the word "hen."
- First sound: /h/
- Middle sound (vowel): /e/
- Last sound: /n/
- Blend: /h/... /e/... /n/... "hen!"
- "The vowel is e. It says /e/."

**Example 2:** Read the word "pig."
- First sound: /p/
- Middle sound (vowel): /i/
- Last sound: /g/
- Blend: /p/... /i/... /g/... "pig!"
- "The vowel is i. It says /i/."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice reading CVC words and identifying the vowel sound with support.

**Activity 1: Read Together (4 minutes)**
Place these word cards face up: mat, bed, fin, hop, hug.

Point to each word. Read it together with the student:
- "What is the first sound? Good. The middle sound? Good. The last sound? Now blend them."

After reading each word, ask: "What is the vowel? What sound does it make?"

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles to blend, say the sounds more slowly and stretch them: "/mmm/... /aaa/... /t/... mmmaaaat... mat!" Then have the student try.*

*If the student struggles to identify the vowel: Point to the middle letter. "This letter is the vowel. Is it a, e, i, o, or u? What sound does it make?"*

**Activity 2: Word Building (3 minutes)**
Use letter cards or write letters on small pieces of paper. Build the word "hat" together.

"Let's change the vowel. What if I take out the a and put in an o?" (hot)
"Now let's change it to u." (hut)
"And to i?" (hit)

Let the student read each new word. This shows how changing the vowel changes the whole word.

**Activity 3: Vowel Sound Sorting (3 minutes)**
Place the five vowel labels (a, e, i, o, u) across the table. Give the student 5 word cards: cat, pet, sit, log, sun.

"Read each word. Listen to the vowel sound in the middle. Put the word under the right vowel."

Do the first one together as a model. Let the student do the rest.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student places a word in the wrong column, read the word together slowly. Emphasize the middle sound. "Cat... /a/. Which vowel says /a/?"*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads and sorts CVC words independently.

**Activity: Sort the Word Cards**
Give the student the remaining word cards (15-20 cards from the word list). The five vowel labels remain on the table.

"Read each word and sort it under the right vowel. Take your time — sound out each word carefully."

### Difficulty Tiers

**★ On-level (10 words):**
Sort these words by vowel sound: hat, red, pig, hot, cup, bag, hen, lid, mop, nut

**★★ Stretch (5 additional words):**
Add these words to the sort: sad, leg, zip, log, run

**★★★ Challenge (5 additional words + writing):**
Add these words: map, pet, dig, pot, tub

Then: "Pick one vowel sound. Write three MORE words that have that vowel sound in the middle. They can be any CVC words you can think of."

### Interleaved Review
- "Write your first name." (Kindergarten handwriting review)
- "What sound does the letter w make? What about the letter y?" (Letter-sound review for less common consonants)

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can read CVC words and identify short vowel sounds.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Read these words aloud: **fun, set, nap, hip, cot** (Student reads all 5 correctly)
2. "What is the vowel sound in the word 'bed'?" (Answer: /e/)
3. "Sort these 3 words under the right vowel: rug, pin, jam" (rug = u, pin = i, jam = a)

**Mastery = Student reads at least 4 of 5 words correctly AND sorts at least 2 of 3 words correctly.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which vowel sounds the student confuses (short e and short i are the most commonly confused pair).
- Practice with just two vowel sounds at a time using minimal pairs (e.g., "pen" vs. "pin," "bed" vs. "bid").
- Repeat the sorting activity with fewer cards, focusing on the confused vowels.
- Repeat this lesson next session before moving to Lesson 2.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 2 (Consonant Digraphs: sh, ch). Continue to include CVC word reading in warm-ups for future lessons.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who sort and read CVC words easily:**
- Play "Vowel Swap": write a CVC word, then change just the vowel to make a new word. How many real words can you make? (e.g., bat → bet → bit → bot → but — are they all real words?)
- Play "Rhyme Time": pick a CVC word and list as many rhyming words as possible (cat, hat, mat, sat, rat, bat, fat, pat).
- Write a silly sentence using as many CVC words as possible: "The big red bug sat on a hot tin cup."

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson is diagnostic.** Its main purpose is to find out how solid the student's CVC foundation is before building new patterns on top of it. If the student reads CVC words fluently and sorts by vowel sound without difficulty, this lesson may take only 20 minutes. That is fine — use remaining time for the extension activities.

**The most common confusion** is between short e (/e/ as in "egg") and short i (/i/ as in "itch"). These sounds are very close, especially in some regional accents. If the student struggles with this pair, do NOT worry — it is extremely common. Practice with minimal pairs: "pen" vs. "pin," "set" vs. "sit," "bed" vs. "bid." Exaggerate the mouth position: /e/ has a wider, more open mouth; /i/ has a narrower, more smiling mouth.

**Short o and short u** can also be confusing. Again, use minimal pairs: "hot" vs. "hut," "cop" vs. "cup," "not" vs. "nut."

**Growth mindset note:** If the student feels this is "baby stuff" because it is review, reframe it: "Great readers always warm up their skills, just like athletes stretch before a game. This is making sure your reading muscles are strong for the new stuff coming next!"

**Don't worry about:** Long vowel sounds (those come in Lessons 6-8), words with blends or digraphs (Lessons 2-5), or reading sentences (Lessons 9-10). Keep today focused on single CVC words.
