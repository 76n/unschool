# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 9

## Topic
Reading Connected Text

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Apply all phonics patterns learned in Lessons 1-8 to read short decodable passages
- Use decoding strategies (sound it out, look for known patterns) when encountering unfamiliar words
- Self-correct when a decoded word does not make sense in the sentence
- Answer simple comprehension questions about what they read

## Prerequisites
- All prior lessons in this unit (short vowels, digraphs, blends, CVCe, vowel teams)
- The student should be able to read individual words with these patterns before attempting connected text

## Materials Needed
- This lesson plan (contains three decodable passages)
- Paper and pencil
- A bookmark or index card (to use as a reading guide under each line)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick review of all phonics patterns to activate decoding skills before reading.

**Spaced Review — Pattern Sprint:**
Read one word for each pattern type. Go quickly but accurately:
1. CVC: "fog" (short vowel)
2. Digraph: "chip" (ch)
3. Beginning blend: "frog" (fr)
4. Ending blend: "best" (st)
5. CVCe: "take" (silent e, long a)
6. Vowel team ai/ay: "rain" (ai)
7. Vowel team ee/ea: "keep" (ee)
8. Vowel team oa/ow: "boat" (oa)
9. Vowel team igh: "night" (igh)

"Fantastic! You just read nine words using nine different patterns. Now you're going to use all of those patterns together to read SENTENCES and SHORT STORIES. This is a big step — you are becoming a real reader."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach strategies for reading connected text (sentences and passages) and model the process.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Connected text:** Sentences and paragraphs — words that go together to tell something or share information. Not just single words, but actual reading!
- **Decode:** To figure out a word by looking at the letters and using your phonics patterns.
- **Self-correct:** When you read a word wrong, notice it doesn't make sense, and fix it.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Explain the shift from words to sentences.**
"Until today, you've been reading single words. That was important — you needed to build your pattern toolbox. Now you're going to read sentences. The same patterns apply! The difference is that you read word by word, left to right, and the words work together to tell you something."

**Step 2: Teach the decoding strategy.**
"When you get to a word you don't know, here is what to do:"

Write these steps on a card:
1. **Look** at the word. Do you see a pattern you know? (digraph, blend, vowel team, silent e?)
2. **Sound it out** using the patterns.
3. **Blend** the sounds together.
4. **Check:** Does it make sense in the sentence? If yes, keep going! If no, try again.

"Let me show you what this looks like."

**Step 3: Model reading a sentence.**
Write this sentence: "The goat went down the road to the green tree."

Read it aloud, thinking out loud at each word:
- "The — I know this word: 'the.'"
- "goat — I see oa: /oh/. /g/ /oh/ /t/... goat."
- "went — /w/ /e/ /n/ /t/... went. I see an ending blend: -nt."
- "down — /d/ /ow/ /n/... down."
- "the — 'the' again."
- "road — oa again! /r/ /oh/ /d/... road."
- "to — I know this word: 'to.'"
- "the — 'the.'"
- "green — ee vowel team: /gr/ /ee/ /n/... green. And gr is a beginning blend!"
- "tree — ee again: /tr/ /ee/... tree. Tr is a blend!"

"The goat went down the road to the green tree. Does that make sense? Yes! I can picture a goat walking down a road toward a big green tree."

### Worked Examples

**Example: Self-correction.**
Write: "The ship had a bright light."

Pretend to misread: "The ship had a bright... lit? Wait, that doesn't look right. I see i-g-h — that's the igh pattern: /iy/. And then t at the end. /l/ /iy/ /t/... light! The ship had a bright light. That makes sense!"

"See what I did? I made a mistake, noticed it, and fixed it. That's called self-correcting, and great readers do it all the time."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Read decodable passages together with facilitator support.

**Passage 1: "The Goat on the Road"**

Read this passage together. The student reads aloud; the facilitator helps when needed.

> A goat went down the road. The road was long. The goat saw a big oak tree. "I will rest," said the goat. The goat sat in the shade and ate a green leaf. "This is a fine day," said the goat.

**Comprehension questions** (after reading):
- "Where did the goat go?" (Down the road)
- "What did the goat see?" (A big oak tree)
- "What did the goat do under the tree?" (Rested and ate a green leaf)

*Facilitator prompts during reading:*
- *If the student gets stuck on a word: "Do you see a pattern you know? Look at the vowels." Give them 5-10 seconds before helping.*
- *If the student reads "shade" as "shad": "I see an e at the end. What does silent e do?" (Makes the vowel say its name.) "Try again."*
- *If the student reads a word wrong but it does not make sense: "Let's read that sentence again. Does '___' make sense here?" Encourage self-correction.*
- *If the student struggles significantly: read the passage in echo-reading style. You read one sentence, the student repeats it. Then try the next passage with the student reading first.*

**Passage 2: "The Ship at Night"**

> The ship went out at night. The sea was dark and deep. A bright light shone from the ship. "I can see the way!" said the man. The ship sailed slow. Then the sun came up, and the light was not needed.

**Comprehension questions:**
- "When did the ship go out?" (At night)
- "What shone from the ship?" (A bright light)
- "What happened at the end?" (The sun came up, and the light was not needed)

*If the student is doing well: let them read this passage more independently. Only intervene when they are stuck for more than 5 seconds.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads a passage independently and answers comprehension questions.

**Passage 3: "Rain and Snow"**

> It was a gray day. The rain came down fast. The rain hit the road and made big drops. Then it got cold. The rain became snow! The snow was white and light. It fell slow and soft. The trees and the road looked bright and clean. "I like the snow," said the girl. "But I need my coat!"

**★ On-level — Comprehension questions (answer in writing or orally):**
1. "What happened first — rain or snow?" (Rain came first)
2. "What color was the snow?" (White)
3. "What did the girl say she needed?" (Her coat)

**★★ Stretch:**
4. "Why did the rain become snow?" (It got cold)
5. "Find two words in the passage with vowel teams and write them down." (Possible: rain, road, snow, white, light, clean, coat, need — any two)

**★★★ Challenge:**
6. "Find one word in the passage for each pattern: a CVC word, a digraph word, a blend word, a CVCe word, and a vowel team word." (Examples: big = CVC, the = digraph, drops = blend, made = CVCe, rain = vowel team)
7. "Write one sentence about what YOU would do on a snowy day. Try to use at least one vowel team word."

### Interleaved Review
- Write the word "light" from memory. (igh pattern spelling review)
- "What is the silent e rule?" (When a word ends in e, the vowel says its name.)

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Assess whether the student can read connected text and comprehend what they read.

**Exit Ticket:**

Read this mini-passage aloud:

> The bee sat on a pink and white rose. It was a fine day. The bee flew to the oak tree and then went home.

1. Accuracy: Student reads the passage with no more than 2 errors.
2. "Where did the bee sit first?" (On a pink and white rose)
3. "Where did the bee go next?" (To the oak tree, then home)

**Mastery = Student reads with 2 or fewer errors AND answers at least 1 comprehension question correctly.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which phonics patterns caused the errors. Return to those specific lessons for targeted review.
- If the student can decode individual words but struggles with sentences: practice reading very short sentences (3-4 words) and build up gradually. "The cat sat." "I see the bee." "It is a big ship."
- If comprehension is the issue (student reads accurately but cannot answer questions): re-read the passage together. Ask questions DURING reading, not just after: "What happened so far?"
- This lesson can be repeated with new passages.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 10 (Fluency and Expression). Continue to read decodable passages daily.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who read the passages fluently:**
- Re-read Passage 3 and count how many vowel team words they can find. (rain, road, snow, white, light, clean, coat, need, girl)
- Write their own 3-sentence story using words from the unit. Help with spelling as needed — the goal is creative writing, not perfect spelling.
- Read the passages to a family member, a pet, or a stuffed animal. "Teaching" what you've read builds confidence and comprehension.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson is a milestone.** The student is transitioning from reading isolated words to reading connected text. This is a huge step. Be encouraging, patient, and celebratory.

**Expect the student to read slowly.** Connected text is much harder than isolated words because the student must decode each word AND hold the meaning of the sentence in memory. Reading that is slow and halting is completely normal at this stage. Speed comes with practice (Lesson 10 addresses fluency).

**High-frequency words in the passages:** The passages include some common words that the student may not decode phonetically but may recognize by sight: "the," "a," "to," "I," "said," "was," "it," "and," "at," "out," "not," "but," "my." If the student has learned these as sight words in kindergarten, great. If not, simply tell them the word and move on. Sight word instruction is important but is not the focus of this phonics lesson.

**Self-correction is a sign of strength, not weakness.** If the student reads a word wrong and then fixes it, praise this explicitly: "I love that you caught that and fixed it. That's exactly what good readers do."

**Do not ask the student to read a passage they are not ready for.** If the student struggled with Lessons 6-8 (long vowels and vowel teams), the passages will be very difficult. Go back and strengthen those patterns first. Connected text works best when the student can decode at least 90% of the words in the passage.

**The passages are carefully controlled.** Almost every word in the passages can be decoded using the patterns taught in Lessons 1-8. The few exceptions are common sight words. This is intentional — decodable text is designed so that the student CAN actually read it using what they know.

**Growth mindset note:** "You just read a STORY. Not just words — a story! That is a really big deal. Remember when we started this unit with simple words like 'cat' and 'dog'? Look how far you've come."
