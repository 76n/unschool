# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 2

## Topic
Consonant Digraphs: sh and ch

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain that a digraph is two letters that make one sound
- Identify the /sh/ and /ch/ sounds at the beginning and end of words
- Read at least 8 words containing sh or ch
- Write at least 3 words containing sh or ch from dictation

## Prerequisites
- Short vowel CVC word reading (Lesson 1 mastery)
- Letter-sound knowledge for s, h, c (individual sounds)

## Materials Needed
- Index cards with "sh" and "ch" written on them (one card each)
- Word cards (prepare before lesson — see word list below)
- Letter cards or magnetic letters
- Paper and pencil
- Two sorting labels: "sh" and "ch"

**Word cards to prepare:**
ship, shop, shed, shin, shell, fish, dish, wish, rush, cash, chin, chop, chip, chat, chess, rich, much, such, lunch, bench

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review short vowel CVC words from Lesson 1 and activate prior knowledge.

**Spaced Review:**
1. Read these CVC words: "hat, web, fin, dot, mud" (short vowel review)
2. "What vowel sound do you hear in the word 'sun'?" (/u/)
3. "Change the vowel in 'cat' to /o/. What word do you get?" (cot)

**Bridge to new learning:**
"You've been reading words where each letter makes its own sound. Today we're going to learn something new — sometimes TWO letters work together to make just ONE sound. How cool is that?"

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the concept of digraphs, specifically sh and ch.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Digraph:** Two letters that work as a team to make one single sound. You cannot hear the separate letters — you only hear the team sound.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the sh digraph.**
Hold up the "sh" card. Say:
"These two letters — s and h — are a team. When they stand next to each other, they do NOT say /s/ and then /h/. They say one brand-new sound: /sh/."

Say /sh/ and have the student repeat. Put your finger to your lips in the "quiet" gesture: "The /sh/ sound is like when you say 'shhhh, be quiet!' "

"Let's listen for /sh/ in a word. Listen: 'ship.' /sh/... /i/... /p/. Ship. The /sh/ is at the beginning."

"Now listen: 'fish.' /f/... /i/... /sh/. Fish. The /sh/ is at the end!"

**Step 2: Introduce the ch digraph.**
Hold up the "ch" card. Say:
"Here's another team: c and h. Together they say /ch/."

Say /ch/ and have the student repeat. "The /ch/ sound is like a train: 'ch-ch-ch-ch!' "

"Listen: 'chip.' /ch/... /i/... /p/. Chip. The /ch/ is at the beginning."

"Now listen: 'rich.' /r/... /i/... /ch/. Rich. The /ch/ is at the end!"

**Step 3: Contrast digraphs with individual letter sounds.**
"Remember — s by itself says /s/ (like 'sun'). h by itself says /h/ (like 'hat'). But s and h TOGETHER say /sh/ (like 'ship'). They make a completely new sound."

"Same with c and h. C by itself says /k/ (like 'cat'). H by itself says /h/. But c and h together say /ch/ (like 'chin')."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Read the word "shop."
- "I see s-h at the beginning. That's a digraph! It says /sh/."
- "Then o says /o/. Then p says /p/."
- "Blend: /sh/... /o/... /p/... shop!"

**Example 2:** Read the word "chat."
- "I see c-h at the beginning. That's a digraph! It says /ch/."
- "Then a says /a/. Then t says /t/."
- "Blend: /ch/... /a/... /t/... chat!"

**Example 3:** Read the word "wish."
- "I see w: /w/. Then i: /i/. Then s-h at the end: /sh/."
- "Blend: /w/... /i/... /sh/... wish!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice reading digraph words with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Read Together (4 minutes)**
Show these word cards one at a time: shin, chop, fish, chin, rush.

For each word:
- "Do you see a digraph? Where is it — beginning or end?"
- "What sound does the digraph make?"
- "Now read the whole word."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student tries to sound out "sh" as /s/ /h/, cover the "sh" with your finger and say: "Remember, these two letters are a team. They say /sh/ — one sound." Then uncover and have the student try again.*

*If the student confuses sh and ch: say both sounds. "/sh/ is a quiet, blowing sound. /ch/ is a short, choppy sound — like a sneeze! Listen: /sh/... /ch/... /sh/... /ch/. Which one do you hear in 'ship'?"*

**Activity 2: Build Words (3 minutes)**
Use letter cards. Lay out: sh, i, p.
"What word do these make?" (ship)

Now swap "sh" for "ch": ch, i, p.
"What word now?" (chip)

Try another set: sh, o, p → shop. Swap to ch, o, p → chop.

"See how changing the digraph changes the whole word?"

**Activity 3: Beginning or End? (3 minutes)**
Read each word aloud. Student says whether the digraph is at the beginning or end:
- "shell" (beginning — sh)
- "much" (end — ch)
- "shed" (beginning — sh)
- "rich" (end — ch)
- "dish" (end — sh)

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads and sorts digraph words independently, then practices writing.

**Activity: Sort and Write**

**★ On-level:**
Sort these 10 word cards under "sh" or "ch":
ship, chin, fish, chat, wish, chop, shell, such, shop, chip

Then write these 3 words from dictation (facilitator says the word, student writes it):
1. "ship" (s-h-i-p)
2. "chin" (c-h-i-n)
3. "fish" (f-i-s-h)

**★★ Stretch:**
Add these 4 words to the sort: dish, chess, rush, lunch

Write 2 more words from dictation:
4. "shed" (s-h-e-d)
5. "much" (m-u-c-h)

**★★★ Challenge:**
Write a sentence using at least two digraph words. Example: "The fish is in the dish." or "I will chat with Chad on the ship."

### Interleaved Review
- Read these CVC words (Lesson 1 review): pet, tub, log, map, dig
- "What vowel sound is in 'net'?" (/e/)

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can read and identify sh and ch digraphs.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Read these words aloud: **shed, chip, rush, bench** (Student reads all 4)
2. "What two letters make the /sh/ sound?" (s and h)
3. "What two letters make the /ch/ sound?" (c and h)
4. "Is the digraph at the beginning or end of the word 'lunch'?" (end)

**Mastery = Student reads at least 3 of 4 words correctly AND correctly identifies both digraphs.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Focus on whichever digraph is weaker (sh or ch — not both at once).
- Practice with just 5 words for the weaker digraph: build them with letter cards, read them, write them.
- Use the "team" metaphor repeatedly: tape sh or ch cards together to reinforce that they are one unit.
- Repeat this lesson next session.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (Consonant Digraphs: th, wh). Continue to include sh/ch words in warm-ups.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who master sh and ch quickly:**
- Go on a "digraph hunt" around the house — find objects that have /sh/ or /ch/ sounds in their names (shoes, chair, shirt, kitchen, shower, cheese).
- Play "Digraph or Not?": facilitator says a word, student gives a thumbs up if it has a digraph, thumbs down if not. Mix digraph and CVC words: shop (up), cup (down), chin (up), sit (down), fish (up), red (down).
- Read this silly sentence: "Chuck ate chips and cherries on his chair in the shed."

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Key concept for this lesson:** The hardest part is not reading the digraph words — it is understanding that two letters can make one sound. Many students initially want to say /s/ then /h/ for "sh." Be patient and consistent: every time, remind them "these are a team."

**Physical cue:** Have the student cover the two digraph letters with one finger (not two). This reinforces that sh is one unit.

**Spelling note:** When writing digraph words, some students write "sip" instead of "ship" — they hear the /sh/ but only write the s. Remind them: "The /sh/ sound needs BOTH letters: s AND h."

**ch can also say /k/:** In some words (like "school" or "Christmas"), ch says /k/. Do NOT bring this up in this lesson. For now, ch = /ch/. The irregular pronunciations come later.

**Pacing:** If the student needs more time with sh before introducing ch, split this lesson across two sessions. Day 1: sh only. Day 2: ch, then practice both.
