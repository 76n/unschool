# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 6

## Topic
Long Vowel Patterns: The Silent e (CVCe)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain the "silent e" rule: when a word ends in e, the vowel before it says its name (long sound)
- Read at least 10 CVCe words accurately
- Transform CVC words into CVCe words by adding silent e (and explain how the vowel sound changes)
- Distinguish between short vowel CVC words and long vowel CVCe words

## Prerequisites
- Short vowel CVC word reading (Lesson 1 mastery)
- Consonant digraphs and blends (Lessons 2-5)

## Materials Needed
- Letter cards or magnetic letters (including multiple e cards)
- Word cards (prepare CVC/CVCe pairs — see list below)
- Paper and pencil
- Small sticky notes or index cards with "e" written on them (for the transformation activity)

**CVC/CVCe word pairs to prepare on cards:**
cap/cape, tap/tape, mad/made, can/cane, pan/pane, hop/hope, not/note, rob/robe, cod/code, mop/mope, kit/kite, hid/hide, pin/pine, rip/ripe, dim/dime, tub/tube, cut/cute, cub/cube, hug/huge

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review prior phonics patterns and assess short vowel fluency.

**Spaced Review:**
1. Read these words with beginning blends: "glad, trip, slim" (Lesson 4 review)
2. Read these words with ending blends: "hand, must, belt" (Lesson 5 review)
3. Read these digraph words: "chip, them, whip" (Lessons 2-3 review)
4. "What short vowel sound is in the word 'hop'?" (/o/) "What about 'cap'?" (/a/)

**Bridge to new learning:**
"So far, you've been reading words with SHORT vowel sounds — like 'hop' where the o says /o/. Today, you're going to learn a magic trick. One little letter can change a short vowel into a LONG vowel — and that letter is... e! But here's the really cool part: the e itself is totally silent. It doesn't make any sound at all. It just changes the other vowel."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the CVCe (silent e) pattern and demonstrate how it transforms short vowel words.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Short vowel:** The quick sound a vowel makes in a CVC word. /a/ as in "cap," /o/ as in "hop."
- **Long vowel:** When a vowel "says its name." Long a sounds like the letter name "A" (as in "cape"), long o sounds like the letter name "O" (as in "hope").
- **Silent e:** An e at the end of a word that makes no sound but tells the vowel before it to say its long sound (its name).
- **CVCe word:** A word with the pattern consonant-vowel-consonant-e, like "cape" (c-a-p-e) or "kite" (k-i-t-e).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Demonstrate with "cap" and "cape."**
Write "cap" on paper. Read it together: "/k/ /a/ /p/ — cap. The a says /a/ — its short sound."

Now add an e at the end: "cap**e**"

"Watch what happens when I add an e to the end. The e doesn't make any sound — it's silent. But it reaches over the p and taps the a on the shoulder and says 'Say your name!' Now the a says /ay/ — its long sound!"

Read it: "/k/ /ay/ /p/ — cape! Like a superhero's cape!"

**Step 2: Demonstrate with more pairs.**
Do the same with these pairs, using the "silent e reaches over and taps the vowel" story:

- "hop" → "hope": "The o changes from /o/ to /oh/. Hop... hope!"
- "kit" → "kite": "The i changes from /i/ to /iy/. Kit... kite!"
- "cub" → "cube": "The u changes from /u/ to /yoo/. Cub... cube!"

**Step 3: State the rule.**
"Here is the pattern: when you see a word that ends in e, the e is silent, and it makes the vowel before it say its NAME — its long sound. We call this the 'silent e' rule or the 'magic e' rule."

"Short a says /a/. Long a says /ay/.
Short e says /e/. Long e says /ee/.
Short i says /i/. Long i says /iy/.
Short o says /o/. Long o says /oh/.
Short u says /u/. Long u says /yoo/."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Read the word "made."
- "I see an e at the end. This is a silent e word!"
- "The vowel is a. Silent e makes it say its name: /ay/."
- "m: /m/. a: /ay/. d: /d/. The e is silent."
- "Put it together: /m/... /ay/... /d/... made!"

**Example 2:** Read the word "pine."
- "E at the end — silent e word!"
- "The vowel is i. Silent e makes it say /iy/."
- "/p/... /iy/... /n/... pine!"

**Example 3:** Read the word "note."
- "E at the end — silent e!"
- "The vowel is o. It says /oh/."
- "/n/... /oh/... /t/... note!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice the silent e transformation and read CVCe words with support.

**Activity 1: Magic e Transformation (4 minutes)**
Place CVC word cards on the table. Have small "e" cards ready.

Show "cap." Read it together: "cap."
"Now put the magic e at the end." Student places the e card. "Read the new word." (cape)
"What happened to the vowel sound?" (It changed from /a/ to /ay/.)

Do the same with:
- hop → hope
- pin → pine
- cut → cute
- not → note

*Facilitator prompt: If the student still reads the short vowel even with the e present (reads "cape" as "cap"), cover the e and say: "Without the e, it says 'cap.' Now uncover the e. The e is silent, but it tells the a to say its name. What does a say when it says its name? /ay/. So the word is...?"*

*If the student tries to pronounce the final e: "Remember, the e is a silent helper. It doesn't make any sound. It just changes the vowel. We don't say 'cap-eh' — we say 'cape.'"*

**Activity 2: Read CVCe Words (3 minutes)**
Show these word cards one at a time: tape, hide, robe, dime, tube.

For each word:
- "Do you see a silent e?"
- "What is the vowel? What long sound does it make?"
- "Read the whole word."

**Activity 3: CVC or CVCe? (3 minutes)**
Mix CVC and CVCe word cards. Student reads each word and says whether the vowel is short or long:
- "can" (short a) vs. "cane" (long a)
- "rip" (short i) vs. "ripe" (long i)
- "cod" (short o) vs. "code" (long o)
- "tub" (short u) vs. "tube" (long u)

*If the student is uncertain: "Look at the end of the word. Is there an e? If yes, the vowel says its name. If no, the vowel says its short sound."*

**Purpose:** Student reads and writes CVCe words independently.

**★ On-level:**
Read each word and write "short" or "long" next to the vowel:
1. cape (long a)
2. hop (short o)
3. kite (long i)
4. tub (short u)
5. hope (long o)
6. pin (short i)
7. cube (long u)
8. mad (short a)

Write these 3 words from dictation:
1. "tape" (t-a-p-e)
2. "hide" (h-i-d-e)
3. "note" (n-o-t-e)

**★★ Stretch:**
Read these CVCe words: robe, dime, pane, huge, code

Add the silent e to make a new word. Write both words:
- "cap" → ___ (cape)
- "dim" → ___ (dime)
- "rob" → ___ (robe)

**★★★ Challenge:**
Write a sentence using at least two CVCe words. Example: "I hope to ride my bike and fly a kite."

Then try this riddle: "I am a CVC word that means 'not wild.' Add a silent e and I become a word for what your face does when you're happy." (tam... no. Think: "tame" = not wild. No — the CVC word is actually "not a perfect riddle." Better riddle: "I am 'pin' — add an e and I become a tree." (pine))

- "What beginning blend do you hear in 'grin'?" (gr)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student understands and can apply the silent e rule.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Read these CVCe words aloud: **lake, bite, rope, cute** (Student reads all 4)
2. "What happens to the vowel sound when you add a silent e to the end of a CVC word?" (The vowel says its name / makes the long sound)
3. "Add a silent e to 'hop.' What is the new word? What changed?" (hope — the o changed from /o/ to /oh/)

**Mastery = Student reads at least 3 of 4 words correctly AND correctly explains the silent e rule.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Focus on one vowel at a time. Start with a (cap/cape, tap/tape, mad/made) until the student consistently reads long a words, then add o, then i, then u.
- Use the physical "add the e" activity repeatedly — the hands-on transformation makes the abstract rule concrete.
- Post a reminder card: "When I see e at the end, the vowel says its NAME."
- Repeat this lesson next session. Consider splitting across two sessions: Day 1 = a and o pairs; Day 2 = i and u pairs, plus mixed practice.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (Vowel Teams: ai, ay, ee, ea). Continue to include CVCe words in warm-ups.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is one of the most important lessons in the unit.** The CVCe pattern appears in hundreds of common English words. If the student learns this one rule, their reading vocabulary expands enormously.

**The "magic e" or "bossy e" story helps.** Young children remember rules better when they are connected to a story or character. The idea that the e "reaches over" the consonant to tap the vowel and say "say your name!" is a powerful mnemonic. Use it consistently.

**Common errors:**
- Reading the e as a separate sound ("cap-ee" instead of "cape") — Remind: "The e is silent. We don't say it. It just changes the vowel."
- Ignoring the e entirely (reading "cape" as "cap") — Point to the e. "This e is there for a reason. What does it tell the a to do?"
- Over-applying the rule (reading all words as if they have silent e) — This is actually a GOOD sign that the student has learned the rule. Gently redirect: "Check — is there an e at the end of this word? No? Then the vowel keeps its short sound."

**Silent e with blends and digraphs:** Words like "shake," "whale," "grape," and "throne" combine silent e with digraphs or blends. These are not introduced here but will appear naturally in later reading. If a student encounters one, help them break it apart: "shake" = sh (digraph) + a (long, because of silent e) + k + e (silent).

**Not all final-e words follow the rule.** Words like "have," "give," "come," and "love" have a final e but a short vowel. Do NOT bring these up in this lesson. The student needs to internalize the regular pattern first. Exceptions come later.

**Growth mindset note:** "Learning the silent e rule is like getting a key that unlocks hundreds of new words. Some of those words might trick you at first, and that's completely okay. Keep trying — you're building real reading power."
