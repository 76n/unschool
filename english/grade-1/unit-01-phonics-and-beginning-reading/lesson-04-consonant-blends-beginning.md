# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 4

## Topic
Beginning Consonant Blends

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain the difference between a blend and a digraph (blend = you hear both sounds; digraph = you hear one new sound)
- Identify and produce beginning l-blends (bl, cl, fl, gl, pl, sl) and r-blends (br, cr, dr, fr, gr, tr)
- Read at least 10 words with beginning consonant blends
- Sort words by blend type

## Prerequisites
- Short vowel CVC word reading (Lesson 1 mastery)
- Consonant digraphs sh, ch, th, wh (Lessons 2-3 mastery)

## Materials Needed
- Index cards with blends written on them: bl, cl, fl, gl, pl, sl, br, cr, dr, fr, gr, tr
- Word cards (prepare before lesson — see word list below)
- Letter cards or magnetic letters
- Paper and pencil
- Sorting labels: "l-blends" and "r-blends"

**Word cards to prepare:**
black, clap, flag, glad, plan, sled, block, clip, flat, glen, plus, slim, brag, crab, drip, frog, grab, trip, brick, crop, drum, fresh, grin, trap

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review digraphs from Lessons 2-3 and short vowels.

**Spaced Review:**
1. Read these digraph words: "shell, chin, this, when" (Lessons 2-3 review)
2. Read these CVC words: "mug, pen, hop, lid" (Lesson 1 review)
3. "What digraph do you hear in 'bath'?" (th) "In 'wish'?" (sh) "In 'chat'?" (ch)

**Bridge to new learning:**
"You know that digraphs are two letters that make ONE sound. Today you'll learn about blends. Blends are also two letters side by side, but this time you can hear BOTH letters. It's different from a digraph — and once you know the difference, you'll be able to read a ton of new words!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce consonant blends and contrast them with digraphs.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Blend:** Two consonant letters side by side where you hear BOTH sounds. The sounds are blended together quickly, but both are there.
- **l-blend:** A blend where the second letter is l (bl, cl, fl, gl, pl, sl).
- **r-blend:** A blend where the second letter is r (br, cr, dr, fr, gr, tr).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Contrast blends with digraphs.**
Write "ship" and "slip" on paper, side by side.

"Look at these two words. 'Ship' starts with sh — a digraph. You hear ONE sound: /sh/. But 'slip' starts with sl — a blend. Listen carefully: /s/... /l/... You can hear BOTH sounds, /s/ and /l/, sliding together quickly."

Say "ship" and "slip" several times. Have the student repeat.

"In a digraph, the two letters lose their own sounds and make a new one. In a blend, the two letters KEEP their sounds — you just say them fast."

**Step 2: Introduce l-blends.**
Show the blend cards one at a time: bl, cl, fl, gl, pl, sl.

For each, model the blend and a word:
- "bl: /b/... /l/ — block. Listen: /bl/... /o/... /k/. Block."
- "cl: /k/... /l/ — clap. /kl/... /a/... /p/. Clap."
- "fl: /f/... /l/ — flag. /fl/... /a/... /g/. Flag."
- "gl: /g/... /l/ — glad. /gl/... /a/... /d/. Glad."
- "pl: /p/... /l/ — plan. /pl/... /a/... /n/. Plan."
- "sl: /s/... /l/ — sled. /sl/... /e/... /d/. Sled."

"Notice that the second letter is always l. These are called l-blends."

**Step 3: Introduce r-blends.**
Show the blend cards: br, cr, dr, fr, gr, tr.

- "br: /b/... /r/ — brag. /br/... /a/... /g/. Brag."
- "cr: /k/... /r/ — crab. /kr/... /a/... /b/. Crab."
- "dr: /d/... /r/ — drip. /dr/... /i/... /p/. Drip."
- "fr: /f/... /r/ — frog. /fr/... /o/... /g/. Frog."
- "gr: /g/... /r/ — grab. /gr/... /a/... /b/. Grab."
- "tr: /t/... /r/ — trip. /tr/... /i/... /p/. Trip."

"And these all have r as the second letter. R-blends!"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Read the word "flat."
- "I see f-l at the beginning. That's an l-blend. I hear BOTH sounds: /f/ and /l/."
- "Then a: /a/. Then t: /t/."
- "Blend it all: /fl/... /a/... /t/... flat!"

**Example 2:** Read the word "grin."
- "I see g-r at the beginning. That's an r-blend: /g/ and /r/."
- "Then i: /i/. Then n: /n/."
- "Blend: /gr/... /i/... /n/... grin!"

**Example 3: Blend vs. Digraph contrast.**
Write "chop" and "clap" side by side.
- "Chop starts with ch — a digraph. ONE sound: /ch/."
- "Clap starts with cl — a blend. TWO sounds: /k/ and /l/."
- "See the difference?"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice reading blend words and distinguishing blends from digraphs.

**Activity 1: Read l-Blend Words (3 minutes)**
Show word cards: black, clip, flag, glad, plan, slim.

For each: "What blend do you see at the beginning? Can you hear both sounds? Now read the word."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student adds a vowel between the blend letters (says "buh-lack" instead of "black"), say: "Try to slide the two sounds together without putting a sound in between. Listen: /bl/... not /buh-luh/. /bl/ack. Now you try."*

**Activity 2: Read r-Blend Words (3 minutes)**
Show word cards: brag, drip, frog, grin, trap, crop.

Same process: identify the blend, hear both sounds, read the word.

*If the student struggles with r-blends: r-blends are generally harder than l-blends. Say the two sounds slowly, then speed up: "/t/... /r/... /tr/... /tr/ip... trip." Give extra practice with the hardest one (often dr or tr).*

**Activity 3: Blend or Digraph? (4 minutes)**
Show these word cards one at a time. Student reads the word and then says "blend" or "digraph":
- flag (blend — fl)
- ship (digraph — sh)
- trip (blend — tr)
- chin (digraph — ch)
- glad (blend — gl)
- this (digraph — th)
- crab (blend — cr)
- when (digraph — wh)

*If the student is unsure: "Can you hear TWO separate sounds at the start, or just ONE? If two, it's a blend. If one new sound, it's a digraph."*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads, sorts, and writes blend words independently.

**★ On-level (10 words):**
Sort these words under "l-blend" or "r-blend":
clap, drip, flag, grin, plan, trip, block, frog, sled, brag

Write these 3 words from dictation:
1. "flag" (f-l-a-g)
2. "trip" (t-r-i-p)
3. "clap" (c-l-a-p)

**★★ Stretch (5 additional words):**
Add to the sort: slim, crop, glen, trap, flat

Write 2 more from dictation:
4. "grin" (g-r-i-n)
5. "sled" (s-l-e-d)

**★★★ Challenge:**
Write a sentence that uses at least two blend words. Example: "The frog did a flip on the flat rock."

Then try this: "What word do you get if you change the blend in 'trip' to 'dr'?" (drip) "What about 'fl' to 'sl' in 'flag'?" (slag — not a common word, but the student can try the sound!)

### Interleaved Review
- Read these digraph words: "fish, much, path, whip" (Lessons 2-3 review)
- "What short vowel sound do you hear in 'drum'?" (/u/)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can read beginning blend words and distinguish blends from digraphs.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Read these words aloud: **clip, drum, flag, grab, slim, trap** (Student reads all 6)
2. "What is the difference between a blend and a digraph?" (In a blend, you hear both sounds. In a digraph, two letters make one new sound.)
3. "Is 'shop' a blend or a digraph?" (digraph — sh) "Is 'plan' a blend or a digraph?" (blend — pl)

**Mastery = Student reads at least 5 of 6 words correctly AND correctly identifies blend vs. digraph in question 3.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- If the student reads blends but confuses blend vs. digraph labels: the reading is what matters most. Continue to practice the contrast, but do not hold back advancement if the student can decode the words.
- If the student cannot decode blend words: focus on l-blends first (they are typically easier). Practice 5-6 l-blend words until comfortable, then add r-blends.
- Consider splitting this lesson across two sessions: Day 1 = l-blends, Day 2 = r-blends.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 5 (Ending Consonant Blends). Continue to include beginning blend words in warm-ups.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who master beginning blends quickly:**
- "How many bl words can you think of?" (black, block, blue, blend, blank, blot, bless, etc.) Try each blend family.
- Play "Blend Swap": start with "trip" — change tr to dr (drip), then to gr (grip), then to fl (flip). How many real words can you make by changing just the blend?
- Go on a blend hunt: find 5 objects in the room that start with a blend (clock, plate, crayon, drawer, glass, etc.).

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The biggest challenge in this lesson** is not reading the blends — it is distinguishing blends from digraphs conceptually. Some students will read all the words fine but get confused when asked to sort or label. That is okay. The important skill is READING. The labeling reinforces understanding but is secondary.

**Inserting a vowel between blend letters** (saying "buh-lack" for "black") is extremely common. It happens because the student is trying to sound out each letter separately, as they did with CVC words. Coach them to slide the sounds together: "Say /b/. Now say /l/. Now say them fast together: /bl/. Faster: /bl/. Good! Now add the rest: /bl/ack."

**This lesson has a lot of blends.** The student does NOT need to master all 12 blends in one session. The goal is to understand the concept (two letters, both sounds heard) and read several examples. Mastery of all specific blends develops through continued exposure in upcoming lessons and warm-ups.

**If you split into two sessions:** Session 1 covers l-blends (bl, cl, fl, gl, pl, sl) with the same lesson structure. Session 2 covers r-blends and adds the blend vs. digraph sorting. This is a perfectly reasonable approach, especially for students who need more time.

**Growth mindset note:** "This is a big lesson with lots of new patterns. You do not need to remember them all perfectly today. The more you read, the more natural blends will feel. Every reader builds these skills one step at a time."
