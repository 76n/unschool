# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 5

## Topic
Ending Consonant Blends

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify consonant blends at the end of words
- Read at least 10 words with ending consonant blends (-nd, -nk, -nt, -mp, -st, -sk, -lk, -lt)
- Spell words with ending blends from dictation
- Distinguish between beginning blends, ending blends, and digraphs

## Prerequisites
- Short vowel CVC word reading (Lesson 1 mastery)
- Consonant digraphs (Lessons 2-3)
- Beginning consonant blends (Lesson 4 mastery)

## Materials Needed
- Index cards with ending blends: -nd, -nk, -nt, -mp, -st, -sk, -lk, -lt
- Word cards (prepare before lesson — see word list below)
- Letter cards or magnetic letters
- Paper and pencil
- Sorting labels for ending blend groups

**Word cards to prepare:**

**Purpose:** Review beginning blends and digraphs from prior lessons.

**Spaced Review:**
1. Read these beginning blend words: "clap, frog, sled, trip" (Lesson 4 review)
2. Read these digraph words: "shop, this, chin, when" (Lessons 2-3 review)
3. "Is the word 'drum' a blend or a digraph at the beginning?" (blend — dr)
4. Read these CVC words: "wig, hen, cob" (Lesson 1 review)

**Bridge to new learning:**
"You already know that blends can come at the BEGINNING of words — like 'flag' and 'trip.' Today you'll discover that blends can also come at the END of words! Once you know this, you can read words like 'hand,' 'jump,' and 'fast.' "

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce ending consonant blends and practice decoding words that end with two consonant sounds.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Ending blend:** Two consonant letters at the end of a word where you hear both sounds. Same idea as a beginning blend, just in a different position.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Show that blends can be at the end.**
Write "hand" on paper. Point to each part:
"Let's read this word. /h/... /a/... and then at the end I see n-d. Those are both consonants, and I can hear both sounds: /n/ /d/. It's a blend at the END."

"Blend it all together: /h/... /a/... /n/... /d/... hand!"

**Step 2: Introduce common ending blends with examples.**
Write each blend and a key word:

- "-nd: /n/ /d/ — hand" (Say: "Listen to the end of 'hand.' /n/ then /d/. Both sounds.")
- "-nk: /n/ /k/ — sink" ("At the end of 'sink': /n/ then /k/.")
- "-nt: /n/ /t/ — tent" ("At the end of 'tent': /n/ then /t/.")
- "-mp: /m/ /p/ — jump" ("At the end of 'jump': /m/ then /p/.")
- "-st: /s/ /t/ — fast" ("At the end of 'fast': /s/ then /t/.")
- "-sk: /s/ /k/ — mask" ("At the end of 'mask': /s/ then /k/.")
- "-lk: /l/ /k/ — milk" ("At the end of 'milk': /l/ then /k/.")
- "-lt: /l/ /t/ — melt" ("At the end of 'melt': /l/ then /t/.")

"Just like beginning blends, you hear BOTH sounds — they are just at the end of the word instead of the beginning."

**Step 3: Demonstrate the full decoding process.**
"When you see a word with an ending blend, start from the beginning like always. Sound out each part, and at the end, blend the last two sounds together."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Read the word "camp."
- "/k/... /a/... now I see m-p at the end: /m/ /p/."
- "Put it together: /k/... /a/... /m/... /p/... camp!"

**Example 2:** Read the word "best."
- "/b/... /e/... now s-t at the end: /s/ /t/."
- "Put it together: /b/... /e/... /s/... /t/... best!"

**Example 3:** Read the word "pink."
- "/p/... /i/... now n-k at the end: /n/ /k/."
- "Put it together: /p/... /i/... /n/... /k/... pink!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice reading ending blend words with support.

**Activity 1: Read Ending Blend Words (4 minutes)**
Show these word cards one at a time: sand, tank, hunt, damp, dust, desk, milk, belt.

For each word:
- "What blend do you see at the end?"
- "Read the whole word."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student drops the final blend and says "san" instead of "sand," point to the last two letters: "Don't forget these two letters at the end. What sounds do they make? /n/ /d/. Now put the whole word together."*

*If the student struggles to blend four sounds: break the word into two parts. "Sand" = "san" + "d." Say the first part, then add the last sound. Or try: "sa" + "nd."*

**Activity 2: Word Building with Ending Blends (3 minutes)**
Use letter cards. Build "band."

"Let's change the ending blend. Take off -nd and put -nk." (bank)
"Now change -nk to -nt." (bant — not a real word, but the student can decode it!)
"Let's try -mp." (bamp — also not real, but good practice!)
"Okay, change the beginning. Put 'l' at the front and -nd at the end." (land)

*This activity shows that ending blends are swappable, just like beginning sounds.*

**Activity 3: Beginning Blend, Ending Blend, or Digraph? (3 minutes)**
Say each word. Student identifies whether the special pattern is a beginning blend, ending blend, or digraph:
- "trip" (beginning blend — tr)
- "hand" (ending blend — nd)
- "ship" (digraph — sh)
- "desk" (ending blend — sk)
- "flag" (beginning blend — fl)
- "bath" (digraph — th)

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student can read and spell ending blend words.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Read these words aloud: **sand, pink, must, belt, camp** (Student reads all 5)
2. "What ending blend do you hear in the word 'desk'?" (-sk)
3. Write the word "hunt" from dictation. (h-u-n-t)

**Mastery = Student reads at least 4 of 5 words correctly AND spells "hunt" with all 4 letters correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which ending blends are most difficult. Focus on the two most common ones first: -nd and -st.
- Use word families to build fluency: hand, band, sand, land; fast, last, past, mast.
- Have the student clap each sound in the word separately before blending: /h/ (clap) /a/ (clap) /n/ (clap) /d/ (clap) = hand.
- Repeat this lesson next session.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 6 (Long Vowel Patterns: Silent e). Continue to include ending blend words in warm-ups.

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who master ending blends quickly:**
- Words with BOTH beginning and ending blends: stand, blast, brand, crisp, clamp, stump, brisk, frost. Can the student read these?
- Play "Rhyme Families": pick an ending blend and list rhyming words. -and: band, hand, land, sand, stand. -ink: pink, sink, wink, think, drink.
- Dictation challenge: facilitator says 5 words in a row, student writes them all, then checks together.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Ending blends feel different from beginning blends** because the student has to hold more sounds in working memory. With a beginning blend word like "flag," the student decodes left to right: /fl/... /a/... /g/. With an ending blend word like "fast," by the time they get to -st, they need to remember the /f/ and /a/ they already decoded. Some students will need to attempt the word multiple times — that is fine.

**Dropping the final consonant** is the most common error. Students may read "hand" as "han" or "desk" as "des." This is because they are used to CVC words with one consonant at the end. Consistently point to the final two letters and remind them: "There are two sounds at the end. Don't stop too soon!"

**The -nk blend is special.** In -nk, the n does not make its usual /n/ sound — it makes the "ng" sound /ng/. So "sink" is actually /s/ /i/ /ng/ /k/, not /s/ /i/ /n/ /k/. Do not explain this to the student — they will produce it correctly from natural speech. If they read "sink" and it sounds right, they are doing it correctly even if the technical phonetics are more complex than what we teach.

**Words with both beginning AND ending blends** (like "stand" or "blast") are very advanced for this lesson. They are included only in the challenge tier. If the student can read them, that is wonderful. If not, they will encounter these words again and build up to them naturally.

**Growth mindset note:** "You are learning to read longer and more interesting words now. Remember, every great reader started exactly where you are, figuring out one sound at a time."
