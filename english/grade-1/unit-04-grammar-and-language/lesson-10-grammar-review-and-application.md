# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Topic
Grammar Review and Application — Cumulative Review with a Mini-Writing Project

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify and use nouns (common, proper, possessive), verbs, and adjectives in their own writing
- Write a short piece (4-6 sentences) that demonstrates correct end punctuation and conventional spelling
- Edit their own writing using a grammar checklist covering all Unit 4 concepts

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of common nouns, proper nouns, and possessive nouns (Lessons 1-3)
- Understanding of pronouns and singular/plural nouns (Lessons 4-5)
- Ability to identify and use action verbs (Lesson 6)
- Ability to identify and use adjectives (Lesson 7)
- Knowledge of end punctuation: periods, question marks, exclamation points (Lesson 8)
- Knowledge of CVC and CVCe spelling patterns and common sight words (Lesson 9)
- Ability to write complete sentences (Unit 3, Lesson 2)

## Materials Needed
- Chart paper or whiteboard and marker
- Pencil and lined paper (at least 2 sheets — one for drafting, one for the final copy)
- Colored pencils or crayons (at least 4 colors for the grammar highlight activity)
- The Grammar Checklist (see Direct Instruction — write or print this before the lesson)
- Eraser
- All anchor charts from Lessons 1-9 (if available) — these serve as reference during writing

**Prepare before the lesson:**
Write or print the Grammar Checklist on a separate piece of paper or on chart paper so the student can refer to it while editing. (See Step 3 in Direct Instruction for the checklist.)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review all key grammar concepts from the unit in a fast, fun format.

**Activity: Grammar Lightning Round**
Say: "Today is our big review day! You have learned SO much grammar in this unit. Let's see how much you remember with a quick lightning round. I will ask a question and you answer as fast as you can. Ready? Go!"

1. "What is a noun?" (A word that names a person, place, thing, or animal.)
2. "Give me an example of a proper noun." (Any name that starts with a capital letter — Sam, Texas, Monday, etc.)
3. "What is a verb?" (An action word — something you can do.)
4. "Act out a verb — any verb!" (Student acts out a verb — jump, clap, dance, etc.)
5. "What is an adjective?" (A describing word — it tells more about a noun.)
6. "Give me an adjective that describes a dog." (Big, small, fluffy, brown, happy, loud, etc.)
7. "What end mark goes at the end of a question?" (Question mark — ?)
8. "What end mark shows strong feeling?" (Exclamation point — !)
9. "Spell the sight word 'said.'" (S-A-I-D.)
10. "What is the pattern for 'cake' — CVC or CVCe?" (CVCe.)

Say: "Ten for ten — or close to it! You have learned nouns, verbs, adjectives, punctuation, and spelling. Today you are going to put ALL of these skills together and write something amazing. Then you are going to edit your own work like a real author."

*Facilitator note: The lightning round is intentionally fast and fun. It is a confidence booster — the student realizes they know a lot! If they miss one, gently give the answer and say, "That's okay — we'll practice that one more today." Keep the energy positive.*

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the mini-writing project and the Grammar Checklist for self-editing.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Edit:** To look over your writing and fix mistakes. Authors always edit their work!
- **Grammar checklist:** A list of things to check in your writing — nouns, verbs, adjectives, punctuation, and spelling.
- **Draft:** Your first try at writing something. It does not have to be perfect — that is what editing is for.
- **Final copy:** Your writing after you have edited and fixed it. This is the polished version.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the mini-writing project.**
Say: "Today you are going to write a short piece about ONE of these topics. You get to choose!"

Write the topic choices on chart paper:

| Pick a Topic! |
|---|
| 1. My Favorite Animal |
| 2. My Best Day Ever |
| 3. What I Like to Do After School |
| 4. A Person I Love |

Say: "You will write 4 to 6 sentences about your topic. This is not just a grammar worksheet — this is REAL WRITING. You are going to use everything you have learned: nouns, verbs, adjectives, punctuation, and good spelling."

Give the student a moment to choose their topic.

**Step 2: Model a short piece together.**
Say: "Before you write on your own, let me show you how I would do it. I am going to pick Topic 1 — My Favorite Animal — and write 4 sentences."

Write on chart paper, thinking aloud:

"My favorite animal is a dog." — "I started with a capital letter. 'Dog' is a common noun. 'Favorite' is an adjective. I ended with a period because it is a telling sentence."

"I have a big brown dog named Max." — "'Big' and 'brown' are adjectives — they describe the dog. 'Max' is a proper noun — it starts with a capital letter. 'Named' is a verb."

"Do you know what he likes to do?" — "This is a question, so I used a question mark at the end. 'Do' and 'likes' are verbs."

"He runs and jumps all day!" — "This shows excitement, so I used an exclamation point. 'Runs' and 'jumps' are action verbs."

Say: "See? I used nouns, verbs, adjectives, all three end marks, and I checked my spelling. Now let's look at the tool that will help YOU do the same thing."

**Step 3: Introduce the Grammar Checklist.**
Write (or reveal the pre-made version of) this checklist:

---

**My Grammar Checklist**

Read your writing and check each box:

- [ ] **Nouns:** Did I use nouns (people, places, things, animals)? Did I capitalize proper nouns?
- [ ] **Verbs:** Does every sentence have a verb (action word)?
- [ ] **Adjectives:** Did I use adjectives to make my writing interesting? (At least 2 adjectives in my piece.)
- [ ] **Capital Letters:** Does every sentence start with a capital letter?
- [ ] **End Punctuation:** Does every sentence end with a period, question mark, or exclamation point?
- [ ] **Spelling:** Did I spell CVC and CVCe words correctly? Did I check my sight words (said, was, have, they, what, where)?
- [ ] **Complete Sentences:** Does every sentence have a subject (who/what) AND a verb (does/is)?

---

Say: "After you write your draft, you will go through this checklist and check each box. If you find a mistake, fix it! Then you will write your final copy with all the fixes. This is what real authors do."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Editing for missing end punctuation.
- Draft sentence: "I love my cat"
- "Let me check the Grammar Checklist. End punctuation — does this sentence end with a period, question mark, or exclamation point? NO! I need to add one. This is a telling sentence, so I will add a period: 'I love my cat.'"

**Example 2:** Editing for missing adjectives.
- Draft sentence: "I have a dog."
- "The checklist says I should use adjectives. Can I make this more interesting? What does my dog look like? 'I have a big fluffy dog.' Better!"

**Example 3:** Editing for spelling.
- Draft sentence: "I rid my bik to the park."
- "'Rid' — is that right? I hear /iy/ — that is a long vowel. I need the CVCe pattern: R-I-D-E. 'Ride.'"
- "'Bik' — same thing. Long /iy/. B-I-K-E. 'Bike.'"
- Fixed: "I ride my bike to the park."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice with support.

**Activity 1: Planning and Drafting (7 minutes)**
The student writes their draft (4-6 sentences) on their topic.

Before writing, help the student plan orally:
- "Tell me about your topic. What do you want to say?"
- "What nouns will you use? Any proper nouns?"
- "What verbs — what actions happen?"
- "What adjectives can you use to help the reader picture it?"
- "Can you include at least one question or exclamation?"

Let the student talk through their ideas, then say: "Great plan! Now write it down. Do not worry about making it perfect — that is what the checklist is for."

While the student writes, observe quietly. Offer help ONLY if the student is stuck:
- If stuck on a word: "Stretch it out. What sounds do you hear?"
- If stuck on what to say next: "What else do you want to tell me about [topic]?"
- If they forget punctuation: Do NOT correct yet — they will catch it during editing.

*Facilitator prompt: Resist the urge to correct mistakes during drafting. The editing process with the checklist IS the learning. Let the student make mistakes so they can find and fix them.*

**Activity 2: Checklist Editing (3 minutes)**
After the student finishes the draft, go through the Grammar Checklist together.

Read each item aloud. The student looks at their writing and checks:
- "Nouns — can you circle the nouns?" (Student circles them.)
- "Verbs — can you underline the verbs?" (Student underlines them.)
- "Adjectives — did you use at least 2? Can you draw a box around them?" (If fewer than 2, add one now.)
- "Capital letters — does every sentence start with one?" (Fix any that do not.)
- "End punctuation — does every sentence have one?" (Add any missing ones.)
- "Spelling — do your CVC and CVCe words look right? Check your sight words." (Fix any misspellings.)
- "Complete sentences — does each one have a who/what AND a does/is?" (Fix any fragments.)

After checking, the student makes corrections on the draft using a different colored pencil.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student finds and fixes a mistake, celebrate: "You caught that all by yourself! That is exactly what good writers do." Self-editing is a skill — praise the process.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices independently.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Write the final copy of your piece on clean paper, incorporating all the edits from the checklist.
2. After writing the final copy, do one more pass with the checklist to make sure everything is correct.
3. Color-code your final copy:
   - Circle all NOUNS in blue.
   - Underline all VERBS in red.
   - Draw a box around all ADJECTIVES in green.
   - Highlight all END MARKS in yellow (or draw a star next to each one).

**Stretch:**
Complete the On-level tasks, PLUS:
4. Add 2 more sentences to your piece. At least one must be a question and one must use an exclamation point.
5. Label one common noun, one proper noun, and one possessive noun in your piece. (If you do not have all three, add a sentence that includes the missing type.)

**Challenge:**
Complete the On-level and Stretch tasks, PLUS:
6. Write a SECOND short piece (3-4 sentences) on a DIFFERENT topic from the list. Use the Grammar Checklist independently — no help this time.
7. After finishing, trade your second piece with the facilitator. The facilitator reads it aloud using the correct expression for each end mark. Does it sound right?
8. At the bottom of your paper, write one sentence that tells what you learned about grammar in this unit. Example: "I learned that nouns name things, verbs show actions, and adjectives make writing interesting!"

### Interleaved Review
- "Read your writing aloud. When you come to a period, use your telling voice. When you come to a question mark, use your asking voice. When you come to an exclamation point, use your excited voice." (Lesson 8 review — end punctuation and reading expression)
- "Find a CVC word and a CVCe word in your writing. Label each one." (Lesson 9 review — spelling patterns)
- "What type of writing is your piece — opinion, informative, or narrative? How do you know?" (Unit 3, Lesson 1 review — writing types)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. "Read me your favorite sentence from your piece. Tell me the noun, the verb, and any adjectives in that sentence." (Answer: Student identifies all parts correctly.)
2. "Why do writers use adjectives in their writing?" (Answer: To describe nouns / to make writing more interesting / to help the reader picture what they are writing about.)
3. "What are the three types of end marks and when do you use each one?" (Answer: Period for telling, question mark for asking, exclamation point for strong feeling.)
4. "Spell the word 'home.' What pattern is it?" (Answer: H-O-M-E, CVCe.)
5. "What is the first thing you do when you edit your writing?" (Answer: Use the grammar checklist / check nouns, verbs, adjectives, capitals, end marks, spelling, and complete sentences.)

**Mastery = Student correctly answers at least 4 of 5 questions AND successfully completes the mini-writing project with at least 5 of 7 checklist items met in the final copy.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which specific grammar concept(s) the student struggled with. Return to the original lesson for that concept:
  - Nouns → Review Lessons 1-3
  - Verbs → Review Lesson 6
  - Adjectives → Review Lesson 7
  - End punctuation → Review Lesson 8
  - Spelling → Review Lesson 9
- Simplify the writing task: reduce to 3 sentences and use the checklist with heavy support.
- Repeat the checklist editing process daily for a week with any writing the student does.

### If Mastery Is Met
Congratulations — Unit 4 is complete! The student is ready for more advanced grammar concepts in future units. Continue using the Grammar Checklist as a revision tool in all future writing.

## Extension (Optional)
- **Grammar Detective Story:** Write a silly 5-sentence story. Intentionally put in 5 grammar mistakes (missing capitals, wrong end marks, missing adjectives, misspelled words, incomplete sentences). Give it to someone else and see if they can find all 5 mistakes.
- **Parts of Speech Poster:** Create a colorful poster with three columns: Nouns, Verbs, Adjectives. Fill each column with at least 10 words. Decorate it and hang it up as a writing reference.
- **Grammar Journal:** Start a grammar journal. Each day, write 2-3 sentences about your day. Use the Grammar Checklist to edit every entry. Over time, the checklist will become automatic.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is the most important lesson in the unit because it asks the student to APPLY everything.** Lessons 1-9 taught individual grammar concepts in isolation. This lesson brings them all together in a real writing task. It is one thing to circle a verb in someone else's sentence — it is another thing entirely to choose the right verb, spell it correctly, and punctuate the sentence, all in your own writing. Expect the student to need support, and be patient.

**The Grammar Checklist is the lasting takeaway from this unit.** Long after the student forgets the specific activities from Lessons 1-9, the checklist will remain as a practical editing tool. Print it, laminate it, tape it to the desk — whatever makes it easy for the student to use it every time they write. The goal is for the checklist to eventually become internalized, so the student automatically checks for nouns, verbs, adjectives, capitals, end marks, spelling, and complete sentences without needing the physical list.

**Do not expect perfection in the mini-writing project.** A first-grader who writes 4 sentences, uses mostly correct punctuation, includes some adjectives, and catches a spelling error during editing is performing exceptionally well. The PROCESS of writing, checking, and fixing is more important than the product. Celebrate effort and improvement over accuracy.

**How to handle a student who is overwhelmed.** Some students feel anxious about putting it all together. If you see frustration, simplify: "Let's just write ONE sentence first. We will check it together. Then we will write the next one." Building sentence by sentence removes the pressure of "writing a whole piece." You can also reduce the checklist to 3 items for the first pass (capitals, end marks, complete sentences) and add the other items in a second pass.

**Connecting forward:** The skills from this unit — identifying parts of speech, using end punctuation, spelling with patterns, and self-editing — will support every future writing task. In Grade 2, the student will build on this foundation with more complex grammar (collective nouns, irregular plurals, possessives). The Grammar Checklist will grow with the student, adding new items as new concepts are learned. Remind the student: "You now have tools that real writers use. Every time you write, you are practicing grammar."

8. Exclamation point (!)

- The student should correctly identify nouns, verbs, and adjectives in their own writing.
- Every sentence should begin with a capital letter and end with appropriate punctuation.
- CVC words should follow the CVC pattern; CVCe words should include the silent e.
- Sight words (said, was, have, they, what, where, come, does, give, were) should be spelled correctly.
- Every sentence should be complete (subject + verb).

**Independent Practice (Color-Coding):**
- Blue circles around all nouns.
- Red underlines under all verbs.
- Green boxes around all adjectives.
- Stars or highlights on all end marks.
- Answers vary based on student writing — facilitator checks for accuracy.

1. Answers vary — student should correctly identify noun(s), verb(s), and adjective(s) in their chosen sentence.
2. Adjectives describe nouns / make writing more interesting / help the reader picture what is being described.
3. Period = telling sentence; question mark = asking sentence; exclamation point = strong feeling.
4. H-O-M-E, CVCe.
5. Use the grammar checklist (check nouns, verbs, adjectives, capitals, end marks, spelling, complete sentences).
