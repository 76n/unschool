# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 4, Lesson 1

## Topic
Common Nouns: Person, Place, Thing, Animal

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define a noun as a word that names a person, place, thing, or animal
- Sort a set of common nouns into the four categories (person, place, thing, animal)
- Identify common nouns within simple sentences

## Prerequisites
- Ability to read simple sentences with CVC and CVCe words (Unit 1 phonics skills)
- Understanding of how words work together in sentences (Unit 2 reading comprehension)
- Experience writing complete sentences with a capital letter and period (Unit 3 writing foundations)

## Materials Needed
- Chart paper or large whiteboard
- 4 colored markers or crayons (one color per noun category — e.g., red for person, blue for place, green for thing, orange for animal)
- 16 index cards or slips of paper with pre-written nouns (see Direct Instruction)
- 4 paper bags, cups, or bowls labeled: Person, Place, Thing, Animal
- Pencil and paper for the student
- Optional: small toy figures, stuffed animals, or household objects for hands-on sorting

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge by having the student notice and name things around them.

**Activity: Name It!**
Say to the student: "Let's play a quick game. I want you to look around the room and name everything you can see in 30 seconds. Ready? Go!"

Let the student call out items. After 30 seconds, say: "Great job! You named so many things. Now let me ask you a few more questions."

- "Can you name a person in your family?" (Accept any family member.)
- "Can you name a place you like to go?" (Accept any place.)
- "Can you name an animal you like?" (Accept any animal.)

Write their answers on the board in a quick list.

"You just named a bunch of different words — some were things, some were people, some were places, and some were animals. Did you know there is a special name for all of these words? Today we are going to learn what that name is!"

*Facilitator note: This warm-up builds excitement and shows the student that nouns are all around them — they already know hundreds of nouns without realizing it. Keeping the energy high at the start makes the grammar lesson feel like a game rather than a chore.*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the concept of a noun and the four categories of common nouns.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Noun:** A word that names a person, place, thing, or animal.
- **Common noun:** A noun that names any general person, place, thing, or animal (not a specific name). Examples: girl, park, cup, dog.
- **Category:** A group of things that go together.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce what a noun is.**
Say: "A noun is a naming word. It names a person, a place, a thing, or an animal. Almost everything you can point to or think about has a noun for its name."

Write on the chart paper in big letters:

**A NOUN is a word that names a:**
- **Person** (someone) — teacher, mom, boy
- **Place** (somewhere) — park, school, home
- **Thing** (something) — book, ball, chair
- **Animal** (a creature) — cat, dog, bird

"Let's learn a little chant to help us remember. Repeat after me:"

*Chant (clap or tap the table on each beat):*
"Person, place, thing, animal — that's a noun!
Person, place, thing, animal — write it down!"

Practice the chant 2-3 times together.

**Step 2: Model sorting nouns into the four categories.**
Set out the four labeled bags or bowls. Say: "Now I am going to show you some words, and together we will figure out which category each noun belongs to."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Hold up the card that says "teacher."
"The word is 'teacher.' Hmm, is a teacher a person, a place, a thing, or an animal? Well, a teacher is someone who helps you learn. A teacher is a person! So I am going to put this card in the Person bag."
Place the card in the Person container.

**Example 2:** Hold up the card that says "park."
"The word is 'park.' Is a park a person? No, a park is not someone. Is it an animal? No. Is it a thing you can hold? Not exactly — a park is somewhere you go. A park is a place!"
Place the card in the Place container.

**Example 3:** Hold up the card that says "book."
"The word is 'book.' Can I hold a book? Yes! Is it a person or animal? No. Is it a place I go to? No. A book is a thing!"
Place the card in the Thing container.

**Example 4:** Hold up the card that says "cat."
"The word is 'cat.' A cat is alive and has four legs. A cat is an animal!"
Place the card in the Animal container.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice sorting nouns and identifying them in sentences with support.

**Activity 1: Sort the Rest (5 minutes)**
Give the student the remaining 12 noun cards (cards 5-16). Read each one together.

For each card, ask:
- "Is this word a person, a place, a thing, or an animal?"
- Have the student place it in the correct container.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student is unsure, ask guiding questions: "Can you hold it in your hand? Then it might be a thing. Is it alive and not a person? Then it might be an animal. Is it somewhere you could go? Then it might be a place. Is it someone? Then it is a person."*

**Sorting answers:**
- mom — Person
- school — Place
- ball — Thing
- dog — Animal
- baby — Person
- store — Place
- cup — Thing
- fish — Animal
- farmer — Person
- kitchen — Place
- bike — Thing
- bird — Animal

**Activity 2: Noun Hunt in Sentences (5 minutes)**
Say: "Now let's find the nouns hiding inside sentences. I will read a sentence, and you tell me which word is the noun."

Read these sentences aloud (write them on the board if possible):

1. "The girl ran fast." — Ask: "Which word is the noun?" (girl)
2. "I see the lake." — Ask: "Which word is the noun?" (lake)
3. "A frog jumped." — Ask: "Which word is the noun?" (frog)
4. "She put the box down." — Ask: "Which word is the noun?" (box)
5. "We went to the library." — Ask: "Which word is the noun?" (library)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student picks the wrong word, say: "Let's check — is that word a person, place, thing, or animal? Which word in the sentence IS a person, place, thing, or animal?"*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices identifying and categorizing nouns independently.

**Activity: My Noun Collection Page**
The student creates a page divided into four sections labeled: Person, Place, Thing, Animal. In each section, they write at least 2 nouns and draw a small picture for each one.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
Write 2 nouns in each of the four categories (8 total). Draw a picture for each noun.
- Use the noun cards for ideas if needed.
- Invented spelling is accepted.

**Stretch:**
Write 3 nouns in each category (12 total) WITHOUT looking at the noun cards. Try to think of nouns that were not used in today's lesson.

**Challenge:**
Write 3 nouns in each category (12 total), then write a complete sentence using one noun from each category. For example: "The farmer went to the store to get a hat for his pig."

### Interleaved Review
- "Read your noun list back to me. Sound out any tricky words." (Phonics review from Unit 1)
- "Pick one of your nouns and tell me a fact about it." (Informative writing connection from Unit 3)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student understands what a noun is and can categorize them.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What is a noun?" (Answer: A word that names a person, place, thing, or animal)
2. "Is the word 'doctor' a person, place, thing, or animal?" (Answer: person)
3. "Is the word 'beach' a person, place, thing, or animal?" (Answer: place)
4. "Listen to this sentence: 'The rabbit hopped away.' What is the noun?" (Answer: rabbit)

**Mastery = Student defines a noun correctly AND answers at least 3 of 3 sorting/identification questions correctly.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Go back to the chant: "Person, place, thing, animal — that's a noun!" Practice it several times.
- Use real objects around the room. Pick up a cup, a toy animal, point to the student (person), point out the window to a place. Name each one and say: "This is a noun because it is a ___."
- Do 5 more oral sorting examples before the next lesson.
- Repeat this lesson next session before moving to Lesson 2.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 2 (Proper Nouns and Capital Letters). Keep the noun anchor chart posted — it will be used for the rest of this unit.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who grasp common nouns quickly:**
- Play "Noun Scavenger Hunt": walk through the house and write down 5 nouns in each category that you find (person in a photo, places named on a map or book, things you can touch, animals on clothing or toys).
- Play "Noun Charades": Take turns acting out a noun (without speaking) and guessing what it is. After guessing, say whether it is a person, place, thing, or animal.
- Start a "Noun Journal" — a small notebook where the student adds 3 new nouns each day, sorted by category. This can continue throughout the unit.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is the very first grammar lesson in this curriculum, so take your time and keep it joyful.** Many children have never heard the word "noun" before, and that is perfectly fine. The goal today is simply for the student to walk away knowing that nouns are naming words and that they come in four flavors: person, place, thing, and animal. Do not rush through the categories — linger on the ones that are less intuitive.

**The "thing" category often trips students up** because it is so broad. A cup is a thing, but so is a cloud, a song, or a thought. For this first lesson, stick to concrete, holdable things. Abstract nouns (like "love" or "happiness") are far beyond Grade 1 expectations and should be avoided entirely.

**Physical sorting is powerful.** When students physically pick up a card and drop it into a labeled container, they engage motor memory alongside verbal memory. If you can add real objects (a toy dog for "animal," a small cup for "thing"), even better. Multi-sensory experiences make grammar stick.

**Connect to reading and writing whenever possible.** After the lesson, when you read a book together, pause and ask: "Can you spot a noun on this page?" During writing time, ask: "What noun are you going to write about?" This helps the student see grammar not as an isolated skill but as a tool they use every time they read and write.

**The chant is optional but highly recommended.** Young children learn categories faster when there is a rhythmic, repeatable phrase. Feel free to make up a melody if that suits your student better. The goal is a quick verbal cue they can pull up when asked, "What is a noun?"
