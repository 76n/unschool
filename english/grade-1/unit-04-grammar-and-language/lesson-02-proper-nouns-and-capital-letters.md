# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 4, Lesson 2

## Topic
Proper Nouns and Capital Letters

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain the difference between a common noun and a proper noun
- Identify proper nouns as specific names of people, places, and pets that begin with a capital letter
- Correctly capitalize proper nouns when writing them

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of what a noun is and the four noun categories: person, place, thing, animal (Lesson 1)
- Ability to form uppercase and lowercase letters (Kindergarten handwriting and Unit 3 writing)
- Familiarity with the concept that sentences begin with capital letters (Unit 3, Lesson 2)

- Noun anchor chart from Lesson 1 (person, place, thing, animal)

- Two different colored markers (e.g., black for common nouns, red for proper nouns)
- 12 index cards or slips of paper with noun pairs (see Direct Instruction)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect to the student's own name and the names of people and places they know.

**Activity: The Name Game**
Say to the student: "I have a question for you. What is your name?"

After they answer, say: "That is your special name — nobody else in your family has that exact same name. Now tell me — what is the name of your pet?" (If no pet, ask for the name of a friend or stuffed animal.) "What is the name of the street you live on, or the town you live in?"

Write each name on the board, making the capital letter extra large and in a bright color.

"Look at all these names. Notice anything special? They all start with a BIG letter — a capital letter. Do you know why?"

Let the student guess. Then say: "These are special nouns called proper nouns. Today we are going to learn what makes a proper noun different from a regular noun and why proper nouns always need a capital letter."

*Facilitator note: Starting with the student's own name makes the concept immediately personal and meaningful. Every child knows their own name starts with a capital letter — this lesson helps them understand why.*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the difference between common and proper nouns and the rule that proper nouns are always capitalized.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Common noun:** A general naming word — it could be any person, place, thing, or animal. Examples: girl, park, dog.
- **Proper noun:** A specific naming word — it names one particular person, place, or animal. Examples: Emma, Central Park, Buddy.
- **Capital letter:** An uppercase letter (A, B, C...). Proper nouns always start with a capital letter.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the concept with a side-by-side comparison.**
Draw a T-chart on chart paper:

| Common Noun | Proper Noun |
|---|---|
| any girl | Emma |
| any park | Central Park |
| any dog | Buddy |

Say: "Remember from Lesson 1 — a noun is a word that names a person, place, thing, or animal. But some nouns are general, and some nouns are specific."

"If I say 'girl,' you could think of ANY girl in the world. But if I say 'Emma,' you know I mean one particular girl named Emma. 'Girl' is a common noun — it is common because it could be anyone. 'Emma' is a proper noun — it is the proper, specific name."

**Step 2: Teach the capital letter rule.**
Point to the proper nouns on the chart. Say: "Look at all the proper nouns. What do you notice about the first letter?"

Let the student observe that they all start with capital letters.

"Here is the rule: Proper nouns ALWAYS start with a capital letter, even if they are in the middle of a sentence. That capital letter is like a flag that says, 'Hey! I am a special name!'"

Write on the chart:
**Rule: Proper nouns always start with a capital letter.**

**Step 3: Walk through the noun pairs.**
Hold up the first pair of cards: "girl" and "Emma."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** girl / Emma
"'Girl' is a common noun — it could be any girl. 'Emma' is a proper noun — it is the specific name of one girl. Watch how I write them: girl (lowercase g) but Emma (capital E). The proper noun gets the capital letter."

**Example 2:** park / Central Park
"'Park' is a common noun — it could be any park anywhere. 'Central Park' is a proper noun — it is the specific name of one famous park. Notice that BOTH words in 'Central Park' start with capital letters. When a proper noun has more than one word, each important word gets a capital letter."

**Example 3:** dog / Buddy
"'Dog' is a common noun — any dog in the world. 'Buddy' is a proper noun — it is this one specific dog's name. Dog gets a lowercase 'd,' but Buddy gets a capital 'B.' If you were writing a story about Buddy, every time you wrote his name, you would use a capital B."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice distinguishing common from proper nouns and capitalizing correctly with support.

**Activity 1: Sort the Pairs (5 minutes)**
Spread out the remaining 9 noun pair cards (pairs 4-12) face up. Read each pair together.

For each pair, ask:
- "Which one is the common noun — the general word?"
- "Which one is the proper noun — the specific name?"
- "How do you know the proper noun is proper?" (It names a specific person/place/animal and starts with a capital letter.)

Have the student sort them into two piles: common nouns on the left, proper nouns on the right.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student is unsure, ask: "Could there be a hundred different ones of this? Then it is probably common. Is there only one specific one with this name? Then it is proper."*

**Sorting answers:**
- city (common) / New York (proper)
- school (common) / Lincoln Elementary (proper)
- store (common) / Target (proper)
- cat (common) / Whiskers (proper)
- park (common) / Central Park (proper)
- teacher (common) / Mrs. Garcia (proper)
- lake (common) / Lake Michigan (proper)
- street (common) / Maple Street (proper)
- grandma (common) / Grandma Rose (proper)

**Activity 2: Fix the Capitals (5 minutes)**
Say: "Oh no! Someone wrote these sentences and forgot to capitalize the proper nouns. Can you find the proper nouns and fix them?"

Write these sentences on the board (proper nouns intentionally lowercase):

1. My friend emma has a pet fish.
2. We drove to the store on maple street.
3. My dog buddy likes to run in the park.
4. mrs. garcia reads to us every day.
5. I want to visit new york someday.

Read each sentence aloud. Have the student point to the proper noun and tell you which letter should be capitalized. Then fix it together on the board.

*Facilitator prompt: "Remember — proper nouns are specific names. Look for the word that names one specific person, place, or animal."*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices identifying and writing proper nouns independently.

**Activity: My Proper Nouns Page**
The student creates a page with three sections: People I Know, Places I Know, Animals I Know. In each section, they write at least 2 proper nouns (specific names) and their matching common nouns.

Example format:
- Person: My **brother** is named **Sam**. (common noun: brother, proper noun: Sam)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
Write 2 proper nouns in each section (6 total). For each one, also write the common noun it matches. Make sure every proper noun starts with a capital letter.
- People I Know: (e.g., Mom — her name is Maria)
- Places I Know: (e.g., school — Lincoln Elementary)
- Animals I Know: (e.g., dog — Rex)

**Stretch:**
Write 3 proper nouns in each section (9 total) and use each proper noun in a complete sentence. Make sure the proper noun is capitalized correctly in the sentence.

**Challenge:**
Write 3 proper nouns in each section (9 total). For each proper noun, write two sentences — one using the common noun and one using the proper noun. For example: "I have a cat. Her name is Mittens." Underline the common noun in one sentence and the proper noun in the other.

### Interleaved Review
- "Read your sentences aloud to me. Point to each word as you read it." (Fluency review from Unit 1)
- "Look at your sentences. Does each one start with a capital letter and end with a period?" (Writing conventions from Unit 3, Lesson 2)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student understands proper nouns and the capitalization rule.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What is the difference between a common noun and a proper noun?" (Answer: A common noun is a general name for any person, place, thing, or animal. A proper noun is the specific name of a particular person, place, or animal and always starts with a capital letter.)
2. "I will say two words. Tell me which one is the proper noun: 'dog' or 'Spot.'" (Answer: Spot)
3. "How should I write the name of your school — with a capital letter or a lowercase letter at the beginning?" (Answer: Capital letter, because it is a proper noun)
4. "What is wrong with this sentence: 'I went to see dr. jones.'" (Answer: "Dr. Jones" should have capital letters because it is a proper noun — a specific person's name.)

**Mastery = Student explains the difference between common and proper nouns AND correctly identifies or capitalizes at least 3 of 3 examples.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the T-chart and add more examples using the student's own life (their friends' names, their town, their pet or favorite character).
- Practice writing 5 proper nouns on paper, emphasizing the capital letter each time.
- Play "Common or Proper?" — say a noun aloud and have the student hold up one finger for common, two for proper.
- Repeat this lesson next session before moving to Lesson 3.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (Singular and Plural Nouns). Keep the T-chart posted alongside the noun anchor chart from Lesson 1.

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who grasp proper nouns quickly:**
- Play "Name That Noun": The facilitator says a common noun (e.g., "restaurant"), and the student tries to name a proper noun that matches (e.g., "McDonald's"). Switch roles.
- Write a short letter to a friend or family member. Underline every proper noun in the letter. Count how many proper nouns you used.
- Look at a book you have at home. Find 5 proper nouns in the book. Write each one and explain whether it names a person, place, or animal (or sometimes a thing, like a ship name).

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The key insight in this lesson is the idea of "general vs. specific."** A common noun points to a whole category (any dog), while a proper noun points to one individual (Buddy). For many first graders, this is their first encounter with the idea that language has layers of specificity. Take your time with the paired examples — the side-by-side comparison is what makes the concept click.

**Capitalization is a convention, not a concept.** Understanding what a proper noun IS requires thinking; remembering to capitalize it requires practice and habit. Do not be discouraged if the student understands proper nouns perfectly but still forgets to capitalize them in their writing. That is normal. Gentle, consistent reminders over the coming weeks are more effective than drilling today.

**Some edge cases may come up.** The student might ask: "Is 'Mom' a proper noun?" The answer is nuanced — "Mom" is proper when used as a name ("I told Mom") but common when used as a category ("my mom"). For Grade 1, it is fine to say: "When you use 'Mom' instead of her real name, we capitalize it." Do not over-complicate this.

**Connect back to reading constantly.** Every time you read together for the rest of this unit, pause occasionally and say: "I see a proper noun on this page. Can you find it? How do you know it is a proper noun?" This ongoing practice is far more valuable than any single lesson.

**This lesson pairs naturally with the student's writing.** In future writing assignments, watch for proper nouns and gently ask: "Is that a specific name? What kind of letter does it need?" Over time, the capital-letter habit will become automatic.
