# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 4, Lesson 5

## Topic
Pronouns: Replacing Nouns to Avoid Repetition

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define a pronoun as a word that takes the place of a noun
- Identify and use the personal pronouns: I, he, she, it, we, they, me, him, her, us, them
- Replace a noun with the correct pronoun in a sentence to avoid repetition

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of what a noun is: person, place, thing, animal (Lesson 1)
- Familiarity with common and proper nouns (Lessons 1 and 2)
- Ability to form plural nouns (Lesson 3) and possessive nouns (Lesson 4)

## Materials Needed
- Chart paper or large whiteboard
- Markers in two colors (one for nouns, one for pronouns)
- 10 index cards with nouns and 10 matching index cards with pronouns (see Direct Instruction)
- Pencil and paper for the student
- Optional: small toy figures or puppets to act out sentences with noun-pronoun swaps

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Show the student what happens when we use the same noun over and over — it sounds silly and repetitive.

**Activity: The Silly Paragraph**
Say to the student: "I am going to read you a short story. Listen carefully and tell me what sounds weird about it."

Read this paragraph aloud with exaggerated emphasis on the repeated name:

"**Sam** went to the park. **Sam** brought **Sam's** dog. **Sam's** dog ran fast. **Sam** threw a ball. **Sam's** dog caught the ball. **Sam** was happy. **Sam's** dog was happy too."

Ask: "What did you notice?" The student will likely say something like "You said Sam too many times!" or "It sounds funny."

"You are exactly right! Saying 'Sam' over and over sounds strange. In real life, we would not talk like that. Instead, we would use different little words to replace Sam's name. Listen to this version:"

Read the improved paragraph:

"**Sam** went to the park. **He** brought **his** dog. **The dog** ran fast. **Sam** threw a ball. **It** caught the ball. **He** was happy. **It** was happy too."

"Much better! Those little words — 'he,' 'his,' 'it' — are called pronouns. A pronoun is a word that takes the place of a noun so we do not have to keep repeating the same name. Today we are going to learn all about pronouns!"

*Facilitator note: The "silly paragraph" technique is very effective because it gives students an immediate, felt reason to care about pronouns. They can hear the problem, and they want to fix it. This motivation carries through the whole lesson.*

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the personal pronouns and teach the student when to use each one.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Pronoun:** A word that takes the place of a noun. Instead of saying the noun again, we use a pronoun.
- **Subject pronoun:** A pronoun that does the action in a sentence (I, he, she, it, we, they).
- **Object pronoun:** A pronoun that receives the action (me, him, her, it, us, them).

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce the subject pronouns.**
Write on the chart paper:

**Pronouns — Words That Replace Nouns**

| When We Talk About... | We Use This Pronoun |
|---|---|
| Myself | **I** |
| One boy or man | **he** |
| One girl or woman | **she** |
| One thing or animal | **it** |
| Myself and others | **we** |
| More than one person, animal, or thing | **they** |

"These are called subject pronouns. They do the action in the sentence. Let me show you how they work."

Show examples, writing the noun in one color and the pronoun replacement in another:
- "**The boy** kicked the ball." → "**He** kicked the ball."
- "**The girl** sang a song." → "**She** sang a song."
- "**The dog** barked loudly." → "**It** barked loudly."
- "**Mom and I** went to the store." → "**We** went to the store."
- "**The birds** flew away." → "**They** flew away."

"See how the pronoun takes the noun's place? The sentence means the same thing, but it sounds smoother."

**Step 2: Introduce the object pronouns.**
"Sometimes the noun is not doing the action — it is receiving the action. In that case, we use a different set of pronouns."

Add to the chart:

| Instead of... | We Say... |
|---|---|
| (to/for) me | **me** |
| (to/for) a boy or man | **him** |
| (to/for) a girl or woman | **her** |
| (to/for) a thing or animal | **it** |
| (to/for) us | **us** |
| (to/for) them | **them** |

Show examples:
- "I gave the ball to **the boy**." → "I gave the ball to **him**."
- "Dad helped **the girl**." → "Dad helped **her**."
- "She told **Mom and me** a story." → "She told **us** a story."

"Do not worry too much about the names 'subject' and 'object.' The important thing is knowing which pronoun sounds right. Your ears will help you!"

**Step 3: Practice choosing the right pronoun with a quick chant.**

*Chant (point to different people/directions as you say each pronoun):*
"I, he, she, it — one at a time!
We and they — that's more than fine!
Me, him, her, us, them — we're done!
Pronouns make our sentences fun!"

Practice 2-3 times.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "**Emma** likes to draw."
"Who is Emma? A girl. What pronoun replaces a girl's name? 'She.' So we can say: '**She** likes to draw.'"

**Example 2:** "**The books** fell off the shelf."
"'The books' — that is more than one thing. What pronoun do we use for more than one? 'They.' So: '**They** fell off the shelf.'"

**Example 3:** "I sat next to **Jake**."
"Jake is receiving the action here — I sat next to him. Jake is a boy, so: 'I sat next to **him**.'"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice matching nouns to pronouns and replacing nouns in sentences with support.

**Activity 1: Noun-Pronoun Match (5 minutes)**
Spread out the 10 noun cards and 10 pronoun cards face up.

Say: "Match each noun card with the pronoun that could replace it."

Go through the first 2 together, then let the student work with guidance.

*Facilitator prompt: For each noun, ask: "Is this a boy, a girl, a thing, or more than one? That will help you pick the right pronoun."*

**Matching answers:**
- the boy → he
- the girl → she
- the dog → it
- Mom and Dad → they
- the book → it
- Sam and I → we
- the teacher → she (or he, depending on context — both are acceptable)
- the cats → they
- Grandma → she
- the ball → it

**Activity 2: Pronoun Swap (5 minutes)**
Say: "I will read a sentence with a noun. You tell me which pronoun should replace it, and then say the new sentence."

1. "**The cat** is sleeping." → What pronoun? (**It**) → "It is sleeping."
2. "**Maria** ran to school." → What pronoun? (**She**) → "She ran to school."
3. "**The boys** played tag." → What pronoun? (**They**) → "They played tag."
4. "I gave flowers to **Grandma**." → What pronoun? (**her**) → "I gave flowers to her."
5. "**Dad and I** went fishing." → What pronoun? (**We**) → "We went fishing."
6. "The teacher read to **the children**." → What pronoun? (**them**) → "The teacher read to them."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student is unsure, ask: "Is this person doing the action or receiving the action? Is it a boy, girl, thing, or many?" Then point to the chart.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices using pronouns independently in writing.

**Activity: Pronoun Power-Up**
The student completes three parts:

**Part 1: Choose the Right Pronoun**
Fill in the blank with the correct pronoun.

1. ___ is my best friend. (talking about a girl named Lily) → ___
2. The frog jumped. ___ landed on a rock. → ___
3. Mom called ___. (talking about me) → ___
4. ___ are going to the park. (talking about you and your brother) → ___
5. I gave the toy to ___. (talking about a boy named Ben) → ___
6. The kittens are cute. ___ are fluffy. → ___

**Part 2: Rewrite Without Repeating**
Rewrite each pair of sentences by replacing the repeated noun with a pronoun in the second sentence.

1. "Jake likes pizza. Jake eats pizza every Friday."
2. "The bird sat on a branch. The bird sang a pretty song."
3. "Mom baked cookies. I helped Mom."

**Part 3: Write Your Own**
Write 2 sentences about someone you know. Use the person's name in the first sentence and a pronoun in the second sentence.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
Complete Part 1 (6 fill-in-the-blank items) and Part 2 (3 sentence rewrites). Refer to the pronoun chart if needed.

**Stretch:**
Complete all three parts. In Part 3, write 3 pairs of sentences (6 sentences total) using at least 3 different pronouns. Try not to look at the chart.

**Challenge:**
Complete all three parts. In Part 3, write a short 5-sentence paragraph about your family or friends. Use at least 4 different pronouns. Underline every pronoun you use.

### Interleaved Review
- "Read your sentences aloud. Sound out any tricky words." (Phonics review from Unit 1)
- "Look at your paragraph from Part 3. Can you find a common noun? A proper noun?" (Noun types from Lessons 1 and 2)
- "Pick one sentence from Part 2. Can you find any plural nouns?" (Plural nouns from Lesson 3)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Confirm the student understands what pronouns are and can use them correctly.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What is a pronoun?" (Answer: A word that takes the place of a noun.)
2. "What pronoun would you use instead of 'the dog'?" (Answer: it)
3. "What pronoun would you use instead of 'Emma'?" (Answer: she)
4. "Listen to these sentences: 'Ben got a new bike. Ben rides Ben's bike every day.' What is the problem, and how would you fix it?" (Answer: "Ben" is repeated too many times. Fix: "Ben got a new bike. He rides his bike every day." or "Ben got a new bike. He rides it every day.")

**Mastery = Student defines a pronoun correctly AND selects the correct pronoun for at least 2 of 2 examples AND identifies the repetition problem and suggests a pronoun fix.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the "silly paragraph" from the warm-up. Read it again and fix it together, one sentence at a time.
- Use toy figures: hold up a boy toy and say "He." Hold up a girl toy and say "She." Hold up two toys and say "They." Practice associating the pronoun with the visual.
- Do 5 more oral pronoun swaps: say a sentence with a noun, have the student say it back with a pronoun.
- Repeat this lesson next session before moving on.

### If Mastery Is Met
Congratulations — the student has completed all 5 lessons in the Grammar and Language unit! Review key concepts from all lessons using the unit assessment. Keep the pronoun chart posted for reference during future reading and writing activities.

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who grasp pronouns quickly:**
- Play "Pronoun Detective": Read a page from a favorite book together. Every time the student spots a pronoun, they clap (or put a sticky note on it). Count how many pronouns are on the page. Then find the noun each pronoun replaced.
- Write a short story (4-6 sentences) about a pet or favorite animal. First, write the story using the animal's name in every sentence. Then rewrite it using pronouns where possible. Compare the two versions — which one sounds better?
- Bonus challenge: Can the student spot the pronoun "you" in everyday conversation? "You" is a pronoun too! Keep a tally of how many times family members use the word "you" in one hour.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pronouns are one of the most practical grammar tools a first grader can learn.** Every time a student writes "Sam... Sam... Sam..." in their journal, pronouns are the solution. This lesson gives them a concrete tool to improve their own writing immediately. After this lesson, gently remind them during writing time: "You wrote 'the dog' three times. Can you use a pronoun for some of those?"

**Do not stress the terms "subject pronoun" and "object pronoun."** These labels are introduced in the lesson for the facilitator's reference, but the student does not need to memorize them. What matters is that the student can choose the right pronoun by how the sentence sounds. Most first graders already use pronouns correctly in speech — this lesson helps them transfer that spoken knowledge to writing.

**The most common error is using "me" as a subject pronoun.** Students often say "Me and Jake went to the park" instead of "Jake and I went to the park." You can model this gently: "If Jake were not there, would you say 'Me went to the park' or 'I went to the park'? Right — 'I went to the park.' So it is 'Jake and I.'" Do not over-correct in this lesson; awareness is the first step.

**This lesson is the capstone of the Grammar and Language unit.** It ties together everything the student has learned: nouns (Lesson 1), proper nouns (Lesson 2), plural nouns (Lesson 3), and possessive nouns (Lesson 4). Pronouns exist because of nouns — they are the "replacement players." Making this connection explicit helps the student see grammar as a coherent system rather than a collection of random rules.

**After completing this unit, look for opportunities to reinforce all five concepts during everyday reading and writing.** The best grammar instruction happens not in isolated lessons but in the moments when a student is reading a real book or writing a real story and you pause to notice the grammar at work. "Look — the author used 'they' instead of writing 'the children' again. That is a pronoun! You learned about those."
