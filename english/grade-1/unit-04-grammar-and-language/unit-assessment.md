# Unit Assessment — Grade 1 ELA, Unit 4: Grammar & Language

**Grade:** 1
**Unit:** 4 — Grammar & Language
**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** One-on-one with facilitator (oral responses and writing samples)
**Estimated Duration:** 30-40 minutes (may be split into two sessions if needed)
**Materials:** Pencil, lined paper, eraser

---

## Assessment Overview

This assessment covers all Unit 4 learning objectives:

| Section | Skill Assessed | Lesson Source | Items | Points |
|---------|---------------|---------------|-------|--------|
| A | Nouns (common, proper, singular/plural, possessive) | Lessons 1-4 | 5 | 5 |
| B | Pronouns (subject and object) | Lesson 5 | 4 | 4 |
| C | Verbs and adjectives | Lessons 6-7 | 5 | 5 |
| D | End punctuation (., ?, !) | Lesson 8 | 4 | 4 |
| E | Spelling patterns (CVC, CVCe, sight words) | Lesson 9 | 4 | 4 |
| F | Applied grammar in writing | Lesson 10 | 3 | 3 |
| -- | **Total** | -- | **25** | **25** |

**Mastery threshold:** 85% overall (21 of 25 points) AND at least 1 correct item in each section.

---

## Administration Instructions

This assessment includes both oral questions and writing tasks. For the oral sections, read each question aloud and record the student's response. For the writing sections, the student writes on lined paper while you observe.

There are no time limits on individual items. If the student is stuck on any item for more than 30 seconds (oral) or 2 minutes (written), say: "That's okay. Do your best and let's move on."

Before beginning, say: "Today I want to see how much you have learned about grammar and language. You will answer some questions and do some writing. There are no tricks — just show me what you know. You can take your time."

Provide the student with sharpened pencils, an eraser, and lined paper.

If the student becomes fatigued, take a short break or split the assessment across two sessions (Sections A-C in session one, Sections D-F in session two).

**Important notes:**
- Do NOT provide grammar hints during the assessment (e.g., do not say "remember the capital letter rule" or "think about what kind of noun that is").
- DO encourage the student: "You are doing a great job. Keep going."
- For oral items, accept reasonable phrasing. The student does not need to use technical grammar terminology perfectly — the key is whether they demonstrate understanding.
- For written items, accept invented spelling for non-target words. If the student's handwriting is unclear, ask: "Can you read that word to me?" and score based on their oral response.

## Section A: Nouns — Common, Proper, Singular/Plural, Possessive (Lessons 1-4)

### Common and Proper Nouns (Lessons 1-2)

**A1.** (Oral) Say: "I am going to say some words. Tell me if each one is a common noun or a proper noun."

Read each word one at a time:
- "dog"
- "Texas"
- "teacher"
- "Maria"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correctly identifies all 4 (dog = common, Texas = proper, teacher = common, Maria = proper) | 1 point |
| Correctly identifies 3 of 4 | 0.5 points |
| Correctly identifies fewer than 3 | 0 points |

**A2.** (Written) Write this sentence on paper and show it to the student:

> "sam and his dog went to the park in dallas."

Ask: "Some words in this sentence need capital letters because they are proper nouns — special names. Circle or fix the words that should start with a capital letter."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Capitalizes "Sam" AND "Dallas" (both proper nouns identified) | 1 point |
| Capitalizes only one of the two proper nouns | 0.5 points |
| Does not capitalize either proper noun, or capitalizes common nouns instead | 0 points |

*Facilitator note: If the student also capitalizes the first word "sam" because it begins the sentence, that is correct and should be counted as part of identifying "Sam" as needing a capital. Do not deduct points if the student does NOT capitalize common nouns like "dog" or "park."*

### Singular and Plural Nouns (Lesson 3)

**A3.** (Oral/Written) Say: "Make each word I say into more than one — the plural. Tell me the word, then write it."

Read each word one at a time:
- "cat"
- "bus"
- "box"
- "dog"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correctly pluralizes all 4 (cats, buses, boxes, dogs) — both orally and in writing | 1 point |
| Correctly pluralizes 3 of 4 | 0.5 points |
| Correctly pluralizes fewer than 3 | 0 points |

*Facilitator note: The key distinction being assessed is whether the student knows when to add -s (cat → cats, dog → dogs) versus -es (bus → buses, box → boxes). Score based on the written form. Accept reasonable pronunciation for the oral portion.*

### Possessive Nouns (Lesson 4)

**A4.** (Oral) Say: "Listen to this sentence: 'The tail of the cat is fluffy.' Can you say that a shorter way using apostrophe-s to show the tail belongs to the cat?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| "The cat's tail is fluffy" or any equivalent that correctly uses 's | 1 point |
| Attempts possessive but places apostrophe incorrectly or omits it | 0.5 points |
| Cannot form the possessive or does not attempt | 0 points |

**A5.** (Written) Ask: "Write a sentence about something that belongs to a person or an animal. Use apostrophe-s to show who it belongs to."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Writes a sentence with a correctly formed possessive noun (apostrophe + s in the right place, e.g., "The dog's bone is big." or "Mom's car is red.") | 1 point |
| Writes a sentence that shows ownership but the apostrophe is missing or misplaced | 0.5 points |
| Does not produce a possessive noun | 0 points |

*Facilitator note: Do not deduct for spelling errors in non-target words. The target skill is the correct use of apostrophe-s to show ownership.*

**Section A total: 5 points**

## Section B: Pronouns (Lesson 5)

**B1.** (Oral) Say: "I am going to read a sentence. Tell me which word — which pronoun — could replace the name."

Read: "**Ben** likes to play outside."

Ask: "What word could we use instead of Ben?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| He | 1 point |
| Another subject pronoun that would logically work | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**B2.** (Oral) Read: "**Anna and I** went to the store."

Ask: "What one word could we use instead of 'Anna and I'?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| We | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**B3.** (Oral) Read: "I gave the book to **Lily**."

Ask: "What word could we use instead of Lily?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Her | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

*Facilitator note: This item tests object pronouns. The correct answer is "her," not "she." If the student says "she," say "Listen again: I gave the book to ___" and re-score. If they self-correct to "her," give full credit.*

**B4.** (Written) Write these sentences on paper with blanks. Read each sentence aloud and ask the student to write the correct pronoun in the blank.

1. "Mom made lunch. ___ is a good cook." (She)
2. "The dogs are barking. ___ are loud!" (They)
3. "I see Tom. I will wave to ___." (him)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| All 3 correct (She, They, him) | 1 point |
| 2 of 3 correct | 0.5 points |
| Fewer than 2 correct | 0 points |

*Facilitator note: Accept capitalization in any form since these are fill-in-the-blank items. The focus is on selecting the correct pronoun.*

**Section B total: 4 points**

## Section C: Verbs and Adjectives (Lessons 6-7)

### Action Verbs (Lesson 6)

**C1.** (Oral) Say: "Tell me the action verb in this sentence — the word that tells what someone does."

Read: "The rabbit hops across the yard."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Hops | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**C2.** (Oral) Read: "Sara painted a beautiful picture."

Ask: "What is the action verb?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Painted | 1 point |
| Incorrect or no response | 0 points |

**C3.** (Oral) Ask: "Can you give me three action verbs — three words that tell what a person or animal can do?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Names 3 or more action verbs (e.g., run, jump, eat, swim, read, write) | 1 point |
| Names 2 action verbs | 0.5 points |
| Names 1 or 0 action verbs, or names non-verbs | 0 points |

### Adjectives (Lesson 7)

**C4.** (Oral) Say: "An adjective is a describing word. Listen to this sentence and tell me the adjectives."

Read: "The tiny kitten slept on the soft blanket."

Ask: "What are the adjectives — the describing words?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Names both "tiny" and "soft" | 1 point |
| Names one of the two | 0.5 points |
| Cannot name either adjective | 0 points |

**C5.** (Written) Ask: "Write a sentence about an animal. Use at least one adjective — a word that describes what the animal looks like, sounds like, or feels like."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Writes a sentence with at least one clear adjective describing the animal (e.g., "The big brown bear is strong." or "I saw a fuzzy cat.") | 1 point |
| Writes a sentence about an animal but with no clear adjective | 0.5 points |
| Does not write a sentence, or sentence has no animal and no adjective | 0 points |

*Facilitator note: Accept any adjective — color, size, shape, texture, sound, emotion. The student does not need to label it as an adjective; they just need to use one in context. Do not deduct for spelling.*

**Section C total: 5 points**

## Section D: End Punctuation (Lesson 8)

**D1.** (Oral) Ask: "What are the three types of end punctuation you can put at the end of a sentence?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Names all 3: period, question mark, exclamation point (accept "exclamation mark") | 1 point |
| Names 2 of 3 | 0.5 points |
| Names fewer than 2 | 0 points |

**D2.** (Written) Write these three sentences on paper WITHOUT end punctuation. Read each one aloud with the appropriate expression/intonation to signal the sentence type.

1. "I like to play at the park" (statement — read in a flat, declarative tone)
2. "Where is my backpack" (question — read with rising intonation)
3. "Watch out for that ball" (exclamation — read with urgency and excitement)

Ask: "Add the correct end mark to each sentence."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| All 3 correct (period, question mark, exclamation point) | 1 point |
| 2 of 3 correct | 0.5 points |
| Fewer than 2 correct | 0 points |

**D3.** (Written) Write these sentences on paper with the wrong end punctuation:

1. "Can I have some water." (should be ?)
2. "The fire is spreading!" (this one is CORRECT — it is a distractor)
3. "My name is Jack?" (should be .)

Ask: "Look at these sentences. Some have the wrong end mark. Fix any that are wrong."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correctly fixes #1 (changes . to ?) AND #3 (changes ? to .) AND leaves #2 unchanged | 1 point |
| Fixes one of the two errors correctly, or fixes both but also incorrectly changes #2 | 0.5 points |
| Does not fix any errors or changes them all incorrectly | 0 points |

**D4.** (Written) Ask: "Write three sentences: one that is a telling sentence, one that is a question, and one that is an exclamation. Make sure to use the correct end mark for each."

Score each sentence independently:

| Component | Score |
|-----------|-------|
| Telling sentence with a period | 0 or 1/3 |
| Question with a question mark | 0 or 1/3 |
| Exclamation with an exclamation point | 0 or 1/3 |

**Scoring detail for D4:**

| Sentence types | Score |
|---|---|
| All 3 sentences are correct type with correct end mark | 1 point |
| 2 sentences correct type with correct end mark | 0.5 points |
| 1 sentence correct type with correct end mark | 0.25 points |
| None correct | 0 points |

*Facilitator note: For D4, accept any sentence content as long as it matches the intended type. For example, "I like dogs." is a valid telling sentence. "Do you like dogs?" is a valid question. "I love dogs!" is a valid exclamation. Do not deduct for spelling or capitalization errors — focus only on whether the sentence type matches the end punctuation.*

**Section D total: 4 points**

## Section E: Spelling Patterns (Lesson 9)

**E1.** (Written — CVC words) Say: "I am going to say some words. Write each word on your paper."

Dictate one at a time, using each in a sentence:
1. "hot" — "The soup is hot."
2. "pet" — "I have a pet fish."
3. "wig" — "She wore a wig."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| All 3 spelled correctly | 1 point |
| 2 of 3 spelled correctly | 0.5 points |
| Fewer than 2 spelled correctly | 0 points |

**E2.** (Written — CVCe words) Dictate one at a time:
1. "name" — "Write your name."
2. "bike" — "I ride my bike."
3. "home" — "Let's go home."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| All 3 spelled correctly | 1 point |
| 2 of 3 spelled correctly | 0.5 points |
| Fewer than 2 spelled correctly | 0 points |

*Facilitator note: The key pattern is that the student includes the silent e. If the student writes "nam," "bik," or "hom" (CVC without the silent e), this shows the CVCe pattern has not been internalized. If the student writes "naym" or "biek," give partial credit — they are showing awareness of the long vowel sound even though the spelling is non-standard.*

**E3.** (Written — Sight words) Dictate one at a time:
1. "said" — "She said hello."
2. "have" — "I have a cat."
3. "were" — "They were happy."
4. "come" — "Come to my house."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| All 4 spelled correctly | 1 point |
| 3 of 4 spelled correctly | 0.5 points |
| Fewer than 3 spelled correctly | 0 points |

*Facilitator note: These are high-frequency words that do not follow regular phonics patterns perfectly. Students are expected to have memorized these from Unit 1 and Lesson 9 practice. "sed" for "said," "hav" for "have," "wer" for "were," and "cum" for "come" are common phonetic approximations that show the student has not memorized the conventional spelling.*

**E4.** (Oral/Written) Show the student these two words written on paper: "hop" and "hope"

Ask: "These two words look almost the same. What is different about how they sound? Why?"

Then say: "Now I will say two words. Write each one."

Dictate: "cap" — "Put on your cap."
Then: "cape" — "The superhero wore a cape."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Explains the CVC/CVCe difference (e.g., "the e makes the vowel say its name") AND correctly spells both "cap" and "cape" | 1 point |
| Explains the difference OR correctly spells both words, but not both | 0.5 points |
| Cannot explain the difference AND misspells one or both words | 0 points |

**Section E total: 4 points**

## Section F: Applied Grammar in Writing (Lesson 10)

This section assesses the student's ability to apply multiple grammar and language skills in connected writing.

**F1.** (Written — Error correction) Write this passage on paper and show it to the student:

> "the cat name is whiskers. she have soft fur and big eyes? whiskers like to chase mouses around the house"

Ask: "This passage has some mistakes. Read it carefully and fix as many mistakes as you can."

The full list of errors:
1. Lowercase "the" should be "The" (beginning of sentence)
2. "cat" should be possessive: "cat's"
3. "whiskers" (first use, as a name) should be capitalized: "Whiskers" (proper noun — the cat's name)
4. "she" should be capitalized: "She" (beginning of sentence)
5. "have" should be "has" (subject-verb agreement)
6. The question mark after "eyes" should be a period (it is a statement, not a question)
7. "whiskers" (second use) should be capitalized: "Whiskers"
8. "like" should be "likes" (subject-verb agreement)
9. "mouses" should be "mice" (irregular plural) — accept "mouses" changed to any reasonable attempt
10. Missing period at the end of the last sentence

| Revisions | Score |
|---|---|
| Fixes 5 or more errors | 1 point |
| Fixes 3-4 errors | 0.5 points |
| Fixes fewer than 3 errors | 0 points |

*Facilitator note: This item integrates multiple skills from the entire unit. Do not prompt the student toward any specific error. Simply observe which types of errors they catch naturally. Record which errors the student fixed and which they missed — this is valuable diagnostic information. The most commonly caught errors will be capitalization at the beginning of a sentence and the wrong end punctuation. The possessive apostrophe and subject-verb agreement are more advanced and may be missed.*

**F2.** (Written) Ask: "Write 3-4 sentences about your favorite place to go. Try to use everything you have learned in this unit: different kinds of nouns, a pronoun, an action verb, an adjective, and correct end punctuation."

Give the student lined paper. Allow 5-7 minutes.

Score using this rubric:

| Component | Score |
|-----------|-------|
| Nouns and pronouns: Uses at least one proper noun correctly capitalized AND at least one pronoun | 0 or 0.25 or 0.5 |
| Verbs and adjectives: Uses at least one clear action verb AND at least one adjective | 0 or 0.25 or 0.5 |
| Conventions: Begins sentences with capital letters AND uses correct end punctuation on most sentences | 0 or 0.25 or 0.5 |

**Scoring detail for F2:**

| Nouns and pronouns | Score |
|---|---|
| Uses at least one proper noun with correct capitalization AND at least one pronoun | 0.5 points |
| Uses a proper noun (but not capitalized) OR uses a pronoun (but no proper noun), or has one but not the other | 0.25 points |
| No proper nouns and no pronouns evident | 0 points |

| Verbs and adjectives | Score |
|---|---|
| Uses at least one clear action verb AND at least one adjective (describing word) | 0.5 points |
| Uses an action verb but no adjective, or an adjective but no clear action verb | 0.25 points |
| Neither verbs nor adjectives are clearly present | 0 points |

| Conventions | Score |
|---|---|
| Most sentences (3 of 3, or 3 of 4) begin with a capital letter and end with correct punctuation | 0.5 points |
| Some sentences (about half) have correct capitalization and end punctuation | 0.25 points |
| Few or no sentences have correct capitalization and end punctuation | 0 points |

*Note: Do NOT deduct points for spelling errors in this section. Evaluate grammar usage, parts of speech variety, and conventions only. A piece that reads "I like to go to Grandmas hous. She has a big yrd. We run and play. It is so fun!" earns full marks — proper noun (Grandma's, even without apostrophe the capitalization counts), pronouns (She, We, It), action verbs (run, play, go), adjective (big, fun), and correct conventions throughout.*

**F3.** (Oral — Reflection) After the student finishes writing, ask: "Read your sentences to me. Can you find one noun, one verb, and one adjective in what you wrote?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correctly identifies all 3 parts of speech in their own writing | 0.5 points |
| Correctly identifies 2 of 3 | 0.25 points |
| Correctly identifies fewer than 2 | 0 points |

**Section F total: 3 points**

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Next Steps |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Mastered** | 21-25 (85-100%) with at least 1 correct in each section | Solid grammar and language foundations across all skill areas | This is the final Grade 1 ELA unit. Celebrate completion! The student is ready for Grade 2 ELA. Continue reinforcing grammar in daily writing. |
| **Approaching** | 18-20 (70-84%) | Most grammar skills are developing; specific areas need reinforcement | Identify weak sections and reteach (1-2 sessions each). The student may begin Grade 2 ELA while continuing targeted grammar practice from the reteaching guide below. |

## Section-Specific Reteaching Guide

| If the student struggles in... | What it tells you | Reteach with... |
|-------------------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Section A (Nouns)** | Cannot distinguish noun types, form plurals, or use possessives | Return to Lessons 1-4. Start with the noun sort: have the student physically sort picture cards into person, place, thing, and animal. For proper vs. common, use the "Does it have a special name?" test. For plurals, practice with objects on the table — "one cup, two cups; one box, two boxes" — and have the student listen for the -s vs. -es pattern. For possessives, use the physical possession strategy: hand the student an object and say "This is the dog's bone — where does the apostrophe go?" |
| **Section B (Pronouns)** | Cannot replace nouns with the correct pronoun | Return to Lesson 5. Use the pronoun swap game: read sentences aloud and have the student physically hold up a pronoun card (he, she, it, they, we, him, her, them, us) to replace the noun. Start with subject pronouns (he, she, it, they, we) before introducing object pronouns (him, her, them, us). The most common error is using subject pronouns where object pronouns belong — practice with "I gave it to ___" sentence frames. |
| **Section C (Verbs & Adjectives)** | Cannot identify or produce action verbs or adjectives | Return to Lessons 6-7. For verbs, play the "Act It Out" game: say a verb and the student acts it out, then the student acts and you guess. This builds the verb-as-action connection physically. For adjectives, use the "Describe the Mystery Object" game: put an object in a bag, and the student must describe it using only adjectives without naming it. Return to the anchor chart that distinguishes nouns, verbs, and adjectives. |
| **Section D (End Punctuation)** | Cannot match sentence types to correct end marks | Return to Lesson 8. Practice with the voice-to-punctuation connection: read sentences with exaggerated intonation (flat for statements, rising for questions, loud for exclamations) and have the student hold up the correct punctuation card. Then reverse — show a punctuation mark and have the student make up a sentence that matches. Practice until the voice-punctuation link is automatic. |
| **Section E (Spelling)** | Cannot spell CVC or CVCe words, or sight words | Return to Lesson 9. For CVC words, use sound boxes (Elkonin boxes) — the student pushes a counter into each box as they segment sounds, then writes the letter for each sound. For CVCe words, use the "magic e" wand from Unit 1 — the student adds the silent e to a CVC word and hears the vowel change. For sight words, use the look-say-cover-write-check method with the specific words missed. Practice 3-5 words per day, not more. |
| **Section F (Applied Grammar)** | Cannot apply grammar skills in connected writing or cannot find errors | Return to Lesson 10. Start with single-sentence error correction before progressing to multi-sentence passages. Use a color-coded editing system: red pencil for capitals, blue for end punctuation, green for spelling. Practice with the student's own writing from previous units — editing their own familiar text is less intimidating than editing unfamiliar text. For grammar application in writing, use sentence frames that gradually release scaffolding. |

## Detailed Rubric by Skill

### Noun Knowledge (Section A)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Common vs. proper nouns | Consistently distinguishes common from proper nouns; capitalizes proper nouns correctly in writing | Usually distinguishes the two types; may forget to capitalize some proper nouns | Cannot reliably tell common from proper nouns; capitalization is inconsistent or absent |
| Singular and plural nouns | Correctly forms plurals using both -s and -es; knows when each suffix is needed | Forms most plurals correctly; may add -s when -es is needed (e.g., "boxs") | Cannot form plurals reliably; may not understand the concept of singular vs. plural |
| Possessive nouns | Correctly uses apostrophe-s to show ownership in writing and speech | Understands that possessives show ownership but omits or misplaces the apostrophe | Does not understand possessive nouns; cannot express ownership using 's |

### Pronoun Usage (Section B)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Subject pronouns | Correctly replaces nouns with he, she, it, we, they in subject position | Correctly uses most subject pronouns; may confuse he/she or mix up singular/plural | Cannot replace nouns with pronouns; repeats the noun or guesses randomly |
| Object pronouns | Correctly uses him, her, them, us in object position | Understands the concept but uses subject pronouns in object position (e.g., "I gave it to she") | Cannot distinguish subject from object pronouns; does not attempt pronoun substitution |

### Verbs and Adjectives (Section C)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Action verbs | Identifies action verbs in sentences and generates multiple examples independently | Identifies some action verbs but may confuse verbs with nouns or adjectives | Cannot identify action verbs; does not understand what a verb does in a sentence |
| Adjectives | Uses adjectives in writing and identifies them in sentences; understands that adjectives describe nouns | Uses some adjectives in writing but cannot reliably identify them when asked | Does not use adjectives in writing; cannot explain what an adjective does |

### End Punctuation (Section D)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Identifies punctuation types | Names all three end marks and explains when each is used | Names 2 of 3 end marks; may confuse when to use question marks vs. exclamation points | Cannot name end marks or explain their purposes |
| Applies punctuation in writing | Consistently uses correct end punctuation in own writing; can fix incorrect punctuation in others' writing | Uses periods consistently but may omit or misuse question marks and exclamation points | Rarely or never uses end punctuation; does not recognize when punctuation is wrong |

### Spelling Patterns (Section E)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| CVC words | Spells CVC words accurately using knowledge of short vowel sounds | Spells most CVC words correctly; occasional vowel confusion (e.g., short e/i) | Cannot spell CVC words; omits or substitutes vowels frequently |
| CVCe words | Spells CVCe words accurately; includes the silent e; can explain CVC vs. CVCe difference | Sometimes includes the silent e; may write CVC form for CVCe words | Does not include the silent e; treats CVCe words as CVC |
| Sight words | Spells common sight words from memory | Spells some sight words correctly; uses phonetic approximations for others | Cannot spell sight words; relies entirely on phonetic approximation |

### Applied Grammar in Writing (Section F)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Error correction | Identifies and corrects 6+ errors across multiple grammar categories (capitalization, punctuation, possessives, agreement) | Identifies and corrects 3-5 errors; tends to catch capitalization and punctuation but miss agreement or possessives | Identifies and corrects 0-2 errors; may not recognize that errors are present |
| Grammar in connected writing | Writing demonstrates variety of parts of speech (nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives) with correct conventions | Writing uses some grammar variety but may lack adjectives or pronouns; conventions are partially correct | Writing is limited in grammar variety; conventions are largely absent |
| Metalinguistic awareness | Can identify nouns, verbs, and adjectives in own writing when asked | Can identify 1-2 parts of speech in own writing | Cannot identify parts of speech in own writing |

## Facilitator Observations (Record During Assessment)

Use these prompts to guide your observations during the assessment. These notes do not affect scoring but provide valuable information for planning future instruction.

- Does the student self-correct when speaking or writing? (This suggests developing metalinguistic awareness.)
- Which noun types does the student handle most confidently — common, proper, plural, or possessive?
- Does the student confuse subject and object pronouns (e.g., "her went to the store"), and if so, which direction (subject for object, or object for subject)?
- When identifying action verbs, does the student look for "doing" words or guess based on word position?
- Does the student use adjectives naturally in their writing, or only when explicitly prompted?
- How does the student approach the punctuation items — by reading the sentence aloud to hear the intonation, or by looking at it visually?
- During spelling dictation, does the student sound out each word, write from memory, or use a combination?
- On the error correction task (F1), which types of errors does the student catch first? Which does the student miss entirely?
- How confident does the student appear with grammar terminology (noun, verb, adjective, pronoun)? Can they use the terms, or do they understand the concepts but not the labels?
- Does the student show awareness that grammar serves communication — that correct grammar helps the reader understand?

These observations help distinguish between students who understand grammar conceptually but need practice with conventions, and students who need reteaching of the underlying concepts.
