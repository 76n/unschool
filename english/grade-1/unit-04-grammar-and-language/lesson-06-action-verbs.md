# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 4, Lesson 6

## Topic
Action Verbs (Words That Show Action: run, jump, eat, read, sing)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define a verb as a word that shows an action (something you can do)
- Identify action verbs in spoken and written sentences
- Use action verbs correctly in subject-verb pairs to write complete sentences

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of nouns as naming words for people, places, things, and animals (Lessons 1-3)
- Ability to write complete sentences with a subject and a verb (Unit 3, Lesson 2)
- Knowledge of common and proper nouns (Lessons 1-2)

## Materials Needed
- Chart paper or whiteboard and marker
- Pencil and lined paper
- Index cards with action verbs written on them (see Warm-Up): run, jump, eat, read, sing, hop, clap, swim, write, draw, kick, sleep, dance, cook, fly
- Index cards with noun subjects: "The dog," "My friend," "The bird," "I," "The cat," "Mom"
- Crayons or colored pencils (two colors — one for nouns, one for verbs)

**Prepare before the lesson:**
Write each verb on its own index card (large enough for the student to read). Write each noun/subject phrase on a separate card in a different color or with a different border so the student can tell them apart.

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about nouns and build excitement for learning a new type of word.

**Activity: Simon Says — Action Edition**
Play a quick round of Simon Says using action words:

Say: "We have been learning about nouns — words that name people, places, things, and animals. Today we are going to learn about a brand-new type of word. But first, let's play a game!"

- "Simon says... JUMP!" (Student jumps.)
- "Simon says... CLAP!" (Student claps.)
- "Simon says... SPIN!" (Student spins.)
- "STOMP!" (No "Simon says" — student should not stomp.)
- "Simon says... SIT DOWN!" (Student sits.)

After the game, say: "All of those words I said — jump, clap, spin, stomp, sit — are called ACTION WORDS. They tell us something you can DO. Today we are going to learn that action words have a special grammar name. They are called VERBS."

*Facilitator note: Starting with a physical game gets energy flowing and makes the abstract concept of "verb" concrete. The student literally performed the verbs before learning the term. This is intentional — the body remembers what the mind might forget.*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce verbs as action words and teach students how to find them in sentences.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Verb:** A word that shows an action — something you can do. Run, jump, eat, read, and sing are all verbs.
- **Action:** Something that happens — something a person, animal, or thing does.
- **Subject-verb pair:** The "who" (noun) and the "what they do" (verb) that work together in a sentence. In "The dog runs," the subject is "the dog" and the verb is "runs."

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Define verbs with examples.**
Write on chart paper:

| Nouns (naming words) | Verbs (action words) |
|---|---|
| dog | runs |
| girl | jumps |
| bird | sings |
| baby | eats |
| boy | reads |

Say: "Remember — nouns are naming words. They tell us WHO or WHAT. Verbs are different. Verbs are ACTION words. They tell us what someone DOES."

Point to each verb: "Can you run? (Yes!) Can you jump? (Yes!) Can you sing? (Yes!) Can you eat? (Yes!) Can you read? (Yes!) These are all things you can DO. That is what makes them verbs."

**Step 2: The "Can you DO it?" test.**
Say: "Here is a trick to figure out if a word is a verb. Ask yourself: 'Can I DO it?' If the answer is yes, it is probably a verb."

Practice together:
- "Swim — can you DO it? Yes! Swim is a verb."
- "Table — can you DO it? No! Table is a noun."
- "Dance — can you DO it? Yes! Dance is a verb."
- "House — can you DO it? No! House is a noun."
- "Write — can you DO it? Yes! Write is a verb."

**Step 3: Finding verbs in sentences.**
Write these sentences on chart paper:

1. "The cat sleeps on the bed."
2. "My brother kicks the ball."
3. "The bird flies in the sky."

For each sentence, model the thinking:
- Read the sentence aloud.
- Ask: "What is happening in this sentence? What is someone or something DOING?"
- Underline the verb.

For sentence 1: "The cat sleeps on the bed. What is the cat DOING? Sleeping! So 'sleeps' is the verb." Underline "sleeps."

**Step 4: Subject-verb pairs.**
Say: "Every sentence needs a NOUN (the who) and a VERB (the what they do). These two work together like best friends. We call them a subject-verb pair."

Point back to sentence 1: "WHO is doing something? The cat. WHAT is the cat doing? Sleeps. 'The cat sleeps' — that is our subject-verb pair."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "The dog runs in the park."
- "Let's find the verb. What is happening? The dog is RUNNING. 'Runs' is the verb."
- "Who is running? The dog. So the subject-verb pair is 'The dog runs.'"
- "Can I DO running? Yes! So 'runs' passes the verb test."

**Example 2:** "I eat an apple."
- "What am I DOING? Eating! 'Eat' is the verb."
- "Who is eating? I am. The subject-verb pair is 'I eat.'"
- "Can I DO eating? Yes! Verb confirmed."

**Example 3:** "The frog jumps over the log."
- "What is happening? Jumping! 'Jumps' is the verb."
- "Who is jumping? The frog. Subject-verb pair: 'The frog jumps.'"
- Let the student try the "Can I DO it?" test on their own this time.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice with support.

**Activity 1: Act It Out! (5 minutes)**
Spread the verb cards face down on the table. The student picks a card, reads the verb, and acts it out. Then the student uses that verb in a complete sentence.

Example flow:
- Student picks "swim." Student pretends to swim.
- Facilitator: "Great acting! Now put that verb in a sentence."
- Student: "The fish swims in the ocean."
- Facilitator: "Perfect! 'Swims' is the verb. Who swims? The fish. Subject-verb pair: 'The fish swims.'"

Do this for at least 5 verb cards: run, jump, eat, read, sing (or whichever the student draws).

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles to form a sentence, offer a frame: "[Someone] [verbs] [somewhere or something]." For example: "The ___ swims in the ___."*

**Activity 2: Match the Subject and Verb (5 minutes)**
Lay out the noun/subject cards and verb cards. The student matches a subject card to a verb card to make a sentence that makes sense.

Possible matches:
- "The dog" + "runs" → "The dog runs."
- "My friend" + "reads" → "My friend reads."
- "The bird" + "sings" → "The bird sings."
- "I" + "eat" → "I eat."
- "The cat" + "sleeps" → "The cat sleeps."
- "Mom" + "cooks" → "Mom cooks."

After each match, the student reads the sentence aloud and identifies the subject-verb pair.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student makes a funny combination like "The bird cooks," enjoy the silliness! Then ask: "Is 'cooks' still a verb? Can you DO cooking? Yes! So it is a verb — even if it is silly for a bird to cook."*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices independently.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Circle the verb in each sentence:
   - "The boy kicks the ball." (kicks)
   - "My sister reads a book." (reads)
   - "The rabbit hops in the grass." (hops)
   - "Dad cooks dinner." (cooks)
2. Write 3 sentences about things you like to do. Underline the verb in each sentence.

**Stretch:**
Complete the On-level tasks, PLUS:
3. Fill in the missing verb to complete each sentence:
   - "The baby ___." (cries, sleeps, laughs, etc.)
   - "The fish ___ in the water." (swims, splashes, etc.)
   - "My teacher ___ a story." (reads, tells, writes, etc.)
4. Write 2 sentences where the same noun does two different actions. Example: "The dog runs. The dog eats."

**Challenge:**
Complete the On-level and Stretch tasks, PLUS:
5. Write 4 sentences about your morning routine. Each sentence must have a different verb. Underline every verb.
   Example: "I wake up early. I brush my teeth. I eat breakfast. I walk to school."
6. Bonus: Can you write one sentence that has TWO verbs? Example: "The cat runs and jumps."

### Interleaved Review
- "Look at your sentences. Circle all the NOUNS in a different color." (Lessons 1-3 review — nouns)
- "Find a proper noun in this sentence: 'Sam kicks the ball.' Which word is the proper noun and how do you know?" (Lesson 2 review — proper nouns start with a capital letter)
- "Read this sentence: 'The dog runs fast.' Does it start with a capital letter? Does it end with a period?" (Unit 3, Lesson 2 review — complete sentences)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What is a verb?" (Answer: A word that shows an action — something you can do.)
2. "Is the word 'jump' a noun or a verb? How do you know?" (Answer: Verb — because you can DO jumping.)
3. "Find the verb in this sentence: 'The girl sings a song.'" (Answer: sings)
4. "What is the subject-verb pair in 'My cat sleeps on the couch'?" (Answer: My cat sleeps.)

**Mastery = Student correctly answers at least 3 of 4 questions, including identifying a verb in a sentence.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the Act It Out activity with new verbs (walk, talk, wave, paint, throw). Physical movement helps solidify the concept.
- Use the "Can you DO it?" test with 10 words — mix nouns and verbs. Let the student sort them into two piles.
- Practice finding verbs in simple sentences from a favorite book by reading a page together and pointing to the action words.
- Repeat this lesson next session before moving to Lesson 7.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (Adjectives — Describing Words). Continue to have the student identify verbs in sentences during future writing activities.

---

## Extension (Optional)
- **Verb Charades:** Write 10 verb cards. Take turns picking a card and acting out the verb. The other person guesses the verb.
- **Verb Hunt:** Open any book to a random page. How many verbs can the student find in one paragraph? Circle them all.
- **Verb Poem:** Write a short poem using at least 5 action verbs. Example: "I run and jump and play all day. I sing and dance and shout hooray!"

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Verbs are the engine of every sentence.** Without a verb, you just have a fragment (like "The dog" — which the student learned is incomplete in Unit 3, Lesson 2). This lesson builds directly on that foundation. When the student understands verbs, they understand WHY sentences need that second piece.

**The "Can you DO it?" test is the single most useful tool in this lesson.** First-graders are concrete thinkers. Asking "Can you do it?" turns an abstract grammar category into a physical question they can answer with their bodies. Use this test consistently in future lessons whenever a student is unsure if a word is a verb.

**Keep it physical.** Six- and seven-year-olds learn grammar best when they can move. The Simon Says game, the Act It Out activity, and even just standing up and performing a verb before writing it in a sentence — all of these connect the body to the brain. If your student is restless, add MORE movement, not less.

**Common confusion: "Is 'sleeps' an action?"** Some students think actions must be big and obvious (run, jump, kick) and resist calling quieter words like "sleeps," "reads," or "thinks" verbs. Explain: "If you can DO it, it is an action — even if it is a quiet, calm action. Sleeping is something you DO every night!"

**Connecting to writing:** After this lesson, whenever the student writes sentences in any subject, gently ask: "What is the verb in your sentence?" This builds the habit of thinking about sentence structure. In Lesson 10, the student will use this skill as part of a grammar checklist for their own writing.
