# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 4, Lesson 7

## Topic
Adjectives — Describing Words (Color, Size, Shape, Number, Feeling)

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define an adjective as a word that describes a noun (tells more about it)
- Identify adjectives in spoken and written sentences
- Use adjectives to improve sentences by adding detail (adjective + noun pairs)

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of nouns as naming words (Lessons 1-3)
- Understanding of verbs as action words (Lesson 6)
- Ability to write complete sentences with a subject and a verb (Unit 3, Lesson 2)

## Materials Needed
- Chart paper or whiteboard and marker
- Pencil and lined paper
- A small collection of household objects in different colors, sizes, and shapes (e.g., a red cup, a big book, a small ball, a round plate, a soft pillow — 6-8 items)
- Index cards with adjectives written on them: big, small, red, blue, green, happy, sad, round, tall, soft, fast, three, old, new, loud, quiet
- Index cards with nouns: dog, ball, cat, house, tree, flower, bird, car
- Crayons or colored pencils

**Prepare before the lesson:**
Gather 6-8 household objects that are easy to describe. Write adjective cards and noun cards on separate index cards (use different colors or markings to tell them apart).

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review nouns and verbs and introduce the idea that words can tell us MORE about a noun.

**Activity: Mystery Bag Describing Game**
Place 3-4 small objects in a bag or behind your back. Without showing the student, describe one object using only adjectives:

Say: "I am going to describe something, and you have to guess what it is. Ready?"

- "It is round. It is small. It is red." (a small red ball)
- "It is soft. It is big. It is white." (a pillow)
- "It is long. It is yellow. It is thin." (a pencil)

After the student guesses, say: "How did you figure it out? You used the DESCRIBING WORDS I gave you! Words like 'round,' 'small,' 'red,' 'soft,' 'big' — these words helped you picture the object in your mind. Today we are going to learn that describing words have a special name. They are called ADJECTIVES."

*Facilitator note: This activity shows the student the PURPOSE of adjectives before teaching the rule. When they experience how describing words help them understand, the grammar concept clicks more easily.*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce adjectives as words that describe nouns and teach five categories of adjectives.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Adjective:** A word that describes a noun. It tells us more about the noun — what it looks like, how many there are, or how it feels.
- **Describe:** To tell about something using words — what color it is, how big it is, what shape it is, how it makes you feel.
- **Adjective + noun pair:** An adjective and a noun that go together, like "red ball" or "big dog."

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Show the five types of adjectives.**
Write this chart on chart paper:

| Type | What It Tells | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Color | What color? | red, blue, green, yellow, pink |
| Size | How big or small? | big, small, tiny, huge, tall, short |
| Shape | What shape? | round, square, flat, long, thin |
| Number | How many? | one, two, three, many, few |
| Feeling | How does it feel or seem? | happy, sad, soft, hard, loud, quiet, pretty |

Say: "Adjectives are describing words. They answer questions like: What COLOR is it? How BIG is it? What SHAPE is it? How MANY are there? How does it FEEL?"

**Step 2: Demonstrate with real objects.**
Hold up each household object and model describing it:

Hold up a red cup: "This is a cup. 'Cup' is a noun — a thing. Now let me describe it. It is RED. It is SMALL. It is ROUND. The words 'red,' 'small,' and 'round' are all adjectives. They describe the noun 'cup.'"

Hold up a big book: "This is a book — noun. It is BIG. It is HEAVY. It is BLUE. Those are all adjectives describing the book."

**Step 3: Adjectives make sentences better.**
Write on chart paper:

- **Without adjectives:** "I have a dog."
- **With adjectives:** "I have a big, brown dog."

Say: "Both sentences are correct. But which one helps you picture the dog better? The second one! The adjectives 'big' and 'brown' help the reader SEE the dog in their mind."

Write another pair:
- "The ball bounced." → "The small red ball bounced."

Say: "Adjectives are like adding color to a black-and-white picture. They make your writing come alive!"

**Step 4: Where adjectives go in a sentence.**
Say: "Adjectives usually go RIGHT BEFORE the noun they describe."

Write and point:
- "The **tall** tree" — "tall" comes before "tree"
- "A **happy** girl" — "happy" comes before "girl"
- "**Three** cats" — "three" comes before "cats"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "The fluffy cat sleeps on the soft bed."
- "Let's find the adjectives. What word describes 'cat'? Fluffy! What kind of cat? A FLUFFY cat."
- "What word describes 'bed'? Soft! What kind of bed? A SOFT bed."
- "The adjectives are 'fluffy' and 'soft.'"

**Example 2:** "Two little birds sing a pretty song."
- "How many birds? Two — that is a number adjective."
- "What kind of birds? Little — that is a size adjective."
- "What kind of song? Pretty — that is a feeling adjective."
- "Three adjectives: two, little, pretty."

**Example 3:** Improve a plain sentence.
- Start with: "I see a house."
- "Let's add adjectives. What COLOR could the house be? (Red, white, blue...) How BIG? (Big, small, tall...) Let's try: 'I see a big red house.'"
- "Now the reader can picture it!"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice with support.

**Activity 1: Adjective + Noun Match (5 minutes)**
Spread the adjective cards and noun cards on the table. The student picks one adjective card and one noun card and puts them together to make an adjective + noun pair. Then the student uses the pair in a sentence.

Example flow:
- Student picks "soft" and "cat." → "soft cat"
- Student says: "The soft cat sleeps on my lap."
- Facilitator: "Wonderful! 'Soft' is the adjective. It describes the noun 'cat.' What type of adjective is 'soft'?" (Feeling.)

Do at least 5 pairings. Encourage the student to try different types of adjectives (color, size, shape, number, feeling).

*Facilitator prompt: If the student picks a combination that seems odd (like "three flower"), help them use it: "Three flowers grow in the garden. See? 'Three' tells us how many!"*

**Activity 2: Describe This! (5 minutes)**
Place 3 household objects in front of the student, one at a time. For each object, the student names at least 3 adjectives that describe it, then writes a sentence using at least one adjective.

Object 1 (e.g., an apple): "Red, round, small. → The small red apple is on the table."
Object 2 (e.g., a shoe): "Big, brown, old. → I wear my old brown shoes."
Object 3 (e.g., a blanket): "Soft, blue, warm. → The soft blue blanket is on my bed."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student can only think of one adjective, prompt with questions: "What COLOR is it? How BIG is it? How does it FEEL when you touch it?" These questions match the five types on the chart.*

---

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices independently.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Circle the adjective(s) in each sentence:
   - "The big dog runs fast." (big)
   - "I eat a red apple." (red)
   - "She has three new books." (three, new)
   - "The little bird sings a pretty song." (little, pretty)
2. Add an adjective to make each sentence more interesting. Rewrite the whole sentence:
   - "I see a cat." → (e.g., "I see a fluffy cat.")
   - "The boy has a ball." → (e.g., "The happy boy has a blue ball.")

**Stretch:**
Complete the On-level tasks, PLUS:
3. Write 3 sentences about your bedroom. Each sentence must have at least one adjective. Underline every adjective.
   Example: "I have a soft blue pillow. My small desk is brown. Two big posters are on my wall."
4. For each sentence you wrote, tell what TYPE of adjective you used (color, size, shape, number, or feeling).

**Challenge:**
Complete the On-level and Stretch tasks, PLUS:
5. Write a "describing paragraph" about your favorite animal (3-4 sentences). Use at least 6 different adjectives. Underline each adjective and draw an arrow to the noun it describes.
   Example: "I love big, friendly dogs. They have soft, warm fur. My brown dog has two floppy ears. He is the best dog in the whole wide world."
6. Bonus: Rewrite this plain paragraph by adding adjectives to make it more interesting:
   "I have a room. There is a bed. There is a desk. I have toys." → (e.g., "I have a small blue room. There is a soft white bed. There is a big brown desk. I have many fun toys.")

### Interleaved Review
- "Look at your sentences. Circle all the NOUNS in one color and all the VERBS in another color." (Lessons 1-3 and Lesson 6 review — nouns and verbs)
- "Find the verb in this sentence: 'The tall tree grows in the yard.' What is the action word?" (Lesson 6 review — verbs; answer: grows)
- "Is 'Sarah' a common noun or a proper noun?" (Lesson 2 review — proper nouns)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What is an adjective?" (Answer: A word that describes a noun — it tells more about the noun.)
2. "Name the five types of adjectives we learned." (Answer: Color, size, shape, number, feeling.)
3. "Find the adjective in this sentence: 'The fast rabbit hops away.'" (Answer: fast)
4. "Add an adjective to this sentence: 'I have a _____ dog.'" (Answer: Any appropriate adjective — big, little, brown, fluffy, happy, etc.)

**Mastery = Student correctly answers at least 3 of 4 questions, including finding an adjective in a sentence.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Return to the household objects activity. For each object, ask the five questions: What color? How big? What shape? How many? How does it feel? Write the adjectives on sticky notes and attach them to the object.
- Play "Add an Adjective" orally: say a noun and the student adds an adjective. "Ball." → "Red ball." "Cat." → "Fluffy cat." Do 10 of these before trying written work again.
- Repeat this lesson next session before moving to Lesson 8.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 8 (End Punctuation). Continue asking the student to add adjectives to their writing in all future lessons.

## Extension (Optional)
- **Adjective Scavenger Hunt:** Walk around the house or look out a window. Find 5 things and describe each one with at least 2 adjectives. Write them down: "I see a tall green tree. I see a small white cloud."
- **Silly Adjective Sentences:** Roll two dice (or pick two random adjective cards) and combine them with a noun for a silly sentence: "The tiny purple elephant danced on the round blue table."
- **Adjective Art:** Draw a picture of an imaginary animal. Then write 5 sentences describing it using adjectives: "It has long green ears. It has a round pink nose."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Adjectives are the "paint" of writing.** Nouns give you the outline. Verbs make things move. But adjectives add color, size, and texture. First-graders are naturally descriptive when they talk ("Look at the BIG truck! It's SO red!"), so this lesson channels what they already do into formal grammar knowledge.

**Use the five types as a framework, not a strict rule.** The categories (color, size, shape, number, feeling) give the student a way to THINK about describing. When a student is stuck ("I can't think of an adjective!"), you can prompt: "What COLOR is it? How BIG is it?" This scaffold is much more helpful than just saying "Add a describing word."

**Common confusion: adjectives vs. nouns.** Some students confuse adjectives with nouns because they think about the thing being described rather than the describing word itself. If the student circles "ball" instead of "red" in "the red ball," point to each word and ask: "Is 'ball' telling us the name of something or describing something? It is the NAME — so it is a noun. Is 'red' a name or a description? It is a description — so it is the adjective."

**Connecting to writing:** From this lesson forward, whenever the student writes (in any subject), encourage them to "add adjectives." This is an easy, concrete revision strategy. In Lesson 10, the student will use a grammar checklist that includes "Did I use adjectives to make my writing interesting?" This lesson lays the foundation for that self-editing skill.

**Do not worry about adjective order rules.** In English, adjectives follow a specific order (quantity-opinion-size-age-shape-color-origin-material-purpose), but this is far too advanced for first grade. If the student writes "the red big dog" instead of "the big red dog," gently model the more natural order but do not make it a formal lesson point. They will internalize the order naturally through reading and listening.

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice Activity 1 (Adjective + Noun Match):**
- Any adjective + noun combination that makes sense is correct. Examples: big dog, red ball, happy cat, small house, round flower, tall tree, green bird, fast car.

**Independent Practice — On-level (Circle the Adjective):**
1. big
2. red
3. three, new
4. little, pretty

**Independent Practice — On-level (Add an Adjective):**
- Answers will vary. Any appropriate adjective is correct. Examples:
  - "I see a fluffy cat." / "I see a black cat." / "I see a small cat."
  - "The happy boy has a blue ball." / "The tall boy has a round ball."

**Independent Practice — Stretch (Types of Adjectives):**
- Answers will vary. Student should correctly identify the type (color, size, shape, number, or feeling) for each adjective used.

**Exit Ticket:**
1. A word that describes a noun — tells more about it (what it looks like, how many, how it feels).
2. Color, size, shape, number, feeling.
3. The adjective is "fast."
4. Any appropriate adjective (big, small, fluffy, brown, happy, etc.).
