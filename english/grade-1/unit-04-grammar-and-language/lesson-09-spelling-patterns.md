# English Language Arts — Grade 1 — Unit 4, Lesson 9

## Topic
Spelling Patterns — CVC Words, CVCe Words, and Common Sight Words

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Spell CVC (short vowel) and CVCe (silent-e long vowel) words correctly by identifying the vowel pattern
- Read and spell at least 10 common sight words from memory (said, was, have, they, what, where, come, does, give, were)
- Connect spelling patterns to phonics knowledge to decode and encode unfamiliar words

## Prerequisites
- Knowledge of short vowel CVC words and long vowel CVCe (silent-e) words (Unit 1, Lessons 1 and 6)
- Familiarity with high-frequency sight words from Kindergarten (Unit K-2)
- Ability to write complete sentences with correct end punctuation (Unit 3, Lesson 2; Unit 4, Lesson 8)

## Materials Needed
- Chart paper or whiteboard and marker
- Pencil and lined paper
- Letter tiles, magnetic letters, or small letter cards (a full alphabet set plus extra vowels and common consonants)
- Index cards or flashcards with sight words written on them: said, was, have, they, what, where, come, does, give, were
- Two sorting baskets, boxes, or labeled sections of the table: "CVC (Short Vowel)" and "CVCe (Long Vowel)"
- Word cards for sorting (prepare these — see Guided Practice): cat, hat, map, bed, hop, sit, cake, lake, made, bike, ride, home, rope, cute, tube

**Prepare before the lesson:**
Write each word from the sorting list on a separate index card. Write sight words on flashcards large enough for easy reading. Have letter tiles or magnetic letters set up and ready.

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate phonics knowledge and bridge to spelling.

**Activity: Quick Sound Stretch**
Say: "Before we work on spelling today, let's warm up our sound-stretching muscles. I am going to say a word, and you stretch it out into its sounds. Ready?"

- "cat" → /k/ /a/ /t/ (3 sounds)
- "bike" → /b/ /iy/ /k/ (3 sounds — remind the student the e is silent)
- "hop" → /h/ /o/ /p/ (3 sounds)
- "name" → /n/ /ay/ /m/ (3 sounds — silent e)

After stretching, say: "Great! When you stretch a word into its sounds, you are already doing the hardest part of spelling. Today we are going to learn two spelling PATTERNS that help you spell hundreds of words. We are also going to practice some tricky words that do not follow the patterns — we call those sight words."

Then say: "Remember all the grammar we have been learning — nouns, verbs, adjectives, punctuation? Spelling is what makes those words LOOK right on the page. Good spelling helps people read what you write."

*Facilitator note: Sound stretching (segmenting) is the phonemic awareness skill that underlies spelling. If the student can hear the individual sounds, they can match letters to those sounds. This warm-up reinforces that connection.*

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach two predictable spelling patterns (CVC and CVCe) and introduce common sight words that do not follow patterns.

### Key Vocabulary
- **CVC word:** A word with the pattern Consonant-Vowel-Consonant. The vowel makes its SHORT sound. Examples: cat (c-a-t), hop (h-o-p), sit (s-i-t).
- **CVCe word:** A word with the pattern Consonant-Vowel-Consonant-e. The final e is silent and makes the vowel say its LONG sound (its name). Examples: cake (c-a-k-e), bike (b-i-k-e), home (h-o-m-e).
- **Sight word:** A common word that does not follow regular spelling patterns. You have to learn it "by sight" — by memorizing how it looks. Examples: said, was, have.
- **Spelling pattern:** A regular way that letters and sounds work together in words.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Review the CVC pattern.**
Write on chart paper:

| CVC Pattern |
|---|
| **C**onsonant - **V**owel - **C**onsonant |
| The vowel says its SHORT sound. |

Build the word "cat" with letter tiles: c - a - t.
Say: "C-A-T. The pattern is consonant-vowel-consonant. The vowel is A, and it makes the short sound: /a/. Cat."

Build more CVC words together:
- h-o-p: "H-O-P. Short /o/. Hop."
- s-i-t: "S-I-T. Short /i/. Sit."
- b-e-d: "B-E-D. Short /e/. Bed."
- r-u-g: "R-U-G. Short /u/. Rug."

Say: "When you see or hear a short vowel word, you know the pattern is CVC. This helps you SPELL it: listen for the beginning sound, the short vowel in the middle, and the ending sound."

**Step 2: Review the CVCe pattern.**
Write on chart paper:

| CVCe Pattern |
|---|
| **C**onsonant - **V**owel - **C**onsonant - **e** |
| The vowel says its LONG sound (its name). The e is SILENT. |

Build the word "cake" with letter tiles: c - a - k - e.
Say: "C-A-K-E. The pattern is consonant-vowel-consonant-e. The e at the end is SILENT, but it tells the A to say its name: /ay/. Cake."

Build more CVCe words:
- b-i-k-e: "Long /iy/. Bike."
- h-o-m-e: "Long /oh/. Home."
- c-u-t-e: "Long /yoo/. Cute."
- m-a-d-e: "Long /ay/. Made."

Say: "The spelling trick for CVCe words: when you hear a LONG vowel sound and the word ends with a consonant sound, add the silent e at the end."

**Step 3: Show how the pattern changes the word.**
This is the key moment. Build "hop" → then add e → "hope."

- "H-O-P. Short /o/. Hop — like a bunny hops."
- Add the e: "H-O-P-E. Now the O says its name: /oh/. Hope — like 'I hope it is sunny tomorrow.'"
- "One little silent e changed the WHOLE word! This is why spelling patterns matter."

Do one more: "cap" → "cape."

**Step 4: Introduce sight words.**
Say: "Now, some very common words do NOT follow these patterns. They are tricky! We call them SIGHT WORDS because you have to learn them by sight — by memorizing how they look."

Show each sight word flashcard, one at a time. For each word:
- Read it aloud.
- Spell it aloud together (letter by letter).
- Say what is tricky about it.

| Word | What Is Tricky |
|---|---|
| said | Looks like it should say "sayed" but sounds like "sed" |
| was | The a makes an /uh/ sound, not /a/ or /ay/ |
| have | Has an e at the end but the a is SHORT (not long!) |
| they | The ey makes the long /ay/ sound |
| what | The a makes an /uh/ sound |
| where | The ere makes an /air/ sound |
| come | Has an e at the end but the o is SHORT |
| does | Looks like it should rhyme with "toes" but sounds like "duz" |
| give | Has an e at the end but the i is SHORT |
| were | Sounds like "wur" — the e does not follow the rule |

Say: "These words break the rules, so we just have to REMEMBER them. Let's practice a trick: look at the word, say it, spell it, say it again. Look-Say-Spell-Say!"

Practice Look-Say-Spell-Say with 5 of the sight words.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Spell the word "bed."
- "I hear /b/ /e/ /d/. Three sounds."
- "Is the vowel short or long? SHORT — /e/."
- "Short vowel = CVC pattern."
- "B-E-D. Bed."

**Example 2:** Spell the word "ride."
- "I hear /r/ /iy/ /d/. Three sounds."
- "Is the vowel short or long? LONG — /iy/ — the I says its name."
- "Long vowel = CVCe pattern. I need the silent e."
- "R-I-D-E. Ride."

**Example 3:** Spell the word "said."
- "Hmm, I hear /s/ /e/ /d/. It sounds like 'sed.'"
- "But 'said' is a tricky sight word. It is spelled S-A-I-D, not S-E-D."
- "This one I just have to remember. Look-Say-Spell-Say: SAID — S, A, I, D — SAID."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice with support.

**Activity 1: Word Sort (5 minutes)**
Set up two sorting areas labeled "CVC (Short Vowel)" and "CVCe (Long Vowel)."

Give the student the 15 word cards. The student reads each word, decides if the vowel is short (CVC) or long (CVCe), and places it in the correct pile.

CVC words: cat, hat, map, bed, hop, sit
CVCe words: cake, lake, made, bike, ride, home, rope, cute, tube

For each word, ask: "Is the vowel short or long? How do you know?"

*Facilitator prompt: If the student is unsure, say: "Look at the end of the word. Is there a silent e? If yes, the vowel is long. If no, the vowel is short." This connects back to Unit 1, Lesson 6.*

After sorting, read all the CVC words aloud, then all the CVCe words aloud. "Hear the difference? Short vowels in this pile, long vowels in that pile."

**Activity 2: Sight Word Practice (5 minutes)**
Use the Look-Say-Spell-Say method with all 10 sight words.

Round 1: Show each flashcard. Student reads the word.
Round 2: Show each flashcard. Student spells the word aloud while looking at it.
Round 3: Say the word aloud WITHOUT showing the card. Student tries to spell it from memory (on paper or aloud).

Keep track of which words the student gets right and which need more practice. Set the tricky ones aside for extra review.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student misspells a sight word, do not just correct them. Show the card and say: "Let's look at it together. The tricky part is [point to the tricky part]. Let's spell it three times: W-A-S, W-A-S, W-A-S." Repetition with visual support builds memory.*

*Game variation: Play "Sight Word Memory." Lay the flashcards face down. The student flips one, reads it, and spells it. If correct, they keep it. If not, it goes back face down. Continue until all cards are collected.*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices independently.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**
1. Spell each word. Write CVC or CVCe next to it:
   - "map" (m-a-p, CVC)
   - "lake" (l-a-k-e, CVCe)
   - "hot" (h-o-t, CVC)
   - "bike" (b-i-k-e, CVCe)
   - "rug" (r-u-g, CVC)
   - "home" (h-o-m-e, CVCe)
2. Write each sight word from memory (facilitator says the word, student writes it):
   - said, was, have, they, what

**Stretch:**
Complete the On-level tasks, PLUS:
3. Transform these CVC words into CVCe words by adding silent e. Write both words:
   - hop → hope
   - mad → made
   - cut → cute
   - pin → pine
   - rob → robe
4. Write the remaining sight words from memory:
   - where, come, does, give, were

**Challenge:**
Complete the On-level and Stretch tasks, PLUS:
5. Write 4 sentences. Each sentence must include at least one CVC word, one CVCe word, and one sight word. Underline the CVC word, circle the CVCe word, and draw a box around the sight word.
   Example: "They said the cute cat was hiding." (They = sight word boxed, cute = CVCe circled, cat = CVC underlined, said = sight word boxed, was = sight word boxed)
6. Think of 2 CVC words and 2 CVCe words that were NOT on today's lists. Write them and label the pattern.

### Interleaved Review
- "In the sentence 'The big cat runs home,' find the adjective, the verb, and the nouns." (Lesson 7 review: big = adjective; Lesson 6 review: runs = verb; Lessons 1-3 review: cat, home = nouns)
- "Add the correct end mark to this sentence: 'Where does the dog live'" (Lesson 8 review: question mark)
- "Is 'dog' a common noun or a proper noun?" (Lessons 1-2 review: common noun)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. "Spell the word 'cake.' What pattern is it — CVC or CVCe?" (Answer: C-A-K-E, CVCe.)
2. "Spell the word 'sit.' What pattern is it?" (Answer: S-I-T, CVC.)
3. "Spell the sight word 'said.'" (Answer: S-A-I-D.)
4. "If I hear a long vowel sound in a short word, which pattern should I use to spell it — CVC or CVCe?" (Answer: CVCe — add the silent e.)
5. "Spell the sight word 'they.'" (Answer: T-H-E-Y.)

**Mastery = Student correctly spells at least 4 of 5 words and correctly identifies at least one pattern (CVC or CVCe).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Focus on CVC vs. CVCe separately. Practice CVC words until the student can spell 5 from dictation, then add CVCe words.
- For sight words, reduce the list to 5 words (said, was, have, they, what) and practice with the Look-Say-Spell-Say method daily for a week.
- Use letter tiles to build and rebuild words physically — the hands-on manipulation helps.
- Repeat this lesson next session before moving to Lesson 10.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 10 (Grammar Review and Application). Continue practicing sight words in daily reading and writing.

## Extension (Optional)
- **Word Family Houses:** Draw a "house" for each word family. The -at house has: cat, hat, bat, mat, sat, rat, flat. The -ake house has: cake, lake, make, bake, take, wake, shake. How many words can the student put in each house?
- **Sight Word Sentences:** Use all 10 sight words to write a silly story. Every sentence must include at least one sight word. Example: "They said a bear was in the house. 'What does it want?' I asked. 'Give it some cake!' said Mom."
- **Spelling Detective:** Read a page from a book together. Find 3 CVC words, 3 CVCe words, and 3 sight words from the list. Write them down and label each one.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson bridges phonics and grammar.** Until now, Unit 4 has focused on the types and roles of words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, punctuation). This lesson shifts to how words are SPELLED. The connection is important: students need to know what words mean AND how to write them correctly. Spelling is a tool that supports all the other grammar skills learned in this unit.

**CVC and CVCe patterns are the two most productive spelling patterns in early reading.** Together, they cover a large percentage of the one-syllable words first-graders encounter. By making these patterns explicit, you give the student a strategy for spelling unknown words: "Is the vowel short or long? Short = CVC. Long = CVCe." This is far more powerful than memorizing individual words.

**Sight words require a different approach.** Pattern-based words can be sounded out. Sight words cannot — or at least not reliably. The Look-Say-Spell-Say method (also called "visual spelling") builds a mental picture of the word. Repetition is key. Do not expect mastery of all 10 sight words in one lesson. Plan to review 2-3 sight words at the start of every session for the next several weeks.

**Common errors and how to address them:**
- **Writing "hom" instead of "home":** The student heard the sounds correctly but forgot the silent e. Remind them: "The O says its name — /oh/. When the vowel says its name, we need that silent e at the end."
- **Writing "sed" instead of "said":** The student is spelling phonetically, which is actually logical! Praise the attempt: "You heard the sounds perfectly. But 'said' is a tricky sight word. It is spelled S-A-I-D. Let's practice it three more times."
- **Confusing CVC and CVCe:** The student writes "bik" for "bike" or "cate" for "cat." Practice the pairs side by side: "hop / hope," "mad / made," "cut / cute." The contrast builds the pattern recognition.

**Connecting to writing:** In Lesson 10, the student will write a mini-project and use a grammar checklist that includes "Did I spell CVC and CVCe words correctly?" and "Did I check my sight words?" This lesson gives them the tools to do that self-checking.
