# English Language Arts — Grade 7 — Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Topic
Integrated Grammar and Language Review

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Apply all unit skills (phrases, clauses, sentence types, coordinate adjective commas, and spelling) in the context of authentic reading and writing
- Edit a passage for multiple types of grammar and convention errors simultaneously
- Compose original writing that demonstrates deliberate sentence type choices, correct comma usage, and correct spelling

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of the four phrase types and their functions (Lessons 1, 3)
- Ability to distinguish independent and dependent clauses (Lesson 2)
- Ability to identify and construct all four sentence types (Lessons 4-6)
- Understanding of choosing sentence types for effect (Lesson 7)
- Ability to use commas with coordinate adjectives (Lesson 8)
- Knowledge of spelling rules and patterns (Lesson 9)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector)
- Printed or projected review passages (one for editing, one for analysis)
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Colored pencils (full color-coding set from the unit)
- All reference materials from the unit (Sentence Type Flowchart, Coordinate Adjective Decision Tree, Spelling Rules Reference Card, Subordinating Conjunction List)
- Grammar and Language Review Checklist (provided below)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate all unit skills simultaneously and build confidence before the integrated review.

**Activity: Grammar Lightning Round**

Say: "I am going to show you six quick items — one from each major topic in this unit. Answer each one in your notebook as fast as you can. You have three minutes."

Write on the board:
1. **Phrase type:** What type of phrase is underlined? "The students, *eager to start the experiment*, gathered around the lab table."
2. **Clause type:** Independent or dependent? "Although the weather forecast was promising"
3. **Sentence type:** Simple, compound, complex, or compound-complex? "The choir rehearsed in the auditorium, and the band practiced in the music room."
4. **Coordinate adjectives:** Comma or no comma? "A fierce loyal dog guarded the farm."
5. **Spelling:** Correct the spelling: "The goverment announced that the new libary would open in Febuary."
6. **Sentence choice:** Which sentence type would be most effective for a dramatic one-line conclusion to a story?

After three minutes, review quickly.

Say: "Today is our final lesson before the unit assessment. We are putting everything together — not as separate skills but as tools that work together in real reading and real writing. When you edit a paragraph, you do not check for just one thing at a time. You look at the whole picture."

**Quick Answers:**
1. Appositive phrase (renames "students")
2. Dependent (starts with "although")
3. Compound (two independent clauses joined by ", and")
4. Comma: "A fierce**,** loyal dog" (reversal: "loyal, fierce" — fine; "and" test: "fierce and loyal" — fine; coordinate)
5. "The **government** announced that the new **library** would open in **February**."
6. Simple sentence — short and impactful

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model how to apply all unit skills together when editing and composing, using an integrated checklist approach.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Integrated editing:** The process of checking a piece of writing for multiple types of errors and opportunities for improvement simultaneously
- **Grammar and Language Review Checklist:** A systematic list of items to check when editing — phrases, clauses, sentence types, commas, spelling
- **Revision for effect:** Going beyond error correction to make deliberate choices that strengthen the writing's impact
- **Conventions:** The accepted rules of written English, including grammar, punctuation, and spelling

### Instruction Notes

Say: "In real writing — an essay, a story, a lab report — grammar skills do not come one at a time. You need them all at once. Today I am going to show you how to edit a passage using an integrated approach."

**The Grammar and Language Review Checklist:**

Write on the board:

1. **Sentence Types:** Is there variety? Are simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences used deliberately? Does the sentence type match the purpose?
2. **Phrases and Clauses:** Are phrases and clauses functioning correctly? Are dependent clauses punctuated properly (comma when they come first)?
3. **Coordinate Adjectives:** Are commas used between coordinate adjectives? Are cumulative adjectives left without commas?
4. **Spelling:** Are all words spelled correctly? Check the five rules: doubling, silent-e drop, y-to-i, i-before-e, prefixes.
5. **Grade 6 Carry-Forward:** Subject-verb agreement? Pronoun-antecedent agreement? Nonrestrictive elements punctuated? Standard English register?

### Worked Example — Editing a Passage

Say: "Let me show you how to edit a passage using the checklist. I am going to read through the passage once for each checklist item."

Write the original passage on the board:

*"The science fair was held in the gymnasuim on Friday. The judges reviewed every project. The students waited nervously. The judges were impressed by the creative intresting displays. Although many projects were excelent the first place award went to a seventh grader who built a solar-powered water purifier, and the audience cheered when her name was anounced. The principel congradulated her in front of the entire school."*

Say: "Let me work through the checklist."

**Pass 1 — Sentence Types:**
"I count six sentences. Sentences 1-3 are all simple sentences in a row — that creates a choppy, monotonous feel. I should combine some for variety. Sentence 5 is compound-complex, which is great for the climactic moment. Sentence 6 is simple — fine for a closing statement."

**Pass 2 — Phrases and Clauses:**
"Sentence 5 has a dependent clause ('Although many projects were excellent') that comes first — it needs a comma after 'excellent.' The relative clause ('who built a solar-powered water purifier') is correctly placed. The dependent clause ('when her name was announced') is at the end, so no comma needed before it."

**Pass 3 — Coordinate Adjectives:**
"'Creative interesting displays' — reversal: 'interesting creative' — sounds fine. 'And' test: 'creative and interesting' — fine. These are coordinate adjectives. Need a comma: 'creative, interesting.'"

**Pass 4 — Spelling:**
"'Gymnasuim' → gymnasium. 'Intresting' → interesting. 'Excelent' → excellent. 'Anounced' → announced. 'Principel' → principal. 'Congradulated' → congratulated."

**Pass 5 — Grade 6 Carry-Forward:**
"Subject-verb agreement looks correct throughout. No pronoun issues. Register is appropriate."

Write the revised passage:

*"The science fair was held in the gymnasium on Friday, and the judges carefully reviewed every project while the students waited nervously. The judges were impressed by the creative, interesting displays. Although many projects were excellent, the first place award went to a seventh grader who built a solar-powered water purifier, and the audience cheered when her name was announced. The principal congratulated her in front of the entire school."*

Say: "I combined the first three choppy sentences into one compound-complex sentence, fixed the coordinate adjective comma, added the missing comma after the introductory dependent clause, and corrected all six spelling errors. The passage now reads more smoothly and professionally."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students apply the integrated editing approach with facilitator support.

Say: "Use the Grammar and Language Review Checklist to edit the following passage. Find and fix all errors, and suggest at least one revision for sentence variety."

**Passage to Edit:**

*"The seventh grade class took a feild trip to the natural history museum last Thursday. The museum was huge. The exhibits were fasinating. The students explored the dinosaur hall. They saw a complete Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton. The tall impressive fossil towered over the students and the guide explained that it was one of the best preserved specimans in the country. Although the planetarium show was the highlight of the trip everyone agreed that the hands-on geology exhibit was equally memorible. The students definately want to return next year because they beleive there is still more to discover."*

Work through the first three sentences together, modeling each checklist pass. For the remaining sentences, have students work independently, then compare with a partner.

**Expected Corrections and Revisions:**

Spelling fixes:
- "feild" → "field"
- "fasinating" → "fascinating"
- "specimans" → "specimens"
- "memorible" → "memorable"
- "definately" → "definitely"
- "beleive" → "believe"

Coordinate adjective fix:
- "tall impressive fossil" → "tall**,** impressive fossil" (reversal: "impressive, tall" — fine; "and" test: "tall and impressive" — fine; coordinate)

Comma fix:
- "Although the planetarium show was the highlight of the trip**,** everyone agreed" — dependent clause first needs a comma

Sentence variety revision (sentences 2-5 are all simple):
- Suggested revision: "The museum was huge, and the exhibits were fascinating." (Combine 2-3 into compound.) "As the students explored the dinosaur hall, they saw a complete Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton." (Combine 4-5 into complex with time clause.)

**Revised passage:**
*"The seventh-grade class took a field trip to the natural history museum last Thursday. The museum was huge, and the exhibits were fascinating. As the students explored the dinosaur hall, they saw a complete Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton. The tall, impressive fossil towered over the students, and the guide explained that it was one of the best-preserved specimens in the country. Although the planetarium show was the highlight of the trip, everyone agreed that the hands-on geology exhibit was equally memorable. The students definitely want to return next year because they believe there is still more to discover."*

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students demonstrate ability to apply all unit skills independently through editing and original composition.

**Part A — Edit the Passage (items 1-5):**
Use the Grammar and Language Review Checklist to edit the following passage. For each correction, identify which skill you are applying (sentence type, phrase/clause punctuation, coordinate adjective comma, spelling, or Grade 6 skill).

*"Last Saturday our school hosted its annual talent show in the auditorium. The performences was incredible. A group of eighth graders who had been rehersing for weeks performed a complecated dance routine. The energetic synchronized routine received a standing ovation from the audience and the judges awarded them first place. Although a seventh grader delivered a beautful powerfull spoken-word poem she recieved second place. The show was a truely unforgettable evening for everbody who attended."*

1. List all spelling errors and their corrections.
2. Identify and fix any subject-verb agreement errors.
3. Identify and fix any coordinate adjective comma issues.
4. Identify and fix any missing commas for dependent clauses.
5. Suggest one revision for sentence variety and explain your choice.

**Part B — Original Composition (items 6-8):**

6. Write a paragraph (5-6 sentences) about a school event, a game, or a class project. Your paragraph must include:
   - At least three different sentence types (label each one)
   - At least one pair of coordinate adjectives with a correct comma
   - At least one dependent clause punctuated correctly
   - No spelling errors
7. Underline one phrase in your paragraph and identify its type and function.
8. Circle one sentence and explain why you chose that sentence type for that moment in the paragraph.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Part A (items 1-5) and Part B item 6 only.
**Stretch:** Complete all of Part A and Part B.
**Challenge:** Complete all of Part A and Part B, then exchange paragraphs with a partner. Edit each other's work using the Grammar and Language Review Checklist. Write a brief note to your partner identifying one strength and one suggestion for improvement.

### Interleaved Review
9. (Grade 6 review — L.6.1c) Correct any agreement errors: "Everyone in the seventh-grade classes need to bring their permission slip to the office before Wednesday."
10. (Grade 6 review — L.6.1e) Rewrite in standard academic English: "The results of the experiment was like super different from what we thought was gonna happen."

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Edit this sentence for ALL errors (there are at least three): "Although the wether was terrable, the dedicated determined athletes competed in the outdoor track meet and they achived several personal records."
2. Name the five categories on the Grammar and Language Review Checklist.

**Expected Answers:**
1. Corrections: (a) "wether" → "weather" (spelling); (b) "terrable" → "terrible" (spelling); (c) "dedicated determined" → "dedicated**,** determined" (coordinate adjectives — reversal and "and" tests both pass); (d) "achived" → "achieved" (spelling — i before e); (e) "meet and they" → "meet**,** and they" (comma before "and" joining two independent clauses in a compound-complex sentence). Corrected: "Although the weather was terrible, the dedicated, determined athletes competed in the outdoor track meet, and they achieved several personal records."
2. (1) Sentence types/variety, (2) Phrases and clauses (punctuation and function), (3) Coordinate adjective commas, (4) Spelling, (5) Grade 6 carry-forward (agreement, nonrestrictive elements, standard register).

**Mastery = both items correct, with all errors identified and checklist categories accurately named.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the checklist one category at a time — have the student practice editing for just one error type per pass
- Provide a model paragraph that demonstrates all skills correctly and have the student annotate it, labeling each skill in action
- Focus additional practice on the student's weakest area (based on exit ticket performance)
- Allow the student to use all reference materials (flowchart, decision tree, spelling card) during editing practice

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready for the Unit Assessment
- Encourage the student to use the Grammar and Language Review Checklist when editing all future writing assignments

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson is a review and integration lesson, not a day for new content. Its purpose is to help students see how all the individual skills from Lessons 1-9 work together in authentic reading and writing contexts.
- The editing passage in Guided Practice and Independent Practice should be approached as a real-world task. In real writing, errors come in all types simultaneously — the student must shift between skills fluidly.
- Encourage students to develop a personal editing routine. Some students prefer to read through once for each checklist item (five passes). Others prefer to catch everything in one or two passes. Both approaches are valid — the goal is thoroughness, not a specific method.
- If students struggle with the integrated approach, it is an indication that they need additional practice with the underlying skills. Use exit ticket data to identify which skills need reinforcement before the unit assessment.
- This is an excellent day for peer editing. Having students edit each other's work builds both their analytical skills and their social learning. Remind students to be specific and kind in their feedback — "I noticed a comma splice in sentence 3" is more helpful than "your grammar is wrong."
- Connect to Unit 3 explicitly: if students have argumentative essays they wrote earlier in the year, this is a perfect opportunity to revisit those essays with the Grammar and Language Review Checklist. Students will likely find errors and opportunities for improvement they missed the first time — and that is the point.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice — Part A

**1. Spelling errors:**
- "performences" → "performances"
- "rehersing" → "rehearsing"
- "complecated" → "complicated"
- "beautful" → "beautiful"
- "powerfull" → "powerful"
- "recieved" → "received" (i before e, except after c)
- "truely" → "truly" (drop the e before -ly — exception)
- "everbody" → "everybody"

**2. Subject-verb agreement:**
- "The performences was incredible" → "The performances **were** incredible." ("Performances" is plural, so the verb must be "were," not "was.")

**3. Coordinate adjective comma:**
- "beautful powerfull spoken-word poem" → "beautiful**,** powerful spoken-word poem." Reversal: "powerful, beautiful" — fine. "And" test: "beautiful and powerful" — fine. Coordinate → comma. Note: "spoken-word" is a compound adjective that combines with "poem" as a unit — cumulative with the preceding adjectives.
- "energetic synchronized routine" — Reversal: "synchronized energetic" — slightly awkward. These are arguably cumulative (quality + manner). Accept either answer with reasoning.

**4. Missing comma for dependent clause:**
- "Although a seventh grader delivered a beautiful, powerful spoken-word poem**,** she received second place." Dependent clause first → comma needed.

**5. Sentence variety revision:**
Answers vary. Example: The first three sentences (simple, simple, complex) could be varied. "Last Saturday, our school hosted its annual talent show in the auditorium, and the performances were incredible." (Combines sentences 1-2 into a compound sentence.) The student should explain that combining reduces choppiness and shows a connection between the event and the quality of the performances.

### Independent Practice — Part B
**6-8.** Evaluate based on rubric criteria: minimum three sentence types correctly labeled, at least one coordinate adjective pair with comma, at least one correctly punctuated dependent clause, no spelling errors, and clear explanation of sentence type choice.

### Interleaved Review
**9.** "Everyone in the seventh-grade classes **needs** to bring **his or her** (or **their**) permission slip to the office before Wednesday." "Everyone" is singular, so the verb should be "needs" (not "need"). The pronoun "their" is widely accepted as a singular pronoun for indefinite antecedents like "everyone," but "his or her" is also correct. (L.6.1c)

**10.** "The results of the experiment were significantly different from what we had hypothesized." OR "The results of the experiment differed substantially from our hypothesis." The original has several nonstandard features: "was" should be "were" (subject is "results," plural); "like super different" is informal slang; "gonna" is a spoken contraction of "going to" not used in academic writing. (L.6.1e)
