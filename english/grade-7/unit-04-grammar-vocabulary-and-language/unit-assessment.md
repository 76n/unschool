## Section 1: Phrases and Clauses (L.7.1a) — 15 points

### Part A: Identification (6 points — 1 point each)

**Directions:** For each sentence, identify the underlined element as one of the following: prepositional phrase, appositive phrase, participial phrase, infinitive phrase, independent clause, or dependent clause.

1. *Determined to qualify for the state championship*, the relay team practiced twice a day for three weeks.

2. The mural on the east wall of the gymnasium, *a tribute to the school's founding families*, was painted by the advanced art class.

4. The community garden *behind the middle school* provides fresh vegetables to the local food bank.

5. The students gathered in the library *to prepare their group presentations for Friday's symposium*.

6. *Cheering from the bleachers*, the fans urged the basketball team through the final minutes of the game.

### Part B: Function (6 points — 2 points each)

**Directions:** For each sentence, identify the function of the underlined phrase or clause. State whether it functions as an adjective, an adverb, or a noun, and explain what it modifies, renames, or replaces.

## Answer Key

### Section 1: Phrases and Clauses (L.7.1a) — 15 points

**Part A (6 points):**
1. **Participial phrase** — "Determined to qualify for the state championship" begins with the past participle "determined" and describes "the relay team."
2. **Appositive phrase** — "a tribute to the school's founding families" renames "the mural on the east wall of the gymnasium."
3. **Dependent clause** — "Because the assembly ran longer than expected" contains a subject ("assembly") and verb ("ran") but begins with the subordinating conjunction "because," making it dependent.
4. **Prepositional phrase** — "behind the middle school" begins with the preposition "behind" and ends with the noun "school."
5. **Infinitive phrase** — "to prepare their group presentations for Friday's symposium" begins with "to" + verb "prepare."
6. **Participial phrase** — "Cheering from the bleachers" begins with the present participle "cheering" and describes "the fans."

**Part B (6 points):**
7. "That we watched during science class" is a dependent (relative) clause functioning as an **adjective** — it modifies "the documentary," identifying which documentary. (Restrictive — no commas.) (2 points: 1 for function, 1 for explanation)
8. "To earn the community service award" is an infinitive phrase functioning as an **adverb** — it modifies "must complete," explaining the purpose or reason. (2 points)
9. "Whoever submits the most creative design" is a dependent (noun) clause functioning as a **noun** — it serves as the subject of the sentence. (What will have their artwork displayed? Whoever submits the most creative design.) (2 points)

**Part C (3 points):**
10. Explanation (1 point): A phrase is a group of related words that does not contain a subject and a verb working together. A clause is a group of words that does contain a subject and a verb. A dependent clause has a subject and verb but cannot stand alone as a sentence.
Original sentence (1 point): Answers vary. Example: "After the game ended, the fans celebrated in the parking lot."
Correct labels (1 point): "After the game ended" = dependent clause; "in the parking lot" = prepositional phrase.

### Section 2: Sentence Types (L.7.1b) — 15 points

**Part A (5 points):**
11. **CX (Complex)** — One independent clause ("the outdoor track meet continued as scheduled") + one dependent clause ("Although the temperature dropped below freezing overnight").
12. **S (Simple)** — One subject ("student council president") with a compound verb ("organized and promoted"). One independent clause.
13. **CD (Compound)** — Two independent clauses ("The orchestra performed a challenging piece at the spring concert" + "the audience responded with a standing ovation") joined by ", and."
14. **CC (Compound-Complex)** — Two independent clauses ("the auditorium erupted in applause" + "several teachers wiped tears from their eyes") + one dependent clause ("When the principal announced the winners of the essay contest").
15. **S (Simple)** — Compound subject ("My lab partner and I") with compound verb ("measured, recorded, and submitted"). One independent clause.

**Part B (5 points):**
16. (2.5 points) "**Because** the drama teacher spent months preparing the cast**,** the school play sold out every performance." OR "The school play sold out every performance **because** the drama teacher spent months preparing the cast." (Complex sentence with subordinating conjunction showing cause-effect. Comma required if dependent clause comes first. Deduct 0.5 for missing comma or incorrect conjunction. Deduct 1 if sentence is not complex.)

17. (2.5 points) "**When** the forecast predicted thunderstorms**,** the field day organizers moved all activities indoors**,** **but** the students still had an incredible time." OR "**Although** the forecast predicted thunderstorms **and** the field day organizers moved all activities indoors**,** the students still had an incredible time." (Compound-complex with at least two independent clauses and at least one dependent clause. Must show both cause and contrast. Deduct 0.5 for punctuation errors. Deduct 1 if not compound-complex.)

**Part C (5 points):**
18. (2 points) Sentence (D) — "Silence fell." — is a **simple sentence**. It is effective here because its brevity creates dramatic emphasis. After the longer compound and complex sentences before it, this short sentence stops the reader, mirrors the sudden silence it describes, and builds anticipation for what comes next. (1 point for correct identification, 1 point for explanation of effect.)

19. (3 points) Sentence (E) is a **compound-complex sentence**. It contains one dependent clause ("When the first notes filled the gymnasium") and two independent clauses ("the audience leaned forward in their seats" + "several parents reached for their phones to record the moment") joined by ", and." The author chose this structure to capture the moment of the first notes and its two simultaneous effects in a single flowing sentence. Breaking it apart would lose the sense of everything happening at once — the music, the audience leaning forward, and the parents reaching for phones are all part of one unified moment. (1 point for correct identification, 1 point for clause breakdown, 1 point for explanation of why this structure serves the moment.)

### Section 3: Coordinate Adjectives (L.7.2a) — 10 points

**Part A (6 points):**
20. **Coordinate** → "The exhausted**,** triumphant runners." Reversal: "triumphant, exhausted" — sounds natural. "And" test: "exhausted and triumphant" — sounds natural. Both are quality/condition adjectives.
21. **Cumulative** → no comma. "A sleek new smartphone." Reversal: "new sleek smartphone" — awkward. "Sleek" is a quality adjective; "new" is an age adjective — different categories.
22. **Coordinate** → "The thoughtful**,** well-organized presentation." Reversal: "well-organized, thoughtful" — sounds natural. "And" test: "thoughtful and well-organized" — sounds natural.
23. **Cumulative** → no comma. "Antique wooden rocking chair." Reversal: "wooden antique" — awkward. "Antique" is age; "wooden" is material — different categories in the natural adjective order.
24. **Coordinate** → "A vast**,** mysterious cave system." Reversal: "mysterious, vast" — sounds natural. "And" test: "vast and mysterious" — sounds natural. Both are descriptive quality adjectives.
25. **Cumulative** → no comma. "Thirty identical blue laboratory coats." Reversal fails between any pair: "identical thirty" — wrong; "blue identical" — awkward. Number + quality + color + purpose — all different categories.

**Part B (4 points):**
26. (2 points) Answers vary. Example: "The clever, resourceful detective examined the clues." Test: Reversal — "resourceful, clever detective" — sounds fine. "And" test — "clever and resourceful detective" — sounds fine. Both pass → coordinate → comma. (1 point for correct sentence with comma, 1 point for showing the test.)
27. (2 points) Answers vary. Example: "She wore a beautiful silk scarf to the ceremony." Explanation: "Beautiful" is an opinion adjective and "silk" is a material adjective — they are from different categories in the adjective order. Reversal test: "silk beautiful scarf" — sounds wrong. Therefore, they are cumulative, and no comma is needed. (1 point for correct sentence without comma, 1 point for explanation.)

### Section 4: Spelling (L.7.2b) — 10 points

**Part A (5 points):**
28. **occurrence** — Double the r (stress on final syllable: oc-CUR; one vowel + one consonant → double), then add -ence.
29. **noticeable** — Keep the e before -able so the c stays soft (/s/ sound). Exception to the drop-silent-e rule.
30. **happily** — Change y to i before -ly (consonant + y → change y to i).
31. **unnecessary** — Add prefix un- without changing the base word. The prefix ends in n and the base word starts with n → double n.
32. **hoping** — Drop the silent e before -ing (suffix starts with a vowel).

**Part B (5 points):**
33. "committe" → **committee** (double m, double t, double e at the end)
34. "truely" → **truly** (drop the e from "true" before -ly)
35. "enviroment" → **environment** (the n before m: environ-ment)
36. "atheletes" → **athletes** (ath-letes — no extra syllable in the middle)
37. "beleives" → **believes** (i before e in "believe": b-e-l-i-e-v-e-s)

### Section 5: Applied Grammar in Context (All Standards) — 10 points

**Part A (6 points — 1 point each):**

The six errors and their corrections:

1. **"exiting" → "exciting"** — Spelling error (L.7.2b). The correct spelling is "exciting" (excite + -ing, drop the silent e).

2. **"impressive creative projects" — missing comma** → "impressive**,** creative projects" — Coordinate adjective comma (L.7.2a). Reversal: "creative, impressive" — fine. "And" test: "impressive and creative" — fine. Coordinate → comma needed.

3. **Missing comma after introductory dependent clause** → "Although many students entered impressive, creative projects**,** the judges had a difficult time choosing a winner." — Phrase/clause punctuation (L.7.1a/L.7.1b). Dependent clause beginning with "although" comes first → comma needed.

4. **"A eighth grader" → "An eighth grader"** — Grammar/article error. "Eighth" begins with a vowel sound → "an."
Note: If students identify this as "not one of the unit standards," accept an alternative sixth error. The passage also contains: **"a innovative affordable" → "an innovative, affordable"** — combines article correction and coordinate adjective comma.

5. **"innovative affordable" — missing comma** → "innovative**,** affordable" — Coordinate adjective comma (L.7.2a). Reversal: "affordable, innovative" — fine. Coordinate → comma needed.

6. **"principel" → "principal"** — Spelling error (L.7.2b). The correct spelling is "principal" (the head of a school).

Note for scoring: Accept any six legitimate errors. The passage is designed to include the following error types: 2 spelling errors, 2 coordinate adjective comma errors, 1 dependent clause punctuation error, and 1 article error. If a student identifies only five of the six, award proportional credit.

**Part B (4 points):**

Answers vary. A strong response will include at least three different sentence types, maintain all original information, and create smooth, effective flow.

Example revision:
"(CX) After the debate team traveled to the state capitol, they competed against fourteen other schools in a grueling tournament. (CD) The first two rounds went smoothly, but the semifinal round was intense. (CC) Although the final round was the closest match of the day, our team won by a narrow margin, and the victory felt even sweeter because of how hard-fought it was. (S) Everyone celebrated on the bus ride home."

Scoring rubric:
- Uses at least three different sentence types: 1 point
- Labels all sentence types correctly: 1 point
- Maintains all original information: 1 point
- Writing flows smoothly and sentence types serve the content effectively: 1 point

## Accommodations

- **Extended time:** Students with documented needs may receive up to 1.5x the standard time.
- **Reference materials:** Below-level students may use the Spelling Rules Reference Card. All students should work without the Sentence Type Flowchart or Coordinate Adjective Decision Tree, as these are the skills being assessed.
- **Oral response option:** For Part C of Section 1 and Part B of Section 5, students who struggle with written production may dictate their responses to the facilitator.
- **Reduced items:** For students significantly below level, the facilitator may select a representative subset of items from each section while maintaining the section point ratios.
