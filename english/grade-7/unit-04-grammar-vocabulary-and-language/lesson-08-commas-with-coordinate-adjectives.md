# English Language Arts — Grade 7 — Unit 4, Lesson 8

## Topic
Commas with Coordinate Adjectives

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Distinguish between coordinate adjectives and cumulative adjectives
- Apply the two-part test (reversal test and "and" test) to determine whether adjectives are coordinate
- Correctly use commas to separate coordinate adjectives and omit commas between cumulative adjectives

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of adjectives as words that describe nouns (Grade 5-6)
- Ability to use commas correctly in compound sentences and after introductory clauses (Lessons 4-6)
- Familiarity with nonrestrictive element punctuation from Grade 6 (L.6.2a)
- Experience with descriptive writing using multiple adjectives (Grade 5-6)

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector)
- Printed or projected example sentences
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Coordinate Adjective Decision Tree (visual handout or poster)
- Index cards for adjective-sorting activity
- Colored pencils (red for commas)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Reveal that students already have intuition about adjective order and set up the need for a formal rule.

**Activity: Which Sounds Right?**

Say: "Read each pair of descriptions aloud. Which one sounds natural? Write A or B in your notebook."

Write on the board:
1. (A) a bright, cheerful classroom  (B) a cheerful, bright classroom
2. (A) a green old sweater  (B) an old green sweater
3. (A) an exciting, fast-paced movie  (B) a fast-paced, exciting movie
4. (A) her three new library books  (B) her new three library books

After two minutes, discuss.

Say: "For items 1 and 3, both orders sound fine — you could say it either way. For items 2 and 4, only one order sounds natural — 'an old green sweater' and 'her three new library books.' This is not random. English has a hidden rule about adjective order, and it tells us something important about commas. When you can reverse the adjectives and it still sounds right, you need a comma between them. When you cannot reverse them, you do not. Today we learn why and how."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the distinction between coordinate and cumulative adjectives and the two-part test for determining which type you have.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Coordinate adjectives:** Two or more adjectives that independently and equally modify the same noun; they can be reversed or connected with "and" without changing the meaning
- **Cumulative adjectives:** Two or more adjectives that build upon each other in a specific order; the adjective closer to the noun combines with the noun to form a unit that the outer adjective then modifies
- **Reversal test:** Switching the order of the adjectives to see if the sentence still sounds natural
- **"And" test:** Inserting "and" between the adjectives to see if the sentence still sounds natural

### Instruction Notes

Say: "When two or more adjectives appear before a noun, you need to decide: are they coordinate or cumulative? This decision tells you whether to use a comma."

Write on the board:
- **Coordinate:** The adjectives independently describe the noun. Each one could stand alone with the noun. → USE A COMMA.
- **Cumulative:** The adjectives build in a specific order. The inner adjective combines with the noun, and the outer adjective modifies that combination. → NO COMMA.

**The Two-Part Decision Tree:**

Say: "Here is your decision tree. Ask two questions."

Write on the board:

**Question 1: Can you reverse the adjectives and have the sentence still sound natural?**
**Question 2: Can you put "and" between the adjectives and have the sentence still sound natural?**

If YES to BOTH → coordinate adjectives → USE A COMMA.
If NO to EITHER → cumulative adjectives → NO COMMA.

**Coordinate Adjective Examples:**

Write on the board:
- "The **talented, dedicated** musician practiced every day."
  - Reversal test: "The dedicated, talented musician" — sounds fine. PASS.
  - "And" test: "The talented and dedicated musician" — sounds fine. PASS.
  - Both pass → coordinate → comma.

- "It was a **long, exhausting** rehearsal."
  - Reversal: "It was an exhausting, long rehearsal." — sounds fine. PASS.
  - "And" test: "It was a long and exhausting rehearsal." — sounds fine. PASS.
  - Both pass → coordinate → comma.

**Cumulative Adjective Examples:**

Write on the board:
- "She wore a **bright red** jacket."
  - Reversal test: "She wore a red bright jacket." — sounds wrong. FAIL.
  - No need to check the second question. Cumulative → no comma.

- "He carried **three heavy** textbooks."
  - Reversal: "He carried heavy three textbooks." — sounds wrong. FAIL.
  - Cumulative → no comma.

- "They visited the **old brick** building."
  - Reversal: "They visited the brick old building." — sounds wrong. FAIL.
  - Cumulative → no comma.

**Why Does This Happen?**

Say: "In English, adjectives follow a natural order: opinion → size → age → shape → color → origin → material → purpose. When adjectives are from the same category (two opinion words, two descriptive qualities), they are coordinate — equal partners. When they are from different categories in the natural order, they are cumulative — they stack up in a specific sequence."

Write on the board:
- "A **beautiful, spacious** apartment" — both are opinion/quality adjectives → coordinate → comma.
- "A **beautiful old** apartment" — opinion (beautiful) + age (old) → cumulative → no comma.
- "A **small wooden** box" — size (small) + material (wooden) → cumulative → no comma.
- "A **sturdy, reliable** backpack" — both are quality adjectives → coordinate → comma.

Say: "You do not need to memorize the entire adjective order list. Just use the two-part test — it works every time."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:**
Write: "The energetic young puppy chased its tail around the living room."

Say: "Let me apply the test. 'Energetic young puppy.' Reversal: 'The young energetic puppy.' Hmm — that sounds slightly awkward. 'Energetic' is a quality/opinion adjective, and 'young' is an age adjective — different categories. 'And' test: 'The energetic and young puppy.' That also sounds a bit forced. These are cumulative adjectives. No comma."

**Example 2:**
Write: "The curious enthusiastic students crowded around the science demonstration."

Say: "'Curious enthusiastic students.' Reversal: 'The enthusiastic curious students.' That sounds perfectly fine. 'And' test: 'The curious and enthusiastic students.' Also fine. Both are personality/quality adjectives — same category. These are coordinate. I need a comma: 'The curious, enthusiastic students.'"

**Example 3:**
Write: "My grandmother baked a delicious warm apple pie."

Say: "There are three adjectives here. Let me test them in pairs. 'Delicious warm' — reversal: 'warm delicious.' Sounds okay. 'And' test: 'delicious and warm.' Sounds fine. Coordinate → comma between 'delicious' and 'warm.' Now test 'warm apple' — reversal: 'apple warm pie.' Sounds wrong. Cumulative → no comma. Result: 'My grandmother baked a delicious, warm apple pie.'"

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students apply the two-part test with facilitator support and build confidence in their comma decisions.

Say: "For each sentence, apply the reversal test and the 'and' test to the underlined adjectives. Then decide: comma or no comma? Write the sentence correctly."

1. The tall graceful dancer performed a solo during the recital.
2. He wore his favorite blue basketball jersey to the game.
3. The patient experienced tutor helped me understand the math problem.
4. She packed a small rectangular lunch container in her backpack.
5. The inspiring powerful speech moved the audience to tears.
6. We explored the ancient stone ruins during our field trip.
7. The enthusiastic supportive fans cheered through every quarter of the game.
8. My older brother drives a beat-up white pickup truck.

Work through items 1-3 together, performing both tests aloud for each one. For items 4-8, have students work independently, then compare with a partner.

**Expected Answers:**
1. "The tall**,** graceful dancer" — Reversal: "graceful, tall" — fine. "And" test: "tall and graceful" — fine. COORDINATE → comma.
2. "His favorite blue basketball jersey" — Reversal: "blue favorite basketball jersey" — wrong. CUMULATIVE → no comma.
3. "The patient**,** experienced tutor" — Reversal: "experienced, patient" — fine. "And" test: "patient and experienced" — fine. COORDINATE → comma.
4. "A small rectangular lunch container" — Reversal: "rectangular small" — wrong. CUMULATIVE → no comma.
5. "The inspiring**,** powerful speech" — Reversal: "powerful, inspiring" — fine. "And" test: "inspiring and powerful" — fine. COORDINATE → comma.
6. "The ancient stone ruins" — Reversal: "stone ancient" — wrong. CUMULATIVE → no comma.
7. "The enthusiastic**,** supportive fans" — Reversal: "supportive, enthusiastic" — fine. "And" test: "enthusiastic and supportive" — fine. COORDINATE → comma.
8. "A beat-up white pickup truck" — Reversal: "white beat-up" — awkward. CUMULATIVE → no comma.

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students independently determine whether adjectives are coordinate or cumulative and punctuate accordingly.

**Directions:** For items 1-8, decide whether the adjectives are coordinate or cumulative. Add a comma if needed. If no comma is needed, write "no comma." For items 9-10, follow the specific directions.

1. The dedicated hardworking students earned the highest grades in the class.
2. She adopted a playful little orange kitten from the shelter.
3. We ate lunch at a quiet cozy restaurant near the museum.
4. The old wooden fence needed to be repaired before winter.
5. His thoughtful well-researched essay won the writing contest.
6. They hiked through a dense dark forest on the way to the campsite.
7. The new Italian leather shoes were a birthday gift from his grandmother.
8. We watched a suspenseful gripping documentary about ocean exploration.
9. Write two original sentences: one with coordinate adjectives (comma needed) and one with cumulative adjectives (no comma needed). Apply the two-part test and show your work.
10. Find and correct the comma error in this sentence: "The exhausted, young soccer players dragged themselves off the field after the overtime match."

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-8.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-10.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-10, then write a descriptive paragraph (4-5 sentences) about a place you enjoy visiting. Include at least two sentences with coordinate adjectives and two with cumulative adjectives. Mark each pair of adjectives as coordinate (C) or cumulative (CU).

### Interleaved Review
11. (Grade 6 review — L.6.1c) Choose the correct verb: "Neither the coach nor the players (seem / seems) worried about the upcoming match."
12. (Grade 6 review — L.6.3a) Rewrite this passage using a mix of sentence types: "The cafeteria was loud. The food was mediocre. Students complained about the menu. Nothing changed."

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Apply the two-part test: are the adjectives coordinate or cumulative? "The bright colorful mural covered the entire wall." Add a comma if needed.
2. Explain why no comma is needed: "She bought a beautiful silk scarf."
3. What are the two questions in the coordinate adjective test?

**Expected Answers:**
1. Reversal: "colorful bright mural" — sounds fine. "And" test: "bright and colorful mural" — sounds fine. **Coordinate** → "The bright**,** colorful mural covered the entire wall."
2. Reversal: "silk beautiful scarf" — sounds wrong. "Beautiful" is an opinion adjective and "silk" is a material adjective — different categories. These are **cumulative adjectives**, so no comma is needed.
3. (1) Can you reverse the order of the adjectives and have the sentence still sound natural? (2) Can you put "and" between the adjectives and have the sentence still sound natural? If YES to both, use a comma. If NO to either, no comma.

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 items correct, with correct comma usage and accurate application of the test.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice the two tests with very clear examples first — coordinate: "a kind, generous person" (both personality traits); cumulative: "a large red barn" (size + color)
- Have the student say each reversed version aloud — the "ear test" is reliable for most speakers
- Provide the adjective order chart (opinion → size → age → shape → color → origin → material → purpose) as a visual reference
- Focus on the most common coordinate pairs (two opinion/quality words) and the most common cumulative patterns (size + color, age + material) before introducing trickier cases

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready to proceed to Lesson 9, which covers spelling rules and patterns
- Encourage the student to apply the coordinate adjective comma rule in all future writing

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Adjective Order Experiment:** Write the following adjectives in the correct English order before each noun: (a) wooden / large / old → ___ ___ ___ table; (b) silk / elegant / blue → ___ ___ ___ dress; (c) round / tiny / silver → ___ ___ ___ pendant. Explain which pairs would need commas.
2. **Published Sentence Hunt:** Find five sentences in a book or article that use two or more adjectives before a noun. Determine whether each pair is coordinate or cumulative. Were they punctuated correctly?
3. **Descriptive Writing Challenge:** Write a paragraph describing your ideal room. Use at least three pairs of coordinate adjectives (with commas) and three pairs of cumulative adjectives (without commas). Underline and label each pair.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The two-part test (reversal + "and") is the most practical tool for students and should be the primary focus. The adjective order chart is helpful for understanding WHY the tests work, but most students will do better relying on the tests themselves rather than memorizing the order.
- Some students may be confused by cases where the reversal sounds "okay but not great." When in doubt, the "and" test is usually more decisive. If "and" sounds natural between the adjectives, they are coordinate.
- Be aware that some adjective pairs fall in a gray area — reasonable people might disagree about whether they are coordinate or cumulative. In these cases, validate the student's reasoning rather than insisting on a single correct answer. The goal is the ability to apply the test and make a defensible decision.
- The standard example from L.7.2a is helpful to share: "It was a fascinating, enjoyable movie" (coordinate — both are opinion adjectives) versus "He wore an old green shirt" (cumulative — age + color). This exact contrast appears in the standard itself.
- Connect to descriptive writing: when students use vivid adjectives in their narratives and descriptions, proper comma usage prevents confusion and makes the writing cleaner. A missing comma between coordinate adjectives can momentarily trip up a reader.
