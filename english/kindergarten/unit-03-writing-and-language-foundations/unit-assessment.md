# Unit Assessment — Kindergarten ELA, Unit 3: Writing & Language Foundations

**Grade:** Kindergarten
**Unit:** 3 — Writing & Language Foundations
**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** One-on-one with facilitator (oral responses, writing samples, and drawing — not a traditional written test)
**Estimated Duration:** 25-30 minutes
**Materials:** Blank paper (4 sheets), pencil, crayons, sentence frame strips (optional support), noun/verb word cards (8 cards: 4 nouns, 4 verbs)

---

## Assessment Overview

This assessment covers all Unit 3 learning objectives:

| Section | Skill Assessed | Lesson Source | Items |
|---------|---------------|---------------|-------|
| A | Drawing and dictating a narrative | Lesson 1 | 3 |
| B | Writing conventions (capital, spaces, period) | Lesson 2 | 5 |
| C | Nouns and verbs | Lesson 3 | 6 |
| D | Opinion writing | Lesson 4 | 4 |
| E | Informative writing | Lesson 5 | 4 |
| F | Identifying writing types | Lesson 6 | 3 |
| -- | **Total** | -- | **25** |

**Mastery threshold:** 85% overall (21 of 25 points) AND at least 1 correct item in each section.

---

## Administration Instructions

This assessment combines oral responses, hands-on tasks, and writing samples. Read each instruction aloud. Give the student time to think, draw, and write. There are no strict time limits on individual items — let the student work at their own pace.

**Important guidelines:**
- Accept invented spelling throughout. Score writing based on ideas and conventions (capital, spaces, period), NOT on correct spelling.
- For writing tasks, the student may dictate if they cannot write independently — but note this on the scoring sheet. Independent writing earns full credit; dictated responses also earn full credit for content but should be noted for planning purposes.
- Do NOT correct errors during the assessment. If the student asks for help spelling a word, say: "Just write the sounds you hear. You are doing great."
- For the noun/verb section, prepare word cards in advance: **dog, bed, sun, hat** (nouns) and **run, hop, sit, dig** (verbs).

Create a comfortable atmosphere. Say: "Today we are going to do some writing and talking. You have learned so much about writing in this unit! Just show me what you can do. There are no wrong answers — I just want to see how you think."

## Section A: Drawing and Dictating a Narrative (Lesson 1)

Give the student a blank sheet of paper. Say: "Draw a picture that tells a story — something that happened to you. It can be about anything. Then tell me your story so I can write it down, OR you can write the words yourself."

Allow 5-6 minutes for drawing and composing.

**A1. Drawing:** Does the student's drawing depict a recognizable event or scene?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Drawing shows identifiable content related to a story (people, animals, places, actions) | 1 point |
| Drawing is random marks with no identifiable content, or student refuses to draw | 0 points |

**A2. Narrative content:** Does the student produce (through dictation or writing) at least 2 sentences that tell a story?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student produces 2 or more sentences that describe something that happened (events, actions) | 1 point |
| Student produces fewer than 2 sentences or sentences do not describe events | 0 points |

**A3. Narrative sequence:** Does the story show any sense of order (beginning and end, first and then, cause and effect)?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Story has recognizable sequence — events are in an order that makes sense (e.g., "I went to the park. I played on the slide.") | 1 point |
| Events are listed randomly with no connection or order, or only 1 event is mentioned | 0 points |

## Section B: Writing Conventions (Lesson 2)

**B1.** Write this sentence incorrectly on paper: **the dog is big**

Say: "Something is wrong with this sentence. Can you find what is wrong and tell me how to fix it?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student identifies the missing capital letter (should be "The") | 1 point |
| Student does not identify the error | 0 points |

**B2.** Write this sentence incorrectly on paper: **The cat is fat**

Say: "Something is missing at the end of this sentence. What is it?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student identifies the missing period | 1 point |
| Student does not identify the error | 0 points |

**B3.** Write this on paper: **Mydog can run.**

Say: "Something is wrong here. Can you find it?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student identifies the missing space between "My" and "dog" | 1 point |
| Student does not identify the error | 0 points |

**B4.** Say: "Tell me the three rules for writing a sentence."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student names all three: capital letter at the beginning, spaces between words, period at the end (in any order, using their own words) | 1 point |
| Student names only 1 or 2 rules, or cannot state any | 0 points |

**B5.** Say: "Write this sentence for me: 'I like my cat.'" (Dictate the sentence clearly. The student writes it independently.)

Score the student's written sentence:

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Sentence includes a capital letter at the beginning, spaces between all words, and a period at the end (spelling does not need to be perfect — "I LIK MI KAT." earns full credit if conventions are correct) | 1 point |
| Missing one or more conventions (no capital, missing spaces, no period) | 0 points |

## Section C: Nouns and Verbs (Lesson 3)

Prepare 8 word cards: **dog, bed, sun, hat, run, hop, sit, dig**. Shuffle them.

**C1.** Say: "What is a noun?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student explains that a noun is a naming word — it names a person, place, animal, or thing (accept student's own wording, e.g., "a word for a thing") | 1 point |
| Student cannot explain or gives an incorrect definition | 0 points |

**C2.** Say: "What is a verb?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student explains that a verb is an action word — it tells what someone or something does (accept student's own wording, e.g., "a word for something you do") | 1 point |
| Student cannot explain or gives an incorrect definition | 0 points |

**C3-C6.** Present 4 word cards one at a time (2 nouns and 2 verbs — choose any 4 from the set). For each card, the student reads the word and says whether it is a noun or a verb.

**C3.** Card: _____ (noun or verb)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student correctly identifies the word as a noun or verb | 1 point |
| Incorrect | 0 points |

**C4.** Card: _____ (noun or verb)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correct identification | 1 point |
| Incorrect | 0 points |

**C5.** Card: _____ (noun or verb)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correct identification | 1 point |
| Incorrect | 0 points |

**C6.** Card: _____ (noun or verb)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correct identification | 1 point |
| Incorrect | 0 points |

## Section D: Opinion Writing (Lesson 4)

Give the student a blank sheet of paper. Say: "Write your opinion about something you like. Draw a picture at the top and write your opinion at the bottom. Tell me what you like and WHY you like it."

You may display the sentence frame **I like ___ because ___.** as optional support. Allow 4-5 minutes.

**D1. Opinion stated:** Does the student state a clear opinion (what they like)?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student names something they like or prefer (in writing or dictation) | 1 point |
| Student does not state a preference | 0 points |

**D2. Reason given:** Does the student provide a reason for their opinion?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student gives a reason (a "because" — even a simple one like "because it is fun" or "because I just do") | 1 point |
| No reason is given | 0 points |

**D3. Drawing matches writing:** Does the drawing relate to the opinion?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Drawing clearly relates to the written or dictated opinion | 1 point |
| Drawing is unrelated or absent | 0 points |

**D4. Fact vs. opinion distinction:** Say: "Is this a fact or an opinion: 'Ice cream is the best food'?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student correctly identifies it as an opinion | 1 point |
| Student says it is a fact or does not know | 0 points |

## Section E: Informative Writing (Lesson 5)

Give the student a blank sheet of paper. Say: "Pick something you know a lot about — an animal, a food, a toy, anything. Draw a picture and write facts about it. Teach me something true about your topic."

You may display the sentence frames **A ___ has/can/is ___.** as optional support. Allow 4-5 minutes.

**E1. Topic named:** Does the student name a clear topic?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student identifies what they are writing about (in writing, dictation, or drawing) | 1 point |
| No clear topic | 0 points |

**E2. Fact provided:** Does the student provide at least one fact about the topic (not an opinion)?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student provides at least 1 true statement about the topic (e.g., "A dog has four legs," "A fish can swim") | 1 point |
| Student provides only opinions or no factual content | 0 points |

**E3. Second fact or detail:** Does the student provide a second piece of factual information?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student provides a second fact or detail about the topic | 1 point |
| Only one fact provided or no additional detail | 0 points |

**E4. Fact vs. opinion in context:** Point to the student's writing and ask: "Are these facts or opinions?" Then ask: "How do you know they are facts?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student correctly identifies their statements as facts and gives a reason (e.g., "Everyone would agree," "It is true," "You can see it") | 1 point |
| Student cannot distinguish or explains incorrectly | 0 points |

## Section F: Identifying Writing Types (Lesson 6)

Read each sentence aloud. The student identifies the writing type: **Story (Narrative)**, **Opinion**, or **Informative (Facts)**.

**F1.** "I went to my grandma's house and we baked a cake."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student identifies this as a story/narrative (it tells what happened) | 1 point |
| Incorrect | 0 points |

**F2.** "I like pizza because the cheese is so good."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student identifies this as an opinion (it says what someone likes and why) | 1 point |
| Incorrect | 0 points |

**F3.** "A rabbit has long ears and a short tail."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Student identifies this as informative/facts (it teaches something true about a rabbit) | 1 point |
| Incorrect | 0 points |

## Rubric and Mastery Determination

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Next Steps |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Mastered** | 21-25 (85-100%) with at least 1 correct in each section | Student has solid writing and language foundations: composes narratives, opinions, and informative texts; uses sentence conventions; identifies nouns and verbs | Advance to Unit 4. Continue daily writing practice. Encourage the student to write for real purposes (letters, lists, labels, stories). |
| **Approaching** | 18-20 (70-84%) | Partial mastery — most skills are developing but some areas need reinforcement | Identify weak sections and reteach those specific skills (1-2 sessions each). The student may begin Unit 4 while continuing to practice weak areas from Unit 3. |
| **Developing** | Below 18 (<70%) | Significant gaps in one or more foundational areas | Return to the weakest section and reteach from that lesson forward. Do not advance to Unit 4 until at least "Approaching" level is reached. |

## Section-Specific Reteaching Guide

| If the student struggles in... | What it tells you | Reteach with... |
|-------------------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Section A (Narrative)** | The student has difficulty composing a story with events in sequence | Return to Lesson 1. Practice oral storytelling daily: "Tell me something that happened today — beginning, middle, end." Use three-finger prompts (first, then, last). Draw stories together and narrate them before writing. If the student can tell stories orally but not in writing, the issue is transcription — continue with dictation while building writing stamina. |
| **Section B (Conventions)** | The student has not internalized sentence writing rules (capital, spaces, period) | Return to Lesson 2. Use the three-rule checklist (capital? spaces? period?) after every sentence. Practice "Fix the Sentence" games daily — write broken sentences for the student to repair. Use the spacer tool consistently. Write one sentence per day as routine practice. Color-code capitals and periods to make them visually prominent. |
| **Section C (Nouns & Verbs)** | The student cannot distinguish naming words from action words | Return to Lesson 3. Use physical activities: hold up objects for nouns, act out movements for verbs. Sort real objects and actions, not just word cards. Play "Noun or Verb?" throughout the day. Return to the simplest examples (cat/dog vs. run/jump) before introducing less obvious words. |
| **Section D (Opinion Writing)** | The student cannot state an opinion with a reason, or cannot distinguish opinions from facts | Return to Lesson 4. Practice "This or That?" choices daily with "Why?" follow-ups. Keep the sentence frame posted and practice filling it in orally before writing. Play "Fact or Opinion?" games with simple statements. If the student can state opinions orally but not in writing, the bottleneck is writing mechanics — address Section B skills first. |
| **Section E (Informative Writing)** | The student cannot produce factual statements about a topic, or confuses facts with opinions | Return to Lesson 5. Practice the "everyone agrees" test for facts vs. opinions. Use the sentence frames (A ___ has/can/is ___) heavily. Start with very concrete, visible topics (look at a picture of an animal and describe what you see). Read simple non-fiction books together and identify facts on each page. |
| **Section F (Writing Types)** | The student cannot identify the three writing types when given examples | Return to Lesson 6. Use the three label cards as a sorting activity — read sentences aloud and have the student sort them. Focus on the purpose of each type: "A story tells what happened. An opinion tells what you like. Facts teach something true." Practice with multiple examples before re-assessing. |

## Detailed Rubric by Skill

### Narrative Composition (Section A)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Story content | Draws a detailed picture and produces 3+ sentences that tell a coherent story with beginning, middle, and end | Draws a picture and produces 2 sentences with related content, but story may lack clear sequence | Draws a picture but produces only 1 sentence or no sentences; story content is minimal |
| Use of drawing and writing | Drawing and writing work together — the picture supports and extends the written text | Drawing is present but loosely connected to the writing | Drawing and writing are unrelated, or one is absent |

### Writing Conventions (Section B)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Capital letters | Consistently begins sentences with a capital letter without reminders | Usually remembers capitals but occasionally forgets; self-corrects when prompted | Rarely uses capital letters at the beginning of sentences |
| Spacing | Leaves clear spaces between all words; writing is readable | Leaves spaces between most words but occasionally runs words together | Frequently runs words together; spacing is inconsistent |
| End punctuation | Consistently places a period at the end of sentences | Sometimes remembers the period; may need a reminder | Rarely uses end punctuation |

### Nouns and Verbs (Section C)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Definitions | Gives clear definitions of noun and verb in own words | Gives a partial definition (e.g., "a noun is a thing" without mentioning people, places, or animals) | Cannot define noun or verb |
| Sorting | Correctly sorts all 4 words | Correctly sorts 2-3 of 4 words | Sorts 0-1 words correctly; appears to be guessing |

### Opinion Writing (Section D)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Opinion + reason | States a clear opinion and provides a specific reason | States an opinion but reason is vague or missing ("because I like it") | Cannot state a clear opinion or gives no reason |
| Fact vs. opinion | Consistently distinguishes facts from opinions and can explain why | Can identify some facts and opinions but is not consistent | Cannot distinguish facts from opinions |

### Informative Writing (Section E)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Factual content | Provides 2+ facts about a clear topic; facts are accurate and relevant | Provides 1 fact about a topic; may include an opinion mixed with facts | Cannot provide factual statements; writing is all opinion or off-topic |
| Topic focus | Writing stays on topic throughout | Writing is mostly on topic with some wandering | No clear topic; writing jumps between unrelated ideas |

## Cumulative Review Items

These are NOT scored but provide diagnostic information for planning Unit 4:

**R1.** After all writing is complete, ask: "Which kind of writing was your favorite — the story, the opinion, or the facts?" (Assesses the student's writing identity and preference.)

**R2.** Point to the student's writing samples from the assessment. "Can you read your writing back to me?" (Assesses whether the student can read their own writing — an important self-monitoring skill.)

**R3.** "If you could write about anything in the world, what would you write about?" (Assesses motivation and topic awareness for future writing instruction.)

Note responses — they help calibrate pacing and engagement strategies for Unit 4.

---

## Facilitator Observations (Record During Assessment)

- Does the student approach writing with confidence, or do they hesitate and seek reassurance?
- Does the student use invented spelling freely, or do they avoid words they cannot spell?
- How much support does the student need with letter formation? Does handwriting slow the composition process significantly?
- Can the student read their own writing back, or do they forget what they wrote?
- Does the student use the sentence frames independently, or do they need the frames displayed?
- Does the student show awareness of audience — do they write AS IF someone will read it?
- How does the student handle the noun/verb task — quickly and confidently, or with hesitation and guessing?
- Which writing type does the student produce most easily? Which is hardest?

These observations inform differentiation decisions for Unit 4 and beyond.
