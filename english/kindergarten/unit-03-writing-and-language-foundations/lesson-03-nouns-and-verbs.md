# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 3, Lesson 3

## Topic
Nouns and Verbs

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify nouns as "naming words" — words that name a person, place, animal, or thing
- Identify verbs as "action words" — words that tell what someone or something does
- Sort a set of familiar words into nouns and verbs
- Use at least one noun and one verb in an original spoken or written sentence

## Prerequisites
- Unit 2 complete (reads 10 high-frequency words, reads simple sentences)
- Lesson 2 complete (can write a simple sentence with a capital letter, spaces, and a period)
- Can speak in complete sentences

## Materials Needed
- Index cards or paper strips (at least 16 — 8 for nouns, 8 for verbs)
- Crayons or markers in two colors (e.g., blue for nouns, red for verbs)
- Two sorting containers or two sections on a table (labeled "Naming Words" and "Action Words")
- Pencil and lined paper
- Small objects or picture cards of common items (optional — a toy cat, a ball, a cup, etc.)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate awareness of different kinds of words by acting them out.

**Activity: Do What I Say**
Say: "I'm going to say some words. Some of them are things you can DO. When I say a word you can do, show me! Act it out!"

Call out these words one at a time, pausing after each:
- **Jump** (student jumps)
- **Cat** (student may look confused — say: "Can you 'cat'? That is not something you do, is it? 'Cat' is a thing, not an action!")
- **Clap** (student claps)
- **Book** (student pauses — "Can you 'book'? Nope — that is a thing!")
- **Run** (student runs in place)
- **Cup** (thing, not an action)

Say: "Some words are things you can DO — like jump, clap, and run. Other words are THINGS — like cat, book, and cup. Today we are going to learn the names for these two kinds of words."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the concepts of nouns and verbs using concrete examples and a simple sorting system.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Noun:** A naming word — it names a person, a place, an animal, or a thing (examples: mom, park, dog, ball)
- **Verb:** An action word — it tells what someone or something does (examples: run, sit, eat, jump)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce nouns.**
Say: "Words that name things are called NOUNS. A noun is a naming word. Let me show you."

Hold up or point to objects around you as you name them: "Cat — that is a noun. It names an animal. Cup — that is a noun. It names a thing. Mom — that is a noun. It names a person. Park — that is a noun. It names a place."

Write the word NOUN on a card. Say: "A noun names a person, a place, an animal, or a thing. Let's say it: A noun is a NAMING word."

Give examples from words the student already knows:
- **cat** — noun (names an animal)
- **dog** — noun (names an animal)
- **bed** — noun (names a thing)
- **mom** — noun (names a person)

Write each word on a card and place it in the "Naming Words" pile.

**Step 2: Introduce verbs.**
Say: "Words that tell what you DO are called VERBS. A verb is an action word. Let me show you."

Act out each word as you say it: "Run — that is a verb. It tells what I do." (Run in place.) "Sit — verb." (Sit down.) "Eat — verb." (Pretend to eat.) "Jump — verb." (Jump.)

Write the word VERB on a card. Say: "A verb tells what someone or something does. Let's say it: A verb is an ACTION word."

Give examples:
- **run** — verb (tells what you do)
- **sit** — verb (tells what you do)
- **eat** — verb (tells what you do)
- **hop** — verb (tells what you do)

Write each word on a card and place it in the "Action Words" pile.

**Step 3: Connect to sentences.**
Write: **The cat can run.**

Point to each word: "The — this is a little helper word. Cat — is 'cat' a naming word or an action word?" (Naming word — noun.) "Can — helper word. Run — naming or action?" (Action word — verb.)

Say: "Every sentence needs BOTH a noun and a verb. The noun tells us WHO or WHAT. The verb tells us what they DO. The cat (who) can run (what it does)."

Write: **My dog can sit.**
Ask: "What is the noun? What is the verb?" (Dog is the noun, sit is the verb.)

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Write these words on cards: **ball, hop, fish, eat**

Pick up "ball." Say: "Ball — can I do a 'ball'? No, 'ball' is a thing. It is a NOUN — a naming word." Place in the Naming Words pile.

Pick up "hop." Say: "Hop — can I do a 'hop'? YES!" (Hop once.) "Hop is something I do. It is a VERB — an action word." Place in the Action Words pile.

**Example 2:** Pick up "fish." Say: "Fish — is this a thing or something you do? A fish is an animal — it is a NOUN." Place in Naming Words.

Pick up "eat." Say: "Eat — is this a thing or something you do?" (Pretend to eat.) "Eat is something I do. It is a VERB." Place in Action Words.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** The student practices sorting words into nouns and verbs with facilitator support.

**Activity: Word Sort**

Prepare these 12 word cards (mix them up so nouns and verbs are not grouped):

**Nouns:** cat, dog, bed, cup, hat, bug
**Verbs:** run, sit, hop, nap, hug, dig

Lay out two sorting areas labeled "Naming Words (Nouns)" and "Action Words (Verbs)."

Say: "I have some word cards. Let's sort them together. For each word, ask yourself: Is this a thing I can name? Or is this something I can do?"

Hand the student one card at a time. For each card:
1. The student reads the word (these are CVC words they should be able to decode from Unit 1).
2. The student decides: noun or verb?
3. The student places the card in the correct pile.

*Facilitator prompts:*
- "Read the word. Now, can you DO this, or is it a THING?"
- "Try acting it out. Can you act out this word? If you can act it out, it is probably a verb."
- "Can you hold it, touch it, or see it as a thing? Then it is probably a noun."

*If the student struggles:* Do the first 4 cards together, modeling your thinking. "Bed — can I 'bed'? No. But I can sleep IN a bed. Bed is a thing. It goes in the naming words pile."

After sorting all 12 cards, review both piles. Read each pile aloud: "Our naming words are: cat, dog, bed, cup, hat, bug. Our action words are: run, sit, hop, nap, hug, dig."

**Sentence Building (remaining time):**
Say: "Now let's put a noun and a verb together to make a sentence. Pick one card from each pile."

If the student picks "cat" and "nap," help them build a sentence: "The cat can nap." Write it together, using the sentence rules from Lesson 2 (capital, spaces, period).

Build 2-3 sentences this way.

---

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** The student identifies nouns and verbs and writes sentences using them.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Give the student 6 new word cards (3 nouns, 3 verbs): **sun, fox, pan, run, sit, mix**. The student sorts them independently into nouns and verbs. Then the student writes one sentence using a noun and a verb from the cards.

**Stretch:** Same as on-level, plus the student writes 2 sentences, each using a different noun-verb pair. The student circles the noun in blue and the verb in red in each sentence.

**Challenge:** The student generates their own list: "Name 3 nouns and 3 verbs." (Student says or writes them.) Then the student writes 2-3 sentences using their own nouns and verbs, underlining nouns and circling verbs.

### Interleaved Review
After writing sentences, check for the three sentence rules from Lesson 2: capital letter, spaces, period. Say: "Read your sentence. Does it start with a capital letter? Are there spaces? Is there a period?" This keeps Lesson 2 conventions active.

Also point to any sight words in the student's sentences: "Can you find a word from our sight word list?" (the, a, I, is, it, and, to, in, we, my)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What is a noun?" (A naming word — a word that names a person, place, animal, or thing.)
2. "What is a verb?" (An action word — a word that tells what someone or something does.)
3. Show the student these 4 words one at a time: **hat, run, dog, sit**. "Tell me — is this a noun or a verb?"
   - hat = noun
   - run = verb
   - dog = noun
   - sit = verb

**Mastery = correctly answers items 1 and 2 (definitions, in student's own words) AND correctly sorts at least 3 of 4 words in item 3.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Play "Noun or Verb?" throughout the day: point to things and name them (nouns), then act out actions (verbs). Ask: "Is that a naming word or an action word?"
- Use two colored bins or bags — put toy objects in the "noun" bin and write action words on cards for the "verb" bag
- Read simple sentences aloud and have the student clap on nouns and stomp on verbs
- Repeat this lesson next session with different word sets

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Opinion Writing — My Favorite). Continue using noun and verb language naturally: "What a great verb you used!" "Can you add a noun to that sentence?"

---

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who want to do more:**
- Play "Noun Hunt" — walk around the room and name 10 nouns you can see. Write them down in a list.
- Play "Verb Charades" — the facilitator acts out a verb, and the student guesses the action word. Then switch roles.
- Create a "Noun and Verb Book" — on each page, the student draws a noun and a verb and writes a sentence combining them: "The bug can dig." "A dog can run."
- Explore words that can be both: "Can you 'fish'? Yes — you can go fishing! So 'fish' can be a naming word AND an action word." (Advanced concept — only introduce if the student shows strong understanding.)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Keep it concrete and physical.** Five-year-olds learn grammar through their bodies, not through definitions. Acting out verbs, touching objects for nouns, and sorting physical cards all build the concept far more effectively than worksheets or drills. If the student can consistently act out verbs and point to nouns, they understand the concept — even if they cannot recite a perfect definition.

**Do not expect mastery of edge cases.** Some words (like "fish," "run," "play") can be both nouns and verbs depending on context. Do not address this unless the student raises it. For kindergarten, the goal is the basic concept: naming words vs. action words.

**Use familiar CVC words.** All the example nouns and verbs in this lesson are simple CVC or short words the student can decode. This keeps the cognitive load on grammar, not on reading difficulty.

**This lesson sets up future writing.** Understanding nouns and verbs helps students compose more deliberately. When a student knows they need "a naming word and an action word," they can build sentences more independently. This directly supports the opinion and informative writing coming in Lessons 4-5.

**L.K.1b alignment:** This lesson addresses Language standard L.K.1b — "Use frequently occurring nouns and verbs." The focus is on identification and use in simple sentences, not on formal grammatical analysis.
