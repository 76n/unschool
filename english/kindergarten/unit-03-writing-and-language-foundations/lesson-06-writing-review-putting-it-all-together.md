# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 3, Lesson 6

## Topic
Writing Review — Putting It All Together

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify and produce all three types of writing taught in this unit: narrative, opinion, and informative
- Write a simple sentence that follows conventions (capital letter, spaces, period)
- Identify nouns and verbs in sentences
- Choose the correct writing type for a given purpose (telling a story, sharing an opinion, teaching facts)

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-5 of this unit complete
- Can draw and dictate or write to compose narratives, opinions, and informative texts
- Understands nouns, verbs, capital letters, spaces, and periods

## Materials Needed
- Blank paper (at least 4 sheets — one for each writing type plus extra)
- Pencil, crayons, and eraser
- Three label cards or signs: **Story (Narrative)**, **Opinion**, **Informative (Facts)**
- Sentence frame strips from previous lessons:
  - Opinion: **I like ___ because ___.**
  - Informative: **A ___ has/can/is ___.**
- The student's writing from Lessons 1, 4, and 5 (if saved — for reviewing their own work)
- A "spacer" tool for word spacing

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review all three writing types by identifying them in examples.

**Activity: What Kind of Writing?**
Place the three label cards on the table: **Story**, **Opinion**, **Informative**.

Read each sentence aloud and ask the student to point to the correct label:

1. "I went to the park and played on the swings." (Story — it tells what happened)
2. "I like ice cream because it is cold and sweet." (Opinion — it says what someone likes)
3. "A frog can jump very far." (Informative — it teaches a fact)
4. "My mom and I baked cookies. They were yummy." (Story — it tells what happened)
5. "A bird has wings and a beak." (Informative — it teaches facts)
6. "Red is the best color." (Opinion — it says what someone thinks)

After each one, briefly confirm: "Yes! That is a ___ because ___."

Say: "You know THREE kinds of writing now! Today we are going to practice all three."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review and consolidate all unit skills before the student writes independently.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Narrative (story):** Writing that tells what happened — it has a beginning, middle, and end
- **Opinion:** Writing that says what you like or think and gives a reason why
- **Informative:** Writing that teaches facts about a topic
- **Conventions:** The rules of writing — capital letter, spaces, period
- **Noun:** A naming word (person, place, animal, thing)
- **Verb:** An action word (what someone or something does)

### Instruction Notes

**Review 1: The three writing types.**
Hold up each label card and review:

"STORY writing tells what happened. You draw a picture and write about something that happened to you — or make up a story. Remember Lesson 1? You drew a picture and told me a story."

"OPINION writing tells what you LIKE or THINK. You say what you like and WHY. Remember Lesson 4? You wrote 'I like ___ because ___.'"

"INFORMATIVE writing teaches FACTS. You pick a topic and share true things about it. Remember Lesson 5? You wrote facts like 'A ___ has ___ ' or 'A ___ can ___.'"

**Review 2: Sentence conventions.**
Write a sentence with all three rules missing: **the dog can run**

Say: "What is wrong with this sentence? Let's fix it together."
- Missing capital T: fix to **T**
- Check for spaces: spaces are present (good)
- Missing period: add **.** at the end

Final: **The dog can run.**

Write another broken sentence: **iseea cat.** (no capital, no spaces except before "cat")
Fix together: **I see a cat.**

**Review 3: Nouns and verbs.**
Write: **The big cat can jump.**
Ask: "Find the noun — the naming word." (cat)
Ask: "Find the verb — the action word." (jump)

Write: **My dog is in the bed.**
Ask: "Noun?" (dog, bed — there can be more than one!) "Verb?" (is)

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 — Narrative:** "Let's write a quick story sentence. Something that happened. 'I went to the park.' That is a story — it tells what happened." Write: **I went to the park.**

**Example 2 — Opinion:** "Now an opinion. 'I like the park because I can swing.' That says what I like and why." Write: **I like the park because I can swing.**

**Example 3 — Informative:** "Now a fact. 'A park has swings and slides.' That teaches someone about parks." Write: **A park has swings and slides.**

Say: "Three different kinds of writing — all about the same topic! A story about the park, an opinion about the park, and facts about the park."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** The student writes one example of each writing type with facilitator support.

**Activity: Three-Page Writing Challenge**

Give the student 3 sheets of paper. At the top of each, write one label:
- Page 1: **My Story**
- Page 2: **My Opinion**
- Page 3: **My Facts**

Say: "You are going to write THREE pieces today — one of each kind. Pick a topic you want to write about. You can use the SAME topic for all three, or pick different topics."

Let the student choose a topic. A single topic for all three is recommended because it simplifies the task and shows how the same subject can be written about in different ways.

**Page 1: Story (Narrative)**
Say: "Draw a picture and write a sentence that tells a story — something that happened. Use your topic."

*Facilitator prompts:*
- "What happened? Tell me a story about ___."
- "What happened first? What happened next?"
- "Write it down. Remember: capital, spaces, period."

Help as needed. Target: 1-2 sentences.

**Page 2: Opinion**
Say: "Now write your opinion about your topic. Tell me what you LIKE about it and WHY."

*Facilitator prompts:*
- "Do you like this? Why?"
- "Use our frame: I like ___ because ___."
- "Check your sentence rules."

Target: 1-2 sentences.

**Page 3: Informative (Facts)**
Say: "Now teach me something about your topic. Write a FACT — something true that everyone would agree on."

*Facilitator prompts:*
- "What does ___ have? What can ___ do? What is ___ like?"
- "Use our frame: A ___ has/can/is ___."
- "Is that a fact or an opinion? Remember — a fact is true for everyone."

Target: 1-2 sentences.

After all three pages are done, lay them out side by side. Say: "Look at what you did! You wrote a story, an opinion, AND informative writing. You are a real writer!"

*If time allows,* ask the student to find a noun and a verb on each page.

---

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** The student demonstrates the ability to choose and use the correct writing type.

**Activity: You Choose**

Say: "I am going to give you a job. You pick which kind of writing to use."

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Give the student ONE prompt: "Your friend has never seen snow. Write something to TEACH them about snow." The student should recognize this as informative writing and write 1-2 fact sentences with a drawing. (Accept any topic-appropriate response.)

**Stretch:** Give the student TWO prompts:
1. "Tell me about a time you played outside." (Narrative — 1-2 sentences)
2. "What is your favorite thing to play?" (Opinion — 1-2 sentences)
The student writes both, identifying which is a story and which is an opinion.

**Challenge:** Give the student all three prompts:
1. "Write a story about your family." (Narrative)
2. "Write your opinion about a food." (Opinion)
3. "Teach me about an animal." (Informative)
The student writes all three independently (2+ sentences each), labeling each with its type. Student checks own work for conventions, nouns, and verbs.

### Interleaved Review
After writing, do a comprehensive review:
- **Unit 2 sight words:** "Can you find any of our sight words in your writing?" (the, a, I, is, it, and, to, in, we, my)
- **Lesson 2 conventions:** "Check every sentence: capital, spaces, period."
- **Lesson 3 grammar:** "Circle one noun in blue. Circle one verb in red."
- **Lesson 4-5 writing types:** "Point to your opinion sentence. Point to your fact sentence."

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. "Name the three kinds of writing we learned." (Story/narrative, opinion, informative/facts.)
2. The facilitator says: "I want to tell my friend what I think about a movie. Which kind of writing should I use?" (Opinion.)
3. The facilitator says: "I want to teach someone about fish. Which kind of writing?" (Informative.)
4. "Tell me the three rules for writing a sentence." (Capital letter at the beginning, spaces between words, period at the end.)
5. The facilitator writes: **My cat can run.** "Find the noun and the verb." (Noun: cat. Verb: run.)

**Mastery = correctly answers at least 4 of 5 items.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the specific area of weakness (writing types, conventions, or grammar) before the unit assessment
- Create a reference chart for the student's workspace: three writing types with an example of each
- Practice daily: "Write one sentence. Is it a story, opinion, or fact?"
- Reteach the weakest lesson before proceeding to the unit assessment

### If Mastery Is Met
The student is ready for the Unit 3 Assessment. Celebrate their growth as a writer! Encourage daily writing practice of any kind between this lesson and the assessment.

---

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who want to do more:**
- Create a "Writer's Portfolio" — collect the student's best work from each lesson in the unit. The student chooses one story, one opinion piece, and one informative piece. Staple them together as a keepsake.
- Have the student "publish" a piece of writing by reading it aloud to family members and answering questions about it.
- Play "Writing Roulette" — write the three writing types on cards, pull one randomly, and the student must write that type about a topic the facilitator chooses. Quick rounds build flexibility.
- Start a daily journal: the student writes (or dictates) one sentence each day about anything they want. Over time, they will naturally use all three writing types.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson is a review, not new instruction.** The goal is consolidation and confidence. If the student struggles, it means one of the earlier lessons needs reinforcement — not that this lesson needs to be taught differently. Identify the gap and go back to the specific lesson.

**Three writing types is a lot for kindergarten.** Most kindergarteners will have a preferred type (usually narrative — stories come naturally at this age). That is fine. The goal is EXPOSURE to all three types and the ability to identify them, not equal mastery of all three. If the student can consistently produce one type well and identify the other two, that is a strong outcome.

**The "same topic, three types" approach is powerful.** Writing about dogs as a story ("I took my dog to the park"), an opinion ("I like dogs because they are fluffy"), and informative text ("A dog has four legs and a tail") shows the student that the SAME subject can be written about in different ways for different purposes. This is a foundational understanding of audience and purpose in writing.

**Conventions should be becoming more automatic.** By Lesson 6, the student should need fewer reminders about capital letters, spaces, and periods. If conventions are still a struggle, continue using the checklist (capital? spaces? period?) as a self-check tool, but do not let convention struggles overshadow composition confidence.

**Celebrating progress matters.** Compare the student's writing from Lesson 1 (drawing + dictation) to their writing today. In many cases, the growth will be visible and impressive. Pointing this out builds writing identity: "Look how much you have learned! You are a WRITER."
