# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 3, Lesson 2

## Topic
Writing Simple Sentences

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Write a simple sentence with a capital letter at the beginning
- End a sentence with a period
- Leave spaces between words when writing
- Distinguish between a correct sentence (capital, spaces, period) and an incorrect one

## Prerequisites
- Unit 2 complete (reads 10 high-frequency words: the, a, I, is, it, and, to, in, we, my)
- Can form recognizable letters (uppercase and lowercase) from Unit 1
- Lesson 1 complete (understands that stories and ideas can be put into written words)

## Materials Needed
- Lined paper or paper with a dotted midline (helps with letter sizing) — at least 4 sheets
- Pencil and eraser
- Crayons (for drawing after writing)
- Index cards or paper strips with the 10 sight words from Unit 2
- A "spacer" tool: a popsicle stick, a finger, or a small piece of cardboard the width of a finger (used to create spaces between words)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect reading sentences (Unit 2 skill) to the new idea of WRITING sentences.

**Activity: Read It, Then Write It**
Write this sentence on paper in large print: **I see a cat.**

Say: "Can you read this sentence?" Let the student read it.

Then ask: "What do you notice about how this sentence looks? Look at the very beginning. Look at the very end." Guide the student to observe:
- The "I" at the beginning is a capital letter
- There is a period (.) at the end
- There are spaces between each word

Say: "When someone wrote this sentence, they followed three rules. Today, we are going to learn those three rules so YOU can write your own sentences."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the three conventions of a simple written sentence: capital letter at the start, spaces between words, and a period at the end.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Sentence:** A group of words that tells a complete thought
- **Capital letter:** A big letter (uppercase) — we use one at the start of every sentence and for the word "I"
- **Period:** The dot at the end of a sentence that tells the reader to stop
- **Spaces:** The gaps between words so readers can tell where one word ends and the next begins

### Instruction Notes

**Rule 1: Start with a capital letter.**
Write on paper in front of the student: **t**he cat is big.

Say: "Something is wrong with this sentence. Can you spot it?" If the student does not notice, point to the "t" and say: "The first letter of a sentence must always be a CAPITAL letter — a big letter. Watch me fix it."

Cross out the lowercase "t" and write a capital "T" above it: **T**he cat is big.

Say: "Every time we start a new sentence, the very first letter gets to be BIG — it is a capital letter. That is Rule Number One."

Write on paper: **I like to run.** Point to the "I" and say: "The word 'I' is always a capital letter — even in the middle of a sentence. That is because 'I' is special. It is always big."

**Rule 2: Put spaces between words.**
Write on paper, squished together with no spaces: **Thecatisbig.**

Say: "Can you read this? It is hard because the words are all smushed together!" Now write it correctly: **The cat is big.**

Say: "See the difference? The spaces help us see each word. When we write, we need to leave a space between every word. Watch — I put my finger down after each word to make a space before I write the next word."

Demonstrate: Write "The" then place your finger (or the spacer tool) right after it. Write "cat" starting where your finger ends. Place the spacer again. Write "is." Spacer. Write "big."

Say: "Your finger (or this stick) is your spacer. Every time you finish a word, put the spacer down, and start the next word on the other side. That is Rule Number Two."

**Rule 3: End with a period.**
Write: **The cat is big**

Say: "This sentence looks good — capital letter, spaces — but something is missing at the end. We need a PERIOD." Add a period: **The cat is big.**

Say: "A period is a little dot that tells the reader: STOP. The sentence is done. Every sentence needs one at the end. That is Rule Number Three."

**Summary — The Three Rules:**
Hold up fingers as you count: "Rule 1: Capital letter at the beginning. Rule 2: Spaces between every word. Rule 3: Period at the end."

Say: "Let's say it together: Capital, spaces, period!"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Say: "Let's write a sentence together. I will say the sentence first: 'My dog is big.' Now let's write it."

Write aloud, narrating every step:
- "My — that is the first word, so I need a capital M." Write **M**
- "y." Write **y** to make **My**
- "Now I need a space." Place spacer.
- "d-o-g." Write **dog**
- "Space." Place spacer.
- "i-s." Write **is**
- "Space." Place spacer.
- "b-i-g." Write **big**
- "And what do I need at the end? A period!" Write **.**

Read the whole sentence: **My dog is big.** "Capital M, spaces, period — all three rules!"

**Example 2:** Say: "Let's try another one: 'I like to run.'"

This time, ask the student to help:
- "What letter do I start with? What kind of letter?" (Capital I)
- Write each word, asking the student: "Do I need a space here?" (Yes, after every word)
- At the end: "What goes at the very end?" (A period)

Read together: **I like to run.**

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** The student writes sentences with facilitator support, practicing all three rules.

**Activity: Copy and Write**

**Part 1: Guided Copy (5 minutes)**
Write these two sentences clearly at the top of a sheet of lined paper:

1. **I see a cat.**
2. **We like to run.**

Say: "Copy each sentence on the line below. Remember the three rules: capital letter, spaces, period."

Watch the student copy. Check for:
- Does the student start with a capital letter?
- Does the student leave spaces? (Remind them to use the spacer tool)
- Does the student include the period?

If the student forgets a rule, point to the checklist you can create together:
- [ ] Capital letter?
- [ ] Spaces?
- [ ] Period?

*Facilitator prompts:*
- "Check your first letter. Is it a capital?"
- "Use your spacer between words."
- "You finished the sentence! What goes at the end?"

**Part 2: Dictated Writing (5 minutes)**
Say: "Now I'm going to say a sentence and you write it. Ready? The sentence is: 'It is a big dog.'"

Repeat the sentence slowly. The student writes it. Provide help with letter formation if needed, but focus on the three conventions (capital, spaces, period). Accept invented spelling for words the student does not know by sight — for example, "dog" might be written as "DOG" or "DOg" and that is fine.

If the student succeeds, try a second dictated sentence: "My cat is fat."

*If the student struggles with the physical writing:* Let the student dictate the sentence to you. Write it in dotted letters. The student traces your dotted letters. This still practices the conventions visually.

*If the student forgets spaces:* Hand them the spacer tool and say: "After every word, put the spacer down. Then start the next word."

---

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** The student writes original sentences using the three rules.

Say: "Now you are going to write your OWN sentence. Think of something you want to say. It can be about anything — your family, your pet, your favorite food, what you did today."

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Student writes 1 original sentence with a capital letter, spaces, and a period. Invented spelling is accepted. Example: "I LIK MY MOM." (Correct use of capital, spaces, and period even though "like" is spelled inventedly.)

**Stretch:** Student writes 2 original sentences, each with a capital letter, spaces, and a period. Student draws a picture to go with the sentences (connecting to Lesson 1 skills).

**Challenge:** Student writes 3 original sentences that tell a short story (beginning, middle, end). Each sentence uses capital letters, spaces, and a period. Student attempts to use at least 3 sight words from Unit 2.

### Interleaved Review
After writing, spread out the 10 sight word cards from Unit 2. Say: "Can you find any of these words in the sentences you wrote?" The student identifies matching words. This reinforces sight word recognition in the context of the student's own writing.

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Write this sentence incorrectly: **the cat is big** (no capital, no period). Ask: "What is wrong with this sentence? Can you fix it?" (Student should identify the missing capital T and the missing period.)
2. Write: **Mydog is big.** Ask: "What is missing?" (A space between "My" and "dog.")
3. Say: "Tell me the three rules for writing a sentence." (Capital letter at the beginning, spaces between words, period at the end.)

**Mastery = correctly answers all 3 items.** Item 1 checks recognition of two conventions. Item 2 checks spacing awareness. Item 3 checks recall of the rules.

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice writing one sentence per day with the three-rule checklist
- Have the student be a "sentence detective" — show them sentences you write (some correct, some with errors) and let them find the mistakes
- Use physical spacers consistently until spacing becomes automatic
- Repeat this lesson next session with different example sentences

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (Nouns and Verbs). Continue daily sentence writing practice to build fluency and automaticity with the three rules.

---

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who want to do more:**
- Write a "letter" to a family member: one sentence telling them something. Practice all three rules. Deliver the letter.
- Play "Fix the Sentence" — the facilitator writes deliberately broken sentences (missing capitals, missing periods, no spaces) and the student plays editor, circling and fixing each error with a red crayon.
- Label objects around the room with sentences: "The cup is red." "My bed is big." Each label must follow all three rules.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Invented spelling is expected and welcomed.** At this stage, the lesson's primary goal is sentence conventions (capital, spaces, period) — NOT correct spelling. A sentence like "I LIK MY KAT." demonstrates mastery of all three conventions even though "like" and "cat" are spelled inventedly. Praise the conventions: "You remembered your capital letter, your spaces, AND your period! Beautiful sentence!"

**Do not correct spelling unless asked.** If the student asks "How do you spell ___?", help them. Otherwise, let invented spelling stand. Correcting spelling at this stage discourages risk-taking and slows composition. Spelling will be addressed explicitly in future units.

**The spacer tool is essential for many kindergarteners.** Children at this age often run words together because they do not yet have an internalized sense of word boundaries in writing (even if they can identify spaces when reading). A physical spacer — finger, popsicle stick, piece of cardboard — makes the abstract concept concrete. Use it consistently until the student no longer needs it.

**Left-to-right writing:** Most students will write left to right by this point (after Unit 2 concepts of print), but some may still need reminders. Place a green dot at the left margin as a "start here" cue if needed.

**L.K.2 alignment:** This lesson addresses Language standard L.K.2 — "Demonstrate command of the conventions of standard English capitalization, punctuation, and spelling when writing: a. Capitalize the first word in a sentence and the pronoun I. b. Recognize and name end punctuation. d. Spell simple words phonetically, drawing on knowledge of sound-letter relationships."

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice Part 1 (Copy):**
1. I see a cat. (Capital I, spaces, period)
2. We like to run. (Capital W, spaces, period)

**Guided Practice Part 2 (Dictation):**
1. It is a big dog. (Capital I, spaces, period)
2. My cat is fat. (Capital M, spaces, period)

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Needs a capital T at the beginning and a period at the end: **The cat is big.**
2. Needs a space between "My" and "dog": **My dog is big.**
3. Three rules: capital letter at the beginning, spaces between words, period at the end
