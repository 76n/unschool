# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 3, Lesson 4

## Topic
Opinion Writing — My Favorite

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- State an opinion about a favorite thing (food, animal, color, toy, etc.)
- Draw a picture and write or dictate at least one sentence that states their opinion
- Use the sentence frame "I like ___ because ___" to structure an opinion
- Identify the difference between a fact and an opinion

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1 complete (drawing and dictating to tell a story)
- Lesson 2 complete (writing simple sentences with capital, spaces, period)
- Lesson 3 complete (understands nouns and verbs)
- Can write or dictate at least one complete sentence

## Materials Needed
- Blank paper (at least 3 sheets — top half for drawing, bottom half for writing)
- Pencil and crayons
- A sentence frame strip written in large print: **I like ___ because ___.**
- Two different snacks, toys, or picture cards for the facilitator to model choosing a favorite (e.g., an apple and a banana, or a picture of a cat and a picture of a dog)
- Optional: stickers or stamps to mark the student's finished opinion piece

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the concept of opinions by making a simple choice and defending it.

**Activity: This or That?**
Hold up two items or pictures (e.g., an apple and a banana). Say: "Which one do you like better — the apple or the banana?"

The student picks one. Then ask: "Why do you like that one better?"

Listen to the student's answer. Then say: "You just shared your OPINION! An opinion is what YOU think. It is what you like, or what you believe. Not everyone has the same opinion. I might like the banana, and you might like the apple — and we are BOTH right! That is what makes opinions special."

Do 2-3 more rounds with simple choices:
- Red or blue?
- Dogs or cats?
- Playing outside or playing inside?

Each time, ask "Why?" to get the student to practice giving a reason.

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the student how to express an opinion in writing using a sentence frame.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Opinion:** What you think or feel about something — your favorite, what you like, what you believe is best
- **Reason:** Why you think that — the "because" part
- **Fact:** Something that is true for everyone (the sky is blue) — NOT the same as an opinion
- **Sentence frame:** A sentence with blanks that helps you write (like a pattern to follow)

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Fact vs. Opinion.**
Say: "Before we write opinions, let's make sure we know what an opinion IS."

Write: **A cat has four legs.** Say: "Is this a fact or an opinion? It is a FACT — all cats have four legs. Everyone agrees."

Write: **Cats are the best pet.** Say: "Is this a fact or an opinion? It is an OPINION — some people think cats are best, and some people think dogs are best. It is what YOU think."

Do 2 more examples:
- "The sun is hot." (Fact)
- "Summer is the best season." (Opinion)

Say: "Facts are true for everyone. Opinions are what YOU think. Today we are going to write our opinions."

**Step 2: Model opinion writing.**
Display the sentence frame: **I like ___ because ___.**

Say: "This sentence frame helps us write our opinion. Watch me use it."

Think aloud: "Hmm, what is my favorite animal? I love dogs. So I'll say: 'I like dogs because they are fun to play with.'"

Write the sentence on paper (large, clear print): **I like dogs because they are fun.**

Point out: "See — I told my opinion (I like dogs) AND I told why (because they are fun). That is what makes opinion writing strong — you say what you like AND you say why."

Draw a quick picture of a dog above the sentence. Say: "My picture shows what I am writing about. The picture and the words go together."

**Step 3: Show a second example.**
Say: "Let me write another opinion. What is my favorite color? Blue! 'I like blue because it is the color of the sky.'"

Write: **I like blue because it is the color of the sky.**

Say: "Both of my opinions follow the same pattern: I like SOMETHING because REASON."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Say: "Pretend my favorite food is pizza. How would I fill in the sentence frame?" Point to: **I like ___ because ___.** "I like PIZZA because IT IS YUMMY." Write: **I like pizza because it is yummy.**

**Example 2:** "Now pretend my favorite toy is a ball. I like BALL because I CAN THROW IT." Write: **I like my ball because I can throw it.** Point out the noun ("ball") and verb ("throw") from Lesson 3.

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** The student composes their own opinion piece with facilitator support.

**Activity: My Favorite Thing**

Say: "Now it is YOUR turn! What is your favorite thing? It can be your favorite food, animal, color, toy, game — anything you love."

Give the student a moment to think. If they need help choosing, offer: "What makes you smile the most? What do you want to talk about?"

Once the student chooses a topic, guide them through the process:

**Step 1: Talk about it first.**
Say: "Tell me about your favorite ___. What is it? Why do you like it?"

Let the student talk. Listen for the opinion and the reason.

**Step 2: Draw it.**
Say: "Draw a picture of your favorite ___." Give 3-4 minutes for drawing.

**Step 3: Write the opinion sentence.**
Display the sentence frame again: **I like ___ because ___.**

Say: "Now let's write your opinion. Use our sentence frame. What goes in the first blank?" (The student's favorite thing.) "What goes in the second blank — the 'because' part?" (The student's reason.)

Help the student write the sentence on the paper below their drawing. Depending on the student's writing level:
- **If the student can write independently:** Let them write. Assist with spelling only if asked. Accept invented spelling.
- **If the student needs support:** Write the sentence frame ("I like ___ because ___.") and let the student fill in the blanks.
- **If the student cannot write yet:** The student dictates and the facilitator writes. The student then reads it back, pointing to words.

Remind: "Check your three rules! Capital letter? Spaces? Period?"

*Facilitator prompts:*
- "What do you like? Tell me in one sentence."
- "Why do you like it? What is the reason?"
- "Great! Now let's put that into writing."

*If the student cannot give a reason:* Ask leading questions. "What is fun about it? How does it make you feel? What can you do with it?" Accept any reason, even simple ones: "because it is red" or "because I just do."

---

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** The student writes a second opinion piece with less support.

Say: "Let's write another opinion — a different one this time. Pick something NEW that you like. Draw it at the top and write your opinion at the bottom."

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Student draws a picture and writes or dictates 1 opinion sentence using the sentence frame: "I like ___ because ___." Capital letter, spaces, and period are present.

**Stretch:** Student writes 2 sentences: the opinion sentence AND one more detail sentence. For example: "I like cats because they are soft. My cat is black." Student draws a picture to match.

**Challenge:** Student writes 3 sentences: an opinion sentence, a reason sentence, and a closing sentence. For example: "I like my bike. I like it because I can go fast. My bike is the best!" Student does not need the sentence frame — writes freely.

### Interleaved Review
After writing, review Lesson 3 concepts. Point to the student's sentences and ask: "Can you find a NOUN — a naming word — in your sentence?" Then: "Can you find a VERB — an action word?" Also check for Lesson 2 conventions: "Does your sentence start with a capital letter? Do you have spaces? Is there a period at the end?"

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What is an opinion?" (What you think, what you like, what you believe — something not everyone agrees on.)
2. "Is this a fact or an opinion: 'Pizza is the best food'?" (Opinion.)
3. Show the student their writing from today. "Read me your opinion. What do you like, and why?" (Student states their opinion and reason, either reading from paper or recalling.)

**Mastery = correctly answers items 1 and 2 AND can state their opinion with a reason in item 3.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice "This or That?" choices daily and always ask "Why?"
- Read aloud books where characters state opinions and ask the student: "Is that a fact or an opinion?"
- Post the sentence frame on the wall and have the student fill it in orally each day about something new
- Repeat this lesson next session with a different topic

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 5 (Informative Writing — Teaching About Something). Encourage the student to share opinions throughout the day using "I like ___ because ___."

---

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who want to do more:**
- Create a "My Favorites Book" — one page for favorite food, one for favorite animal, one for favorite color, one for favorite game. Each page has a drawing and an opinion sentence.
- Interview a family member: "What is your favorite ___? Why?" Write down their opinion too. Compare: "We have different opinions — and that is okay!"
- Write an opinion about a book: "I like this book because ___." Draw the favorite part.
- Write a "persuasion" piece: "You should try ___!" (very early persuasive writing)

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The sentence frame is a scaffold, not a requirement.** The "I like ___ because ___" frame gives students a safe starting structure. As students gain confidence, they should move away from the frame and express opinions in their own words. Do not insist on the exact frame if the student naturally writes something like "Pizza is yummy because I love cheese." That is a valid opinion sentence.

**"Because" is the key word.** The most important skill in this lesson is connecting an opinion to a reason. Even if the reason is simple ("because I like it"), the student is learning that opinions need support. This is the earliest foundation of argumentative writing.

**Accept simple reasons without judgment.** "I like red because it is red" is a circular reason, but it is still a reason. Gently prompt for more detail ("What do you like about the color red? Does it remind you of anything?") but do not reject the original response.

**Invented spelling remains fully accepted.** A student who writes "I LIK PEZA BEKUZ IT IS YUME" has demonstrated opinion writing, sentence structure, spacing, and a capital letter. That is a successful piece of kindergarten opinion writing.

**W.K.1 alignment:** This lesson addresses Writing standard W.K.1 — "Use a combination of drawing, dictating, and writing to compose opinion pieces in which they tell a reader the topic or name of the book they are writing about and state an opinion or preference about the topic or book."

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice and Independent Practice:** All answers are student-generated. There are no fixed correct answers. Evaluate based on:
- Does the student state an opinion (what they like)?
- Does the student provide a reason (why they like it)?
- Does the drawing relate to the opinion?
- Does the sentence follow conventions (capital, spaces, period)?

**Exit Ticket:**
1. An opinion is what you think or feel about something — not everyone agrees (accept student's own wording)
2. "Pizza is the best food" is an opinion
3. Student reads or states their opinion with a reason
