# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 2, Lesson 2

## Topic
High-Frequency Words Set 1: the, a, I, is, it

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read the words **the**, **a**, **I**, **is**, and **it** on sight (without sounding out)
- Identify these words in simple sentences
- Understand that some words appear very often in reading and are learned "by heart"

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1 (Concepts of Print) complete
- Letter recognition for all 26 letters (Unit 1)

## Materials Needed
- Word cards for: **the, a, I, is, it** (large, clear print — one word per card)
- A second set of the same word cards for matching games
- A simple picture book or printed sentences containing these words
- Crayons and paper
- Sand/salt tray or whiteboard (optional, for multi-sensory practice)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge and connect to the new skill.

**Activity: Word Spotting**
Open a picture book to any page. Say: "Today we're going to learn some very special words — words that show up again and again in almost every book. Let me show you one."

Write "the" on a piece of paper in large print. Say: "This word is 'the.' It is one of the most common words in English. Let's see how many times we can find it on this page."

Have the student scan the page and point to every instance of "the." Count them together. Say: "See? It's everywhere! If you can read this one word, you can already read part of every page."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the five Set 1 high-frequency words.

### Key Vocabulary
- **High-frequency word:** A word that appears very, very often in books and writing. We see these words so much that we learn to read them instantly — by heart — without needing to sound them out.
- **Sight word:** Another name for a high-frequency word — a word you know "on sight."

### Instruction Notes

**Why these words are special:**
Say: "Some words are easy to sound out — like 'cat' or 'sit.' You hear the sounds and figure out the word. But some words are tricky — they don't follow the regular sound rules, or they are so short and common that it's faster to just memorize them. These are called sight words. We're going to learn five today."

**Introduce each word one at a time:**

**Word 1: the**
Hold up the card. Say: "This word is 'the.' T-H-E. Say it with me: 'the.'"
Point out: "This word is tricky because 'th' makes a special sound — /th/ — that you can't figure out just from the letter sounds you know. That's why we learn it by heart."
Have the student say "the" three times while looking at the card.

**Word 2: a**
Hold up the card. Say: "This word is 'a.' Just one letter! A. Say it with me: 'a.'"
Point out: "When 'a' is a word all by itself, it sounds like /uh/. 'I see a dog.' It's the smallest word, but it's one of the most important."

**Word 3: I**
Hold up the card. Say: "This word is 'I.' When you talk about yourself, you use 'I.' Notice it's always a capital letter — even in the middle of a sentence."
Have the student say: "I like dogs. I see a cat. I am happy." Emphasize "I" each time.

**Word 4: is**
Hold up the card. Say: "This word is 'is.' I-S. Say it with me: 'is.'"
Use it in context: "The cat is big. The dog is small. My name is ___."

**Word 5: it**
Hold up the card. Say: "This word is 'it.' I-T. Say it with me: 'it.'"
Use it in context: "I see it. It is red. Is it big?"

**Quick comparison — "is" vs. "it":**
Hold both cards up. Say: "These two look very similar. Both start with 'i.' But 'is' ends with S, and 'it' ends with T. Let's look carefully." Point to the last letter of each. "S... T. 'Is'... 'it.' Different ending letter, different word."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Write "I see a cat." on paper. Point to each word. "Which word is 'I'? Which word is 'a'?"

**Example 2:** Write "The cat is it." on paper. Ask: "Can you find the word 'the'? The word 'is'? The word 'it'?"

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice recognizing the five words with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Flash Card Practice (3 minutes)**
Shuffle the five word cards. Hold them up one at a time in random order. The student reads each word as fast as they can.

Go through the stack 3 times. On the first pass, help with any words the student forgets. On the second and third pass, give the student a moment to try before helping.

*Facilitator prompt: "Look at the word. Do you remember it? Take your time."*

*If the student struggles with a word:* Say: "This word is ___. Let's spell it together: [spell it]. Now say the word again: ___. Let's look at it one more time." Place it back in the stack to appear again soon.

**Activity 2: Word Hunt (3 minutes)**
Open the picture book. Say: "I'm going to read this page. Every time you hear one of our five words, tap the table." Read slowly. After reading, go back and have the student point to each sight word on the page.

**Activity 3: Multi-Sensory Writing (4 minutes)**
For each word:
1. The student looks at the word card
2. Says the word aloud
3. Spells the letters aloud while writing the word (in the sand/salt tray, on paper, or in the air)
4. Reads the word they just wrote

Do this for all five words. Say: "When we write a word, it helps our brain remember it. Eyes see it, mouth says it, hand writes it — three ways to learn!"

---

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices reading the words with minimal support.

**Activity: Sentence Reading**
Write these sentences on paper (large, clear print). The student reads each sentence, pointing to each word:

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Read each word card when shown (flash card drill with no help).
- the
- a
- I
- is
- it

**Stretch:** Read simple sentences containing the five words.
1. I see a cat.
2. It is big.
3. The cat is it.
4. I see it.
5. Is it a cat?

Note: The student may need help with "see," "cat," and "big" — these are decodable CVC words. Encourage the student to sound them out using Unit 1 skills, while reading the sight words automatically.

**Challenge:** Match each word card to the correct word in a sentence. Write sentences on paper, leaving blanks. The student places the correct word card in each blank.
- ___ cat is big. (The)
- I see ___ dog. (a)
- ___ is red. (It)
- The ball ___ here. (is)
- ___ like it. (I)

### Interleaved Review
Review concepts of print from Lesson 1: point to a sentence and ask "How many words?" and "Where do I start reading?"

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Show the word card "the." "What is this word?" (the)
2. Show the word card "is." "What is this word?" (is)
3. Show the word card "I." "What is this word?" (I)
4. Write "It is a cat." Ask the student to read the sentence. (Student reads the full sentence, recognizing at least "It," "is," and "a" by sight)

**Mastery = correctly reads at least 4 of the 5 word cards AND reads the sentence with no more than one sight word error.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice the word cards daily (2-3 minutes, just the five words, shuffled)
- Post the word cards on the refrigerator or a wall where the student sees them often
- Play "word bingo" or "word memory" (matching game) with the cards
- Repeat this lesson next session, focusing on the specific words the student missed

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (High-Frequency Words Set 2) next session. Continue reviewing Set 1 words as warm-up in every future lesson — these words must stay automatic.

---

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who learn the five words quickly:**
- Go on a "word safari" — find the five words in books, on cereal boxes, in mail, on signs. The student keeps a tally of how many times they spot each word.
- Write silly sentences using only the sight words and CVC words: "It is a hat. I see the hat. The hat is it!"
- Introduce the concept of a "word wall" — tape the word cards on a wall where the student can see them during reading and writing time. As new words are learned in future lessons, they get added to the wall.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** Most kindergarteners will need 2 sessions to solidly learn all 5 words. If the student remembers 3 words after the first session but forgets 2, that is completely normal. Review and repetition will get them there.

**"Sounding out" vs. "sight reading":** You may notice the student trying to sound out these words using their Unit 1 phonics skills. This is a GOOD sign — it means they are applying what they learned. For decodable words like "is" and "it" (short i + consonant), sounding out works fine. For "the," it doesn't work with basic phonics knowledge. Gently redirect: "Great try sounding it out! This one is a tricky word — it's 'the.' Some words we just have to remember."

**Why "I" is always capitalized:** If the student asks why "I" is always a big letter, explain: "When we write about ourselves using just the letter I, it's always a capital letter. That's a special rule just for the word I."

**The word "a":** This tiny word can confuse students because the letter A has a name (ay) and a common sound (/a/ as in apple), but the word "a" is usually pronounced /uh/ in connected speech. Don't overanalyze this — just model the natural pronunciation in sentences.

**Multi-sensory learning is essential for sight words.** Unlike decodable words (where the student has a phonics strategy), sight words are learned through repeated exposure and memory. The more sensory channels involved (seeing, saying, hearing, writing, tracing), the stronger the memory trace.
