# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 2, Lesson 4

## Topic
Reading Simple Sentences

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read simple sentences composed of high-frequency words and decodable CVC words
- Use finger-pointing to track each word while reading
- Use picture clues and beginning sounds to figure out unfamiliar words
- Demonstrate understanding of what the sentence says (not just decoding the words)

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-3 complete (concepts of print and all 10 high-frequency words)
- CVC blending from Unit 1 (can blend /c/-/a/-/t/ into "cat")

## Materials Needed
- All 10 high-frequency word cards (the, a, I, is, it, and, to, in, we, my)
- Simple sentences written on sentence strips or paper (provided in lesson)
- Simple drawings or pictures to accompany each sentence
- Letter cards from Unit 1 (for building CVC words)
- Pencil and paper

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review all 10 sight words and warm up CVC blending.

**Activity: Two-Part Warm-Up**

Part 1 — Sight Word Speed Drill (2 minutes):
Shuffle all 10 word cards. Student reads each one. Aim for automatic recognition — no sounding out needed.

Part 2 — Sound Blending Review (3 minutes):
Say these sounds slowly. The student blends them into words:
- /c/ - /a/ - /t/ → cat
- /d/ - /o/ - /g/ → dog
- /b/ - /i/ - /g/ → big
- /r/ - /e/ - /d/ → red
- /s/ - /u/ - /n/ → sun

Say: "Great! Today we're going to put your sight words AND your blending skills together to read real sentences."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model how to read a sentence by combining sight word recognition and phonics decoding.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Sentence:** A group of words that tells us something complete. It starts with a capital letter and ends with a period, question mark, or exclamation point.
- **Decode:** Figuring out a word by sounding out the letters

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Model reading a sentence.**
Write this sentence in large print: **I see a cat.**

Draw a simple picture of a cat next to it.

Say: "Watch me read this sentence. I'm going to point to each word as I read it."

Point to "I" — "I know this sight word: 'I.'"
Point to "see" — "Let me sound this out: /s/-/ee/... 'see.' I know the word 'see'!"
Point to "a" — "I know this sight word: 'a.'"
Point to "cat" — "Let me sound this out: /c/-/a/-/t/... 'cat'!"

"Now let me read the whole sentence together smoothly: 'I see a cat.' Does that make sense? Look at the picture — yes, there's a cat! The sentence says I see a cat."

**Step 2: Model a second sentence with a think-aloud.**
Write: **The dog is big.**

Draw a large dog.

"Let me read this one."
- "The" — "Sight word! 'The.'"
- "dog" — "/d/-/o/-/g/... 'dog.'"
- "is" — "Sight word! 'is.'"
- "big" — "/b/-/i/-/g/... 'big.'"
- "The dog is big. That makes sense — look at the picture, the dog IS big!"

**Step 3: Teach the strategy.**
Say: "Here's what good readers do when they read a sentence:
1. Point to the first word.
2. Ask yourself: Is this a sight word I know? If yes, say it!
3. If it's not a sight word, look at the letters and sound it out.
4. Move to the next word. Point, read. Point, read.
5. When you finish the sentence, go back and read it smoothly — all the words together.
6. Ask yourself: Does it make sense?"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Write: **It is a red hat.** (Draw a red hat.)
Guide the student through each word:
- "It" — sight word → "it"
- "is" — sight word → "is"
- "a" — sight word → "a"
- "red" — sound out → /r/-/e/-/d/ → "red"
- "hat" — sound out → /h/-/a/-/t/ → "hat"
- Read it all together: "It is a red hat." Check the picture — yes!

**Example 2:** Write: **My dog can sit.** (Draw a dog sitting.)
- "My" — sight word → "my"
- "dog" — sound out → /d/-/o/-/g/ → "dog"
- "can" — sound out → /c/-/a/-/n/ → "can"
- "sit" — sound out → /s/-/i/-/t/ → "sit"
- Together: "My dog can sit."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads sentences with facilitator support.

**Activity: Read Together**
Write these sentences on paper (large, clear print). Draw a simple picture next to each. The student reads each sentence with your support.

1. **I see a big dog.** (picture: large dog)
2. **The cat is in the box.** (picture: cat in a box)
3. **We like to run and hop.** (picture: children running and hopping)
4. **My hat is red.** (picture: red hat)
5. **Is it a bug?** (picture: a bug)

For each sentence:
- Student points to each word
- Reads sight words automatically
- Sounds out CVC words
- Reads the sentence again smoothly
- Tells you what the sentence means

*Facilitator prompts:*
- "Is that a sight word you know, or do you need to sound it out?"
- "Look at the first letter. What sound does it make? Now blend the sounds."
- "Read it again, a little faster this time — make it sound like talking."
- "What did that sentence tell us?"

*If the student struggles with a word:*
- For sight words: "Remember, this one is ___. You learned it in Lesson 2/3."
- For CVC words: "Let's sound it out together: /___/ - /___/ - /___/. What word is that?"
- If the student is stuck and frustrated: "Look at the picture. What do you think this word might be? Let's check — does the first letter match?"

---

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads sentences with minimal support.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Read these sentences with finger-pointing. Facilitator helps with non-CVC words only.
1. The cat sat on a mat.
2. I can see the sun.
3. My dog is big and fat.

**Stretch:** Read these slightly longer sentences.
1. We like to run in the mud and get wet.
2. I see a big red bus and a van.
3. My cat and my dog sit in the sun.

**Challenge:** The student reads a sentence, then draws a picture that matches what the sentence says (demonstrating comprehension, not just decoding).
- Read: "The fat cat sat on a red hat."
- Draw what you read.
- Read: "I see a big dog in my bed."
- Draw what you read.

### Interleaved Review
After reading sentences, do a quick sight word check: shuffle word cards and have the student read 5 cards chosen at random.

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Write "I see a big red bug." Student reads the sentence aloud with finger-pointing. (Target: reads sight words automatically, sounds out "big," "red," "bug")
2. "What does this sentence tell us?" Student explains the meaning. (Target: "I can see a bug that is big and red" or similar)
3. Write "My cat is in the box." Student reads independently. (Target: reads all words correctly, demonstrating both sight word recognition and CVC decoding)

**Mastery = reads both sentences with no more than 1 error AND explains the meaning of at least one sentence.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice reading the guided practice sentences daily (same sentences — repeated reading builds fluency)
- If the struggle is with sight words, return to Lessons 2-3 flash card practice
- If the struggle is with CVC decoding, review Unit 1 Lesson 4 blending activities
- If the student can decode words individually but struggles to read them in a sentence, practice with 2-word phrases first ("a cat," "the dog," "is big") before building to full sentences

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 5 (Retelling Familiar Stories) next session. Continue sentence reading practice as warm-up.

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who read sentences fluently:**
- Write silly sentences for the student to read and illustrate: "A big red cat sat on my hat and a bug!"
- Have the student dictate a sentence for you to write, then read it back to you
- Read a page from a simple picture book together, with the student reading as many words as possible
- Introduce the concept of question sentences — they end with a question mark and ask something: "Is it a cat?" vs. "It is a cat."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is a milestone lesson.** The student is reading connected text for the first time. Even if the reading is slow, halting, and requires help on several words, this is genuine reading. Celebrate it explicitly: "You just read a sentence! You are a reader!"

**Speed is not the goal.** Accuracy and comprehension are. A student who slowly and carefully reads "I see a cat" and can tell you what it means is doing better than a student who rattles off the words quickly with no understanding.

**The "Does it make sense?" habit:** Start building this now. Every time the student finishes a sentence, ask: "Does that make sense?" This teaches self-monitoring — the most important reading comprehension strategy. If the student reads "I see a cat" as "I see a car" but the picture shows a cat, the "does it make sense?" question prompts them to self-correct.

**Mixed strategy reading is expected.** The student will sight-read some words and sound out others. That is exactly how beginning reading works. Over time, as more words become automatic through repeated exposure, the ratio shifts toward sight reading. Do not push the student to stop sounding out — let the transition happen naturally.

**CVC words the student should be able to decode:** cat, hat, bat, mat, sat, dog, log, hog, big, pig, wig, red, bed, bug, rug, mug, sun, run, fun, bun, box, fox, cup, pup. If a sentence includes a non-CVC word (like, run, hop, see, get, wet), provide it for the student and explain: "This word is ___. We'll learn how to read words like this later."

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice:**
1. I see a big dog.
2. The cat is in the box.
3. We like to run and hop. (help with "like," "run," "hop")
4. My hat is red.
5. Is it a bug?

**Independent Practice — On-level:**
1. The cat sat on a mat.
2. I can see the sun. (help with "can," "see")
3. My dog is big and fat.

**Independent Practice — Stretch:**
1. We like to run in the mud and get wet.
2. I see a big red bus and a van.
3. My cat and my dog sit in the sun.

**Exit Ticket:**
1. "I see a big red bug." (I = sight, see = help or known, a = sight, big = /b/-/i/-/g/, red = /r/-/e/-/d/, bug = /b/-/u/-/g/)
2. Any response showing the student understands the sentence is about seeing a big red bug.
3. "My cat is in the box." (My = sight, cat = decode, is = sight, in = sight, the = sight, box = decode)
