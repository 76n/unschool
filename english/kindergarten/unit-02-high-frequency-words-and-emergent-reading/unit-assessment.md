# Unit Assessment — Kindergarten ELA, Unit 2: High-Frequency Words & Emergent Reading

**Grade:** Kindergarten
**Unit:** 2 — High-Frequency Words & Emergent Reading
**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** One-on-one with facilitator (oral reading, pointing tasks, and retelling — not a written test)
**Estimated Duration:** 20-25 minutes
**Materials:** All 10 high-frequency word cards, a printed copy of the assessment story ("My Pet"), paper and pencil, Beginning/Middle/End cards (optional support for retelling section)

---

## Assessment Overview

This assessment covers all Unit 2 learning objectives:

| Section | Skill Assessed | Lesson Source | Items |
|---------|---------------|---------------|-------|
| A | Concepts of print | Lesson 1 | 4 |
| B | High-frequency word recognition | Lessons 2-3 | 10 |
| C | Sentence reading (sight words + CVC decoding) | Lesson 4 | 3 |
| D | Story reading | Lesson 6 | 1 (scored on 4 criteria) |
| E | Story retelling (characters, setting, events) | Lessons 5-6 | 4 |
| -- | **Total** | -- | **25** |

**Mastery threshold:** 85% overall (21 of 25 points) AND at least 1 correct item in each section.

---

## Administration Instructions

This assessment is entirely oral and hands-on. Read each instruction aloud. Record the student's responses. For reading items, listen to the student read aloud and note accuracy.

There are no time limits. Let the student work at their own pace. If the student is stuck for more than 10 seconds on a word, say: "Let's move on to the next one." For retelling, give the student time to think — if they pause for more than 15 seconds, offer a gentle prompt: "What else do you remember?"

Do NOT provide hints, corrections, or word-reading help during the assessment. If the student asks for help on a word, say: "Just try your best. You can skip it if you're not sure."

Create a comfortable atmosphere. Say: "We're going to do some reading and story-telling today. Some parts might be easy and some might be a little tricky. Just show me what you can do — you've been learning so much!"

## Section A: Concepts of Print (Lesson 1)

Use any page from a picture book with at least two lines of text.

**A1.** Hand the student the book. "Show me where I should start reading on this page."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Points to the first word on the top-left of the text area | 1 point |
| Points to the picture, the title, or the wrong location | 0 points |

**A2.** "Now show me which direction I read — which way do I go?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Indicates or sweeps left to right | 1 point |
| Indicates right to left, top to bottom only, or random direction | 0 points |

**A3.** Point to a sentence on the page. "How many words are in this sentence?" (Choose a sentence with 4-6 words.)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correctly counts the number of words (pointing to each one) | 1 point |
| Incorrect count (counts letters, skips words, or guesses) | 0 points |

**A4.** Point to a single letter within a word, then point to a full word. "Which one is a letter and which one is a word?"

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correctly identifies the letter and the word | 1 point |
| Reverses them or cannot distinguish | 0 points |

## Section C: Sentence Reading (Lesson 4)

Write each sentence in large, clear print on paper. The student reads each sentence aloud, pointing to each word. Score based on accuracy of sight word recognition and CVC decoding.

**C1.** "I see a big red bug."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Reads the sentence with no more than 1 word error (reads at least 5 of 6 words correctly) | 1 point |
| 2 or more word errors | 0 points |

**C2.** "My cat is in the box."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Reads with no more than 1 error (at least 5 of 6 words correct) | 1 point |
| 2 or more errors | 0 points |

**C3.** "We like to run and hop."

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Reads with no more than 1 error (at least 5 of 6 words correct) | 1 point |
| 2 or more errors | 0 points |

Note: "see," "like," "run," and "hop" are not from the taught sight word or CVC sets. If the student sounds them out or reads them incorrectly, count this as the allowed 1 error. The primary target is sight word recognition within sentences.

## Section D: Story Reading (Lesson 6)

Write the following story in large, clear print. Draw a simple picture next to each sentence.

---

**My Pet**

I have a pet. *(picture: child with a small animal)*

It is a fat cat. *(picture: a round, fat cat)*

My cat can sit and nap. *(picture: cat sitting, then napping)*

The cat is in my lap. *(picture: cat in child's lap)*

I like my cat a lot! *(picture: child smiling, hugging cat)*

---

Say: "Here is a little story. Please read it aloud to me. Point to each word as you read."

Score the story reading on 4 criteria:

**D1. Sight word accuracy:** How many of the sight words does the student read correctly? (Sight words in the story: I, a, it, is, a, my, and, the, is, in, my, I, my, a, to — 15 sight word instances using 7 unique words)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Reads 12 or more sight word instances correctly (80%+) | 1 point |
| Reads fewer than 12 correctly | 0 points |

**D2. CVC decoding:** How well does the student decode the CVC words? (CVC words: pet, fat, cat, sit, nap, cat, lap, cat, lot — 9 instances)

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Correctly decodes 7 or more CVC word instances (some may be slow/sounded out — that counts) | 1 point |
| Decodes fewer than 7 correctly | 0 points |

**D3. Tracking:** Does the student point to each word accurately while reading?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Points to each word in sequence, left to right, with accurate one-to-one correspondence for most of the story | 1 point |
| Frequently points to the wrong word, skips words, or does not track | 0 points |

**D4. Fluency and self-monitoring:** Does the reading sound like natural speech (at least some of the time)? Does the student notice and attempt to self-correct errors?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| At least some sentences are read smoothly (not purely word-by-word throughout). Student self-corrects at least once OR makes no errors to correct. | 1 point |
| Entire story is read in a halting word-by-word manner with no self-correction attempts when errors occur | 0 points |

## Section E: Story Retelling (Lessons 5-6)

After the student reads "My Pet," say: "Great reading! Now close the story (turn the paper over) and tell me about it. Retell the story in your own words."

If the student needs support, you may offer ONE prompt: "Use your retelling hand — who, where, beginning, middle, end." Do not provide additional prompts or questions during the retelling.

**E1. Characters:** Does the student identify the characters?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Names or describes at least 1 character (the child/narrator, the cat) | 1 point |
| Does not mention any character | 0 points |

**E2. Setting:** Does the student identify where the story happens?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Describes or implies the setting (at home, in the child's lap, etc.) | 1 point |
| Does not mention or imply a setting | 0 points |

**E3. Events:** Does the student describe what happens in the story?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Mentions at least 2 events from the story (e.g., the cat sits and naps, the cat is in the child's lap, the child likes the cat) | 1 point |
| Mentions 0-1 events | 0 points |

**E4. Sequence:** Are the events described in approximately correct order?

| Response | Score |
|----------|-------|
| Events are described in roughly correct sequence (beginning information before ending information) | 1 point |
| Events are described in random or reversed order, or only 1 event is mentioned (sequence not assessable) | 0 points |

## Rubric and Mastery Determination

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Next Steps |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Mastered** | 21-25 (85-100%) with at least 1 correct in each section | Student has solid emergent reading skills: sight words, print concepts, decoding, and comprehension | Advance to Unit 3 (Writing & Language Foundations). Continue daily reading of simple texts and sight word review. |
| **Approaching** | 18-20 (70-84%) | Partial mastery — most skills are developing but some areas need reinforcement | Identify weak sections and reteach those specific skills (1-2 sessions each). The student may begin Unit 3 while continuing to practice weak areas from Unit 2. |
| **Developing** | Below 18 (<70%) | Significant gaps in one or more foundational areas | Return to the weakest section and reteach from that lesson forward. Do not advance to Unit 3 until at least "Approaching" level is reached. |

## Section-Specific Reteaching Guide

| If the student struggles in... | What it tells you | Reteach with... |
|-------------------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Section A (Concepts of Print)** | The student has not internalized how print works on a page | Daily read-alouds where the student points to words. Use green/red dot cues at line starts and ends. Practice counting words in spoken sentences (hold up fingers). Return to Lesson 1 activities. |
| **Section B (Sight Words)** | The student has not memorized enough high-frequency words for automatic recognition | Return to Lessons 2-3. Use the see-say-spell-write routine daily. Post word cards in visible locations. Play matching and memory games. Focus on the specific missed words. Consider reducing to 3 words at a time if 5 is overwhelming. |
| **Section C (Sentence Reading)** | The student can read words in isolation but struggles with connected text | Practice reading the same short sentences repeatedly (re-reading builds fluency). Start with 2-word phrases before building to full sentences. Use finger-pointing consistently. Return to Lesson 4 practice sentences. |
| **Section D (Story Reading)** | The student cannot sustain reading across multiple sentences | Continue practicing with "Sam the Cat" and "The Red Bug" from Lesson 6 until fluent. Re-read the same story daily. If decoding is the primary issue, return to Unit 1 blending practice. If sight words are the issue, return to Sections B reteaching. |
| **Section E (Retelling)** | The student reads words but does not process meaning / cannot retell | Pause after each sentence during read-alouds and ask "What did that sentence tell us?" Use the retelling hand and Beginning/Middle/End cards. Practice retelling familiar fairy tales before attempting retelling of newly-read stories. Return to Lesson 5 activities. |

## Detailed Rubric by Skill

### Concepts of Print (Section A)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Directionality | Consistently starts at the top left and tracks left to right without prompting | Usually starts correctly but occasionally needs reminding; may not track consistently across lines | Does not reliably start at the top left or track left to right |
| Word concept | Correctly identifies individual words, counts words in sentences, distinguishes letters from words | Can point to individual words when asked but sometimes counts letters as words or miscounts | Cannot reliably distinguish a word from a letter or count words in a sentence |

### High-Frequency Word Recognition (Section B)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Automatic recognition | Reads 9-10 of the 10 words correctly within 3 seconds each | Reads 7-8 words correctly, some with slight hesitation | Reads fewer than 7 words correctly; relies on sounding out or guessing |
| Confusable words | Correctly distinguishes similar words (is/it/in) | Occasionally confuses similar words but self-corrects when looking more carefully | Frequently confuses similar words and does not notice the difference |

### Sentence and Story Reading (Sections C-D)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Accuracy | Reads sentences with high accuracy (90%+ words correct) | Reads most words correctly but makes errors on 2-3 words per sentence | Makes frequent errors; struggles with both sight words and CVC decoding in connected text |
| Tracking | Accurately points to each word while reading with one-to-one correspondence | Points to most words correctly but occasionally gets out of sync | Cannot reliably point to the correct word while reading |
| Fluency | Some sentences are read smoothly; shows signs of natural phrasing | Reads entirely word-by-word but with consistent accuracy | Reading is labored; long pauses between words; frequent stops |
| Self-monitoring | Notices when something doesn't make sense; attempts to self-correct | Occasionally notices errors but does not always attempt correction | Does not notice errors; no self-correction attempts |

### Story Retelling (Section E)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Characters | Names both the narrator and the cat without prompting | Names one character; mentions the other only with prompting | Cannot name or describe any character from the story |
| Setting | Describes where the story takes place | Gives a vague or partial description of the setting | Does not mention or describe the setting |
| Events | Describes 3+ events from the story | Describes 1-2 events | Cannot describe any specific events |
| Sequence | Retells events in correct chronological order | Retells events but some are out of order | Lists events randomly or cannot maintain any sequence |

## Cumulative Review Items

These are NOT scored but provide diagnostic information for planning Unit 3:

**R1.** After the retelling, ask: "What did you like about this story?" (Assesses personal response — a precursor to opinion writing in Unit 3.)

**R2.** "Can you say a sentence about a pet? Any sentence you want." (Assesses oral language composition — the foundation for written composition in Unit 3.)

**R3.** "Can you write one word from the story?" (Any word — the student's name, "cat," "my," etc.) (Assesses writing readiness for Unit 3.)

Note responses — they help calibrate pacing and support level for Unit 3.

---

## Facilitator Observations (Record During Assessment)

- Does the student approach reading with confidence or reluctance?
- Which sight words are automatic vs. require thought?
- Does the student use phonics to decode unfamiliar words, or does the student guess based on first letter or pictures?
- Does the student's reading sound word-by-word throughout, or is some phrasing emerging?
- Does the student retell eagerly or reluctantly?
- Does the retelling include the student's own words and reactions, or is it a rote recitation?
- Does the student connect the story to personal experience ("I have a cat too!")?

These observations help shape the approach to Unit 3 and inform differentiation decisions.
