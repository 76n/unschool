# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 2, Lesson 6

## Topic
Reading and Retelling — Putting It All Together

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read a simple story composed of high-frequency words and decodable CVC words
- Retell the story including characters, setting, and major events in sequence
- Demonstrate all Unit 2 skills together: concepts of print, sight word recognition, phonics decoding, and comprehension

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-5 complete
- All 10 high-frequency words at or near automatic recognition
- Can retell a story (characters, setting, beginning/middle/end)

## Materials Needed
- All 10 high-frequency word cards (for warm-up)
- The "Sam the Cat" story (provided below) — write it out in large, clear print on paper or sentence strips, one sentence per line, with simple illustrations
- Beginning/Middle/End cards from Lesson 5
- Crayons and paper
- A new short picture book for guided practice (optional)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick cumulative review of all Unit 2 skills.

**Activity: Triple Check**

Part 1 — Sight Words (2 minutes): Shuffle all 10 word cards. Student reads each. Target: all 10 correct with minimal hesitation.

Part 2 — CVC Blending (1 minute): Blend these: /m/-/a/-/p/ → map, /h/-/o/-/t/ → hot, /p/-/e/-/n/ → pen

Part 3 — Concepts of Print (2 minutes): Open a book to any page. Student points to where reading starts, demonstrates left-to-right tracking, counts the words in one sentence, and points to one letter and one word.

Say: "You've practiced all these skills separately. Today you're going to use them ALL TOGETHER to read a little story and tell it back to me."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Model reading a short story and retelling it.

### The "Sam the Cat" Story

Write this story on paper in large, clear print. Draw a simple picture next to each sentence.

---

*Sam the Cat*

Sam is a big cat. *(picture: a large cat)*

Sam is in my bed. *(picture: cat lying on a bed)*

I see Sam and I pat him. *(picture: child patting the cat)*

Sam can run and hop. *(picture: cat running and jumping)*

We like Sam a lot! *(picture: child hugging the cat)*

---

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Model reading the story.**
Say: "I'm going to read this little story. Watch how I point to each word and think about what's happening."

Read each sentence aloud, pointing to each word. Think aloud:
- "Sam is a big cat." → "So Sam is a character — a big cat."
- "Sam is in my bed." → "The setting is my bed — that's where Sam is."
- "I see Sam and I pat him." → "Now I'm petting Sam."
- "Sam can run and hop." → "Sam is active — he runs and hops!"
- "We like Sam a lot!" → "The ending tells us how we feel about Sam — we like him!"

**Step 2: Model the retelling.**
Say: "Now I'm going to retell this story. I'll use my retelling hand."
- Thumb (Characters): "The characters are Sam the cat and the person telling the story — 'I.'"
- Pointer (Setting): "The setting is at home, in the bedroom."
- Middle finger (Beginning): "In the beginning, we learn that Sam is a big cat and he's on the bed."
- Ring finger (Middle): "In the middle, the person pets Sam, and Sam can run and hop."
- Pinky (End): "At the end, we learn that everyone likes Sam a lot!"

**Step 3: Highlight the connection between reading and retelling.**
Say: "See what we just did? First, I read the words carefully — using sight words and sounding out. Then I thought about what the story means — who, where, what happened. Reading is BOTH of those things: saying the words AND understanding the story."

---

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads the story and retells it with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Student Reads "Sam the Cat" (5 minutes)**
The student reads the story aloud, pointing to each word.

For each sentence:
- Let the student try every word before helping
- For sight words (is, a, in, my, I, and, we): student should recognize on sight
- For CVC words (Sam, big, cat, bed, pat, him, can, run, hop, lot): student sounds out
- For other words (see, like): provide if needed, or let the student try

After the student reads each sentence, ask: "What did that sentence tell us?"

After reading the whole story, say: "Great reading! Now let's retell it."

*Facilitator prompts:*
- "Is that a sight word? Yes — read it quickly!"
- "Sound it out: what's the first sound? The middle? The end? Blend them together."
- "Read that sentence one more time, smooth and fast this time."
- "Does that make sense?"

**Activity 2: Student Retells "Sam the Cat" (5 minutes)**
Lay out the Beginning/Middle/End cards. Guide the student:

- "Who are the characters?" (Sam the cat and the narrator/I)
- "Where does the story happen?" (At home, in the bedroom)
- Point to Beginning: "What did we learn at the start?" (Sam is a big cat, he's on the bed)
- Point to Middle: "What happens in the middle?" (The person pets Sam, Sam runs and hops)
- Point to End: "How does the story end?" (Everyone likes Sam)

Then: "Now retell the whole story to me, start to finish, in your own words."

---

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads and retells with minimal support.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Student re-reads "Sam the Cat" independently (no facilitator help) and retells it using the Beginning/Middle/End cards.

**Stretch:** Student reads a NEW short story (provided below) and retells it.

*The Red Bug*

A bug is on my hat. *(picture: bug on a hat)*

It is a red bug. *(picture: red bug close up)*

The bug can hop and run. *(picture: bug hopping)*

I like the red bug! *(picture: child smiling at the bug)*

After reading, retell: Who? Where? What happened?

**Challenge:** Student writes (or dictates) their OWN 3-4 sentence story about a pet or animal. Then reads it aloud and retells it. Use sight words and CVC words.

Example template:
- ___ is a ___. (character introduction)
- ___ is in ___. (setting)
- ___ can ___. (event)
- I like ___! (ending)

### Interleaved Review
After reading and retelling, review 5 letter sounds from Unit 1 to maintain phonics foundations.

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Comprehensive formative assessment for Unit 2 skills.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Student reads a sentence not seen before: "My dog is in the big red bed." (Tests sight words + CVC decoding + tracking)
2. "Tell me about the story you read today. Who was it about? Where did it happen? What happened in the beginning, middle, and end?" (Tests comprehension and retelling)

**Mastery = reads the new sentence with no more than 1 error AND retells the story with at least: 1 character named, setting described, and 1 event from each story part.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Continue daily practice reading "Sam the Cat" and "The Red Bug" until fluent
- Review specific weak areas:
  - Sight words not automatic → return to Lessons 2-3 flash card practice
  - CVC decoding struggles → return to Unit 1 blending practice
  - Retelling weak → use picture walk-throughs and the retelling hand with familiar fairy tales
- Do NOT move to Unit 3 until the student can read simple sentences and retell a story at a basic level

### If Mastery Is Met
The student is ready for the Unit 2 Assessment. After passing the assessment, advance to Unit 3 (Writing & Language Foundations).

---

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who read and retell confidently:**
- Read a simple leveled reader together (if available) and retell it
- Change the "Sam the Cat" story — make it about a dog, a fish, or the student's own pet. Read the new version together.
- Start a "reading log" — each day the student reads a short text and draws/writes about what happened in the story
- Play "character interview" — pretend to be Sam the cat and answer questions: "Sam, where do you like to sleep? What do you like to do?"

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This is a celebration lesson.** The student is reading a real story and understanding it. Even if the reading is slow and the retelling is brief, this is a major milestone. The student has gone from knowing letter sounds to reading and comprehending connected text in just six lessons. Acknowledge this explicitly.

**Re-reading is powerful.** Having the student re-read "Sam the Cat" multiple times is intentional. Re-reading builds fluency (reading becomes smoother and faster), confidence (the student feels successful), and comprehension (the student notices details they missed the first time). It is not "boring" or "too easy" — it is one of the most effective reading strategies at this level.

**The stories in this lesson are deliberately simple.** They use only high-frequency words and CVC words the student can decode. This is by design — the student needs to experience success reading connected text before encountering more complex words. Complexity will increase gradually in later units.

**Writing their own story (Challenge tier):** If the student attempts to write their own story, accept invented spelling enthusiastically. A student who writes "mi dg is bg" for "My dog is big" is demonstrating phonemic awareness, letter-sound correspondence, AND composition skills simultaneously. This is impressive, not something that needs correction. Correct spelling will develop with time and exposure.

**Transition to Unit 3:** Unit 3 (Writing & Language Foundations) builds directly on this lesson. Students who can read "I see a cat" are ready to write "I see a dog." The reading-writing connection is strong at this stage — each skill reinforces the other.

## Answer Key

**"Sam the Cat" Retelling Guide:**
- Characters: Sam (a cat) and the narrator ("I"/the child)
- Setting: At home, in the bedroom
- Beginning: Sam is a big cat. He is on the bed.
- Middle: The narrator sees Sam and pats him. Sam can run and hop.
- End: They (we) like Sam a lot.

**"The Red Bug" Retelling Guide:**
- Characters: A red bug and the narrator ("I")
- Setting: On a hat / outside
- Beginning: There is a bug on the narrator's hat.
- Middle: The bug is red. It can hop and run.
- End: The narrator likes the red bug.

**Exit Ticket:**
1. "My dog is in the big red bed." — sight words: my, is, in, the; CVC words: dog, big, red, bed
2. Retelling should include Sam (character), home/bed (setting), and at least 1 event per story section.
