# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 2, Lesson 3

## Topic
High-Frequency Words Set 2: and, to, in, we, my

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Read the words **and**, **to**, **in**, **we**, and **my** on sight
- Read all 10 high-frequency words from Sets 1 and 2 (the, a, I, is, it, and, to, in, we, my)
- Identify these words within simple sentences

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 2 complete (Set 1 words: the, a, I, is, it — mastered or approaching mastery)
- Concepts of print (Lesson 1)

## Materials Needed
- Word cards for Set 2: **and, to, in, we, my** (one word per card, large clear print)
- Word cards from Set 1 (the, a, I, is, it) for cumulative review
- Paper and pencil or crayons
- Sand/salt tray or whiteboard (optional)
- A simple picture book or printed sentences

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review Set 1 words and build confidence before adding new words.

**Activity: Speed Round Review**
Shuffle the five Set 1 word cards. Hold them up one at a time. The student reads each word as quickly as possible.

Say: "Let's warm up with the words you already know. Ready? Read each word when I hold it up."

Go through the stack twice. Celebrate quick, accurate responses: "You read that so fast! You really know that word."

If the student hesitates on any Set 1 word, briefly review it: "Remember, this one is ___. Let's say it together." If the student misses more than 2 of the Set 1 words, consider spending this session reviewing Set 1 instead of introducing Set 2.

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the five Set 2 high-frequency words.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "You already know five sight words. Today we're adding five more. By the end of today, you'll know TEN words — and that's enough to start reading lots of sentences!"

**Introduce each word one at a time:**

**Word 1: and**
Hold up the card. Say: "This word is 'and.' A-N-D. Say it with me: 'and.'"
Use in context: "I like cats and dogs. You and I can read. Red and blue."
Point out: "You can actually sound this one out: /a/-/n/-/d/. It follows the rules! But it comes up so often that it's faster to know it by heart."

**Word 2: to**
Hold up the card. Say: "This word is 'to.' T-O. Say it with me: 'to.'"
Use in context: "I go to school. We like to play. Give it to me."
Point out: "This word is a little tricky — the O says /oo/, not /o/. That's why we learn it as a sight word."

**Word 3: in**
Hold up the card. Say: "This word is 'in.' I-N. Say it with me: 'in.'"
Use in context: "The cat is in the box. I am in my room. Put it in the bag."
Point out: "You can sound this one out too: /i/-/n/. Short and simple."

**Word 4: we**
Hold up the card. Say: "This word is 'we.' W-E. Say it with me: 'we.'"
Use in context: "We like to read. We go to the park. We see a dog."
Point out: "'I' means just me. 'We' means me AND someone else — you and I together."

**Word 5: my**
Hold up the card. Say: "This word is 'my.' M-Y. Say it with me: 'my.'"
Use in context: "This is my book. My cat is big. I like my dog."
Point out: "The Y at the end of 'my' makes the /eye/ sound. That's a special thing Y does sometimes."

**Quick comparison — easily confused pairs:**
- "in" vs. "is" vs. "it": All start with 'i.' Hold all three cards up. "In ends with N. Is ends with S. It ends with T. Look at that last letter — that's how you tell them apart."
- "we" vs. "my": "We is about US — you and me. My is about ME — something that belongs to me."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** Write "We go to the park." Point to each word. Ask: "Can you find 'we'? Can you find 'to'? Can you find 'the'?"

**Example 2:** Write "My cat and I sit in the sun." Ask: "How many sight words can you find? Let's circle them." (my, and, I, in, the — five sight words)

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice all 10 words together.

**Activity 1: All Ten Flash Cards (4 minutes)**
Shuffle all 10 word cards (Sets 1 and 2 combined). The student reads each card.

Go through the stack twice. On the first pass, help with new words as needed. On the second pass, let the student try each word independently for 3-4 seconds before helping.

Keep a mental note of which words are automatic (read instantly) and which require hesitation or help. The hesitation words need more practice.

**Activity 2: Sentence Building (3 minutes)**
Lay all 10 word cards on the table. Say: "Let's build sentences using our word cards."

Build these together by placing cards in order:
1. "I see the cat." (use "I" and "the" cards; write "see" and "cat" on scrap paper to fill gaps)
2. "We go to my house." (use "we," "to," and "my" cards)
3. "It is in the bag." (use "it," "is," "in," "the" cards)

The student reads each sentence after building it.

**Activity 3: Multi-Sensory Practice for New Words (3 minutes)**
For the 2-3 words the student found hardest during flash card practice:
1. Look at the word card
2. Say the word
3. Spell the letters aloud
4. Write the word (in sand, on paper, or in the air)
5. Say the word again
6. Close eyes and spell it from memory

---

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student reads all 10 words and sentences containing them with minimal support.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Read each of the 10 word cards when shown (flash card drill).

**Stretch:** Read these sentences (pointing to each word):
1. I see a big cat and a dog.
2. We go to the park.
3. It is in my bag.
4. My cat is in the box.
5. I like to see the sun and the moon.

Note: Words not in the sight word sets (see, big, cat, dog, go, park, bag, box, like, sun, moon) — encourage sounding out for CVC words and provide help for others.

**Challenge:** The student dictates a sentence using at least 3 of the new sight words. The facilitator writes it. Then the student reads it back, pointing to each word. Example: "We go to my school and I like it."

### Interleaved Review
- Review 3-4 letter sounds from Unit 1 (show letter cards, student says the sound)
- Practice concepts of print: "Where do I start reading? Which way do I go?"

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Shuffle all 10 word cards. Student reads each one. Record which words are read correctly.
2. Write "We like to go in my car." Student reads the sentence. (Target: recognizes at least the sight words "we," "to," "in," "my" within the sentence)

**Mastery = reads at least 8 of 10 word cards correctly AND reads the sentence recognizing at least 3 of the 4 target sight words.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Separate the known words from unknown words. Practice the unknown words 3 times daily using the see-say-spell-write routine.
- Post new word cards next to Set 1 cards in a visible location.
- Play matching/memory games with the word cards.
- Read the practice sentences together daily, with the student pointing to each word.
- Repeat this lesson focusing on missed words before moving to Lesson 4.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Reading Simple Sentences) next session. Continue reviewing all 10 words as warm-up.

---

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who learn the ten words quickly:**
- Create a personal "word ring" — punch a hole in each word card and put them on a metal ring or piece of yarn. The student can flip through and practice anywhere.
- Play "word snap" — two players each flip a card from the shuffled stack. If they match, say the word and snap! First to snap wins both cards.
- Start a "words I can read" journal. Each day the student writes one sentence using sight words and draws a picture to go with it.
- Look for the 10 words in environmental print — store signs, food labels, book covers.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** Adding 5 new words while maintaining 5 old words is cognitively demanding for kindergarteners. If the student learns 3 new words solidly but struggles with 2, that is a successful session. Prioritize retention over speed.

**The cumulative challenge:** From this point forward, every lesson reviews ALL previously learned sight words. This spaced review is essential. Without it, students learn new words but forget old ones. The warm-up review is not optional — it is a core part of the learning process.

**"and" and "in" are decodable:** These words follow regular phonics patterns. If the student sounds them out, that is perfectly fine and should be encouraged. Over time, they will transition from sounding out to automatic recognition simply through repeated exposure.

**The word "to":** This word has an irregular pronunciation (the O says /oo/, not the expected /o/). Students who try to sound it out may say "toh." Gently correct: "Good try sounding it out! This word is actually 'to' — it's one of those tricky ones."

**Building confidence:** By the end of this lesson, the student knows 10 words. Take a moment to celebrate: "You can read TEN words now! That means you can start reading real sentences in real books. Let's find a page where you know most of the words." This matters — the student needs to feel like a reader, not just a memorizer.
