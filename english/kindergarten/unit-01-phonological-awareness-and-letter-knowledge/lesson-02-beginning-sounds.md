# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 1, Lesson 2

## Topic
Isolating Beginning Sounds

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify the beginning sound (onset) of a spoken word
- Match words that share the same beginning sound

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: Rhyming Words (student can recognize that words have distinct sound parts)

## Materials Needed
- 12-15 picture cards or small objects with clear beginning sounds (e.g., ball, bat, bus; cat, cup, cow; mouse, mop, moon; sun, soap, sock; fish, fan, fork)
- Two plates, bowls, or paper circles for sorting
- Alphabet chart (optional — for reference, not primary instruction)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge and review rhyming from Lesson 1.

**Spaced Review — Rhyming**
Say: "Before we learn something new, let's remember our rhyming words from last time."

1. "Do **cake** and **lake** rhyme?" (Yes)
2. "Do **tree** and **book** rhyme?" (No)
3. "Tell me a word that rhymes with **top**." (hop, mop, stop, pop, drop, etc.)

Say: "Great work! You remember that rhyming words sound the same at the END. Today, we are going to listen to the BEGINNING of words instead."

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the concept of isolating the first sound in a word.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Beginning sound:** The very first sound you hear when you say a word
- **Sound (phoneme):** A single sound your mouth makes — like /m/ or /s/ or /b/

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce beginning sounds.**
Say: "Every word starts with a sound. Listen carefully and I'll show you."

Say the word "mouse" slowly, stretching the first sound: "**Mmmmm**ouse. The first sound in 'mouse' is /m/. My lips press together: /mmm/."

Say the word "sun" slowly: "**Sssss**un. The first sound in 'sun' is /s/. The air comes out like a snake: /sss/."

Say the word "fish" slowly: "**Fffff**ish. The first sound in 'fish' is /f/. My top teeth touch my bottom lip: /fff/."

**Step 2: Show the student how to isolate the sound.**
Say: "When I want to find the beginning sound, I say the word very slowly and listen for what my mouth does first."

Model with "ball": "Bbbball. /b/ — that's the beginning sound. I feel my lips pop apart."

Model with "lake": "Llllake. /l/ — that's the beginning sound. I feel my tongue go up behind my top teeth."

**Step 3: Practice the question format.**
Say: "I'm going to ask you a question a lot today: 'What sound does _____ start with?' Let me show you how to answer."

"What sound does 'moon' start with?" Think aloud: "Mmmoon... /m/! Moon starts with /m/."

"What sound does 'dog' start with?" Think aloud: "Dddog... /d/! Dog starts with /d/."

**Tip for facilitators:** Start with continuant sounds (/m/, /s/, /f/, /l/, /n/, /r/, /z/) — these can be stretched and held, making them easier for the student to hear. Stop sounds (/b/, /d/, /t/, /k/, /g/, /p/) are harder to isolate because they are brief. Introduce stop sounds after the student has success with continuant sounds.

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "What sound does **sit** start with?"
Think aloud: "I say the word slowly: sssssit. The first sound I hear is /s/. Sit starts with /s/."

**Example 2:** "What sound does **top** start with?"
Think aloud: "I say the word slowly: t-top. The first thing my mouth does is /t/ — my tongue taps behind my top teeth. Top starts with /t/."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice isolating beginning sounds together with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: What Sound Does It Start With? (5 minutes)**
Say a word. The student identifies the beginning sound.

1. "What sound does **map** start with?" (/m/)
2. "What sound does **nose** start with?" (/n/)
3. "What sound does **fan** start with?" (/f/)
4. "What sound does **run** start with?" (/r/)
5. "What sound does **bed** start with?" (/b/)
6. "What sound does **pig** start with?" (/p/)

*Facilitator prompt: After each answer, confirm: "Yes! [Word] starts with /[sound]/. Nice listening!"*

*If the student struggles:* Stretch the beginning sound dramatically: "Mmmmmmmap. What's the first sound? /mmm/. Map starts with /m/." If the student says the whole word instead of just the first sound, say: "You said 'map' — that's the whole word. I just want the tiny sound at the very beginning. Listen: /m/. That's the beginning sound of map."

*If the student says the letter name instead of the sound:* Say: "You said the letter's name! That's close. The letter M makes the sound /m/. When I ask 'What sound does it start with?' I want the sound: /m/."

**Activity 2: Beginning Sound Sort (5 minutes)**
Place two plates or paper circles on the table. Label one "S words" and one "M words" (say the sounds, not the letter names).

Spread out 6 picture cards: 3 that start with /s/ (sun, soap, sock) and 3 that start with /m/ (mouse, mop, moon).

Say: "Let's sort these pictures. If the word starts with /s/, put it on this plate. If it starts with /m/, put it on the other plate."

Pick up each card together. Say the word, stretch the beginning sound, and sort.

*Facilitator prompt: "Sun. Sssssun. What do you hear at the beginning? /s/! Where does it go?"*

*If the student struggles:* Do the first two together, then let the student try the next ones. If the student puts a card in the wrong pile, say: "Let's check. Mmmop. /m/. Does that sound like /s/ or /m/?" Guide them to self-correct.

---

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices identifying beginning sounds with minimal support.

### Difficulty Tiers

**★ (On-level):** Tell me the beginning sound.
1. What sound does "leg" start with? (/l/)
2. What sound does "net" start with? (/n/)
3. What sound does "van" start with? (/v/)
4. What sound does "zip" start with? (/z/)
5. What sound does "hat" start with? (/h/)

**★★ (Stretch):** Do these words start with the same sound?
1. "Ball" and "boat" — same beginning sound? (Yes — both start with /b/)
2. "Fish" and "door" — same beginning sound? (No — /f/ and /d/)
3. "Cake" and "cup" — same beginning sound? (Yes — both start with /k/)
4. "Rug" and "rain" — same beginning sound? (Yes — both start with /r/)
5. "Lamp" and "peach" — same beginning sound? (No — /l/ and /p/)

**★★★ (Challenge):** Think of a word that starts with the same sound.
1. "Tell me a word that starts with the same sound as **goat**." (/g/ — girl, go, game, green, etc.)
2. "Tell me a word that starts with the same sound as **table**." (/t/ — top, ten, toy, tree, etc.)
3. "Tell me a word that starts with the same sound as **water**." (/w/ — walk, wish, worm, etc.)

### Interleaved Review — Rhyming (from Lesson 1)
4. "Tell me a word that rhymes with **ring**." (sing, king, wing, thing, etc.)
5. "Do **make** and **bake** rhyme?" (Yes)

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment to gauge mastery.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What sound does **red** start with?" (/r/)
2. "What sound does **go** start with?" (/g/)
3. "Do **milk** and **mud** start with the same sound?" (Yes — both /m/)

**Mastery = Student correctly identifies the beginning sound in at least 2 of the 3 items.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Spend an extra session on beginning sounds before moving on. Focus on continuant sounds (/s/, /m/, /f/, /n/) which are easier to isolate.
- Throughout the day, play "What sound does _____ start with?" using objects and foods during meals and activities.
- Have the student put their hand in front of their mouth and feel the first sound (the air or the lip position).
- Repeat this lesson with different word lists.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (Ending and Middle Sounds) next session. Continue to practice beginning sounds in warm-ups.

---

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who isolate beginning sounds easily:**
- Play "I Spy" by beginning sound: "I spy something that starts with /b/." The student looks around the room and guesses.
- Do a "Beginning Sound Treasure Hunt" — the student walks around the house and collects 3 things that start with a given sound.
- Introduce the idea that some words start with the same sound as the student's name. Find as many as possible.

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** Most kindergarteners need this lesson at full length. Beginning sounds are a significant cognitive step from rhyming because the student must focus on a single sound rather than a word-ending pattern.

**Sound vs. letter name:** At this stage, we are working with sounds, not letter names. If the student says "M" instead of /m/, gently redirect: "That's the letter's name. The sound it makes is /m/." Letter names are formally taught in Lessons 5-6.

**Continuant sounds first:** The lesson deliberately uses words starting with continuant consonants (/m/, /s/, /f/, /n/, /l/, /r/) in early activities because these sounds can be stretched: "mmmm," "ssss," "ffff." Stop consonants (/b/, /d/, /t/, /k/, /g/, /p/) are introduced after the student gets the concept. If a student struggles with stop consonants, that is normal and expected.

**Articulation note:** Some children at age 4-5 have typical developmental speech patterns (saying "wabbit" for "rabbit," or "fum" for "thumb"). Do not correct speech articulation in this lesson — focus on whether the child can identify the beginning sound, even if they pronounce it differently. If you suspect a speech concern, note it and see the "When to Seek Help" section in the Facilitator Guide.

**Don't worry about:** Ending sounds or middle sounds (those come in Lesson 3), connecting sounds to written letters (that comes in Lessons 5-6), or whether the student uses the "correct" term for the sound (as long as they can produce it, naming conventions are secondary).
