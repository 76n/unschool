# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 1, Lesson 3

## Topic
Isolating Ending Sounds and Introducing Middle Vowel Sounds

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify the ending sound of a spoken word
- Begin to identify the middle (vowel) sound in simple three-sound (CVC) words
- Sort words by their ending sound

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: Rhyming Words
- Lesson 2: Beginning Sounds (student can isolate the first sound in a word)

## Materials Needed
- 10-12 picture cards or small objects with clear ending sounds (e.g., bus, dress, miss; cat, boat, net; drum, jam, gum; leaf, roof; dog, pig, mug)
- Two plates, bowls, or paper circles for sound sorting
- 5 small objects or picture cards for CVC words: cat, mop, bus, pig, bed (for middle sounds)
- Crayons and paper (optional)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge and review beginning sounds and rhyming from Lessons 1-2.

**Spaced Review — Beginning Sounds and Rhyming**

1. "What sound does **fork** start with?" (/f/) — review from Lesson 2
2. "What sound does **nap** start with?" (/n/) — review from Lesson 2
3. "Tell me a word that rhymes with **boat**." (coat, goat, moat, note, float, etc.) — review from Lesson 1
4. "Do **pig** and **pan** start with the same sound?" (Yes — both start with /p/) — review from Lesson 2

Say: "You're getting really good at hearing the beginning of words. Today, we are going to listen to the END of words — and even the MIDDLE!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the student to isolate ending sounds, then introduce middle vowel sounds.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Ending sound:** The very last sound you hear when a word is finished
- **Middle sound:** The sound in the middle of a short word (between the beginning and ending sounds)
- **Vowel sound:** A sound that is open and loud — /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/ — every word has at least one

### Instruction Notes

**Part A: Ending Sounds (6-7 minutes)**

**Step 1: Introduce ending sounds.**
Say: "You already know how to hear the beginning sound of a word. Now let's listen to the LAST sound — the ending sound."

Model with "bus": Say the word slowly, stretching the ending: "Bu-**sss**. The last sound I hear in 'bus' is /s/. The word ends with /s/."

Model with "cat": "Ca-**t**. The last sound I hear in 'cat' is /t/."

Model with "drum": "Dru-**mmm**. The last sound I hear in 'drum' is /m/."

**Step 2: Show the student how to find the ending sound.**
Say: "To find the ending sound, I say the word slowly and listen for the very last thing my mouth does."

Model: "What's the ending sound in 'leaf'? I say it slowly: lea-**fff**. /f/ — that's the ending sound!"

Model: "What's the ending sound in 'dog'? Do-**g**. /g/ — that's the ending sound!"

**Step 3: Practice together.**
Say each word and ask the student to tell you the ending sound:
1. "map" (/p/)
2. "bell" (/l/)
3. "run" (/n/)
4. "wish" (/sh/)
5. "bed" (/d/)

Confirm each answer: "Yes! 'Map' ends with /p/. Great listening!"

**Part B: Middle Sounds (4-5 minutes)**

**Step 4: Introduce middle sounds.**
Say: "Now here is something amazing. Short words have THREE sounds — a beginning, a middle, and an ending. Let me show you."

Hold up a picture of a cat (or a toy cat). Say: "Cat. Watch — I can break it into three sounds."

Hold up three fingers. Touch each finger as you say: "/k/ - /a/ - /t/. Three sounds! The beginning is /k/, the ending is /t/, and the MIDDLE sound is /a/."

**Step 5: Model with more words.**
Use the same three-finger technique:

"Mop: /m/ - /o/ - /p/. The middle sound is /o/."

"Pig: /p/ - /i/ - /g/. The middle sound is /i/."

"Bed: /b/ - /e/ - /d/. The middle sound is /e/."

**Step 6: Acknowledge this is hard.**
Say: "Middle sounds are tricky! They hide between the other sounds. It's okay if this is hard right now — you're training your ears to hear something really small. You're doing something that even some big kids find tricky, so keep trying and you'll get it."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (Ending Sound):** "What's the ending sound in **bun**?"
Think aloud: "I say the word slowly: bu-nnn. The last sound is /n/. Bun ends with /n/."

**Example 2 (Middle Sound):** "What's the middle sound in **hot**?"
Think aloud: "I break the word into its three sounds: /h/ - /o/ - /t/. The beginning is /h/, the ending is /t/, and the middle is /o/. The middle sound in 'hot' is /o/."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice ending and middle sounds together with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Ending Sound Identification (4 minutes)**
Say a word. The student identifies the ending sound.

1. "What's the ending sound in **lip**?" (/p/)
2. "What's the ending sound in **mom**?" (/m/)
3. "What's the ending sound in **buzz**?" (/z/)
4. "What's the ending sound in **tag**?" (/g/)
5. "What's the ending sound in **leaf**?" (/f/)

*Facilitator prompt: "Say the word slowly with me. Liiiii-p. What's the very last sound?"*

*If the student struggles:* Stretch the ending sound dramatically: "li-**ppp**." Place your hand under your chin or by your mouth so the student can watch your mouth position at the end of the word. If the student gives the beginning sound instead, say: "That's the beginning sound — great ear! Now listen for the LAST sound, at the very end."

**Activity 2: Ending Sound Sort (3 minutes)**
Set up two sorting plates. Label one "ends with /t/" and one "ends with /n/."

Use these 6 words (say them aloud, show a picture or object if available):
- **cat** (ends with /t/)
- **sun** (ends with /n/)
- **hat** (ends with /t/)
- **man** (ends with /n/)
- **boat** (ends with /t/)
- **fun** (ends with /n/)

Say each word together, identify the ending sound, and sort.

*If the student struggles:* Do the first three together, then let the student try. Stretch the ending sound to make it obvious: "ca-**ttt**."

**Activity 3: Middle Sound Practice (3 minutes)**
Use three-finger counting. Hold up three fingers for each word and touch them as you say the sounds.

Together, find the middle sound in:
1. "**cup**" — /k/-/u/-/p/ — middle sound is /u/
2. "**sit**" — /s/-/i/-/t/ — middle sound is /i/
3. "**log**" — /l/-/o/-/g/ — middle sound is /o/
4. "**bag**" — /b/-/a/-/g/ — middle sound is /a/

*Facilitator prompt: "Let's break it apart with our fingers. /s/... /i/... /t/. What's the sound in the middle? The one our middle finger touched?"*

*If the student struggles with middle sounds:* This is expected. Say: "Middle sounds are the hardest to hear. Let's just focus on the beginning and ending for now, and we'll practice the middle more next time." Do not push this if the student is frustrated. Middle sound mastery develops over multiple lessons.

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices with minimal support.

### Difficulty Tiers

**★ (On-level):** Identify the ending sound.
1. What's the ending sound in "cup"? (/p/)
2. What's the ending sound in "him"? (/m/)
3. What's the ending sound in "red"? (/d/)
4. What's the ending sound in "miss"? (/s/)
5. What's the ending sound in "rug"? (/g/)

**★★ (Stretch):** Do these words end with the same sound?
1. "Cat" and "hat" — same ending sound? (Yes — both end with /t/)
2. "Bus" and "bed" — same ending sound? (No — /s/ and /d/)
3. "Fun" and "man" — same ending sound? (Yes — both end with /n/)
4. "Dog" and "pig" — same ending sound? (Yes — both end with /g/)
5. "Lip" and "lot" — same ending sound? (No — /p/ and /t/)

**★★★ (Challenge):** Tell me the middle sound.
1. What's the middle sound in "hop"? (/o/)
2. What's the middle sound in "big"? (/i/)
3. What's the middle sound in "pet"? (/e/)

### Interleaved Review (from Lessons 1-2)
4. "What sound does **leg** start with?" (/l/) — beginning sounds review
5. "Tell me a word that rhymes with **bell**." (fell, sell, well, tell, yell, shell, spell, etc.) — rhyming review

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment to gauge mastery.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. "What's the ending sound in **jam**?" (/m/)
2. "What's the ending sound in **net**?" (/t/)
3. "What's the middle sound in **cat**?" (/a/)

**Mastery = Student correctly identifies the ending sound in at least 2 of the first 2 items. Middle sound (item 3) is a bonus — not required for mastery at this stage.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Spend an extra session focused on ending sounds before moving on.
- Practice ending sounds throughout the day: "We just ate a ba-**nana**. What's the last sound? /uh/." Start with words that end in continuant sounds (/m/, /n/, /s/, /l/, /f/) since these are easier to stretch and hear.
- Use physical cues: have the student put up three fingers and touch the last finger as you say the ending sound.
- Repeat this lesson with different word lists.

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Blending and Segmenting) next session. Continue to practice ending sounds in warm-ups. If the student also got the middle sound correct, celebrate the achievement.

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who identify ending and middle sounds easily:**
- Play "Sound Detective" — say a three-sound word and ask the student to tell you ALL three sounds: beginning, middle, and ending. (/d/-/o/-/g/ for "dog")
- Say two words and ask: "Do these have the same MIDDLE sound?" (cat and hat — yes, both /a/; cat and cut — no, /a/ and /u/)
- Challenge: "I'm thinking of a word. It starts with /b/, the middle sound is /e/, and it ends with /d/. What's the word?" (bed)

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson has two parts (ending sounds and middle sounds). If the student needs more time on ending sounds, it is perfectly fine to split this across two sessions — ending sounds in session 1, middle sounds in session 2. Ending sound isolation should be solid before introducing middle sounds.

**Middle sounds are the hardest:** Of beginning, ending, and middle sounds, the middle vowel sound is the most difficult for young children to isolate. This is because vowel sounds are "coarticulated" — they blend into the consonants around them. If the student cannot hear the middle sound yet, that is completely normal. Keep practicing in future lessons and they will develop this skill.

**Vowel confusion is normal:** At this stage, students may confuse short vowel sounds with each other (saying /e/ when the middle sound is /i/, for example). Short vowels sound very similar to young ears. Gently correct and move on — this discrimination develops with exposure and practice.

**Two-part lesson design:** This lesson intentionally introduces middle sounds as an extension of ending sounds, not as a fully separate lesson. Students who are ready will begin hearing all three positions. Students who need more time will solidify ending sounds and encounter middle sounds more fully in Lesson 4 (blending and segmenting), where they will hear each sound position in context.

**Don't worry about:** Whether the student can name vowels or consonants (that vocabulary comes in Lessons 5-6), connecting sounds to written letters (that comes later), or getting middle sounds perfect right now (this is just the introduction).
