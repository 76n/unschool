# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 1, Lesson 5

## Topic
Letter Recognition and Formation: A-M

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Recognize and name the uppercase and lowercase forms of letters A through M
- State the primary sound each letter makes (letter-sound correspondence)
- Form (write) each uppercase and lowercase letter A through M with proper stroke order

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-4: Phonological awareness skills (the student can hear individual sounds in words)
- Ability to hold a pencil or crayon with a functional grip

## Materials Needed
- Letter cards for Aa through Mm (index cards with uppercase on one side, lowercase on the other — or both on the same side)
- Alphabet chart with picture cues (A-apple, B-ball, C-cat, etc.)
- Pencil or crayon and lined or blank paper (wide lines are best for kindergarten)
- A tray with sand, salt, or rice for multi-sensory letter writing (a baking sheet with a thin layer of salt works well)
- 10-12 small objects or picture cards that start with letters A-M (e.g., apple, ball, cat, dog, egg, fish, goat, hat, igloo, jar, kite, leaf, mouse)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge and review phonological awareness skills from Lessons 1-4.

**Spaced Review — Blending, Segmenting, and Sound Isolation**

1. **Blending:** "/h/ ... /a/ ... /t/ — What word?" (hat) — review from Lesson 4
2. **Segmenting:** "Break the word 'cup' into its sounds." (/k/-/u/-/p/) — review from Lesson 4
3. **Beginning sound:** "What sound does 'moon' start with?" (/m/) — review from Lesson 2
4. **Rhyming:** "Tell me a word that rhymes with 'bed.'" (red, fed, led, said, head, etc.) — review from Lesson 1

Say: "You've been learning to hear all the sounds in words. Now comes the exciting part — we're going to learn the LETTERS that make those sounds. Letters are the way we write sounds down so people can read them!"

---

## Direct Instruction (12-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce letters A-M — their names, sounds, and how to write them.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Letter:** A written symbol that stands for a sound
- **Uppercase (capital):** The big form of a letter (A, B, C)
- **Lowercase (small):** The small form of a letter (a, b, c)
- **Letter name:** What we call the letter (e.g., "Ay" for A, "Bee" for B)
- **Letter sound:** The sound the letter makes in words (e.g., /a/ for A as in "apple," /b/ for B as in "ball")

### Instruction Notes

**Important pacing note:** This lesson covers 13 letters. Do NOT try to teach all 13 in a single session. Plan to cover 4-5 letters per session, spending 2-3 sessions on this lesson. Below is a suggested grouping:

- **Session 1:** A, B, C, D, E (5 letters)
- **Session 2:** F, G, H, I, J (5 letters, with brief review of A-E)
- **Session 3:** K, L, M (3 letters, with review of A-J)

**For each letter, follow this 3-step routine:**

**Step 1: Introduce the letter name and sound.**
Hold up the letter card. Say: "This is the letter [name]. It makes the sound /[sound]/. [Name] says /[sound]/."

Have the student repeat: "[Name] says /[sound]/."

Show the picture cue: "A says /a/, like 'apple.'" Point to the apple picture on the alphabet chart.

**Step 2: Show uppercase and lowercase.**
Say: "The big letter is called uppercase or capital [name]. The small letter is called lowercase [name]. They look a little different, but they make the same sound."

Point to both forms on the letter card.

**Step 3: Teach letter formation.**
Model writing the letter while describing the strokes aloud. Then the student practices using multiple modalities:
1. **Air writing:** Trace the letter in the air with a finger while saying the strokes
2. **Sand/salt tray:** Write the letter in the sand/salt tray with a finger
3. **Paper:** Write the letter with a pencil or crayon on paper

**Letter-by-Letter Guide (A through M):**

---

**Aa — /a/ as in "apple"**
- Uppercase A: "Start at the top. Slant down to the left. Go back to the top and slant down to the right. Draw a line across the middle."
- Lowercase a: "Start like a little circle going left. When you get back to the top of the circle, go straight down."
- Cue word: "A says /a/ — apple."

**Bb — /b/ as in "ball"**
- Uppercase B: "Start at the top. Go straight down. Go back to the top and make a bump to the right. Make another bump to the right at the bottom."
- Lowercase b: "Start at the top. Go straight down. Go back up to the middle and make a bump to the right."
- Cue word: "B says /b/ — ball."

**Cc — /k/ as in "cat"**
- Uppercase C: "Start at the top right. Curve around to the left, down, and to the right — like a half circle."
- Lowercase c: "Same as the big C, just smaller."
- Cue word: "C says /k/ — cat."

**Dd — /d/ as in "dog"**
- Uppercase D: "Start at the top. Go straight down. Go back to the top and make a big bump to the right."
- Lowercase d: "Start like a little c. Then go up tall to the top and straight back down."
- Cue word: "D says /d/ — dog."

**Ee — /e/ as in "egg"**
- Uppercase E: "Start at the top. Go straight down. Go back to the top and draw a line to the right. Draw a line to the right from the middle. Draw a line to the right from the bottom."
- Lowercase e: "Start in the middle with a line to the right. Curve up, around to the left, down, and curl out."
- Cue word: "E says /e/ — egg."

**Ff — /f/ as in "fish"**
- Uppercase F: "Start at the top. Go straight down. Go back to the top and draw a line to the right. Draw a line to the right from the middle."
- Lowercase f: "Start at the top right. Curve to the left and go straight down. Then draw a short line across the middle."
- Cue word: "F says /f/ — fish."

**Gg — /g/ as in "goat"**
- Uppercase G: "Start like a C — curve around. Stop at the middle right side and draw a short line to the left."
- Lowercase g: "Start like a little a — make a circle. Then go straight down below the line and curve to the left."
- Cue word: "G says /g/ — goat."

**Hh — /h/ as in "hat"**
- Uppercase H: "Draw a line straight down. Lift up and draw another line straight down to the right. Connect them with a line across the middle."
- Lowercase h: "Start at the top. Go straight down. Go back up to the middle and make a hump to the right, then straight down."
- Cue word: "H says /h/ — hat."

**Ii — /i/ as in "igloo"**
- Uppercase I: "Draw a line across the top. Draw a line straight down from the middle. Draw a line across the bottom."
- Lowercase i: "Draw a short line straight down. Lift up and put a dot above it."
- Cue word: "I says /i/ — igloo."

**Jj — /j/ as in "jar"**
- Uppercase J: "Draw a line across the top. From the right end, go straight down, then curve to the left at the bottom."
- Lowercase j: "Go straight down below the line, then curve to the left. Lift up and put a dot above."
- Cue word: "J says /j/ — jar."

**Kk — /k/ as in "kite"**
- Uppercase K: "Draw a line straight down. Lift up and go to the middle. Draw a line slanting up to the right. Go back to the middle and draw a line slanting down to the right."
- Lowercase k: "Start at the top. Go straight down. Lift up and go to the middle. Draw a short line slanting in, then a line slanting out to the right."
- Cue word: "K says /k/ — kite."

**Ll — /l/ as in "leaf"**
- Uppercase L: "Start at the top. Go straight down. Draw a line to the right along the bottom."
- Lowercase l: "Start at the top. Go straight down. That's it — just a tall line!"
- Cue word: "L says /l/ — leaf."

**Mm — /m/ as in "mouse"**
- Uppercase M: "Start at the bottom left. Go straight up. Slant down to the middle. Slant back up. Go straight down."
- Lowercase m: "Start at the middle. Go straight down. Go back up to the middle and make a hump. Make another hump."
- Cue word: "M says /m/ — mouse."

---

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (Letter-Sound Connection):** Show the letter card for F.
"This letter is F. F makes the sound /f/. What's a word that starts with /f/? Fish! F says /f/ like in 'fish.' Can you think of another word that starts with /f/?" (fun, fork, five, foot, family, etc.)

**Example 2 (Connecting to Phonological Awareness):**
"Remember in Lesson 2, we learned that 'mouse' starts with the sound /m/? NOW you know which letter makes that sound — it's M! When you see the letter M, you know it says /m/."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice letter recognition, sounds, and formation with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Letter Flashcard Review (3 minutes)**
Shuffle the letter cards for the letters covered in today's session (4-5 letters). Hold up each card and ask:
- "What letter is this?" (name)
- "What sound does it make?" (sound)

Go through the stack 2-3 times. Mix up the order each time.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student hesitates, point to the alphabet chart picture cue: "Here's a hint — apple! A says /a/."*

*If the student struggles:* Reduce to 3 letters and practice those until solid before adding more. It is better to know 3 letters well than 5 letters poorly.

**Activity 2: Sound-to-Letter Match (3 minutes)**
Place 4-5 letter cards face up on the table. Say a sound and ask the student to point to the letter that makes that sound.

"Point to the letter that makes the sound /b/." (B)
"Point to the letter that makes the sound /d/." (D)
"Point to the letter that makes the sound /a/." (A)

*If the student struggles:* Say the cue word: "Which letter says /b/ like 'ball'?" Point to the alphabet chart if needed.

**Activity 3: Letter Formation Practice (4 minutes)**
The student writes each letter covered today using the three modalities:
1. Air writing (with verbal stroke description)
2. Sand/salt tray writing
3. Paper writing (2-3 times each)

*Facilitator prompt: Describe the strokes as the student writes: "Start at the top. Go straight down. Now back up to the middle and make a bump..."*

*If the student struggles with formation:* Use hand-over-hand guidance for the first attempt — place your hand gently over the student's hand and form the letter together. Then let the student try alone. Focus on the general shape, not perfection. Say: "Your brain is learning a new path for your hand to follow. It gets easier every time you practice!"

*If the student reverses a letter:* Gently redirect: "Let's check — b starts with the line going down, then the bump goes this way (to the right). Let's try it again." Do not over-correct — reversals are developmentally normal and will self-correct with practice.

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices with minimal support.

### Difficulty Tiers

**★ (On-level):** Letter recognition — point to the correct letter.
The facilitator spreads out letter cards for the session's letters. Say each letter name and have the student point to it.
1. "Find the letter C."
2. "Find the letter A."
3. "Find the letter E."
4. "Find the letter B."
5. "Find the letter D."

**★★ (Stretch):** Letter-sound matching — match the object to the letter.
Lay out letter cards (for this session's letters) and small objects or picture cards. The student matches each object to the letter it starts with.
- Apple goes with A
- Ball goes with B
- Cat goes with C
- Dog goes with D
- Egg goes with E

(Adjust objects based on which letters are being covered in the current session.)

**★★★ (Challenge):** Write the letter from hearing the sound.
The facilitator says a sound, and the student writes the letter that makes that sound.
1. "Write the letter that makes the /a/ sound." (A/a)
2. "Write the letter that makes the /b/ sound." (B/b)
3. "Write the letter that makes the /d/ sound." (D/d)

### Interleaved Review (from Lessons 1-4)
4. **Blending:** "/b/ ... /u/ ... /s/ — What word?" (bus) — review from Lesson 4
5. **Ending sound:** "What's the ending sound in 'dog'?" (/g/) — review from Lesson 3
6. **Rhyming:** "Do 'fan' and 'man' rhyme?" (Yes) — review from Lesson 1

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment to gauge mastery for the letters covered in today's session.

**Exit Ticket** (adjust based on which letters were covered):

1. Show the letter card for [letter covered today, e.g., B]. "What letter is this?" (B)
2. "What sound does [same letter] make?" (/b/)
3. "Write the letter [a different letter covered today, e.g., D] for me — uppercase and lowercase."

**Mastery = Student correctly names the letter AND says its sound AND produces a recognizable written form (uppercase or lowercase — either counts).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Continue practicing the current set of letters next session before adding new ones.
- Use multi-sensory repetition: write letters in sand, form them with playdough, trace them on textured surfaces.
- Sing the alphabet song, pausing at the letters being learned: "A, B, C, D... wait, what sound does D make?"
- Label items around the house with their starting letter on a sticky note.

### If Mastery Is Met
In the next session, introduce the next group of letters. Continue reviewing previously learned letters at the start of each session — mixing them into the flashcard stack.

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who recognize letters easily:**
- **Letter hunt:** Walk around the house and find each letter on food boxes, book covers, signs, or labels
- **Name connection:** Write the student's name and identify each letter: "Your name starts with the letter J. J says /j/!"
- **Letter art:** Turn letters into drawings (turn B into a butterfly, S into a snake, etc.)
- **Alphabet book:** Start making a personal alphabet book — one page per letter with a drawing of something that starts with that letter

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing is critical:** Do NOT try to cover all 13 letters (A-M) in a single 30-40 minute session. Plan for 2-3 sessions of 4-5 letters each. Each session should follow the full lesson structure (warm-up through check for understanding) but focus on only a subset of letters plus review of previously learned ones.

**Multi-session plan:**
- Session 5a: Teach A, B, C, D, E. Check for understanding on these 5.
- Session 5b: Review A-E (flashcards), then teach F, G, H, I, J. Check for understanding on F-J.
- Session 5c: Review A-J (flashcards), then teach K, L, M. Check for understanding on K-M. Then do a cumulative check of all A-M.

**Letter names vs. letter sounds:** Both matter, but for reading readiness, sounds are more immediately useful. If you have to prioritize, prioritize sounds. A child who knows that B says /b/ is closer to reading than a child who knows the letter's name is "Bee" but cannot connect it to /b/.

**b/d confusion:** This is the single most common letter confusion in early literacy. It is NOT a sign of dyslexia — it is a normal stage. Helpful strategies:
- "b has its bump on the right (like a belly), d has its bump on the left"
- "Make two fists with thumbs up — your left hand makes a b, your right hand makes a d"
- Practice the two letters in separate sessions before comparing them

**Handwriting expectations:** At age 4-6, letter formation will be imperfect. Letters may be uneven, oversized, or wobbly. This is completely normal. Focus on: (1) correct general shape, (2) correct stroke direction (top to bottom, left to right), (3) the student can tell the difference between their letters. Do not demand neatness — it comes with motor development over months.

**Multi-sensory approaches matter:** Research shows that children learn letter forms faster when they use multiple modalities — seeing, hearing, saying, tracing, and writing. The sand/salt tray is not a gimmick; it provides tactile feedback that strengthens the motor memory for each letter shape.

**Don't worry about:** Perfect handwriting (focus on recognition and sound), upper/lowercase confusion (the student may prefer one form — that's fine for now), or memorizing all 13 letters immediately (mastery is built across multiple sessions).
