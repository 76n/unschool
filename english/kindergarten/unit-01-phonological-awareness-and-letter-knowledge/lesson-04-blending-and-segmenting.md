# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 1, Lesson 4

## Topic
Blending and Segmenting Sounds

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Blend 3 individual sounds spoken aloud into a whole word (e.g., /c/-/a/-/t/ = "cat")
- Segment a spoken word into its individual sounds (e.g., "dog" = /d/-/o/-/g/)
- Use clapping or tapping to represent each sound in a word

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 2: Beginning Sounds (student can isolate the first sound)
- Lesson 3: Ending and Middle Sounds (student has initial exposure to all three sound positions)

## Materials Needed
- 10-12 picture cards or small objects with simple CVC (consonant-vowel-consonant) names: cat, dog, bus, cup, hat, pig, mop, bed, sun, rug, net, pot
- 3 small blocks, coins, or tokens (for representing sounds)
- A flat surface (table or floor)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge and review skills from Lessons 1-3.

**Spaced Review — Beginning Sounds, Ending Sounds, and Rhyming**

1. "What sound does **goat** start with?" (/g/) — beginning sounds review
2. "What's the ending sound in **top**?" (/p/) — ending sounds review
3. "Do **mouse** and **house** rhyme?" (Yes) — rhyming review
4. "What's the middle sound in **hop**?" (/o/) — middle sounds review (bonus)

Say: "You've been learning to hear the sounds at the beginning, end, and even the middle of words. Today, we are going to put sounds TOGETHER to make words, and break words APART into sounds. This is like a superpower for reading!"

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach blending (putting sounds together) and segmenting (taking words apart).

### Key Vocabulary
- **Blend:** To push sounds together to make a word — like connecting train cars to make a train
- **Segment:** To break a word apart into its separate sounds — like pulling train cars apart

### Instruction Notes

**Part A: Blending (5-6 minutes)**

**Step 1: Introduce blending with a metaphor.**
Say: "I'm going to say a word very slowly, one sound at a time. Your job is to figure out what word I'm saying. It's like a puzzle — you put the sounds together."

**Step 2: Model blending.**
Say the sounds slowly with short pauses: "/c/ ... /a/ ... /t/." Then sweep your hand from left to right and say: "Cat! When I push those sounds together, they make 'cat.'"

Do it again with physical support: Place 3 blocks in a row on the table. Touch each block as you say a sound: "/c/" (touch block 1) ... "/a/" (touch block 2) ... "/t/" (touch block 3). Then push the blocks together and say: "Cat!"

**Step 3: Model more examples.**
Use blocks each time:
- "/d/ ... /o/ ... /g/" — push blocks together — "Dog!"
- "/s/ ... /u/ ... /n/" — push blocks together — "Sun!"
- "/m/ ... /o/ ... /p/" — push blocks together — "Mop!"

**Step 4: Let the student try.**
Say: "/b/ ... /u/ ... /s/." Ask: "What word is that?"

Give the student the three blocks. Have them touch each block as you repeat the sounds, then push them together and say the word. If they get "bus," celebrate: "You blended the sounds into a word!"

**Part B: Segmenting (5-6 minutes)**

**Step 5: Introduce segmenting.**
Say: "Now we're going to do the opposite. I'll say a whole word, and you break it into pieces — one sound at a time."

**Step 6: Model segmenting with clapping.**
Say: "Listen — 'hat.' I'm going to clap for each sound."

Clap and say: "/h/" (clap) — "/a/" (clap) — "/t/" (clap). "Hat has 3 sounds: /h/, /a/, /t/."

**Step 7: Model with blocks.**
Place 3 blocks in a row. Say "cup." Touch each block as you segment: "/k/" (touch block 1) — "/u/" (touch block 2) — "/p/" (touch block 3). "Cup: /k/-/u/-/p/. Three sounds."

**Step 8: Model more examples.**
- "Pig": /p/ (clap) — /i/ (clap) — /g/ (clap). "Three sounds."
- "Red": /r/ (clap) — /e/ (clap) — /d/ (clap). "Three sounds."
- "Net": /n/ (clap) — /e/ (clap) — /t/ (clap). "Three sounds."

**Step 9: Let the student try.**
Say: "Your turn. Break the word 'dog' into its sounds. Clap for each sound."

Guide as needed: "What's the first sound? /d/. Clap! What comes next? /o/. Clap! And the last sound? /g/. Clap! You segmented 'dog' into three sounds!"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (Blending):** "I say /r/ ... /u/ ... /g/. What word is that?"
Think aloud: "I heard three sounds: /r/, /u/, /g/. Let me push them together: r-u-g... rug! The word is 'rug.'"

**Example 2 (Segmenting):** "Break the word 'pot' into its sounds."
Think aloud: "Pot. I say it slowly: /p/ ... /o/ ... /t/. That's three sounds. I'll clap for each: /p/ (clap) /o/ (clap) /t/ (clap). Pot has three sounds: /p/, /o/, /t/."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice blending and segmenting together with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Blending — "Guess My Word" (5 minutes)**
The facilitator says sounds slowly. The student blends and says the word. Use blocks or hand-sweeping.

1. /h/ ... /o/ ... /p/ — "What word?" (hop)
2. /b/ ... /e/ ... /d/ — "What word?" (bed)
3. /f/ ... /i/ ... /n/ — "What word?" (fin)
4. /l/ ... /e/ ... /g/ — "What word?" (leg)
5. /t/ ... /u/ ... /b/ — "What word?" (tub)

*Facilitator prompt: "Push the sounds together. Say them faster and faster: /h/..../o/..../p/... hop!"*

*If the student struggles:* Start with just two sounds first: "/a/ ... /t/ — what word? At!" Then add the beginning sound: "/k/ ... /a/ ... /t/ — cat!" Also try saying the sounds faster, with shorter pauses between them. Some students need the sounds nearly connected before they hear the word.

*If the student says a wrong word:* "Let's listen again. /b/... /e/... /d/. The first sound is /b/, like in 'ball.' The middle is /e/. The end is /d/. /b/-/e/-/d/... bed!" Repeat the correct word and move on cheerfully.

**Activity 2: Segmenting — "Break It Apart" (5 minutes)**
The facilitator says a whole word. The student breaks it into individual sounds using claps, taps, or blocks.

1. "Break apart the word **sit**." (/s/ - /i/ - /t/)
2. "Break apart the word **mud**." (/m/ - /u/ - /d/)
3. "Break apart the word **hen**." (/h/ - /e/ - /n/)
4. "Break apart the word **fox**." (/f/ - /o/ - /x/)
5. "Break apart the word **lip**." (/l/ - /i/ - /p/)

*Facilitator prompt: "What's the first sound? Good. What comes next in the middle? And the last sound?"*

*If the student struggles:* Segment the word yourself first, then have the student repeat your segmentation. Say: "Watch me — 'sit': /s/ (clap) /i/ (clap) /t/ (clap). Now you do it with me." Gradually remove your support. If needed, use the blocks — push them apart one at a time as each sound is said.

*If the student gives only two sounds (says /s/-/it/ instead of /s/-/i/-/t/):* Say: "You found two pieces! Let's break it even smaller. Listen to the end: /i/-/t/. Those are two sounds. So the whole word has three: /s/-/i/-/t/."

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices with minimal support.

### Difficulty Tiers

**★ (On-level):** Blend these sounds into words.
1. /r/ - /a/ - /n/ — what word? (ran)
2. /p/ - /e/ - /t/ — what word? (pet)
3. /g/ - /u/ - /m/ — what word? (gum)
4. /w/ - /i/ - /n/ — what word? (win)
5. /b/ - /a/ - /g/ — what word? (bag)

**★★ (Stretch):** Segment these words into sounds (clap for each).
1. "jam" — how many sounds? what are they? (3: /j/ - /a/ - /m/)
2. "wet" — how many sounds? what are they? (3: /w/ - /e/ - /t/)
3. "fox" — how many sounds? what are they? (3: /f/ - /o/ - /x/)
4. "hug" — how many sounds? what are they? (3: /h/ - /u/ - /g/)

**★★★ (Challenge):** Blend and segment — both directions.
1. /sh/ - /i/ - /p/ — what word? (ship — note: /sh/ is one sound even though it uses two letters)
2. Segment the word "frog" into sounds. (4 sounds: /f/ - /r/ - /o/ - /g/)
3. /s/ - /t/ - /o/ - /p/ — what word? (stop — 4 sounds)

### Interleaved Review (from Lessons 1-3)
4. "What sound does **lamp** start with?" (/l/) — beginning sounds review
5. "What's the ending sound in **dress**?" (/s/) — ending sounds review
6. "Tell me a word that rhymes with **pig**." (big, dig, wig, fig, jig, etc.) — rhyming review

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment to gauge mastery.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. **Blending:** "/m/ ... /a/ ... /p/ — What word is that?" (map)
2. **Blending:** "/d/ ... /u/ ... /k/ — What word is that?" (duck)
3. **Segmenting:** "Break the word 'fun' into its sounds." (/f/ - /u/ - /n/)

**Mastery = Student correctly blends at least 1 of 2 blending items AND correctly segments item 3 (all 3 sounds).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Spend an extra session on blending and segmenting before moving on. These are critical pre-reading skills.
- For blending difficulty: Use the block method and say the sounds faster, gradually increasing the speed. Some students need the sounds almost connected before they can hear the whole word.
- For segmenting difficulty: Start with two-sound words ("up": /u/-/p/; "at": /a/-/t/; "in": /i/-/n/) and work up to three-sound words.
- Play "Robot Talk" throughout the day — the facilitator speaks in segmented sounds and the student translates: "Time to eat /l/-/u/-/n/-/ch/!"

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 5 (Letter Recognition and Formation: A-M) next session. Continue to practice blending and segmenting in warm-ups — this skill is the bridge to reading.

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who blend and segment easily:**
- Try 4-sound words: "Stop" (/s/-/t/-/o/-/p/), "frog" (/f/-/r/-/o/-/g/), "trip" (/t/-/r/-/i/-/p/)
- Play "Add a Sound": "Say 'at.' Now put /k/ at the beginning. What word?" (cat). "Now put /b/ at the beginning instead." (bat)
- Play "Change a Sound": "Say 'cat.' Change the /k/ to /b/. What's the new word?" (bat). "Change the /a/ to /u/." (but)
- Play "Robot Talk" in reverse — the student segments words for the facilitator to guess

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson combines two complementary skills — blending and segmenting. If the student grasps blending quickly but struggles with segmenting (or vice versa), split this across two sessions. Blending is typically easier because the student hears all the sounds and just needs to connect them. Segmenting requires the student to take a whole word apart, which is cognitively harder.

**Blocks are powerful:** The physical blocks (or coins, or buttons) make abstract sounds concrete. Each block represents one sound. Pushing them together shows blending; pulling them apart shows segmenting. Keep using them as long as they help.

**CVC words only:** This lesson uses only CVC (consonant-vowel-consonant) words with 3 sounds. Words with blends (like "stop" or "frog") or digraphs (like "ship" or "chip") appear only in the Challenge tier. The core skill is 3-sound blending and segmenting.

**Common error — syllable segmenting instead of sound segmenting:** A student might clap "pu-ppy" (2 syllables) instead of "/p/-/u/-/p/-/ee/" (4 sounds). If this happens, say: "Those are the two parts (syllables) of the word. Now let's break it into even TINIER pieces — the individual sounds." Stick to one-syllable words in this lesson to avoid this confusion.

**Connection to reading:** This lesson is the bridge between phonological awareness (hearing sounds) and phonics (connecting sounds to letters). When a student can blend /c/-/a/-/t/ into "cat," they are one step away from sounding out the written word C-A-T. This connection is made explicit in Lessons 5-6.

**Don't worry about:** Spelling (this is all oral), letter names or shapes (that comes next lesson), 4-sound words (those are only in the extension), or speed (accuracy first, fluency develops over weeks).
