# English Language Arts — Kindergarten — Unit 1, Lesson 6

## Topic
Letter Recognition and Formation: N-Z, Plus Cumulative Review of All 26 Letters

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Recognize and name the uppercase and lowercase forms of letters N through Z
- State the primary sound each letter makes (letter-sound correspondence)
- Form (write) each uppercase and lowercase letter N through Z with proper stroke order
- Identify all 26 letters by name and sound (cumulative review)

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 5: Letter Recognition and Formation A-M (student can name and produce sounds for letters A-M)
- Ability to hold a pencil or crayon with a functional grip

## Materials Needed
- Letter cards for Nn through Zz (index cards with both uppercase and lowercase forms)
- Complete set of letter cards Aa through Zz (for cumulative review)
- Alphabet chart with picture cues
- Pencil or crayon and lined or blank paper
- Sand/salt tray for multi-sensory letter writing
- 10-13 small objects or picture cards that start with letters N-Z (e.g., nest, octopus, pig, queen, ring, sun, turtle, umbrella, van, watermelon, fox/x-ray, yarn, zipper)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge and review letters A-M plus phonological awareness skills.

**Spaced Review — Letters A-M and Phonological Awareness**

1. **Letter review:** Hold up 4-5 random letter cards from A-M. For each, ask: "What letter? What sound?" — review from Lesson 5
2. **Blending:** "/r/ ... /e/ ... /d/ — What word?" (red) — review from Lesson 4
3. **Segmenting:** "Break the word 'jam' into its sounds." (/j/-/a/-/m/) — review from Lesson 4
4. **Beginning sound + letter connection:** "What sound does 'fish' start with? Which letter makes that sound?" (/f/, F) — connecting Lessons 2 and 5

Say: "Great job remembering your first 13 letters! Today we're going to learn the rest of the alphabet — letters N through Z. When you're done, you'll know ALL 26 letters!"

---

## Direct Instruction (12-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce letters N-Z — their names, sounds, and how to write them.

### Key Vocabulary
- Same as Lesson 5: **letter, uppercase, lowercase, letter name, letter sound**
- **Alphabet:** All 26 letters in order — A through Z

### Instruction Notes

**Important pacing note:** Like Lesson 5, this lesson covers 13 letters and should be spread across 2-3 sessions. Suggested grouping:

- **Session 1:** N, O, P, Q, R (5 letters)
- **Session 2:** S, T, U, V, W (5 letters, with brief review of N-R)
- **Session 3:** X, Y, Z (3 letters, with review of N-W, then cumulative review of all A-Z)

**Follow the same 3-step routine from Lesson 5 for each new letter:**
1. Introduce the letter name and sound (with picture cue)
2. Show uppercase and lowercase forms
3. Teach letter formation (air writing, sand/salt tray, paper)

**Letter-by-Letter Guide (N through Z):**

---

**Nn — /n/ as in "nest"**
- Uppercase N: "Start at the bottom left. Go straight up. Slant down to the bottom right. Go straight up."
- Lowercase n: "Start at the middle. Go straight down. Go back up to the middle and make one hump to the right, then straight down."
- Cue word: "N says /n/ — nest."

**Oo — /o/ as in "octopus"**
- Uppercase O: "Start at the top. Make a big circle going to the left, down, around, and back up to the top."
- Lowercase o: "Same as the big O, just smaller."
- Cue word: "O says /o/ — octopus."

**Pp — /p/ as in "pig"**
- Uppercase P: "Start at the top. Go straight down. Go back to the top and make a bump to the right."
- Lowercase p: "Start at the middle. Go straight down below the line. Go back up to the middle and make a bump to the right."
- Cue word: "P says /p/ — pig."
- **Note:** Help the student see how P and B are different: "P has a bump at the top, B has bumps at the top AND bottom."

**Qq — /kw/ as in "queen"**
- Uppercase Q: "Make a big O, then add a small tail slanting to the right at the bottom."
- Lowercase q: "Start like a little circle going left. Then go straight down below the line and add a tiny kick to the right."
- Cue word: "Q says /kw/ — queen. Q almost always has its friend U right next to it!"
- **Note:** Help the student see how q and d are different: "q has its circle on the LEFT and its line on the RIGHT going down. d has its circle on the LEFT too, but d stays on the line."

**Rr — /r/ as in "ring"**
- Uppercase R: "Start at the top. Go straight down. Go back to the top and make a bump to the right (like P). Then from the middle, slant a leg down to the right."
- Lowercase r: "Start at the middle. Go straight down. Go back up to the middle and make a small curve to the right — but stop. Don't go all the way down."
- Cue word: "R says /r/ — ring."

**Ss — /s/ as in "sun"**
- Uppercase S: "Start at the top right. Curve to the left, then curve back to the right — like a snake."
- Lowercase s: "Same as the big S, just smaller."
- Cue word: "S says /s/ — sun."

**Tt — /t/ as in "turtle"**
- Uppercase T: "Draw a line across the top. Draw a line straight down from the middle of it."
- Lowercase t: "Start above the middle. Go straight down past the middle line. Then draw a short line across at the middle."
- Cue word: "T says /t/ — turtle."

**Uu — /u/ as in "umbrella"**
- Uppercase U: "Start at the top left. Go straight down, curve along the bottom, and go straight back up to the top right."
- Lowercase u: "Start at the middle. Go down, curve along the bottom, and come back up. Then go straight down."
- Cue word: "U says /u/ — umbrella."

**Vv — /v/ as in "van"**
- Uppercase V: "Start at the top left. Slant down to a point at the bottom. Slant back up to the top right."
- Lowercase v: "Same as the big V, just smaller."
- Cue word: "V says /v/ — van."

**Ww — /w/ as in "watermelon"**
- Uppercase W: "Start at the top left. Slant down, slant up to the middle, slant down again, and slant back up to the top right. Like two V's side by side!"
- Lowercase w: "Same as the big W, just smaller."
- Cue word: "W says /w/ — watermelon."

**Xx — /ks/ as in "fox"**
- Uppercase X: "Slant a line from top left to bottom right. Then slant a line from top right to bottom left. They cross in the middle."
- Lowercase x: "Same as the big X, just smaller."
- Cue word: "X says /ks/ — we hear it at the END of words like 'fox' and 'box.' X can also say /z/ at the beginning of some words, like 'xylophone.'"

**Yy — /y/ as in "yarn"**
- Uppercase Y: "Slant a short line from the top left to the middle. Slant a short line from the top right to meet it at the middle. Then go straight down from the middle."
- Lowercase y: "Make a v shape starting from the middle. Then continue the right line down below the base line and curve it to the left."
- Cue word: "Y says /y/ — yarn."

**Zz — /z/ as in "zipper"**
- Uppercase Z: "Draw a line across the top going right. Slant down to the bottom left. Draw a line across the bottom going right."
- Lowercase z: "Same as the big Z, just smaller."
- Cue word: "Z says /z/ — zipper."

---

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (Letter-Sound Connection):** Show the letter card for T.
"This letter is T. T makes the sound /t/. Let's think of words that start with /t/ — turtle, table, ten, tree, toy! You already knew the sound /t/ from our beginning sounds practice. Now you know which letter makes it!"

**Example 2 (Connecting to Blending):**
"Remember when we blended /s/-/u/-/n/ into 'sun'? Now you know the letters! S makes /s/, U makes /u/, N makes /n/. If you saw those letters written down — S, U, N — you could read the word 'sun'! That's what reading is — looking at letters, saying their sounds, and blending them together."

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice letter recognition, sounds, and formation with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Letter Flashcard Review (3 minutes)**
Shuffle the letter cards for today's session (4-5 new letters plus 3-4 review letters from A-M or earlier in N-Z). Hold up each card and ask:
- "What letter?"
- "What sound?"

Go through the stack 2-3 times, mixing up the order.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student hesitates, point to the alphabet chart: "Look — sun! S says /s/."*

*If the student struggles:* Reduce to 3 new letters plus 2 review letters. Mastery of fewer letters is better than shaky knowledge of more.

**Activity 2: Letter Sorting — Uppercase and Lowercase (3 minutes)**
Spread out 5-6 uppercase letter cards and their lowercase matches. The student matches each uppercase letter to its lowercase partner.

"Can you find the big A and the little a? Put them together."

*If the student struggles:* Start with 3 very different-looking pairs (like A/a, B/b, O/o) before adding pairs that look similar.

**Activity 3: Letter Formation Practice (4 minutes)**
The student writes each new letter using three modalities:
1. Air writing (with verbal stroke description from the facilitator)
2. Sand/salt tray writing
3. Paper writing (2-3 times each, both uppercase and lowercase)

*Facilitator prompt: Say the strokes aloud as the student writes. "Start at the top. Go straight down. Now back up to the middle and make a hump..."*

*If the student struggles with formation:* Use hand-over-hand guidance for the first attempt. Then let the student try alone. Say: "Each time you write it, your hand remembers a little more. Keep practicing and it will get easier!"

*If the student reverses a letter:* Common reversals at this stage include p/q, b/d, and s/z. For p/q: "p goes down below the line with a bump on the RIGHT. q goes down below the line with a circle on the LEFT." Use consistent verbal cues.

## Independent Practice (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices with minimal support.

### Difficulty Tiers

**★ (On-level):** Letter recognition — name the letter and its sound.
The facilitator holds up 6-8 letter cards (mix of today's new letters and A-M review). Student says the letter name and sound.

1. Show S — "S, /s/"
2. Show D — "D, /d/"
3. Show T — "T, /t/"
4. Show G — "G, /g/"
5. Show N — "N, /n/"
6. Show P — "P, /p/"

**★★ (Stretch):** Letter-sound matching with objects.
Lay out 5-6 letter cards and matching objects or picture cards. The student matches each object to the letter it starts with.
- Nest goes with N
- Ring goes with R
- Sun goes with S
- Turtle goes with T
- Pig goes with P

**★★★ (Challenge):** Cumulative review — full alphabet.
Shuffle all 26 letter cards (or as many as the student has learned so far). Go through the stack, saying each letter name and sound. The facilitator notes which letters are solid and which need more practice.

Bonus challenge: "Can you put all the letters in ABC order?"

### Interleaved Review (from Lessons 1-5)
4. **Blending:** "/p/ ... /o/ ... /t/ — What word?" (pot) — review from Lesson 4
5. **Rhyming:** "Tell me a word that rhymes with 'sun.'" (fun, bun, run, etc.) — review from Lesson 1
6. **Segmenting:** "Break the word 'leg' into sounds." (/l/-/e/-/g/) — review from Lesson 4
7. **Letter from Lesson 5:** "Write the letter that makes the /f/ sound." (F/f) — review from Lesson 5

**Mastery = Student correctly names AND sounds at least 22 of 26 letters (85%). Note which letters are missed for targeted review.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify the specific letters the student misses. These are the letters to focus on — not all 26.
- Create a "practice stack" of just the missed letters. Review these 3-5 times per session at the start of each session.
- Use multi-sensory strategies for the difficult letters: form them with playdough, trace them on sandpaper, write them with a wet finger on a chalkboard or dark paper.
- Continue with the unit assessment, noting areas that need ongoing practice. The student does not need to wait for perfect letter knowledge before assessing — the assessment will pinpoint where they are.

### If Mastery Is Met
The student is ready for the Unit Assessment. Continue reviewing all 26 letters in daily warm-ups to maintain automaticity — the goal is that letter recognition becomes instant, not effortful.

## Extension (Optional)

**For students who know all 26 letters solidly:**
- **Write your name:** The student writes their full first name (and last name if ready), naming each letter and its sound
- **Secret messages:** The facilitator writes a simple CVC word (cat, dog, sun) and the student sounds out each letter to read the word. This is the beginning of reading!
- **Alphabet challenge:** "Can you say the alphabet backward from Z to A?" (Very challenging — a fun goal to work toward, not an expectation.)
- **Vowel discovery:** "Five of our letters are special — they're called vowels: A, E, I, O, U. Can you find all five on the alphabet chart?" Introduce the idea that every word needs at least one vowel.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing across sessions:** This lesson is designed for 2-3 sessions, just like Lesson 5. The final session should include a cumulative review of all 26 letters. A suggested plan:
- Session 6a: Teach N, O, P, Q, R. Review A-M with flashcards.
- Session 6b: Teach S, T, U, V, W. Review A-R with flashcards.
- Session 6c: Teach X, Y, Z. Cumulative review of all A-Z. Administer the cumulative check.

**Tricky letters in this set:**
- **Q** is rare and almost always appears with U. Teach them as a pair: "Q and U are best friends. They always go together."
- **X** is unusual because its main sound /ks/ is heard at the end of words (fox, box, six), not the beginning. The student may struggle to think of words "starting with X." That is fine — acknowledge that X is a special letter.
- **V** and **W** can be visually confusing. Emphasize: "V has a sharp point. W has TWO sharp points — like two V's."

**p/b/d/q confusion:** These four letters use the same two shapes (a vertical line and a circle) in different arrangements. This confusion is extremely common and developmentally normal. Strategies:
- Teach b and d in separate sessions (they are in separate lessons here — b in Lesson 5, d in Lesson 5, p and q in Lesson 6)
- Use consistent verbal cues tied to formation: "b: line down, bump right. d: circle left, line down."
- Use the "bed" trick: make two fists with thumbs up — the left hand looks like b, the right hand looks like d, and together they spell "bed."

**The cumulative review is a celebration:** Frame the final review of all 26 letters as an achievement, not a test. Say: "Let's see how many letters you know now. When we started, you were just learning to hear sounds in words. Now you know the letters that MAKE those sounds. That's amazing growth!"

**Don't worry about:** Letters the student already knew before this unit (some students enter kindergarten knowing many letters — let them shine), perfect penmanship (recognition and sound knowledge matter more at this stage), or the order of letters (alphabetical order is useful for the alphabet song but not essential for reading).
