# Unit Assessment — Kindergarten ELA, Unit 1: Phonological Awareness & Letter Knowledge

**Grade:** Kindergarten
**Unit:** 1 — Phonological Awareness & Letter Knowledge
**Assessment Type:** Summative (unit-level)
**Administration:** One-on-one with facilitator (this assessment is oral, hands-on, and includes brief writing tasks — not a written test)
**Estimated Duration:** 20-25 minutes
**Materials:** Letter cards Aa-Zz, 10-12 picture cards, pencil and paper, alphabet chart (for reference ONLY if needed during reteaching — do NOT provide during assessment)

---

## Assessment Overview

This assessment covers all Unit 1 learning objectives:

| Section | Skill Assessed | Lesson Source | Items |
|---------|---------------|---------------|-------|
| A | Rhyming | Lesson 1 | 4 |
| B | Sound isolation (beginning and ending) | Lessons 2-3 | 4 |
| C | Blending and segmenting | Lesson 4 | 4 |
| D | Letter recognition (name and sound) | Lessons 5-6 | 6 |
| E | Letter-sound matching | Lessons 5-6 | 3 |
| F | Letter formation (writing) | Lessons 5-6 | 4 |
| -- | **Total** | -- | **25** |

**Mastery threshold:** 85% overall (21 of 25 items correct) AND at least 1 correct item in each section.

---

## Administration Instructions

Read each item aloud. Record the student's response. For writing items, observe the student's letter formation and collect the writing sample.

There are no time limits on any item. Let the student work at their pace. If the student is stuck for more than 15 seconds on any item, say: "That's okay, let's try the next one."

Do NOT provide hints, corrections, or the alphabet chart during the assessment. If the student asks for help, say: "Just try your best. It's okay if you're not sure."

Create a comfortable atmosphere before beginning. Say: "We're going to do some listening and letter activities together. There are no tricks. Just show me what you know. Some might be easy, some might be a little tricky, and that's perfectly fine. You've been learning so much — let's see what you remember!"

## Rubric and Mastery Determination

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Next Steps |
|-------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Mastered** | 21-25 (85-100%) with at least 1 correct in each section | Student has solid phonological awareness and letter knowledge foundations | Advance to Unit 2. Continue spaced review of phonological awareness and letter knowledge in warm-ups. |
| **Approaching** | 18-20 (70-84%) | Partial understanding — some skills are solid, others need reinforcement | Review the specific sections where the student scored lowest. Reteach those skills (1-2 sessions each) before advancing. The student may begin Unit 2 while continuing to practice weak areas. |
| **Developing** | Below 18 (<70%) | Significant gaps in foundational phonological awareness or letter knowledge | Return to the earliest section where the student struggled. Reteach from that point forward using the reteaching guide below. Do not advance to Unit 2 until at least "Approaching" level is reached. |

---

## Section-Specific Reteaching Guide

| If the student struggles in... | What it tells you | Reteach with... |
|-------------------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Section A (Rhyming)** | The student has difficulty hearing word-ending patterns | Daily nursery rhyme practice. Return to Lesson 1. Play "Do these rhyme?" games at meals and during transitions. Use strongly contrasting pairs first (cat/hat vs. cat/tree) before subtle pairs. |
| **Section B (Sound Isolation)** | The student cannot isolate individual sounds within words | Return to Lesson 2 (beginning sounds) using only continuant sounds (/m/, /s/, /f/, /n/) that can be stretched. Once beginning sounds are solid, move to Lesson 3 (ending sounds). Play "I Spy" by beginning sound daily. If difficulty persists after 2 weeks, consider a hearing screening. |
| **Section C (Blending/Segmenting)** | The student cannot combine or break apart sounds | Return to Lesson 4. Start with 2-sound words (/a/-/t/ = at) and build to 3-sound words. Use blocks or tokens to make sounds visual and physical. Play "Robot Talk" daily (speak in segmented sounds and have the student translate). |
| **Section D (Letter Recognition)** | The student cannot reliably name letters or state their sounds | Create a "focus stack" of only the missed letters. Practice these 3-5 times per session using multi-sensory methods (sand tray, playdough letters, letter cards). Add 1-2 new letters to the stack only when the current ones are solid. Review Lessons 5-6 for specific letters. |
| **Section E (Letter-Sound Matching)** | The student knows letter names but cannot connect them to sounds | Emphasize the picture cue for each letter ("B says /b/ like ball"). Practice with the alphabet chart. Play matching games: say a sound, student finds the letter. Focus on sounds, not just names. |
| **Section F (Letter Formation)** | The student has difficulty writing letters | Increase multi-sensory writing practice: sand/salt tray, air writing, tracing with finger on textured surfaces. Use verbal stroke descriptions consistently. Focus on 3-4 letters at a time. If fine motor skills are a broad concern (not just letters), consider consulting an occupational therapist. |

## Detailed Rubric by Skill

### Rhyming (Section A)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Recognizes rhyming pairs | Correctly identifies rhyming and non-rhyming pairs with confidence | Correctly identifies most pairs but occasionally confuses rhyming with alliteration (same beginning sound) | Cannot reliably distinguish rhyming from non-rhyming pairs |
| Produces rhyming words | Generates rhyming words (real or nonsense) independently when given a starter word | Can produce a rhyme with a prompt or a choice ("Does 'lake' or 'dog' rhyme with cake?") | Cannot produce a rhyming word even with support |

### Sound Isolation (Section B)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Isolates beginning sounds | Identifies the beginning sound in words accurately, including both continuant and stop consonants | Identifies beginning sounds for continuant consonants (/m/, /s/, /f/) but struggles with stop consonants (/b/, /t/, /k/) | Cannot reliably isolate the beginning sound; may say the whole word or a different sound |
| Isolates ending sounds | Identifies the ending sound in words accurately | Identifies ending sounds with support (stretching the sound) but not independently | Cannot isolate ending sounds |

### Blending and Segmenting (Section C)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Blends sounds into words | Blends 3 sounds into a word correctly and quickly | Blends sounds into a word when sounds are said slowly with short pauses; needs repetition | Cannot blend 3 separate sounds into a word even with support |
| Segments words into sounds | Segments CVC words into 3 individual sounds accurately | Segments words into 2 parts (onset-rime, e.g., /h/-/at/) but not into 3 individual sounds | Cannot segment a word into its component sounds |

### Letter Knowledge (Sections D, E, F)

| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Letter recognition (name) | Names 22+ of 26 letters correctly (uppercase or lowercase) | Names 15-21 letters correctly | Names fewer than 15 letters |
| Letter-sound correspondence | States the correct sound for 22+ of 26 letters | States the correct sound for 15-21 letters | States the correct sound for fewer than 15 letters |
| Letter formation | Writes recognizable forms of most letters (uppercase and lowercase) with generally correct stroke patterns | Writes recognizable forms of some letters; several letters are reversed or unrecognizable | Writes very few recognizable letters; significant difficulty with formation |
| Sound-to-letter matching | Correctly points to the letter when given its sound | Matches most sounds to letters but confuses some similar sounds (e.g., /b/ and /d/) | Cannot reliably match sounds to their letters |

## Cumulative Review Items

These items review and connect skills across the unit. They are NOT scored as part of the unit assessment but provide useful diagnostic information for planning next steps.

**R1.** "I'm going to say sounds. Tell me the word AND point to the letters: /c/ ... /a/ ... /t/." (Place letter cards C, A, T on the table.) Can the student connect oral blending to written letters?

**R2.** "What letter does your name start with? What sound does that letter make?" (Connects personal relevance to letter-sound knowledge.)

**R3.** Show a simple three-letter word written on paper (e.g., "sun"). "Can you point to each letter, say its sound, and try to read the word?" (This previews the decoding skill taught in Unit 2. Many students will not be able to do this yet — that is completely expected.)

Note the student's responses to these items in your observations — they inform readiness and pacing for Unit 2.

## Facilitator Observations (Record During Assessment)

Use this space to note qualitative observations:

- Does the student approach the activities with confidence or hesitation?
- Which phonological awareness tasks seem automatic vs. effortful?
- Does the student use any strategies (stretching sounds, looking at mouth position, using fingers for segmenting)?
- How does the student hold the pencil? Is letter formation fluid or labored?
- Which specific letters does the student confuse? (Note the exact confusion pairs: b/d, p/q, m/n, etc.)
- Does the student self-correct any errors?
- Does the student show frustration or anxiety at any point?

These notes help inform differentiation decisions and pacing for Unit 2.
