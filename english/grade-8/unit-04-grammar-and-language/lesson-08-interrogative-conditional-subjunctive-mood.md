# English Language Arts — Grade 8 — Unit 4, Lesson 8

## Topic
Verb Moods: Interrogative, Conditional, and Subjunctive

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify and form sentences in the interrogative, conditional, and subjunctive moods
- Explain when each mood is used (asking real questions, expressing hypotheticals/conditions, expressing wishes or contrary-to-fact statements)
- Write original sentences correctly in each of the three moods, including the subjunctive forms "were" and "be"

## Prerequisites
- Indicative and imperative moods (Lesson 7, L.8.1c)
- Familiarity with modal auxiliaries (would, could, might, should) from Grade 7 (L.7.1)
- Understanding of conditional "if/then" sentence structures from Grade 7 reading/writing
- Ability to identify main and subordinate clauses (Grade 7, L.7.1)

## Materials Needed
- Student notebook or grammar journal
- Lesson 8 handout (or projected sentences)
- "Mood Detector" anchor chart from Lesson 7 (to be extended today)
- Highlighters (one for the mood-carrying verb form, one for the key signal word/phrase)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of conditional and wishful thinking through relatable scenarios; surface the language structures students already use for these.

**Activity: Real or Not Real?**

Display these sentences and ask: "Is each sentence describing something real, something possible, or something the speaker knows is not true right now?"

1. *If I finish my homework by 5 PM, I can go to the game tonight.*
2. *If I were a professional athlete, I would train six hours a day.*
3. *I wish our team were playing in the championship this year.*
4. *What would happen if the school switched to a four-day week?*

Say: "Sentence 1 describes a realistic condition — it might happen. Sentences 2, 3, and 4 describe things the speaker knows are NOT currently true: she is not a professional athlete, the team is not in the championship, the school has not switched schedules. English grammar has a special mood for these non-real, hypothetical situations — the subjunctive. And a separate mood for asking questions — the interrogative. Let's unpack all three."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Define and illustrate interrogative, conditional, and subjunctive moods; highlight key signal words and verb forms; extend the Mood Detector.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Interrogative mood:** The mood used to ask a question. Formed through subject-auxiliary inversion or "do/does/did" insertion.
- **Conditional mood:** The mood used to express actions or states that depend on a condition. Often uses modal auxiliaries: would, could, might, should.
- **Subjunctive mood:** The mood used to express wishes, demands, hypothetical situations, or conditions contrary to fact. Key forms: "were" (for all subjects in contrary-to-fact conditions), and base form of the verb after verbs of demand/recommendation.

### Instruction Notes

**Interrogative Mood:**

Say: "The interrogative mood is used to ask questions — specifically, questions about reality (not hypothetical questions, which lean toward conditional or subjunctive). It's formed by inverting the subject and auxiliary verb, or by adding 'do/does/did.'"

Write on the board:
- Standard inversion: *Has the data been verified by the research team?*
- Do/does/did insertion: *Do the results support the original hypothesis?*
- Wh-question: *When did the school first implement this policy?*

Say: "You already know how to form questions — the key today is recognizing that interrogative is a grammatical mood. Most questions in everyday English are interrogative mood."

**Conditional Mood:**

Say: "Conditional mood expresses what would, could, should, or might happen given a particular condition. It signals possibility, not reality. The key markers are the modal auxiliaries: would, could, might, should."

Write on the board:
- *If we practice every day this week, we could win the tournament.* (possible condition)
- *She would have scored higher if she had reviewed the notes.* (past counterfactual)
- *You might enjoy the documentary if you're interested in marine biology.*

Structure: **If + [condition], + modal + base verb**
Note: The "if" clause itself is often in the indicative or subjunctive mood; the main clause is in the conditional.

**Subjunctive Mood:**

Say: "The subjunctive is the mood for the 'unreal.' It covers three main uses."

Write the three uses:

**Use 1 — Contrary-to-fact conditions (hypotheticals):**
- Signal: "If I/he/she/it **were**..." (NOT "was" — this is the key subjunctive marker)
- *If she were the team captain, she would change the warm-up routine.*
- *If the deadline were a week later, I could do much more research.*

Say: "The subjunctive uses 'were' for all persons — not just 'you' and 'they,' but 'I,' 'he,' 'she,' 'it.' This is the giveaway. 'If I was' is informal but 'If I were' is the standard subjunctive form for hypotheticals."

**Use 2 — Wishes:**
- Signal: "I wish + **were**" or "I wish + past tense"
- *I wish the weather were warmer for the outdoor performance.*
- *She wishes she were better at reading music notation.*
- *He wishes the competition were on a different weekend.*

**Use 3 — Demands, recommendations, and requirements (that-clauses):**
- Signal: verb of demand/recommendation/requirement + "that" + subject + **base form**
- Verbs that trigger this: demand, recommend, suggest, require, insist, propose, ask (formally)
- *The coach insisted that every player arrive on time for the bus.*
- *The teacher recommended that Marcus revise his opening paragraph.*
- *The rules require that each competitor complete the course independently.*

Say: "Notice: it's NOT 'that Marcus revises' or 'that every player arrives.' After verbs of demand or recommendation, we use the base form — no third-person -s, no past tense. This is the subjunctive base form."

**Extend the Mood Detector chart (add to existing chart):**
> - Is the sentence asking a question (subject-auxiliary inversion or do/does/did)? → Interrogative
> - Does the sentence use would/could/might/should + a main verb, expressing what might happen under a condition? → Conditional
> - Does the sentence use "If + were" (contrary-to-fact), "I wish + were," or "that + base verb" after a verb of demand or recommendation? → Subjunctive

### Worked Examples

| Sentence | Mood | Key Signal |
|----------|------|-----------|
| Are the results from the final trial included in the report? | Interrogative | Subject-aux inversion; asking a real question |
| If the power stays on, we could finish the project by tonight. | Conditional (main clause) | "could" + base verb; possible condition |
| If I were in charge of the school schedule, I would add a study hall period. | Subjunctive (if-clause) + Conditional (main clause) | "If I were" = contrary-to-fact subjunctive |
| I wish the match were on a Friday instead of a Monday morning. | Subjunctive | "I wish + were" = wish for a non-real situation |
| The regulations demand that all submissions be received by midnight. | Subjunctive | "demand that + base form" (be, not is) |
| Would you change anything about the school's homework policy? | Conditional/Interrogative | "would" + question form; hypothetical question |

## Guided Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Apply the Mood Detector to identify all five moods (adding interrogative, conditional, subjunctive to Lesson 7's indicative and imperative); practice forming correct subjunctive constructions.

**Part A — Mood Detector (all five moods):** Label each sentence with its mood: IND, IMP, INT (interrogative), COND (conditional), or SUBJ (subjunctive).

1. The committee requires that each entry include a written explanation.
2. Have you ever read a novel that was entirely written in second person?
3. She would perform better in competition if she slept more before events.
4. Bring your laptop fully charged to every class meeting.
5. I wish the library were open on Sunday evenings.
6. The school updated its technology guidelines this semester.
7. If I were more comfortable with public speaking, I would join the debate team.
8. Could the science fair be held outside in the courtyard this year?

**Part B — Subjunctive Correction:** Each sentence contains an incorrect verb form where the subjunctive is needed. Rewrite correctly.

1. If I was the mayor, I would add more parks and green spaces to the city.
2. The director suggested that the lead actor memorizes the monologue before Thursday.
3. I wish my best friend was attending the same school next year.
4. The handbook states that every student submits a portfolio at the end of the year.

**Facilitator support:** For Part B, item 4: "states" is indicative — it's reporting a fact. The "that" clause here is a noun clause reporting what the handbook says, not a demand. So "submits" may actually be correct indicative mood. Discuss: Is "states that every student submits" (factual report) different from "requires that every student submit" (demand)? Yes — the verb in the main clause determines whether the subjunctive base form is needed.

## Independent Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Independently identify all five moods, correct subjunctive errors, and produce sentences in the three new moods.

**Part A — Five-Mood Identification:** Label each sentence: IND, IMP, INT, COND, or SUBJ.

1. The principal recommended that the new schedule be tested for one semester.
2. Record your observations in the data table as you complete each trial.
3. If the project were due today, half the class would be unprepared.
4. How many students enrolled in the advanced music program this year?
5. She could have made the final performance if she had practiced the high note.
6. I wish the audition were on a day when I didn't have three other exams.
7. The team captain insisted that all players attend the Sunday practice.
8. What role does social media play in the way teenagers experience friendship?
9. Scientists would be able to predict earthquakes more accurately if the technology existed.
10. Share your draft with a partner and ask for specific feedback on your introduction.

**Part B — Error Correction:** Rewrite each sentence with the correct subjunctive form.

1. "If she was more organized, she would forget fewer deadlines."
2. "My coach always insists that I keeps a training log after every session."
3. "I wish the competition was scheduled for after exam week."
4. "The club president proposed that everyone brings a dish to the end-of-year celebration."

**Part C — Sentence Writing:**

Write one original sentence for each of the three new moods:
1. **Interrogative** — Ask a genuine question about school life, science, or technology.
2. **Conditional** — Use a modal auxiliary (would/could/might) to describe something that might happen under a condition.
3. **Subjunctive** — Use "If I were," "I wish," or "verb of demand + that + base form" to express something non-real, desired, or required.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A and B; write any 2 of the 3 sentences in Part C.
**Stretch:** Complete all parts; in Part C, write sentences of at least 15 words each and include a subordinate clause in the conditional sentence.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts; then write a paragraph of 5–6 sentences that includes at least one example of each of the five moods (IND, IMP, INT, COND, SUBJ). Label each mood in the margin.

### Interleaved Review
1. *Lesson 7 review — Indicative vs. Imperative:* Label the mood and explain: "Keep your phone on silent during the performance."
2. *Lesson 2 review — Participial phrases:* Add correct punctuation and identify the participial phrase: "Exhausted from a week of finals she closed her laptop and went to sleep."

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. What is wrong with this sentence? Rewrite it correctly: "If he was more confident, he would try out for the school play."
2. Identify the mood and explain: "The director requires that each scene be rehearsed at least three times before the final performance."
3. Write one sentence using the subjunctive mood to express a wish.

**Expected Answers:**
1. "Was" should be "were" — subjunctive mood for a contrary-to-fact condition. Fix: "If he were more confident, he would try out for the school play."
2. Subjunctive mood — "require that" + base form "be" (not "is"). Verbs of requirement trigger the subjunctive base form in the that-clause.
3. Any correctly formed wish sentence using "I wish + were" or "I wish + past tense." Sample: "I wish the weather were warm enough to hold the ceremony outside."

**Mastery = 2 out of 3 correct, including item 3 (production)**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Focus reteaching on the two key subjunctive markers: (1) "If + were" for contrary-to-fact conditions, and (2) "verb of demand/recommendation + that + base form." These two patterns cover the majority of subjunctive usage in Grade 8 writing. Practice with 4 targeted sentences before moving on.

### If Mastery Is Met
Invite the student to write one "What if?" question (interrogative) plus the hypothetical answer (conditional + subjunctive) as a creative warm-up for Lesson 9.

## Extension (Optional)
1. Write a short "wishful thinking" paragraph (5–6 sentences) about a change you would make to your school, city, or the world. Use at least three subjunctive constructions (wishes, contrary-to-fact conditions, or demands). Label each.
2. Find three examples of the subjunctive mood in a novel, poem, or song lyrics you know well. Copy each sentence or line and explain which type of subjunctive it is (wish, contrary-to-fact, or demand/recommendation).
3. Debate the following in writing (3–4 sentences): "If I were president of the school board, I would change ____." Use at least two different verb moods (subjunctive, conditional, and/or imperative) in your response. Label each mood.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The subjunctive "were" form is the conceptual centerpiece of this lesson. Students who understand "If I were" (not "If I was") have grasped the essential subjunctive pattern. The "verb of demand + that + base form" pattern is important for formal writing but can be introduced as a secondary focus if time is short.
- In Part B of guided practice, item 4 is a genuine gray area — make sure to discuss it. If the main clause verb is "states" (reports a fact), the embedded "that" clause uses indicative. If the main clause verb is "requires" or "demands" (expresses a requirement), the embedded clause uses subjunctive base form. This is a sophisticated distinction that even careful adult writers sometimes miss.
- Students often confuse conditional mood with subjunctive mood because both involve hypotheticals. The distinction: the "if-clause" in a contrary-to-fact condition is subjunctive ("If I were"); the main result clause ("I would...") is conditional. Both are operating together.
- The interrogative mood is the most straightforward of the three new moods. Don't spend too long on it — students know how to form questions. The goal is simply naming and recognizing it as a grammatical category.
- For the challenge extension (paragraph with all five moods), circulate and check that students are not forcing moods artificially. The goal is natural integration; a labored example of every mood in one paragraph can feel stilted.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

**Part A:**
1. SUBJ — "requires that + be" (base form in that-clause)
2. IMP — base form "record"; understood "you"
3. SUBJ (if-clause) / COND (main clause) — "If the project were" = contrary-to-fact subjunctive; "would be" = conditional
4. INT — wh-question "How many"
5. COND — "could have made" = past conditional; past counterfactual
6. SUBJ — "I wish + were" = wish for non-real condition
7. SUBJ — "insisted that + attend" (base form in that-clause)
8. INT — wh-question "What role"
9. COND — "would be able to" = conditional main clause; "if the technology existed" = indicative condition (this is a realistic but currently false condition, bordering on subjunctive — accept either COND or SUBJ for the whole)
10. IMP — base forms "share" and "ask"; understood "you"

**Part B:**
1. "If she were more organized, she would forget fewer deadlines."
2. "My coach always insists that I keep a training log after every session." (base form "keep," no -s)
3. "I wish the competition were scheduled for after exam week."
4. "The club president proposed that everyone bring a dish to the end-of-year celebration." (base form "bring," no -s)

**Part C — Sample Answers:**
1. (Interrogative) "Has the school district announced whether the arts program will receive additional funding next year?"
2. (Conditional) "If the team practiced together three times a week, they could significantly improve their coordination before the regional tournament."
3. (Subjunctive) "I wish the school library were open on weekend evenings so students had a quiet place to study during exam season."

### Interleaved Review
1. Imperative mood — "keep" is a base form verb with an understood "you" subject; the sentence issues an instruction or direction.
2. "Exhausted from a week of finals, she closed her laptop and went to sleep." — Comma after the introductory participial phrase "Exhausted from a week of finals." The phrase modifies "she."

### Exit Ticket
1. Error: "was" should be "were" (contrary-to-fact subjunctive). Fix: "If he were more confident, he would try out for the school play."
2. Subjunctive — "require that + base form 'be'" — verbs of requirement take the subjunctive base form in the that-clause. "Be" (not "is") confirms the subjunctive.
3. Sample: "I wish the final exam were a project instead of a multiple-choice test."
