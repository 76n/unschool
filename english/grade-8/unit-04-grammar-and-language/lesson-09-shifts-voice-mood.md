# English Language Arts — Grade 8 — Unit 4, Lesson 9

## Topic
Recognizing and Correcting Shifts in Voice and Mood

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify inappropriate shifts in verb voice (active/passive) and verb mood within sentences and across paragraphs
- Apply the "Shift Scanner" strategy to locate and correct voice and mood shifts in student-level writing
- Revise a passage to maintain consistent and purposeful voice and mood throughout

## Prerequisites
- Active and passive voice and the Voice Flip strategy (Lessons 5–6, L.8.1b)
- All five verb moods: indicative, imperative, interrogative, conditional, and subjunctive (Lessons 7–8, L.8.1c)
- Understanding of parallel structure from Grade 7 grammar work
- Experience with revision and editing for clarity and consistency (Grade 8 Units 1–3)

## Materials Needed
- Student notebook or grammar journal
- Lesson 9 handout (or projected passages)
- Two colors of highlighter (one for voice shifts, one for mood shifts)
- "Shift Scanner" reference card (created during instruction)
- Optional: a recent student writing draft for the revision section

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Let students experience the disorienting effect of voice and mood shifts before naming the grammatical concept.

**Activity: Something Feels Off**

Display the following paragraph. Ask the student to read it and mark anywhere it feels "off" or inconsistent, even if they can't yet name the problem:

> "The documentary crew arrived at the research station before sunrise. The equipment was carefully unloaded by them, and the first interview was conducted. Then they begin setting up the cameras while a map of the habitat is being reviewed by the lead researcher. If the weather cooperates, the footage could be captured in one day. They hiked to the observation post, and the morning light was used to film the first segment."

Say: "You probably noticed some awkward shifts. Let's name exactly what's happening: the sentence switches unpredictably between active and passive voice, and there's an unexpected tense/mood shift ('begin'). Today we're going to build a strategy for catching and fixing these shifts."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Define inappropriate shifts; distinguish between acceptable variation and problematic inconsistency; introduce the Shift Scanner strategy.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Shift in voice:** An abrupt, unmotivated change from active to passive voice (or vice versa) within a sentence or between closely related sentences, creating an inconsistency in perspective and agency.
- **Shift in mood:** An abrupt, unmotivated change from one verb mood to another (e.g., from indicative to imperative, or from conditional to indicative) without a clear rhetorical reason.
- **Shift Scanner:** A revision strategy for systematically checking verb voice and mood consistency sentence by sentence in a passage.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Not all voice or mood variation is a problem. Changing from active to passive voice between paragraphs can be intentional. Moving from indicative to imperative is natural in a how-to piece. A 'shift' becomes a problem when it's unmotivated — when it confuses the reader or disrupts the logic of the passage."

**What makes a shift "inappropriate":**

1. **Inconsistent subject perspective within a sentence:**
   - Problematic: *Marcus studied the play diagram, and then the ball was thrown by him.*
   - Why it's wrong: Marcus is the active agent in the first clause; the sentence then flips to passive mid-thought, pulling the reader's attention away from Marcus without reason.
   - Fix: *Marcus studied the play diagram and then threw the ball.*

2. **Unexplained mood shift within a paragraph:**
   - Problematic: *Every student should prepare their materials the night before. Also, phones need to be charged. Then you put your work in the folder and submit it first thing.*
   - Analysis: Shifts from imperative ("prepare") to passive/indicative ("need to be charged") to imperative again ("put," "submit") — the inconsistency makes the register feel uncontrolled.
   - Fix: Commit to imperative throughout if this is a set of instructions: *Prepare your materials the night before. Charge your phone. Place your work in the folder and submit it first thing.*

3. **Shift from conditional/subjunctive to indicative within one thought:**
   - Problematic: *If the weather were to clear up, the game could start on time, and the team starts warming up at noon.*
   - Why it's wrong: "could start" is conditional (dependent on weather), but "starts" is indicative (states a fact). They can't logically coexist — the game hasn't started yet.
   - Fix: *If the weather were to clear up, the game could start on time, and the team would begin warming up at noon.*

**Introduce the Shift Scanner strategy:**

Write on the board:
> **Shift Scanner — Steps:**
> 1. Read the passage and underline every main verb (or verb phrase).
> 2. Label each: Act (active), Pass (passive), IND, IMP, COND, SUBJ.
> 3. Look for any change in voice or mood label that seems unmotivated.
> 4. Ask: "Is there a good reason for this shift?" If yes — keep it. If no — revise.
> 5. When revising, decide which voice/mood is correct for the passage's purpose and commit to it.

**Distinguish intentional variation from problematic shifts:**
- Acceptable: A science lab report uses passive voice throughout (consistent, purposeful).
- Acceptable: An essay moves from indicative (body paragraphs) to imperative (call-to-action conclusion).
- Problematic: A narrative randomly switches between active and passive within a paragraph, with no shift in focus or perspective.

## Guided Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Apply the Shift Scanner to identify and classify shifts; practice revising for consistency.

**Part A — Identifying Shifts:** For each item, label whether the shift in voice or mood is (A) acceptable (intentional, appropriate) or (P) problematic (unmotivated, inconsistent). Explain in one sentence.

1. [Two consecutive sentences in a how-to article] *Mix the ingredients until the batter is smooth. The pan should be greased before pouring in the batter.*
2. [Two consecutive sentences in a sports narrative] *She drove through the lane and laid the ball softly off the glass. The defensive player was beaten by her step-back move the possession before.*
3. [A lab report] *The solution was heated to boiling and then allowed to cool. We poured the cooled solution into the filtration apparatus.*
4. [Instructions for a school project] *Research your topic thoroughly. Then you should be writing a rough draft. Include at least three sources.*

**Part B — Shift Scanner in Action:**

Apply the Shift Scanner to the following passage. Underline every verb or verb phrase, label each (Act/Pass, and mood if clear), then mark the shifts with a star. Revise the passage so the voice and mood are consistent.

> "The robotics team prepared carefully for the state competition. Three different challenges had to be completed by them in under an hour. The team's strategy was reviewed every evening that week. On competition day, the team arrives at the venue early and the equipment is set up quickly. They competed well in the first two challenges. But the final task was not completed successfully."

**Facilitator support:** In Part A, item 3: A shift from passive to active in a lab report is problematic — scientific writing typically maintains passive throughout; the "we poured" introduces a personal pronoun that breaks the convention. Item 2 is also problematic: the narrative shifts away from the hero mid-paragraph for no clear reason.

## Independent Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Independently use the Shift Scanner on a longer passage, revise for consistency, and reflect on voice/mood choices.

**Part A — Full Shift Scanner:** Apply the Shift Scanner to the following student essay excerpt. Mark every problematic shift with a star, then rewrite the passage eliminating the inappropriate shifts. Maintain the original meaning.

> "Technology has changed the way teenagers communicate. Text messages and social media posts were sent by most students every day. The speed of communication has increased dramatically, but some important things are being lost. Students used to write letters or make phone calls. Now they scroll through feeds and emojis were used instead of full sentences. If teenagers were more thoughtful about their digital communication, important skills would not be lost. But most students don't even realize a problem exists, and the habit was formed early in childhood."

**Part B — Error Correction (sentence level):** Fix the voice or mood shift in each sentence.

1. "Priya opened her laptop, logged into the system, and then the assignment was submitted."
2. "If the school were to add a makerspace, students could build real projects, and they learn practical engineering skills."
3. "The coach asked the team to warm up for ten minutes, and then the drills were explained to them."
4. "He reviewed the rubric carefully and would notice that two sections were incomplete."

**Part C — Revision of Own Writing (Optional):**

Take a paragraph from a recent writing assignment (argumentative, narrative, or informational). Apply the Shift Scanner:
1. Underline every verb.
2. Label voice and mood.
3. Mark any inappropriate shifts.
4. Revise and bring the before/after to Lesson 10.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A (mark shifts and revise 4–5 sentences) and B; Part C is optional.
**Stretch:** Complete all parts; in Part A, write a brief explanation after your revision noting what shift pattern appeared most often and why it is problematic.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts including Part C; then write 3–4 sentences of analysis: "What are the most common voice and mood shifts students make, and what writing habits lead to them?" Draw on your own revision experience.

### Interleaved Review
1. *Lesson 6 review — Choosing voice:* A student writes: "The new cafeteria menu was voted on by the student council." Is passive voice appropriate here? Explain. If not, revise.
2. *Grade 7/Grade 8 — Parallel structure:* Is there a parallel structure error in this sentence? Fix it if so: "She enjoys writing poetry, to paint watercolors, and reading historical novels."

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. What is wrong with this sentence? Fix it: "Selena designed the poster layout, and then the colors were chosen by her."
2. Apply the Shift Scanner label to each verb phrase in this sentence: "If the team had practiced more consistently, they could win the tournament, and the trophy is displayed in the hallway."
3. Write one pair of sentences (2 sentences) on any topic that demonstrate consistent active voice throughout.

**Expected Answers:**
1. Voice shift: The sentence begins in active voice (Selena designed...) and shifts to passive mid-sentence without reason. Fix: "Selena designed the poster layout and then chose the colors." (Both verbs active, both with Selena as subject.)
2. "had practiced" — SUBJ/conditional (past perfect in if-clause); "could win" — COND; "is displayed" — PASS/IND (inappropriate shift — should be conditional: "would be displayed").
3. Any two sentences on the same topic with active voice throughout and no unmotivated shift.

**Mastery = 2 out of 3 correct, including item 3 (production)**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Focus the reteach on one type of shift: voice first (active/passive), since it's more concrete. Use three pairs of sentences and have the student label voice, then identify whether the shift is appropriate or not. Build the Shift Scanner habit one dimension at a time.

### If Mastery Is Met
Ask the student to apply the Shift Scanner to the opening paragraph of a current writing draft before Lesson 10. Bring any flagged shifts to discuss at the start of the integrated review lesson.

## Extension (Optional)
1. Find an example of a voice or mood shift in a piece of student-level published writing (school newspaper, online article by a student writer). Identify the shift, explain why it is problematic, and propose a revision.
2. Rewrite the warm-up paragraph from today's lesson (the documentary crew passage) so that it maintains consistent active voice throughout. Then rewrite it again in consistent passive voice. Compare the effect of each version.
3. Write a paragraph of 6–8 sentences about a challenging experience you've had or one you've observed. After writing it, apply the Shift Scanner to your own paragraph. Note any shifts you find — were they intentional?

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The Shift Scanner is the culminating strategy of the voice and mood sequence. It bridges all previous grammar knowledge (Lessons 5–8) with revision practice. Treat it as a genuine revision tool, not just a grammar exercise.
- The key conceptual challenge here is distinguishing between intentional variation and problematic shifting. Students often wonder: "How do I know if a shift is OK?" The answer: if you can explain why the voice or mood changed (new focus, new purpose, new speaker stance), it's likely intentional. If you cannot articulate a reason, revise.
- In the independent practice passage (student essay excerpt), there are multiple shifts. Don't expect perfect identification of every one — look for the student to find at least three and revise them coherently.
- Part C (revision of own writing) is the highest-value task in this lesson if a student has a draft available. The ability to self-edit for voice and mood consistency is a hallmark of skilled, mature writers.
- Exit ticket item 2 is a good discussion starter: the shift from conditional to indicative in a single sentence is the kind of subtle error that even skilled writers make. Celebrate students who catch it.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

**Part A — Revised Passage (sample):**
> "Technology has changed the way teenagers communicate. Most students send text messages and social media posts every day. The speed of communication has increased dramatically, but some important things are being lost. Students used to write letters or make phone calls. Now they scroll through feeds and use emojis instead of full sentences. If teenagers were more thoughtful about their digital communication, they would not lose these important skills. But most students don't even realize a problem exists, and they formed the habit early in childhood."

Shifts marked (stars):
- "were sent by most students" → "most students send" (passive → active; no reason for passive here)
- "emojis were used" → "use emojis" (passive → active; the actor is students)
- "the habit was formed" → "they formed the habit" (passive → active; consistent with active voice throughout)

**Part B:**
1. Fix: "Priya opened her laptop, logged into the system, and then submitted the assignment." (All active; Priya is the agent throughout.)
2. Fix: "If the school were to add a makerspace, students could build real projects and learn practical engineering skills." (Both main-clause verbs in conditional: "could build," "learn" — parallel.)
3. Fix: "The coach asked the team to warm up for ten minutes, and then explained the drills." (Both verbs active with "coach" as subject.)
4. Fix: "He reviewed the rubric carefully and noticed that two sections were incomplete." (Past indicative "noticed" — "would notice" implies a conditional that hasn't been established; both verbs should be past indicative.)

### Interleaved Review
1. Passive is acceptable here if the emphasis is on the menu being voted on (the outcome). However, if the student council's action is what matters, active is better: "The student council voted on the new cafeteria menu." Both can be justified; the key is having a reason for the choice.
2. Parallel structure error: "to paint watercolors" breaks the -ing gerund pattern. Fix: "She enjoys writing poetry, painting watercolors, and reading historical novels." (All gerunds: writing, painting, reading.)

### Exit Ticket
1. Voice shift mid-sentence. Fix: "Selena designed the poster layout and then chose the colors." (Both verbs active; Selena remains the agent.)
2. "had practiced" — conditional/subjunctive past perfect (if-clause); "could win" — COND; "is displayed" — PASS, IND — PROBLEMATIC SHIFT. Should be: "would be displayed" (conditional, maintaining the hypothetical frame).
3. Sample: "The developer released the software update at midnight. The new version fixed three major bugs and improved the app's loading speed." (Both sentences active; consistent voice.)
