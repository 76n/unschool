# English Language Arts — Grade 8 — Unit 4: Grammar and Language

## Unit 4 Assessment

### Overview

This assessment measures student mastery of all four L.8.1 language standards addressed in Unit 4.

**Standards Assessed:**
| Standard | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| L.8.1a | Explain the function of verbals (gerunds, participles, infinitives) in general and their function in particular sentences |

| L.8.1c | Form and use verbs in the indicative, imperative, interrogative, conditional, and subjunctive mood |

## Answer Key

### Section 1: Verbals

**1.** B — Subject of the sentence. The entire gerund phrase "Finishing the documentary in time for the film festival" occupies the subject position.

**2.** C — "connecting with her audience" is a gerund phrase functioning as a predicate nominative (subject = "her greatest strength"). Explanation: (A) "humming" is a participle (modifies generator); (B) "practicing" is part of a progressive verb phrase; (D) "Connected" is a past participle.

**3.** D — Adverb modifying "stayed." The infinitive phrase explains why she stayed after school — it tells the reason/purpose.

**4.** C — "Running toward the finish line, the medal felt almost within reach." The medal is not running. Dangling participial phrase — the agent "she" is absent from the sentence. (A), (B), and (D) correctly place the participial phrase next to the noun it logically modifies.

**5.** "Winning the regional debate championship" — G (Gerund phrase); Function: Subject of the sentence.

**6.** "to improve your typing speed" — INF (Infinitive phrase); Function: Adjective modifying "way."

**7.** "Exhausted from three consecutive days of competition" — PP (Participial phrase); Function: Adjective modifying "swimmer."

**8.** "reviewing every marked error from the previous attempt" — G (Gerund phrase); Function: Object of the preposition "by."

---

### Section 2: Active and Passive Voice

**9.** C — "The regional competition results were posted on the district website." Subject ("results") receives the action; verb = "were posted" (be + past participle). (A), (B), (D) are all active voice.

**10.** B — "The city's maintenance crew installed the new park benches last Tuesday." Agent ("maintenance crew") becomes subject; active past-tense verb "installed"; tense preserved.

**11.**
a. Label: A (Active). Voice Flip (passive): "200 native wildflowers were planted along the school's walking trail by the environmental club." (Accept version with agent omitted: "200 native wildflowers were planted along the school's walking trail.")
b. Label: P (Passive). Voice Flip (active): "The school displayed the championship trophy in the main hallway for the entire school year." (Award full credit for correct tense and agent placed as subject.)

**12.** Error: "was choosing" — incorrect; passive voice requires be + **past participle**, and the past participle of "choose" is "chosen," not "choosing." Corrected: "The winning essay was **chosen** by a panel of three judges for publication in the literary magazine."

**13.** Passive voice is not the most effective choice here because the student's achievement (Deja Williams earning the scholarship) is the story — she should be the subject, not the receiver of a passive construction. Revised: "Deja Williams, a tenth-grade student at Franklin High School, earned a scholarship for excellence in environmental science." (Award full credit for a clear explanation and a correctly revised sentence.)

---

### Section 3: Verb Moods

**14.** A — "If the cafeteria served more vegetarian options, more students would choose them." The if-clause uses "served" as a contrary-to-fact condition (subjunctive); the main clause uses "would choose" (conditional). The if-clause is the subjunctive. (Note: accept this as the correct answer even though the main clause is conditional — the sentence demonstrates subjunctive mood in the if-clause.) (B) is indicative; (C) is indicative; (D) is imperative.

**15.** B — "She would have performed better in the audition if she had practiced the final measure." Past conditional construction with "would have" + past participle. (A) "begins" is incorrectly indicative in a conditional frame (should be "will begin" or "could begin"); (C) is indicative; (D) is imperative.

**16.** C — Subjunctive. After the verb "require," the that-clause uses the subjunctive base form ("complete" — no third-person -s, no tense marking).

**17.**
a. INT — Subject-auxiliary inversion ("Have the results...been shared"); asking a question about reality.
b. IMP — Base form verb "review"; understood "you" subject; giving a directive.
c. SUBJ — "I wish + were" = wish for a non-real condition.
d. COND — "could proceed" = modal + base verb expressing a possible outcome dependent on a condition.

**18.** "If I **were** better at reading music notation, I would join the school orchestra without hesitation." ("was" → "were"; subjunctive mood required for contrary-to-fact conditional.)

---

### Section 4: Shifts in Voice and Mood

**19.** Type: Shift in voice (active to passive).
Explanation: The sentence begins with Marcus as the active agent ("packed," "double-checked") and then unmotivatedly shifts to passive voice ("was taken by him"), interrupting the consistent agent focus without purpose.

**20.** Assessment: Problematic — the shift from imperative ("Prepare," "Label," "Record") to indicative passive ("should be followed") in sentence 3 breaks the consistent imperative register of what is clearly a set of instructions.
Explanation: The passage is a set of lab directions; all sentences should use imperative mood. "The procedure should be followed" shifts to a passive/modal indicative construction that sounds indirect and inconsistent. Corrected sentence 3: "Follow the procedure exactly as written in the manual."

**21.** Corrected: "She researched the topic for two weeks, drafted an outline, and then wrote the first version of the paper." (Active voice throughout; she is the agent of all three verbs.)

**22.** Corrected: "If the school were to add a recording studio, students could produce original music, and the community **would learn** about their talent through annual showcases." ("learns" → "would learn"; maintaining the conditional mood established by "could produce" in the same hypothetical frame.)

### Section 5: Integrated Application

**Part A — Errors in the Original Paragraph (5 errors):**

| # | Original Error | Correction | Standard |
|---|---------------|------------|----------|
| 1 | "helped them to be ready" | "helped prepare them for the competition" (or "helped them prepare") — awkward infinitive; minor, but the cleaner revision removes the passive infinitive | L.8.1a / L.8.1b |
| 2 | "Three practice rounds were run by them each week" — passive with named agent | "They ran three practice rounds each week" — active voice; agent is the team, which is the consistent subject | L.8.1b |
| 3 | "the school would sends" — incorrect verb form in conditional | "the school would send" — conditional mood requires base form after "would" | L.8.1c |
| 4 | "hopes to performing" — incorrect verbal | "hopes to perform" — infinitive after "hopes"; base form required | L.8.1a |
| 5 | "the victory might finally be achieved by them" — passive in conditional; agent named unnecessarily | "they might finally achieve victory" — active voice; consistent with agent throughout; mood maintained as conditional | L.8.1b / L.8.1d |

**Sample Corrected Paragraph:**
> "The school's science olympiad team has spent months preparing for the regional tournament. Training intensively every afternoon helped prepare them for the competition. The team captain, knowing the material better than anyone, often coached the newer members after practice. They ran three practice rounds each week and always kept detailed notes. If they were to place in the top three, the school would send a team to the state competition. Everyone on the team hopes to perform at their best on tournament day. Working together as a unit, they might finally achieve the victory they have worked so hard for."

**Part B — Sample Acceptable Sentences:**

- "Memorizing the entire periodic table in one weekend [G — subject] turned out to be less helpful than she had expected." (Gerund phrase as subject)

- "Does the school offer any summer programs in computer science?" (Interrogative)
