# English Language Arts — Grade 8 — Unit 4, Lesson 5

## Topic
Active and Passive Voice

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify whether a sentence is written in active or passive voice and explain the structural difference
- Form passive voice sentences correctly using a form of "be" plus a past participle
- Convert sentences between active and passive voice using the "Voice Flip" strategy

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of subject, verb, and direct object in a sentence (Grade 7, L.7.1)
- Familiarity with past participles (Lesson 2 of this unit, L.8.1a)
- Understanding of transitive verbs (verbs that take a direct object) from Grade 7 grammar work
- Ability to identify the main verb and helping verbs in a sentence (Grade 7)

## Materials Needed
- Student notebook or grammar journal
- Lesson 5 handout (or projected sentences)
- One color of highlighter for the subject, another for the verb phrase
- "Voice Flip" strategy reference card (created during instruction)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Surface students' intuitive sense of who is "doing" vs. "receiving" an action before introducing formal vocabulary.

**Activity: Who Did It?**

Display these two sentences side by side:
1. *The school's software team launched a new app for student feedback.*
2. *A new app for student feedback was launched by the school's software team.*

Ask: "In sentence 1, who is doing the launching?" (The software team.) "In sentence 2, who is doing the launching?" (Still the software team — but is the team in the subject slot? No. The app is.)

Say: "The subject of sentence 2 receives the action rather than performing it. That's the difference between active and passive voice — not what the action is, but which participant occupies the subject slot. Today we're going to understand how this works structurally and how to flip between them."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Define active and passive voice structurally; teach the formation of passive voice; introduce the Voice Flip strategy.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Active voice:** The grammatical subject of the sentence performs the verb's action. Structure: Subject → Action → Object.
- **Passive voice:** The grammatical subject receives the verb's action. Structure: Object (as subject) → "be" verb + past participle → (by + agent, optional).
- **Agent:** The performer of the action in a passive sentence, introduced by "by" (often optional or omitted).
- **Voice Flip:** A strategy for consciously converting a sentence between active and passive voice to examine the effect of each.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "Active voice follows the most natural storytelling pattern: who did something, what they did, and to what. Active voice is usually clearer and more direct."

Write on the board:
- **Active:** Subject + verb + object
- **Passive:** Object (promoted to subject) + be + past participle (+ by + agent)

**Forming Passive Voice — Step by Step:**

Active: *The students designed a new app.*
Step 1: Move the object ("a new app") to the subject slot.
Step 2: Change the verb to the appropriate form of "be" + past participle.
Step 3: (Optional) Add "by + original subject" at the end.
Passive: *A new app was designed by the students.*

Tense must be maintained:
- Active (present): *The committee selects the winner.* → Passive: *The winner is selected by the committee.*
- Active (past): *The engineer repaired the circuit.* → Passive: *The circuit was repaired by the engineer.*
- Active (future): *The judges will announce the results.* → Passive: *The results will be announced by the judges.*
- Active (present perfect): *Scientists have discovered a new protein.* → Passive: *A new protein has been discovered by scientists.*

Write the **Voice Flip** strategy on the board:
> **Voice Flip:**
> 1. Find the action (verb) in the sentence.
> 2. Identify who is performing it (the agent) and what is receiving it (the patient).
> 3. In active voice: agent is subject. In passive voice: patient is subject.
> 4. Adjust the verb: active = base verb; passive = be + past participle.

**Identifying Voice in a Sentence — Key Questions:**
- Is the subject the one performing the action? → Active
- Is the subject receiving the action? → Passive
- Does the verb include a form of "be" + past participle? → Strong signal of passive (but check: "The cake was delicious" has "be" + adjective — not passive voice)

**Important note on be + adjective:**
Say: "Not every sentence with 'was' or 'is' is passive. 'The trophy was enormous' uses 'was' + an adjective — it describes a state, not a passive action. Passive voice requires 'be' + a past participle that comes from a transitive verb."

### Worked Examples

| Sentence | Voice | Key Evidence |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| The hurricane damaged hundreds of homes. | Active | Subject (hurricane) performs the action; standard subject-verb-object order |
| Hundreds of homes were damaged by the hurricane. | Passive | Subject (homes) receives the action; "were damaged" = be + past participle |
| The scientist published her findings last month. | Active | Subject (scientist) performs the action |
| Her findings were published in a leading journal. | Passive | Subject (findings) receives the action; agent omitted |
| The coach was thrilled with the result. | Neither — state of being | "Was thrilled" = be + adjective (thrilled here describes emotional state, not a true passive action from a transitive verb applied to the coach as receiver) |

## Guided Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Identify voice in sentences; apply Voice Flip to convert active to passive and passive to active.

**Part A — Identify the Voice:** Label each sentence A (Active), P (Passive), or N (Neither — be + adjective/state of being).

1. The documentary was filmed over three years in three countries.
2. Our class produced a short film for the district arts festival.
3. The final scores were posted at midnight.
4. Every participant was nervous before the competition began.
5. A new world record was broken at the championship meet.
6. The team captain announced the starting lineup.
7. The cafeteria roof was replaced over spring break.
8. Her performance in the recital was brilliant.

**Part B — Voice Flip:** Convert each sentence. For active sentences, rewrite in passive voice. For passive sentences, rewrite in active voice. Label whether the agent is included or omitted.

1. The principal revised the school's attendance policy.
2. The project was submitted two days before the deadline. (Assume students submitted it.)
3. Researchers identified three new species in the rainforest.
4. The trophy was awarded to the team with the most community service hours.

**Facilitator support:** In Part A, item 4 ("was nervous") — discuss: "Nervous" is an adjective describing an emotional state, not a past participle of a transitive verb applied to the participants as receivers. Compare with item 5: "was broken" — the record received the action of breaking, which comes from the transitive verb "break." This is passive.

## Independent Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Identify voice independently, apply Voice Flip accurately, and write original sentences in both active and passive voice.

**Part A — Identification:** Label each sentence Active (A) or Passive (P). Circle the verb phrase.

1. The environmental group planted five hundred trees along the riverbank.
2. The riverbank had been eroded by years of flooding and construction.
3. A new app update was released without any announcement.
4. Her photography has won multiple regional awards.
5. The experiment was conducted three times to confirm the results.
6. The drama club rehearses every Tuesday and Thursday after school.

**Part B — Error Correction:** A student tried to convert sentences between voices but made errors. Identify the mistake and rewrite the sentence correctly.

1. Original (active): *The coach praised the team's effort.*
   Student's passive: *The team's effort was praising the coach.*

2. Original (passive): *The mural was painted by a group of local artists.*
   Student's active: *A group of local artists is painting the mural.*

3. Original (active): *Three students identified an error in the research paper.*
   Student's passive: *An error was being identified in the research paper by three students were.*

**Part C — Sentence Writing:**

Write four original sentences:
1. A sentence in **active voice** about something happening in school or sports.
2. A sentence in **passive voice** about the same event or topic.
3. A sentence in **active voice** about science or technology.
4. A sentence in **passive voice** about science or technology, with the agent omitted.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Parts A and B; write sentences 1 and 2 in Part C.
**Stretch:** Complete all parts; in Part C, write all four sentences and briefly note why you might choose one voice over the other for each topic.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts; then examine a paragraph from a textbook or nonfiction article and identify every passive voice construction. Note whether the agent is included or omitted. What effect do the passive constructions create?

### Interleaved Review
1. *Lesson 3 review — Infinitives:* Identify the infinitive phrase and name its function: "To become a professional data analyst, she enrolled in every available coding course."
2. *Grade 7/Grade 8 — Modifiers:* Is there a dangling or misplaced modifier in this sentence? If so, fix it: "Worn out after a six-hour drive, the hotel bed felt amazing."

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Label each sentence Active (A) or Passive (P) and briefly explain how you know:
   - a. "A new policy was announced by the superintendent this morning."
   - b. "The superintendent announced a new policy this morning."
2. Apply the Voice Flip: rewrite sentence (a) from item 1 in active voice, and sentence (b) in passive voice.
3. Write one original sentence in passive voice with the agent omitted. What kind of writing situation might make omitting the agent a reasonable choice?

**Expected Answers:**
1. a. Passive — subject ("policy") receives the action; verb = "was announced" (be + past participle). b. Active — subject ("superintendent") performs the action.
2. Voice Flip a → active: "The superintendent announced a new policy this morning." Voice Flip b → passive: "A new policy was announced by the superintendent this morning."
3. Any passive sentence without a "by + agent" phrase (e.g., "The experiment was conducted three times"). Appropriate situations: agent unknown, agent unimportant, formal/scientific writing, etc.

**Mastery = 2 out of 3 correct, including item 3 (production)**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Have the student practice the Voice Flip with 3 simple subject-verb-object sentences before moving on. Emphasize the be + past participle formula for passive voice formation. Use short, clear sentences to remove complexity.

### If Mastery Is Met
Ask the student to find one active and one passive construction in something they have read recently (textbook, article, novel). Bring both examples to Lesson 6, where we discuss when to use each voice purposefully.

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. Analyze a short paragraph from a science textbook. Count how many sentences are in passive voice. Why might science writers use passive voice so often? Write 2–3 sentences explaining your hypothesis.
2. Take a paragraph you have written recently and apply the Voice Flip to every sentence. Then compare the two versions: which reads more clearly? Which sounds more formal? Write a brief reflection.
3. Find a news headline in passive voice (e.g., "Three students honored at ceremony"). Rewrite it in active voice. Does the active version feel more or less appropriate for a headline? Explain.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The most important conceptual distinction: passive voice is NOT just having a "be" verb. Many students overflag sentences as passive because of "was" + an adjective or "was" + an active verb in a past tense clause. The test is: is the subject receiving an action performed by something else? And does the verb consist of be + past participle of a transitive verb?
- The Voice Flip is the key strategy here. Practice it explicitly — say the steps out loud when converting sentences. Students benefit from seeing the structural transformation spelled out in slow motion.
- For Part B of independent practice (error correction), item 1 is the most common student error: flipping "praised" into a progressive ("was praising") rather than passive ("was praised"). This is worth isolating.
- Keep the emotional register neutral when discussing passive voice as "weaker." Many students have been told to "never use passive voice." The goal of this lesson is structural understanding; Lesson 6 addresses the purposeful choice between voices.
- Watch for students who write passive voice but forget the appropriate tense of "be." A past-tense active sentence ("she completed the project") should become "the project was completed" — not "the project is completed."

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

**Part A:**
1. A — "planted" is the active verb; subject (group) performs the action
2. P — "had been eroded" = had been + past participle; subject (riverbank) receives the action
3. P — "was released" = was + past participle; subject (update) receives the action
4. A — "has won" is the active verb; subject (photography) performs the action
5. P — "was conducted" = was + past participle; subject (experiment) receives the action
6. A — "rehearses" is the active verb; subject (club) performs the action

**Part B:**
1. Error: "praising" is a present participle, not a past participle. Passive requires be + past participle. Fix: "The team's effort was praised by the coach."
2. Error: Tense shifted from past ("was painted") to present progressive ("is painting"). Fix: "A group of local artists painted the mural."
3. Error: Subject/verb agreement broken ("three students were"). Fix: "An error in the research paper was identified by three students."

**Part C — Sample Answers:**
1. (Active) The point guard drove to the basket and converted a layup with two seconds left.
2. (Passive) A layup was converted with two seconds left, winning the game.
3. (Active) Engineers at the lab designed a sensor that detects air quality in real time.
4. (Passive) A sensor capable of detecting air quality in real time was developed last year. (Agent omitted.)

### Interleaved Review
1. "To become a professional data analyst" — infinitive phrase functioning as an **adverb** (modifies "enrolled" — why/for what purpose she enrolled). Alternatively: it can be read as an adverb modifying the whole clause.
2. Dangling modifier — "Worn out after a six-hour drive" should modify a person, but the subject is "the hotel bed." Fix: "Worn out after a six-hour drive, she collapsed gratefully onto the hotel bed."

### Exit Ticket
1. a. Passive — subject = "policy" (receiver); "was announced" = be + past participle. b. Active — subject = "superintendent" (performer); "announced" is a straightforward past-tense active verb.
2. a → active: "The superintendent announced a new policy this morning." b → passive: "A new policy was announced by the superintendent this morning."
3. Sample: "The trophy was presented at the end of the ceremony." (Agent omitted.) Appropriate for: formal announcements, when the giver is unknown or unimportant, or when the receiver (the trophy recipient) deserves the emphasis.
