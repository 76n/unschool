# English Language Arts — Grade 8 — Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Topic
Integrated Grammar and Language Review

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify and label gerunds, participles, and infinitives (and their phrases) across a variety of sentences and paragraphs, explaining each one's grammatical function
- Identify verb voice (active/passive) and verb mood (indicative, imperative, interrogative, conditional, subjunctive) in context
- Apply the full suite of Unit 4 revision strategies (Verbal ID Test, Voice Flip, Mood Detector, Shift Scanner) to edit a multi-paragraph passage for grammar accuracy and stylistic quality

## Prerequisites
- All Lessons 1–9 of Unit 4 (L.8.1a–d): verbals, active/passive voice, five verb moods, and shift recognition
- Experience with paragraph-level revision and editing (Grade 8 Units 1–3)
- Familiarity with all named strategies: Verbal ID Test, Voice Flip, Verbal Choice, Mood Detector, Shift Scanner

## Materials Needed
- Student notebook or grammar journal
- Lesson 10 handout (or projected passage and exercises)
- All four reference cards/anchor charts from the unit (Verbal ID Test, Voice Flip, Mood Detector, Shift Scanner)
- Three colors of highlighter or pencil (one for verbals, one for voice markers, one for mood markers)
- Optional: student writing draft for self-editing

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate all four standards simultaneously through a rapid identification task; build confidence before the integrated work.

**Activity: Strategy Round-Up**

Display the following four sentences — one to illustrate each standard. Ask the student to quickly identify the grammatical feature named for each:

1. (L.8.1a) "Convincing her teammates to trust the new strategy was the hardest part of the season." — *What type of verbal is "convincing her teammates to trust the new strategy," and what is its grammatical function?*
2. (L.8.1b) "The final score was announced by the referee after a two-minute video review." — *What voice? If you flip it to the other voice, what changes?*
3. (L.8.1c) "If the technology existed today, scientists could map the ocean floor completely within a decade." — *What mood is the if-clause in? What mood is the main clause in?*
4. (L.8.1d) "She saved the document carefully and then the work was submitted." — *Is there a shift? If so, name it and fix it.*

Say: "You've built a toolkit over the last nine lessons. Today you're going to use every tool at once — on increasingly complex sentences and a full paragraph. Think of it as a practice run for the unit assessment."

---

## Direct Instruction (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Consolidate all four standards into an integrated framework; model the full editing process on a short passage; address any lingering misconceptions before the assessment.

### Key Vocabulary (Review)
- **Verbal:** Gerund (noun), participle (adjective), or infinitive (noun/adjective/adverb) — verb forms that function as other parts of speech.
- **Voice:** Active (subject acts) or passive (subject receives action).
- **Mood:** The grammatical mode of the verb — indicative, imperative, interrogative, conditional, or subjunctive.
- **Shift:** An unmotivated change in voice or mood within a sentence or passage.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "We've learned four distinct grammar concepts this unit. They're not four separate things — they work together in every sentence. A passive verb can be in indicative mood. A participial phrase can appear in a sentence with a conditional main verb. Let's practice seeing them simultaneously."

**Model the Integrated Edit Process:**

Display this short passage and think aloud through a complete edit:

> "Maria has been working on her environmental science research for three months. She collected water samples from five different sites, and each sample was analyzed by her for contaminants. Testing the samples carefully revealed surprising results. She hopes to present the findings at the spring science symposium, and the data should be published by her in the school research journal. If she does a good job, she can win a regional award and recognition is given to her school's program."

**Step 1 — Identify verbals:**
- "working" (line 1) — part of verb phrase "has been working," not a verbal
- "Testing the samples carefully" (line 3) — gerund phrase, subject
- "to present the findings" (line 4) — infinitive phrase, noun (direct object of "hopes")
- "to publish" — embedded in passive construction

**Step 2 — Label voice:**
- "has been working" — active; "was analyzed by her" — passive (unnecessary; revise to "she analyzed each sample")
- "should be published by her" — passive with named agent (she should publish); revise to active: "she should publish the data"
- "recognition is given to her school's program" — passive; in conditional context, should be conditional: "her school's program would receive recognition"

**Step 3 — Label mood:**
- Lines 1–2: indicative; line 3: indicative; line 4: indicative; "If she does... she can win" — conditional; "recognition is given" — indicative shift inside a conditional frame (problematic)

**Step 4 — Shift Scanner:**
- "was analyzed by her" — unmotivated passive shift (Maria is active agent throughout); fix
- "recognition is given" — mood shift from conditional to indicative; fix

**Revised passage (model):**
> "Maria has been working on her environmental science research for three months. She collected water samples from five different sites and analyzed each sample for contaminants. Testing the samples carefully revealed surprising results. She hopes to present the findings at the spring science symposium and to publish the data in the school research journal. If she does well, she could win a regional award, and her school's program would receive well-deserved recognition."

Say: "Notice that the revision also improved the style — fewer words, cleaner sentences. Good grammar editing and good writing revision are often the same thing."

**Quick Misconception Review (address whichever came up in exit tickets during Lessons 1–9):**
- Gerund vs. participle: "The Verbal ID Test still works — can you replace the -ing phrase with 'it' or 'this'?"
- Was/were (subjunctive): "Contrary-to-fact conditions use 'were' for all subjects, including 'I.'"
- Passive is not always wrong: "Passive is wrong only when it's unmotivated or creates a problematic shift."
- Voice and mood are separate dimensions: "A sentence can be passive and indicative, or active and subjunctive."

## Guided Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Collaboratively apply the integrated edit process to a new passage; practice labeling all four grammar dimensions simultaneously.

**Integrated Passage Analysis:**

Apply all four strategies to the following passage. Work through it sentence by sentence.

> "The drama club held its annual one-act festival last Friday evening. Four original plays were written and directed by students. Performing in front of a live audience is always nerve-wracking, but most students say it teaches them more than any classroom exercise. The first play, told entirely through movement and music, was the audience's favorite. If the festival were held twice a year, more students could participate. Every performer in the final show was amazing and the applause was deafening. The director insisted that each cast member write a reflection after the performance."

Tasks:
1. Underline every verbal phrase and label it: G (gerund), PP (participial), INF (infinitive).
2. Circle every passive construction. Mark each: (K) keep or (R) revise.
3. Label the mood of the main verb in each sentence.
4. Use the Shift Scanner: mark any inappropriate shifts.
5. Revise sentences 2 and 6 to eliminate problems.

**Facilitator support:** Guide the student through each sentence before they attempt the next. Pay particular attention to sentence 7 ("insisted that each cast member write") — this is correct subjunctive base form ("write," not "writes") and should be kept.

## Independent Practice (10–12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Independently complete a full integrated grammar edit of a longer student-level passage; write original sentences demonstrating mastery of all four standards.

**Part A — Full Integrated Edit:**

Apply all four strategies to the following passage. Label verbals (G/PP/INF), mark voice shifts, label moods, and apply the Shift Scanner. Then write a revised version of the full passage.

> "Running a school newspaper is harder than most students expect. The reporters have to gather facts, conduct interviews, and every article has to be submitted by them by Thursday evening. Writing accurate, fair stories requires careful attention to both evidence and language. The editor, responsible for reviewing every article before publication, works long hours each week. If she were less dedicated, the quality of the paper would suffer noticeably. She also recommends that every reporter reads at least two professional news articles each week. The photography team, equally committed, captures the images that bring each story to life. During production nights, the layout software is operated by students who stay until the work is completed."

**Part B — Production Tasks:**

Write four original sentences — one demonstrating each standard:

1. (L.8.1a) A sentence containing a gerund or infinitive phrase that you label and identify by function.
2. (L.8.1b) A sentence in passive voice where the passive is clearly the better choice — explain why in a parenthetical note.
3. (L.8.1c) A sentence in the subjunctive mood (use "If I/he/she were" or "verb of demand + that + base form").
4. (L.8.1d) Write one sentence that contains a voice or mood shift, then write the corrected version.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete Part A (label and revise 4–5 sentences that contain errors) and Part B items 1 and 3.
**Stretch:** Complete the full edit in Part A (all sentences) and all four items in Part B.
**Challenge:** Complete all parts; then write an original paragraph of 6–8 sentences on any topic of your choice. The paragraph must deliberately include: one gerund phrase as subject, one participial phrase (opening a sentence), one conditional or subjunctive sentence, and consistent active voice throughout (or a single deliberate and justifiable passive). Label each feature in the margin.

### Interleaved Review
1. *Grade 8 Unit 2 — Complex sentences:* Identify the dependent clause and its type (noun, adjective, or adverb): "Although the team had less practice time than their opponents, they managed to outperform them in every event."
2. *Grade 7 — Commas and coordinate adjectives:* Should a comma appear between the two adjectives in this sentence? "She wore a long elegant dress to the awards ceremony." Explain your reasoning.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. In the following sentence, identify: (a) the verbal phrase and its type and function; (b) the voice; (c) the mood.
   *"Inspired by the challenge, she decided to design an app that helps students track their study habits."*

2. Fix the shift: "The student council voted on the new policy, and then the results were announced by the president."

3. Name all four unit strategies and briefly describe what each one is used for (one phrase per strategy is enough).

**Expected Answers:**
1. (a) "Inspired by the challenge" = participial phrase (adjective modifying "she"); "to design an app that helps students track their study habits" = infinitive phrase (noun — direct object of "decided"). (b) Active voice — she is the agent who decided. (c) Indicative mood — statement of what she did.
2. Voice shift: "were announced by the president" is passive, but the sentence begins in active voice with "the student council voted." Fix: "The student council voted on the new policy, and then the president announced the results."
3. Verbal ID Test — identify gerunds vs. participles (can you replace it with "it"?); Voice Flip — convert between active and passive to examine effect; Mood Detector — identify which mood a verb is in; Shift Scanner — locate and fix unmotivated voice or mood shifts.

**Mastery = 2 out of 3 correct, including item 3 (production from memory)**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
Before the assessment, offer a 10-minute review session targeting the student's specific weak area. If verbals are the difficulty, focus on the Verbal ID Test with 5 sentences. If voice/mood are the difficulty, focus on the Mood Detector and Shift Scanner with 4–5 sentences.

### If Mastery Is Met
Confirm readiness for the unit assessment. Preview the assessment structure briefly: 5 sections, 60 points, covering all four standards. Remind the student to use the strategies — they are permitted during the assessment if recorded in their grammar journal.

## Extension (Optional)
1. Write a short article (8–10 sentences) for a school newspaper about a real or imaginary event. Apply all four standards deliberately: include at least one verbal phrase of each type (gerund, participle, infinitive), maintain consistent active voice except where passive is better, use at least two different moods, and apply the Shift Scanner to your own draft before submitting.
2. Review a piece of writing you produced earlier in Grade 8 (argumentative, informational, or narrative). Apply the full Shift Scanner. How many voice or mood issues do you find? Were they intentional or errors? Revise any problematic shifts.
3. Teach it back: without looking at your notes, explain all four Unit 4 strategies (Verbal ID Test, Voice Flip, Mood Detector, Shift Scanner) to someone else — a family member, friend, or study partner. Use one example sentence for each strategy.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- This lesson runs 50–55 minutes — protect the time for the full independent practice passage in Part A. If necessary, shorten the guided practice by completing only 3–4 sentences collaboratively, then releasing the student to work independently on the remainder.
- The warm-up "Strategy Round-Up" is deliberately rapid-fire. It is meant to be a confidence-builder, not a comprehensive review. Don't slow down on any single item; save depth for the guided and independent practice.
- In the guided practice passage analysis, sentence 7 ("insisted that each cast member write") is an intentional correct example of subjunctive mood — make sure the student recognizes it as such and does not "correct" it.
- For the production task in Part B, item 4 (write a sentence with a shift, then correct it) is metacognitive — it requires the student to deliberately produce an error. This is a high-level grammar skill and worth praising when done well.
- After this lesson, students should feel prepared for the unit assessment. A brief preview of the assessment format at the end of the lesson (5 sections, 60 points, ~45–55 minutes) can reduce anxiety and help students allocate time well.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice

**Part A — Analysis and Revision:**

Verbals:
- "Running a school newspaper" (sentence 1) — G, subject
- "Writing accurate, fair stories" (sentence 3) — G, subject
- "responsible for reviewing every article before publication" (sentence 4) — PP (participial phrase), modifies "editor"; "reviewing every article" contains an embedded gerund
- "to design an app..." — INF (if the model sentence appeared)
- "Equally committed" (sentence 7) — PP modifying "photography team"
- "that bring each story to life" (sentence 7) — embedded relative clause (not a verbal phrase, note for discussion)

Voice issues:
- "every article has to be submitted by them by Thursday evening" (sentence 2) — passive with named agent; revise to active: "they have to submit every article by Thursday evening"
- "is operated by students" (sentence 8) — passive; revise to active: "students operate the layout software"

Mood issues:
- "she also recommends that every reporter reads" (sentence 6) — error: subjunctive required after "recommends that"; fix: "reads" → "read" (base form)
- "is completed" (sentence 8) — indicative; inside a temporal clause "until the work is completed" — this is acceptable indicative; no shift

Shift issues:
- Sentence 2: shift from active/imperative-register (reporters have to gather...) to passive (has to be submitted by them) — unmotivated; revise
- Sentence 6: "reads" should be "read" (subjunctive base form error)
- Sentence 8: passive acceptable here — journalism/production context; consider keeping

**Sample Revised Passage:**
> "Running a school newspaper is harder than most students expect. The reporters have to gather facts, conduct interviews, and submit every article by Thursday evening. Writing accurate, fair stories requires careful attention to both evidence and language. The editor, responsible for reviewing every article before publication, works long hours each week. If she were less dedicated, the quality of the paper would suffer noticeably. She also recommends that every reporter read at least two professional news articles each week. The photography team, equally committed, captures the images that bring each story to life. During production nights, students operate the layout software and stay until the work is complete."

**Part B — Sample Answers:**
1. "Mastering the subjunctive mood [G — subject] takes consistent practice and a willingness to question your own assumptions."
2. "The winner of the regional science fair was announced at the closing ceremony." (Passive is better — the winner/announcement is the news, not the announcer.) [L.8.1b — appropriate passive, agent omitted]
3. "The committee required that every participant submit their entry form before midnight on Friday." [Subjunctive: "submit" = base form after "required that"]
4. With shift: "She wrote the introduction and then the rest of the essay was revised." / Corrected: "She wrote the introduction and then revised the rest of the essay." [Active voice throughout, shift removed]

### Interleaved Review
1. "Although the team had less practice time than their opponents" — dependent adverb clause (modifies the main clause by expressing a contrast/concession; introduced by "although").
2. "long" and "elegant" are cumulative adjectives (one modifies the noun + the other), not coordinate adjectives. You cannot naturally say "elegant and long dress" or reverse them with equal naturalness. No comma needed. (If the student argues comma, ask them to apply the "and" test: "a long and elegant dress" — this sounds plausible, so a comma could be defensible. Accept either with reasoning.)

### Exit Ticket
1. (a) "Inspired by the challenge" = participial phrase, adjective modifying "she"; "to design an app that helps students track their study habits" = infinitive phrase, noun (direct object of "decided"). (b) Active voice. (c) Indicative mood.
2. Fix: "The student council voted on the new policy, and then the president announced the results." (Active throughout; president as agent of the announcement.)
3. Verbal ID Test — distinguish gerunds from participles by testing substitution with "it/this." Voice Flip — convert a sentence between active and passive to examine what changes in emphasis. Mood Detector — identify which of the five moods a verb is in. Shift Scanner — systematically check a passage for unmotivated changes in voice or mood.
