# English Language Arts — Grade 6 — Unit 4, Lesson 4

## Topic
Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement: Special Cases

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Apply pronoun-antecedent agreement rules to collective noun antecedents (team, family, class)
- Select correct pronouns for indefinite pronoun antecedents (everyone, someone, each, few, several)
- Determine correct pronoun agreement for compound antecedents joined by "and," "or," and "nor"
- Edit a passage containing multiple pronoun-antecedent errors involving special cases

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of basic pronoun-antecedent agreement from Lesson 3
- Ability to identify pronouns and trace them to their antecedents
- Knowledge of indefinite pronouns from Lesson 2 (subject-verb agreement context)
- Familiarity with compound subjects from Lesson 2

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector)
- Printed or projected example sentences
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Colored pencils (green for pronouns, orange for antecedents)
- Indefinite pronoun reference chart (from Lesson 2)
- Index cards for sorting activities

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review Lesson 3 basics and set up the challenge of trickier antecedents.

**Activity: Quick Agreement Fix**

Say: "Fix the pronoun-antecedent error in each sentence. You have two minutes."

Write on the board:
1. The astronauts completed his mission successfully. (his → their)
2. A good student always turns in your homework on time. (your → his or her / their)
3. The old computer crashed, and it lost all of their data. (their → its)

Review answers. Then say: "Those were fairly straightforward — you could spot the antecedent and check the pronoun. But what happens when the antecedent is a word like 'team' or 'everyone' or 'either Marcus or his sisters'? Those are the special cases we tackle today."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach pronoun-antecedent agreement for collective nouns, indefinite pronouns, and compound antecedents.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Collective noun:** A noun that names a group of people, animals, or things acting as a unit (team, family, class, audience, committee, flock)
- **Indefinite pronoun antecedent:** When an indefinite pronoun (everyone, each, few) serves as the antecedent that a later pronoun refers to
- **Compound antecedent:** Two or more nouns joined by a conjunction (and, or, nor) that a pronoun refers to

### Instruction Notes

**Special Case 1: Collective Nouns**

Say: "Collective nouns name groups — team, family, class, jury, audience. The question is: does the group act as one unit, or do the members act as individuals?"

Write on the board:
- **Acting as one unit (singular):** The **team** celebrated **its** victory with a trophy ceremony. (The team acts together → "its")
- **Acting as individuals (plural):** The **team** put on **their** individual jerseys before the game. (Each member does something separately → "their")

Say: "If you can substitute 'the members of the team' and the sentence still makes sense, use a plural pronoun. If the group is doing one thing together, use a singular pronoun."

**Special Case 2: Indefinite Pronoun Antecedents**

Say: "In Lesson 2, we learned that indefinite pronouns can be singular, plural, or either. The same rules apply when an indefinite pronoun is the antecedent for another pronoun."

Write on the board:
- **Always singular → singular pronoun:** **Everyone** should bring **his or her** lunch tomorrow. (Also accept: **Everyone** should bring **their** lunch.)
- **Always plural → plural pronoun:** **Several** of the students forgot **their** pencils.
- **Either → depends on context:** **All** of the cake had lost **its** frosting. / **All** of the runners had finished **their** warm-ups.

Say: "Remember — for the 'always singular' indefinite pronouns, both 'his or her' and singular 'they/their' are accepted in this class."

**Special Case 3: Compound Antecedents**

Say: "This works just like compound subjects in Lesson 2. Two antecedents joined by 'and' are plural. Two antecedents joined by 'or' or 'nor' — the pronoun agrees with the nearer antecedent."

Write on the board:
- **Joined by "and" (plural):** **Marcus and Elena** submitted **their** projects early.
- **Joined by "or" (nearest):** Either **Marcus** or **his brothers** will bring **their** van. (nearest = brothers, plural → their)
- **Joined by "nor" (nearest):** Neither **the brothers** nor **Marcus** has finished **his** project. (nearest = Marcus, singular → his)

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (collective noun):**
Write: "The audience clapped their hands in unison."

Say: "Is the audience acting as one unit or as individuals? They are clapping together, in unison — one unit. So I should use 'its.' But wait — 'the audience clapped its hands' sounds awkward because audiences are made up of people. In cases like this, it is often better to rewrite: 'The audience members clapped their hands in unison.' When a collective noun sounds awkward with either pronoun, rewriting is the best strategy."

**Example 2 (indefinite pronoun):**
Write: "Each of the girls brought their own sleeping bag to the campout."

Say: "'Each' is always singular. Strictly speaking, the pronoun should be 'her' since we know the antecedent refers to girls: 'Each of the girls brought her own sleeping bag.' However, 'their' is also widely accepted here."

**Example 3 (compound antecedent):**
Write: "Neither the cat nor the kittens have eaten its dinner."

Say: "The compound antecedent is joined by 'nor,' so I check the nearest noun — 'kittens,' which is plural. The pronoun should be 'their': 'Neither the cat nor the kittens have eaten their dinner.'"

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students practice with all three special cases, receiving immediate feedback.

Say: "For each sentence, identify the special case (collective noun, indefinite pronoun, or compound antecedent), then choose the correct pronoun or fix the error."

1. The committee announced (its / their) decision after a long debate. [collective — acting as one unit]
2. Each of the boys prepared (his / their) presentation for Friday. [indefinite — "each" is singular]
3. Sam and his cousins rode (his / their) bikes to the park. [compound — "and" → plural]
4. Nobody remembered to charge (his or her / your) tablet before the field trip. [indefinite — "nobody" is singular]
5. The family gathered at (its / their) favorite restaurant for Grandma's birthday. [collective — acting as one unit]
6. Either the teacher or the students will share (her / their) ideas during the discussion. [compound — "or," nearest = students, plural]
7. Many of the spectators left (his / their) seats during halftime. [indefinite — "many" is always plural]
8. Neither the twins nor Kayla has turned in (her / their) permission slip. [compound — "nor," nearest = Kayla, singular]

Work through items 1-3 together. For items 4-8, have students work independently, then discuss with a partner.

---

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students demonstrate independent mastery of special-case pronoun-antecedent agreement.

**Directions:** For items 1-6, find and correct the pronoun-antecedent error. For items 7-8, choose the correct pronoun. For items 9-10, write original sentences.

1. The jury announced their verdict after deliberating for two days. (Hint: Is the jury acting as one unit here?)
2. Somebody left your backpack in the hallway near the gym.
3. Both the principal and the vice principal shared her opening remarks at the assembly.
4. Few of the trees in the park had lost their leaves by early October.
5. The class worked on their group projects quietly at separate tables. (Hint: Are class members acting individually?)
6. Neither Aisha nor her brothers brought their lunches — only Aisha forgot hers.
7. Everyone on the swim team dried off and wrapped (himself or herself / themselves) in a towel.
8. The orchestra finished (its / their) final rehearsal before the spring concert.
9. Write a sentence using a collective noun (such as "the band" or "the family") as the antecedent. Use the correct pronoun.
10. Write a sentence with a compound antecedent joined by "and." Use the correct pronoun.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-8.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-10.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-10, plus edit the following passage by correcting all pronoun-antecedent errors (there are four): "The science club met after school to plan their annual fair. Everyone brought their favorite experiment idea. Neither the president nor the treasurer remembered to bring their notes. Each member voted for the project they liked best, and the group announced their final choice."

### Interleaved Review
11. (Grade 5 review — L.5.1d) Find and correct the verb tense error: "The students are studying for the test right now, and yesterday they also study for two hours."
12. (Grade 5 review — L.5.2a) Add correct punctuation: "My favorite pizza toppings are pepperoni mushrooms green peppers and extra cheese."

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Choose the correct pronoun: "Each of the players on the volleyball team grabbed (her / their) water bottle during the timeout."
2. Correct the error: "The flock of geese changed their direction and flew south."
3. Choose the correct pronoun: "Either the coach or the athletes will present (his / their) goals for the season."

**Expected Answers:**
1. **her** (Also accept **their.** "Each" is singular; since the players are on a volleyball team and no gender is specified, both are acceptable.)
2. "The flock of geese changed **its** direction and flew south." (The flock acts as one unit when changing direction together → singular pronoun "its.")
3. **their** (Nearest antecedent is "athletes" — plural → "their.")

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 items correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review one special case at a time before combining
- Provide a decision flowchart: Is the antecedent a collective noun, indefinite pronoun, or compound? → Follow the matching rule
- For collective nouns, practice the substitution test: "Can I replace this with 'the members of'?"
- Use physical index cards with pronouns and antecedents for matching practice
- Pair the student with an on-level peer for additional guided practice

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready to proceed to Lesson 5 on recognizing variations from standard English
- Encourage the student to apply pronoun-antecedent agreement when editing any writing assignment

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Collective Noun Collection:** List ten collective nouns (jury, herd, flock, etc.). For each, write two sentences: one where the group acts as a unit (singular pronoun) and one where members act individually (plural pronoun).
2. **Agreement Editor:** Take a paragraph from your own recent writing and highlight every pronoun. Trace each one to its antecedent. Check agreement and fix any errors.
3. **Teaching Poster:** Create a poster that explains the three special cases of pronoun-antecedent agreement with examples. Use color coding for pronouns and antecedents.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- Collective noun agreement is genuinely difficult and even adult writers disagree on it. When in doubt, encourage students to rewrite for clarity rather than agonizing over the "correct" pronoun. "The team members packed their bags" is clearer than debating whether "the team packed its/their bags."
- The challenge passage in the Challenge tier has some items that are debatable (e.g., "everyone brought their"). Use this as a discussion opportunity: when is singular "they" appropriate, and when might a stricter interpretation be expected?
- Students who speak languages with grammatical gender (Spanish, French, Arabic) may have additional questions about how English handles gender in pronouns. Acknowledge that English is simpler in some ways (no gendered articles) but more complex in others (the evolving use of singular "they").
- Make sure students see the connection between Lessons 1-2 (subject-verb agreement) and Lessons 3-4 (pronoun-antecedent agreement). The same tricky categories — collective nouns, indefinite pronouns, compound constructions — cause problems in both areas.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. Corrected: "The jury announced **its** verdict after deliberating for two days." (The jury acts as one unit when announcing a single verdict → singular.)
2. Corrected: "Somebody left **his or her** backpack in the hallway near the gym." (Also accept "their." "Somebody" is singular and third person — "your" is second person.)
3. Corrected: "Both the principal and the vice principal shared **their** opening remarks at the assembly." (Compound antecedent with "and" → plural → "their.")
4. **Correct as written.** ("Few" is always plural → "their" is correct.)
5. **Correct as written.** (The class members are working at separate tables — acting individually → plural pronoun "their" is appropriate.)
6. Corrected: "Neither Aisha nor her brothers brought **their** lunches — only Aisha forgot **hers**." (Nearest antecedent to first pronoun is "brothers" — plural → "their." The second clause refers specifically to Aisha → "hers." Note: the original sentence has a logic problem: if neither brought lunches, then the brothers also forgot, not only Aisha. Accept reasonable corrections.)
7. **themselves** (Also accept "himself or herself." "Everyone" is grammatically singular, but "themselves" is widely accepted with "everyone" in modern usage.)
8. **its** (The orchestra finishes one rehearsal together — acting as a unit → singular.)
9. Answers vary. Example: "The band played **its** final song of the evening."
10. Answers vary. Example: "My brother and my cousin packed **their** fishing gear the night before the trip."

**Challenge passage corrected:** "The science club met after school to plan **its** annual fair. Everyone brought **his or her** favorite experiment idea. (Accept "their" as well.) Neither the president nor the treasurer remembered to bring **his or her** notes. (Accept "their" — nearest antecedent "treasurer" is singular, but both members are mentioned.) Each member voted for the project **he or she** liked best (accept "they"), and the group announced **its** final choice."

### Interleaved Review
11. Corrected: "The students are studying for the test right now, and yesterday they also **studied** for two hours." ("Study" should be "studied" to match the past-time signal "yesterday.")
12. Corrected: "My favorite pizza toppings are pepperoni, mushrooms, green peppers, and extra cheese." (Commas separate items in a series, with Oxford comma before "and.")
