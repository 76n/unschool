# English Language Arts — Grade 6 — Unit 4, Lesson 3

## Topic
Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Define "antecedent" and explain how pronouns must agree with their antecedents in number and person
- Identify the antecedent for a given pronoun in a sentence or passage
- Correct sentences where the pronoun does not agree with its antecedent
- Write original sentences demonstrating correct pronoun-antecedent agreement

## Prerequisites
- Understanding of subject-verb agreement from Lessons 1-2
- Ability to identify pronouns (he, she, it, they, them, his, her, its, their) from Grade 5
- Knowledge of singular and plural nouns (Grade 4-5)
- Familiarity with sentence structure and identifying subjects

## Materials Needed
- Whiteboard and markers (or projector)
- Printed or projected example sentences
- Student notebooks or lined paper
- Colored pencils (green for pronouns, orange for antecedents)
- Pronoun reference chart (subject, object, possessive forms)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge of pronouns and connect to the concept of agreement.

**Activity: Pronoun Swap**

Say: "I am going to read three sentences aloud. In each one, I have replaced a noun with a pronoun. Tell me which noun the pronoun replaces."

Write on the board:
1. "Maria finished **her** science project early." (her → Maria)
2. "The wolves howled, and **they** woke up the entire campground." (they → wolves)
3. "The old bicycle had a flat tire, so Dad fixed **it** last weekend." (it → bicycle)

Call on students for each answer. Then say: "The noun that a pronoun replaces is called the antecedent. That word comes from Latin and means 'the thing that comes before.' Today we are going to learn how pronouns and antecedents must agree — just like subjects and verbs have to agree."

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the rules of pronoun-antecedent agreement with clear examples and a checking strategy.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Antecedent:** The noun or noun phrase that a pronoun refers to or replaces
- **Pronoun-antecedent agreement:** The rule that a pronoun must match its antecedent in number (singular/plural) and person (first/second/third)
- **Number agreement:** Singular antecedents take singular pronouns; plural antecedents take plural pronouns
- **Person agreement:** First person (I, we), second person (you), and third person (he, she, it, they) must remain consistent

### Instruction Notes

Say: "The rule is straightforward: if the antecedent is singular, the pronoun must be singular. If the antecedent is plural, the pronoun must be plural. Let me show you."

Write on the board:
- **Singular:** The **girl** picked up **her** backpack. (girl = singular → her = singular)
- **Plural:** The **girls** picked up **their** backpacks. (girls = plural → their = plural)

Say: "This seems easy, right? But errors happen when the antecedent and pronoun are far apart in the sentence, or when the antecedent is tricky — like a collective noun or an indefinite pronoun. We will tackle those in the next lesson. Today, let us get the basics solid."

**Three-Step Checking Strategy:**

Write on the board:
1. **Find the pronoun.** Circle it.
2. **Find the antecedent.** Draw an arrow from the pronoun back to the noun it replaces.
3. **Check agreement.** Do they match in number? If not, fix it.

Say: "Let me also address something important. In everyday English, many people use 'they' to refer to a single person when we do not know the person's gender, or when the person uses 'they/them' pronouns. This is increasingly accepted in standard English. For example: 'A student should bring their notebook to class.' Historically, grammar rules required 'his or her,' but singular 'they' has a long history and is widely used today. In this class, we will accept both forms."

Write on the board:
- **Traditional:** A student should bring **his or her** notebook to class.
- **Singular they:** A student should bring **their** notebook to class.
- **Both are acceptable in this class.**

### Worked Examples

**Example 1 (singular antecedent, clear match):**
Write: "The dog wagged their tail when the owner came home."

Say: "Step 1 — the pronoun is 'their.' Step 2 — the antecedent is 'dog' — one dog, singular. Step 3 — 'their' is typically plural. Since 'dog' is singular, I should use 'its.'"

Correct: "The dog wagged **its** tail when the owner came home."

**Example 2 (plural antecedent, clear match):**
Write: "The hikers packed his supplies the night before the trip."

Say: "Step 1 — pronoun is 'his.' Step 2 — antecedent is 'hikers' — plural. Step 3 — 'his' is singular. They do not match. Since 'hikers' is plural, I need 'their.'"

Correct: "The hikers packed **their** supplies the night before the trip."

**Example 3 (correct sentence):**
Write: "The orchestra members tuned their instruments before the concert."

Say: "Step 1 — pronoun: 'their.' Step 2 — antecedent: 'members' — plural. Step 3 — 'their' is plural. They match. This sentence is correct."

**Example 4 (person agreement):**
Write: "When a person exercises regularly, you feel more energetic."

Say: "Here the antecedent is 'a person' — that is third person. But the pronoun is 'you' — second person. They do not match in person. I need to stay in third person."

Correct: "When a person exercises regularly, **he or she feels** more energetic." (Or: "When a person exercises regularly, **they feel** more energetic.")

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students apply the three-step strategy to identify and correct pronoun-antecedent agreement errors.

Say: "For each sentence, find the pronoun, find the antecedent, and check agreement. If the sentence has an error, fix it. If it is correct, write 'correct.'"

1. The cat stretched and cleaned (its / their) paws in the sunlight.
2. Each runner laced (his or her / their) shoes before the race.
3. The musicians carried (its / their) instruments onto the stage.
4. A good friend always keeps (your / his or her) promises.
5. The planets in our solar system follow (its / their) own orbits around the sun.
6. The old oak tree dropped (its / their) leaves early this autumn.
7. When students finish the test, (you / they) should turn it in at the front desk.
8. The team captain encouraged (her / their) teammates before the championship game.

Work through items 1-3 together on the board, drawing arrows from each pronoun to its antecedent. For items 4-8, have students work independently, then compare with a partner.

Say: "Notice item 2 — 'each runner' is singular, so both 'his or her' and 'their' are acceptable here. And for item 8 — the answer depends on the captain's gender. If we know the captain is female, 'her' is correct. If the gender is unknown, 'their' works too."

---

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Students demonstrate independent ability to identify and correct pronoun-antecedent agreement errors.

**Directions:** For items 1-6, find and correct the pronoun-antecedent error. For items 7-8, choose the correct pronoun. For items 9-10, write original sentences.

1. The birds in the nest chirped loudly, waiting for its mother to return with food.
2. Every student must bring your permission slip to the office before Friday.
3. The firefighters rushed to the scene and put on his protective gear.
4. A responsible pet owner takes their animal to the veterinarian for regular checkups.
5. The tall sunflowers swayed in the breeze, bending its heavy heads toward the ground.
6. My sister loves reading, and she checks out books from the library every week so you always have something new.
7. When the soccer team won the tournament, (it / they) celebrated with a pizza party.
8. Neither the coach nor the players remembered to bring (his / their) water bottles.
9. Write a sentence about a plural subject (such as "the neighbors" or "the scientists") and use the correct pronoun to refer back to it.
10. Write a sentence about a singular subject and use the correct pronoun to refer back to it.

### Difficulty Tiers
**On-level:** Complete items 1-8.
**Stretch:** Complete items 1-10.
**Challenge:** Complete items 1-10, plus write a short paragraph (4-5 sentences) about a school activity. Use at least four different pronouns, each with a clear antecedent. Underline each pronoun and draw an arrow to its antecedent.

### Interleaved Review
11. (Grade 5 review — L.5.1e) Fill in the blanks with the correct correlative conjunction pair: "_______ the art class _______ the music class is offered on Wednesdays."
12. (Grade 5 review — L.5.3a) Reduce this wordy sentence: "Due to the fact that the weather was bad, the game was canceled and they did not play it."

---

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Find and correct the pronoun-antecedent error: "The volunteers organized the food drive, and he collected over two hundred cans."
2. Choose the correct pronoun: "Every camper must keep (his or her / your) flashlight within reach at night."

**Expected Answers:**
1. "The volunteers organized the food drive, and **they** collected over two hundred cans." (Antecedent "volunteers" is plural → pronoun must be "they," not "he.")
2. **his or her** (Also accept **their.** Antecedent "every camper" is third person — "your" is second person and does not agree in person.)

**Mastery = 2 out of 2 items correct with correct identification of the agreement error.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the three-step strategy using simple sentences with close-together pronouns and antecedents
- Create a pronoun chart on an index card: singular pronouns (he, she, it, his, her, its) and plural pronouns (they, them, their)
- Have the student circle every pronoun in a short passage and draw an arrow to each antecedent before checking agreement
- Practice with oral exercises before returning to written correction

### If Mastery Is Met
- Student is ready to proceed to Lesson 4, which covers special cases of pronoun-antecedent agreement (collective nouns, indefinite pronouns, compound antecedents)
- Encourage the student to check pronoun-antecedent agreement in their own writing drafts

---

## Extension (Optional)
1. **Pronoun Agreement Editor:** Take a piece of your own writing from another class and circle every pronoun. Draw an arrow to each antecedent. Check that every pair agrees. Fix any errors you find.
2. **Partner Correction Game:** Write five sentences with deliberate pronoun-antecedent errors. Exchange with a partner. Race to find and fix all five.
3. **Antecedent Detective:** Read a paragraph from a novel or article. List every pronoun you find and identify its antecedent. Note whether each pair agrees correctly.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- The most common error students make is using "their" to refer to a singular antecedent (e.g., "the dog wagged their tail"). While singular "they" is accepted for people when gender is unspecified, it does not apply to animals or objects with obvious singular reference. Help students distinguish these cases.
- The shift from third person to second person ("a student should bring your notebook") is extremely common in student writing. Emphasize that the person of the pronoun must stay consistent with the antecedent throughout the sentence.
- Some students may struggle to find antecedents that appear in a previous sentence. Use examples where the antecedent is in one sentence and the pronoun in the next: "The park rangers posted warning signs. They wanted hikers to stay on the marked trails."
- Be sensitive to students whose home language patterns may differ from standard English pronoun-antecedent agreement. Frame this as expanding their language toolkit rather than correcting their speech.

## Answer Key

### Independent Practice
1. Corrected: "The birds in the nest chirped loudly, waiting for **their** mother to return with food." (Antecedent "birds" is plural → "their")
2. Corrected: "Every student must bring **his or her** permission slip to the office before Friday." (Also accept "their." The error was "your" — second person does not match third-person antecedent.)
3. Corrected: "The firefighters rushed to the scene and put on **their** protective gear." (Antecedent "firefighters" is plural → "their")
4. **Correct as written.** ("A responsible pet owner" is singular, and singular "they/their" is accepted for a generic person whose gender is unspecified.)
5. Corrected: "The tall sunflowers swayed in the breeze, bending **their** heavy heads toward the ground." (Antecedent "sunflowers" is plural → "their")
6. Corrected: "My sister loves reading, and she checks out books from the library every week so **she** always has something new." (Antecedent is "sister" — third person, singular → "she," not "you")
7. **they** (The team celebrated — members acting individually → "they." Also accept "it" if the student views the team as a single unit.)
8. **their** (Compound antecedent with "nor" — nearest antecedent "players" is plural → "their")
9. Answers vary. Example: "The scientists published **their** research findings in a respected journal."
10. Answers vary. Example: "The principal announced **her** plans for a new after-school program."

### Interleaved Review
11. **Both** the art class **and** the music class is offered on Wednesdays. (Also accept: **Either** ... **or** if the student reasons that only one class can be on Wednesday.)
12. Reduced: "Because of bad weather, the game was canceled." (Or: "The game was canceled due to bad weather.")

See Expected Answers above.
