# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 2, Lesson 6

> **Methodology Reference:** [Teaching Methodology Framework](../../../methodology/teaching-methodology-framework.md)

## Topic
Relationships Between Events — Cause and Effect, Sequence

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Describe cause-and-effect relationships in informational text using the frame "_____ happened because _____"
- Identify the sequence of events or steps in an informational passage using signal words (first, then, next, finally)
- Recognize signal words that indicate cause-and-effect (because, so, as a result, therefore, since) and sequence (first, then, next, after, finally, before)

## Prerequisites
- Ability to determine main idea and identify supporting details (Lessons 3-5)
- Ability to navigate text features (Lesson 2)
- Understanding of informational text purpose (Lesson 1)

## Materials Needed
- Printed or displayed copy of the passage "Volcanoes: Fire from Below" (included below)
- Signal Words Anchor Chart (to build together — two columns: Cause-Effect words, Sequence words)
- Cause-and-Effect graphic organizer (two connected boxes: Cause --> Effect)
- Sequence chain graphic organizer (4-5 connected boxes in a row with arrows)
- Highlighters (2 colors — one for cause-effect signal words, one for sequence signal words)
- Paper and pencil

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about cause and effect from everyday life and review main idea skills.

**Activity: Everyday Causes and Effects**

Say to the student:

*"Before we read today, I want you to think about something that happens every day. I am going to describe something that happened, and you tell me WHY it happened — the cause."*

1. "The sidewalk is wet." Why? (It rained. / Someone sprayed a hose.)
2. "The flowers in the garden grew taller." Why? (They got water and sunlight.)
3. "The boy missed the school bus." Why? (He woke up late. / He was not ready on time.)

Say: *"You just identified CAUSES and EFFECTS. The effect is what happened. The cause is WHY it happened. This is something we do all the time in real life, and today you are going to learn how to find cause and effect in informational text."*

**Spaced Review — Main Idea (Lesson 3):**

*"Quick review: What is the difference between the topic and the main idea?"* (The topic is WHAT the text is about in a word or phrase; the main idea is WHAT THE AUTHOR SAYS about the topic in a sentence — the most important point.)

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach two types of relationships found in informational text — cause and effect, and sequence — with emphasis on identifying signal words.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Cause** — The reason something happens (WHY)
- **Effect** — What happens as a result (WHAT happened)
- **Sequence** — The order in which events or steps happen
- **Signal words** — Words that give clues about how ideas in a text are connected
- **Cause-effect signal words** — because, so, as a result, therefore, since, due to, this caused, for this reason
- **Sequence signal words** — first, second, then, next, after, before, finally, last, during, meanwhile

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Cause and Effect (4-5 minutes)**

Say: *"When you read informational text, the author often explains WHY things happen. When one event causes another event to happen, we call that a cause-and-effect relationship. The cause comes first — it is the reason. The effect comes second — it is what happens because of the cause."*

Write on the anchor chart:

**Cause and Effect**
Cause = WHY (the reason) --> Effect = WHAT HAPPENED (the result)

Say: *"Authors use special signal words to show cause and effect. When you see these words, it is a clue that the author is connecting a cause to an effect."*

Write the signal words: **because, so, as a result, therefore, since, due to, this caused, for this reason**

Model with an example:

> "Many plants in the desert have long roots **because** they need to reach water deep underground."

Say: *"The signal word is 'because.' What is the effect — what do the plants have? Long roots. What is the cause — why do they have long roots? They need to reach water deep underground."*

Write: Cause: Plants need to reach deep water --> Effect: They have long roots.

Give a second example:

> "The river flooded, **so** many animals had to move to higher ground."

Say: *"The signal word is 'so.' What is the cause? The river flooded. What is the effect? Animals had to move to higher ground."*

**Step 2: Sequence (4-5 minutes)**

Say: *"The second kind of relationship we will learn today is sequence — the ORDER in which things happen. When an author describes a process, a procedure, or a series of events, they use sequence signal words to show what comes first, second, third, and so on."*

Write on the anchor chart:

**Sequence**
The ORDER in which events or steps happen: First --> Then --> Next --> Finally

Write the signal words: **first, second, then, next, after, before, finally, last, during, meanwhile**

Model with an example:

> "**First**, the caterpillar hatches from an egg. **Then**, it eats leaves and grows bigger. **Next**, it forms a chrysalis around itself. **Finally**, it emerges as a butterfly."

Say: *"The signal words — first, then, next, finally — tell me the ORDER. This is a sequence. I can picture it like a chain of events, one leading to the next."*

**Step 3: Quick check (1-2 minutes)**

Read each sentence and ask the student: "Is this cause-effect or sequence?"

- "The ice cream melted because it was left in the sun." (Cause-effect — signal word: because)
- "First, wash your hands. Then, gather the ingredients." (Sequence — signal words: first, then)
- "The strong winds caused the tree to fall over." (Cause-effect — signal word: caused)

### Worked Examples

**Worked Example 1: Finding cause and effect in a paragraph**

Display this paragraph:

> Polar bears have white fur, which helps them blend in with the snow and ice. **Because** they are so well camouflaged, polar bears can sneak up on seals without being seen. Polar bears also have a thick layer of fat under their skin. **As a result**, they can stay warm even in temperatures that drop below negative 30 degrees.

Say: *"I see two cause-and-effect relationships here. Let me find them."*

*"Relationship 1: The signal word is 'because.' Cause: Polar bears are well camouflaged (white fur blends with snow). Effect: They can sneak up on seals without being seen."*

*"Relationship 2: The signal word is 'as a result.' Cause: Polar bears have thick fat under their skin. Effect: They can stay warm in extremely cold temperatures."*

Fill in two cause-effect organizers.

**Worked Example 2: Finding a sequence in a paragraph**

Display this paragraph:

> How do birds build a nest? **First**, the bird chooses a safe location, usually in a tree branch or a sheltered spot. **Next**, it gathers building materials like twigs, grass, leaves, and sometimes mud. **Then**, the bird weaves the materials together, shaping them into a cup or bowl. **Finally**, the bird lines the inside of the nest with soft materials like feathers or animal fur to keep the eggs warm.

Say: *"I see sequence signal words: first, next, then, finally. This is a step-by-step process."*

Fill in the sequence chain:
1. Choose a safe location
2. Gather materials (twigs, grass, leaves, mud)
3. Weave materials into a cup shape
4. Line the inside with soft materials

**Purpose:** The student identifies cause-and-effect and sequence relationships in a passage with facilitator support.

**Activity: Relationship Detective — "Volcanoes: Fire from Below"**

Provide the student with the following passage. Read it together, then analyze the relationships.

---

### Reading Passage: "Volcanoes: Fire from Below"

**What Is a Volcano?**

A **volcano** is an opening in the Earth's surface where hot, melted rock called **magma** pushes up from deep underground. The Earth's outer layer, called the **crust**, is made of huge pieces called **tectonic plates** that fit together like a puzzle. These plates are always moving very slowly. **Because** the plates move, they sometimes push against each other or pull apart. When this happens, cracks form in the crust, and magma from below can rise up through these cracks. When magma reaches the surface, it is called **lava**.

**How a Volcanic Eruption Happens**

A volcanic eruption follows a series of steps. **First**, pressure builds up deep underground as hot magma collects in a large pool called a **magma chamber**. **Then**, the pressure becomes so great that the magma begins to push upward through cracks and channels in the rock. **Next**, the magma forces its way to the surface. **As a result** of all this pressure, the volcano erupts — shooting lava, ash, and hot gases into the air. **Finally**, the lava flows down the sides of the volcano and begins to cool and harden into solid rock.

**Effects of Volcanic Eruptions**

Volcanic eruptions can be both destructive and helpful. Eruptions destroy forests, bury towns, and make the air dangerous to breathe **because** of the thick ash clouds they produce. **However**, volcanoes also help the Earth. **Since** volcanic ash is full of minerals, the soil near volcanoes is some of the richest and most fertile soil in the world. Over time, volcanic eruptions also create new land — the Hawaiian Islands were formed entirely by underwater volcanoes. **Therefore**, even though eruptions are dangerous, they play an important role in shaping the Earth.

---

**Facilitator Instructions:**

**Part 1: Signal Word Hunt**

Say: *"Let's start by finding the signal words. Use one color to highlight cause-and-effect signal words and another color for sequence signal words."*

Work together to find:
- Cause-effect: "Because" (Section 1), "As a result" (Section 2), "because" (Section 3), "Since" (Section 3), "Therefore" (Section 3)
- Sequence: "First," "Then," "Next," "Finally" (all in Section 2)

**Part 2: Cause-and-Effect Organizer**

Say: *"Section 1 describes a cause-and-effect relationship. What causes cracks to form in the Earth's crust?"*

Guide the student to fill in:
- Cause: Tectonic plates move and push against each other or pull apart.
- Effect: Cracks form in the crust, and magma rises through the cracks.

Say: *"Now find a cause-and-effect relationship in Section 3."*

Guide toward:
- Cause: Volcanic ash is full of minerals.
- Effect: Soil near volcanoes is very rich and fertile.

**Part 3: Sequence Chain**

Say: *"Section 2 describes a sequence — the steps of a volcanic eruption. Can you put the steps in order using the signal words as clues?"*

Guide the student to fill in the sequence chain:
1. (First) Pressure builds as magma collects in the magma chamber.
2. (Then) Pressure becomes so great that magma pushes upward.
3. (Next) Magma forces its way to the surface.
4. (As a result) Volcano erupts — lava, ash, and gases shoot out.
5. (Finally) Lava flows down the volcano and cools into solid rock.

**Part 4: Connection to Main Idea**

Say: *"One more question: What is the main idea of Section 3?"*

Guide: Volcanic eruptions can be both destructive and helpful.

Say: *"How do the cause-and-effect relationships in Section 3 support this main idea?"*

Guide: The cause-effect relationships show BOTH sides — the destruction (ash makes air dangerous) and the help (ash makes soil fertile, eruptions create new land). The relationships prove the main idea.

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** The student identifies cause-and-effect and sequence relationships independently.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:**

**Passage 1: Cause and Effect**

> Wildfires can spread very quickly in dry weather. **Because** there has been no rain for weeks, the trees and grass become extremely dry. A single spark from lightning or a campfire can start a blaze. The dry plants catch fire easily, and **since** there is no moisture to slow it down, the fire spreads rapidly. **As a result**, thousands of acres of forest can burn in just a few days.

1. Find two cause-and-effect relationships. Write each one using the frame: "___ happened because ___." (2 points)
2. Circle or list the signal words that helped you find them. (1 point)

**Passage 2: Sequence**

> How does a tadpole become a frog? **First**, a frog lays eggs in water. **After** a few days, tiny tadpoles hatch from the eggs. Tadpoles breathe through gills and swim using a tail. **Then**, over several weeks, the tadpole begins to change. It grows back legs, **then** front legs. **Next**, its tail starts to shrink. **Finally**, the tadpole's gills are replaced by lungs, and it can breathe air. It is now a frog.

3. List the steps of this sequence in order (at least 5 steps). (2 points)
4. What signal words did the author use to show the order? List at least four. (1 point)

**Stretch:**

Complete the On-level tasks, plus:

5. Look at the volcano passage, Section 3. The author says volcanoes are both "destructive and helpful." Write one cause-and-effect pair showing destruction and one showing how volcanoes help. (2 points)

6. Why do you think authors use signal words? How do signal words help the reader? Write 2-3 sentences. (1 point)

**Challenge:**

Complete the On-level and Stretch tasks, plus:

7. Write your own short paragraph (3-4 sentences) that describes a cause-and-effect relationship. It must be about a real topic (not fiction) and include at least two signal words. Underline the signal words. (2 points)

### Interleaved Review

**Review from Lesson 4 — Supporting Details:**

8. Look at Passage 1 about wildfires. The main idea is: "Wildfires can spread very quickly in dry weather." Which details SUPPORT this main idea? List two. (1 point)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

1. Read this sentence: "The town flooded because heavy rains lasted for three days." What is the cause? What is the effect? (1 point)
2. Read these sentences: "First, the farmer plants seeds in the soil. Then, she waters them every day. Finally, the plants begin to sprout." Is this an example of cause-and-effect or sequence? How do you know? (1 point)
3. Name two signal words that tell you something is a cause-and-effect relationship. (1 point)
4. Name two signal words that tell you something is a sequence. (1 point)

**Mastery = 3 out of 4 correct**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice with everyday examples before returning to text: "You fell down because the floor was wet." (Cause: wet floor. Effect: fell down.) "First I wake up. Then I brush my teeth. Next I eat breakfast."
- Use physical cause-effect cards: write causes on one set and effects on another, then have the student match them
- Have the student highlight ONLY signal words in a passage before trying to identify the relationships
- Re-assess before moving to Lesson 7

### If Mastery Is Met
- The student is ready for Lesson 7: Relationships Between Concepts
- Encourage the student to notice cause-and-effect and sequence in everyday reading (news articles, science texts, even recipes)

## Extension (Optional)
- **Signal Word Collector:** The student starts a running list of signal words they find in any text they read. They categorize each word as cause-effect or sequence. See how many they can collect by the end of the unit.
- **Cause-Effect Chain:** Some events have chain reactions — one effect becomes the cause of the next event. Have the student create a chain: "The ice melted (effect of heat) --> Water flowed into the river (effect of melting) --> The river flooded (effect of extra water) --> Animals moved to higher ground (effect of flooding)."
- **Sequence Challenge:** The student writes the steps for a familiar process (making a sandwich, getting ready for school, planting a seed) using at least four sequence signal words.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
- **Cause and effect can be tricky at this age:** Third graders sometimes confuse the cause with the effect. Use the "Why?" test: "Why did _____ happen?" The answer to "why" is the cause. The thing that happened is the effect.
- **Some sentences contain BOTH cause-effect and sequence:** The volcano passage's second section uses sequence signal words (first, then, next, finally) AND a cause-effect signal word (as a result). Help the student see that these can coexist — the eruption follows a sequence, but the eruption itself is the EFFECT of the pressure building up.
- **Common misconception — everything in order is "sequence":** Students may think any paragraph where sentences come one after another is a sequence. Clarify that sequence specifically means the ORDER MATTERS — the events are described in the order they happen, and changing the order would change the meaning. Signal words are the clearest indicator.
- **Common misconception — "because" always shows cause-effect:** While "because" is a strong signal word, students should learn to think about whether the relationship truly shows one thing causing another, not just one thing happening alongside another.
- **Connection to standards:** This lesson addresses RI.3.3 (describe the relationship between a series of historical events, scientific ideas or concepts, or steps in technical procedures in a text, using language that pertains to time, sequence, and cause/effect). Lesson 7 will expand to additional text structures.
- **All passages are original:** Written specifically for this curriculum.

**On-level:**
1. Cause-effect relationship 1: "The trees and grass became extremely dry because there was no rain for weeks." (Or: "The fire spread rapidly because there was no moisture to slow it down.") Cause-effect relationship 2: "Thousands of acres burned because dry plants catch fire easily and the fire spreads without moisture." (Accept any two clearly stated cause-effect pairs.) (2 points)
2. Signal words: because, since, as a result. (1 point)
3. Sequence: (1) Frog lays eggs in water. (2) Tiny tadpoles hatch. (3) Tadpole grows back legs, then front legs. (4) Tail starts to shrink. (5) Gills replaced by lungs — now a frog. (2 points — accept any correct 5-step list)

**Stretch:**
5. Destruction: Eruptions destroy forests and bury towns because of thick ash clouds (cause: ash; effect: destruction/dangerous air). Help: Since volcanic ash is full of minerals, soil near volcanoes is rich and fertile (cause: mineral-rich ash; effect: fertile soil). (2 points)
6. Accept answers explaining that signal words help the reader understand HOW ideas are connected — they show whether things are in order (sequence) or whether one thing caused another (cause-effect). Without signal words, the reader would have to guess the relationship. (1 point)

**Challenge:**
7. Accept any factual paragraph with a clear cause-and-effect relationship and at least two underlined signal words. Example: "Pandas are endangered because their bamboo forest habitats are being cut down. Since bamboo is the main food that pandas eat, losing bamboo forests means pandas cannot find enough to eat. As a result, the panda population has gotten much smaller over the years." (2 points)
