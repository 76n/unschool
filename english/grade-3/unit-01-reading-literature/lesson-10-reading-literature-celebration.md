# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 1, Lesson 10

## Topic
Reading Literature Celebration

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Demonstrate comprehension of a new passage using all skills learned in this unit (text evidence, character analysis, vocabulary in context)
- Present a character analysis (book talk or character presentation) using text evidence
- Reflect on their growth as a reader throughout this unit

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-9 (all unit skills)
- Character trait word bank and feelings word list from earlier lessons

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Reading passage provided in this lesson (print or display)
- Character trait word bank from Lesson 3
- Crayons, colored pencils, or markers (for character poster or presentation visual)
- All completed work from the unit (in the folder or binder)
- Optional: a favorite book the student has been reading on their own

**Purpose:** Celebrate the skills learned in this unit and build excitement for the culminating activities.

**Activity: Unit Skills Parade**

Say: "Today is our Reading Literature Celebration! You have worked hard in this unit and learned some powerful reading skills. Before we celebrate, let's review everything you have learned. For each skill, give me a thumbs up if you feel confident, a sideways thumb if you feel okay, or a thumbs down if you want more practice."

Go through each skill:

1. "I can ask questions about a story and answer them using TEXT EVIDENCE." (Lessons 1-2)
2. "I can describe a character using TRAITS and back it up with evidence from what they say, do, think, and feel." (Lesson 3)
3. "I can explain how a character's ACTIONS cause events in a story." (Lesson 4)
4. "I can describe how a character's FEELINGS change and explain what MOTIVATES them." (Lesson 5)
5. "I can use CONTEXT CLUES to figure out the meaning of unfamiliar words." (Lesson 6)
6. "I can recognize SIMILES and IDIOMS and explain what they really mean." (Lesson 7)
7. "I can identify the four STORY ELEMENTS: character, setting, problem, solution." (Lesson 8)

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Read a new passage together and model how all unit skills work together during reading.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Book talk:** A short presentation where you tell others about a character or book and why it is interesting. You use text evidence to support your points.
- **Character presentation:** A focused presentation about one character — who they are, what they do, how they feel, and how they change.
- **Reflection:** Looking back at your own learning and thinking about how you have grown.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Read the Culminating Passage (7 minutes)**

Say: "I am going to introduce you to one more story. This time, as we read, I want you to use ALL of your skills — notice characters, track feelings, look for text evidence, figure out tricky words, and identify story elements. This is your chance to show everything you have learned."

Read this passage together (student reads aloud with facilitator support as needed):

> **The Map That Changed Everything**
>
> For as long as she could remember, Nia had been afraid of getting lost. She always stayed close to her parents, never wandered off the path, and refused to go anywhere new without someone holding her hand. Her older brother, Tyler, was the opposite — he was fearless. He rode his bike to the end of every street, explored every trail, and once spent a whole Saturday mapping the woods behind their neighborhood.
>
> One Saturday, Tyler was supposed to take Nia to the park three blocks away. But when they reached the first corner, Tyler stopped.
>
> "I have an idea," he said. "I made a map." He pulled a folded piece of paper from his pocket and handed it to Nia. "You lead."
>
> Nia unfolded the map. Tyler had drawn every street, every house, and every tree between their home and the park. He had marked the route with a thick red line and added notes: "Turn left at the house with the blue mailbox," and "Cross the street at the stop sign — look both ways," and "The park is on the right, past the big oak tree."
>
> "I do not need this," Nia said, trying to hand it back. "You know the way."
>
> "But you do not," Tyler said gently. "And someday, you might need to get there without me. The map will help."
>
> Nia looked at the map, then at the street ahead. Her heart was beating fast. But the map was so detailed, so clear, that the route did not look scary anymore. It looked like a path she could actually follow.
>
> She took a deep breath and started walking. At the house with the blue mailbox, she turned left. At the stop sign, she looked both ways and crossed the street. Past the big oak tree, she could see the park.
>
> When they reached the playground, Nia felt a glow of pride spreading through her chest like warm sunshine. She had led the way. She had followed the map. She had done it herself.
>
> "How do you feel?" Tyler asked.
>
> Nia folded the map carefully and put it in her pocket. "Like I could go anywhere," she said.

**Step 2: Quick Discussion — Pulling It All Together (5 minutes)**

After reading, have a brief discussion hitting the major unit skills:

- **Text evidence:** "How do we know Nia was afraid of getting lost? Show me the evidence." (Paragraph 1: "always stayed close to her parents, never wandered off the path, and refused to go anywhere new without someone holding her hand")
- **Character traits:** "What traits describe Nia? What traits describe Tyler?" (Nia: cautious, timid at first, then brave, determined. Tyler: fearless, thoughtful, kind, resourceful.)
- **Character actions and events:** "What did Tyler DO that caused Nia to try?" (He made a detailed map and asked her to lead. Because Tyler gave Nia the map, she felt confident enough to try.)
- **Feelings and motivations:** "How did Nia's feelings change?" (Scared at the beginning, heart beating fast in the middle, pride like warm sunshine at the end.)
- **Vocabulary in context:** "What does 'a glow of pride spreading through her chest like warm sunshine' mean?" (It is a simile — the pride felt warm and expanding, like sunshine. A figurative way to describe a strong happy feeling.)
- **Story elements:** "What are the character, setting, problem, and solution?" (Characters: Nia and Tyler. Setting: their neighborhood, Saturday. Problem: Nia is afraid to go anywhere without someone leading her. Solution: Tyler's map helps her lead the way herself.)

### Worked Examples

Say: "Now let me show you what a character presentation sounds like. I am going to present Tyler."

Model a brief character presentation:

"Tyler is Nia's older brother. He is fearless, thoughtful, and kind. I know he is fearless because the text says he 'rode his bike to the end of every street and explored every trail.' I know he is thoughtful because instead of just leading Nia to the park, he made a detailed map so she could learn to get there on her own. He included notes like 'Turn left at the house with the blue mailbox' to make it as clear as possible. Tyler did not just want to help Nia today — he wanted to help her become independent. His kindness and thoughtfulness are what made it possible for Nia to feel proud at the end of the story."

Say: "See how I used character traits, text evidence, and information about Tyler's motivations? That is what a character presentation includes. Now it is your turn."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student prepares a character presentation with facilitator support.

**Activity: Prepare Your Character Presentation (12 minutes)**

Say: "Now you are going to prepare a presentation about NIA. You can present it as a book talk (telling me about her) or a character presentation (analyzing who she is). Here is what to include:"

Write this checklist on paper:

**Character Presentation Checklist:**
- [ ] Character's name
- [ ] At least 2 character traits with text evidence
- [ ] How the character's feelings changed during the story
- [ ] One cause-and-effect connection (character action --> event)
- [ ] One example of figurative language or vocabulary from the passage
- [ ] Why this character is interesting or what we can learn from them

Give the student 8-10 minutes to prepare. They can:
- Write notes or an outline on paper
- Create a simple poster with the character's name, traits, and key quotes
- Draw a picture of the character with annotations

While the student prepares, be available for questions but let them work independently as much as possible.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student is stuck, guide them with questions: "What traits describe Nia?" "How did she feel at the beginning vs. the end?" "What caused her to change?" Do not write the presentation for them — let them own it.*

After the student presents (2-3 minutes), give specific, evidence-based praise:

- "I liked how you used the exact words from the text when you said ___."
- "You did a great job explaining how Nia's feelings changed — that shows you understand character motivation."
- "You identified a simile AND explained what it meant — that is strong vocabulary work."

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student demonstrates all unit skills independently with a new passage.

Say: "For your final independent practice, you are going to read a brand-new passage and answer questions using ALL the skills from this unit. This is your chance to show everything you have learned."

Have the student read independently:

> **The Longest Recess**
>
> It was the last day before winter break, and the fourth-grade teachers decided to give the students the longest recess anyone could remember — a full forty-five minutes.
>
> Most kids sprinted to the playground. Eliana headed straight for the swings. Marco organized a massive game of capture the flag. But Jonah sat down on a bench by the basketball court and opened his sketchbook.
>
> Jonah was not antisocial — he actually liked his classmates a lot. But he found his peace in drawing. While the others screamed and chased each other, Jonah sketched the scene: kids mid-leap, jackets flying, mouths open in laughter. His pencil moved across the paper as swift as a hummingbird's wings.
>
> Halfway through recess, Eliana wandered over. "What are you drawing?" she asked, peeking over his shoulder.
>
> Jonah tilted the sketchbook so she could see. Eliana's eyes went wide.
>
> "That is AMAZING," she said. "That is Marco! And that is me on the swings! You got my jacket exactly right!"
>
> Soon other kids gathered around, each one excited to find themselves in Jonah's drawing. Marco pointed at his own figure and laughed. "I look like a superhero!"
>
> "You kind of do when you run," Jonah said with a shy smile.
>
> When the bell rang, Jonah carefully tore the page from his sketchbook and handed it to Ms. Torres, their teacher. "For the classroom wall," he said.
>
> Ms. Torres studied the drawing. It was bursting with life — every detail captured, every face recognizable. "Jonah," she said quietly, "this is a gift. You have turned an ordinary recess into something people will remember."
>
> Jonah shrugged, but his cheeks turned pink with pride. As he walked back to class, he thought about what Ms. Torres had said. Maybe drawing was not about sitting on the sidelines. Maybe it was about seeing things that other people missed.

**Questions — Demonstrate Your Skills:**

1. **Text Evidence (Lessons 1-2):** How do you know Jonah is not unfriendly, even though he sits alone? Find and copy the text evidence.

2. **Character Traits (Lesson 3):** Name two character traits that describe Jonah. For each trait, write the evidence from the text.

3. **Character Actions and Events (Lesson 4):** What happened BECAUSE Jonah decided to draw during recess instead of playing? Trace the cause-and-effect chain.

4. **Character Feelings (Lesson 5):** How did Jonah feel when Ms. Torres praised his drawing? What evidence shows this?

5. **Vocabulary/Figurative Language (Lessons 6-7):** Find the simile in the passage. What does it mean? Also, what does "bursting with life" mean?

6. **Story Elements (Lesson 8):** Complete a quick story map: Character, Setting, Problem, Solution.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Answer questions 1, 2, and 6. Answer at least one other question of choice.

**Stretch:** Answer all 6 questions in writing with text evidence.

**Challenge:** Answer all 6 questions with thorough text evidence. Then write a paragraph comparing Jonah to one other character from this unit (Reiko from Lesson 8, Kai from Lesson 9, or any other). Use a Venn diagram to organize your thinking first.

### Interleaved Review

7. **Context clues (Lesson 6):** What does "antisocial" mean? Use context clues from the sentence to figure it out. What clue type is it?
8. **Comparing characters (Lesson 9):** In one sentence, compare Jonah to Reiko (from Lesson 8). How are they alike?

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

This is the final exit ticket for the unit. The student answers one question per skill area:

1. **Text evidence:** What is the most important thing to do when answering a question about a story? (Go back to the text and find the evidence / show where it says that)
2. **Character analysis:** Name a character trait for Jonah and give ONE piece of evidence. (e.g., Talented — his drawing was so good that everyone recognized themselves in it)
3. **Vocabulary:** What does the simile "as swift as a hummingbird's wings" mean? (Very fast)

**Mastery = Student answers all 3 correctly. This indicates readiness for the unit assessment.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the specific skill area(s) that were missed
- Use the student's unit folder to revisit relevant lesson work
- Consider spending an additional session reviewing before the unit assessment
- Focus reteaching on the weakest area

### If Mastery Is Met
- Celebrate! The student is ready for the unit assessment.
- Schedule the assessment for the next session.
- Encourage continued reading over the break between lessons.

## Extension (Optional)

1. **Character Showcase:** The student creates a "gallery" of characters from the unit — one page per character with a drawing, traits, key evidence, and a favorite quote. Display these on a wall or bulletin board.

2. **Letter to a Character:** The student writes a letter to their favorite character from the unit. The letter should include: what they admire about the character, advice they would give, and a question they would ask. The student should use text evidence to show they really know the character.

3. **Reading Goal Setting:** The student sets 2-3 reading goals for the next unit: "I want to get better at ___," "I want to read more books about ___," "I want to practice ___ every day." This builds metacognitive awareness and ownership of learning.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson is about celebration and confidence:** The primary purpose of Lesson 10 is to give the student a positive, affirming experience as a reader. Yes, they are practicing skills, but the tone should be celebratory. They have completed nine lessons of hard work, and this is their moment to shine. Be generous with specific praise. Point out growth: "Remember in Lesson 1 when finding text evidence was new? Now you do it automatically. That is real growth."

**The character presentation is a formative assessment:** As the student presents their analysis of Nia (or any character), listen for evidence of each skill. Can they name traits? Do they cite evidence? Can they track feelings? Do they explain motivation? This gives you valuable information about their readiness for the unit assessment without the pressure of a formal test.

**Adjust the balance based on the student:** If your student is highly confident, spend more time on the independent practice (the new passage) and challenge tier. If your student needs encouragement, spend more time on the character presentation and celebration. The lesson is flexible — the goal is for the student to leave feeling capable and ready.

**Transition to the unit assessment:** After this lesson, the next session should be the unit assessment. Give the student a preview: "Next time, you will take an assessment that covers everything we have learned. It is not a test to trick you — it is a chance to show how much you know. You are going to do great." This reduces anxiety and frames the assessment positively.

## Answer Key

**Independent Practice (The Longest Recess):**

1. **Text evidence:** "Jonah was not antisocial — he actually liked his classmates a lot. But he found his peace in drawing." This directly tells us he likes people; he just prefers drawing.

2. **Character traits:**
   - Talented/Artistic — "His pencil moved across the paper... every detail captured, every face recognizable." Ms. Torres said "this is a gift."
   - Observant — "Maybe it was about seeing things that other people missed." He noticed details about each classmate (Eliana's jacket, Marco looking like a superhero).
   - Other acceptable traits: shy ("with a shy smile"), generous (he gave the drawing to Ms. Torres "for the classroom wall"), thoughtful, humble.

3. **Cause and effect:** Because Jonah drew during recess instead of playing, he created a drawing of all his classmates. Because the drawing was so detailed and lifelike, his classmates gathered around and were excited to find themselves in it. Because he gave the drawing to Ms. Torres, she praised him and it became something the class would remember.

4. **Feelings:** Jonah felt proud but modest — "his cheeks turned pink with pride" but he "shrugged." He also felt reflective: "he thought about what Ms. Torres had said."

5. **Figurative language:** The simile is "as swift as a hummingbird's wings" — it means his pencil moved very fast across the paper, like the quick movement of a hummingbird. "Bursting with life" means the drawing was so full of energy and detail that it looked alive and vibrant.

6. **Story map:**
   - Character: Jonah — artistic, observant, shy, talented
   - Setting: School playground, last day before winter break, 45-minute recess
   - Problem: Jonah sits alone drawing while everyone else plays; he seems separate from the group
   - Solution: His drawing captures the recess scene so perfectly that it brings everyone together, and Ms. Torres recognizes his unique gift

7. **Context clues:** "Antisocial" — the contrast clue is "he actually liked his classmates a lot." The word "not" before "antisocial" tells us it means unfriendly or avoiding people. Clue type: Contrast.

8. **Comparing to Reiko:** Both Jonah and Reiko are talented artists who worry that their drawing talent is not appreciated. Both end up being recognized and praised when they share their art with others.

**Exit Ticket:**
1. Go back to the text and find the evidence / show where it says that.
2. (Any appropriate trait with evidence — see #2 above.)
3. Very fast — his pencil moved very quickly across the paper.
