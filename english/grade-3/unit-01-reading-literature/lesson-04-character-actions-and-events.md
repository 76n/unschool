# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 1, Lesson 4

## Topic
Character Actions and Events

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Explain how a character's actions contribute to the sequence of events in a story
- Identify cause-and-effect relationships between what a character does and what happens next
- Use the sentence frame "Because [character] did ___, [event] happened" to describe cause and effect in stories

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-3 (text evidence, character traits, S.D.T.F. analysis)
- Grade 2 skill: describing how events in a story connect

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Reading passages provided in this lesson (print or display)
- Character trait word bank from Lesson 3
- Index cards or sticky notes (for cause-and-effect matching)
- Optional: colored pencils for drawing cause-and-effect chains

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review character traits from Lesson 3 and activate prior knowledge about cause and effect.

**Activity: Trait to Action**

Say: "In Lesson 3, we learned about character traits — words that describe what a character is like. Today we are going to connect those traits to what HAPPENS in the story. Let's warm up with a quick game."

Read each trait. The student gives an example of an action someone with that trait might take:

1. **Generous:** What might a generous character do? (Share their lunch, give a gift, help someone carry things)
2. **Curious:** What might a curious character do? (Ask lots of questions, explore a new place, open a mysterious box)
3. **Impatient:** What might an impatient character do? (Rush ahead without waiting, interrupt someone, skip steps)

Say: "See how traits lead to actions? And those actions lead to EVENTS in the story. Today we are going to trace that chain: a character IS something, so they DO something, and because of that, something HAPPENS."

*Facilitator note: This warm-up bridges character description (Lesson 3) to cause-and-effect reasoning (this lesson). If the student gives creative or unexpected responses, celebrate that — it shows they understand the trait-to-action connection.*

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach how character actions drive story events through cause-and-effect relationships.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Cause:** The reason something happens. In stories, a character's action is often the cause.
- **Effect:** What happens as a result. In stories, the event that follows a character's action is the effect.
- **Sequence of events:** The order in which things happen in a story — first, then, next, finally.
- **Contribute to:** To help make something happen. A character's actions CONTRIBUTE TO the events in a story — they help cause them.
- **Consequence:** What happens because of an action. Every action has consequences — sometimes good, sometimes bad.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Introduce Cause and Effect in Stories (3 minutes)**

Say: "You have been learning about cause and effect since Grade 2. You know that a CAUSE is the reason something happens, and the EFFECT is what happens as a result. In stories, the characters' ACTIONS are often the causes, and the story EVENTS are the effects."

Write on paper:

**Character Action (CAUSE) --> Story Event (EFFECT)**

Say: "Here is the big idea for today: Characters do not just exist in stories — they DRIVE the story. What happens in a story happens BECAUSE of what the characters do. When a character makes a choice or takes an action, that action pushes the story forward."

**Step 2: The Cause-and-Effect Frame (2 minutes)**

Write:

**"Because [character] [action], [event] happened."**

Examples:
- Because Goldilocks ate the porridge, the three bears were upset.
- Because the boy helped the lost puppy, the puppy's owner gave him a reward.

Say: "This frame helps us connect character actions to story events. We are going to use it a lot today."

### Worked Examples

**Step 3: Model with a Passage (5-7 minutes)**

Read this passage aloud:

> **The Wrong Turn**
>
> Cora was supposed to walk straight home after school, but when she reached the corner of Oak and Pine, she noticed a narrow dirt path she had never seen before. Cora was the most curious person in her family — she could never resist a mystery.
>
> She turned onto the path. It wound through a grove of tall trees and ended at a small pond surrounded by wildflowers. Dragonflies darted over the water, and a frog sat on a rock, blinking at her.
>
> Cora sat on a log and watched the dragonflies for a long time. She completely forgot about going home.
>
> When she finally looked at her watch, it was 5:15. She was supposed to be home at 4:00. Cora jumped up and ran all the way home.
>
> Her mother was standing on the porch with her arms crossed. "Where have you been?" she asked, her voice tight with worry.
>
> "I found an amazing pond," Cora began, but she could see her mother's face. "I am sorry. I should have come home first and asked permission."
>
> Her mother sighed. "I was scared, Cora. We will talk about the pond after dinner. But next time, you come home first."

**Model — Tracing the Cause-and-Effect Chain:**

Say: "Let me trace how Cora's actions drive the events of this story."

Write a chain on paper:

**Cora's trait:** Curious (the text says "she could never resist a mystery")

**Cause-and-Effect Chain:**
1. **Because** Cora noticed a path she had never seen before, **she decided** to turn onto it. (Character action drives the story)
2. **Because** Cora turned onto the path, **she discovered** a beautiful pond with dragonflies. (Action leads to event)
3. **Because** Cora sat watching dragonflies, **she forgot** about going home. (Action leads to consequence)
4. **Because** Cora forgot about the time, **she was late** and her mother was worried. (Consequence builds)
5. **Because** Cora saw her mother's worried face, **she apologized** and admitted she should have asked permission. (Character responds to consequence)

Say: "See how each of Cora's ACTIONS causes the NEXT event? The story moves forward because of what Cora DOES. Her curiosity is the engine of the whole story. If Cora were not curious, she never would have turned onto the path, and none of this would have happened."

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice with support — tracing how character actions cause events.

**Activity 1: Build a Cause-and-Effect Chain Together (6 minutes)**

Read this passage together:

> **The Bridge Builder**
>
> After the big storm, the wooden footbridge over Miller Creek was broken. The creek was too wide and too fast to cross without it. That meant the children who lived on the far side of the creek could not walk to the park.
>
> Tomas lived on the park side. He could see the children across the creek, waving and calling out. They looked disappointed.
>
> "I have to do something," Tomas thought. He went to his garage and found two long boards, some nails, and a hammer. His grandfather, who had been a carpenter, had taught him how to use tools.
>
> Tomas carried the boards to the creek. He laid them across the narrowest part and nailed them together with cross-pieces so they would not slide apart. It was not pretty, but it was sturdy.
>
> One by one, the children crossed Tomas's bridge. The last one across was a small girl who stopped in the middle and looked down at the rushing water.
>
> "Don't look down," Tomas called. "Look at me."
>
> She looked up, smiled, and walked the rest of the way across.
>
> That afternoon, the park was full of children again. And everyone agreed that Tomas had built the best bridge in the neighborhood.

Draw a cause-and-effect chain together. Have the student help fill in each box:

| # | Character Action (CAUSE) | Story Event (EFFECT) |
|---|--------------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | The storm broke the footbridge | Children on the far side could not get to the park |
| 2 | Tomas saw the children looking disappointed | He decided he had to do something |
| 3 | Tomas got boards, nails, and a hammer from his garage | He had the tools to build a new bridge |
| 4 | Tomas built a bridge across the narrowest part | The children could cross again |
| 5 | The small girl was scared on the bridge | Tomas encouraged her: "Look at me" |

*Facilitator prompt: After completing the chain, ask: "What character trait drove Tomas to take action?" (Helpful, determined, kind, caring.) Then ask: "If Tomas had a DIFFERENT trait — say, he was lazy — would this story have happened the same way?" (No! If Tomas were lazy, he would not have built the bridge.) This shows how character traits drive actions, which drive events.*

**Activity 2: Because... Sentences (6 minutes)**

Using the passage above, have the student write three "Because..." sentences:

1. Because Tomas ________________, ________________.
2. Because the girl ________________, Tomas ________________.
3. Because Tomas built the bridge, ________________.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student writes a correct but vague sentence, push for specificity. "Because Tomas helped" is okay, but "Because Tomas carried boards to the creek and nailed them together" is better because it uses specific text evidence.*

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices independently — tracing character actions and their effects on story events.

Have the student read the following passage independently, then complete the activities below.

> **The Painting Contest**
>
> Ms. Rivera announced a class painting contest. The theme was "My Favorite Place." Whoever painted the best picture would have it displayed in the school lobby for a month.
>
> Dion wanted to win badly. He decided to paint the basketball court where he played every weekend with his dad. He spent two weeks working on his painting at home, mixing colors carefully and adding small details like the cracks in the pavement and the chain-link fence around the court.
>
> The night before the contest, Dion's classmate Hana called him. "I spilled water all over my painting," she said, her voice shaking. "It is ruined. I do not have time to start over. I am just going to drop out."
>
> Dion thought for a moment. He had extra paints and a spare canvas board.
>
> "Come over to my house right now," he said. "I have extra supplies. We can work together."
>
> Hana came over, and Dion helped her start a new painting. He gave her tips about mixing green for the garden she wanted to paint. They worked side by side until 9 o'clock.
>
> The next day, both Dion and Hana turned in their paintings. The judge studied each one carefully. When the winner was announced, it was Hana. Her painting of a garden bursting with flowers took everyone's breath away.
>
> Dion clapped along with everyone else. Hana ran over to him. "I could not have done this without you," she said.
>
> Dion shrugged and smiled. "You did the painting," he said. "I just lent you some brushes."

**Activity 1: Cause-and-Effect Chain**

Fill in the chart:

| # | Character Action (CAUSE) | Story Event (EFFECT) |
|---|--------------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | Hana spilled water on her painting | |
| 2 | Dion decided to help Hana | |
| 3 | Dion gave Hana supplies and tips | |
| 4 | Hana turned in her new painting | |

**Activity 2: Because... Sentences**

Write three "Because..." sentences about this story:

1. Because Hana _________________________________, _________________________________.
2. Because Dion _________________________________, _________________________________.
3. Because _________________________________, _________________________________.

**Activity 3: Character Traits and Actions**

Answer in complete sentences with text evidence:
- What character trait does Dion show? How do his ACTIONS prove it?
- Did Dion's kindness cause him to lose the contest? Explain using evidence from the text.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Complete Activities 1 and 2. For Activity 3, answer the first question orally with facilitator support.

**Stretch:** Complete all three activities in writing. For Activity 3, answer both questions with text evidence.

**Challenge:** Complete all three activities. Then write a paragraph (4-5 sentences) explaining whether you think Dion made the right choice. Use text evidence to support your opinion. Consider: what would have happened if Dion had NOT helped Hana?

### Interleaved Review

5. **Text evidence (Lesson 2):** Using the evidence frame "I know ___ because the text says ___," answer this question: How do you know Dion worked hard on his own painting?
6. **Character traits (Lesson 3):** Using the S.D.T.F. chart, list one thing Dion SAYS, one thing he DOES, and one thing he FEELS in this story.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Read this short passage:

> Priya noticed that the class hamster's water bottle was empty. She refilled it during recess while everyone else was playing outside. The next morning, the teacher thanked the whole class for taking care of the hamster, but only Priya knew who had actually done it.

1. What did Priya DO? (She refilled the hamster's water bottle during recess.)
2. What happened BECAUSE of her action? (The hamster had water; the teacher thanked the class.)
3. Write a "Because..." sentence: "Because Priya ___, ___."
4. What character trait does this action show? (Responsible, caring, selfless, humble)

**Mastery = Student correctly identifies the action, its effect, writes a Because sentence, AND names an appropriate trait.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice with simple everyday cause and effect: "Because I forgot my umbrella, I got wet." "Because the alarm did not go off, I was late."
- Return to the model passage (Cora) and walk through the chain again step by step
- Focus on one cause-and-effect pair at a time before building chains
- Repeat this lesson before moving to Lesson 5

### If Mastery Is Met
- Move to Lesson 5 (Character Feelings and Motivations) next session
- During everyday reading, ask: "What happened BECAUSE of what that character did?"

---

## Extension (Optional)

1. **What If?** Rewrite one event in the story by changing a character's action. "What if Dion had NOT helped Hana? How would the story be different?" The student writes or tells an alternative version, showing how different actions lead to different events.

2. **Cause-and-Effect Comic Strip:** The student draws a 4-6 panel comic strip showing a cause-and-effect chain from one of the stories. Under each panel, write the "Because..." sentence.

3. **Action Consequences in Real Life:** Discuss a real-life example: "Think about a time when something you DID caused something to happen. Tell me the cause-and-effect chain." This connects literary analysis to real-world reasoning.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**This lesson bridges character analysis and plot analysis:** In Lesson 3, students learned to describe characters using traits. In this lesson, they learn that those traits drive the story. This is a sophisticated idea for third graders — that characters are not just passengers in a story but the engines that make events happen. Return to this idea repeatedly: "Why did this happen? Because of what the character DID."

**The "Because..." frame is powerful:** Just as the "I know ___ because the text says ___" frame anchored Lesson 2, the "Because [character] did ___, [event] happened" frame anchors this lesson. It gives students a reliable structure for expressing cause-and-effect relationships. Some students will resist using the frame because it feels formulaic. Encourage them: "The frame helps your brain organize the idea. Once you have practiced it many times, you will not need it anymore."

**Not all cause-and-effect is simple:** In the painting contest passage, the cause-and-effect chain raises a complex question: Did Dion's kindness CAUSE him to lose? The text does not directly say this — Hana won because her painting was excellent, not because Dion helped her. But without Dion's help, Hana would not have had a painting to submit. This kind of nuance is wonderful for discussion. Let the student wrestle with it. There is no single right answer.

**Connecting to the real world:** Third graders are at a developmental stage where they are beginning to understand that their actions have consequences beyond the immediate moment. The stories in this lesson model this: Cora's curiosity leads her to a beautiful pond but also causes worry; Dion's generosity costs him nothing tangible but earns him a friend's gratitude. These themes resonate with students' lived experiences and make the literary analysis feel meaningful.
