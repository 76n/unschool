# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 1, Lesson 6

## Topic
Words in Context: Context Clues

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Use context clues to determine the meaning of unfamiliar words in a text
- Identify four types of context clues: definition, synonym, example, and contrast
- Apply a step-by-step strategy for figuring out unknown words while reading

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-5 (text evidence, character analysis, feelings and motivations)
- Ability to read grade-level text with reasonable fluency

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Reading passages provided in this lesson (print or display)
- Index cards (for vocabulary cards)
- Highlighter or colored pencil (for marking context clues)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about figuring out unknown words and review a character analysis skill from Lessons 3-5.

**Activity: Mystery Word**

Say: "I am going to read you a sentence with a word you might not know. See if you can figure out what it means just by reading the rest of the sentence."

1. "The dog was so **famished** after the long walk that it ate three bowls of food." What does "famished" mean? (Very hungry)
2. "The sky was **luminous** — it glowed with the light of a thousand stars." What does "luminous" mean? (Bright, glowing, full of light)
3. "Unlike her quiet sister, Maria was **boisterous** — always laughing loudly and running around." What does "boisterous" mean? (Loud, energetic, rowdy)

Say: "You just figured out three new words without looking them up in a dictionary! How? You used the other words in the sentence to help you. Those other words are called CONTEXT CLUES. Today we are going to learn four different types of context clues and a strategy for using them."

*Facilitator note: If the student does not get all three, that is perfectly fine. The warm-up is designed to show them that they CAN figure out unknown words. Even getting one right demonstrates the concept. Praise any attempts: "You used the clues around the word — that is exactly the skill we are going to practice today."*

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the four types of context clues and a step-by-step strategy for determining word meaning.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Context clues:** Words, phrases, or sentences AROUND an unfamiliar word that help you figure out what it means.
- **Definition clue:** The author tells you the meaning of the word right in the sentence, often after a dash, comma, or the words "which means" or "that is."
- **Synonym clue:** The author uses another word that means the same thing nearby. Look for "or," "also called," or a word in parentheses.
- **Example clue:** The author gives examples that help you understand the word. Look for "such as," "like," "for example," or "including."
- **Contrast clue:** The author uses an opposite word or idea nearby. Look for "but," "unlike," "however," "instead of," or "on the other hand."

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: The Four Types of Context Clues (6 minutes)**

Say: "There are four types of context clues. Think of them as four different tools in your reading toolbox."

Write each one on paper with an example:

**1. Definition Clue — The sentence TELLS you what the word means.**

> The **arid** desert — a place with very little rainfall — stretched for miles.

Say: "The author put the definition right there after the dash: 'a place with very little rainfall.' When you see dashes, commas, or phrases like 'which means' or 'that is,' look for a definition clue."

**2. Synonym Clue — A word that means the SAME thing is nearby.**

> The explorers were **exhausted**, completely worn out from their long journey.

Say: "'Worn out' means the same thing as 'exhausted.' The synonym is right next to the hard word. Look for clue words like 'or' and 'also called,' or just look for another word that seems to mean the same thing."

**3. Example Clue — Examples help you understand the word.**

> The market sold many types of **produce**, such as apples, carrots, lettuce, and peaches.

Say: "Apples, carrots, lettuce, and peaches are all examples of produce — they help you figure out that 'produce' means fruits and vegetables. Look for 'such as,' 'like,' 'for example,' or 'including.'"

**4. Contrast Clue — An OPPOSITE idea is nearby.**

> Unlike her **timid** brother, who hid behind their mother, Elsa ran right up to the stage.

Say: "Elsa ran right up to the stage — that is bold and brave. Her brother is the OPPOSITE — he is 'timid,' which means shy or scared. Look for 'unlike,' 'but,' 'however,' 'instead of,' or 'on the other hand.'"

**Step 2: The Context Clue Strategy (2 minutes)**

Write:

**When You Meet an Unknown Word:**
1. **Stop.** Do not skip it. Circle or underline it.
2. **Reread.** Read the sentence again, and the sentences before and after it.
3. **Look for clues.** Is there a definition, synonym, example, or contrast nearby?
4. **Make a guess.** Use the clues to guess the meaning.
5. **Check.** Substitute your guess back into the sentence. Does it make sense?

### Worked Examples

**Step 3: Model the Strategy (4 minutes)**

Read this passage aloud:

> **The Old Lighthouse**
>
> The lighthouse stood on a **precipice** — a high, steep cliff — overlooking the ocean. It had been there for over a hundred years, but now its walls were **dilapidated**. The paint was peeling, windows were cracked, and the wooden door hung from one hinge. However, the view from the top was **magnificent**, not plain or ordinary at all. The ocean sparkled in every direction, as far as you could see.

**Model — "precipice":**

Say: "I do not know what 'precipice' means. Let me use my strategy."
- Stop: I circled "precipice."
- Reread: "The lighthouse stood on a precipice — a high, steep cliff — overlooking the ocean."
- Look for clues: There is a DEFINITION clue right after the dash: "a high, steep cliff."
- Guess: A precipice is a high, steep cliff.
- Check: "The lighthouse stood on a high, steep cliff overlooking the ocean." Yes, that makes sense!

**Model — "dilapidated":**

Say: "Now let me try 'dilapidated.'"
- Stop: I circled "dilapidated."
- Reread: "Its walls were dilapidated. The paint was peeling, windows were cracked, and the wooden door hung from one hinge."
- Look for clues: These are EXAMPLE clues — peeling paint, cracked windows, a broken door.
- Guess: "Dilapidated" means old and falling apart.
- Check: "Its walls were old and falling apart." Yes, that fits!

**Model — "magnificent":**

Say: "And 'magnificent.'"
- Reread: "The view was magnificent, not plain or ordinary at all."
- Look for clues: CONTRAST clue — "not plain or ordinary." It is the OPPOSITE of plain or ordinary.
- Guess: "Magnificent" means amazing, wonderful, or extraordinary.
- Check: "The view was amazing." Yes!

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice with support — identifying context clue types and determining word meanings.

**Activity 1: Identify the Clue Type (5 minutes)**

Read each sentence together. The student identifies the context clue type and guesses the meaning of the underlined word.

1. "The **terrain**, or land surface, was rocky and uneven."
   - Clue type? (Definition — "or land surface")
   - Meaning? (Land surface, ground)

2. "The children were **jubilant** — they shouted, danced, and threw confetti in the air."
   - Clue type? (Example — shouting, dancing, throwing confetti are examples of jubilant behavior)
   - Meaning? (Very happy, celebrating)

3. "The first path was smooth and easy, but the second was **treacherous**, full of loose rocks and deep holes."
   - Clue type? (Contrast — "smooth and easy" vs. "treacherous")
   - Meaning? (Dangerous, difficult)

4. "The **nimble** squirrel, quick and light on its feet, leaped from branch to branch."
   - Clue type? (Synonym — "quick and light on its feet")
   - Meaning? (Quick, agile, light-footed)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student identifies a different clue type than expected, discuss it. Some sentences contain more than one type of clue. The important thing is that the student uses the clues to figure out the meaning.*

**Activity 2: Context Clues in a Story (7 minutes)**

Read this passage together:

> **The Inventor's Workshop**

>

> The first step was easy, but the second step was **perplexing**. I stared at the diagram, confused. The pieces did not seem to fit.
>
> "Do not get **discouraged**," said Grandpa Leo. "Stay hopeful. Every inventor hits a tricky spot."
>
> I tried again. This time, I turned one piece upside down, and suddenly everything clicked into place.
>

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices independently — using context clues to determine word meanings in a story.

Have the student read the following passage independently, then complete the activities below.

> **The Mapmaker's Daughter**
>
> In a small village surrounded by dense forest — trees packed so closely together that sunlight barely reached the ground — there lived a girl named Suri. Her father was the village mapmaker. He spent his days drawing **meticulous** maps, careful and precise down to every tiny stream and footpath.
>
> Suri wanted to be a mapmaker too, but her father said she was too young for such **arduous** work. "It is long, tiring work," he told her. "You need patience."
>
> One morning, a traveler arrived at the village. He looked **weary** — his shoulders slumped, his eyes were heavy, and he walked as if every step cost him great effort. He needed a map of the forest, but Suri's father was away visiting another village.
>
> "I can help," said Suri. She pulled out her own notebook, where she had been secretly sketching maps of the forest paths she explored.
>
> The traveler looked at her drawings. "These are **remarkable**," he said. "Not ordinary at all. You have real talent."
>
> Suri's heart swelled with pride, but she kept her voice **composed**, calm and steady. "Thank you," she said. "Let me draw you a proper map."
>
> She worked for an hour, adding every detail she could remember — the crooked oak tree where the path split, the stream that dried up in summer, the hill that was slippery after rain. When she handed the finished map to the traveler, he studied it carefully.

> "Your father will be proud," he said.

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Complete Activity 1 for four of the six words. The student may discuss answers orally and the facilitator records them.

**Stretch:** Complete Activity 1 for all six words in writing. Complete Activity 2 — write three original sentences.

**Challenge:** Complete both activities. Then find one additional unfamiliar word in the passage (or a word of your choice), explain what context clues help you understand it, and write it in a new sentence. Also write a short paragraph (3-4 sentences) describing Suri's character traits, using text evidence.

### Interleaved Review

3. **Character traits (Lesson 3):** What character traits describe Suri? Name two traits and support each with text evidence.
4. **Character feelings (Lesson 5):** How did Suri feel when the traveler praised her maps? How do you know? Use the "feeling to action" frame.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Read these sentences and figure out the meaning of the bold word. Name the clue type.

1. "The kitten was **miniature** — so tiny it fit in the palm of my hand."
   Word meaning: ___ Clue type: ___

2. "The lake was **tranquil**, calm and peaceful, without a single ripple on the surface."
   Word meaning: ___ Clue type: ___

3. "While some students were bored, Ava was **enthralled** by the story — she could not look away."
   Word meaning: ___ Clue type: ___

**Mastery = Student correctly identifies the meaning of at least 2 out of 3 words AND identifies at least 2 clue types correctly.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Focus on one clue type at a time (start with definition clues, which are the most explicit)
- Practice with sentences where the context clue is very obvious
- Highlight the signal words (or, but, unlike, such as) in different colors
- Repeat this lesson with additional examples before moving to Lesson 7

### If Mastery Is Met
- Move to Lesson 7 (Figurative Language Introduction) next session
- Encourage the student to use context clues whenever they encounter unfamiliar words in any reading

## Extension (Optional)

1. **Context Clue Cards:** The student creates vocabulary cards for the new words from this lesson. On one side, write the word. On the other, write the definition, the context clue type, and an original sentence. Review the cards periodically for spaced practice.

2. **Clue Type Scavenger Hunt:** The student reads a page from any book and tries to find at least one example of each context clue type (definition, synonym, example, contrast). They may not find all four, but the search builds awareness.

3. **Create Your Own Clues:** The student writes four sentences, each containing an unfamiliar or made-up word, with a different type of context clue in each. The facilitator tries to guess the word meanings. This reversal deepens understanding of how context clues work.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Context clues are a lifelong skill:** The ability to figure out unfamiliar words from context is one of the most practical skills in this entire unit. It is how adults encounter and learn new vocabulary in everyday reading. Third graders are ready to be intentional about this process, and the four clue types give them a framework for what has previously been intuitive guessing.

**Not every word has clear context clues:** Sometimes the surrounding text does not provide enough information to determine a word's meaning. When this happens, it is appropriate to look the word up in a dictionary or glossary. Teach the student: "Context clues are your first tool. If the clues are not enough, a dictionary is your backup tool." This prepares them for the Grade 3 Unit 4 focus on using dictionaries and glossaries.

**Signal words are important:** Teach students to watch for signal words that indicate a context clue is present: "or," "which means," "that is," "also called" (definition/synonym); "such as," "like," "for example," "including" (example); "but," "unlike," "however," "instead of," "on the other hand" (contrast). Highlighting these signal words helps students recognize context clues more quickly.

**This is the beginning of vocabulary in context, not the end:** In this lesson, students learn the mechanics of context clues. In Lesson 7, they will encounter figurative language, where context clues help them realize that a word or phrase does not mean what it literally says. Both lessons build vocabulary skills that will be formalized in Unit 4 (Grammar & Vocabulary) with dictionary and glossary use.
