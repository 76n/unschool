# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 1, Lesson 2

## Topic
Finding Evidence in the Text

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Locate specific words and sentences in a text that answer a given question
- Underline or highlight text evidence to support an answer
- Use the sentence frame "I know ___ because the text says ___" to construct evidence-based answers

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1 (asking questions, right there vs. think and search)
- Ability to read a grade-level passage and identify question words

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Reading passages provided in this lesson (print copies are ideal so the student can underline directly)
- Two colors of highlighter or colored pencil
- Index cards or sticky notes

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review the Lesson 1 concept of text evidence and activate the habit of going back to the text.

**Activity: Quick Evidence Hunt**

Display or read this very short passage:

> The playground was empty when Rosa arrived at 7:30 in the morning. She liked getting there early because she could swing as long as she wanted without waiting in line. The air was cool and the grass was still wet with dew.

Ask:
1. "What time did Rosa arrive at the playground?" (7:30 in the morning)
2. "Why did she like getting there early?" (She could swing as long as she wanted without waiting in line)

After each answer, say: "Now point to the exact words in the text that prove your answer."

Say: "Today we are going to get even better at this. We are going to practice finding evidence like detectives — underlining it, highlighting it, and using it to build strong answers."

*Facilitator note: The warm-up should feel quick and confident. If the student answered well, praise their evidence-finding skills. If they struggled, reassure them: "That is exactly what we are going to practice today. By the end of this lesson, you will be an evidence expert."*

---

## Direct Instruction (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the student how to systematically find, mark, and use text evidence when answering questions.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Evidence:** Proof. In reading, evidence is the words from the text that prove your answer is correct.
- **Underline/Highlight:** To mark the specific words in the text that answer a question. This helps you show your proof.
- **Sentence frame:** A partial sentence that helps you organize your answer. Today we will use: "I know ___ because the text says ___."
- **Cite:** To point to or refer to a specific part of the text. When you cite evidence, you are showing exactly where your proof comes from.

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: Why Evidence Matters (2 minutes)**

Say: "Imagine you are a detective solving a mystery. A detective does not just say 'I think the butler did it.' A detective has to show the PROOF — the fingerprints, the footprints, the clues. Reading is the same way. When we answer questions about a story, we need to show our proof. That proof comes from the words in the text."

Write on paper:

**Strong answer = Your idea + Proof from the text**

Say: "A strong answer has two parts: what you think AND the evidence that proves it. Let me show you what this looks like."

**Step 2: Introduce the Evidence-Finding Steps (3 minutes)**

Write these steps where the student can see them:

**How to Find Evidence:**
1. **Read the question carefully.** What is it asking?
2. **Go back to the text.** Reread the passage (or the part you think has the answer).
3. **Find and mark the evidence.** Underline or highlight the specific words that answer the question.
4. **Build your answer.** Use the sentence frame: "I know ___ because the text says ___."

Say: "These four steps will help you every time you need to answer a question with evidence. Let me model them for you."

### Worked Examples

**Step 3: Model the Four Steps (5 minutes)**

Display this passage:

> **The Rainy Day Plan**
>
> Lucas stared out the window at the heavy rain pouring down. Today was supposed to be the day he and his friends played soccer in the park. Instead, the field was a giant puddle.
>
> "I have an idea," said his older sister, Diana. She pulled out a large cardboard box from the garage and grabbed scissors, tape, and markers.
>
> For the next two hours, Lucas and Diana turned the box into an amazing cardboard castle. They cut out windows, drew a drawbridge, and taped on paper flags. Their little brother, Miguel, helped color the walls.
>
> When their mom came home, she said, "That is the most creative thing I have ever seen!"
>
> Lucas grinned. The rainy day had turned out to be better than soccer.

**Model — Question: Why could Lucas not play soccer?**

Say: "Step 1: I read the question carefully. It is asking WHY Lucas could not play soccer."

Say: "Step 2: I go back to the text. I think the answer is near the beginning..." (Reread paragraph 1.)

Say: "Step 3: I find and mark the evidence." Underline: "heavy rain pouring down" and "the field was a giant puddle."

Say: "Step 4: I build my answer using the frame." Write:

**"I know Lucas could not play soccer because the text says there was 'heavy rain pouring down' and 'the field was a giant puddle.'"**

Say: "See how my answer has two parts? I stated what I know, AND I showed the exact words from the text. That is a strong, evidence-based answer."

**Model — Question: How did Lucas feel about the rainy day by the end of the story?**

Say: "This is a think-and-search question. Let me look at more than one part of the text."

Underline: "Lucas grinned" and "The rainy day had turned out to be better than soccer."

Write: **"I know Lucas ended up happy about the rainy day because the text says he 'grinned' and thought 'the rainy day had turned out to be better than soccer.'"**

## Guided Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice with support — the student finds and marks evidence, then builds answers using the sentence frame.

**Activity 1: Underline the Evidence (6 minutes)**

Say: "Now it is your turn. We are going to read a new passage, and you will find and underline the evidence for each question."

Read this passage together:

> **The Garden Helper**
>
> Every Saturday, Kira walked to Mrs. Yamamoto's house next door to help with the garden. Mrs. Yamamoto was eighty-two years old and could not bend down easily anymore, so Kira did the digging and weeding.
>
> Kira did not mind the hard work. She loved feeling the cool dirt between her fingers. She liked watching tiny green sprouts push up through the soil. And she especially loved the fresh tomatoes Mrs. Yamamoto gave her at the end of each visit.
>
> One Saturday, Kira noticed something new in the garden — a row of small sunflower seeds planted in a perfect line.
>
> "I planted those for you," said Mrs. Yamamoto, smiling. "By the end of summer, they will be taller than you are. Every time you see them, I hope you will remember that helping others always grows something beautiful."
>
> Kira felt a warm glow in her chest. She could not wait to see them bloom.

Ask the following questions one at a time. For each question, have the student:
- Reread the relevant part of the text
- Underline or highlight the evidence
- Say or write the answer using the frame: "I know ___ because the text says ___."

1. **Why does Kira help Mrs. Yamamoto with the garden?**
   Expected evidence: "Mrs. Yamamoto was eighty-two years old and could not bend down easily anymore"

2. **Name two things Kira loves about gardening.**
   Expected evidence: "feeling the cool dirt between her fingers" and "watching tiny green sprouts push up through the soil" (or the tomatoes)

3. **What did Mrs. Yamamoto plant for Kira?**
   Expected evidence: "a row of small sunflower seeds planted in a perfect line"

*Facilitator prompt: If the student underlines too much text (an entire paragraph), say: "You are in the right area! Now can you narrow it down? Which specific words answer the question?" If the student underlines too little (one word), say: "Good start! Can you include a few more words so the evidence makes sense on its own?"*

**Activity 2: Color-Coded Evidence (6 minutes)**

Say: "Now I am going to ask you a bigger question about the story, and you will need to find evidence from MORE than one place. Use one color for the first piece of evidence and a second color for the second piece."

**Question: How do we know that Kira and Mrs. Yamamoto care about each other?**

Guide the student to find:
- Evidence that Kira cares about Mrs. Yamamoto: She walks next door every Saturday to help; she does digging and weeding because Mrs. Yamamoto cannot bend down easily (Color 1)
- Evidence that Mrs. Yamamoto cares about Kira: She gives Kira fresh tomatoes; she planted sunflower seeds for Kira (Color 2)

Have the student write a complete answer using the frame:

"I know Kira and Mrs. Yamamoto care about each other because the text says _____ (Kira's evidence) and also _____ (Mrs. Yamamoto's evidence)."

*Facilitator prompt: This is a great opportunity to praise the student for combining evidence from different parts of the text. Say: "You found evidence in two different paragraphs and put them together. That is exactly what strong readers do!"*

## Independent Practice (10-12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices independently — finding, marking, and using text evidence.

Have the student read the following passage independently, then answer the questions using the evidence frame.

> **The Talent Show**
>
> Nolan loved building things out of cardboard, paper, and tape. His bedroom was full of his creations — a cardboard guitar, a paper airplane collection that covered one whole wall, and a model of the solar system hanging from the ceiling.
>
> When his teacher, Mr. Franklin, announced the school talent show, Nolan felt a knot in his stomach. Most kids would sing, dance, or play an instrument. Nobody was going to build something out of cardboard on stage.
>
> "You should enter," said his best friend, Priya. "Show everyone what you can do."
>
> Nolan thought about it all weekend. On Monday, he told Mr. Franklin he wanted to build something live on stage. Mr. Franklin raised his eyebrows and then smiled. "I love it," he said. "That takes courage."
>
> On the night of the talent show, Nolan walked onto the stage with a pile of cardboard, scissors, and tape. In five minutes, while the audience watched, he built a dragon with moveable wings and a tail that swung back and forth. When he held it up, the audience gasped and then clapped and cheered.
>
> Nolan did not win the talent show. A girl who played violin won first place. But when he walked off stage, Priya was waiting with a huge grin. "That was the coolest thing I have ever seen," she said.
>
> Nolan held his dragon and smiled. He knew she was right.

**Questions — Answer using the evidence frame: "I know ___ because the text says ___."**

1. What is Nolan's hobby? Underline the evidence and write your answer.
2. Why did Nolan feel nervous about the talent show? Underline the evidence and write your answer.
3. Who encouraged Nolan to enter the talent show? What did that person say? Underline the evidence.
4. How did the audience react to Nolan's dragon? Underline the evidence and write your answer.
5. How do we know Nolan felt good about his performance, even though he did not win? Find evidence from MORE than one sentence. (Think and search)

### Difficulty Tiers

**On-level:** Answer questions 1-4 using the sentence frame. Underline evidence for each.

**Stretch:** Answer all 5 questions using the sentence frame. For question 5, use two different colors to mark evidence from two different parts of the text.

**Challenge:** Answer all 5 questions. Then choose your own question about the story, underline the evidence, and write a complete answer using the frame. Also, write a short paragraph (3-4 sentences) explaining whether you think Nolan was brave and use text evidence to support your opinion.

### Interleaved Review

6. **Right there vs. think and search (Lesson 1):** Look at questions 1-5 above. Label each one "RT" (right there) or "TS" (think and search).
7. **Retelling practice (Grade 2 skill):** In your own words, retell what happened at the talent show in 2-3 sentences.

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Exit Ticket:**

Read this very short passage:

> Lila could not find her homework anywhere. She looked in her backpack, on the kitchen table, and under her bed. Finally, she found it in her little brother's toy box. It was wrinkled and had a crayon mark on it, but all her answers were still there.

Answer these questions. For each one, write the answer AND copy the exact text evidence:

1. Where did Lila finally find her homework? Evidence: ___
2. What condition was the homework in? Evidence: ___
3. Using the frame: "I know Lila searched many places because the text says ___."

**Mastery = Student answers all 3 correctly with accurate text evidence (exact or very close wording from the passage).**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice with two-sentence passages where the evidence is very obvious
- Have the student physically circle or highlight before answering aloud
- Use the "evidence sandwich": top = your answer, middle = proof from the text, bottom = restate your point
- Repeat this lesson with additional short passages before moving to Lesson 3

### If Mastery Is Met
- Move to Lesson 3 (Describing Characters) next session
- Continue to use the sentence frame "I know ___ because the text says ___" in all reading discussions

## Extension (Optional)

1. **Evidence Scavenger Hunt:** Give the student a question about a passage and set a timer for 60 seconds. How quickly can they find and underline the evidence? Make it a friendly challenge.

2. **Two-Color Highlighting Challenge:** Give the student a passage with 4-5 questions. Have them use one color for "right there" evidence and a different color for "think and search" evidence. By the end, they will see the difference visually — right there evidence is in one spot, while think and search evidence is scattered across the passage.

3. **Teach-Back:** The student becomes the teacher. They read a passage, write questions about it, and then quiz the facilitator. The student checks whether the facilitator's evidence is correct. (This reversal builds deep understanding of evidence-based answering.)

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**The sentence frame is a scaffold, not a crutch:** The frame "I know ___ because the text says ___" gives students a structure for organizing evidence-based answers. Over time, they will internalize this pattern and no longer need the frame. For now, encourage them to use it consistently. If they resist ("Can I just say the answer?"), explain: "The frame helps you build strong answers. Once it becomes automatic, you can say it in your own way — but for now, the frame helps your brain practice putting the evidence right next to your idea."

**Underlining is a physical anchor:** Having students physically mark the text is not just about organization — it slows down their thinking in a productive way. When a student has to find the specific words and underline them, they are forced to reread carefully and verify that their evidence actually answers the question. If the student is working on a screen and cannot underline, have them write down the exact words on a sticky note or paper.

**Common struggle — paraphrasing instead of citing:** Some students will restate the evidence in their own words instead of pointing to the exact text. Paraphrasing is a valuable skill, but at this stage, the goal is to teach students to locate and use specific text. Gently say: "I love how you put that in your own words. Now can you also show me the EXACT words from the text? Copy the author's words." Later in the year, students will learn to paraphrase effectively — but first, they need to master finding and citing direct evidence.

**Building toward longer written responses:** In this lesson, students are writing short evidence-based answers using a sentence frame. This is the foundation for the paragraph-length responses they will be expected to write later in the year (and in Grades 4-5). Every time they practice the frame "I know ___ because the text says ___," they are building the muscle for constructing evidence-based paragraphs.

## Answer Key

**Guided Practice — Activity 1:**
1. Kira helps because Mrs. Yamamoto is eighty-two years old and cannot bend down easily anymore. (Evidence: "Mrs. Yamamoto was eighty-two years old and could not bend down easily anymore, so Kira did the digging and weeding.")
2. Kira loves feeling the cool dirt between her fingers and watching tiny green sprouts push up through the soil. (Also acceptable: the fresh tomatoes.) Evidence in paragraph 2.
3. Mrs. Yamamoto planted a row of small sunflower seeds in a perfect line. (Evidence: paragraph 3)

**Guided Practice — Activity 2:**
Kira cares about Mrs. Yamamoto because she walks to her house every Saturday to help with the garden (paragraph 1). Mrs. Yamamoto cares about Kira because she gives her fresh tomatoes (paragraph 2) and planted sunflower seeds for her (paragraph 4).

**Independent Practice:**
1. Nolan's hobby is building things out of cardboard, paper, and tape. Evidence: "Nolan loved building things out of cardboard, paper, and tape."
2. Nolan felt nervous because most kids would sing, dance, or play an instrument, and nobody was going to build something out of cardboard on stage. Evidence: "Most kids would sing, dance, or play an instrument. Nobody was going to build something out of cardboard on stage."
3. Priya encouraged Nolan. She said: "You should enter. Show everyone what you can do." Evidence: paragraph 3.
4. The audience gasped and then clapped and cheered. Evidence: "the audience gasped and then clapped and cheered."
5. (Think and search) Evidence from multiple places: (a) Priya said "That was the coolest thing I have ever seen" (paragraph 6); (b) "Nolan held his dragon and smiled" (paragraph 7); (c) "He knew she was right" (paragraph 7). Putting these together shows Nolan felt good about his performance.
6. Labels: 1 = RT, 2 = RT (or TS if student argues they combined two sentences), 3 = RT, 4 = RT, 5 = TS
7. (Retelling) At the talent show, Nolan built a cardboard dragon with moveable wings on stage in five minutes. The audience loved it and cheered. He did not win first place, but his best friend Priya said it was the coolest thing she had ever seen.

**Exit Ticket:**
1. In her little brother's toy box. Evidence: "she found it in her little brother's toy box"
2. It was wrinkled and had a crayon mark on it, but the answers were still there. Evidence: "It was wrinkled and had a crayon mark on it, but all her answers were still there."
3. "I know Lila searched many places because the text says 'She looked in her backpack, on the kitchen table, and under her bed.'"
