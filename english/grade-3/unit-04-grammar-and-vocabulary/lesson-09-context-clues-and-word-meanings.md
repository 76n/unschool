# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 4, Lesson 9

## Topic
Context Clues, Word Parts, and Vocabulary Strategies

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Use four strategies for determining word meanings: context clues, word parts (prefixes and suffixes), dictionary, and glossary
- Identify common prefixes (un-, re-, pre-) and suffixes (-ful, -less, -ly, -er) and use them to figure out word meanings
- Choose the appropriate strategy based on the situation (when to use context clues vs. a dictionary)

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 8: Using a dictionary and glossary
- Unit 1: Introduction to context clues (determining word meanings from surrounding text)
- Ability to read grade-level text independently

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- A children's dictionary (from Lesson 8)
- Index cards (for prefix/suffix matching)
- Colored pencils (optional — for underlining context clues)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate vocabulary strategy thinking with a mystery word.

**Activity: What Does It Mean?**

Write this sentence on paper:

> "The explorers were **famished** after hiking all day without eating, so they quickly cooked a huge meal."

Say: "Read this sentence. You might not know the word 'famished.' But I bet you can figure out what it means WITHOUT a dictionary. What clues in the sentence help you?"

Let the student think. Prompt if needed: "What happened? They hiked all day without eating. Then what did they do? They quickly cooked a huge meal. So what do you think 'famished' means?"

(Very hungry / starving)

Say: "You just used CONTEXT CLUES — the words around the unknown word — to figure out its meaning. That is one of four strategies we are going to learn today for figuring out words you do not know."

**Lesson 8 Review:** "What are three things a dictionary entry tells you about a word?" (Spelling, pronunciation, part of speech, definition, example sentence — any three.)

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach four vocabulary strategies and when to use each one.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Context clues:** Hints from the surrounding words and sentences that help you figure out the meaning of an unknown word
- **Prefix:** A word part added to the BEGINNING of a word that changes its meaning (un-, re-, pre-)
- **Suffix:** A word part added to the END of a word that changes its meaning (-ful, -less, -ly, -er)
- **Word parts:** Prefixes and suffixes that can be added to a base word

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: The Four Strategies (2 minutes)**

Say: "When you come across a word you do not know, you have four tools in your vocabulary toolbox."

Write:

**The Vocabulary Toolbox**
1. **Context Clues** — Look at the words and sentences AROUND the unknown word for hints.
2. **Word Parts** — Break the word apart. Does it have a prefix or suffix you recognize?
3. **Dictionary** — Look it up! Get the exact definition.
4. **Glossary** — If you are reading a textbook, check the glossary in the back.

Say: "You already know how to use a dictionary and glossary from yesterday. Today, we are going to focus on context clues and word parts — the strategies you use FIRST, before reaching for the dictionary."

**Step 2: Context Clues — A Deeper Look (3 minutes)**

Say: "You learned about context clues in Unit 1. Let's go deeper. There are different types of context clues."

Write and explain each type:

**Type 1: Definition Clue** — The sentence actually defines the word for you.
> "A **habitat**, which is the place where an animal lives, must have food and water."
> (The sentence tells you right there: a habitat is the place where an animal lives.)

**Type 2: Example Clue** — The sentence gives examples that help you understand.
> "The pet store had many **reptiles**, such as snakes, lizards, and turtles."
> (Snakes, lizards, and turtles are all reptiles — so a reptile must be a scaly animal.)

**Type 3: Contrast Clue** — The sentence shows the opposite.
> "Unlike her **timid** brother, Maria was bold and brave."
> (Maria is bold and brave. Her brother is the OPPOSITE. So 'timid' means shy or scared.)

**Type 4: General Clue** — You use the overall meaning of the sentence to make a good guess.
> "The storm was **fierce**, with strong winds that knocked down trees and ripped off roofs."
> (Strong winds, knocked down trees, ripped off roofs — the storm was powerful and violent. So 'fierce' means very strong or intense.)

**Step 3: Word Parts — Prefixes and Suffixes (5 minutes)**

Say: "Sometimes you can figure out a word by looking at its PARTS. Many English words are built from a base word plus a prefix (at the beginning) or a suffix (at the end)."

**Common Prefixes:**

| Prefix | Meaning | Examples |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **un-** | not, opposite of | unhappy (not happy), unkind (not kind), unfair (not fair), unlock (reverse locking) |
| **re-** | again, back | rewrite (write again), redo (do again), replay (play again), return (go back) |
| **pre-** | before | preview (see before), preschool (before school), preheat (heat before) |

Say: "If you see a word that starts with 'un-,' you know it means 'NOT' whatever the base word is. Unhappy = not happy. Unkind = not kind. Unfair = not fair. Easy!"

**Common Suffixes:**

| Suffix | Meaning | Examples |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **-ful** | full of | careful (full of care), helpful (full of help), joyful (full of joy), beautiful (full of beauty) |
| **-less** | without | careless (without care), helpless (without help), fearless (without fear), homeless (without a home) |
| **-ly** | in a ___ way (makes an adverb) | quickly (in a quick way), quietly (in a quiet way), happily (in a happy way), sadly (in a sad way) |
| **-er** | one who does | teacher (one who teaches), reader (one who reads), singer (one who sings), player (one who plays) |

Say: "'Careful' means 'full of care.' 'Careless' means 'without care.' See how the suffix completely changes the meaning? If you know the base word AND the suffix, you can figure out the whole word."

**Practice breaking apart words:**
- "Unhelpful" = un + help + ful = NOT full of help = not helpful
- "Rewrite" = re + write = write again
- "Fearless" = fear + less = without fear = brave
- "Preschool" = pre + school = before school

**Step 4: When to Use Which Strategy (2 minutes)**

Say: "So you have four strategies. How do you decide which one to use?"

Write the decision process:

1. **TRY CONTEXT CLUES FIRST.** Read the sentence and look for hints. Can you make a good guess?
2. **CHECK WORD PARTS.** Does the word have a prefix or suffix you recognize? Can you figure out the meaning from the parts?
3. **STILL UNSURE? USE A DICTIONARY OR GLOSSARY.** If context clues and word parts give you a general idea but not the exact meaning, look it up.

Say: "Context clues and word parts are your FIRST tools — they are fast and you do not need to stop reading. The dictionary is your BACKUP — it gives you the exact meaning when the other strategies are not enough."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "What does 'disagreeable' mean in this sentence? 'The disagreeable weather ruined our picnic — it was cold, rainy, and windy.'"

Think aloud: "Let me try context clues first. The weather 'ruined our picnic' — cold, rainy, windy. So 'disagreeable' means unpleasant or not nice. Now let me check word parts: dis + agree + able. 'Dis-' means not. 'Agree' means to be in harmony. 'Able' means capable of. So 'disagreeable' literally means 'not able to agree with' — or unpleasant. Both strategies give me the same answer!"

**Example 2:** "What does 'relocate' mean?"

Think aloud: "I see a prefix! 're-' means again. The base word is 'locate,' which means to find or to be in a place. So 'relocate' means to locate again — to move to a new place."

**Example 3:** "What does 'glistened' mean in this sentence? 'The morning dew glistened on the leaves like tiny diamonds.'"

Think aloud: "Context clues: 'like tiny diamonds' — diamonds sparkle and shine. And dew is water droplets that catch the light. So 'glistened' means sparkled or shined. I do not see a helpful prefix or suffix, but the context clues were enough. If I wanted the exact definition, I could check a dictionary."

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice all four vocabulary strategies with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Strategy Match (5 minutes)**

Say: "For each word below, I want you to figure out the meaning. Tell me WHICH STRATEGY you are using."

1. "The **enormous** elephant was the biggest animal at the zoo."
   - Strategy: Context clues (biggest animal — enormous means very big)

2. "**Reread** the instructions carefully."
   - Strategy: Word parts (re- = again, read = read. Reread = read again.)

3. "The children were **ecstatic** when they heard the news — they jumped up and down, screamed, and hugged each other."
   - Strategy: Context clues (jumped, screamed, hugged — ecstatic means extremely happy, thrilled)

4. "She was a **fearless** explorer."
   - Strategy: Word parts (fear + -less = without fear = brave)

5. "The scientist made an important **discovery** — the **nocturnal** animal sleeps during the day and is active at night."
   - Strategy: Context clues (sleeps during day, active at night — nocturnal means active at night)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student goes straight to "I will look it up in the dictionary," say: "That is a great strategy! But let's try something faster first. Look at the words around the mystery word — do they give you any clues?" Encourage using context clues and word parts before the dictionary.*

**Activity 2: Prefix and Suffix Detective (7 minutes)**

Say: "I will give you a word with a prefix or suffix. Break it apart, tell me the meaning of each part, and then tell me what the whole word means."

Write each word. The student breaks it apart:

1. **unhappy** = un + happy = not happy (sad)
2. **playful** = play + ful = full of play (fun-loving, energetic)
3. **preview** = pre + view = view before (see something before it happens)
4. **careless** = care + less = without care (not paying attention)
5. **rebuild** = re + build = build again
6. **quietly** = quiet + ly = in a quiet way (softly, without much noise)
7. **unfriendly** = un + friend + ly = not in a friendly way (mean, cold)
8. **teacher** = teach + er = one who teaches

*Facilitator prompt: If the student struggles to break a word apart, cover the prefix or suffix with your finger so only the base word shows: "What word do you see? Now add back the prefix — what does 'un' mean?"*

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices vocabulary strategies independently.

**Part A: Context Clues (4 minutes)**

Use context clues to figure out the meaning of the bold word. Underline the clue words that helped you.

1. "The old house was **dilapidated** — the roof had holes, the windows were broken, and the porch was falling apart."
   What does "dilapidated" mean? (Falling apart, in very bad condition, run-down)
   What clue words helped you? (holes in roof, broken windows, porch falling apart)

2. "After running for an hour, Mia was **exhausted** and could barely keep her eyes open."
   What does "exhausted" mean? (Very tired)
   What clue words helped you? (running for an hour, could barely keep her eyes open)

3. "Unlike the **transparent** glass, the wooden door was completely solid and you could not see through it."
   What does "transparent" mean? (See-through, clear)
   What clue words helped you? ("Unlike" signals contrast; you could not see through the door, but you COULD see through the glass)

**Part B: Word Parts (4 minutes)**

Break apart each word and write what it means:

4. redo = _____ + _____ = _____ (re + do = do again)
5. hopeless = _____ + _____ = _____ (hope + less = without hope)
6. preheat = _____ + _____ = _____ (pre + heat = heat before)
7. wonderful = _____ + _____ = _____ (wonder + ful = full of wonder)
8. unkind = _____ + _____ = _____ (un + kind = not kind)
9. slowly = _____ + _____ = _____ (slow + ly = in a slow way)
10. singer = _____ + _____ = _____ (sing + er = one who sings)

**Part C: Choose Your Strategy (2 minutes)**

For each word, decide: would you use CONTEXT CLUES, WORD PARTS, or a DICTIONARY? Explain why.

11. You see the word "unbreakable" in a sentence. (Word parts — un + break + able = not able to be broken. The prefix and suffix tell you the meaning.)

12. You see the word "photosynthesis" in your science book. (Dictionary or glossary — this is a technical word that cannot easily be figured out from context clues or word parts alone.)

### Difficulty Tiers

- **On-level:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
- **Stretch:** Complete all parts, then find two unfamiliar words in a book and figure out their meanings — try context clues and word parts BEFORE using a dictionary. Write the word, your guess, and whether the dictionary confirmed your guess.
- **Challenge:** Write a "Vocabulary Detective Report" for three unfamiliar words you find in a book. For each word, try ALL applicable strategies (context clues, word parts, dictionary) and write what each strategy told you. Which strategy was most helpful for each word?

### Interleaved Review

13. **Lesson 7 Review:** Punctuate this dialogue correctly: *Lets go said Maya* ("Let's go," said Maya.)

14. **Lesson 4 Review:** Write the comparative: funny (funnier), large (larger), good (better).

15. **Unit 2 Review:** Name two text features that help you find information in a nonfiction book. (Headings, bold words, table of contents, index, captions, diagrams, maps, glossary — any two.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick assessment of vocabulary strategy use.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. What does "rewrite" mean? What strategy did you use? (Write again. Word parts: re- means again.)

2. Use context clues to figure out the bold word: "The **arid** desert gets almost no rain, and very few plants can grow there."
   What does "arid" mean? (Very dry.)
   What clues helped you? (almost no rain, very few plants)

3. Name the four vocabulary strategies in your toolbox. (Context clues, word parts, dictionary, glossary.)

**Mastery = Correct meaning for "rewrite" with strategy identified, correct meaning for "arid" with clue words identified, AND at least 3 of 4 strategies named.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Focus on one strategy at a time rather than all four
- For context clues: practice with easier sentences where the meaning is more obvious
- For word parts: start with just the three prefixes (un-, re-, pre-) and practice identifying them in words
- Create a "prefix and suffix reference card" that the student keeps while reading
- If the student relies too heavily on the dictionary, practice with context clues first and check with the dictionary after to build confidence

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 10 (Grammar and Vocabulary Review). Preview: "You now have a full set of grammar and vocabulary tools! Next lesson, we will review EVERYTHING from this unit — verbs, adjectives, commas, quotation marks, and vocabulary strategies — to get ready for the unit assessment."

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**

- **Word Part Inventor:** Make up new words using real prefixes and suffixes, then write definitions. Example: "Unjoyful" = not full of joy. "Presleep" = before sleep. "Re-eat" = eat again. This is silly but reinforces understanding of how word parts work.

- **Vocabulary Journal:** Start a personal vocabulary journal. Every time the student encounters a new word, they write: (1) the word, (2) the sentence where they found it, (3) what they think it means (from context clues or word parts), and (4) the dictionary definition. Over time, this builds a powerful personal reference.

- **Strategy Comparison:** Find three unfamiliar words in different texts. For each word, try BOTH context clues AND word parts (if applicable). Which strategy was more helpful? When are context clues better? When are word parts better? Write a short reflection.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson brings together context clues from Unit 1 and dictionary skills from Lesson 8, adding word parts as a new tool. If the student is already strong with context clues, you can move quickly through Step 2 and spend more time on prefixes and suffixes (Step 3), which are likely newer material.

**Prefix and suffix mastery takes time:** This lesson introduces seven prefixes and suffixes. The student does not need to memorize all of them immediately. The goal is for the student to KNOW that word parts are a strategy and to recognize the most common prefixes (un-, re-). Mastery of all seven will come with repeated exposure over the year.

**Context clues are not always enough:** Some words cannot be figured out from context alone, especially technical vocabulary, words with multiple meanings, or words in sparse contexts. Teach the student that context clues give an APPROXIMATE meaning — the dictionary gives the PRECISE meaning. Both are valuable.

**Connection to reading:** This lesson has the most direct impact on reading comprehension of any lesson in the unit. When a student can independently figure out word meanings while reading, they become a stronger, more confident reader. Reinforce these strategies during every reading session: "There is a word you do not know. What strategy will you try first?"

**The decision process matters:** The goal is not just to KNOW the four strategies — it is to know WHEN to use each one. Emphasize the flow: context clues first (fast, no interruption), word parts second (if applicable), dictionary third (most precise, but requires stopping). This decision-making process builds metacognition — thinking about your own thinking.

**"Un-" is the most productive prefix:** Research shows that "un-" is the most common prefix in English and can be added to hundreds of words. If you only teach one prefix thoroughly, make it "un-." Students who master "un-" can decode many unfamiliar words independently.
