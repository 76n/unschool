# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 4, Lesson 8

## Topic
Using a Dictionary and Glossary

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify the parts of a dictionary entry (entry word, pronunciation, part of speech, definition(s), example sentence)
- Use guide words at the top of a dictionary page to find words efficiently
- Look up a word independently in a print or digital dictionary
- Use a glossary in the back of a textbook to find word meanings

## Prerequisites
- Ability to recite the alphabet in order
- Ability to arrange words in alphabetical order (at least by first letter)
- Basic understanding of parts of speech: noun, verb, adjective (from earlier lessons)

## Materials Needed
- **A children's dictionary** (print dictionary is strongly preferred for this lesson — any children's or student dictionary will work; Scholastic, Merriam-Webster for Kids, or similar)
- **A textbook or nonfiction book with a glossary** in the back (any subject — science, social studies, or reading textbook)
- Pencil and paper
- Index cards (for alphabetical order practice)
- Sticky notes (for marking pages — optional)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice alphabetical order and introduce the idea of a reference tool for words.

**Activity: Alphabet Speed Challenge**

Say: "Today we are going to learn how to use one of the most powerful tools a reader and writer can have: the DICTIONARY. But first, we need to make sure you are comfortable with alphabetical order, because the dictionary is organized alphabetically."

**Quick alphabetical sort:** Write these words on paper (or on index cards). The student arranges them in alphabetical order:

1. monkey, apple, dog, horse, cat

(Alphabetical order: apple, cat, dog, horse, monkey)

2. Now a harder set — words that start with the SAME letter:
   bark, ball, banana, bear, boat

(Alphabetical order: ball, banana, bark, bear, boat)

Say: "When words start with the same letter, you look at the SECOND letter. B-A-L-L comes before B-A-N-A-N-A because 'L' comes before 'N' in the alphabet. This is exactly how the dictionary is organized."

**Lesson 7 Review:** "What punctuation marks do we put around the exact words a character says?" (Quotation marks: " ")

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach how to navigate a dictionary and read a dictionary entry.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Dictionary:** A book (or digital tool) that lists words in alphabetical order and gives their meanings, pronunciations, and other information
- **Entry word:** The word being defined — it is printed in bold at the beginning of the entry
- **Pronunciation:** A guide that shows how to say the word (often in parentheses or slashes)
- **Part of speech:** What kind of word it is — noun (n.), verb (v.), adjective (adj.)
- **Definition:** The meaning of the word
- **Example sentence:** A sentence that shows how the word is used
- **Guide words:** The two words at the TOP of each dictionary page that tell you the first and last words on that page
- **Glossary:** A mini-dictionary at the back of a textbook that defines key words used in that book

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: What Is a Dictionary? (2 minutes)**

Say: "A dictionary is like a word encyclopedia. It lists thousands of words in alphabetical order. For each word, it tells you: how to spell it, how to say it, what kind of word it is, and what it means. Some entries also give you an example sentence."

If you have a print dictionary, hand it to the student and let them flip through it. Say: "Look at how many words are in here! And they are all in alphabetical order — 'A' words first, 'Z' words last."

**Step 2: Parts of a Dictionary Entry (5 minutes)**

Say: "Let's look at what a dictionary entry includes. I am going to show you a sample entry and label each part."

Write (or show from the dictionary) a sample entry:

---

**brave** (brayv) *adjective*
1. Ready to face danger or pain without fear.
   *The brave firefighter ran into the burning building.*
2. Making a fine or impressive appearance.
   *The team wore their brave new uniforms.*

---

Now label each part:

| Part | What It Is | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Entry word** | The word being defined (in bold) | **brave** |
| **Pronunciation** | How to say the word (in parentheses) | (brayv) |
| **Part of speech** | Noun, verb, adjective, etc. (often abbreviated) | *adjective* |
| **Definition 1** | The first meaning of the word | Ready to face danger or pain without fear |
| **Example sentence** | A sentence showing how the word is used | *The brave firefighter ran into the burning building.* |
| **Definition 2** | A second meaning (many words have more than one!) | Making a fine or impressive appearance |

Say: "Many words have more than one definition. When you look up a word, you might need to read ALL the definitions to find the one that fits YOUR sentence."

**Common abbreviations in dictionaries:**
- n. = noun
- v. = verb
- adj. = adjective
- adv. = adverb
- pl. = plural

**Step 3: Guide Words (3 minutes)**

Say: "When you open a dictionary to a page, you will see two words at the very TOP of the page — one on the left and one on the right. These are called GUIDE WORDS."

If using a print dictionary, open to any page and point out the guide words.

Say: "The guide word on the LEFT is the FIRST word on that page. The guide word on the RIGHT is the LAST word on that page. Every other word on that page comes BETWEEN those two guide words alphabetically."

Write an example:
- Guide words at top of page: **flag --- flash**
- Words you would find on this page: flame, flat (YES — these come between flag and flash alphabetically)
- Words you would NOT find on this page: fly, fish (NO — 'fly' comes AFTER 'flash,' and 'fish' comes BEFORE 'flag')

Say: "Guide words save you time. Instead of reading every word on every page, you can look at the guide words and quickly decide: 'Is my word on THIS page, or should I flip forward or backward?'"

**Step 4: How to Look Up a Word — Step by Step (2 minutes)**

Say: "Here is the step-by-step process for looking up a word in a dictionary."

Write the steps:

1. **Think about the first letter.** Open the dictionary to that section. (A words are at the front, M words are in the middle, Z words are at the end.)
2. **Check the guide words** at the top of the page. Does your word come between them alphabetically?
3. **If YES:** Scan down the page for your word.
4. **If NO:** Flip forward or backward and check the guide words again.
5. **Found it!** Read the entry: pronunciation, part of speech, definition(s), example sentence.
6. **If the word has multiple definitions,** try each one in your sentence to see which fits.

**Step 5: What Is a Glossary? (1 minute)**

Say: "A GLOSSARY is like a mini-dictionary in the back of a textbook. It only includes the important words from THAT book. If you are reading a science book and find the word 'habitat,' you can flip to the glossary in the back and find its definition."

If you have a book with a glossary, show it to the student. Say: "Glossaries are shorter and easier to use than a full dictionary because they only have the words you need for that book."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "Let's look up the word 'enormous' in the dictionary."

Walk through each step:
1. "E — so I open to the E section."
2. "I check the guide words. This page says 'engine — equal.' Does 'enormous' come between 'engine' and 'equal'? E-N-O comes after E-N-G (engine) and before E-Q-U (equal)? Yes! Let me scan this page."
3. "Here it is: enormous (ih-NOR-muhs) *adjective* — Very large in size or amount. *The enormous whale swam beneath our boat.*"

**Example 2:** "The word 'bat' has multiple definitions. Let's figure out which one fits this sentence: 'A bat flew out of the cave.'"

Show two definitions:
1. bat *noun* — A piece of wood or metal used to hit a ball in baseball.
2. bat *noun* — A small animal that flies at night and uses sound to find its way.

Think aloud: "Definition 1 is about baseball. Does a baseball bat fly out of a cave? No. Definition 2 is about a flying animal. Does an animal bat fly out of a cave? Yes! Definition 2 is the right one."

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice looking up words in a dictionary with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Dictionary Scavenger Hunt (7 minutes)**

Give the student the dictionary. Say: "Let's look up some words together. Use the steps we learned."

Look up these words (adjust based on your dictionary):

1. **curious** — Find the entry. What part of speech is it? (adjective) What does it mean? (Eager to learn or know something.) Read the example sentence.

2. **journey** — Find the entry. How many definitions does it have? Read them. What part of speech is it? (noun — an act of traveling from one place to another)

3. **vanish** — Find the entry. What part of speech is it? (verb) What does it mean? (To disappear suddenly.) Use it in your own sentence.

For each word, the student should:
- Use guide words to find the right page
- Identify the entry word, pronunciation, part of speech, and definition
- Read the example sentence (if one is provided)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student gets lost, say: "What is the first letter of the word? Open to that section. Now check the guide words — is your word between them? If the word comes BEFORE the left guide word, flip backward. If it comes AFTER the right guide word, flip forward."*

**Activity 2: Glossary Practice (5 minutes)**

If you have a textbook with a glossary, use it. Otherwise, create a simple practice glossary:

---

**Practice Glossary**

**adaptation** (ad-ap-TAY-shun) *noun* — A change in a plant or animal that helps it survive in its environment.

**erosion** (ih-ROH-zhun) *noun* — The wearing away of rock or soil by wind, water, or ice.

**habitat** (HAB-ih-tat) *noun* — The natural place where a plant or animal lives and grows.

**migrate** (MY-grayt) *verb* — To move from one place to another, usually because of the weather or to find food.

**predator** (PRED-uh-tur) *noun* — An animal that hunts and eats other animals.

---

Questions for the student:

1. What does "habitat" mean? (The natural place where a plant or animal lives and grows.)
2. What part of speech is "migrate"? (verb)
3. Which word means "an animal that hunts other animals"? (predator)
4. Use "adaptation" in a sentence. (Answers will vary.)
5. Is a glossary organized in alphabetical order? (Yes!)

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices dictionary and glossary skills independently.

**Part A: Guide Words (3 minutes)**

The guide words on a dictionary page are **planet — plate**. Which of these words would you find on this page? Write YES or NO:

1. play (YES — p-l-a-y comes between planet and plate)
2. please (NO — p-l-e comes after p-l-a-t)
3. plant (YES — p-l-a-n-t comes between planet and plate)
4. place (NO — p-l-a-c comes before p-l-a-n)
5. plaster (YES — p-l-a-s comes between planet and plate)

**Part B: Dictionary Entry Reading (4 minutes)**

Read this dictionary entry and answer the questions:

---

**swift** (swift) *adjective*
1. Moving or capable of moving at great speed; fast.
   *The swift rabbit escaped from the fox.*
2. Happening in a short time; quick.
   *She made a swift decision.*

**swift** *noun*
3. A small bird with long, narrow wings, known for its fast flight.
   *We watched a swift darting through the sky.*

---

6. How many definitions does "swift" have? (three)
7. What parts of speech can "swift" be? (adjective and noun)
8. Which definition fits this sentence: "The swift flew past the window"? (Definition 3 — the bird)
9. Which definition fits this sentence: "The runner was swift and powerful"? (Definition 1 — moving at great speed)
10. Write your own sentence using "swift" as an adjective. (Answers will vary.)

**Part C: Look It Up! (3 minutes)**

Use your dictionary (or the practice glossary) to answer:

11. Look up the word "courage." What does it mean? What part of speech is it? Write the definition in your own words.
12. Look up a word of YOUR choice. Write the word, its part of speech, and its definition.

### Difficulty Tiers

- **On-level:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
- **Stretch:** Complete all parts, then look up three more words in the dictionary. For each word, write the part of speech, the definition, and use it in a sentence.
- **Challenge:** Look up five unfamiliar words from a book you are reading. For each word, write the definition that fits the sentence where you found the word. Explain how you chose the right definition when there were multiple options.

### Interleaved Review

13. **Lesson 6 Review:** Add commas to this sentence: "She packed her toothbrush a towel and her pajamas." (She packed her toothbrush, a towel, and her pajamas.)

14. **Lesson 3 Review:** Change this sentence to future tense: "I walked to the store." (I will walk to the store.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick assessment of dictionary skills.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Name three things you can learn about a word from a dictionary entry. (Any three of: spelling, pronunciation, part of speech, definition/meaning, example sentence.)

2. What are "guide words" and how do they help you? (Guide words are the two words at the top of a dictionary page that show the first and last entry on that page. They help you find words faster by showing whether your word is on that page.)

3. What is the difference between a dictionary and a glossary? (A dictionary is a big book with definitions for thousands of words. A glossary is a short list in the back of a textbook that defines only the key words in that book.)

**Mastery = At least 2 out of 3 questions answered correctly with reasonable explanations.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice alphabetical order skills — if the student cannot alphabetize, dictionary use will be very frustrating
- Start with the glossary (shorter, simpler) before moving to the full dictionary
- Practice looking up one word per day as a mini-activity until the process becomes comfortable
- If using a print dictionary, put alphabet tabs on the pages to make sections easier to find

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 9 (Context Clues and Word Meanings). Preview: "You now know how to use a dictionary and a glossary. But you do not always need a dictionary! Next lesson, you will learn other strategies for figuring out word meanings — like context clues and word parts."

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**

- **Dictionary Race:** The facilitator calls out a word. The student races to find it in the dictionary. Time each lookup. Can the student beat their own time? This builds speed and fluency with dictionary navigation.

- **Word of the Day:** The student opens the dictionary to a random page, points to a word with their eyes closed, and that becomes the Word of the Day. They read the definition, use it in a sentence, and try to use the word in conversation throughout the day.

- **Create Your Own Glossary:** The student picks a topic they love (dinosaurs, space, cooking, video games) and creates a glossary of 10 important words related to that topic. Each entry should include the word, the part of speech, and a definition in the student's own words.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** If the student has never used a print dictionary, plan for two sessions. The first session should focus on understanding dictionary entries and alphabetical order. The second session can focus on guide words and independent lookups. If the student is already familiar with dictionaries, one session is sufficient.

**Print vs. digital dictionaries:** A print dictionary is ideal for this lesson because it teaches the physical skills of navigating a book — flipping pages, using guide words, scanning entries. These are important research skills. However, if a print dictionary is not available, online dictionaries (Merriam-Webster, Dictionary.com) work well. For digital dictionaries, skip the guide words section and focus on typing the word, reading the entry, and choosing the right definition.

**Alphabetical order is the foundation:** If the student struggles with alphabetical order (especially when words share the same first letter), spend extra time on this before attempting dictionary lookups. Use index cards with letters or words and practice sorting. An effective technique: have the student recite the alphabet and point to the word that comes "earlier" or "later."

**Multiple definitions are tricky:** Many common words (like "bat," "run," "set," "light") have many definitions. The student must learn to try each definition in context to find the right one. This is a critical thinking skill that takes practice. Model the process: "Let me try Definition 1 in my sentence. Does it make sense? No? Let me try Definition 2..."

**The glossary as a stepping stone:** Glossaries are simpler and less intimidating than full dictionaries. If the student finds the dictionary overwhelming, start with glossary practice and build up to dictionary use over time.

**Connection to Lesson 9:** This lesson teaches the dictionary as a TOOL. Lesson 9 will teach when to use the dictionary vs. other strategies (context clues, word parts). The two lessons work together: Lesson 8 = HOW to use the dictionary; Lesson 9 = WHEN to use it (and what to try first).
