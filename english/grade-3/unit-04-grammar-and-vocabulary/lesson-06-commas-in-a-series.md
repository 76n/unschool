# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 4, Lesson 6

## Topic
Using Commas in a Series

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Use commas to separate three or more items in a series (list) within a sentence
- Apply the Oxford comma (comma before "and" in a series) consistently
- Write original sentences that include a series of items with correct comma placement

## Prerequisites
- Ability to write complete sentences with end punctuation
- Understanding of nouns, verbs, and adjectives (to create varied series)
- Lessons 1-5 (verb and adjective skills — not directly needed but build grammar awareness)

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Index cards (for sentence-building activity)
- Colored pencils (optional — for highlighting commas)
- A red pencil or pen (optional — for editing practice)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the need for commas in lists through a fun demonstration.

**Activity: The Confusing Grocery List**

Say: "Today we are going to learn about a tiny but powerful punctuation mark: the COMMA. Let me show you why commas matter."

Write this sentence on paper: "I bought apples peanut butter bread and milk at the store."

Say: "Read this sentence out loud."

Let the student try. It will feel rushed and confusing.

Now write: "I bought apples, peanut butter, bread, and milk at the store."

Say: "Now read THIS version. See how the commas help? They are like little pauses that separate each item in the list. Without commas, the reader does not know where one item ends and the next begins."

Ask: "How many items did I buy?" (Four: apples, peanut butter, bread, and milk.)

Say: "Without commas, someone might think I bought 'apples peanut' — is that even a thing? Commas keep lists clear!"

**Lesson 5 Review:** "What is the superlative of 'good'?" (best) "What is the superlative of 'bad'?" (worst)

---

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the rules for using commas in a series.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Comma:** A punctuation mark (,) used to separate items, pause in a sentence, or clarify meaning
- **Series:** A list of three or more items in a sentence
- **Oxford comma (serial comma):** The comma placed before "and" (or "or") in a list of three or more items

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: The Rule (3 minutes)**

Say: "When you list THREE or more items in a sentence, you put a comma after EACH item except the last one. The last item gets an 'and' (or 'or') before it."

Write the pattern:

**Item 1, Item 2, and Item 3.**

Write examples:
- "I like **apples, bananas, and grapes**."
- "She packed **a shirt, shorts, and sandals** for the trip."
- "The dog **ran, jumped, and barked** at the squirrel."

Say: "Count the items. Three items = two commas + 'and.' Let's see a longer list."

Write: "We visited **Texas, Florida, Ohio, and Maine** on our road trip."

Say: "Four items = three commas + 'and.' Five items = four commas + 'and.' The pattern is always: put a comma after every item, then 'and' before the last one."

**Step 2: The Oxford Comma (3 minutes)**

Say: "Did you notice that I put a comma BEFORE the 'and'? That last comma is called the OXFORD COMMA. Some people leave it out, but we are going to ALWAYS use it. Let me show you why."

Write two versions:
- WITHOUT Oxford comma: "I love my parents, Batman and Wonder Woman."
- WITH Oxford comma: "I love my parents, Batman, and Wonder Woman."

Say: "Without the Oxford comma, it sounds like your parents ARE Batman and Wonder Woman! The Oxford comma makes it clear that these are three separate things: (1) my parents, (2) Batman, and (3) Wonder Woman. Always use the Oxford comma — it prevents confusion."

**Step 3: Series Can Be Anything (2 minutes)**

Say: "A series can be a list of nouns, verbs, adjectives, or even phrases. Watch."

Write:
- **Nouns:** "I ate pizza, salad, and cake." (three things)
- **Verbs:** "The cat stretched, yawned, and fell asleep." (three actions)
- **Adjectives:** "The house was old, dark, and creepy." (three descriptions)
- **Phrases:** "We looked under the bed, behind the couch, and inside the closet." (three places)

Say: "As long as you have three or more items in a row, you need commas to separate them."

**Step 4: When You Do NOT Need Commas (2 minutes)**

Say: "You only need commas for THREE or more items. If you only have TWO items connected by 'and,' you do NOT use a comma."

Write:
- TWO items (NO comma): "I like pizza **and** pasta."
- THREE items (commas needed): "I like pizza**,** pasta**,** and salad."

Say: "Two items with 'and' — no comma needed. Three or more items — commas between each one."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "Add commas: 'I need to buy eggs milk and flour.'"

Think aloud: "How many items? Eggs, milk, flour — three items. I need commas after 'eggs' and 'milk,' plus the Oxford comma before 'and': 'I need to buy eggs, milk, and flour.'"

**Example 2:** "Add commas: 'The dog is big friendly and loud.'"

Think aloud: "Three adjectives describing the dog: big, friendly, loud. I need commas: 'The dog is big, friendly, and loud.'"

**Example 3:** "Does this sentence need commas? 'She likes cats and dogs.'"

Think aloud: "How many items? Cats and dogs — only TWO items. No commas needed! The sentence is correct as written."

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice adding commas to sentences and writing sentences with series.

**Activity 1: Add the Commas (5 minutes)**

Say: "These sentences are missing their commas. Add them in the right places."

Write each sentence and let the student add commas:

1. "We saw lions tigers and bears at the zoo."
   → "We saw lions, tigers, and bears at the zoo."

2. "My favorite colors are blue green and purple."
   → "My favorite colors are blue, green, and purple."

3. "She can swim dance and sing."
   → "She can swim, dance, and sing."

4. "Pack your backpack your lunch and your water bottle."
   → "Pack your backpack, your lunch, and your water bottle."

5. "The pizza was hot cheesy and delicious."
   → "The pizza was hot, cheesy, and delicious."

*Facilitator prompt: If the student leaves out the Oxford comma (the comma before "and"), say: "Do not forget the Oxford comma — the comma right before 'and.' We always include it to keep things clear."*

**Activity 2: Sentence Building Game (7 minutes)**

Say: "Now YOU write sentences with commas in a series. I will give you a category, and you write a sentence listing at least three items from that category."

Categories:
1. "Three foods you like" → (e.g., "I like tacos, spaghetti, and ice cream.")
2. "Three things in your room" → (e.g., "My room has a bed, a desk, and a bookshelf.")
3. "Three things you do after school" → (e.g., "After school, I do homework, play outside, and eat a snack.")
4. "Three animals you think are cool" → (e.g., "I think dolphins, eagles, and wolves are cool.")
5. "Three adjectives that describe your best friend" → (e.g., "My best friend is kind, funny, and smart.")

Check each sentence for:
- At least three items in the series
- Commas after each item (except the last)
- Oxford comma before "and"
- Period at the end

*Facilitator prompt: If the student writes "I like tacos and spaghetti and ice cream," say: "When you have three or more items, use commas to separate them instead of 'and' between each one. Use 'and' only before the LAST item."*

## Independent Practice (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices commas in a series independently.

**Part A: Add the Commas (3 minutes)**

Add commas where needed. If no commas are needed, write "correct."

1. "She brought markers crayons and glue to art class."
   (She brought markers, crayons, and glue to art class.)
2. "He likes soccer and basketball."
   (correct — only two items)
3. "The garden had roses tulips daisies and sunflowers."
   (The garden had roses, tulips, daisies, and sunflowers.)
4. "Mom cooked washed the dishes and swept the floor."
   (Mom cooked, washed the dishes, and swept the floor.)
5. "I want a cat and a dog."
   (correct — only two items)

**Part B: Write Sentences With Commas (4 minutes)**

Write a sentence that lists three or more items for each prompt:

6. Three places you would like to visit: _______________________________
7. Three things you see outside: _______________________________
8. Four things you would bring to a desert island: _______________________________

### Difficulty Tiers

- **On-level:** Complete Parts A and B.
- **Stretch:** Complete Parts A and B, then write two more sentences: one with a series of VERBS and one with a series of ADJECTIVES.
- **Challenge:** Write a paragraph (4-5 sentences) about your perfect day. At least two sentences must include a series with commas. Underline each series and circle each comma.

### Interleaved Review

9. **Lesson 4 Review:** Write the comparative form: cold (colder), large (larger), happy (happier).

10. **Lesson 2 Review:** Write the past tense of: go (went), think (thought), give (gave).

11. **Unit 1 Review:** How can you determine the meaning of a word you do not know while reading? (Use context clues — look at the words and sentences around the unknown word for hints about its meaning.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick assessment of comma usage in a series.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Add commas to this sentence: "I need pencils paper and an eraser."
   (I need pencils, paper, and an eraser.)

2. Write a sentence that lists three things you ate today, using commas correctly.

**Mastery = Correct comma placement in item 1 (including Oxford comma) AND a correctly punctuated original sentence with a series.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Practice counting: "How many items are in the list? If three or more, I need commas."
- Use the finger method: hold up one finger for each item in the series. If you hold up three or more fingers, you need commas.
- Write five more sentences with series and practice adding commas with facilitator guidance
- Remind the student: "Comma after EACH item, plus one before 'and.'"

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 7 (Quotation Marks in Dialogue). Preview: "You just mastered commas in a series! Next, we are going to learn how to punctuate DIALOGUE — the words that characters say in a story. You will use quotation marks, commas, and some special rules."

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**

- **Silly Lists:** Write the silliest sentences possible using commas in a series: "For breakfast, I ate a shoe, a cloud, sixteen pickles, and a very confused hamster." The more ridiculous, the better — as long as the commas are correct!

- **Comma Editor:** The facilitator writes five sentences, some with correct commas and some with errors. The student plays "editor" and marks each sentence as correct or incorrect, fixing any errors.

- **Real-World Comma Hunt:** Look through a book, magazine, or website and find five sentences that use commas in a series. Copy them down and count the items in each list.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This is one of the more straightforward grammar lessons in the unit. Most students grasp the concept quickly because lists are intuitive. If the student catches on fast, use the remaining time for extra practice or move to the extension activities. If the student struggles, focus on the counting strategy: "How many items? Three or more? Then you need commas."

**Oxford comma convention:** We teach the Oxford comma (the comma before "and") as the standard convention. This is a style choice — some writing guides omit it. In educational settings, the Oxford comma is widely recommended because it prevents ambiguity. If the student has been taught differently elsewhere, explain: "Different writers make different choices about this comma. In our writing, we always include it."

**Commas in a series vs. other comma uses:** This lesson focuses only on commas in a series. Students may ask about other comma uses (after introductory words, in compound sentences, in addresses, etc.). Acknowledge these briefly: "Yes, commas have many jobs! Today we are focusing on commas in lists. In the next lesson, we will learn about commas in dialogue." Other comma uses are covered in later grades.

**Connection to writing:** Commas in a series appear constantly in writing. Encourage the student to use series in their descriptions: "Instead of writing 'The dog was big. The dog was brown. The dog was friendly,' you can combine it: 'The dog was big, brown, and friendly.' That is more interesting writing!"

**The "and... and... and" habit:** Young writers often connect items with multiple "and"s: "I like cats and dogs and fish and birds." Teach the student to replace the extra "and"s with commas: "I like cats, dogs, fish, and birds." Keep just one "and" — before the last item.
