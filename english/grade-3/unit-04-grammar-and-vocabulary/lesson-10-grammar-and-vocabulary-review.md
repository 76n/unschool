# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 4, Lesson 10

## Topic
Grammar and Vocabulary Cumulative Review

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Demonstrate mastery of all Unit 4 skills: verb tenses, comparative/superlative adjectives, commas in a series, quotation marks in dialogue, dictionary skills, and context clues
- Edit a passage for grammar and punctuation errors
- Write a paragraph using correct grammar, punctuation, and varied vocabulary

## Prerequisites
- Lessons 1-9 (all Unit 4 skills)
- This is a review lesson — all concepts should have been previously taught

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper (and eraser!)
- Colored pencils (for color-coding during editing)
- A dictionary (for vocabulary section)
- The student's notes, flashcards, or reference materials from Lessons 1-9 (optional — for review, not for the final activity)
- Timer (optional)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick-fire review of key concepts from across the unit.

**Activity: Grammar Lightning Round**

Say: "Today is our BIG REVIEW lesson. We are going to go through everything you learned in this unit to make sure you are ready for the assessment. Let's start with a lightning round — answer as fast as you can!"

Ask each question quickly. The student answers orally or writes the answer:

1. "What is the past tense of 'run'?" (ran)
2. "What is the past tense of 'hop'?" (hopped)
3. "What is the superlative of 'big'?" (biggest)
4. "What is the comparative of 'good'?" (better)
5. "What punctuation marks go around spoken words?" (quotation marks)
6. "What comes before 'and' in a list of three items?" (a comma — the Oxford comma)
7. "What does the prefix 'un-' mean?" (not)
8. "Name two things you can find in a dictionary entry." (Any two: spelling, pronunciation, part of speech, definition, example sentence)
9. "What is the future tense of 'eat'?" (will eat)
10. "What does 'fearless' mean?" (without fear; brave)

Say: "Great job! Let's see how well you remember everything when we dig deeper."

## Direct Instruction (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Brief review of each skill area with key reminders before practice.

### Key Vocabulary
This lesson reviews all vocabulary from Lessons 1-9. No new vocabulary is introduced.

### Instruction Notes

Say: "We are going to do a quick review of every skill area in this unit. I will remind you of the key points, and then you will practice."

**Review 1: Verb Tenses (2 minutes)**

Write the quick-reference chart:

| Tense | How to Form It | Example |
|-------|---------------|---------|
| Present | Base verb | I walk. She runs. |
| Past (regular) | Add -ed (watch spelling rules!) | I walked. She hopped. |
| Past (irregular) | Memorize! | I went. She ran. He ate. |
| Future | will + base verb | I will walk. She will run. |

Say: "Remember the three spelling rules for -ed: double the consonant (hopped), drop the silent e (danced), change y to i (carried). And remember the irregular verbs — they do not add -ed."

Quick quiz: "Past tense of 'think'?" (thought) "Past tense of 'smile'?" (smiled) "Future tense of 'go'?" (will go)

**Review 2: Comparative and Superlative Adjectives (2 minutes)**

Write:

| Type | Rule | Example |
|------|------|---------|
| Comparative (-er) | Compare TWO things + "than" | bigger than, taller than |
| Superlative (-est) | Compare THREE or more + "the" | the biggest, the tallest |
| Irregular | Memorize! | good/better/best, bad/worse/worst |
| Long adjectives | more/most instead of -er/-est | more beautiful, most beautiful |

Quick quiz: "Superlative of 'happy'?" (happiest) "Comparative of 'bad'?" (worse) "Do NOT say 'more taller' — why?" (Use only one comparison strategy: -er OR more, not both.)

**Review 3: Commas in a Series (1 minute)**

Say: "Three or more items in a list need commas. Always use the Oxford comma before 'and.' Two items with 'and' do NOT need a comma."

Example: "I like pizza, tacos, and ice cream." (three items — commas)
Example: "I like pizza and tacos." (two items — no comma)

**Review 4: Quotation Marks in Dialogue (2 minutes)**

Write the four rules:
1. Quotation marks around spoken words
2. Comma between spoken words and speaker tag
3. Capitalize the first spoken word
4. New paragraph for each new speaker

Example: "Let's go," said Mom.
Example: Dad asked, "Are you ready?"

**Review 5: Dictionary and Vocabulary Strategies (2 minutes)**

Say: "Four strategies for figuring out word meanings: context clues (first), word parts (second), dictionary (third), glossary (if you are in a textbook)."

Quick review of prefixes and suffixes:
- un- = not, re- = again, pre- = before
- -ful = full of, -less = without, -ly = in a ___ way, -er = one who does

**Review 1 minute:** Ask: "What are the five parts of a dictionary entry?" (Entry word, pronunciation, part of speech, definition, example sentence.)

### Worked Examples

Say: "Now let me show you an editing example. This is similar to what you will do in practice."

Write this paragraph with errors:

> Last summer me and my family goed to the beach. We seed dolphins whales and sea turtles in the ocean. The dolphins was the most beautifulest animals. I thinked they were more faster then the whales. I cant wait to went back next year Mom I yelled.

Say: "Let's find and fix every error together."

Work through it:

1. "goed" → "went" (irregular verb)
2. "seed" → "saw" (irregular verb)
3. "dolphins whales and sea turtles" → "dolphins, whales, and sea turtles" (commas in a series)
4. "the most beautifulest" → "the most beautiful" (do not double — use "most" for long adjectives)
5. "thinked" → "thought" (irregular verb)
6. "more faster" → "faster" (do not double — use -er for short adjectives)
7. "then" → "than" (comparing — use "than")
8. "I cant wait to went" → "I can't wait to go" (future context — base verb with "to")
9. "I yelled" needs quotation marks: "I can't wait to go back next year, Mom!" I yelled.

Corrected:
> Last summer, my family and I went to the beach. We saw dolphins, whales, and sea turtles in the ocean. The dolphins were the most beautiful animals. I thought they were faster than the whales. "I can't wait to go back next year, Mom!" I yelled.

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice all skills in an integrated review with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Skill Stations (7 minutes)**

Work through each "station" together:

**Station 1: Verb Tenses**
Complete the chart:

| Verb | Present | Past | Future |
|------|---------|------|--------|
| eat | eat | _____ | _____ |
| walk | walk | _____ | _____ |
| give | give | _____ | _____ |
| cry | cry | _____ | _____ |
| see | see | _____ | _____ |

(Answers: ate/will eat, walked/will walk, gave/will give, cried/will cry, saw/will see)

**Station 2: Adjectives**
Fill in the comparative and superlative:

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| fast | _____ | _____ |
| good | _____ | _____ |
| pretty | _____ | _____ |
| important | _____ | _____ |

(Answers: faster/fastest, better/best, prettier/prettiest, more important/most important)

**Station 3: Punctuation**
Fix this sentence: "I packed apples crackers and juice for the picnic"
(Answer: "I packed apples, crackers, and juice for the picnic.")

Punctuate this dialogue: Mom said time to go
(Answer: Mom said, "Time to go.")

**Station 4: Vocabulary**
What does "unhelpful" mean? Break it apart.
(un + help + ful = not full of help = not helpful)

Use context clues: "The **fragile** vase broke into a hundred pieces when the cat knocked it off the table."
What does "fragile" mean? (Easily broken, delicate.)

**Activity 2: Error Detective Passage (5 minutes)**

Say: "Here is a passage with EIGHT errors. Find and fix all of them. Use a colored pencil to mark each error."

> Yesterday, my friend and I goed to the park. We bringed our dog our frisbee and a picnic blanket. My friend throwed the frisbee more farther then me. Our dog is the most fastest runner in the whole neighborhood. Watch this said my friend. She throwed it so high that the dog jumps three feet off the ground to catch it.

Errors to find and fix:
1. "goed" → "went"
2. "bringed" → "brought"
3. "our dog our frisbee and a picnic blanket" → "our dog, our frisbee, and a picnic blanket"
4. "throwed" → "threw"
5. "more farther" → "farther"
6. "then" → "than"
7. "most fastest" → "fastest"
8. "Watch this said my friend" → "Watch this," said my friend. (OR: "Watch this!" said my friend.)
9. "jumps" → "jumped" (tense consistency — the passage is in past tense)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student misses an error, give a hint: "Look at the verb in this sentence. Is it past tense? Look at this adjective — did we use the right comparing form?"*

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student demonstrates mastery of all unit skills independently.

**Part A: Mixed Grammar Review (4 minutes)**

1. Write the past tense of: run (ran), make (made), dance (danced), carry (carried)
2. Write the future tense of: eat (will eat), see (will see)
3. Write the comparative of: tall (taller), bad (worse)
4. Write the superlative of: small (smallest), good (best)
5. Add commas: "She speaks English Spanish and French." (She speaks English, Spanish, and French.)
6. Punctuate: *Lets play said Jake* ("Let's play," said Jake.)

**Part B: Edit This Passage (4 minutes)**

Rewrite this passage, fixing all errors (there are at least six):

> My family taked a trip to the mountains last weekend. We hicked up the most steepest trail I ever seed. Mom sayed the view from the top was the most beautifulest thing she had ever saw. We packed sandwiches chips and water for our lunch. It was the goodest trip ever I yelled.

(Corrected: My family took a trip to the mountains last weekend. We hiked up the steepest trail I ever saw. Mom said the view from the top was the most beautiful thing she had ever seen. We packed sandwiches, chips, and water for our lunch. "It was the best trip ever!" I yelled.)

**Part C: Write Using All Skills (4 minutes)**

### Difficulty Tiers

- **On-level:** Write a paragraph (4-5 sentences) about something that happened last weekend. Include at least one past tense irregular verb, one comparative or superlative adjective, and one sentence with a series (commas).
- **Stretch:** Write a paragraph (5-6 sentences) that includes all of the above PLUS at least one line of correctly punctuated dialogue.
- **Challenge:** Write a short story scene (8-10 sentences) that includes: past tense verbs (regular and irregular), a comparative adjective, a superlative adjective, commas in a series, at least two lines of dialogue with correct punctuation, and at least one word with a prefix or suffix. Underline and label each grammar skill used.

### Interleaved Review

7. **Unit 1 Review:** What does it mean to "describe how a character's actions contribute to the events in a story"? (It means to explain how what a character DOES causes things to happen in the story — their choices and actions drive the plot forward.)

8. **Unit 3 Review:** Name three "temporal words" used to show the order of events in a narrative. (First, next, then, after that, later, finally, last — any three.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Final check before the unit assessment.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Write the past tense of "think" and the superlative of "bad." (thought; worst)

2. Add commas and quotation marks: *I brought my pencil my eraser and my notebook said Emma*
   ("I brought my pencil, my eraser, and my notebook," said Emma.)

3. What does "reusable" mean? What strategy did you use? (re + use + able = able to be used again. Word parts.)

**Mastery = All three items answered correctly.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which specific skill areas need more work (verbs? adjectives? punctuation? vocabulary?)
- Create a targeted review focusing only on the weak areas
- Use flashcards or reference charts for the skills that need reinforcement
- Consider taking the unit assessment in two sessions to reduce cognitive load
- Review the relevant lessons before the assessment

### If Mastery Is Met
The student is ready for the Unit Assessment. Say: "You have reviewed everything in this unit and you are doing great! Next time, you will take the Unit 4 Assessment. It will cover all the skills we practiced today. Get a good night's sleep and trust what you know!"

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**

- **Grammar Challenge Story:** Write the most grammatically complex story you can — pack in as many grammar skills as possible (past tense verbs, comparatives, superlatives, commas in series, dialogue with quotation marks, words with prefixes and suffixes). Then count how many different skills you used. Can you get all of them?

- **Teach It Back:** Explain one grammar concept from this unit to a family member, a friend, or a stuffed animal. Teaching something to someone else is the best way to solidify your understanding.

- **Grammar Board Game:** Create a simple board game where each space has a grammar question. Use index cards for question cards: "What is the past tense of 'see'?" "Add commas: 'I like dogs cats and fish.'" "What does 'unhappy' mean?" Play with a family member.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This is a review lesson, not a teaching lesson. Move quickly through the Direct Instruction section — it should feel like a refresher, not a re-teach. Spend the most time on Guided Practice (the Error Detective passage) and Independent Practice (the writing activity). If the student clearly remembers all skills, skip straight to the Error Detective and writing.

**Identifying weak areas:** Use this lesson diagnostically. As the student works through the review, note which skills they handle confidently and which still cause hesitation. If verb tenses are solid but quotation marks are shaky, spend extra time on quotation marks before the assessment.

**The Error Detective approach is powerful:** Finding and fixing errors in someone else's writing is often easier than producing correct writing from scratch. It is also a genuine real-world skill — editing is part of the writing process. If the student enjoys the Error Detective activities, create additional passages with errors for practice.

**Writing as the ultimate assessment:** Part C (writing) is the most important part of this lesson because it shows whether the student can APPLY grammar skills in their own writing — not just identify them in isolated exercises. Encourage the student to write freely, then go back and check their grammar. Draft first, edit second.

**Preparation for the assessment:** After this lesson, the student should feel confident about the upcoming assessment. If they do not, consider: (1) reviewing specific weak areas with targeted practice, (2) allowing the student to keep a grammar reference card during the assessment (a scaffolded approach), or (3) administering the assessment over two sessions to reduce pressure.

**Celebrate progress:** This is the final lesson before the assessment. Take a moment to acknowledge how much the student has learned. At the start of this unit, they may not have known what a comparative adjective was or how to use quotation marks. Now they can do all of it. That is real progress worth celebrating.
