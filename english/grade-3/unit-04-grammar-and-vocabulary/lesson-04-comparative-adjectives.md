# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 4, Lesson 4

## Topic
Comparative Adjectives — Comparing Two Things

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Form comparative adjectives by adding -er, applying the correct spelling rule (doubling, dropping e, changing y to i)
- Use "than" correctly in comparative sentences (taller THAN, faster THAN)
- Write sentences that compare two things using comparative adjectives

## Prerequisites
- Understanding that adjectives are words that describe nouns (big, red, happy)
- Lessons 1-3: Verb tense skills (not directly needed, but builds confidence with grammar patterns)
- Familiarity with spelling rules from Lesson 1 (doubling, dropping e, changing y to i — same rules apply here!)

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Index cards (for comparison game)
- Two objects of different sizes for hands-on comparison (e.g., two pencils of different lengths, two books of different sizes — use whatever is nearby)
- Colored pencils (optional)

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about adjectives and introduce the concept of comparing.

**Activity: Adjective Quick List**

Say: "For the last three lessons, we have been working with VERBS — action words. Today we are switching to a different part of speech: ADJECTIVES. Can you remind me what an adjective is?"

(An adjective is a word that describes a noun — it tells what something is like.)

Say: "Right! Give me five adjectives that describe... this room." (Accept any reasonable adjectives: big, quiet, bright, messy, warm, etc.)

Now hold up two objects (or point to two things in the room) and say: "This book is big. This book is small. But what if I want to COMPARE them? I need a special form of the adjective. This book is BIGGER than that book. Today, we are going to learn how to compare two things using adjectives."

**Lesson 3 Review:** "What are the three verb tenses?" (Present, past, future.) "What word do we put before a verb to make it future tense?" (will)

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach how to form comparative adjectives with -er and the word "than."

### Key Vocabulary
- **Adjective:** A word that describes a noun (tall, fast, happy, cold)
- **Comparative adjective:** A form of an adjective used to compare TWO things (taller, faster, happier, colder)
- **Compare:** To look at two or more things and tell how they are alike or different
- **Than:** The word that connects two things being compared ("taller THAN," "faster THAN")

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: The Basic Rule (3 minutes)**

Say: "When you want to compare two things, you add -er to the end of the adjective. Watch."

Write on paper:
- tall → tall**er** ("A giraffe is taller than a horse.")
- fast → fast**er** ("A cheetah is faster than a dog.")
- cold → cold**er** ("Winter is colder than fall.")
- old → old**er** ("My sister is older than me.")
- small → small**er** ("A mouse is smaller than a cat.")

Say: "See the pattern? We add -er to the adjective. And notice that word 'THAN' — it always shows up in a comparison. Taller THAN. Faster THAN. Colder THAN. The word 'than' is the bridge between the two things you are comparing."

**Step 2: Spelling Rules for -er (5 minutes)**

Say: "Remember the spelling rules we learned for adding -ed to verbs? The same rules apply when we add -er to adjectives! Let me show you."

Write this chart:

**Spelling Rule 1: Doubling the Final Consonant**

| When? | Examples |
|-------|----------|
| The adjective is one syllable, ends in ONE consonant with ONE vowel before it | big → big**g**er, hot → hot**t**er, thin → thin**n**er, sad → sad**d**er, fat → fat**t**er |

Say: "'Big' has one syllable, ends in 'g,' and has one vowel 'i' before it. So we double the 'g' and add -er: bigger. If we did not double it, 'biger' would look weird and we might say it wrong."

**Spelling Rule 2: Dropping the Silent E**

| When? | Examples |
|-------|----------|
| The adjective already ends in a silent e | large → larg**er**, wide → wid**er**, nice → nic**er**, cute → cut**er** |

Say: "'Large' already ends in 'e.' We drop the 'e' and add -er: larger. Not 'largeer.'"

**Spelling Rule 3: Changing Y to I**

| When? | Examples |
|-------|----------|
| The adjective ends in a consonant + y | happy → happ**ier**, funny → funn**ier**, easy → eas**ier**, busy → bus**ier**, pretty → prett**ier** |

Say: "'Happy' ends in a consonant + y. We change the 'y' to 'i' and add -er: happier. Not 'happyer.'"

**Step 3: The "Than" Rule (2 minutes)**

Say: "Every time you use a comparative adjective, you need the word 'than' to show what you are comparing to. Let me show you what happens without 'than.'"

Write:
- WRONG: "A giraffe is taller." (Taller than WHAT? The sentence is incomplete!)
- RIGHT: "A giraffe is taller **than** a horse."

Say: "Without 'than,' the reader does not know what you are comparing. Always finish the comparison!"

**Step 4: What About Longer Adjectives? (2 minutes)**

Say: "There is one more thing. Some adjectives are too long for -er. Instead, we use the word 'more' in front of them."

Write:
- beautiful → **more** beautiful (NOT "beautifuler")
- interesting → **more** interesting (NOT "interestinger")
- important → **more** important (NOT "importanter")
- careful → **more** careful (NOT "carefuler")

Say: "The general rule: if the adjective has ONE or TWO syllables, add -er. If it has THREE or more syllables, use 'more.' We will practice this more in the next lesson."

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "Compare: A cheetah is _____ (fast) _____ a lion."

Think aloud: "'Fast' — one syllable. Does it end in silent e? No. Does it end in consonant + y? No. Is it CVC where I need to double? F-A-S-T... it ends in 'st,' not a single consonant. So I just add -er: faster. And I need 'than' for the comparison. 'A cheetah is faster than a lion.'"

**Example 2:** "Compare: Summer is _____ (hot) _____ spring."

Think aloud: "'Hot' — one syllable, ends in 't,' one vowel 'o' before it. CVC pattern! I need to double the 't': hotter. 'Summer is hotter than spring.'"

**Example 3:** "Compare: This puzzle is _____ (easy) _____ that one."

Think aloud: "'Easy' ends in consonant + y. I change the 'y' to 'i' and add -er: easier. 'This puzzle is easier than that one.'"

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice forming comparative adjectives with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Comparative Form Builder (5 minutes)**

Say: "I will give you an adjective. You tell me the comparative form and which spelling rule you used."

1. tall → (taller — just add -er)
2. big → (bigger — double consonant)
3. nice → (nicer — drop e)
4. happy → (happier — change y to i)
5. long → (longer — just add -er)
6. thin → (thinner — double consonant)
7. wide → (wider — drop e)
8. funny → (funnier — change y to i)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student writes "biger" instead of "bigger," say: "Let's check our spelling rules. Is 'big' a short word? Does it end in one consonant with one vowel before it? B-I-G... yes! So we need to double that 'g' before adding -er."*

**Activity 2: Real-World Comparisons (7 minutes)**

Say: "Now let's compare real things! I will give you two things and an adjective. You write a comparison sentence using the comparative form and 'than.'"

Model the first one: "A mountain and a hill — tall. A mountain is TALLER THAN a hill."

Now the student writes:

1. An elephant and a dog — heavy ("An elephant is heavier than a dog.")
2. A turtle and a rabbit — slow ("A turtle is slower than a rabbit.")
3. Summer and winter — warm ("Summer is warmer than winter.")
4. A lake and a puddle — large ("A lake is larger than a puddle.")
5. A clown and a teacher — funny ("A clown is funnier than a teacher." — or any reasonable answer!)

*Facilitator prompt: Make sure each sentence includes "than." If the student writes "An elephant is heavier," say: "Heavier than WHAT? Add 'than' to finish your comparison."*

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices comparative adjectives independently.

**Part A: Write the Comparative Form (3 minutes)**

Write the comparative (-er) form of each adjective:

1. cold → _______ (colder)
2. hot → _______ (hotter)
3. safe → _______ (safer)
4. pretty → _______ (prettier)
5. fast → _______ (faster)
6. sad → _______ (sadder)
7. brave → _______ (braver)
8. easy → _______ (easier)

**Part B: Complete the Sentence (4 minutes)**

Fill in the blank with the comparative form of the adjective in parentheses:

9. A whale is _______ (big) than a dolphin. (bigger)
10. My new shoes are _______ (clean) than my old ones. (cleaner)
11. Today is _______ (sunny) than yesterday. (sunnier)
12. This chair is _______ (soft) than the bench. (softer)
13. Your dog is _______ (cute) than mine! (cuter)

**Part C: Write Your Own Comparisons (3 minutes)**

### Difficulty Tiers

- **On-level:** Write two sentences comparing things using comparative adjectives and "than."
- **Stretch:** Write four comparison sentences, each using a different spelling rule (just add -er, double consonant, drop e, change y to i).
- **Challenge:** Write a paragraph (4-5 sentences) comparing two animals, two places, or two seasons. Use at least four different comparative adjectives. Underline each comparative adjective and circle each "than."

### Interleaved Review

14. **Lesson 2 Review:** Write the past tense of: go (went), see (saw), make (made), say (said).

15. **Unit 3 Review:** What is the purpose of a "hook" at the beginning of a narrative? (To grab the reader's attention and make them want to keep reading. It can be an action, dialogue, or a surprising question.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick assessment of comparative adjective formation.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Write the comparative form of: big (bigger), nice (nicer), happy (happier).
2. Write a sentence comparing two things using a comparative adjective and "than."

**Mastery = 3 correct comparative forms AND a correct comparison sentence with "than."**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the spelling rules side by side with the verb -ed rules from Lesson 1 — same patterns!
- Practice with physical objects: hold up two things and say the comparison aloud before writing
- Focus on the spelling rule that is causing trouble — create five extra practice items using that rule
- Make a mini reference card: "Short word + -er. Double if CVC. Drop e. Change y to i."

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 5 (Superlative Adjectives). Preview: "You learned to compare TWO things. But what if you want to compare THREE or more? Instead of 'taller,' you use 'tallest.' We will learn that next time!"

---

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**

- **Comparison Riddles:** Write riddles using comparative adjectives. "I am taller than a house but shorter than a mountain. I am greener than a frog. What am I?" (a tree) The student writes three riddles and the facilitator guesses.

- **Superlative Sneak Peek:** Without formal instruction, ask the student: "If 'taller' compares two things, what word would you use to compare three or more? The giraffe is the tall_____ animal." (tallest) This previews Lesson 5.

- **Comparative Adjective Poem:** Write a silly poem using as many comparative adjectives as possible: "My cat is lazier than a rock, / My dog is louder than a clock, / My fish is quieter than a sock..."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson should feel familiar because the spelling rules are the same ones from Lesson 1 (doubling, dropping e, changing y to i). If the student learned those rules well, this lesson will go quickly. If they struggled with the spelling rules in Lesson 1, this is a great opportunity for reinforcement — they are getting the same rules in a new context.

**The "more" rule for longer adjectives:** This lesson briefly introduces "more" for adjectives with three or more syllables (more beautiful, more interesting). The full "more/most" rule is covered more thoroughly in Lesson 5. For now, just make sure the student understands that we do NOT add -er to long words.

**"Double comparison" error:** Students may write "more taller" or "more bigger." This is the most common comparative error. If you see it, say: "I see you used two comparison strategies — -er AND more. We only need one! For short words like 'tall,' we use -er: taller. For long words like 'beautiful,' we use more: more beautiful. Pick one."

**Connection to writing and reading:** When reading, point out comparative adjectives in context: "Look — the author wrote 'The second hill was steeper than the first.' Can you find the comparative adjective?" When the student writes descriptions or comparisons in their own work, encourage them to use the comparative forms they learned today.

**Physical comparisons help:** If the student is struggling with the concept, use physical objects. Pick up two pencils of different lengths: "This pencil is longer than that pencil." Pick up two books of different weights: "This book is heavier than that book." Making comparisons tangible helps cement the concept before moving to writing.
