# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 4 Assessment

## Unit: Grammar & Vocabulary

**Assessment Type:** Summative (end-of-unit)
**Estimated Time:** 40-50 minutes
**Facilitation:** The facilitator reads directions aloud for each section. The student completes written portions independently. For vocabulary items, the student may use a dictionary only for Section D (Dictionary/Glossary Skills). No dictionary for other sections.
**Standards Assessed:** L.3.1d, L.3.1g, L.3.2c, L.3.4d

---

## Assessment Overview

This assessment measures the student's mastery of all skills taught in Unit 4:

| Section | Skill Area | Lessons Assessed | Points |

| **A** | Verb Tenses (regular/irregular past, present, future) | Lessons 1-3 | 6 points |

## Reteaching Guide

### If Section A (Verb Tenses) is below 4/6:

**Regular past tense (Questions 1-3):**
- Return to Lesson 1 and review the three spelling rules (doubling, dropping e, changing y to i)
- Create a set of 10 verb cards and practice forming past tense daily
- Focus on whichever spelling rule is causing errors

**Irregular past tense (Questions 4-5):**
- Return to Lesson 2 and practice with the flashcards
- Focus on the five most common: go/went, see/saw, eat/ate, run/ran, think/thought
- Use irregular verbs in daily conversation: "Where did you go?" "What did you eat?"
- Create a personal "Tricky Verbs" chart to keep during writing

**Tense identification (Question 6):**
- Return to Lesson 3 and review the signal words chart (yesterday = past, today = present, tomorrow = future)
- Practice identifying tenses in sentences from books the student is reading
- Write three sentences a day — one in each tense — and label them

---

### If Section B (Comparative & Superlative Adjectives) is below 3/5:

**Regular forms (Questions 7-8, 10-11):**
- Return to Lessons 4-5 and review the spelling rules (same as verb spelling rules)
- Practice with physical objects: line up three things and say "small, smaller, smallest"
- Create a chart of 10 common adjectives with their comparative and superlative forms

**Irregular forms (Question 9):**
- Focus exclusively on good/better/best and bad/worse/worst
- Post these on a wall chart where the student can see them
- Practice daily: "Is this cookie good, better, or the best?"

**"Double comparison" errors (writing "more taller," "most biggest"):**
- Practice the rule: short words get -er/-est, long words get more/most. NEVER use both.
- Create a sorting game: short adjective cards get -er/-est, long adjective cards get more/most

---

### If Section C (Commas and Quotation Marks) is below 3/5:

**Commas in a series (Questions 12-13):**
- Return to Lesson 6 and practice the counting strategy: three or more items = commas
- Write five list sentences daily and add commas
- Emphasize the Oxford comma

**Quotation marks in dialogue (Questions 14-16):**
- Return to Lesson 7 and review one rule at a time
- Use the color-coding strategy: highlight spoken words in one color, speaker tags in another
- Practice with very simple dialogue first: "Hi," said Sam.
- Look at dialogue in published books and point out each punctuation mark
- If the comma placement (inside quotation marks) is the issue, practice ten short dialogue sentences focusing only on comma placement

---

### If Section D (Dictionary/Glossary Skills) is below 2/3:

- Return to Lesson 8 and practice with a physical dictionary
- Focus on one skill at a time: reading entries first, then guide words
- If alphabetical order is the bottleneck, practice alphabetizing word lists before attempting dictionary lookups
- Practice looking up one "Word of the Day" in the dictionary for a week
- For multiple definitions, practice the "try it in the sentence" strategy with familiar words that have multiple meanings (e.g., bat, run, light)

---

### If Section E (Vocabulary Strategies) is below 2/3:

**Context clues (Question 20):**
- Return to Lesson 9 and practice with sentences where the context is very clear
- Teach the student to ask: "What is happening in the sentence? What do the surrounding words tell me?"
- Practice with one new word per day during reading time

**Word parts (Questions 21-22):**
- Focus on the three most common prefixes first: un- (not), re- (again), pre- (before)
- Practice breaking apart words: cover the prefix/suffix, read the base word, then add the meaning of the prefix/suffix
- Create a prefix/suffix reference card to keep while reading

---

### If Section F (Applied Grammar) is below 5/8:

**Editing (Part 1):**
- Practice the "Error Detective" approach from Lesson 10 with additional passages
- Teach the student to check for one type of error at a time: first read for verb tense errors, then read again for adjective errors, then for comma errors, then for quotation mark errors

**Writing (Part 2):**
- Identify which rubric criterion scored lowest and focus reteaching on that skill
- Have the student write a short paragraph (4-5 sentences) with facilitator support, checking grammar as they go
- Use a writing checklist: "Did I use past tense correctly? Did I include a comparison? Did I use commas in a list? Did I punctuate my dialogue?"
- Draft first, edit second — do not try to get everything perfect on the first pass

**Part 2 — Model mastery-level response:**

> Last summer, my family went on a camping trip in the mountains. We hiked, swam, and roasted marshmallows over a campfire. I saw the biggest waterfall I have ever seen in my life.
