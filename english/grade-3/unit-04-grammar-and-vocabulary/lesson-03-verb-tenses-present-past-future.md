# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 4, Lesson 3

## Topic
Verb Tenses — Present, Past, and Future

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Identify verbs as present tense, past tense, or future tense
- Form all three tenses of regular and irregular verbs
- Maintain consistent verb tense within a piece of writing

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: Regular past tense verbs (-ed with spelling rules)
- Lesson 2: Irregular past tense verbs (go/went, see/saw, etc.)
- Ability to write multi-sentence paragraphs

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Index cards (for tense-sorting game)
- Colored pencils — three colors (for color-coding tenses)
- Irregular verbs flashcards from Lesson 2 (optional review)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review past tense (regular and irregular) and introduce the idea that verbs can show different times.

**Activity: Time Travel Verbs**

Say: "Quick review! Give me the past tense of these verbs — no peeking at your notes."

1. walk → (walked)
2. go → (went)
3. hop → (hopped)
4. eat → (ate)
5. think → (thought)

Say: "Excellent! Those are all PAST tense — they tell about something that already happened. But verbs can also tell about what is happening RIGHT NOW, or what WILL happen in the FUTURE. Today, we are going to learn about all three verb tenses."

Hold up three fingers: "Past — it already happened. Present — it is happening now. Future — it has not happened yet but it will. By the end of today, you will be a verb tense expert!"

**Lesson 2 Review:** "Name three irregular verbs and their past tense forms." (Accept any three from the Lesson 2 list.)

---

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach the three verb tenses and how to form each one, including the concept of tense consistency.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Tense:** The form of a verb that shows WHEN something happens
- **Present tense:** Shows something is happening NOW or happens regularly ("I walk," "She runs")
- **Past tense:** Shows something ALREADY HAPPENED ("I walked," "She ran")
- **Future tense:** Shows something WILL HAPPEN later ("I will walk," "She will run")
- **Consistent:** Staying the same throughout; not changing back and forth

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: The Three Tenses (4 minutes)**

Say: "Every verb can be used in three tenses. Think of tense like a time machine — it tells the reader WHEN the action happens."

Write this chart:

| Tense | When? | Signal Words | Example |
|-------|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Present** | Happening now or happens regularly | today, now, every day, usually, always | I **walk** to school. She **eats** breakfast. |
| **Past** | Already happened | yesterday, last week, last year, ago, once | I **walked** to school. She **ate** breakfast. |
| **Future** | Will happen later | tomorrow, next week, soon, later, will | I **will walk** to school. She **will eat** breakfast. |

Say: "Notice the pattern: present tense is the base verb (walk, eat). Past tense uses -ed for regular verbs or changes for irregular verbs (walked, ate). Future tense uses 'will' before the base verb (will walk, will eat). Future tense is the easiest — you just put 'will' in front!"

**Step 2: Practice With All Three Tenses (4 minutes)**

Say: "Let's practice with a few verbs. I will show you how each verb looks in all three tenses."

Write this chart together:

| Verb | Present | Past | Future |
|------|---------|------|--------|
| play | I play | I played | I will play |
| run | I run | I ran | I will run |
| see | I see | I saw | I will see |
| cook | I cook | I cooked | I will cook |
| make | I make | I made | I will make |
| give | I give | I gave | I will give |

Say: "See how future tense always works the same way? You just add 'will' before the base verb. It does not matter if the verb is regular or irregular — the future tense rule is always the same."

Ask: "What is the future tense of 'think'?" (will think)
Ask: "What is the future tense of 'jump'?" (will jump)
Ask: "What is the future tense of 'go'?" (will go)

Say: "Right! Easy. The tricky part is not forming the tenses — it is keeping them CONSISTENT when you write."

**Step 3: Tense Consistency (4 minutes)**

Say: "When you write a story or a paragraph, you need to pick a tense and STICK WITH IT. If you are telling a story about yesterday, all your verbs should be in the past tense. Watch what happens when a writer mixes tenses."

Write this paragraph:

> **Mixed-up tenses (WRONG):** "Yesterday, I walked to the park. I **see** my friend Marco. We **play** on the swings. Then we went home."

Say: "Can you spot the problem? The story starts in the past tense — 'walked.' But then it switches to present tense — 'see,' 'play.' Then it switches back to past tense — 'went.' This is confusing for the reader! Let's fix it."

Write the corrected version:

> **Consistent tense (RIGHT):** "Yesterday, I walked to the park. I **saw** my friend Marco. We **played** on the swings. Then we went home."

Say: "Now all the verbs are past tense. The story flows smoothly. When you write, ask yourself: 'Am I telling about the past, the present, or the future?' Then make sure ALL your verbs match."

Write one more example showing a present-tense paragraph:

> **Present tense paragraph:** "Every morning, I walk to school. I see my friends at the gate. We talk about our favorite shows. Then we go to class."

Say: "This paragraph is about what happens EVERY morning — so it uses present tense for all the verbs. Consistent!"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "What tense is this sentence? 'The bird flew over the trees.'"

Think aloud: "'Flew' — that is the past tense of 'fly.' And 'over the trees' already happened. This is PAST TENSE."

**Example 2:** "Change this sentence to future tense: 'I eat pizza for dinner.'"

Think aloud: "Present tense is 'I eat.' To make it future, I add 'will' before the base verb: 'I will eat pizza for dinner.' That tells us it has not happened yet but it WILL happen."

**Example 3:** "Find and fix the tense error: 'Tomorrow, I will go to the zoo. I see the lions and I will feed the elephants.'"

Think aloud: "The paragraph is about tomorrow — future tense. 'Will go' and 'will feed' are future tense. But 'see' is present tense! It should be 'will see.' Fixed: 'Tomorrow, I will go to the zoo. I will see the lions and I will feed the elephants.'"

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice identifying and using all three verb tenses with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Tense Sort (5 minutes)**

Write these sentences on index cards or paper strips. The student sorts each one into three piles: PAST, PRESENT, or FUTURE.

1. "She walked to the library." (PAST)
2. "I will bake cookies tomorrow." (FUTURE)
3. "He runs every morning." (PRESENT)
4. "They ate lunch at noon." (PAST)
5. "We will visit Grandma next week." (FUTURE)
6. "The cat sleeps on the couch." (PRESENT)
7. "I thought about the problem all night." (PAST)
8. "She will read her book later." (FUTURE)
9. "They play soccer after school." (PRESENT)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student is unsure, ask: "When is this happening — right now, already done, or not yet?" Also look for signal words like "yesterday," "every," "tomorrow," and "will."*

**Activity 2: Three-Tense Sentences (7 minutes)**

Say: "I will give you a verb. You write three sentences — one in each tense."

Model the first one together:

**Verb: jump**
- Present: The frog jumps into the pond.
- Past: The frog jumped into the pond.
- Future: The frog will jump into the pond.

Now the student does these independently with support:

**Verb: eat**
- Present: ___ (e.g., I eat an apple for a snack.)
- Past: ___ (e.g., I ate an apple for a snack.)
- Future: ___ (e.g., I will eat an apple for a snack.)

**Verb: give**
- Present: ___ (e.g., She gives her friend a gift.)
- Past: ___ (e.g., She gave her friend a gift.)
- Future: ___ (e.g., She will give her friend a gift.)

**Verb: walk**
- Present: ___ (e.g., We walk to school together.)
- Past: ___ (e.g., We walked to school together.)
- Future: ___ (e.g., We will walk to school together.)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student writes "I eated" for the past tense of "eat," say: "Remember our irregular verbs from yesterday! 'Eat' is a tricky one. What did it change to?" (ate)*

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student identifies and uses verb tenses independently and practices tense consistency.

**Part A: Identify the Tense (3 minutes)**

Write the tense of the underlined verb: PRESENT, PAST, or FUTURE.

1. She **ran** to the finish line. (PAST)
2. I **will clean** my room after lunch. (FUTURE)
3. He **reads** a book every night. (PRESENT)
4. They **went** to the museum yesterday. (PAST)
5. We **will play** outside tomorrow. (FUTURE)

**Part B: Change the Tense (4 minutes)**

Rewrite each sentence in the tense shown in parentheses:

6. "I walk to school." → (past tense) (I walked to school.)
7. "She ate a sandwich." → (future tense) (She will eat a sandwich.)
8. "He will see the movie." → (present tense) (He sees the movie.)
9. "They play in the park." → (past tense) (They played in the park.)
10. "We made a cake." → (future tense) (We will make a cake.)

**Part C: Fix the Tense Errors (3 minutes)**

Each paragraph has tense errors. Find and fix them. All verbs should be in the same tense.

11. (All past tense): "Last Saturday, we go to the beach. Mom packed a picnic. We swim in the ocean and build a sandcastle. Then we drove home."

(Fixed: "Last Saturday, we **went** to the beach. Mom packed a picnic. We **swam** in the ocean and **built** a sandcastle. Then we drove home.")

12. (All future tense): "Next week, I will start a new book. I will read a chapter every night. I finished it by Friday. Then I will pick a new one."

(Fixed: "Next week, I will start a new book. I will read a chapter every night. I **will finish** it by Friday. Then I will pick a new one.")

### Difficulty Tiers

- **On-level:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
- **Stretch:** Complete all parts, then write a 4-sentence paragraph about what you did last weekend (all past tense). Then rewrite the same paragraph as if it will happen NEXT weekend (all future tense).
- **Challenge:** Write three paragraphs about the same event — one in past tense, one in present tense, and one in future tense. For example, describe eating breakfast in all three tenses. Each paragraph should be 3-4 sentences.

### Interleaved Review

13. **Lesson 1 Review:** Write the past tense of: dance (danced), stop (stopped), cry (cried). Which spelling rule did you use for each? (Drop e; double consonant; change y to i.)

14. **Unit 1 Review:** When a question asks you to "refer to the text," what should you do? (Find specific words, sentences, or details in the text that support your answer.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick assessment of verb tense identification and formation.

**Exit Ticket:**

1. Identify the tense: "She will visit her aunt next month." (FUTURE)
2. Change to past tense: "I think about the answer." (I thought about the answer.)
3. Find and fix the tense error: "Yesterday, I walked to school and see a rainbow." (see → saw)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Review the tense chart with signal words (yesterday = past, today/every day = present, tomorrow/will = future)
- Practice identifying the tense of sentences from a book the student is reading
- Focus specifically on whichever tense is causing difficulty
- Use the "time travel" metaphor: "Where is the time machine going — past, present, or future?"

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 4 (Comparative Adjectives). Preview: "You are now a verb tense master! Next, we are going to switch from verbs to ADJECTIVES — describing words. We will learn how to compare things: taller, faster, bigger."

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**

- **Time Travel Story:** Write a short story where a character has a time machine. In paragraph 1, they travel to the past (use past tense). In paragraph 2, they come back to the present (use present tense). In paragraph 3, they travel to the future (use future tense). This is a creative and challenging way to practice all three tenses.

- **Tense Detective:** Take any paragraph from a book or story. Use three different colored pencils to underline present tense verbs in one color, past tense verbs in another, and future tense verbs in a third. Are the tenses consistent? If the author switches tenses, can the student explain why?

- **Verb Tense Chart Expansion:** Expand the verb chart from Direct Instruction to include more verbs. How many present-past-future sets can the student complete from memory?

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson ties together Lessons 1 and 2. If the student is still shaky on irregular past tense forms from Lesson 2, spend the first few minutes reviewing with flashcards before introducing the three-tense framework. The future tense is the easiest to learn (just add "will"), so it should go quickly.

**Present tense complications:** In everyday speech, we often use the present progressive ("I am walking") rather than the simple present ("I walk"). For this lesson, focus on simple present tense. The present progressive (am/is/are + -ing) is a Grade 4 concept. If the student asks about "I am walking" vs. "I walk," acknowledge it briefly: "Both are present tense. 'I am walking' means right this second. 'I walk' means I do it regularly. For now, we are focusing on the simple forms."

**Tense consistency is a writing skill:** Identifying tenses is easier than maintaining consistency in one's own writing. The real payoff of this lesson comes when the student writes stories and paragraphs. In future writing sessions (Lesson 10 and beyond), use the "verb check" strategy: after writing a paragraph, go back and underline every verb. Check that they all match the intended tense. This habit builds over time.

**Common tense-switching patterns:** Students often start a story in past tense and then shift to present tense when they get excited about the action. This is natural in oral storytelling. When you notice it in writing, do not interrupt the creative flow — let the student finish the draft, then go back and fix the tenses during editing.

**"Will" vs. "going to":** Students may use "going to" for future tense ("I am going to eat lunch"). This is correct and natural. For this lesson, teach "will" as the standard future tense marker because it is simpler and more consistent. Acknowledge "going to" as another way to express future tense if the student brings it up.
