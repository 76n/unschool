# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 4, Lesson 1

## Topic
Forming the Past Tense of Regular Verbs

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Form the past tense of regular verbs by adding -ed, applying the correct spelling rule (base rule, doubling, dropping silent e, changing y to i)
- Identify the three sounds of -ed (/t/, /d/, /id/)
- Use regular past tense verbs correctly in sentences

## Prerequisites
- Ability to identify verbs (action words) in simple sentences
- Understanding that verbs can tell about things that already happened
- Basic spelling skills including recognition of consonants and vowels

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Index cards (for verb sorting game)
- Colored pencils (optional — for color-coding -ed endings)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge about verbs and the concept of past tense.

**Activity: Verb Brainstorm**

Say: "Today we are starting a new unit all about grammar and vocabulary — the tools that make your reading and writing stronger. We are going to begin with VERBS. Can you remind me what a verb is?"

(A verb is an action word — it tells what someone does.)

Say: "Great! Let's brainstorm. I'm going to give you one minute. Tell me as many verbs as you can think of — action words. Go!"

Write down the student's verbs as they say them. Aim for 8-10 verbs. If the student gets stuck, prompt: "What did you do this morning? What did you eat? What do you do at recess?"

After the brainstorm, say: "Wonderful list! Now, all of these verbs tell what someone DOES right now. But what if I want to talk about something that already happened — yesterday, or last week? I need to change the verb to show that it happened in the PAST. That is what we are going to learn today."

**Unit 1 Review:** "What does it mean to 'refer to the text' when answering a question about a story?" (It means to point to specific words or sentences in the text to support your answer.)

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach how to form the past tense of regular verbs with -ed, including spelling rules and the three sounds of -ed.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Verb:** A word that shows an action (run, eat, play) or a state of being (is, am, was)
- **Past tense:** The form of a verb that shows something already happened
- **Regular verb:** A verb that forms the past tense by adding -ed
- **Consonant:** Any letter that is not a vowel (b, c, d, f, g, h, j, k, l, m, n, p, q, r, s, t, v, w, x, y, z)
- **Vowel:** The letters a, e, i, o, u

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: The Basic Rule (2 minutes)**

Say: "Most verbs form the past tense by adding -ed to the end. Watch."

Write on paper:
- play → play**ed** ("Yesterday, I played outside.")
- walk → walk**ed** ("She walked to the store.")
- jump → jump**ed** ("The frog jumped into the pond.")
- rain → rain**ed** ("It rained all day.")

Say: "See? We just add -ed. Easy! But here is where it gets interesting — there are three SPECIAL spelling rules for certain words."

**Step 2: Three Spelling Rules (6 minutes)**

Say: "Not every verb is as simple as 'play + ed.' Some verbs need a small spelling change before we add -ed. Let me show you the three rules."

Write this chart:

**Spelling Rule 1: Doubling the Final Consonant**

| Rule | When to Use It | Examples |
|------|---------------|----------|
| Double the last consonant, then add -ed | When the verb is one syllable, ends in ONE consonant, and has ONE vowel before that consonant (CVC pattern) | hop → hop**p**ed, stop → stop**p**ed, plan → plan**n**ed, clap → clap**p**ed |

Say: "Look at 'hop.' It is one short syllable. It ends in the consonant 'p.' Before the 'p,' there is one vowel — 'o.' So we DOUBLE the 'p' and then add -ed: hopped. If we did not double it, we would get 'hoped' — and that is a completely different word!"

**Spelling Rule 2: Dropping the Silent E**

| Rule | When to Use It | Examples |
|------|---------------|----------|
| Drop the final silent e, then add -ed | When the verb already ends in a silent e | bake → bak**ed**, smile → smil**ed**, dance → danc**ed**, move → mov**ed** |

Say: "The word 'bake' already ends in the letter 'e.' If we just stuck -ed on the end, we would get 'bakeed' — that looks weird! So we drop the 'e' first, then add -ed: baked."

**Spelling Rule 3: Changing Y to I**

| Rule | When to Use It | Examples |
|------|---------------|----------|
| Change the y to i, then add -ed | When the verb ends in a consonant + y | carry → carr**ied**, hurry → hurr**ied**, worry → worr**ied**, try → tr**ied** |

Say: "When a verb ends in a consonant followed by 'y,' we change the 'y' to 'i' before adding -ed. So 'carry' becomes 'carried.' But be careful — if there is a VOWEL before the 'y,' like in 'play' (a + y), we just add -ed normally: played."

**Step 3: The Three Sounds of -ed (4 minutes)**

Say: "Here is a cool secret about -ed. Even though it is always SPELLED the same way, it actually makes THREE different sounds. Listen carefully."

Write:

| Sound of -ed | When It Happens | Examples |
|-------------|----------------|----------|
| /t/ (sounds like "t") | After voiceless consonant sounds: k, p, s, sh, ch, f | walked (walk-t), jumped (jump-t), washed (wash-t), laughed (laugh-t) |
| /d/ (sounds like "d") | After voiced consonant sounds and vowel sounds: b, g, l, m, n, r, v, z, and all vowels | played (play-d), rained (rain-d), called (call-d), hugged (hug-d) |
| /id/ (sounds like "id") | After t or d sounds | wanted (want-id), needed (need-id), landed (land-id), painted (paint-id) |

Say each word slowly and have the student listen for the ending sound:
- "Walk-t. Jumped. Do you hear the /t/ sound at the end?"
- "Play-d. Rained. Do you hear the /d/ sound?"
- "Want-id. Need-id. Do you hear that extra syllable — /id/?"

Say: "You do NOT need to memorize which sound goes with which — your mouth will figure it out naturally when you say the word. But it is cool to know that -ed has three sounds, right?"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "Let's change this verb to past tense together: 'The dog _____ (bark) at the squirrel.'"

Think aloud: "The verb is 'bark.' Does it end in silent e? No. Does it end in consonant + y? No. Is it a short CVC word where I need to double? B-A-R-K... it ends in 'rk,' not a single consonant after a single vowel. So I just add -ed: barked. 'The dog barked at the squirrel.' And if I say it out loud — 'bark-t' — the -ed makes the /t/ sound."

**Example 2:** "Now try: 'She _____ (dance) on the stage.'"

Think aloud: "The verb is 'dance.' It ends in a silent e! So I need to drop the e first, then add -ed: danced. 'She danced on the stage.' And the sound? 'Danc-t' — that is the /t/ sound."

**Example 3:** "One more: 'He _____ (stop) at the corner.'"

Think aloud: "The verb is 'stop.' Is it one syllable? Yes. Does it end in one consonant? Yes — 'p.' Is there one vowel before the consonant? S-T-O-P... the 'o' is one vowel. So I double the 'p' and add -ed: stopped. 'He stopped at the corner.' The sound? 'Stop-t' — /t/ again!"

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice forming past tense verbs with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Spelling Rule Sort (6 minutes)**

Write these verbs on index cards (or on paper): walk, hop, smile, cry, talk, drop, wave, hurry, cook, skip, like, dry

Say: "Let's sort these verbs into groups based on which spelling rule we use when we add -ed."

Create four columns:
- **Just add -ed** (no change needed)
- **Double the consonant + ed**
- **Drop the silent e + d**
- **Change y to i + ed**

Work through the first two together, then let the student try the rest with your support.

**Answers:**
- Just add -ed: walk → walked, talk → talked, cook → cooked
- Double + ed: hop → hopped, drop → dropped, skip → skipped
- Drop e + d: smile → smiled, wave → waved, like → liked
- Change y to i + ed: cry → cried, hurry → hurried, dry → dried

*Facilitator prompt: If the student puts "hop" in the "just add -ed" column, say: "Let's check. Is 'hop' a short one-syllable word? Does it end in one consonant with one vowel before it? H-O-P... yes! So we need to double that 'p.' If we just wrote 'hoped,' that would be a different word — it would say 'hoped' like 'I hoped for a pony.' We want 'hopped' like 'The bunny hopped.'"*

**Activity 2: Past Tense Sentence Building (6 minutes)**

Say: "Now let's use past tense verbs in sentences. I'll give you a present tense sentence, and you change it to past tense."

1. "The kids play in the yard." → "The kids _____ in the yard." (played)
2. "Maria tries to catch the ball." → "Maria _____ to catch the ball." (tried)
3. "The baby claps her hands." → "The baby _____ her hands." (clapped)
4. "We bake cookies after school." → "We _____ cookies after school." (baked)
5. "The cat chases the mouse." → "The cat _____ the mouse." (chased)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student writes "chaseed" or "bakeed," say: "Hmm, 'chase' already ends in an 'e.' What is our rule for words that end in silent e? We drop it first, then add -ed. So chase becomes chased."*

After each answer, ask: "What sound does the -ed make in that word? /t/, /d/, or /id/?"

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices forming and using regular past tense verbs independently.

**Part A: Form the Past Tense (4 minutes)**

Write the past tense of each verb:

1. jump → _______ (jumped)
2. carry → _______ (carried)
3. hope → _______ (hoped)
4. rain → _______ (rained)
5. plan → _______ (planned)
6. race → _______ (raced)
7. worry → _______ (worried)
8. grab → _______ (grabbed)

**Part B: Fill in the Blank (4 minutes)**

Use the correct past tense form of the verb in parentheses:

9. Yesterday, we _______ (walk) to the library. (walked)
10. The baby _______ (cry) all night. (cried)
11. She _______ (smile) at the funny joke. (smiled)
12. He _______ (trip) over the rock and fell. (tripped)
13. We _______ (study) for the spelling test last week. (studied)

### Difficulty Tiers

- **On-level:** Complete Parts A and B.
- **Stretch:** Complete Parts A and B, then write three original sentences using past tense verbs — one using the doubling rule, one using the drop-e rule, and one using the change-y-to-i rule.
- **Challenge:** Complete Parts A and B, then write a short paragraph (4-5 sentences) about something that happened yesterday, using at least five different past tense verbs. Underline each past tense verb.

### Interleaved Review

14. **Unit 1 Review (Reading):** What is the difference between a character's actions and a character's feelings? Give an example. (Actions are what a character DOES — like running, hiding, or laughing. Feelings are what a character FEELS — like happy, scared, or excited. Sometimes actions show feelings: "She jumped up and down" shows she is excited.)

15. **Unit 3 Review (Writing):** What are three things a good narrative includes? (Characters, setting, events told in order, details, and a satisfying ending. Accept any three.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick assessment of regular past tense formation.

**Exit Ticket:**

Write the past tense of each verb:

1. dance → _______ (danced)
2. stop → _______ (stopped)
3. hurry → _______ (hurried)

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct, with correct spelling.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Identify which spelling rule is causing trouble (doubling, dropping e, or changing y to i)
- Create a mini reference card with the three rules and examples
- Practice 5 more verbs using that specific rule before moving on
- Have the student say the rule out loud before applying it: "The word ends in silent e, so I drop the e and add -ed."

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 2 (Irregular Verbs). Preview: "Great work! You just learned how MOST verbs form the past tense. But guess what? Some verbs are rule-breakers! They do NOT add -ed at all. Tomorrow we will learn these tricky verbs."

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**

- **Three Sounds Hunt:** Read a paragraph aloud (any book or passage). The student listens for past tense verbs ending in -ed and sorts them by sound: /t/, /d/, or /id/. This trains the ear and reinforces the concept.

- **Silly Sentence Challenge:** Write five silly sentences using past tense verbs with all three spelling rules. Example: "The frog hopped onto the cake, smiled at the queen, and hurried away before dinner." The sillier, the better!

- **Verb Spelling Rule Poster:** Create a colorful poster showing all three spelling rules with examples. Use one color for the doubling rule, another for the drop-e rule, and a third for the change-y rule. Keep this poster as a reference for future writing.

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson introduces quite a few spelling rules. If the student seems overwhelmed, focus first on the basic rule (just add -ed) and the concept that -ed = past tense. Then introduce the three special rules one at a time in subsequent sessions. The three sounds of -ed are interesting but not essential for spelling — if the student finds them confusing, skip that section and focus on the spelling rules.

**Why spelling rules matter here:** The spelling rules are not arbitrary — they protect pronunciation. Doubling the consonant in "hopped" prevents it from being read as "hoped." Dropping the silent e prevents the awkward "danceed." These are functional rules that help the student spell correctly AND read correctly.

**Connection to writing:** Throughout the rest of this unit and in future writing, remind the student to use past tense when telling stories about things that happened. The narrative writing skills from Unit 3 combine naturally with the verb tense skills from this lesson. If the student writes a story with present tense verbs ("The dog runs to the park"), gently ask: "Is this happening right now, or did it already happen? If it already happened, what do we need to change?"

**Common errors to watch for:**
- "Stoped" instead of "stopped" (forgot to double)
- "Danceed" instead of "danced" (forgot to drop the e)
- "Plaied" instead of "played" (over-applied the y-to-i rule — "play" has a vowel before the y, so it just adds -ed)
- "Cryed" instead of "cried" (forgot to change y to i)

**Dialect note:** Some students may use forms like "I walk to school yesterday" without the -ed ending. This is a feature of certain English dialects and is not incorrect speech. In these materials, we are teaching standard written English conventions. Frame it as: "In our writing, we show past tense by adding -ed to the verb."
