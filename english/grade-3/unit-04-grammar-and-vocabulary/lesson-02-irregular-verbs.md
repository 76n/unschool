# English Language Arts — Grade 3 — Unit 4, Lesson 2

## Topic
Common Irregular Past Tense Verbs

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- Recognize that irregular verbs do NOT follow the -ed rule and must be memorized
- Correctly use at least 10 common irregular past tense verb forms in sentences
- Distinguish between regular and irregular verbs when forming past tense

## Prerequisites
- Lesson 1: Regular past tense verbs (-ed with spelling rules)
- Ability to identify verbs in sentences
- Understanding of what "past tense" means (something that already happened)

## Materials Needed
- Pencil and paper
- Index cards (for flashcards and matching game — 24 cards needed)
- Colored pencils (optional)
- Timer (optional, for timed challenge)

---

## Warm-Up (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review regular past tense from Lesson 1 and introduce the idea that some verbs are "rule-breakers."

**Activity: Regular Past Tense Quick Check**

Say: "Before we start today's lesson, let's review what we learned last time. Give me the past tense of these verbs."

Write each verb; the student writes the past tense:

1. walk → _______ (walked)
2. hop → _______ (hopped)
3. smile → _______ (smiled)
4. carry → _______ (carried)

Say: "Perfect! You remembered the -ed rules. Now, I want you to try something. What is the past tense of 'go'?"

Pause and let the student think. They may say "goed." If they do, say: "That is a great guess! You used the -ed rule. But listen to this: 'Yesterday, I goed to the store.' Does that sound right?"

(No — it sounds weird.)

Say: "Right! 'Go' does not become 'goed.' It becomes 'went.' Yesterday, I WENT to the store. 'Go' is an IRREGULAR verb — it does not follow the -ed rule. Today, we are going to learn a bunch of these tricky verbs."

**Lesson 1 Review:** "What are the three spelling rules for adding -ed?" (1. Double the consonant, 2. Drop the silent e, 3. Change y to i.)

---

## Direct Instruction (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Teach common irregular past tense verbs through explicit instruction and pattern-finding.

### Key Vocabulary
- **Irregular verb:** A verb that does NOT form the past tense by adding -ed — the whole word changes
- **Memorize:** To learn something so well that you can remember it without looking

### Instruction Notes

**Step 1: The Big Idea (2 minutes)**

Say: "Regular verbs follow a pattern — you add -ed. Irregular verbs do not have a pattern. The word changes, and you just have to memorize what it changes to. The good news is that you already KNOW most of these because you use them when you talk. You say 'I went to the park,' not 'I goed to the park.' You just need to learn to recognize them and spell them correctly in your writing."

**Step 2: The Irregular Verbs List (7 minutes)**

Say: "Here are the most common irregular verbs that third graders need to know. I am going to teach them to you in groups so they are easier to remember."

Write this chart, working through each group together:

**Group 1: Verbs That Change Their Vowel Sound**

| Present | Past | Memory Tip |
|---------|------|-----------|
| run | **ran** | u → a |
| come | **came** | The vowel changes |
| give | **gave** | The vowel changes |
| eat | **ate** | eat → ate (the "eat" is hiding inside "ate"!) |

Say: "Notice how the vowel in the middle changes? Run becomes ran — the 'u' changes to 'a.' Come becomes came. Give becomes gave. These are all short words where the vowel shifts."

**Group 2: Verbs That Change Completely**

| Present | Past | Memory Tip |
|---------|------|-----------|
| go | **went** | Totally different word! Just memorize it. |
| see | **saw** | A whole new word |
| take | **took** | Take/took — they both start with 't' |
| make | **made** | Make/made — they both start with 'm' |

Say: "These verbs change so much that the past tense looks like a completely different word. 'Go' and 'went' do not look alike at all! These are the trickiest ones."

**Group 3: Verbs With Unusual Changes**

| Present | Past | Memory Tip |
|---------|------|-----------|
| say | **said** | Spelled S-A-I-D, not "sayed" |
| think | **thought** | Spelled T-H-O-U-G-H-T — a long word! |

Say: "Say' becomes 'said' — you have probably seen this word in books many times: 'she said,' 'he said.' And 'think' becomes 'thought' — that is a long word but a very important one."

**The Complete List — All Together:**

| Present (Now) | Past (Already Happened) |
|--------------|------------------------|
| go | went |
| see | saw |
| run | ran |
| eat | ate |
| give | gave |
| take | took |
| come | came |
| make | made |
| say | said |
| think | thought |

Say: "That is ten irregular verbs. You already know most of them from talking. Now you need to recognize them and spell them correctly when you write."

**Step 3: Regular vs. Irregular — How to Tell (3 minutes)**

Say: "So how do you know if a verb is regular or irregular? Unfortunately, there is no rule that tells you. You just have to learn which ones are irregular. But here is a trick: if you try adding -ed and it sounds WRONG, the verb is probably irregular."

Demonstrate: "Let's test some verbs."
- "Walked. Does 'walked' sound right? YES — it is regular."
- "Goed. Does 'goed' sound right? NO — 'go' is irregular. The past tense is 'went.'"
- "Jumped. Does 'jumped' sound right? YES — regular."
- "Eated. Does 'eated' sound right? NO — 'eat' is irregular. The past tense is 'ate.'"

### Worked Examples

**Example 1:** "Fill in the blank: 'Last night, Mom _____ (make) spaghetti for dinner.'"

Think aloud: "The verb is 'make.' Can I add -ed? 'Maked'? That does not sound right. 'Make' is irregular — it becomes 'made.' Last night, Mom made spaghetti for dinner."

**Example 2:** "Fill in the blank: 'We _____ (see) a rainbow after the storm.'"

Think aloud: "The verb is 'see.' Can I add -ed? 'Seed'? No, 'seed' is a thing you plant! 'See' is irregular — it becomes 'saw.' We saw a rainbow after the storm."

**Example 3:** "Fill in the blank: 'I _____ (think) about the answer for a long time.'"

Think aloud: "'Think' plus -ed would be 'thinked.' That sounds wrong. 'Think' is irregular — it becomes 'thought.' I thought about the answer for a long time. And notice — 'thought' is spelled T-H-O-U-G-H-T. That is a tricky spelling."

## Guided Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice irregular past tense verbs with facilitator support.

**Activity 1: Flashcard Making (5 minutes)**

Say: "Let's make flashcards! These will help you memorize the irregular verbs."

Give the student 12 index cards (or have them fold paper into 12 sections). On each card:
- Front: the present tense verb
- Back: the past tense verb

Cards to make: go/went, see/saw, run/ran, eat/ate, give/gave, take/took, come/came, make/made, say/said, think/thought

After making the cards, quiz the student: hold up the present tense side, and the student says the past tense. Go through all ten. Then shuffle and go again.

*Facilitator prompt: If the student says "goed" or "eated," do not say "wrong." Instead say: "I hear you using the -ed rule — that is smart! But remember, 'go' is a tricky one. It becomes... do you remember?" Give the student a moment to recall before providing the answer.*

**Activity 2: Regular or Irregular? (7 minutes)**

Say: "I am going to say a verb. You tell me: is it REGULAR (add -ed) or IRREGULAR (it changes)? Then tell me the past tense."

1. play → (regular — played)
2. go → (irregular — went)
3. jump → (regular — jumped)
4. eat → (irregular — ate)
5. smile → (regular — smiled)
6. run → (irregular — ran)
7. walk → (regular — walked)
8. think → (irregular — thought)
9. carry → (regular — carried)
10. see → (irregular — saw)
11. cook → (regular — cooked)
12. take → (irregular — took)

*Facilitator prompt: If the student is unsure whether a verb is regular or irregular, say: "Try adding -ed. Does it sound right? If 'played' sounds right, it is regular. If 'goed' sounds wrong, it is irregular."*

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student practices irregular past tense verbs independently.

**Part A: Write the Past Tense (3 minutes)**

Write the past tense of each irregular verb:

1. go → _______ (went)
2. see → _______ (saw)
3. eat → _______ (ate)
4. give → _______ (gave)
5. think → _______ (thought)
6. come → _______ (came)
7. make → _______ (made)
8. say → _______ (said)
9. run → _______ (ran)
10. take → _______ (took)

**Part B: Fill in the Blank — Choose the Correct Past Tense (4 minutes)**

Choose the correct past tense form of the verb in parentheses:

11. She _______ (run) all the way home. (ran)
12. They _______ (go) to the beach last summer. (went)
13. "I want pizza," he _______ (say). (said)
14. We _______ (eat) lunch at noon. (ate)
15. Grandma _______ (give) me a book for my birthday. (gave)
16. He _______ (take) his dog for a walk. (took)

**Part C: Error Detective (3 minutes)**

Each sentence has a verb error. Find the mistake and fix it:

17. Yesterday, I goed to my friend's house. (goed → went)
18. She eated all her vegetables at dinner. (eated → ate)
19. We seed a deer in the backyard last night. (seed → saw)
20. He runned faster than everyone else. (runned → ran)

### Difficulty Tiers

- **On-level:** Complete Parts A, B, and C.
- **Stretch:** Complete all parts, then write four original sentences — each using a different irregular past tense verb from the list.
- **Challenge:** Complete all parts, then write a paragraph (5-6 sentences) about a trip or event that happened in the past, using at least six different irregular past tense verbs. Underline each irregular verb.

### Interleaved Review

21. **Lesson 1 Review:** Write the past tense of these regular verbs: hop (hopped), dance (danced), worry (worried).

22. **Unit 2 Review:** What is a "text feature" in an informational text? Name two examples. (Text features help readers find information. Examples: headings, bold words, diagrams, maps, captions, table of contents, glossary.)

## Check for Understanding (3 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick assessment of irregular past tense verb knowledge.

**Exit Ticket:**

Write the past tense of each verb:

1. go → _______ (went)
2. eat → _______ (ate)
3. think → _______ (thought)

Use one of these irregular past tense verbs in a sentence:

4. ________________________________________________________

**Mastery = 3 out of 3 correct verb forms AND a correct sentence using an irregular past tense verb.**

### If Mastery Is Not Met
- Focus on the five most common irregular verbs first: go/went, see/saw, eat/ate, run/ran, say/said
- Practice with the flashcards daily for 3-5 minutes
- Use the verbs in conversation throughout the day: "What did you eat for lunch?" "Where did you go after school?"
- Consider a second session on this lesson before moving to Lesson 3
- Create a personal "Tricky Verbs" chart that the student can keep and reference during writing

### If Mastery Is Met
Move to Lesson 3 (Verb Tenses — Present, Past, Future). Preview: "You now know regular AND irregular past tense verbs. Next time, we will look at ALL three verb tenses — present, past, and future — and learn how to keep them straight when you write."

## Extension (Optional)
**For students who demonstrate quick mastery:**

- **More Irregular Verbs:** Teach additional irregular verbs beyond the core ten: write/wrote, read/read (same spelling, different pronunciation!), fly/flew, sing/sang, swim/swam, know/knew, grow/grew, bring/brought, catch/caught, teach/taught. These are bonus verbs — the student does not need to memorize all of them now.

- **Irregular Verb Memory Game:** Use the flashcards face-down. Turn over two cards at a time. If they are a matching present/past pair (e.g., "go" and "went"), keep them. If not, turn them back over. Play until all pairs are matched.

- **Past Tense Story:** Write a silly story where every verb is in the past tense. Try to use as many irregular verbs as possible. Example: "A frog went to the store. He saw a hat. He thought it was beautiful. He took the hat and ate a sandwich..."

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes

**Pacing:** This lesson is memorization-heavy. Some students will learn all ten irregular verbs in one session; others will need two sessions. Do not rush. It is better to solidly memorize five verbs than to shakily know ten. If you split this into two sessions, teach Groups 1 and 2 (eight verbs) in the first session and Groups 3 plus review in the second.

**Why irregular verbs are hard:** English has more irregular verbs than most languages. Young children actually learn irregular forms first through speech ("I went," "I saw") but then overgeneralize the -ed rule as they learn it ("I goed," "I seed"). This overgeneralization is a SIGN OF LEARNING — the child has internalized the -ed rule so well that they apply it everywhere. Gently redirect without discouraging.

**The "sound test":** The strategy of adding -ed and checking if it sounds right works well for native English speakers and students who have been immersed in English. For English language learners, the sound test may be less reliable. For these students, emphasize the memorization approach with flashcards and repeated practice.

**Connection to reading:** When the student reads books, they encounter irregular past tense verbs constantly ("she thought," "he went," "they ran"). Point these out during reading time: "Look — the author used 'ran.' What is the present tense of 'ran'? (run) That is one of our irregular verbs!"

**Spelling of "said" and "thought":** These two words are particularly tricky to spell. "Said" is pronounced "sed" but spelled S-A-I-D. "Thought" has the silent -ough- pattern. Both are high-frequency words that the student will see and use constantly. Extra spelling practice with these two is worthwhile.

**Do not overwhelm:** The ten verbs in this lesson are the most essential. There are hundreds of irregular verbs in English, but these ten cover the vast majority of what a third grader needs. Additional irregular verbs can be introduced gradually throughout the year.
