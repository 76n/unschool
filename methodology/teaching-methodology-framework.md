## 1. Learning Philosophy

### Vision

Every child can build confidence, independence, and deep understanding in math and English. Our materials are designed so that a student who works through them consistently will not just pass tests — they will *think mathematically* and *communicate powerfully*.

### Core Principles

#### 1.1 Mastery-Based Progression

Students advance when they demonstrate understanding, not when a calendar says it's time to move on.

- **What this means in practice:** Each unit has clear mastery criteria. A student who hasn't mastered prerequisite skills receives targeted reinforcement before advancing. A student who has mastered them moves forward without unnecessary repetition.
- **No "coverage" mentality:** We would rather a student deeply understand fewer topics than superficially encounter many. Depth creates the foundation for later acceleration.

#### 1.2 Concrete-to-Abstract Progression

New concepts begin with tangible, sensory experiences and gradually move toward symbolic representation.

- **Math example (K-2):** Addition starts with physical objects (counters, blocks), moves to pictorial representations (number lines, ten-frames), then to symbolic notation (3 + 4 = 7).
- **Math example (6-8):** Proportional reasoning starts with hands-on scaling activities (recipes, maps), moves to ratio tables, then to algebraic expressions.
- **English example (K-2):** Letter sounds begin with oral language and physical letter formation, then connect to reading and writing.
- **English example (6-8):** Argumentative writing starts with verbal debate and opinion-sharing, moves to structured outlines, then to formal essays.

This follows a **CRA progression** (Concrete → Representational → Abstract) in math and an analogous **Experience → Model → Independent Practice** arc in English.

#### 1.3 Scaffolded Instruction

Every new skill is introduced with support that is gradually removed as the student gains competence.

- **I Do → We Do → You Do:** The facilitator models the skill, practices it together with the student, then the student works independently.
- **Worked examples before practice:** Students see completed examples with reasoning made visible before attempting problems on their own.
- **Prompt fading:** Writing prompts, sentence starters, and graphic organizers are provided early and gradually removed.

#### 1.4 Growth Mindset and Productive Struggle

Difficulty is not a sign of failure — it is where learning happens.

- Materials explicitly normalize mistakes as part of learning.
- Problems include "challenge" tiers that are designed to stretch students, not to be completed perfectly.
- Feedback language focuses on effort, strategy, and growth ("You tried a different approach — that's exactly how mathematicians think") rather than innate ability.

#### 1.5 Interleaving and Spaced Practice

Forgetting is natural. Our materials are designed to fight it.

- **Spaced review:** Each lesson includes a brief warm-up that revisits skills from prior units — not just the previous lesson.
- **Interleaved practice:** Problem sets mix current-topic problems with review problems from different topics, strengthening retrieval and discrimination.
- **Cumulative assessments:** Unit assessments include items from earlier units to reinforce long-term retention.

#### 1.6 Language-Rich Instruction Across Subjects

Math and English are not siloed. Mathematical reasoning requires precise language; reading comprehension requires logical reasoning.

- Math lessons include vocabulary instruction and require students to explain their thinking in words.
- English lessons include structured reasoning tasks (identifying evidence, evaluating arguments) that build logical thinking.

## 2. Scope and Sequence Overview

### 2.1 Grade Bands

Materials are organized into three grade bands, each with distinct developmental characteristics:

| Band | Grades | Key Developmental Traits |
|------|--------|--------------------------|
| **Early Elementary** | K-2 | Concrete thinking, learning to decode, building number sense, developing fine motor skills |
| **Upper Elementary** | 3-5 | Transitioning to abstract thinking, reading to learn (not just learning to read), multiplicative reasoning |
| **Middle School** | 6-8 | Abstract reasoning, formal argumentation, proportional and algebraic thinking, identity development |

### 2.2 Mathematics Scope and Sequence

#### Kindergarten
- **Counting and Cardinality:** Count to 100; one-to-one correspondence; compare quantities
- **Operations:** Addition and subtraction within 10 using objects and drawings
- **Place Value:** Compose and decompose numbers 11-19 into tens and ones
- **Geometry:** Identify and describe 2D and 3D shapes
- **Measurement:** Compare measurable attributes (length, weight)

#### Grade 1
- **Operations:** Add and subtract within 20; understand the meaning of the equal sign
- **Place Value:** Understand tens and ones; compare two-digit numbers
- **Measurement:** Measure lengths using non-standard and standard units; tell time to the hour and half-hour
- **Geometry:** Distinguish between defining and non-defining attributes of shapes; partition shapes into halves and quarters
- **Data:** Organize and interpret data with up to three categories

#### Grade 2
- **Operations:** Fluent addition and subtraction within 20; add and subtract within 100; introduction to multiplication as equal groups
- **Place Value:** Understand hundreds, tens, and ones; skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s
- **Measurement:** Measure and estimate lengths in standard units; solve word problems involving money and time
- **Geometry:** Recognize and draw shapes; partition rectangles into rows and columns of equal-size squares
- **Data:** Create and interpret picture graphs and bar graphs

#### Grade 3
- **Multiplication and Division:** Understand as equal groups, arrays, and area; fluency within 100; properties of operations
- **Fractions:** Understand as parts of a whole; represent on a number line; compare fractions with the same numerator or denominator
- **Operations:** Add and subtract within 1,000; round to the nearest 10 or 100
- **Measurement:** Solve problems involving time, mass, and liquid volume; understand area and perimeter
- **Data:** Scaled picture graphs and bar graphs; generate measurement data and display on line plots

#### Grade 4
- **Multi-Digit Operations:** Fluent multi-digit addition and subtraction; multi-digit multiplication (up to 4-digit by 1-digit); introduction to long division
- **Fractions:** Equivalence; compare fractions with unlike denominators; add and subtract fractions with like denominators; multiply fractions by whole numbers
- **Decimals:** Understand decimal notation for fractions (tenths and hundredths); compare decimals
- **Geometry:** Draw and identify lines, angles, and rays; classify shapes by properties; recognize line symmetry
- **Measurement:** Convert measurement units within a system; solve problems involving area and perimeter

#### Grade 5
- **Operations:** Fluent multi-digit multiplication; divide multi-digit dividends by two-digit divisors
- **Fractions:** Add, subtract, multiply, and divide fractions and mixed numbers
- **Decimals:** All four operations with decimals to hundredths; understand place value to thousandths
- **Geometry:** Graph points on the coordinate plane; classify 2D figures by properties
- **Volume:** Understand and measure volume of rectangular prisms
- **Algebraic Thinking:** Write and interpret numerical expressions; analyze patterns and relationships

#### Grade 6
- **Ratios and Proportional Relationships:** Understand ratios; use ratio reasoning to solve problems; unit rates
- **The Number System:** Divide fractions by fractions; compute fluently with multi-digit decimals; introduce negative numbers; absolute value; coordinate plane in all four quadrants
- **Expressions and Equations:** Write, evaluate, and simplify expressions; solve one-step equations and inequalities
- **Geometry:** Area of polygons; surface area and volume of 3D figures; nets
- **Statistics:** Recognize statistical questions; measures of center and variability; dot plots, histograms, box plots

#### Grade 7
- **Proportional Relationships:** Recognize and represent proportional relationships; constant of proportionality; percent problems
- **The Number System:** Operations with rational numbers (including negatives); convert between fractions, decimals, and percents
- **Expressions and Equations:** Simplify expressions using properties; solve multi-step equations and inequalities
- **Geometry:** Scale drawings; cross-sections of 3D figures; area and circumference of circles; angle relationships; surface area and volume
- **Statistics and Probability:** Random sampling; draw inferences; probability models; compound events

#### Grade 8
- **The Number System:** Irrational numbers; approximate irrational numbers
- **Expressions and Equations:** Integer exponents; scientific notation; solve linear equations (one variable); systems of two linear equations
- **Functions:** Define, evaluate, and compare functions; use functions to model relationships; linear vs. nonlinear
- **Geometry:** Transformations (translations, rotations, reflections, dilations); congruence and similarity; Pythagorean Theorem; volume of cylinders, cones, and spheres
- **Statistics:** Construct and interpret scatter plots; two-way tables; line of best fit (informal)

### 2.3 English Language Arts Scope and Sequence

#### Kindergarten
- **Phonemic Awareness:** Recognize and produce rhyming words; isolate and blend individual sounds
- **Phonics:** Know letter-sound correspondences for all single letters; read common high-frequency words
- **Reading:** Retell familiar stories with key details; identify characters, settings, and major events
- **Writing:** Use a combination of drawing, dictating, and writing to compose opinions, informative texts, and narratives
- **Language:** Print upper- and lowercase letters; use frequently occurring nouns and verbs; capitalize the first word in a sentence

#### Grade 1
- **Phonics:** Know consonant digraphs, vowel teams, and common spelling patterns; decode one-syllable words
- **Reading Fluency:** Read grade-level text with purpose and understanding
- **Comprehension:** Ask and answer questions about key details; identify the main topic; describe the connection between events or ideas
- **Writing:** Write opinion, informative, and narrative pieces with structure (introduction, details, closure)
- **Language:** Use common, proper, and possessive nouns; use conventional spelling for common words; use end punctuation

#### Grade 2
- **Phonics:** Know spelling-sound correspondences for common vowel teams; decode two-syllable words with long vowels
- **Fluency:** Read grade-level text orally with accuracy and expression
- **Comprehension:** Ask and answer who, what, where, when, why, how; recount stories and determine their central message; describe how reasons support points in a text
- **Writing:** Write opinion, informative, and narrative pieces with focus, organization, and development; begin revision and editing
- **Language:** Use collective nouns, irregular plural nouns, reflexive pronouns; use an apostrophe for possessives; consult reference materials for spelling

#### Grade 3
- **Reading Literature:** Ask and answer questions referring explicitly to the text; describe characters and explain how their actions contribute to events; determine the meaning of words and phrases
- **Reading Informational:** Determine main idea; describe the relationship between events, procedures, or concepts; use text features to locate information
- **Writing:** Write opinion pieces with reasons; write informative texts with facts and definitions; write narratives with dialogue and sequence; conduct short research projects
- **Language:** Form and use regular and irregular verbs; form and use comparative and superlative adjectives; use commas and quotation marks in dialogue; use dictionaries and glossaries

#### Grade 4
- **Reading Literature:** Determine theme; describe characters, settings, and events in depth; compare and contrast point of view; explain structural differences between poems, drama, and prose
- **Reading Informational:** Determine main idea with supporting details; explain events, procedures, or concepts; compare and contrast a firsthand and secondhand account; interpret information from charts, graphs, and diagrams
- **Writing:** Develop opinion, informative, and narrative writing with greater sophistication; use transitional words and phrases; type a minimum of one page in a single sitting
- **Language:** Use relative pronouns and adverbs; form and use progressive verb tenses; order adjectives; use correct capitalization; choose words and phrases for effect

#### Grade 5
- **Reading Literature:** Quote accurately when explaining what the text says; determine theme and summarize; compare and contrast characters, settings, or events; analyze how visual elements contribute to meaning
- **Reading Informational:** Quote accurately; determine two or more main ideas; explain relationships between concepts; integrate information from multiple texts on the same topic
- **Writing:** Introduce topics clearly; develop points with facts, details, and quotes; use varied transitional phrases; produce writing appropriate to task, purpose, and audience
- **Language:** Use correlative conjunctions; use verb tense to convey times, sequences, and conditions; use punctuation for items in a series; expand, combine, and reduce sentences for interest

#### Grade 6
- **Reading Literature:** Cite textual evidence; determine theme and how it develops; analyze how a sentence, chapter, or scene fits into the overall structure; explain how an author develops point of view
- **Reading Informational:** Cite textual evidence; determine central idea and how it is conveyed; analyze how a key individual, event, or idea is introduced and elaborated; determine author's point of view or purpose; integrate information from different media
- **Writing:** Write arguments with claims, reasons, and evidence; write informative texts examining a topic; write narratives with relevant descriptive details; draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis
- **Language:** Ensure subject-verb and pronoun-antecedent agreement; recognize variations from standard English; use punctuation (commas, parentheses, dashes) to set off nonrestrictive elements; vary sentence patterns for meaning and style

#### Grade 7
- **Reading Literature:** Cite several pieces of textual evidence; determine theme and analyze its development; analyze how an author develops and contrasts points of view; compare and contrast a fictional portrayal with a historical account
- **Reading Informational:** Cite several pieces of evidence; determine two or more central ideas; analyze how the author distinguishes their position from others; evaluate the argument and claims in a text; trace and evaluate the argument and specific claims
- **Writing:** Write arguments with clear reasons and relevant evidence acknowledging opposing claims; write informative texts that examine a topic with relevant facts, definitions, and details; write narratives using techniques such as dialogue, pacing, and description
- **Language:** Explain the function of phrases and clauses; choose among simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences; use a comma to separate coordinate adjectives; spell correctly

#### Grade 8
- **Reading Literature:** Cite the strongest textual evidence; determine theme and analyze its development including relationship to characters, setting, and plot; analyze how differences in points of view create effects such as suspense or humor; analyze how a modern work draws on themes, patterns of events, or character types from traditional works
- **Reading Informational:** Cite the strongest textual evidence; determine central idea and analyze its development; analyze how a text makes connections among ideas; delineate and evaluate the argument and specific claims, assessing reasoning and evidence; analyze two or more texts with conflicting information
- **Writing:** Write arguments with claims supported by clear reasons and relevant evidence; acknowledge and distinguish counterclaims; write informative texts with well-chosen facts, definitions, concrete details, quotations; write narratives that use techniques to sequence events logically
- **Language:** Explain the function of verbals (gerunds, participles, infinitives); form and use verbs in active and passive voice; form and use verbs in the indicative, imperative, interrogative, conditional, and subjunctive moods; recognize and correct inappropriate shifts in verb voice and mood

## 3. Lesson Structure Template

Every lesson follows this consistent structure. Timing is flexible based on the recommended session length for each grade band.

### Recommended Session Lengths

| Band | Recommended Session | Minimum Effective Session |
|------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| K-2 | 30-40 minutes | 20 minutes |
| 3-5 | 40-50 minutes | 30 minutes |
| 6-8 | 50-60 minutes | 40 minutes |

### Template

```
# [Subject] — Grade [X] — Unit [Y], Lesson [Z]

## Topic
[Concise topic title]

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to:
- [Specific, measurable objective 1]
- [Specific, measurable objective 2]

## Prerequisites
- [Prior skill or lesson required]

## Materials Needed
- [List of physical or printed materials]

---

## Warm-Up (5-10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Activate prior knowledge and review spaced-practice items.

[2-4 brief review problems or discussion prompts from prior units]

---

## Direct Instruction (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Introduce the new concept with explicit modeling.

### Key Vocabulary
- **[Term]:** [Student-friendly definition]

### Instruction Notes
[Step-by-step explanation of the concept. Include visual aids,
manipulative suggestions, or read-aloud notes as applicable.]

### Worked Examples
[1-2 fully worked examples with reasoning shown at each step]

---

## Guided Practice (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Practice the new skill together with facilitator support.

[3-5 problems or activities completed WITH the student.
Include prompts for the facilitator: "Ask: What do you notice?"
"If the student struggles with ___, try ___."]

---

## Independent Practice (10-15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Student works alone to build fluency and confidence.

[5-10 problems or a structured task.
Include a mix of current-topic and interleaved review items.
Mark difficulty: ★ (on-level), ★★ (stretch), ★★★ (challenge).]

---

## Check for Understanding (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Quick formative assessment to gauge mastery.

[1-3 "exit ticket" items that directly assess the lesson objectives.
Include mastery threshold: e.g., "Mastery = 2 out of 3 correct."]

### If Mastery Is Not Met
[Specific re-teaching suggestion or practice pointer]

### If Mastery Is Met
[Extension activity or preview of next lesson]

---

## Extension (Optional)
[Enrichment activity for students who finish early or demonstrate
advanced understanding. Real-world connections, puzzles, or
cross-curricular links.]

---

## Teacher/Facilitator Notes
[Tips for common misconceptions, discussion questions,
pacing adjustments, and differentiation suggestions]

## Answer Key
[Answers for all practice problems and check-for-understanding items]
```

## 4. Assessment Methodology

### 4.1 Assessment Philosophy

Assessment exists to inform instruction, not to rank students. We use assessment to answer: *What does this student understand? What do they need next?*

### 4.2 Types of Assessment

| Type | Purpose | Frequency | Examples |
|------|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Formative (in-lesson)** | Check understanding during instruction | Every lesson | Exit tickets, observation checklists, verbal questioning |
| **Practice Assessment** | Build fluency and identify gaps | 2-3 times per unit | Problem sets with self-checking, timed fluency drills (math), reading response journals |
| **Unit Assessment** | Measure mastery of unit objectives | End of each unit | Written assessment covering all unit objectives + cumulative review items |
| **Cumulative Assessment** | Measure long-term retention | End of each quarter or trimester | Comprehensive assessment covering all units completed to date |

### 4.3 Mastery Criteria

Mastery is defined per-objective, not per-assessment. A student demonstrates mastery when they can:

1. **Solve or perform** the skill accurately in varied contexts (not just the exact format they practiced).
2. **Explain** their reasoning or process (age-appropriately).
3. **Apply** the skill to an unfamiliar problem or text.

#### Mastery Thresholds

| Level | Score Range | Meaning | Action |
|-------|------------|---------|--------|
| **Mastered** | 85-100% | Student owns this skill | Advance to next topic; include in spaced review |
| **Approaching** | 70-84% | Partial understanding with gaps | Targeted reteaching on specific gaps; additional practice before advancing |
| **Developing** | Below 70% | Significant gaps remain | Return to prerequisite skills; use alternative instructional approach |

### 4.4 Rubric Design Principles

All rubrics follow these rules:

1. **Observable and specific:** Criteria describe what the student *does*, not vague qualities. "Uses at least two pieces of text evidence" not "shows good understanding."
2. **Leveled performance:** Each criterion has 3-4 performance levels with clear descriptions of what each level looks like.
3. **Student-facing language:** Rubrics are written so that students (and parents) can understand what is expected and how to improve.
4. **Aligned to objectives:** Every rubric criterion maps directly to a stated learning objective.

#### Standard Rubric Format

```
| Criterion | Mastered (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| [Skill]   | [Description of mastery-level performance] | [Description of approaching-level performance] | [Description of developing-level performance] |
```

### 4.5 Formative vs. Summative Balance

- **80% formative, 20% summative.** Most assessment is woven into daily instruction.
- Formative assessment informs the *next* lesson. If most students miss the exit ticket, reteach before moving on.
- Summative assessments confirm what formative assessment already indicated — they should not be surprising.

## 5. Differentiation Framework

### 5.1 Guiding Principle

Every lesson is designed for the on-level student and includes built-in pathways for students above and below grade level. Differentiation is not a separate program — it is embedded in the materials.

### 5.2 Three Tiers of Support

| Tier | Student Profile | Strategy |
|------|----------------|----------|
| **Tier 1: Core** | On grade level | Standard lesson as written |
| **Tier 2: Scaffolded** | Below grade level or struggling with prerequisites | Additional scaffolds activated within the same lesson |
| **Tier 3: Extended** | Above grade level or demonstrates early mastery | Extension activities and deeper exploration within the same lesson |

### 5.3 Scaffolding Strategies (Tier 2)

These are built into lesson materials and activated when needed:

**Math:**
- Provide manipulatives or visual models longer (delay transition to abstract)
- Reduce the number of practice problems but increase guided practice time
- Offer partially completed worked examples (student fills in missing steps)
- Use smaller numbers or simpler contexts for the same skill
- Provide a "help card" with key steps or formulas

**English:**
- Provide sentence starters and graphic organizers
- Pre-teach vocabulary before reading
- Use shorter or adapted texts at a lower Lexile level covering the same theme/skill
- Offer word banks for writing tasks
- Allow oral responses before written responses
- Pair reading with audio support

### 5.4 Extension Strategies (Tier 3)

**Math:**
- Open-ended problems with multiple solution paths
- "Prove it" tasks requiring written justification
- Cross-curricular connections (apply math to science or social studies contexts)
- Problems with larger numbers, more complex contexts, or additional constraints
- Introduce the next concept in the sequence as a discovery activity

**English:**
- Read a more complex text on the same theme and compare
- Write in a different genre or for a different audience
- Analyze author's craft at a deeper level (why did the author make this structural choice?)
- Research and present on a related topic
- Peer teaching: explain the concept to another student

### 5.5 How Differentiation Appears in Lesson Plans

Every lesson plan includes differentiation cues at these points:

1. **Guided Practice:** "If the student struggles with ___, try ___" notes.
2. **Independent Practice:** Problems marked with difficulty tiers (★ / ★★ / ★★★). Tier 2 students focus on ★ problems. Tier 3 students include ★★★ problems.
3. **Check for Understanding:** "If mastery is not met" section with specific reteaching moves.
4. **Extension:** Optional enrichment for early finishers or Tier 3 students.

## 6. Parent/Teacher Facilitation Model

### 6.1 Design Assumption

These materials are designed to be used by a **caring adult who is not necessarily a trained teacher** — a parent, tutor, or community volunteer. The materials do the heavy instructional lifting; the facilitator provides the human connection, encouragement, and structure.

### 6.2 Facilitator Roles

| Role | What It Looks Like |
|------|-------------------|
| **Guide** | Read the lesson plan; follow the script for direct instruction; ask the suggested questions |
| **Observer** | Watch the student work during guided and independent practice; note where they struggle or succeed |
| **Encourager** | Provide specific, effort-focused praise; normalize mistakes; celebrate growth |
| **Decision-maker** | Use the check-for-understanding results to decide: advance, reteach, or extend |

### 6.3 What Facilitators Do NOT Need to Do

- Invent explanations or examples (these are provided)
- Grade subjectively (rubrics and answer keys are provided)
- Know the content deeply in advance (teacher notes explain the "why" behind each lesson)
- Create a pacing schedule (scope and sequence provides recommended pacing)

### 6.4 Session Structure for Facilitators

1. **Before the session (2-3 minutes):** Read the lesson plan. Review the teacher notes. Gather materials.
2. **During the session:** Follow the lesson structure. Use the suggested prompts and questions. Watch the student and note observations.
3. **After the session (2-3 minutes):** Review the check-for-understanding results. Record mastery status. Decide on next session's plan (advance, reteach, or extend).

### 6.5 Facilitator Guides

Each unit includes a **Facilitator Guide** with:

- **Unit overview:** What the student will learn and why it matters
- **Prerequisite check:** Quick assessment to confirm the student is ready for this unit
- **Pacing guidance:** Recommended number of sessions and how to adjust if needed
- **Common misconceptions:** What students typically get wrong and how to address it
- **Conversation starters:** Questions to ask the student to deepen understanding
- **When to seek help:** Signs that the student may need additional support beyond these materials

### 6.6 Self-Directed vs. Facilitated Use

| Mode | Grade Band | Description |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Fully facilitated** | K-2 | Adult is present and actively guiding throughout the session |
| **Partially facilitated** | 3-5 | Adult introduces the lesson, supports guided practice, then monitors independent work; student can do warm-up and independent practice more autonomously |
| **Guided independence** | 6-8 | Student reads instruction and works through practice independently; adult checks in for guided practice and reviews check-for-understanding results; adult available for questions |

As students mature, the facilitation model shifts toward coaching and accountability rather than direct instruction.

## Appendix A: Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Mastery** | Demonstrated ability to perform a skill accurately, explain reasoning, and apply to new contexts |
| **Scaffolding** | Temporary support structures that help a student access content above their independent level |
| **Interleaving** | Mixing practice problems from different topics in a single session |
| **Spaced practice** | Revisiting previously learned material at increasing intervals |
| **CRA progression** | Concrete → Representational → Abstract: a sequence for introducing mathematical concepts |
| **Exit ticket** | A brief (1-3 item) assessment at the end of a lesson to check for understanding |
| **Formative assessment** | Assessment used during instruction to guide teaching decisions |
| **Summative assessment** | Assessment used after instruction to measure overall learning |
| **Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3** | Levels of instructional support: core, scaffolded, and extended |

## Appendix B: Alignment Notes

This framework is designed to align with widely adopted standards (e.g., Common Core State Standards) while remaining adaptable to other state or international standards. Specific standard alignments will be noted in individual lesson plans.
